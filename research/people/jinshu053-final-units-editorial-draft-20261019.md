## Funeral address closing

### s0166 — 庶光來葉，永世不泯。」

Current literal and idiomatic both render the sense, but the literal closes
with a single quotation mark and the idiomatic with a double quotation mark.
The preceding source s0143 starts the funeral address, not a separate
speaker at each sentence. In coordination with the selected s0143–s0165
repairs, propose:

- Literal: `May this glory reach generations to come, never fading through the ages."`
- Idiomatic: `May that glory shine on future generations and never fade."`

Review precisely what `光` refers to and the surrounding nested-quotation
convention. The current text's “light” is defensible; this is not a source
restoration.

## Early biography (IDs s0199–s0205)

- **s0199** `時望氣者言廣陵有天子氣，故封為廣陵王，邑五萬戶。`: keep
  literal. Idiomatic “Astrologers” is potentially too specific for observers
  of qi, and “fifty thousand households” should be understood as fief size,
  not inhabitants. Proposed idiomatic: `At the time, readers of omens said
  that Guangling had the aura of an emperor, so Yu was enfeoffed as Prince of
  Guangling with a fief of fifty thousand households.`
- **s0200** `以劉寔為師，孟珩為友，楊准、馮蓀為文學。`: both fields
  preserve names and roles; keep pending normal office-title review. Do not
  silently treat `文學` as necessarily literary writing rather than an office.
- **s0201** `惠帝即位，立為皇太子。`: keep both; the idiomatic identifies Yu
  from context. Standardize “Emperor Hui” consistently elsewhere if desired.
- **s0202** `盛選德望以為師傅，以何劭為太師，王戎為太傅，楊濟為太保，裴楷為少師，張華為少傅，和嶠為少保。`:
  keep both pending independent review of the six conventional paired office
  translations. “德望” includes both virtue and reputation, reflected in the
  literal; proposed idiomatic to make that explicit: `The court selected men
  renowned for virtue as his tutors and guardians: He Shao as Grand Tutor,
  Wang Rong as Grand Mentor, Yang Ji as Grand Guardian, Pei Kai as Junior
  Tutor, Zhang Hua as Junior Mentor, and He Qiao as Junior Guardian.`
- **s0203** `元康元年，出就東宮，又詔曰：「遹尚幼蒙，今出東宮，惟當賴師傅群賢之訓。`:
  both current fields wrongly say Yu *left* the Eastern Palace. The earlier
  separate bilingual draft proposes the needed correction and an open quote;
  review that draft rather than create a conflicting duplicate here.
- **s0204** `其遊處左右，宜得正人使共周旋，能相長益者。」`: current both
  approximate the point, but “mutually” may imply equal improvement rather
  than benefit to the prince. Proposed literal: `Those who associate with him
  and attend him should be upright men who can keep him company and help him
  to improve."` Proposed idiomatic: `The men around him, whether at leisure
  or in attendance, should be upright companions who can help him grow."`
  Review syntactic attachment of `相` and maintain quotation continuity.
- **s0205** `於是使太保衛瓘息庭、司空泰息略、太子太傅楊濟息毖、太子少師裴楷息憲、太子少傅張華息禕、尚書令華暠息恆與太子游處，以相輔導焉。`:
  current literal retains offices, idiomatic drops four of them; both render
  `司空泰` ambiguously as “Director of Works Tai,” which a reader could
  mistake for a surname. Proposed literal: `Thereupon the sons of Grand
  Guardian Wei Guan, Ting; of the Director of Works Tai, Lue; of the Crown
  Prince's Grand Mentor Yang Ji, Bi; of the Crown Prince's Junior Tutor Pei
  Kai, Xian; of the Crown Prince's Junior Mentor Zhang Hua, Yi; and of the
  Minister Director Hua Gao, Heng, were made companions of the crown prince,
  to help guide him.` Proposed idiomatic: `The court therefore had Ting, son
  of Grand Guardian Wei Guan; Lue, son of Tai, the Director of Works; Bi, son
  of the crown prince's Grand Mentor Yang Ji; Xian, son of his Junior Tutor
  Pei Kai; Yi, son of his Junior Mentor Zhang Hua; and Heng, son of Minister
  Director Hua Gao, keep the crown prince company and guide him.`
  HOLD on the precise office nomenclature and any expansion of Tai's surname:
  [Jinshu 037](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/晉書/卷037) places Lue with Hua
  Heng in this Eastern Palace cohort and identifies the royal Sima family
  context, while [Jinshu 053](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/晉書/卷053) here
  says only 泰. Verify identity across both records and extraction before
  adding “Sima” as an English expansion; do not add it by assumption.

## Historian's assessment and encomium

### s0189 — 既而中宮凶忍，久懷危害之心，外戚諂諛，競進讒邪之說；

- Literal: `Then the empress was cruel and ruthless, long harboring an intent to harm him, while her relatives by marriage flattered her and vied to advance malicious slanders;`
- Idiomatic: `The empress had long harbored cruel designs against him, and her relatives competed to flatter her and spread malicious accusations;`
- Review: 中宮 denotes the empress in context. `危害` is harm, not necessarily a previously formulated plan to murder. 外戚 are relatives by marriage; “her” is contextual, not stated by that word alone.

### s0190 — 坎牲之謀已構，斃犬之譖遂行；

- Literal: `The plot of the buried sacrifice had been laid; the slander of the dead dog was then carried out;`
- Idiomatic: `A plot like that of the buried sacrifice was devised, and a slander like that of the dead dog was put into effect;`
- HOLD for allusive rendering and explanatory notes: [循陔纂聞](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/mid-page/7555251822035402798) explicitly explains 坎牲 as the plot against Song prince Cuo and 斃犬 as the slander against Jin prince Shen Sheng. [大學衍義](https://www.shidianguji.com/mid-page/7371189837489143859) quotes the buried-sacrifice account. The latter allusion concerns a dog that died after tasting poisoned sacrificial food, not a “dog-burial” slander. These are comparisons; do not recast them as literal events in Yu's own biography.

### s0191 — 一人乏探隱之聰，百辟無爭臣之節。

- Literal: `The one man lacked the discernment to uncover what was hidden, and the many officials lacked the resolve of remonstrating ministers.`
- Idiomatic: `The ruler lacked the insight to see the hidden plot, and none of his officials showed the resolve to remonstrate.`
- Review: “the one man” alludes to the emperor; the idiomatic's “none” is a strong reading of 無, preserve if reviewer agrees. No need to name Emperor Hui in the text itself.

### s0192 — 遂使冤逾楚建，酷甚戾園。

- Literal: `Thus his wrong surpassed that of Prince Jian of Chu, and the cruelty exceeded that of the Prince Li of Han.`
- Idiomatic: `The injustice he suffered surpassed even that of Prince Jian of Chu; the cruelty was greater than in the case of Han's Prince Li.`
- Review: 戾園 is the burial precinct associated with Han Prince Li, not a prison. [循陔纂聞](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/mid-page/7555251822035402798) explicitly identifies both comparisons. The proposed idiomatic expands the allusions; consider a footnote rather than obscure them.

### s0193 — 雖復禮備哀榮，情深憫慟，亦何補於荼毒者哉！

- Literal: `Though the rites were complete in their mourning and honors and the feeling of pity and grief was deep, how could that make amends to the one who had suffered such cruelty?`
- Idiomatic: `However complete the funeral honors and however deep the grief, what could they do for the man who had suffered such cruelty?`
- Review: do not translate 荼毒 as proof of death by poison; s0123 specifies death by pestle.

### s0194 — 贊曰：湣懷聰穎，諒惟天挺。

- Literal: `The encomium says: Minhuai was intelligent and gifted, truly endowed by Heaven.`
- Idiomatic: `The encomium says: Minhuai's intelligence and gifts were truly bestowed by Heaven.`

### s0195 — 皇祖鐘心，庶僚引領。

- Literal: `His imperial grandfather centered his affection on him; the many officials craned their necks.`
- Idiomatic: `His imperial grandfather cherished him, and the officials looked on with eager expectation.`
- Review: 鐘心 conveys concentrated concern or affection; the idiomatic's eager expectation explains 引領 without asserting, as the current text does, that officials specifically awaited his reign.

### s0196 — 震宮肇建，儲德不恢。

- Literal: `When the heir was first established in the Eastern Palace, the virtues of the heir did not flourish.`
- Idiomatic: `Once he was made heir, his virtue failed to grow.`
- Review HOLD: confirm exact syntax `震宮肇建` — the Eastern Palace is a metaphor for the heir, and this need not assert a building was constructed. The idiomatic is paraphrastic; compare context and the office/installation sequence.

### s0197 — 掇蜂構隙，歸胙生災。

- Literal: `Picking the bee brought estrangement; the returned sacrificial meat brought disaster.`
- Idiomatic: `As in the story of the picked bee, a rift was contrived; as with the returned sacrificial meat, disaster followed.`
- HOLD: both are literary analogies, not bees or actual sacrificial meat in the immediate narrative of Yu. [Ministry of Education dictionary](https://dict.revised.moe.edu.tw/dictView.jsp?ID=47899&q=1&word=%E6%8E%87) identifies 掇蜂譖子; [Zuo tradition discussion](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/SK0404/chapter/1m16wzur4nytl) connects 歸胙 to the plot against Shen Sheng. Verify the primary accounts and decide on notes before publishing; `構隙` is a contrived rift rather than “schemes split cracks.”

### s0198 — 既罹凶忍，徒望歸來。

- Literal: `Once he had fallen victim to such cruelty, the hope of his return was in vain.`
- Idiomatic: `He fell victim to cruelty, and hopes for his return came to nothing.`
- Review: the current idiomatic narrows the verse to death and “restoration”;
  `歸來` is return, possibly echoing Prince Li's Return-and-Longing Terrace.
  The allusion is plausible but not proved and should not be asserted as fact.
