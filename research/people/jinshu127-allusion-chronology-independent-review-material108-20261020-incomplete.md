## Lü Meng, p009

The local s0011 uses the idiom 吳下阿蒙. [Sanguozhi 54, Lü Meng biography,
quoting *Jiangbiao zhuan*](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E4%B8%89%E5%9C%8B%E5%BF%97/%E5%8D%B754),
has Lu Su say 非復吳下阿蒙 to Meng after mentioning Lü Ziming. This is
specific evidence for Lü Meng as the literary referent, not proof that the
later speech is an actual encounter with him. The [Taiwan Ministry of
Education idiom dictionary](https://dict.idioms.moe.edu.tw/idiomView.jsp?ID=-584&la=0&webMd=2)
identifies the allusion similarly, and cites this Jinshu sentence.

[Sanguozhi 55, Gan Ning biography](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E4%B8%89%E5%9C%8B%E5%BF%97/%E5%8D%B755)
explicitly names 呂蒙 with Gan Ning in Sun Quan's force at Xiaoyao Ford under
建安二十年. The separate, later [Zizhi tongjian 67](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%B3%87%E6%B2%BB%E9%80%9A%E9%91%91/%E5%8D%B7067)
places that Hefei campaign and retreat in the eighth lunar month under its
heading 建安二十年（公元二一五年）. Its month anchor avoids treating the
entire lunar regnal year as identical to a Western calendar year. Subject to
the evidence model's handling of external witnesses, **AD 215 is a separately
sourced activity attestation**, not the date of s0011, nor the limits of an
activity interval. The linked modern year heading is editorial apparatus,
not a date written by the classical author.

Sanguozhi 54 says he died aged 42 after the Guan Yu episode; it gives neither
a Western date nor an explicit death-year heading. [Zizhi tongjian 68](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%B3%87%E6%B2%BB%E9%80%9A%E9%91%91/%E5%8D%B7068)
places Guan Yu's capture in the twelfth month and then relates Lü's illness,
partial recovery and death within its Jian'an 24 narrative. Narrative grouping
does not give an exact Western day, and the lunar-month boundary matters. The
note correctly leaves precise birth and death endpoints unresolved on this
evidence; no assertion that 219 is impossible is implied.

## Wu Chen, p012

In [Zuozhuan, Cheng 8](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%98%A5%E7%A7%8B%E5%B7%A6%E6%B0%8F%E5%82%B3/%E6%88%90%E5%85%AC),
the autumn account says 晉侯使申公巫臣如吳 and describes his request to
pass through Ju. [Fu Xun's later rearrangement](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%98%A5%E7%A7%8B%E5%B7%A6%E5%82%B3%E5%B1%AC%E4%BA%8B_(%E5%9B%9B%E5%BA%AB%E5%85%A8%E6%9B%B8%E6%9C%AC)/%E5%8D%B719)
explicitly labels that passage 八年秋; it is not an independent witness to
the event. [Yang Zhaogui, p. 42](https://cup.cuhk.edu.hk/chinesepress/journal/ICS58/ICS58_41-64.pdf),
explicitly equates Cheng 8 with 前583; another paper cited in the research
note does the same and quotes the embassy. Thus **BC 583 is the conventional
year of this separate autumn embassy account**, not a date stated by the
Jinshu s0016 speaker or the date of the earlier remonstrance. It cannot be
used as a birth/death year or activity endpoint. Whether the current schema
can cite such external person-level evidence still needs a production-path
decision.

The Zuozhuan's Cheng 7 account includes a Wu mission and earlier backstory
about Xia Ji, while Cheng 8 separately dates an embassy. A modern author
equating Cheng 7 with BC 584 does not by itself establish which narrated
events belong to that particular year: the narrative gathers prior events and
their later consequences. Do not assume that s0016's King Zhuang remonstrance
occurred in 584 or that all references to Wu missions describe one embassy.

## Xia Ji, p013

The [Zuozhuan Cheng 2 narrative](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%98%A5%E7%A7%8B%E5%B7%A6%E6%B0%8F%E5%82%B3/%E6%88%90%E5%85%AC)
first recalls King Zhuang's attempt to take Xia Ji and Wu Chen's objection;
it then tells of Xiang Lao's death at Bi and later events, culminating in the
journey with Wu Chen after Qi's defeat. The same chapter's *jing* records the
battle of An in the sixth month, while its narrative says 齊師新敗 when Wu
Chen and Xia Ji travel. [The scholarly article cited by the original note](https://31653860.s21i.faiusr.com/61/8/ABUIABA9GAAgsoTcuAYooO3lmwc.pdf),
PDF pages 6–7, labels the Cheng 2 section BC 589 and Cheng 8 BC 583. This
corroborates the **section label**; it does not independently establish that
the Cheng 2 section's earlier Zhuang episode occurred in 589. The local
s0016 alludes to that earlier remonstrance, not the subsequent journey or Wu
embassy. The available grouping does not substantiate BC 584 for Xia Ji's
alluded episode; keep any activity year unresolved until the specific event
and evidence representation are independently established.

Further evidence for the **earlier event**, distinct from the 589 section
and the 583 embassy: [Zuozhuan, Xuan 11](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%98%A5%E7%A7%8B%E5%B7%A6%E6%B0%8F%E5%82%B3/%E5%AE%A3%E5%85%AC)
places Chu's campaign against Chen in winter and describes Xia Zhengshu's
execution; it does not itself name Xia Ji in that passage. The later Cheng 2
account explicitly links Zhuang's proposed taking of her and Wu Chen's
remonstrance to that campaign. [Li Yiyan's published reconstruction](https://thup.site.nthu.edu.tw/var/file/210/1210/img/498827403.pdf),
PDF p. 16, conventionally converts Xuan 11 to BC 598, and places her capture
with the campaign; the author labels the chronological sequence estimates.
This is a stronger research lead for the alluded setting than BC 584, but it
does not by itself establish an exact Western date for the remonstrance. A
[study of the Tsinghua bamboo text and Zuozhuan](https://chinese.ncku.edu.tw/var/file/142/1142/img/2248/5502.pdf),
PDF p. 24, highlights differences in narrative arrangement, including
Cheng 2's later retrospection. Resolve the chronology, witness differences
and packet representation independently before recording a year.
