## Findings

The local source says `成王之會` and then `殷公、曹叔`; it supplies no
reign year or cyclical date. A matching earlier textual account in the
transmitted *Yizhoushu*, “Wang hui,” calls the gathering `成周之會` and
locates the Yin duke and Cao Shu at the assembly. Its wording supports
investigating an association with this account; it does **not** make the later
Jin historian an independent eyewitness, supply an absolute year, or prove
that every identification and reported gesture is historically established.
The account needs separate person/identity and editorial assessment.

Two academic chronological reconstructions demonstrably disagree about the
succession after Cheng. Indiana University's Western Zhou inscription teaching
edition lists Cheng 1042–1006 BC. An *Early China* study reports that the
Chronology Project chose Kang's initial year as 1020 BC, that Shaughnessy
chooses 1005 BC, and itself argues for 1010 BC, each under different assumptions.
No one of these is an absolute date for this particular gathering. They do not
prove a universal exhaustive range: do not convert their outer endpoints into
an unqualified `westernInterval`, or attach them as the participants' lifetime
or activity spans. Retain all six research holds pending defensible policy for
representing competing reconstructions and independent date review.

The existing unresolved reason mentions a competing 18-year Cheng reign
without identifying a witness. This investigation has not verified that
specific claim. It should not be presented as established fact merely because
it appears in a retained candidate; replace or corroborate it only in an
appropriately scoped, independently reviewed repair. The two non-temporal
event claims' `explicit` confidence and assertion of a specific bow also merit
editorial review against the full Jin passage; this note does not make changes.
