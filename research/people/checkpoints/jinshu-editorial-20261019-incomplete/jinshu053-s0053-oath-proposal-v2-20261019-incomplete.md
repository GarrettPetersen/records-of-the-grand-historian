The independent reviewer is right that the proposed `if Heaven permits` adds an explicitly conditional marker not present in `皇天許當`; Hu Sanxing does not gloss the construction. The reviewer is also right that the original literal `raw flesh` does not literally represent `毛`, and that `place Jiang over the inner court` risks implying a mere administrative appointment. Adopt the reviewer's revised words for consideration:

- **Literal:** “Chewing hair and drinking blood beneath the three luminaries, High Heaven permits it; we shall sweep away the harm, establish Daowen as king, and make Jiang the inner ruler.”
- **Idiomatic:** “Beneath the sun, moon, and stars, we pledge with hair and blood: Heaven gives its leave, and we shall sweep away the danger, make Daowen king, and make Jiang ruler of the inner court.”

The punctuation and division between `許` and `當`, the anticipatory `it`, and the future auxiliary are interpretive. Another possible construction has Heaven permitting the proposed actions; `當` can express obligation or expectation rather than straightforward prediction. Neither the [Jinshu](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%99%89%E6%9B%B8/%E5%8D%B7053) passage nor the cited [Zizhi Tongjian 83 and Hu Sanxing's commentary](https://ancient-china-books.github.io/zizhitongjian/OEBPS/Text/6227.html) resolves that syntactic issue conclusively. Retain a philological hold where the acceptance workflow requires this ambiguity to be resolved, rather than represent the exact wording as settled.

The source presents a forged, attributed document; the proposed words report its speaker's claimed divine permission and planned acts, not divine approval, an actual oath ceremony or an accomplished appointment. Hu's `謂盟誓也` supports explaining the oath, and his `言將立為后也` supports a royal-consort interpretation, but neither requires importing Jiang Jun's personal name or merging the independently held Jiang records.
