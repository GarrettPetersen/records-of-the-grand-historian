## Source and commentary

The local s0053 reads `茹毛飲血於三辰之下，皇天許當掃除患害，立道文為王，蔣為內主。` The preceding local units frame the text as a draft prepared by Pan Yue and copied under compulsion while the prince was drunk, not reliable evidence of a plot. [Jinshu 53](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%99%89%E6%9B%B8/%E5%8D%B7053) preserves the same clause. The parallel account in [Zizhi Tongjian 83, annotated by Hu Sanxing](https://ancient-china-books.github.io/zizhitongjian/OEBPS/Text/6227.html) §8, carries `蔣氏` where this Jinshu witness has `蔣`. Hu explicitly glosses `茹毛飲血` as a covenant oath (`謂盟誓也`), identifies Daowen with Bin, and understands the proposed `內主` as being established as empress. He identifies the parallel text's Jiang as Bin's mother Jiang Baolin; the same account names her Jiang Jun in its narrative. These are attributed commentary/parallel evidence, not grounds to silently merge local Jiang records or to assert the forged plan happened.

`三辰` conventionally denotes the sun, moon, and stars; “three luminaries” in the literal field preserves the source's collective expression. Hu does **not** in the quoted note explain `皇天許當`. It can be read as invoking heaven's permission before the actions, but whether `許` is asserted, desired, or conditional and the precise force of `當` remain interpretive questions; no definitive grammatical solution is claimed here. A conditional formulation avoids stating as fact that Heaven has already approved, but is still an interpretation, not a literal equivalence. The next sentence's `願成` is a wish for the plan to succeed; that context supports caution about converting the invocation into a guarantee, but does not itself settle the syntax.

## Proposed paired correction, not approved

**Literal:** “Eating raw flesh and drinking blood beneath the three luminaries, if High Heaven permits, we shall sweep away the dangers, establish Daowen as king, and make Jiang the inner ruler.”

**Idiomatic:** “Beneath the sun, moon, and stars, we swear an oath of flesh and blood: if Heaven permits, we shall sweep away the dangers, make Daowen king, and place Jiang over the inner court.”

The literal renders the oath imagery but does not assert its actual performance. The idiomatic “swear an oath of flesh and blood” explains its ritual force following Hu, while retaining the imagery; it does not silently equate Jiang with Jiang Jun or assert a completed appointment. Both preserve the omitted reference to Daowen and the forged document's proposed role for Jiang. “Inner ruler” and “place … over the inner court” retain the local text more cautiously than calling Jiang empress outright; a reviewer may prefer “empress” as an explicitly commentary-based interpretation. The conditional `if` is a flagged interpretive choice: if independent review cannot decide its force, retain the hold and consult a philologist rather than treating it as resolved.
