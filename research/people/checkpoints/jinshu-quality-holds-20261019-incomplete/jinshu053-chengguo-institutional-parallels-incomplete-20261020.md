# Jinshu 053 s0087: institutional parallels for 成國 (research only)

This is a source-finding note, not a translation decision or a chapter review.
The complete final clause `不得王是成國耳` remains held. The parallels below
establish a relevant institutional use, not the grammar or intended outcome of
the disputed quotation.

- `data/jinshu/046.json` SHA-256

  In s0179–s0182, `兼建諸侯而樹籓屏` contrasts the establishment of
  regional lords as a protective screen with merely enfeoffing relatives
  without establishing `成國之制`. In s0202, Guangwu is said to have
  enfeoffed his sons and brothers but not established `成國之制`. In
  s0242–s0244, existing princes receive territory, but their laws resemble
  commanderies and counties rather than `成國之制`; the author argues to
  restore older institutions. These passages establish that enfeoffment or
  territorial allocation alone is not automatically the same as 成國.
- `data/jinshu/020.json` SHA-256
  `ea4c955dcfebe4b9ec6f7ff187319a9f589b9497cb12c158344b469e44d9c0a2`,
  s0364: `王公五等諸侯成國置卿者` associates 成國 with five grades of
  lords and the appointment of ministers in a discussion of mourning rules.
  It does not attest the specific s0087 phrase or Daowen's appointment.

These same-book usages give an institutional reading a concrete parallel and
warn against translating 成國 as a named "Cheng principality" solely because
the characters permit it. They do not prove that 成國 in 053 means an
established fief, that Daowen received or was denied one, where the punctuation
belongs, or how the preceding `不得王是` connects to it. They are not an
independent edition of the 053 letter and cannot determine textual variants.
