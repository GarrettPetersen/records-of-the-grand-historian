## Source check

The named primary text, [春秋左氏傳, 僖公二十四年](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%98%A5%E7%A7%8B%E5%B7%A6%E6%B0%8F%E5%82%B3/%E5%83%96%E5%85%AC#%E5%83%96%E5%85%AC%E4%BA%8C%E5%8D%81%E5%9B%9B%E5%B9%B4),
under that year's commentary, identifies Zi Zang of Zheng and says he fled to
Song and liked `聚鷸冠`. It then relates that the ruler of Zheng heard of it,
sent bandits to lure him, and that they killed him in the eighth month. These
are the Zuo's own narrative and its chronological ordering, not a date stated
in the Jinshu sentence itself. The linking of Jinshu's `鄭子臧` and directly
named `左氏傳` with that passage is a strong source-specific match; it is
still a historical textual attribution, not independent proof of the event.

An academic chronological discussion hosted by the University of Chicago
identifies the same Zuo year heading, Xi's twenty-fourth year, with **636 BCE**:
[Tharsen, comparative phonorhetorical analyses](https://home.uchicago.edu/~jcarlsen/academics/downloads/Tharsen_JCLC6.2_Comparative_Phonorhetorical_Analyses.pdf).
That is an external scholarly *year-label mapping*, not a conversion of a
specified Gregorian day. The matching Zuo anecdote is in the autumn sequence
and its death is placed in lunar month eight; I did not independently run a
calendar converter or verify proleptic Gregorian month/day. Do not invent one.
