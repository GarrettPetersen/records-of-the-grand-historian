# Jinshu 127 s0181–s0194: independent focused editorial review

Reviewer: `/root/master_gate`, a separate Codex review conversation. The checked

This is a focused review of the submitted findings, not a whole-chapter approval
or a date, extraction, identity, or publication approval.

1. **s0181: accept the omission finding; revise both English fields.** The source
   `建立學官，簡公卿已下子弟及二品士門二百人為太學生` states a formal
   selection of 200 and distinguishes the children of dukes and ministers and
   below from the additional `二品士門` category. The idiomatic “elite families”
   omits these distinctions. The current literal “down to second rank gentry
   households” also risks turning the explicit `及` into one continuous range.
   The proposed “second-rank gentry households” is a provisional rendering, not
   an independently established technical definition. Suggested cautious wording:
   “He established educational offices and selected 200 young men from the
   families of dukes and ministers and those below them, as well as from
   second-rank scholar households, as students of the imperial academy.”
   Resolve the institutional meaning of `二品士門` with a reliable source before
   presenting the category as certain; do not infer 200 per category.
2. **s0182: accept the finding for both fields.** In `恭己南面而朝諸侯`, the
   ruler faces south and receives the lords' attendance; “to court the lords”
   wrongly suggests courting them. “I conduct myself respectfully, face south,
   and receive the lords at court” preserves the stated direction and relation.
   This decision covers only that clause, not every stylistic issue in the quote.
3. **s0194: accept the finding, with a qualification.** In the context of Du
   Hong's risky mission and his father's requested support (s0191–s0193, s0195),
   `散所輕之財，招所重之死` contrasts giving away comparatively lightly valued
   wealth with soliciting service involving death. The current “recruit the
   deaths” obscures the sense. “I am giving away wealth, which I value lightly,
   to enlist a man willing to risk his life, which I value greatly” is a plausible
   contextual paraphrase, not a literal claim that the singular word “man” or
   “life” appears in the Chinese. The independent resolution of
   `況為親尊而可吝乎` should preserve the reference to a revered parent/elder;
   avoid asserting an antecedent without a separate contextual decision.
