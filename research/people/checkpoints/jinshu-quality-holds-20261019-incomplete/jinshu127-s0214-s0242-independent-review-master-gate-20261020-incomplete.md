## Findings and decisions

1. **p133 identification: accept hold, not replacement.** Jinshu s0231 gives
   `接` in a list of names associated with Qi and Lu. Shiji 074 s0047 lists
   `接子` with 淳于髡、慎到 and 田駢 among the scholars of Jixia, and s0068
   explicitly describes 接子 and 田駢 as 齊人. That is strong contextual reason
   to investigate 接子; it is not proof from Jinshu alone of a unique identity.
   Shiji 047 s0445 presents `楚狂接輿` meeting Confucius. The similar name does
   not establish the equivalence claimed by candidate p133 (`接予`, marked
   strongly inferred), especially given the different contextual setting. Nor
   does s0231 establish its `BC 350-300` hint. Review and retract or qualify
   unsupported expanded-name/date claims before publication; do not simply
   replace them with a definite identity without a separately reviewed decision.
2. **p143–p147 chronology: accept the five-item source-evidence defect.**
   Candidate attestation array indices 335–339 assign AD 401 and
   `sourceDate.text` `建平次年` to evidence s0234 or s0238. Neither cited source
   unit contains that wording. s0234 opens `先是`, explicitly looking backward,
   so a nearby later event's year cannot simply be inherited. The pinned local
   Tongjian s0004 has 元興二年 and the cyclic year 癸卯, with a modern AD 403
   annotation; s0032–s0033 place the Wang Shi uprising and capture in that annual
   sequence. The external Tongjian witness similarly puts the episode under
   元興二年. This is contrary evidence to treating AD 401 as established from
   Jinshu, not direct evidence fixing each family member's lifetime or activity.
   The review must preserve uncertainty and never insert a nonexistent source
   quotation or mechanically change all five people to AD 403.
   **Witness caution:** the linked Tongjian edition's main text reads `正始` at
   this location, while the pinned local text reads `王始`; a nearby commentary
   in the external edition does refer to `王始`. The shared account and title
   connect the episodes, but the name variant deserves verification and must
   not be silently normalized into a claimed verbatim external quotation.
3. **s0241: accept correction in both fields.** In the condemned speaker's
   `崩即崩矣，終不改帝號`, 崩 is a ruler's death, not physical or political
   collapse. “If I must die, then I die; I will never relinquish my imperial
   title” captures the sense without treating the sovereign claim as fact.
4. **s0216: accept direction-of-conflict finding.** `慕容法及魏師戰` makes
   Murong Fa fight Wei forces; the later `魏師敗績` reports Wei's rout. The
   literal “together with Wei troops fought” is misleading. Do not assert a
   specific topographical interpretation of `摽榆俗` without further evidence.
5. **s0227–s0229 and s0234: accept as scoped omissions requiring repair.**
   Source quantities, offices and the inspection are not reproduced in the
   idiomatic fields, and s0234 explicitly distinguishes the father's and the two
   brothers' titles. Preserve individual roles and avoid adding a narrator's
   adjective “bogus” unsupported by the text. Exact English titles still need
   their own editorial review.
6. **s0236 callbacks and title surfaces: investigation warranted.** The father,
   brothers and office references in s0236 are connected by the immediately
   preceding s0234, but the exact surface spans, candidate accounting and
   collective-reference semantics must be validated before adding person links.
   No person name or identity merge is authorized by this note.
