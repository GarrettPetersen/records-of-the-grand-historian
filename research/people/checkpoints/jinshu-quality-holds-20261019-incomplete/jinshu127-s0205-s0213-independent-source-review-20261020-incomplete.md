# Independent review of Jinshu 127 s0205, s0210, and s0213 proposals

Research only, not a chapter approval or a canonical edit. Reviewed author findings SHA-256 `5c20eb6276a4a186708a23f1765e334d62d1fcfbd39c15e3ac47d37761ba9fad`, against proposed source `jinshu127-s0177-two-dates-reconciled-20261020-incomplete/jinshu/127.json`, SHA-256 `cf2d789947d99dbd56ed75c96eb7e097d85e2699cf73e96268cf2fd22b90c264`. The accompanying p141 extraction SHA-256 is `5f5e8325fd88a44c682cfa3ae475aad26df0841f0b39878b6e478691cf5b3fc8`. I did not author these proposals.

1. **s0205: supported.** Both English fields say `the temple of Prince Jing of Chengyang at Hancheng`, whereas `漢城陽景王廟` is the temple of the Han Prince Jing of Chengyang. The separate local texts `data/jinshu/045.json` (`漢城陽景王章之後`) and `data/hanshu/047.json` (`城陽景王章，孝文二年…`) corroborate the reading of 漢 as the dynasty and 城陽 as the prince's title, not 漢城 as a place. The proposed “temple of Han Prince Jing of Chengyang” corrects this specific issue. Do not construe this as approval of the rest of the long sentence.
2. **s0210: supported, with a precision note.** The source reads `立冶于商山，置鹽官于烏常澤，以廣軍國之用`. Literal reads “He set up smelting works on Mount Shang and salt offices at Wuchang Marsh to broaden military and state use”; idiomatic reads “He opened ironworks on Mount Shang and salt monopolies at Wuchang Marsh to swell the army's coffers.” The idiomatic specifies iron, a monopoly, and a cash destination without those details stated in this sentence and omits the state component of 軍國. The proposed generalization to smelting and resources for army and state is justified. 鹽官 may refer to the salt administration/office rather than just individual officials; “established a salt office at Wuchang Marsh” preserves the institutional sense without deciding the detailed system. This does not exclude an independently established monopoly or iron production; it only avoids treating them as explicit here.
3. **s0213: correction warranted, but change proposed subject of hiding.** Source `中黃門遜進扶德逾城，隱於進舍` has literal “Palace attendant Xun Jin helped De over the wall and hid him in a forward lodging,” and idiomatic “A eunuch helped Murong De over the wall into hiding.” The paired `遜進` / `進舍` supports the personal-name reading Xun Jin and his residence, not “forward lodging”; both fields should preserve his name and location. However, the proposed “and hid him at Jin's residence” adds Xun Jin as agent of the second clause, whereas `隱於進舍` most directly leaves De as its subject: “Palace attendant Xun Jin helped De over the wall, and De hid at Jin's residence.” No inference about the owner's name beyond this local reading or independent dates is made.

These are independent conclusions on three targeted translation issues only. The grammar note is not a rejection of the underlying finding and does not constitute a whole-chapter editorial or date audit. Any changes still require the repository's normal sealed decision, input fingerprint, and downstream revalidation procedures.
