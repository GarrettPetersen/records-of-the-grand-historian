# Jinshu 127: focused translation findings, s0181–s0194 (incomplete)

Research-only proposals, not independently approved or applied. Reviewed source:

This review does not cover the whole chapter, the extraction, dates, or identities.

1. **s0182, both fields — reversed relationship to the lords.** The Chinese
   `恭己南面而朝諸侯` describes De facing south as ruler and receiving the
   lords at court. Both fields instead have “I reverently face south to court
   the lords,” which can suggest he is courting them. Proposed minimal wording:
   “I conduct myself respectfully, face south, and receive the lords at court.”
   This proposal changes neither the surrounding quote nor its attribution.
2. **s0181, idiomatic — material social groups omitted.** `簡公卿已下子弟及
   二品士門二百人為太學生` specifies 200 young men selected from the children
   of high officials and below, and second-rank gentry families, for the
   imperial academy. “He founded an academy with two hundred students from
   elite families” omits the text's two groups and formal selection. The
   precise institutional meaning of 二品士門 needs an independent reviewer;
   do not confidently substitute an invented office or say each group
   contributed 200. A candidate rendering is “He established educational
   offices and selected two hundred young men from the families of dukes and
   ministers and below, and from second-rank gentry households, as students
   of the imperial academy.”
3. **s0194, both fields — awkward and potentially misleading clause.**
   `吾方散所輕之財，招所重之死` contrasts wealth with the risk of death
   undertaken by the envoy. Both fields say “scattering the wealth I lightly
   esteem to recruit the deaths I heavily value,” which is unnatural and can
   make the objective appear to be acquiring deaths. Suggested partial
   rendering: “I am giving away wealth, which I value lightly, to enlist a man
   willing to risk his life, which I value greatly.” The next clause
   `況為親尊而可吝乎` concerns the request for the envoy's father; its exact
   rendering should be reviewed in context, rather than assumed here.
