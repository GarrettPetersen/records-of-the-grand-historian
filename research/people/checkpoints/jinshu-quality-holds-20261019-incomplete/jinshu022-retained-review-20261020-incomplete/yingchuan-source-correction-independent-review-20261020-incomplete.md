## Source and lexical evidence

The quoted local sequence is correct: 022 s0231 is the Yingchuan lord's
hymn heading; s0232–s0235 are its body, and s0236 starts the Jingzhao hymn.
019 s0510 lists retrospective sacrifices to the lords of Yuzhang, Yingchuan
and Jingzhao, among others. 001 s0010–s0012 give Liang as Yuzhang prefect,
Jun (儁) as Yingchuan prefect, and Fang as metropolitan governor, in a
parent–child sequence. This contextual convergence makes Jun a serious
candidate for the dedicatee, but neither by itself proves the antecedent of
each epithet nor supports silently combining the separate extraction records.

I independently checked the [Shangshu Zhengyi, juan 8, Yi Xun](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%B0%9A%E6%9B%B8%E6%AD%A3%E7%BE%A9/%E5%8D%B7%E5%85%AB),
which explicitly interprets `有夏先后` as earlier Xia rulers. This is a sound
parallel for a lord/ruler meaning of 後, not a specific commentary on the Jin
hymn, and does not determine whether 先後 is singular here or whether its
referent ever reigned. The proposed “former lord” can suggest a former ruler;
do not turn a lexical translation into a historical claim of actual kingship.

The [Songshu music treatise text](https://www.xuoda.com/ls/songshu/060.htm)
independently presents the four lines followed by a heading naming the
Yingchuan lord, consistent with the heading's application to the stanza, not
its appearing at the preceding hymn's close. It is another textual transmission,
not proof that its witnesses are independent of Jinshu or that the two epithets
each have a unique, identical referent. I could not inspect the proposed
chaoxing PDF page directly: the available page screenshot timed out. A web
search snippet suggests it contains the passage, but that is not a verified
page reading. Another [transcription of the hymn](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/%E6%BD%81%E5%B7%9D%E5%BA%9C%E5%90%9B%E7%99%BB%E6%AD%8C)
ends with 享祀永年 rather than local 享祚永年. This is an observation of
a variant, not authorization to emend the local Chinese or a provenance
assessment of the transcriptions.

## Paired English proposals

- s0232: I support correcting both current fields' “queen mother” and feminine
  pronoun: neither follows from 先後 alone. “Ah, distant former lord, truly
  attending to Heaven” and “How distant our former lord, who truly serves
  Heaven” are defensible **working alternatives**, not uniquely established
  readings. The added “our” and singular “lord” interpret a referent and
  number not explicit in the four characters. 司 can have a charge or duty;
  “attending to” / “serves” chooses a relationship to Heaven, while a
  locative “in Heaven” remains possible. Preserve that distinction in the
  editorial decision and do not construct an occupation from it. In particular,
  these alternatives do not themselves settle whether 先後 means a single
  dedicatee or a collective ancestral reference.
- s0233: I support removing the idiomatic “founder” and “here,” which go beyond
  皇祖 and 肇臻. “Manifest is the august ancestor; the Lord's blessings first
  arrive” and “Radiant is our august ancestor; the Lord's blessings begin to
  arrive” are plausible working alternatives, but “our,” singular “Lord,”
  English possessive relation and plural “blessings” are interpretive. 帝
  and the source of 祉 need a reasoned determination; the Shangshu lexical
  parallel concerns 先後 and does not prove that 帝 in this hymn is a deity
  or a particular lord. 肇 can signal inception, not necessarily a precisely
  dated first arrival; tense and aspect should remain contextual. The Chinese
  ancestor may be exalted without making the referent literally an emperor.

The two proposed pairs improve the specific identified defects but I do not
approve their final wording as uniquely correct without a review that resolves
or explicitly presents these remaining choices in context. Both source fields
and their resulting English mention surfaces need coordinated review.
