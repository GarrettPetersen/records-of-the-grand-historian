## Text and evidence

The hymn begins `天祚有晉，其命惟新。受終於魏，奄有黎民` (s0124–25),
and is headed as a hymn for Heaven, Earth and the five suburban sacrifices.
It continues `神祗來格，福祿是臻。時邁其猶，昊天子之。祐享有晉，
肇庶戴之` (s0129–31). The later phrase `於猶繹思` (s0133) offers another
instance of 猶 in this hymn, but does not itself settle the meaning. The
separate hymn at s0143 has `皇矣有晉，時邁其德`; another Jin hymn in the
*Song shu* reads `時邁其仁`. Their abstract objects, 德 and 仁, weigh
against treating 時邁 as a clock or the passage of the seasons, but do not by
themselves define 猶 or show exactly what 邁 means in s0130.

The *Odes* hymn [Shi Mai and its transmitted Mao/Zheng commentary](https://wk.100ke.info/zh-hant/%E6%AF%9B%E8%A9%A9%E6%AD%A3%E7%BE%A9/%E5%8D%B7%E5%8D%81%E4%B9%9D)
has `時邁其邦，昊天其子之`, and Zheng Xuan explains the second clause as
Heaven loving the king as a son. The Jin hymn omits the Odes' second 其: do
not silently insert it in the quoted Chinese, but the subject–verb–object
parsing `昊天 / 子 / 之` remains the natural close allusion. The *Song shu*
independently transmits the Jin lyric and its 仁 parallel in its
[music treatise](https://www.wenlinshe.com/tw/chapter/zhongguolishi/song-shu/zhi-di-shi-le-er.html).
The *Odes* has 邦 as object of 邁, traditionally understood as traveling
through the countries; that meaning should not automatically be transferred
to an abstract-object variant. For the possible noun sense ‘plan/counsel’, the
Mao commentary on [Odes, Chang Wu](https://ctext.org/dictionary.pl?char=%E7%8C%B6&if=gb)
glosses 猶 as 謀, but that is a lexical possibility, not a direct commentary
on this Jin line.

## Conclusions and disposition

1. **Approve the p046 false-person finding on the pinned candidate, subject
   to normal host-side workflow and downstream checks.** `天子` here straddles
   the end of the subject 昊天 and the following verb 子; it is not an
   independently stated title. Candidate p046's sole surfaces and all six
   claims derive from s0130. Its ‘ritual officiant’ and ‘upholds the seasonal
   suburban rhythm’ are not present in the source. The lyric's implied object
   might be the Jin ruler or the dynasty represented as a recipient of
   Heaven's favor; it does not identify a distinct individual by name or
   explicitly call that individual 天子. Removal must include dependent
   surfaces, names and claims, with candidate dispositions and identity
   references checked by the normal removal and validation process. This
   finding does not authorize deleting any independently attested person.

2. **Correct the second clause in both English fields.** A close literal
   rendering is ‘August Heaven treats [him/it] as a son’; a natural rendering
   is ‘August Heaven receives [him/it] as its son.’ Square brackets show that
   choosing a specific English referent is interpretive: a singular ruler is
   consistent with the Odes parallel, but no specific ruler's name appears in
   this unit, and the surrounding lyric also praises Jin as a dynasty. Do not
   assert a definite biographical identity merely from the analogy.

3. **Hold the first clause for further philological review.** A tentative
   working literal is ‘[Jin] advances its counsel/plans’; a tentative idiom is
   ‘Jin carries its designs forward.’ These take 猶 as 謀 and 邁 as advance;
   the local parallels support the shape but do not establish these choices or
   fully settle the implicit subject and 其's referent. Do not present them as
   confirmed or preserve the existing invented ‘seasonal rhythm’ while the
   clause is held. Consult a direct reliable commentary or independent
   philological reviewer before finalizing both whole-unit fields.
