This is a **working preview**, not a final editorial, date, identity or
publication approval. It does not modify the canonical chapter, extraction,
sealed packets, queue or locks. The candidate JSON retains the source's
original translator/model metadata **only because its fields are illustrative**;

correction must follow the repository's editorial metadata conventions and

The [Jinshu transcription](https://www.shidianguji.com/book/LS0005/chapter/LS0005_419)
places the hymn under the Yingchuan lord's heading, with 先後, 皇祖 and
帝祉 as in the local text. Its [Yuefu shiji transcription](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E6%BD%81%E5%B7%9D%E5%BA%9C%E5%90%9B%E7%99%BB%E6%AD%8C)
also has those phrases, but differs in the last line (享祀 versus local
享祚); no local Chinese emendation follows. These reproductions are not proof
of independent manuscript lineages. The [Shijing, Daya, Huang Yi](https://www.us-confucius.com/images/shijing.pdf)
uses 既受帝祉 in a poem explicitly invoking 上帝. This is a genuine early
parallel for a heavenly sense of 帝祉, not a direct commentary on this hymn or
proof of its compositional source. It supports considering a heavenly lord;
it does not establish that the candidate's English possessive, singular,
aspect, or number is uniquely correct.

As prior independent review says, 司於天 can express service toward Heaven
or be read locatively, and 先後's number and antecedent remain unresolved.
The working English's singular “former lord” and “our” interpret these
matters. The proposed Lord in 帝祉 is also an interpretation; retain the
independent review hold rather than presenting the text as settled. Candidate
metadata, p055–p057 claims and mentions, identity decisions, and all normal
review gates require coordinated review before applying a production edit.
