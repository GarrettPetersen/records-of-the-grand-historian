## Proposed paired English fields for s0130

The exact transmitted line is `時邁其猶，昊天子之。` The current fields
incorrectly make Heaven's Son the subject who upholds a rhythm.

- Literal: **“Now it advances its course; August Heaven treats it as a child.”**
- Idiomatic: **“Jin carries its course forward, and August Heaven cherishes it
  as its child.”**

This is a contextual interpretation, not a claim that the terse Chinese
explicitly names Jin in this unit. The hymn opens with `天祚有晉` and its next
line is `祐享有晉` (s0124, s0131), making Jin a plausible antecedent. More
specifically identifying the referent as Emperor Wu or another individual is
not warranted by this line; the literal version deliberately leaves it as
“it.” Nor should the English supplement an altar ritual, a date or an
officiant.

The reading of `其猶` remains less secure. Treating `猶` as a cognate of
`猷` (“course,” “plan,” “way”) is linguistically supported but not established
by a direct commentary on this exact line. “Course” is deliberately broad;
“plan” or “way” is a viable alternate, and the meaning of `時` could be
“now,” an adverbial particle, or a reference to the proper time. The existing
“prudent awe” and “seasons march” are not justified by the evidence gathered.
The same author uses `時邁其德` (“advances its virtue”) in the next hymn;
`時邁其仁` (“advances its benevolence”) appears in a separate dance hymn
attributed to Xun Xu. These parallels favor an active reading of `邁` in the
present phrase; they do not prove the exact sense of `猶`.

## Evidence and limits

1. [Book of Jin, music treatise](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/HY2499/chapter/1mb9kqw6e9mie)
   transmits the line as above, attributes these hymns to Fu Xuan, and
   preserves `時邁其德` in the immediately following hymn. The source's
   wording is not silently replaced with another text's variant.
2. [Book of Song, music treatise](https://www.wenlinshe.com/tw/chapter/zhongguolishi/song-shu/zhi-di-shi-le-er.html)
   independently transmits the same hymn with `時邁其猶，昊天子之` and
   preserves the parallel `時邁其仁` in a dance hymn attributed to Xun Xu;
   the latter is a parallel of the genre, not the same author. It also
   preserves `時邁其德` for the following Fu Xuan hymn.
3. [Mao Shi Zhengyi, *Shi Mai*](https://wk.100ke.info/wiki/%E6%AF%9B%E8%A9%A9%E6%AD%A3%E7%BE%A9/%E5%8D%B7%E5%8D%81%E4%B9%9D)
   transmits the older parallel `時邁其邦，昊天其子之`; Zheng Xuan's
   commentary explains the second clause as Heaven loving the ruler as a
   child (`天其子愛之`). This supports reading `昊天 / 子 / 之`, not
   `昊 / 天子 / 之`. The antecedent of the Odes line is the Zhou ruler; its
   identity cannot simply be imported into the distinct Jin hymn.
4. [Kangxi Dictionary entry for 猶 on the Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/dictionary.pl?char=%E7%8C%B6&if=gb)
   cites transmitted *Odes* commentary for `猶` meaning a way or plan,
   including `克壯其猶` and `王猶允塞`. This attests a possible sense, not
   conclusive disambiguation of every occurrence in Fu Xuan's hymn.
