# Independent review: Jinshu 127 s0232, Bi Gan's tomb

**Verdict:** The proposed correction of “closed” to “raised a mound over” is supported. Approve this narrow wording in both English fields, subject to the normal independent editorial decision, fingerprint reconciliation, and fresh downstream checks; this note does not itself publish or approve a chapter. “Heaped earth over” would also work and avoids a possible inference that an entirely new mound was created, but that inference is not required by the proposed wording.

In s0232, the Chinese is `邃答曰：「武王封比干之墓，漢祖祭信陵之墳，皆留心賢哲，每懷往事。` Both the literal and idiomatic English fields currently contain the identical “King Wu of Zhou closed Bi Gan's tomb” wording, and the proposal changes that phrase in both fields while preserving the remaining text and existing open quotation.

I separately scanned unit Chinese for `比干`, `信陵`, or `封` within twelve characters of `墓`, and English fields for Bi Gan/Bigan, Xinling, or “closed” near “tomb”; only s0232 matched. This is a limited string scan, not a claim that no other paraphrase or chapter-wide defect exists.

## Directly inspected evidence

- The directly accessible [Siku edition of Jin shu 127](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/晉書_(四庫全書本)/卷127) independently witnesses the same King Wu/Bi Gan and Han Gaozu/Lord Xinling pairing. It is a textual witness, not independent evidence that the ancient event happened.
- The directly opened [annotated Shiji, Music Treatise](https://www.xuoda.com/ls/shiji2/102.htm), line 29, gives `封王子比干之墓` and explicitly attributes to Zheng Xuan `積土為封` and the explanation that this honored a worthy person. This is a parallel ancient account with a transmitted commentary, not a commentary printed alongside the Jin shu sentence itself. It supports heaping earth into a mound; it does not mean shutting a tomb entrance.
- A directly opened [Wuli tongkao 123](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/SK0367/chapter/1lcutuniq6810), lines 17–19, quotes the related Wucheng explanation `益其土` and the Yueji/Zheng gloss. This later compilation gives further reason not to infer the original creation of the tomb. The proposed “raised a mound over” is consistent with adding earth; it need not imply that the mound was created from nothing.

The proposed translation attributes the action to King Wu without asserting he personally handled the earth, and does not invent a date or dimensions. It does not change the Lord Xinling example. I have not independently evaluated every other word of the unit or established chronology from this passage.
