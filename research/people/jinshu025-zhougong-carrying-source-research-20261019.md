## Primary and scholarly evidence

1. [Jinshu 25, fixed revision 2605811](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=2605811&title=%E6%99%89%E6%9B%B8%2F%E5%8D%B7025),
   in the paragraph on the purple shoulder pouch, states the carrying-and-garment
   origin account and then introduces a different Han-era explanation as
   uncertain. This is a transmitted etiology; s0544 alone does not establish
   the historical incident or its year.
2. [Hanshu 68, fixed revision 1752745](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1752745&title=%E6%BC%A2%E6%9B%B8%2F%E5%8D%B7068)
   records a much later depiction of the Duke carrying Cheng to receive the
   lords, commissioned for Huo Guang, and has Emperor Wu explicitly refer to
   the earlier image during his final illness in 後元二年. The [Taiwan
   Ministry of Education era table](https://dict.revised.moe.edu.tw/appendix.jsp?ID=43&ver=0)
   gives 後元 as 87–88 BC; its second year is conventionally 87 BC. **This is
   a terminus for the image's existence as narrated, not the year of the
   depicted Zhou act or the precise image-commissioning date.** The Hanshu
   episode is outside s0544 and should not be attributed to that unit as if
   its text mentions Huo Guang or supplies this date.
3. Chang Wan-ying / 張琬瑩, [「經學史上的千古公案：『周公稱王』問題的源起與爭議」](https://www.litphil.sinica.edu.tw/newsletter/127/127-7-32.pdf),
   *中國文哲研究通訊* 32.3, printed p. 10 (third PDF page), describes the Han image as
   symbolically showing an infant-size Cheng, situating it in a later tradition.
   This is a modern interpretation of the Han depiction, not a description in
   Jinshu s0544 or independent proof of Cheng's age at the purported carrying.

## Translation check against staging

At staging `7e07e5ffad510cec4b05bab53786b60d44b6efbb`, s0544's literal
translation says the Duke carried King Cheng on his back and does not specify
an age. Its idiomatic translation adds `baby-carrying sash`. The Chinese
sentence does not specify that Cheng was a baby, so this expansion requires
independent editorial review in light of the surrounding source and the
separate tradition above. This is a source-bound review finding, not an
approved replacement, and the Jinshu text still needs to be translated on its
own terms rather than silently importing the later painting.

## Implications and remaining questions

Do not use the image's 87 BC terminus as a lifetime attestation or a date of
the reported Zhou action. Source-aware review should distinguish (a) the
purported old carrying/garment-creation action, which would involve the living
Duke **if** accepted as an actual event, from (b) any later act of recalling
or depicting him, which can be retrospective only when that later event and
its own time are actually supported. A retrospective text describing an old
action is not automatically a dated retrospective event. The current
`retrospective-reference` label and action appear to blend these two senses;
an independent reviewer must assess event ownership, available schema, whether
the exact word `infant` is justified by this chapter, and how best to preserve
the uncertain etiology without inventing a date. Do not add the Hanshu painting
to this chapter's evidence or change non-temporal content via the date-repair
interface merely to close the hold.

These sources do not determine Cheng's age or establish an absolute Zhou date.
They do not resolve the other 025 findings or replace a fresh independent
complete audit of the eventual current source and extraction.
