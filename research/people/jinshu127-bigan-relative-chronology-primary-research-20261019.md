## Primary narrative evidence

1. [Shiji 38, Song Weizi hereditary house, fixed Wikisource revision 8737306](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=8737306&title=%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98%2F%E5%8D%B7038):
   the transmitted narrative describes Prince Bi Gan remonstrating with Shang
   king Zhou and being killed, then describes King Wu's defeat of Zhou. The
   account says `乃遂殺王子比干`; it supplies no Western year for his death
   in this passage.
2. [Shiji 4, Zhou annals, fixed Wikisource revision 7909147](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=7909147&title=%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98%2F%E5%8D%B7004):
   in its sequence following the defeat of Shang, the account says King Wu
   ordered Hong Yao to honor Bi Gan's tomb: `命閎夭封比干之墓`. The passage
   does not assign the tomb action a Western calendar year.

Read together as the Shiji's transmitted literary tradition, these passages
corroborate the relative sequence: Bi Gan's death under Zhou precedes King Wu's
post-conquest treatment of his tomb. They support treating the Jinshu mention
as a later remembrance of an honor to a deceased person, not as evidence of
Bi Gan living or acting on the tomb-action date. The two chapters of the same
later historical work are not independent contemporary witnesses; they do
not independently prove the events actually occurred or justify extracting an
absolute year from the narrative.

## What remains unresolved

Neither passage substantiates `BC 1100` as Bi Gan's activity date, nor gives an
exact birth, death, conquest, tomb-action, or Jinshu speech year. Lack of
support here does **not** prove 1100 impossible. A qualified source-based
assessment is needed for any numerical or bounded chronology; do not invent
one or conflate life dates, a tomb action, and the date of a later speech.
