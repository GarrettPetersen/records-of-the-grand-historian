## Findings

I checked the exact pinned Hanshu 98, Taiping Yulan 688 and Hanshu 99 middle
URLs in the research dossier by retrieving their actual HTML. Hanshu 98's
paragraph begins with the interval after Wang Mang's usurpation, reports his
change from black to yellow sable, and describes the dowager's contrary
instruction to her staff. The following paragraph dates her death to
建國五年二月癸丑. The dowager's living response is evidence for an upper
terminus, **conditional on the source's chronology and attribution being
historically reliable**, not an explicitly dated decree for the change.

The pinned Taiping Yulan 688 indeed repeats that account as an explicitly
attributed Hanshu quotation, not an independent contemporary witness. The
pinned Hanshu 99 middle explicitly places the removal of the Han designation
at 始建國元年正月朔. The linked Ministry of Education index lists 始建國
as 9–13; by the usual ordinal convention its fifth year is the conventional
year-label AD 13. These items support the dossier's characterization, not
an exact Gregorian date for either the change or the dowager's death.

Important qualification: the Han designation's removal, the accession,
and the start of 始建國元年 need not be the same chronological event.
Hanshu 99 upper describes Wang Mang's accession on an earlier 戊辰 and then
prescribes a new year beginning on 癸酉; see [Hanshu 99](https://www.chineseclassic.com/content/1546).
The source that places the sable account after usurpation therefore does not
by itself show it occurred after the era's year-one first day, and an AD 9
lower bound must not be inferred solely from that era's 9–13 year labels.
No conversion of either cyclical day or any proleptic-Gregorian lower bound
was independently verified here. AD 13 is the conventional year label of
the *terminal death*, not an assertion the change occurred in AD 13. Any
`westernBounds` value must remain consistent with independently established
calendar boundaries and the schema's inclusive/exclusive semantics.
