In context the item is the high-mountain cap. Candidate p052 claim-162's

## Longer parallel and interpretation

[Sui shu 12, fixed revision 2010651](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=2010651&title=%E9%9A%8B%E6%9B%B8%2F%E5%8D%B712)
attributes a longer account to the *Fuzi*. It says Emperor Ming changed the
shape of the high-mountain cap, removed its cylindrical element, and had it
made like a cap with a headcloth; it then says envoys and messengers wore it.
This is a later surviving quotation, not proof of a direct contemporary
eyewitness or of every detail of a lost original. It does not date the change.
[Tongdian 57's parallel](https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=en&node=555188)
also describes Ming's modification, compares the cap with tongtian and
yuanyou, and describes changes to its parts and the wearers. It is another
later compilation and may rely on shared material; it does not give a year.

These parallels materially undermine reading s0444 as a decree relegating
*officials* to lower rank, or as proof that the modified cap was **exclusively**
reserved for a specific rank. They support that the cap was changed, but the
short Jinshu phrase `卑下` could refer to reduced physical height or lesser
relative status; the parallels do not by themselves conclusively settle that
distinction. An independent classical-Chinese editorial reviewer should decide
the exact rendering and the two candidate event strings. Preserve the actual
source wording and do not import all later Sui/Tongdian details into s0444 as
if explicitly stated there.
