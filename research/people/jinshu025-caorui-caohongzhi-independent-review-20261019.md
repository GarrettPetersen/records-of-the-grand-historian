# Independent review: Jinshu 025 Cao Rui and Cao Hongzhi chronology

Bounded source review only; not a curation receipt, an independent full-chapter
date audit, or approval for publication. I did not edit the candidate or any
canonical source. The checked candidate and source files have SHA-256,
respectively, `ee2528391c5ac3daa3b675c517f7701903925abd699131c9e5b10e9d83a0564c`
and `2f1980cd20d124b450e4e1eecfa7319a610a5fbe0669851206442603eed43ab4`.
I inspected claims 161, 162, 194 and 195, including owners and source strings;
they match the proposal's before-values and remain explicitly unresolved.

## Claim 195: Cao Hongzhi's advice

The readable [Taiping yulan 687 transcription](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%A4%AA%E5%B9%B3%E5%BE%A1%E8%A6%BD/0687)
attributes to 晉氏要事 a named Cao Hongzhi collective proposal under 哀帝隆和元年;
it has the distinctive Liqiu reading and proposed change from 緗 to 素. This is
good evidence that it concerns the s0517 consultation, not merely any event of
Emperor Ai's reign. It is a later quotation of an earlier attributed work, not
the independent survival of that original. The indexed Shidianguji transcription
instead has 晉書要事, a source-title variant. Preserve both attributions; do not
count these transcriptions as two independent historical witnesses. The
Shidianguji page fetch was inconsistent during this review, so use the directly
readable vol. 687 link above rather than relying on its cached search snippet.

The Jinshu imperial annal dates the renaming to 隆和元年正月壬子:
https://www.shidianguji.com/book/LS0005/chapter/LS0005_105 . The cited
[Gregorian conversion table](https://sangokushi-biyori.com/reference-gregorian-calendar-362),
which identifies the Academia Sinica converter as its source, places the end
of lunar Longhe 1 in January 363: month 11 day 30 = 363-01-01 and month 12 day
29 = 363-01-30 in its proleptic Gregorian table. The independent table is a
secondary computation, not the historical source of the year. The bounded
AD 362–363 interval for the dated advice is supported as a conservative
calendar-year envelope, assuming the project interprets year intervals as
possible dates of an event, not duration of continuous service. Liqiu describes
the ritual being discussed; it does not date the discussion to that day.
Retain the exact Jinshu sourceDate and explain that the year comes from the
external quotation. No precise day is established for this proposal.

## Claim 194: imperial acceptance

Jinshu identifies Emperor Ai as following the doctors' advice; a later
compilation [quoting Songshu](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/GJTS28/chapter/1lpf5snkp5np0)
also states early in his reign that the emperor accepted the proposal. These
support the owner and connection, but neither provides the acceptance's exact
year. The yulan quotation is a year-dated report of Cao's proposal and change
to white. Its short unpunctuated sequence does not expressly identify the
emperor as the agent of implementation or establish that the imperial assent
itself occurred in that year; `改為素` could be understood within the proposal
rather than as a separately dated assent. Accordingly, I recommend retaining
claim 194 as a separate research hold unless the full independent date reviewer
accepts and explicitly documents the additional same-episode inference. Do not
silently copy claim 195's year across. The distinct events could have occurred
at different times even if historically related.

For the discrepancy, the Songshu 15 memorial transcription
https://www.qj.hk/mobile/jingdian/book/60/4406.html says the emperor should
wear 緗 before describing the change to 素, in tension with the opposite
wording in yulan and the Songshu 18 account. Neither this conflict nor the
later Song reversal alone changes the proposal's year; preserve the variant
without emendation. The research proposal's scanned Songshu 18 page was not
independently re-rendered in this review, so I do not certify its detailed
pagination or the identity of the subject of every later `遂不改` in that scan.

## Claims 161 and 162: Cao Rui's cap alteration

[Jinshu 25](https://ancient-china-books.github.io/jinshu/OEBPS/Text/1155.html)
quotes Fuzi explicitly attributing alteration of the high-mountain cap to 魏明帝.
This is a reported act of that ruler, not a later reception of him. The
[Wei regnal table](https://dict.revised.moe.edu.tw/appendix.jsp?ID=2&la=0)
separately places Wei Mingdi, distinct from Jin Mingdi, in AD 226–239. The
Sanguozhi Mingdi annal, e.g.
https://dudianji.com/book/%E4%B8%89%E5%9B%BD%E5%BF%97/article/%E9%AD%8F%E4%B9%A6%E4%B8%89%20%E6%98%8E%E5%B8%9D%E7%BA%AA ,
identifies him as 曹睿 and records accession and death; the act is consistent
with imperial authority in that reign. I support AD 226–239 as a *possible
event window*, not a dated reform or a reign-duration activity, for both
the p052 attestation and its matching reformer role, subject to independent
full audit. Exact accession/death Gregorian days were not independently
converted here and are not required to justify this broad year envelope.
景初's contemporaneous change of month numbering complicates fine conversion;
do not infer a precise death or act date from the year label. The proposition
that 卑下 describes physical lowering was independently editorially reviewed
elsewhere and is not re-adjudicated by this narrow date review.

These recommendations leave the chapter's remaining holds, independent
date-review, identity and publication gates open. Each source and endpoint
should be reproduced in the eventual reviewer evidence, with no source-year
misattributed to Jinshu itself.
