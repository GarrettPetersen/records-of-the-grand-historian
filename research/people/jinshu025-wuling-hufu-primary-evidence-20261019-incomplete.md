## Evidence and limits

Jinshu 025 s0427 attributes to Hu Guang the yearless statement that Zhao
Wuling made Hu dress and adorned his head with gold and sable. The separate
[Shiji 43, Zhao hereditary house](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E5%8D%B7043&oldid=7903603)
has a detailed, temporally structured account of Wuling's clothing reform:
the nineteenth-year narrative begins with the spring first-month assembly
(`data/shiji/043.json` s0436), then describes the king's proposal and,
at s0462, `於是遂胡服矣`. It subsequently describes his uncle accepting
the dress and the issue of an order, before the twentieth-year heading.
This is primary textual evidence for the action *adopting Hu dress* in a
nineteenth-year narrative, not simply an imported Wuling reign table.

There is an important boundary: the first-month date attaches to the assembly,
not explicitly to the later adoption or order. A historical regnal/lunisolar
year is not automatically identical to a proleptic Gregorian calendar year;
the account's subsequent narrative may summarize events and should not be
compressed into an exact day or month. A scholarly discussion by the
[Academia Sinica Institute of History and Philology](https://www11.ihp.sinica.edu.tw/storage/w2_file/3721NyMmhNa.pdf)
conventionally labels Wuling year 19 as 307 BCE. That label is secondary
chronological context, not itself a demonstrated exact Gregorian conversion
of each event in the narrative. Accessed 2026-10-19 UTC.

Moreover, Shiji s0462 says he adopted Hu dress but does **not** date the
particular `金貂飾首` detail. Jinshu s0427 combines these in one statement;
its retained claim-137 explicitly combines the two in one action. Do not
impose a single exact Western year on the headdress by equating the passages.
Review claim-136's event scope as well: a general reform, his individual
adoption, the order, and the particular headdress need not have identical
dates. If the record system cannot honestly represent the available dated
evidence and remaining uncertainty, retain the hold.
