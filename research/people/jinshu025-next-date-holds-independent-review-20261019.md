## Wuling, claims 135–136

I separately read [Shiji 43, Zhao house](https://www.wenlinshe.com/tw/chapter/zhongguolishi/shi-ji/zhao-shi-jia/zhao-shi-jia-yuan-wen-ji-zhu-shi.html): its Wuling year-19 heading precedes a narrative in which he himself adopts Hu dress and then promotes it. [Ye Guoliang's academic article](https://ejournals.lib.hkbu.edu.hk/index.php/sinohumanitas/article/download/2133/2099/2475), printed p. 90 (PDF page 12), associates year 19 with 307 BC. The evidence does not date the gold-and-sable decoration. The candidate's claim 135 refers generically to his Hu-dress reform; claim 136 combines the adoption with that separate ornament. The witness supports treating the adoption as the source-attributed episode but does not justify copying its year to claim 136's ornament.

An additional scholarly discussion of the [Bamboo Annals and Hu-dress reform](https://szhd.cbpt.cnki.net/portal/journal/portal/client/paper/3a60bb1de615206e932f93756a14f724), footnote 1, contrasts a 24th-year/302 BC account with Shiji's 19th-year/307 BC entry; it suggests these might be separate waves of orders. This is a competing interpretation, not proof that Wuling personally first adopted Hu dress in 302 BC or that the ornament was dated then. Preserve that nuance and do not conflate later official orders with his own adoption.

The conventional 307 BC label is **not yet** an independently established proleptic Gregorian year envelope. Multiple early calendar reconstructions and year-start conventions exist; the journal's passing year label does not specify endpoints. Therefore the current plan's precaution is correct: retain claim 135's hold pending an appropriate dated calendar reconstruction or authoritative history explaining the convention and identifying the possible Western-year boundaries. Do not encode a guessed BC 307 point or an arbitrary broad window; claim 136 also remains unresolved. A future reviewer must independently assess the source-to-claim equivalence and any repair.

## Wang Mang, claim 140

[Hanshu 98](https://www.shidianguji.com/book/LS0002/chapter/1j7m8xrcgicl3_105) explicitly places Wang Mang's yellow-sable change and the dowager's contrary instruction before its report of her death in 建國五年二月癸丑. Her response provides a source-based *inference* that the change predates her death, conditional on the account being reliable; it is not an exact date of the change. [Yan Ruihua, Hunan University](https://www.sinoss.net/uploadfile/2017/0425/20170425084911546.pdf), p. 6, explicitly associates her death in that year/month with AD 13; [Cullen, Heavenly Numbers](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780191047534_A35486445/preview-9780191047534_A35486445.pdf) independently summarizes Wang Zhengjun as dying in 13 CE, with ancient Western dates in that book stated to use Julian reckoning. Neither makes the yellow-sable reform an AD 13 point date.

An important calendar complication: [Yang Shuda's 1932 discussion](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c6/NLC404-01J000012-8608_%E6%B8%85%E8%8F%AF%E5%AD%B8%E5%A0%B1_1932%E5%B9%B48%E5%8D%B71%E6%9C%9F.pdf), *Qinghua Journal*, printed p. 45, states that a second month beginning 己卯 contained no 癸丑 and flags an apparent error. A separately compiled [Xin calendar table](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A7%8B%E5%BB%BA%E5%9B%BD) instead explicitly uses 丑正 and lists the second month in 13 CE as beginning 庚戌 on January 31; counting the sexagenary cycle inclusively gives 癸丑 as day four. The table is not independent scholarly proof of a precise conversion. [Hanshu's accession passage](https://www.wenlinshe.com/chapter/zhuzibaijia/er-shi-wu-shi/han-shu-wang-mang-jian-xin-chao.html) and the classical [Lidai changshu jiyao](https://www.shidianguji.com/book/CADAL02094010/chapter/1lb0ulzwuz1h0) attest distinct Xin 丑正 and Han 寅正 month-number conventions, making this a plausible explanation for the discrepancy, but I have not proved exactly which convention Yang applied. Do not adopt a specific death day or assert that the source is definitively erroneous on this basis.

**Disposition:** A year-only upper bound `onOrBefore: {era: "AD", year: 13, precision: "year"}` is a defensible conservative *candidate* under the cited scholarly death-year placement and the Hanshu's contingent narrative inference. It is not a precise conversion of the death day, not a lower bound, and not an exact year of the change. The project documentation explicitly distinguishes an inclusive year bound from an exclusive `before` year bound; the latter would exclude a change earlier in AD 13. Have a separate date reviewer judge the narrative inference, source-year/calendar evidence, uncertainty and matching event-specific hint against the exact current source and packet before the host considers the temporal-only replacement. Preserve the hold if that review declines the inference. No fresh full audit is replaced by this limited research.
