## A source addressing the actual textual conflict

The online [Jinshu 107 text and collation note 3](https://skqs.dazhishi.com/show_gldrnrpkdz.html)
explicitly compares the conflicting regnal labels. Its note says that the
annals place Shi Jilong's death in Yonghe year 5, that Xian'kang year 1
through Yonghe year 5 totals 15 years, and that the transmitted 「太和六年」
should read 「永和五年」. It also notes that *Taiping Yulan* 120 quotes the
Jinshu with the conflicting wording, and infers that the mistake was already
in its source. The note distinguishes an earlier collation attributed to
「周校」 from the later editor's 「今按」. The former happens to say Taihe
year **five** when discussing the conflicting date; the latter explicitly
addresses Taihe year **six**, the actual reading. Do not silently reconcile
these numerals or claim to know which original manuscript was responsible.

The [Taiping Yulan section on Shi Hu](https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=en&node=368969)
transmits that same 「太和六年」 phrase in a quotation attributed to Jinshu.
This is an early *reception* of the Jinshu text, not an independent firsthand
witness of Shi Hu's death. The accessible web transcription has not been
checked against a scan or physical edition, and the collation's claim about
the original text is an editorial inference, not demonstrated manuscript
genealogy. The earlier draft already pins the Jinshu 8 and 107 digital
revisions and verifies the reign conversion.

## Additional sequence evidence

[Jinshu 86, Zhang Chonghua](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E6%99%89%E6%9B%B8/%E5%8D%B7086)
attributes a memorial to him that speaks of Jilong as already dead
(`季龍自斃`). The passage does not itself supply the memorial's year.
The previously cited [jinsung chronicle page](https://jinsung.chronicle.wiki/d/16_09)
is **not the Zizhi Tongjian**: it reproduces the later *Sixteen Kingdoms Spring
and Autumn* tradition, which places the corresponding campaign and memorial
under Yonghe year 8 (352). See also [its volume 73](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8D%81%E5%85%AD%E5%9C%8B%E6%98%A5%E7%A7%8B_%28%E5%B1%A0%E5%96%AC%E5%AD%AB%2C_%E9%A0%85%E7%90%B3%2C_%E5%9B%9B%E5%BA%AB%E5%85%A8%E6%9B%B8%E6%9C%AC%29/%E5%8D%B7073).
By contrast, [Zizhi Tongjian 99, revision 1502618](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=1502618&title=%E8%B3%87%E6%B2%BB%E9%80%9A%E9%91%91%2F%E5%8D%B7099)
places the same sequence of Wang Zhuo's campaigns and Chonghua's request
under Yonghe year 9 (353), and summarizes the request without quoting the
four characters. Jinshu 8 likewise places the campaigns under year 9. The
352/353 disagreement must remain explicit; this note does not resolve which
is right. Both chronologies still precede 371 and add contextual support
against taking the Taihe year 6 death literally, but neither supplies an
independent eyewitness or an exact year of Shi Hu's death. The histories
may share earlier sources; agreement cannot establish independent observation.

## Practical interpretation and remaining gates

Yonghe year 5, conventionally AD 349. The explicit collation now supplies a
source-attributed interpretation of the competing Jinshu 107 wording,
rather than merely a new arithmetic inference. A reviewer could evaluate a
repair recording a 349 historical-year death with a substantive source note:
Jinshu 107 transmits Taihe year 6, its collation treats that reading as an
error and proposes Yonghe year 5, and the separate annals passage places the
death in Yonghe year 5. This note must not claim a verified day-level
Gregorian date or that Jinshu 025 itself specifies a Western year.
