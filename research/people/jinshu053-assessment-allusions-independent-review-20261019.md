# Jinshu 053 assessment allusions: independent partial review

Scope: independent review of `jinshu053-assessment-allusions-proposal-20261019.md`,
against Git object `aee5f25:data/jinshu/053.json` and the historical witnesses
below. This is not a full chapter editorial review, publication approval, date

## s0190 — revise wording; allusion identifications supported

The source is `坎牲之謀已構，斃犬之譖遂行；`. The current literal's
“dog-burial slanders” mistakes 斃 (died) for burial; the idiomatic “forged
prayers” is not the wording of this passage. The proposal correctly identifies
two comparison stories, without making them events in Sima Yu's life. The
[Zuo Tradition, Xiang 26](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/春秋左氏傳/襄公#襄公二十六年)
describes Yi Li staging an apparent covenant with a sacrifice and written
evidence; the imprisoned Song heir Cuo hangs himself when no rescue comes.
The [Zuo Tradition, Xi 4](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/春秋左氏傳/僖公#僖公四年)
describes Lady Li poisoning sacrificial meat returned by Shen Sheng; a dog
and a servant die, after which she accuses him. Both are actual passages in
the linked texts. The much later [Xungai zuanwen](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/mid-page/7555251822035402798)
identifies these two allusions in this very Jinshu passage; treat that as an
interpretive witness, not a contemporary authorial annotation.

Hold the exact proposed English pending revision: “the slander of the dead
dog” can wrongly suggest that the dog was slandered, while “slander ... was
carried out” obscures 遂行 (the slander took effect / was put into effect).
For review, the literal could read: “The plot of the buried sacrifice had
been devised, and the slander over the dead dog took effect.” An idiomatic
version may retain “like” to make the comparison explicit. The explanatory
note's account is substantively supported, but should avoid implying that
the sacrificial story calls the slander victim guilty or that the dog alone
died. This recommendation does not waive independent review of the final
field values or their publication representation.

## s0192 — idiomatic comparison supported; literal wording on hold

The source is `遂使冤逾楚建，酷甚戾園。`. The current literal invents a
“Liyuan prison” and the idiomatic omits the named comparisons. The proposed
idiomatic identifying Prince Jian of Chu and Han's Prince Li is a defensible
interpretation of the allusions, not a claim that a garden was harmed. In the
[Book of Han 63](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/漢書/卷063), Liu Ju is styled
戾太子 and his death and the earlier accusations are recounted. In
[Later Han 56, Zhang Hao](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/後漢書/卷56), the
memorial says 戾園興兵: this is an independent usage associating “Li Garden”
with the prince acting, not literal soldiers raised by the precinct. The
later commentary in the latter page explains his posthumous designation and
memorial precinct; do not conflate the date of that commentary with the
memorial's date. The Qing Xungai zuanwen explicitly identifies both allusions
in this particular Jinshu sentence.

Hold the proposed literal “what Li Garden endured”: it assigns suffering to
the place itself and unnecessarily resolves the metonym. A literal rendering
could retain the expression without personifying the precinct: “Thus the
injustice exceeded that of Jian of Chu, and the cruelty surpassed that of Li
Garden”; an accessible footnote can explain the reference to Liu Ju. “That of
Li Garden” remains metonymic, so retain the note rather than presenting it as
a prison or ordinary toponym. Both the chosen wording and reader-facing note
remain subject to normal final editorial approval.

## s0197 — interpretation supported with version caveat

The source is `掇蜂構隙，歸胙生災。`. Current literal “Gathering wasps”
and current idiomatic “Wasp-and-meat allusions to court intrigue” are not
adequate sentence translations. The proposed literal “Picking the bee sowed
estrangement; returning the sacrificial meat brought disaster” preserves the
parallel and the proposed idiomatic correctly signals comparisons. These are
reasonable draft wordings, conditional on an accessible explanatory note and
independent review of final presentation. The story of poisoned offerings is
the source linked under s0190.

[Taiping yulan 950, Bee](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/SBCK435/chapter/1kgozxcncjz16)
preserves a bee story attributed to *Lienü zhuan*: Boqi attempts to remove a
bee placed on his stepmother's clothing, she accuses him, and his father
suspects him. This Northern Song compilation is a later witness; attribution
alone does not establish the exact form available to the Jinshu compilers.
However, Lu Ji's Western Jin poem
[*Junzi xing*](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sibu_Congkan0587-陸機-陸士衡文集-1-1.djvu/87)
already uses the exact phrase `掇蜂` in `掇蜂滅天道`. That is evidence the
expression was circulating before the Jinshu compilation, not by itself proof
that its author knew precisely the later *Taiping yulan* version. The online
transcription is marked unproofread, and the parallel witness in the
[Six Commentators' Wenxuan, 28](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/六臣註文選_(四庫全書本)/卷28)
corroborates the wording; its much later comment describes a different version
of the story and must not be backdated to Lu Ji. This distinction strengthens
the interpretive basis while retaining the proposal's necessary caution.
