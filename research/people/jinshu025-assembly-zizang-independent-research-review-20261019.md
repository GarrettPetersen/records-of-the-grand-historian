## Assembly

The cited CTP page did not open directly. A separate accessible transcription of
[Yizhoushu 王會解](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/NA03787/chapter/1l3ws7lb39hkj)
has the gathering at 成周, the Yin duke, and Cao Shu, with commentary and
textual variants; an additional [transcription](https://wk.100ke.info/wiki/%E9%80%B8%E5%91%A8%E6%9B%B8/%E5%8D%B7%E4%B8%83)
also has the relevant three names. The editions differ, including 帟/弈 and
the presence of 者 in the inner-terrace line, so neither is proof of the exact
wording in the inaccessible CTP edition. The Cao Shu context in the former
uses punctuation differing from the note. These agree with the note's bounded
association, not independent verification of a specific assembly year or bow.

The [Indiana compilation](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/0b73023a-bc00-4e10-8656-753292abf2e2/content)
does list Cheng 1042–1006 in the chronology table on PDF page 5. The
[Early China article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/early-china/article/chronology-of-western-zhou/0D661C3365537C0D15AD896F72F9DF70)
discusses 1020 and 1005 as competing Kang initial-year reconstructions, and
1010 as the study's preferred result under its assumptions, including the
Boqin inference. Its account of Shaughnessy's 1005 also discusses a second
initial year, 1003; the study rejects his alternative. These are reasoned
reconstructions, not directly dated evidence for s0016–s0017. No exhaustively
bounded event year is established, and I did not verify the note's unverified
18-year claim. Retain the uncertainty and obtain event/identity/editorial
review separately.

## Zi Zang

The directly accessible [Wikisource topical arrangement, volume 42](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%B7%A6%E5%82%B3%E7%B4%80%E4%BA%8B%E6%9C%AC%E6%9C%AB_(%E5%9B%9B%E5%BA%AB%E5%85%A8%E6%9B%B8%E6%9C%AC)/%E5%8D%B742)
sets the Zi Zang episode under Xi 24: it states that he liked collecting the
caps and was killed in the eighth month. It is a later arrangement of the
tradition, not independent contemporary corroboration. The cited modern
annotated Zuo transcription's search-indexed copy explicitly glosses Xi 24 as
636 BCE, but the direct page returned 403 during this review. The cited Taiwan
dissertation could not be opened in the browsing tool, so I did not verify it.
The source's eighth lunar month is not itself a Gregorian conversion, and the
local Jinshu passage does not itself give 636. A future upper-bound proposal
requires calendar-convention review, a careful provenance representation and
fresh independent date audit; this research review does not approve that bound.
