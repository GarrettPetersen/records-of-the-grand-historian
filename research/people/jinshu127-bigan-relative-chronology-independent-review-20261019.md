## Findings

The research note's limited conclusions are supported by the cited texts:

- In [Shiji 38, revision 8737306](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=8737306&title=%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98%2F%E5%8D%B7038), the narrative has King Zhou kill Bi Gan and subsequently describes King Wu defeating Zhou and conquering Yin. That ordering is a claim in the transmitted narrative, not independent proof of the events.
- I confirmed that [Shiji 4, revision 7909147](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?oldid=7909147&title=%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98%2F%E5%8D%B7004) contains `命閎夭封比干之墓` after the account of the defeat; King Wu orders Hong Yao to honor Bi Gan's grave. The same chapter itself also recounts Bi Gan's death before the expedition. The two Shiji chapters are parts of one later work, not two independent contemporary attestations.
- Local Jinshu 127 s0232 refers to King Wu honoring Bi Gan's tomb as an example from the past. This mention attests a recollection concerning the deceased, not Bi Gan's living presence or activity when the tomb was honored. I did not independently establish the identity of the speaker or the date of the speech beyond the text and context presented in the research note.
- Neither cited passage assigns a Western calendar year to Bi Gan's death or the tomb action. They do not establish BC 1100 as a Bi Gan activity date; that narrower finding does not rule out the year on evidence from other sources. The texts' internal regnal and calendrical statements are not themselves a demonstrated conversion to an absolute Western year.

**Disposition:** The research note is an appropriately limited source-based chronology finding. Retain its distinction between a transmitted tradition and independently established history. No numerical date, source repair, current fingerprint validity, or chapter approval follows from this review; any change requires the normal current-input workflow and fresh complete independent date audit.
