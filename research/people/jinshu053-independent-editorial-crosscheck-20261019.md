## Text restoration: two, not one, omitted characters

* `s0170`: staging `太子三子：、臧、尚，並與父同幽金墉。` omits the
  name in an explicit list of three. [Wikisource Jinshu 053](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/晉書/卷053)
  gives `太子三子：虨、臧、尚，並與父同幽金墉。` and
  [Shidianguji Jinshu 053](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/HY2499/chapter/1mb9kswm56c6j)
  also gives `虨、臧、尚`. Restoring precisely `虨` is well supported. Both
  online texts may depend on a common edition; I have not proven independent
  manuscript or edition ancestry. Do not claim they do.
* `s0171`: staging `字道文，永康元年正月，薨。` independently drops the
  same name at the head of Bin's biography. Wikisource has
  `虨字道文，永康元年正月，薨。`; Shidianguji has
  `虨字道文，永康元年正月薨。`. Restore only leading `虨`, retaining
  this project's punctuation. Current English supplies Bin in both rows;
  `s0171` idiomatic additionally asserts “Eldest”, which this sentence alone
  does not state and should not be imported without separate evidence.

## Row-by-row draft assessment

The proposed wording is *locally defensible as an English replacement* for
`s0144`–`s0149`, `s0150`–`s0152`, `s0155`–`s0157`, `s0159`, `s0161`,
`s0162`, `s0165`, and `s0203`. This is limited to the listed rows, not a
whole-chapter approval. Preserve the open quotation across `s0203`–`s0204`.
For `s0155` 莫訟 could also mean that the wrong could not be pleaded rather
than that nobody pleaded it; the draft is reasonable but an explicitly
passive version would avoid narrowing. For `s0153`, both drafts broadly
represent 牝亂, but prefer “disorder wrought by women” over “disorder at the
women's hands”; avoid presenting an unspecified `us` as explicit Chinese.

* `s0154`: accept the proposed allusive reading as defensible, not a literal
  claim the people actually offered a ransom. [Yellow Bird](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/詩經/黃鳥)
  has the parallel hypothetical `如可贖兮，人百其身`; the draft's question
  preserves the lament, although “a hundred lives in exchange for yours” is
  less likely to suggest an actual transaction than “ransom yours.”
* `s0158`: the allusion to the Huguan elder and Qianqiu is sound: [Hanshu
  063](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/漢書/卷063) records Huguan elder Mao's
  memorial, while [Hanshu 066](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/漢書/卷066)
  records Tian Qianqiu's plea and Han Wudi's awakening. But `悟己` is compact
  and the lament is spoken by a different emperor using the precedent for
  himself. “Qianqiu awakened him to his own error” makes the historical Han
  emperor the unambiguous referent and may suppress the speaker's analogy;
  do not silently mark that wording resolved. Prefer a deliberately
  allusive “Huguan remonstrated with his ruler; Qianqiu brought awakening”
  pending a philological resolution of `己`. `Elder` and `emperor` are
  historical expansions and should remain explicitly identified as such.
* `s0160`: the text `皇孫啟建，隆祚爾子` refers contextually to the
  grandson Zang, **the addressee Yu's son**, not necessarily two separate
  people. [Jinshu 053](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/晉書/卷053) later says
  `今立臧為皇太孫`. The draft's two clauses could misleadingly suggest a
  separate grandson benefits a separate son. Prefer “The imperial grandson,
  your son, has been established; may his fortunes flourish,” subject to
  checking the force of 隆祚. Treat 降祚 found in another transcription as a
  variant, not permission to change the pinned Chinese without collation.
* `s0163`: reject “plumes” in both proposed fields as insufficiently sourced.
  The [Duan commentary on 說文 髦](https://www.shuowen.org/view/5706)
  expressly includes horse manes among the senses, and another source treats
  髦 as a flag ornament, so neither feathers nor decorated horse manes is
  established by the character alone. The subsequent `羽蓋` does explicitly
  describe feathered canopies, but cannot prove `華髦` means feather plumes.
  Use “splendid [hair/horse] ornaments” only with the uncertainty kept in the
  note, or retain a research hold until an edition/commentary establishes it.
* `s0164`: feathered canopies and ordered officials are supported. Avoid
  inventing that the officials “stand” stationary in a moving procession;
  “officials in solemn ranks” fits both fields.

## Additional nearby defects outside the draft

This deliberately checks nearby units, not all 209 units. More independent
review is still required, including both fields and later repeated names.

* `s0131` literal's “Commandant of the Guard Yi Ce” reads the verb 策 as part
  of a name. Idiomatic correctly separates Yi from announcing by patent; the
  literal needs matching correction.
* `s0132`–`s0139` idiomatic repeatedly replaces a continuous first-person
  mourning address with summaries, e.g. `s0138` “Imperial rhetoric of inner
  ache.” This is the same class of defect the draft fixes in the later lament,
  and must be reviewed as translated speech, not accepted as chapter closure.
  `s0139` ritual 太牢 should not be asserted to mean *only* an ox sacrifice:
  ritual usage varies; preserve the term or source the exact interpretation.
* `s0141` literal says “eldest-son hemmed mourning,” but 斬衰 is unhemmed;
  [禮記正義](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/禮記正義/40) explicitly links
  不緝 with 斬. The ministers' 齊衰 is a separate hemmed mourning grade.
  Idiomatic omits the emperor's and officials' grades and He Yu's name/role.
* `s0143` 洗馬 is a crown-prince's office rather than evidence that the man
  was personally grooming horses; consult the office-specific source before
  accepting “Groom of the Household.”
* `s0167`, `s0184` idiomatic “canonized” imports a misleading religious
  designation; the source gives posthumous titles.
* `s0173` idiomatic adds “Second son”, not stated in this unit. `s0177`
  idiomatic omits the actual appointment/title and introduces a full personal
  name that needs source support, while `s0178` similarly paraphrases away
  the court offices. `s0188` idiomatic says Yu was enthroned: he was installed
  as heir, not emperor. These are concrete further review targets, not an
  exhaustive defect census.
