### s0154 — 爾之逝矣，誰百其形？

- Before literal: `You have gone—who restores your form?`
- Before idiomatic: `Body lost to murder.`
- Proposed literal: `You have passed away; who could offer a hundred bodies in your place?`
- Proposed idiomatic: `Now that you are gone, who could give a hundred lives to ransom yours?`
- Review note: the wording appears to adapt the lament in [Book of Songs, Qin Wind, Yellow Bird](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/詩經/黃鳥), `如可贖兮，人百其身`; retain the rhetorical question and obtain an independent allusion review.

### s0157 — 悠悠有識，孰不哀慟！

- Before literal: `Far and near anyone with insight—who fails bitter grief!`
- Before idiomatic: `Universal mourning asserted.`
- Proposed literal: `Among the many who understand, who does not grieve bitterly!`
- Proposed idiomatic: `Of all who understand, who could fail to mourn you bitterly!`

### s0158 — 壺關幹主，千秋悟己。

- Before literal: `Huguan's steadfast lord—autumn-thousand years awakened self.`
- Before idiomatic: `Allusion to righteous remonstrance.`
- Proposed literal: `Huguan addressed the ruler; Qianqiu awakened him to his own error.`
- Proposed idiomatic: `The elder of Huguan appealed to his ruler; Qianqiu made the emperor recognize his error.`
- Review note: the parallel alludes to the Huguan elder and Tian Qianqiu advocating for the wronged Han crown prince. Verify the grammatical force of `悟己` and whether the expansion to `elder` and `emperor` is appropriate; see [Hanshu 063](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/漢書/卷063) and [Hanshu 066](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/漢書/卷066). The original does not itself spell out the elder's personal name.

- Before idiomatic: `Justice spans dynasties.`
- Proposed literal: `In different ages the pattern is the same; past and present share one principle.`
- Proposed idiomatic: `Though the ages differ, the pattern is the same: one principle holds in past and present.`

### s0160 — 皇孫啟建，隆祚爾子。

- Before literal: `Imperial grandson begins enthronement—great fortune your sons.`
- Before idiomatic: `Grandson Zang named heir.`
- Proposed literal: `The imperial grandson has been established, extending great fortune to your son.`
- Proposed idiomatic: `The imperial grandson has been established as heir, bringing honor to your son.`
- Review note: `隆祚` admits different readings and another transcription reads `降祚`; this proposal translates the pinned `隆祚`, not a textual emendation. Review the force of `啟建` against the accession sequence, and preserve the source distinction between the imperial grandson and the addressee's son.

## Eastern Palace

### s0203 — 元康元年，出就東宮，又詔曰：「遹尚幼蒙，今出東宮，惟當賴師傅群賢之訓。

- Before literal: `In the first year of Yuankang he moved out to the Eastern Palace; an edict further said: "Yu is still young and untutored; now that he leaves the Eastern Palace he should rely solely on the instruction of his tutors and the worthy assembly.`
- Before idiomatic: `In Yuankang year one he moved to the Eastern Palace, and an edict declared: "Yu is still young and inexperienced; having left the Eastern Palace he must rely on his tutors and worthy mentors for instruction.`
- Proposed literal: `In the first year of Yuankang he went out to take up residence in the Eastern Palace; an edict further said: "Yu is still young and untutored; now that he goes out to the Eastern Palace, he must rely on the instruction of his tutors and the assembled worthy men.`
- Proposed idiomatic: `In the first year of Yuankang he moved to the Eastern Palace. An edict declared: "Yu is still young and inexperienced. Now that he is going to the Eastern Palace, he must depend on his tutors and the other worthy men for instruction.`
- Review note: the open quotation continues in s0204; do not add a closing quote here. The context supports 出 as going out to take up residence, not departing from the Eastern Palace.
