# Jinshu 053 s0092: primary-text parallels for 天 and 差

Research only; not an independent editorial approval, a textual emendation,
or a release-ready replacement. The letter assembly and reviewer holds remain
in force until a separate reviewer evaluates the whole passage. This report
distinguishes probable sense from an asserted fact about what actually occurred.

## Evidence and inference: who is 天?

1. Jinshu 053 s0045–s0046, in the preceding third-person account of the same
   episode, says Jia falsely claimed the sovereign was unwell, summoned the
   crown prince, and had Chen Wu give him wine and jujubes. The description is
   a narrative account and is not independent verification of all details of
   the letter, but it strongly establishes an **imperial court context** for
   the reported summons, rather than an established supernatural summons.
2. The later historical compilation Zizhi tongjian 083 s0172–s0177 explicitly
   presents the empress's claimed summons as the emperor's illness, the wine
   as given `以帝命`, and the accompanying reported speech as `天賜汝酒`.
   Its immediate juxtaposition of `帝命` with `天` is strong contextual
   support for an imperial referent in the parallel episode. It is not an
   independent eyewitness; shared source relationships and the compilation's
   narrative changes have not been collated.
3. The letter itself alternates `天教` (s0081, s0082, s0088), `天`
   (s0092, s0097), and `陛下` (s0091, s0093). The close context makes an
   imperial referent a defensible leading reading, but does not establish that
   every utterance was actually ordered by the emperor. In particular s0081,
   s0082 and s0088 report claims attributed to attendants or the empress.

**Provisional editorial direction:** an English reference to the emperor or
His Majesty, expressed inside the reported speaker's words, is supportable by
these parallels and preferable to presenting Heaven as the verified agent.
It remains an interpretation, and should not be converted into the narrator's
claim that an imperial command or gift really occurred. Whether `教` in each
occurrence contributes an order, or forms part of a circumlocution, and the
syntax of `昨 天教 與` in s0088 still require clause-specific review.

## Evidence and inference: what does 差 mean?

Jinshu 053 s0078 and s0080 establish Daowen's grave illness as the topic before
the empress's reported s0092 `天與汝酒，當使道文差也`. Direct comparison
within the *same history* supplies several clear medical uses:

- Jinshu 047 s0332 `疾病滿百日不差 ... 既差而後更用`: a sick official
  whose illness has not improved is removed, then reused when recovered.
- Jinshu 038 s0382 `幼得篤疾，日冀其差，今遂廢痼`: a severe illness and
  hope for recovery are contrasted with a chronic condition.
- Jinshu 095 s0158 `我病當差不？`: in illness, the speaker asks whether
  recovery will occur; this is a close semantic and syntactic parallel for
  `當 ... 差`, although s0092 also has causative `使`.

These examples materially strengthen **recovery** as the ordinary medical
reading of 差 in this local illness context, and thus “should make Daowen
recover” as a plausible report of the speaker's claim. They do **not** prove
Daowen actually recovered, that wine medically caused recovery, or precisely
whether `當` conveys obligation, expectation, intended effect, or rhetorical
pressure. An [annotated Japanese translation](https://readingnotesofjinshu.com/translation/biographies/vol-53)
instead renders the clause as an adverse outcome. It is a modern interpretation,
not a textual witness; it supplies no explicit note explaining its s0092
parsing. Its competing reading warrants a targeted independent language review,
but these primary parallels show that treating “recover” as merely unsupported
is too strong. Do not silently substitute either interpretation as certain.

## Recommended next step

Have a separate reviewer compare the medical parallels and the literal grammar
`天與汝酒，當使道文差也` in the full quoted exchange. Seek an explicit
choice between recovery and adverse-outcome readings, with a reason for `使`
and `當`, and evaluate whether the implied speaker is presenting a wish,
prediction, or pressure. If recovery is accepted, frame it as the empress's
reported claim, not as a historical or medical fact. Keep the outer letter,
nested-speech delimiters, and all remaining independent review and date/identity
gates intact. The assembly's other s0082, s0087, s0091, s0093, s0098, and
s0104 holds are unaffected by this evidence.
