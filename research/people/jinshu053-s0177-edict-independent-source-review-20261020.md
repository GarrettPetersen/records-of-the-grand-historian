## Proposed rendering

- Literal: `Let Consort Wang be brought back to stand as his mother, and let her be styled Taifei of the Grandson-Heir.`
- Idiomatic: `Bring Consort Wang back to stand as his mother, with the title Taifei of the Grandson-Heir.`

The first clause renders `還` as bringing her back, rather than restoring an
office, and `以母之` as a maternal standing without asserting she was his
biological mother or specifying the practical duties that followed. The
sentence does not specify where she was brought back to. Keep `Taifei` as a
transliteration of `太妃` rather than silently assigning a technical English
equivalent; `of the Grandson-Heir` preserves `太孫`. It is a title, not the
status of a consort to the grandson. This is a prospective edict in a quotation,
not a statement that she had already raised him. Do not insert opening or
closing quotation marks at this intermediate sentence.

The existing literal's `returns to mother him—styled Grand Heir Consort Dowager`
does not mark the edict's directive clearly, while its `Dowager` may imply a
more precise institutional mapping than the evidence in this sentence alone.
The existing idiomatic `Wang Huifeng raised him as heir consort` implies a
completed act and ambiguously makes her a consort of the heir. The new wording
avoids both claims. It does not decide whether a better established English
institutional translation of `太孫太妃` exists; that question remains open.

## Evidence and identity caution

In s0065 `太子妃王氏` and three grandsons are escorted to Jinyong. The quoted
edict restores her maternal position after Zang becomes grandson-heir. The
[Taiping Yulan, juan 149](https://www.guoxuemi.com/wk/%E5%A4%AA%E5%B9%B3%E5%BE%A1%E8%A6%BD/0149)
records a formal appointment of the late crown prince's former consort as
`皇太孫太妃`; this supports the office context but does not license inserting
the extra character `皇` into the pinned s0177 source. The
[Zizhi Tongjian, juan 83](https://www.newman.edu.hk/html/chineseHistoryweb/tungkam/083.htm)
also has the parallel clause `還妃王氏以母之`. Neither parallel alone proves
the editions are independent manuscripts.

The chapter's s0035 names Wang Yan's younger daughter Huifeng as betrothed to
the crown prince; s0065 and s0177 call the consort only `王氏`. Connecting them
is a contextual identity inference, not an explicit occurrence of `惠風` in
s0065 or s0177. Candidate p038 currently presents `Wang Huifeng`/`王氏` as an
explicit personal-name claim supported by those two units. Review that claim's
evidence and inference level in the normal identity workflow; this proposal
does not unilaterally rewrite the person record or create a biological
mother–child relationship. The English `Consort Wang` reference would require
fresh mention and scanner/disposition reconciliation before an artifact could
be accepted.
