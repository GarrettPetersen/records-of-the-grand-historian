The source says: `漢高祖佩之，後世名曰傳國璽，與斬白蛇劍俱為乘輿所寶。`
The seal is the referent carried over from the First Emperor's jade seal in
s0536. The current idiomatic wording, “Liu Bang claimed both the ‘transmission
seal’ and the snake sword as regalia,” loses the stated act of wearing and
the attribution of the name to later generations, and wrongly assigns the
joint claim to Liu Bang. The sentence says the seal and sword were both
treasured by the imperial household; it does not specify that he claimed
the sword or that both became treasured at that moment. The current literal
version retains more information, but “treasured by the imperial carriage”
is an opaque literal rendering of 乘輿, here a metonym for the emperor or
imperial establishment, not a claim about a vehicle's feelings.

The nearby s0538 says the sword was subsequently lost in an armory fire;
s0539–s0540 trace the seal separately. Neither passage justifies adding a
date or attributing the joint treasuring to a specific reign. A later textual
quotation of the Jinshu passage independently preserves the same grammatical
sequence: [Xuelin, juan 4, 璽](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/SK1793/chapter/1k02bpcv0fndn).
This is a textual parallel, not independent proof that the artifacts or events
are historical. The earlier seal research's translation concern is well
founded; its conditional 207 BCE date proposal is separate and is neither
accepted nor rejected by this editorial assessment.

## Exact proposed replacements

Literal:

> Han Gaozu wore it; later generations called it the seal of state transmission. Together with the sword used to slay the white serpent, it was treasured by the imperial court.

Idiomatic:

> Emperor Gaozu of Han wore it. Later generations called it the Seal of State Transmission; together with the sword used to slay the white serpent, it was treasured by the imperial court.

“Imperial court” is an idiomatic metonym for 乘輿, not a claim that the
court was a literal person. The text reports the sword's description without
independently verifying the serpent episode. No specific year, acquisition,
first use, or continuous use is supplied. Do not insert an inference from
the external seal traditions into either translation.
