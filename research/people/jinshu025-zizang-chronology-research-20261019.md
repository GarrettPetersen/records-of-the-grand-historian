[later topical arrangement of the same chronicle](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%B7%A6%E5%82%B3%E7%B4%80%E4%BA%8B%E6%9C%AC%E6%9C%AB_%28%E5%9B%9B%E5%BA%AB%E5%85%A8%E6%9B%B8%E6%9C%AC%29/%E5%8D%B742)
prints the sequence under Xi 24, with killing in the eighth month. The latter
is a later reorganization, not an independent contemporary witness. The
[annotated transcription](https://www.moshuyuan.com/zh-tw/chapter/jingbu/zuo-zhuan/xi-gong/xi-gong-er-shi-si-nian.html)
labels Xi 24 as 636 BCE; that label is a modern annotation, not original
chronicle wording. A [Taiwan university dissertation](https://nhuir.nhu.edu.tw/retrieve/27103/090NHU00076020-006.pdf)
also calls Xi 24 B.C. 636. These are conventional year-label correlations;
this note has not established a proleptic Gregorian day conversion.
