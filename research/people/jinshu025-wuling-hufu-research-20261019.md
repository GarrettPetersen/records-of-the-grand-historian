Jinshu 025 s0427 attributes to Hu Guang the retrospective statement that
King Wuling of Zhao adopted Hu dress and adorned his head with gold and sable.
The statement itself gives no year. The blocked candidate has left the
associated claims unresolved; merely importing the whole reign interval would
not establish the date of the specific action.

## Evidence

1. [Shiji 43, fixed Wikisource revision 7903603](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98%2F%E5%8D%B7043&oldid=7903603),
   in its account of King Wuling's nineteenth year, narrates his discussions
   of Hu dress, his own adoption of the dress, and issuance of a Hu-dress
   order; the twentieth-year entry follows. The sequential narrative is not a
   day-specific conversion of the events and does not explicitly date the
   gold-and-sable detail in Jinshu's later account.
2. Ye Guoliang, [「從出土文物看《儀禮》内容的時代」](https://ejournals.lib.hkbu.edu.hk/index.php/sinohumanitas/article/download/2133/2099/2475),
   *Sino-Humanitas*, no. 21, printed p. 90 (PDF p. 12), explicitly associates
   King Wuling's nineteenth year and his Hu-dress/archery order with BC 307.
   This is a scholar's published chronological placement, not the ancient
   text's own Western-calendar date or a proof of an exact calendar day.

Together these sources give a concrete, source-attributed basis for an
independent reviewer to consider a BC 307 contextual date for Wuling's
adoption/order, instead of an undifferentiated reign span. They do not show
that the particular gold-and-sable head decoration was first made or worn in
that year, nor prove that Hu Guang independently witnessed the fourth-century
BC events. They also do not establish how every date-claim field should be
mapped to that distinction. Separate the reform and the decoration rather
than silently transferring the year to both.
