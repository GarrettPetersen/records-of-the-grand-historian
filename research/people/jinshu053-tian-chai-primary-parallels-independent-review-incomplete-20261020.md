## Verification

- Jinshu 053 s0045–s0046 really does say that Jia falsely claimed the ruler
  was unwell, summoned the prince, then did not see him and sent Chen Wu to
  give wine and jujubes, pressing him to drunkenness. It is a third-person
  narrative of the same episode and is not independent confirmation of the
  letter's alleged utterances. It supports an imperial court setting, not
  direct proof of who ordered or gave anything.
- Zizhi tongjian 083 s0174–s0176 really juxtaposes the empress's false claim
  that the emperor was unwell, Chen Wu acting `以帝命`, and the reported
  `天賜汝酒`. This strongly supports an emperor-directed interpretation in
  that account. But it is a later compilation, with uncollated source
  dependence; its speaker and wording differ from the letter (the compilation
  has Wu pressing the prince, whereas 053 s0091 gives the empress's call).
  It is corroboration of a contextual interpretation, not an independent
  eyewitness or license to import its voice into every clause in 053.
- The letter has `天教` at s0081, s0082 and s0088; `天` at s0092 and s0097;
  `陛下` at s0091 and s0093. All are in the same court dialogue, making an
  imperial referent a defensible leading reading, while these occurrences need
  not be interchangeable in grammar or reference. In s0088 the segmentation
  and force of `昨天教與` remain disputed. Rendering as an emperor reference
  inside reported speech does not establish the truth of an actual imperial
  command. It is a contextual translation choice, not a lexical proof for
  each occurrence of 教.
- Jinshu 047 s0332 indeed contrasts illness not improving after a hundred
  days with reuse after `既差`; 038 s0382 contrasts hope that a severe illness
  will improve with permanent chronic disability; and 095 s0158–s0159 asks
  whether a sick person will recover and replies about that prospect. These
  are genuine same-history examples of medical 差. The 095 question
  `我病當差不` parallels the sequence 當 + 差 but **does not contain 使**:
  none of these examples alone resolves the causation or syntax of 053 s0092.
  The target's s0078 and s0080 do establish Daowen's grave illness context.
  Consequently recovery is a plausible, well-supported lexical reading, but
  the examples do not demonstrate that Daowen recovered, or settle the force
  of 當, the subject of 使, and what action or agent is represented as bringing
  about the result. English `should make` can suggest obligation rather than
  prediction; it is a possible gloss, not a resolved editorial choice.

I checked the cited [Japanese rendering](https://readingnotesofjinshu.com/translation/biographies/vol-53):
it gives an adverse outcome for Daowen in this exchange, and does not provide
an explicit explanatory note immediately on that clause. That modern reading
is useful evidence of disagreement, not another manuscript witness and not in
itself a grammatical argument. I cannot adjudicate its precise implied parse
from the translation alone. The recovery parallels materially strengthen the
positive reading but do not, by themselves, prove the adverse interpretation
impossible. An independent philological decision should explain the whole
sentence in context before either becomes final wording.
