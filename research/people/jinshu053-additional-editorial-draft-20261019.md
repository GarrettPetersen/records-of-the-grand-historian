## Posthumous restoration and funeral

### s0131 — 及賈庶人死，乃誅劉振、孫慮、程據等，冊復太子曰：「皇帝使使持節、兼司空、衛尉伊策故皇太子之靈曰：嗚呼！

- Literal: `After Jia, reduced to a commoner, died, Liu Zhen, Sun Lü, Cheng Ju, and others were executed. A patent restored the crown prince, saying: "The emperor sends the credential-bearing envoy Yi, concurrent Director of Works and Commandant of the Guard, to address the spirit of the late crown prince by patent: Alas!`
- Idiomatic: `After the deposed Jia died, Liu Zhen, Sun Lü, Cheng Ju, and others were executed. The emperor restored the crown prince by patent and sent Yi, Commandant of the Guard and acting Director of Works, with the imperial credentials to address the late prince's spirit: "Alas!`
- Review: `伊策` parses as Yi and the verb 策; Ce is not established as part of the name. Check whether rendering 兼 as “acting” is appropriate here; literal says concurrent. The patent's nested quotation is continuous through s0140.

### s0132 — 維爾少資岐嶷之質，荷先帝殊異之寵，大啟土宇，奄有淮陵。

- Literal: `In your youth you were endowed with an exceptionally intelligent nature; you received the late emperor's extraordinary favor, were granted a great territory, and held Huailing.`
- Idiomatic: `From childhood you showed exceptional intelligence, and the late emperor favored you greatly, granting you a great fief in Huailing.`
- Review HOLD: `淮陵` may be geographic shorthand rather than the name of a single locality. Confirm before accepting “in Huailing”; the Chinese does not say he personally opened the territory.

### s0133 — 朕奉遵遺旨，越建爾儲副，以光顯我祖宗。

- Literal: `I respectfully followed the late emperor's instructions and established you as heir, to bring glory to our ancestors.`
- Idiomatic: `Following the late emperor's wishes, I made you heir so that you might bring honor to our ancestors.`

### s0134 — 祗爾德行，以從保傅，事親孝敬，禮無違者。

- Literal: `You attended to virtuous conduct, followed your guardians and tutors, served your parents with filial reverence, and did not depart from propriety.`
- Idiomatic: `You cultivated virtue under your guardians and tutors, served your parents with filial respect, and observed every propriety.`
- Review HOLD: check the syntax and sense of `祗爾德行，以從保傅`; “cultivated virtue under” is an interpretive smoothing, not an independently established literal parsing.

### s0135 — 而朕昧於凶構，致爾於非命之禍，俾申生、孝己復見於今。

- Literal: `Yet I was blind to the wicked plot and brought you to the calamity of an unnatural death, so that Shen Sheng and Xiao Ji appeared again in our own time.`
- Idiomatic: `Yet I failed to see the wicked plot that led to your untimely death: the tragedies of Shen Sheng and Xiao Ji have been repeated in our day.`

### s0136 — 賴宰相賢明，人神憤怨，用啟朕心，討厥有罪，咸伏其辜。

- Literal: `Thanks to the wisdom of the chief ministers, and to the indignation of humans and spirits, my mind was opened; I punished the guilty, and all met the penalty for their crimes.`
- Idiomatic: `The wisdom of my ministers and the outrage of people and spirits opened my eyes. I punished those responsible, and each received the penalty for the crime.`

### s0137 — 何補於荼毒冤魂酷痛哉？

- Literal: `Yet how can this make amends for the cruel suffering of your wronged spirit, subjected to such torment?`
- Idiomatic: `But what can this do to ease the cruel suffering inflicted on your wronged spirit?`
- Review: assess the boundary of `荼毒` and `冤魂`, avoiding a specific claim that poison caused death; he was killed with a pestle in s0123.

### s0138 — 是用忉怛悼恨，震動於五內。

- Literal: `Therefore I grieve in anguish and regret, shaken to the five inner organs.`
- Idiomatic: `And so grief and regret shake me to my very core.`

### s0139 — 今追復皇太子喪禮，反葬京畿，祠乙太牢。

- Literal: `Now I restore posthumously the funeral rites of a crown prince, return you for burial in the capital region, and perform the yi tai-lao sacrifice.`
- Idiomatic: `I now restore the funeral rites due a crown prince, bring you back for burial near the capital, and perform the yi tai-lao sacrifice.`
- Review HOLD: exact force of `祠乙` is uncertain. `yi` is merely a provisional transliteration, not an established ritual name or recipient. Preserve `tai-lao` provisionally rather than specify species or number; review ritual wording and possible textual corruption against a critical edition. Do not apply this row as written until resolved.

### s0140 — 魂而有靈，尚獲爾心。」

- Literal: `If the soul has awareness, may it yet obtain your heart."`
- Idiomatic: `If your spirit is aware, may it still know your heart."`
- Review HOLD: pronouns and the force of `獲爾心` are obscure. Neither rendering claims a confident solution; seek a commentary or textual collation, and preserve uncertainty rather than guessing.

### s0141 — 帝為太子服長子斬衰，群臣齊衰，使尚書和郁率東宮官屬具吉凶之制，迎太子喪于許昌。

- Literal: `For the crown prince the emperor wore the unhemmed mourning dress due an eldest son; the ministers wore hemmed mourning. He ordered the Minister of the Secretariat He Yu to lead the Eastern Palace officials in preparing the auspicious and mourning rites and to meet the prince's remains at Xuchang.`
- Idiomatic: `The emperor wore the unhemmed mourning dress prescribed for an eldest son, while his ministers wore hemmed mourning. He sent the minister He Yu and the Eastern Palace staff to prepare the prescribed rites and receive the prince's remains at Xuchang.`
- Review: [禮記正義](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/禮記正義/40) relates 斬 to 不緝; do not call it hemmed. Check whether `具吉凶之制` refers to preparing objects, regulations, or both, and the precise English title of 尚書.

### s0142 — 喪之發也，大風雷電，幃蓋飛裂。

- Literal: `When the funeral set out, a great wind, thunder, and lightning arose, and the curtains and canopy were torn away.`
- Idiomatic: `As the funeral procession set out, fierce wind, thunder, and lightning struck; the curtains and canopy were blown apart.`

### s0143 — 又為哀策曰：「皇帝臨軒，使洗馬劉務告于皇太子之殯曰：咨爾遹！

- Literal: `They also composed a funeral proclamation: "The emperor approached the pavilion and directed Liu Wu, a xima officer, to address the crown prince's coffin, saying: 'Ah, Yu!`
- Idiomatic: `A funeral proclamation was also composed: "The emperor came to the pavilion and sent Liu Wu, an officer of the crown prince's household, to address the prince's coffin: 'Alas, Yu!`
- Review HOLD: the exact office translation and force of `臨軒` and `殯` need confirmation. [Taiping Yulan, 太子洗馬](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/SBCK435/chapter/1kgoze3wz5c7u) documents 洗馬 as an Eastern Palace office, not necessarily someone physically grooming horses. Avoid importing a specific functional title not established in this context.

### s0170 — 太子三子：、臧、尚，並與父同幽金墉。

- Literal: `The crown prince's three sons—Bin, Zang, and Shang—were all confined with their father at Jinyong.`

- Text-restoration proposal, **separate from the English decision**: change only `太子三子：、臧、尚` to `太子三子：虨、臧、尚`. Current English's “Bin” is not present in the current Chinese; the proposed Chinese restoration and English must be reviewed together.

### s0171 — 字道文，永康元年正月，薨。

- Text-restoration proposal, **separate from the English decision**: change only initial `字道文` to `虨字道文`, preserving existing punctuation. Current Chinese does not explicitly name Bin here. Do not add “eldest” without separately verified evidence.
- For both restorations [Wikisource Jinshu 053](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/晉書/卷053) and [Shidianguji Jinshu 053](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/HY2499/chapter/1mb9kswm56c6j) contain 虨. ### s0188 — 及於繼明宸極，守器春坊，四教不勤，三朝或闕，豹姿未變，鳳德已衰，信惑奸邪，疏斥正士，好屠酤之賤役，耽苑囿之佚遊，可謂靡不有初，鮮克有終者也。

- Literal: `But once he stood in succession to the throne and guarded the succession in the Eastern Palace, he neglected the four teachings and sometimes missed the three audiences. His leopard-like bearing had not changed, yet his phoenix-like virtue had already declined. He trusted and was misled by the wicked, kept upright men at a distance, favored the lowly occupations of butchers and wine sellers, and indulged in idle excursions in the parks. He could be described as one who made a beginning but seldom carried it through to the end.`
- Idiomatic: `But once he became heir and took up his place in the Eastern Palace, he neglected his instruction and sometimes failed to attend the prescribed audiences. Though his fine promise remained, his virtue had declined: he trusted wicked people, shunned upright men, preferred the lowly work of butchers and wine sellers, and spent his time in idle pleasure in the parks. He had made a promising beginning but failed to see it through.`
- Review HOLD: `繼明宸極` is succession language, not evidence that Yu ever reigned as emperor; do not say he was enthroned. Verify 四教 and 三朝 against Jin ritual usage, `守器` and the rhetorical animal images before treating either proposed rendering as definitive. The idiomatic field currently suppresses the four/three distinction; it needs an independently approved complete rendering, not an unmarked omission.
