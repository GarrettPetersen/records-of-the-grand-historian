耶律伯堅，字壽之，桓州人。 氣豪俠，喜與名士遊。 用薦舉入官，為工部主事。 至元九年，轉保定路清苑縣尹。 初，安肅州苦徐水之害，訴於大司農司，大司農司欲奪水故道，導水使東。 東則清苑境也，地勢不利，果導之，則清苑被其害，而水亦必反故道為災。 伯堅陳其形勢，圖其利害，要大司農司官及郡守行視可否，事遂得已。 縣西有塘水，溉民田甚廣，勢家據以為磑，民以失利來訴。 伯堅命毀磑，決其水而注之田，許以溉田之餘月，乃得堰水置磑。 仍以其事聞於省部，著為定制。 縣居南北之衝，歲為親王大官治供帳於縣西，限以十月成，至明年復撤而新之，吏得併緣侵漁，其費不貲。 伯堅命築公館，以代供帳，其弊遂絕。 凡郡府賦役，於縣有重於他縣者，輒曰：「寧得罪於上，不可得罪於下。 」必詣府力爭之。 在清苑四年，民親戴之如父母，比去而猶思之，立石頌其德焉。 擢為恩州同知。
Yelü Bojian, whose courtesy name was Shouzhi, came from Huanzhou. He was spirited and chivalrous, and loved to keep company with men of renown. Recommended for office, he became a clerk in the Ministry of Works. In 1272 he was reassigned as magistrate of Qingyuan County on the Baoding circuit. Earlier, Ansu Prefecture had long suffered flooding from the Xu River and petitioned the Grand Agricultural Commission, which proposed to seize the river's former channel and divert its waters east. Eastward lay Qingyuan's territory, where the lay of the land was unfavorable; if the diversion went ahead, Qingyuan would be harmed, and the river would surely revert to its old course and bring disaster again. Bojian explained the terrain, mapped out the risks and benefits, and insisted that Grand Agricultural officials and the circuit intendant inspect the site in person; the plan was abandoned. West of the county stood a reservoir whose waters irrigated a vast stretch of farmland; powerful families had monopolized it for water mills, and the peasants sued over their lost irrigation. Bojian ordered the mills torn down, released the water back into the fields, and allowed mills only in the off-season after irrigation—only then might the water be dammed for milling. He also reported the case to the provincial administration, and it was enacted as a permanent rule. The county sat on the main route between north and south; every year quarters and provisions for imperial princes and high officials were erected west of the town, due by the tenth month, then torn down and rebuilt the next year—giving clerks endless chances to skim funds until the cost was beyond reckoning. Bojian had a public guesthouse built to replace the annual encampments, and the abuse ceased. Whenever the prefecture imposed levies or corvée heavier on his county than on others, he would say, "Better to anger my superiors than to wrong the people below." " He always went to the prefectural seat and fought the matter through. In four years at Qingyuan the people loved him as they would their own parents; long after he left they still missed him and set up a stone praising his virtue. He was promoted to vice-prefect of En Prefecture.

段直，字正卿，澤州晉城人。 至元十一年，河北、河東、山東盜賊充斥，直聚其鄉黨族屬，結壘自保。 世祖命大將略地晉城，直以其眾歸之，幕府承製，署直潞州元帥府右監軍。 其後論功行賞，分土世守，命直佩金符，為澤州長官。 澤民多避兵未還者，直命籍其田廬於親戚鄰人之戶，且約曰：「俟業主至，當析而歸之。 」逃民聞之，多來還者，命歸其田廬如約，民得安業。 素無產者，則出粟賑之； 為他郡所俘掠者，出財購之； 以兵死而暴露者，收而瘞之。 未幾，澤為樂土。 大修孔子廟，割田千畝，置書萬卷，迎儒士李俊民為師，以招延四方來學者。 不五六年，學之士子，以通經被選者，百二十有二人。 在官二十年，多有惠政。 朝廷特命提舉本州學校事，未拜而卒。
Duan Zhi, whose courtesy name was Zhengqing, came from Jincheng in Ze Prefecture. In 1274, as bandits overran Hebei, Hedong, and Shandong, Zhi rallied his clansmen and neighbors, built a fortified camp, and held their ground. Kublai sent a great general to take Jincheng; Zhi submitted with his followers, and the field command, acting on imperial warrant, appointed him Right Overseer of the Lu Prefecture Marshal's Office. Later, when merits were rewarded with hereditary fiefs, he was ordered to wear a gold tally and made chief administrator of Ze Prefecture. Many Ze residents had fled the fighting and not returned; Zhi had their fields and houses registered under relatives and neighbors, with the stipulation: "When the owners come back, everything will be divided and restored to them." " Refugees heard of this and many returned; he restored their property as promised, and the people could resume their livelihoods in peace. Those who owned nothing received grain from his stores; those captured and carried off to other prefectures were ransomed at his expense; and the bodies of soldiers left unburied on the field he gathered in and interred. Before long Ze had become a peaceful, thriving country. He greatly restored the Temple of Confucius, endowed a thousand mu of land, assembled ten thousand volumes, and invited the scholar Li Junmin as master to attract students from far and wide. Within five or six years, one hundred twenty-two students versed in the classics had passed selection for office. During twenty years in office he enacted many policies that benefited the people. The court specially appointed him to oversee the prefectural schools, but he died before he could assume the post.

諳都剌，字瑞芝，凱烈氏。 祖阿思蘭，嘗從大將阿術伐宋，仕至冀寧路達魯花赤，子孫因其名蘭，遂以蘭為氏。 諳都剌通經史，兼習諸國語。 成宗時，為翰林院札爾里赤，職書制誥。 會有旨命書籓王添力聖旨，諳都剌曰：「此旨非惟有虧國體，行且為民殃矣。 」帝聞之，謂近臣曰：「小吏如此，真難得也。 」事乃止。 尋授應奉翰林文字，凡蒙古傳記，多所校正。 升待制。 時方選守令，除遼州達魯花赤，以最聞，賜上尊名幣，除集賢直學士。 至順元年，遷襄陽路達魯花赤。 山西大饑，河南行省恐流民入境為變，檄守武關，諳都剌驗其良民，輒聽其度關。 吏曰：「得無違上命乎？ 」諳都剌曰：「吾防奸耳，非仇良民也，可不開其生路耶！ 」既又煮粥以食之，所活數万人。 又城臨漢水，歲有水患，為築堤城外，遂以無虞。 元統二年，除益都路總管。 俗頗悍黠，而諳都剌務興學校，以平易治之。 有上馬賊白晝劫人，久不能捕，諳都剌生擒之，其黨賂宣慰使羅鍋，誣以枉勘，縱其賊。 已而賊劫河間，復被獲，乃盡輸其情，而諳都剌之誣始白，俾再任一考。 親王買奴鎮益都，其府屬病民，諳都剌裁抑之，民以無擾。 至正六年卒，年七十。
Nadula, whose courtesy name was Ruizhi, belonged to the Kailie clan. His grandfather Aslan had followed the great general Aju in the campaigns against Song and rose to darughachi of Jining Circuit; later generations took the syllable Lan from his name as their surname. Nadula was learned in the classics and histories and fluent in several of the empire's languages. Under Emperor Chengzong he served in the Hanlin Academy as jarligchi, drafting imperial edicts and patents. When an order came to draft an edict for the feudatory prince Tianli, Nadula said, "This edict would not only compromise the dignity of the state—it would soon bring disaster on the people as well." " The emperor heard of it and told his close attendants, "For a junior clerk to speak so is truly rare." " The matter was dropped. He was soon appointed Hanlin Attendant for Documents and corrected numerous errors in Mongol-language biographical records. He was promoted to Academician-in-Waiting. When the court was selecting local administrators, he was made darughachi of Liaozhou; renowned for outstanding service, he received imperial gifts of wine and silk and was appointed Direct Scholar of the Hall of Gathered Talents. In 1330 he was transferred to darughachi of Xiangyang Circuit. When Shanxi was stricken by severe famine, the Henan Branch Secretariat, fearing unrest from refugees, ordered Wuguan Pass held; Nadula inspected those who were plainly honest folk and let them cross. A clerk asked, "Will this not defy your superiors' orders?" " Nadula replied, "I am guarding against wrongdoers, not making enemies of honest people—should we not leave them a way to live?" " He also had porridge cooked to feed them, saving tens of thousands of lives. The city also stood on the Han River and suffered annual floods; he built dikes outside the walls, and the danger passed. In 1334 he was appointed circuit intendant of Yidu. The local customs were fierce and cunning, but Nadula devoted himself to schools and governed with a light, even hand. Mounted bandits had long robbed travelers by day without being caught; Nadula captured them alive, but their accomplices bribed Pacification Commissioner Luo Guo to accuse him of wrongful prosecution and secured the bandits' release. Later the bandits struck Hejian, were captured again, and confessed everything; Nadula's innocence was finally established, and he was allowed to serve another term. Imperial Prince Mainu was stationed at Yidu, and his household staff preyed on the people; Nadula curbed them, and the populace was left in peace. He died in 1346 at the age of seventy.

子燮徹堅，同知新喻州事，以孝稱。
His son Xiechejian served as vice-prefect of Xinyu Prefecture and was famed for his filial devotion.

楊景行，字賢可，吉安太和州人。 登延祐二年進士第，授贛州路會昌州判官。 會昌民素不知井飲，汲於河流，故多疾癘； 不知陶瓦，以茅覆屋，故多火災。 景行教民穿井以飲，陶瓦以代茅茨，民始免於疾癘火災。 豪民十人，號十虎，干政害民，悉捕置之法。 乃創學舍，禮師儒，勸民斥腴田以膳士，弦誦之聲遂盛。 調永新州判官，奉郡府命，核民田租，除剷宿弊，姦欺不容，細民賴焉。 改江西行省照磨，轉撫州路宜黃縣尹，理白冤獄之不決者數十事。 升撫州路總管府推官，發擿姦伏，郡無冤獄。 金溪縣民陶甲，厚積而凶險，嘗屢誣陷其縣長吏罷去之，由是官吏畏其人，不敢詰治，陶遂暴橫於一郡。 景行至，以法痛繩之，徙五百里外。 金溪豪僧云住，發人塚墓取財物，事覺，官吏受賄，緩其獄，景行急按之，僧以賄動之，不聽，乃賂當道者，以危語撼之，一不顧，卒治之如法。 由是豪猾屏跡，良民獲安。 轉湖州路歸安縣尹，奉行省命，理荒田租，民無欺弊。 景行所歷州縣，皆有惠政； 所去，民皆立石頌之。 以翰林待制、朝列大夫致仕，年七十四卒。
Yang Jingxing, whose courtesy name was Xiankai, came from Taihe Prefecture in Ji'an. He passed the jinshi examination in 1315 and was appointed assistant prefect of Huichang on the Ganzhou circuit. The people of Huichang had never drawn drinking water from wells but took it from the river, and so suffered frequent epidemics; nor did they know fired tiles and roofed their houses with thatch, which led to many fires. Jingxing taught them to sink wells for drinking water and to use tiles instead of thatch, and they were finally spared epidemics and conflagrations. Ten powerful locals, known as the Ten Tigers, had meddled in government and preyed on the people; he arrested them all and punished them according to law. He founded a school, honored Confucian teachers, and urged the people to donate fertile land to support students; soon the sound of classical study filled the district. Transferred to Yongxin as assistant prefect, he carried out the prefecture's order to audit land rents, uprooted entrenched abuses, tolerated no fraud, and the common people were the beneficiaries. He became Records Examiner of the Jiangxi Branch Secretariat, then magistrate of Yihuang County on the Fuzhou circuit, where he resolved dozens of long-stalled wrongful convictions. Promoted to judicial investigator of the Fuzhou circuit administration, he exposed hidden crimes until the circuit held no wrongful cases. At Jinxi County a man named Tao Jia had grown wealthy and vicious; he had repeatedly framed county magistrates until they were removed, so that officials feared him and dared not prosecute, and Tao tyrannized the entire prefecture. When Jingxing arrived he punished Tao severely under the law and exiled him five hundred li away. A powerful Jinxi monk named Yunzhu had robbed graves for valuables; when the crime came to light, officials took bribes and delayed the case. Jingxing pressed the investigation; the monk tried to bribe him, then bribed higher authorities and threatened him with ominous words, but Jingxing ignored it all and punished the monk by law. Thereafter the violent and cunning kept out of sight, and honest people lived in peace. Transferred to magistrate of Gui'an on the Huzhou circuit, he carried out the secretariat's order to settle rents on abandoned fields, and the people were no longer cheated. Every prefecture and county Jingxing served benefited from his policies; and wherever he left office the people erected stones in his praise. He retired with the ranks of Hanlin Academician-in-Waiting and Grand Master for Court Audience and died at seventy-four.

林興祖，字宗起，福州羅源人。 至治二年，登進士第，授承事郎、同知黃岩州事，三遷而知鉛山州。 鉛山素多造偽鈔者，豪民吳友文為之魁，遠至江淮、燕薊，莫不行使。 友文姦黠悍鷙，因偽造致富，乃分遣惡少四五十人，為吏於有司，伺有欲告之者，輒先事戕之，前後殺人甚眾，奪人妻女十一人為妾，民罹其害，銜冤不敢訴者十餘年。 興祖至官，曰：「此害不除，何以牧民！ 」即張榜禁偽造者，且立賞募民首告。 俄有告者至，佯以不實斥去； 又有告獲偽造二人並贓者，乃鞫之，款成。 友文自至官，為之營救，興祖命並執之。 須臾，來訴友文者百餘人，擇其重罪一二事鞫之，獄立具，逮捕其黨二百餘人，悉置之法。 民害既去，政聲籍甚。 江浙行省丞相別兒怯不花薦諸朝，升南陽知府，改建德路同知，俱未任。 至正八年，特旨遷為道州路總管，行至城外，撞賊已迫其後，相去僅二十里。 時湖南副使哈剌帖木兒屯兵城外，聞賊至，以乏軍需，欲退兵，興祖聞，即夜詣說留之。 哈剌帖木兒曰：「明日得鈔五千錠、桐盾五百，乃可破賊。 」興祖許之。 明日甫入城視事，即以恩信勸諭鹽商，貸鈔五千錠，且取郡樓舊桐板為盾，日中皆備。 哈剌帖木兒得鈔、盾，大喜，遂留，為御賊計。 賊聞新總管至，一日具五百盾，以為大軍且至，中夕遁去。 永明縣洞徭屢竊發為民害，興祖以手榜諭之。 皆曰：「林總管廉而愛民，不可犯也。 」三年不入境。 春旱，蟲食麥苗，興祖為文禱之，大雨三日，蟲死而麥稔。 已而罷興作，賑貧乏，輕徭薄斂，郡中大治，憲司考課，以道州為最。 以年老致仕，終於家。
Lin Xingzu, whose courtesy name was Zongqi, came from Luoyuan in Fuzhou. In 1322 he passed the jinshi examination, was appointed Gentleman for Managing Affairs and vice-prefect of Huangyan, and after three promotions became prefect of Qianshan. Qianshan had long been rife with counterfeiters of paper money; a powerful local named Wu Youwen led them, and their notes circulated as far as the Jiang-Huai region and the Yan-Ji capital area. Youwen was cunning, ruthless, and violent; counterfeiting had made him rich, and he placed forty or fifty thugs as clerks in government offices to spy on accusers and kill them first—he murdered many over the years, seized eleven women as concubines, and for more than a decade the people suffered in silence, too afraid to bring suit. When Xingzu took office he said, "If this scourge is not removed, how can I govern the people?" " He immediately posted notices banning counterfeiting and offered rewards for informants. Soon an informant came forward; Xingzu pretended to dismiss the charge as false and sent him away; then another report arrived that two counterfeiters had been caught with the evidence; he interrogated them until they confessed fully. Youwen came in person to lobby for their release; Xingzu ordered him arrested too. Within moments more than a hundred people came forward to accuse Youwen; Xingzu chose one or two of the gravest charges, secured a swift conviction, arrested more than two hundred accomplices, and punished them all by law. With the people's scourge gone, his reputation for governance spread far and wide. Chancellor Bayiqinbuqa of the Jiang-Zhe Branch Secretariat recommended him to court; he was promoted to prefect of Nanyang and vice-intendant of Jiande Circuit but assumed neither post. In 1348 he was specially transferred by imperial order to circuit intendant of Daozhou; when he reached the outskirts, rebel forces were only twenty li behind him. Hunan Vice Commissioner Qaratemur had troops encamped outside the walls; hearing the rebels approach, he meant to withdraw for lack of supplies, but Xingzu went to him that night and persuaded him to remain. Qaratemur said, "If I have five thousand ingots of paper money and five hundred paulownia shields by tomorrow, I can defeat the rebels." " Xingzu promised to provide them. The next morning, as soon as he entered the city to take up his duties, he persuaded the salt merchants on trust to lend five thousand ingots and had old paulownia boards from the city tower fashioned into shields; by midday everything was ready. Qaratemur received the money and shields, was delighted, and stayed to plan the defense. The rebels heard a new intendant had arrived and, seeing five hundred shields prepared in a single day, assumed a large army was coming and fled at midnight. Yao tribesmen in the caves of Yongming County had repeatedly raided the people; Xingzu addressed them with a personal proclamation. They all said, "Intendant Lin is honest and cares for the people; he must not be provoked." " For three years they did not cross the border to raid. When spring drought brought insects that devoured the wheat shoots, Xingzu wrote a prayer; rain fell heavily for three days, the pests died, and the wheat ripened. He then halted wasteful projects, relieved the poor, lightened corvée and taxes, and brought the circuit to good order; the surveillance commission ranked Daozhou highest in its review. He retired on account of age and died at home.

觀音奴，字志能，唐兀人氏，居新州。 登泰定四年進士第。 由戶部主事，再轉而知歸德府。 廉明剛斷，發擿如神。 民有銜冤不直者，雖數十年前事，皆千里奔走來訴，觀音奴立為剖決，旬日悉清。 彰德富商任甲，抵睢陽，驢斃，令郄乙剖之，任以怒毆郄，經宿而死。 郄有妻王氏、妾孫氏，孫訴於官，官吏納任賄，謂郄非傷死，反抵孫罪，置之獄。 王來訴冤，觀音奴立破械出孫於獄，呼府胥語之曰：「吾為文具香幣，若為吾以郄事禱諸城隍神，令神顯於吾。 」有睢陽小吏，亦預郄事，畏觀音奴嚴明，且懼神顯其事，乃以任所賂鈔陳首曰：「郄實傷死，任賂上下匿其實，吾亦得賂，敢以首。 」於是罪任商而釋孫妾。 寧陵豪民楊甲，夙嗜王乙田三頃，不能得。 值王以飢攜其妻就食淮南，而王得疾死，其妻還，則田為楊據矣。 王妻訴之官，楊行賄，偽作文憑，曰：「王在時已售我。 」觀音奴令王妻挽楊，同就崔府君神祠質之。 楊懼神之靈，先期以羊酒浼巫囑神勿洩其事，及王與楊詣祠質之，果無所顯明。 觀音奴疑之，召巫詰問，巫吐其實曰：「楊以羊酒浼我囑神曰：'我實據王田，幸神勿洩也。 '」觀音奴因訊得其實，坐楊罪，歸其田王氏，責神而撤其祠。 亳州有蝗食民禾，觀音奴以事至亳，民以蝗訴，立取蝗向天祝之，以水研碎而飲，是歲蝗不為災。 後升為都水監官。
Guanyinnu, whose courtesy name was Zhineng, was of Tangut descent and lived in Xin Prefecture. He passed the jinshi examination in 1327. Starting as a clerk in the Ministry of Revenue, he was twice transferred until he became prefect of Guide. Incorrupt, discerning, and resolute, he uncovered hidden crimes as if by divine insight. If anyone nursed an old injustice, even from decades past, they would travel a thousand li to bring their case to him; Guanyinnu would hear them at once, and within ten days every backlog was cleared. A rich Zhangde merchant named Ren Jia reached Suiyang; when his donkey died he ordered Qie Yi to butcher it, and in a rage Ren beat Qie to death overnight. Qie left a wife, Lady Wang, and a concubine, Lady Sun; Sun brought suit, but officials took Ren's bribes, ruled the death was not from beating, and imprisoned Sun on a false charge. Lady Wang came to plead her case; Guanyinnu at once freed Sun from prison, summoned a prefectural clerk, and said, "Prepare incense and offerings—I want you to pray to the City God about Qie's death and bring the truth to light." " A minor Suiyang clerk involved in the case, fearing Guanyinnu's rigor and divine exposure, confessed with the bribe notes in hand: "Qie died of the beating; Ren bought off officials to hide it, and I took bribes too—I confess." " Ren was punished and Lady Sun was released. A Ningling magnate named Yang Jia had long coveted three qing of Wang Yi's fields but could never get them. When famine struck, Wang took his wife to Huainan for food; Wang fell ill and died there, and by the time his wife returned, Yang had seized the fields. Wang's wife brought suit, but Yang bribed officials and forged a deed claiming, "Wang sold the land to me while he was still alive. " Guanyinnu had Wang's wife take Yang by the arm and go with him to the shrine of Lord Cui to settle the matter before the god. Fearing divine retribution, Yang had already bribed the shaman with sheep and wine to keep the god silent; when Wang and Yang went to the shrine, nothing was revealed. Guanyinnu grew suspicious, summoned the shaman, and the shaman confessed: "Yang bribed me with sheep and wine and told the god, 'I really seized Wang's land—please don't expose me.' '" Guanyinnu got to the bottom of it, punished Yang, restored the fields to Lady Wang, and demolished the shrine in rebuke of the god. When locusts devoured the crops at Bozhou, Guanyinnu happened to be there on business; the people appealed to him, and he at once took locusts, prayed heavenward, ground them in water, and drank the mixture—and that year the plague passed without harm. He was later promoted to Director of Waterworks.

周自強，字剛善，臨江路新喻州人。 好學能文，練於吏事，以文法推擇為吏。 泰定間，廣西洞徭反，自強往見徭酋，說以禍福，中其要害，徭酋立為罷兵，貢方物，納款請命。 事聞於朝，特旨超授廣西兩江道宣慰司都事。 轉饒州路經歷，遷婺州路義烏縣尹。 周知民情，而性度寬厚，不為刻深。 民有以爭訟訴於庭者，一見即能知其曲直，然未遽加以刑責，必取經典中語，反覆開譬之，令其誦讀講解。 若能悔悟首實，則原其罪； 若迷謬怙惡不悛，然後繩之以法不少貸。 民畏且愛，獄訟頓息。 民間田稅之籍多失實，以故差徭不平，自強出令履畝核之，民不能欺，文簿井井可考，於是賦役平均，貧富樂業。 其聽訟決獄，物無遁情，黠吏欲以片言欺惑之不可得。 由是政治大行，聲譽籍甚。 部使者數以廉能舉於朝，選授撫州路金溪縣尹，階奉議大夫，政績愈著。 以亞中大夫、江州路總管致仕。
Zhou Ziqiang, styled Gangshan, was from Xinyu Prefecture in Linjiang Circuit. He loved learning, wrote well, and was skilled in administrative work; selected by statute, he entered government service as a clerk. During the Taiding reign, the cave Yao of Guangxi rose in revolt; Ziqiang went to their chieftain, argued the costs of war and peace, and hit the mark—the chieftain at once laid down arms, sent tribute, and sued for mercy. When word reached court, an imperial directive bypassed the usual ranks and made him Chief Secretary of the Guangxi Liangjiang Pacification Commission. He served as Supervisor of Raozhou Circuit, then was appointed magistrate of Yiwu County in Wuzhou Circuit. He knew the people well and was by nature generous rather than harsh. When litigants came before him, he could tell right from wrong at a glance, yet he never rushed to punish; instead he drew passages from the classics, explained them again and again, and made the parties recite and discuss them. If they repented and confessed, he pardoned them; if they persisted in wrongdoing, only then did he apply the law without mercy. The people feared and loved him in equal measure, and lawsuits dwindled away. Land-tax registers in the countryside were often wrong, skewing corvée assignments; Ziqiang ordered a field-by-field survey, and with no room to cheat, the records became reliable—taxes and labor duties were fair, and rich and poor alike could live in peace. In court he missed nothing, and no clever clerk could mislead him with a word. His administration flourished, and his fame spread far and wide. Inspectors repeatedly recommended him to court for integrity and competence; he was appointed magistrate of Jinxi County in Fuzhou Circuit with the rank of Counselor-Director, and his record grew still brighter. He retired as intendant of Jiangzhou Circuit with the rank of Sub-Counselor of the Palace.
9
白景亮，字明甫，南陽人。 明法律，善書算。 由征東行省譯史有勞，超遷南恩知州，升沔陽府尹，奏最於朝，特授衢州路總管。 先是，為郡者於民間徭役，不盡校​​田畝以為則，吏得併緣高下其手，富民或優有餘力，而貧弱不能勝者，多至破產失業。 景亮深知其弊，乃始核驗田畝以均之，役之輕重，一視田之多寡，大小家各使得宜，咸便安之，由是民不勞而事易集，他郡邑皆取以為法。 郡學之政久弛，從祀諸賢無塑像，諸生無廩膳，祭服樂器有缺，景亮皆為備之，儒風大振，縉紳稱頌焉。 景亮性廉介勤苦，自奉甚薄，妻尤儉約，惟以脫粟對飯而已。 部使者嘗上其事，特詔褒美，賜以宮錦，改授台州路總管。 卒於官。
Bai Jingliang, styled Mingfu, was from Nanyang. He knew the law and was adept at writing and arithmetic. For distinguished service as a translator-clerk in the Eastern Expedition Branch Secretariat, he was promoted to prefect of Nan'en, then to prefect of Mianyang; after reporting the year's best results at court, he was specially appointed intendant of Quzhou Circuit. Before his time, prefectural corvée was not tied rigorously to acreage; clerks manipulated the rolls at will, and while the wealthy often had labor to spare, the poor were driven to ruin. Jingliang saw the abuse clearly and began verifying acreage so corvée matched landholding; large and small households alike found the burden fair, work got done without crushing the people, and other jurisdictions copied his method. The prefectural school had long been neglected—honored sages had no statues, students no stipends, ritual robes and instruments were missing; Jingliang supplied them all, Confucian learning revived, and the gentry sang his praises. Jingliang was incorruptible and frugal, living on very little; his wife was even more sparing—they ate nothing but unpolished grain with their rice. When an inspector reported this to court, an imperial edict commended him, granted palace brocade, and transferred him to intendant of Taizhou Circuit. He died in office.
10
王艮，字止善，紹興諸暨人。 尚氣節，讀書務明理以致用，不苟事言說。 淮東廉訪司辟為書吏，遷淮西。 會例革南士，就為吏於兩淮都轉運鹽使司，以歲月及格，授廬州錄事判官。 淮東宣慰司辟為令史，以廉能稱。 再調峽州總管府知事，又辟江浙行省掾史。 會朝廷復立諸市舶司，艮從省官至泉州，建言：「若買舊有之船以付舶商，則費省而工易集，且可絕官吏侵欺掊克之弊。 」中書省報如艮言。 凡為船六宗，省官錢五十餘萬緡。
Wang Gen, styled Zhishan, was from Zhuji in Shaoxing. He prized integrity, read to grasp principle and put it to use, and disdained idle talk. The Huaidong Surveillance Commission recruited him as a clerk, and he was later transferred to Huaixi. When a regulation barred southern degree-holders, he became a clerk in the Lianghuai Salt Transport Commission; after his years of service qualified, he was appointed assistant magistrate of Luzhou. The Huaidong Pacification Commission recruited him as chief clerk, and he won a reputation for integrity and competence. He was transferred to clerk of the Xiangzhou intendant's office, then recruited as a clerk of the Jiangzhe Branch Secretariat. When the court restored the maritime trade offices, Gen accompanied the provincial officials to Quanzhou and proposed: "If the government buys existing vessels and assigns them to merchant fleets, costs fall, work proceeds quickly, and officials can no longer skim and squeeze. " The Central Secretariat approved Gen's proposal. Six large ships were built in all, saving more than five hundred thousand strings of official funds.
11
歷建德縣尹，除兩浙都轉運鹽使司經歷。 紹興路總管王克敬，以計口食鹽不便，嘗言於，行省，未報，而克敬為轉運使，集議欲稍損其額，以紓民力。 沮之者以為有成籍不可改，艮毅然曰：「民實寡而強賦多民之錢，今死、徙已眾矣，顧重改成籍而輕棄民命乎！ 且浙右之郡，商賈輻輳，未嘗以口計也。 移其所賦，散於商旅之所聚，實為良法。 」於是議歲減紹興食鹽五千六百引。 尋有復排前議者，艮欲辭職去，丞相聞之，亟遣留艮，而議遂定。
He served as magistrate of Jiande County, then was appointed supervisor of the Liangzhe Salt Transport Commission. Wang Kejing, intendant of Shaoxing Circuit, had found the per-capita salt levy burdensome and once petitioned the Branch Secretariat without reply; when he became salt commissioner, he convened a meeting to trim the quota and ease the people's load. Opponents argued the established register could not be changed; Gen declared firmly: "The people are dwindling while we force heavy levies on them—many have already died or fled! Would we rather preserve the register than save their lives! Besides, the prefectures of western Zhejiang teem with merchants and were never meant to be taxed by head count. Shifting the levy to where travelers and merchants congregate is truly the better policy. " The assembly agreed to cut Shaoxing's annual salt quota by five thousand six hundred yin. When opponents later revived the old plan, Gen threatened to resign; the chancellor heard and urgently ordered him to stay, and the reform held.
12
遷海道漕運都萬戶府經歷。 紹興之官糧入海運者十萬石，城距海十八里，歲令有司拘民船以備短送，吏胥得併緣以虐民。 及至海次，主運者又不即受，有折缺之患。 艮執言曰：「運戶既有官賦之直，何復為是紛紛也！ 」乃責運戶自載糧入運船。 運船為風所敗者，當核實除其數，移文往返，連數歲不絕，艮取吏牘披閱，即除其糧五萬二千八百石、鈔二百五十萬緡，運戶乃免於破家。
He was transferred to supervisor of the Maritime Grain Transport Office. Shaoxing shipped one hundred thousand shi of tribute grain by sea; the city stood eighteen li inland, and each year officials requisitioned civilian boats for the short haul—giving clerks room to torment the people. At the coast the transport officers delayed acceptance, inviting shortages and losses. Gen protested: "Transport households already receive official payment—why all this needless trouble! " He ordered transport households to load their own grain directly onto the transport ships. Grain lost when ships were wrecked in storms should be verified and written off, yet documents shuttled back and forth for years; Gen reviewed the files and at once cancelled fifty-two thousand eight hundred shi of grain and two million five hundred thousand strings of notes, saving transport households from ruin.
13
遷江浙行省檢校官。 有詣中書訴松江富民包隱田土，為糧一百七十餘萬石； 沙盪，為鈔五百餘萬緡； 宜立官府糾察收追之。 中書移行省議，遣官驗視，而鬆江獨當十九。 艮至松江，條陳曲折，以破其誑妄，言其「不過欲竦朝廷之聽而報宿怨，且冀創立衙門，為徼名爵計耳。 萬一民心動搖，患生不測，豈國家培養根本之策哉！ 」艮言上，事遂寢。
He was transferred to inspecting secretary of the Jiangzhe Branch Secretariat. Someone petitioned the Central Secretariat claiming Songjiang's wealthy had concealed land worth more than 1.7 million shi of grain tax; and sand flats worth more than five million strings of notes; and urged that new offices be created to investigate and recover the revenue. The Central Secretariat referred the matter to the Branch Secretariat and sent officials to inspect; Songjiang alone would bear nine-tenths of the burden. Gen went to Songjiang, laid out the case in detail, and exposed the fraud, declaring: "This is nothing but an attempt to alarm the court, settle old scores, and win new offices for the sake of fame. If popular sentiment is shaken and unforeseen trouble follows, how is that a policy for nurturing the state's foundation! " When his report reached the court, the proposal was shelved.
14
除江西行省左右司員外郎。 吉之安福有小吏，誣民欺隱詭寄田租​​九千餘石，初止八家，前後數十年，株連至千家，行省數遣官按問，吏已伏其虛誑，而有司喜功生事者，复勒其民報合徵糧六百餘石，憲司援詔條革去，終莫能止。 艮到官，首言：「是州之糧，比元經理已增一千一百餘石，豈復有欺隱詭寄者乎？ 準憲司所擬可也。 」行省用艮言，悉蠲之。 艮在任歲餘，以中憲大夫、淮東道宣慰副使致仕。 卒年七十一。
He was appointed assistant director in the Left and Right Offices of the Jiangxi Branch Secretariat. At Anfu in Ji, a petty clerk falsely accused locals of concealing more than nine thousand shi of land tax; what began with eight households spread over decades to a thousand; though the clerk confessed to fraud and the surveillance commission cited an edict to cancel the levy, ambitious officials kept pressing for six hundred more shi, and nothing could stop them. On taking office Gen said at once: "This prefecture's grain quota already exceeds the Yuan baseline by more than eleven hundred shi—how could concealment still be possible? The surveillance commission's proposal should stand. " The Branch Secretariat followed Gen's advice and remitted the entire levy. After little more than a year in office, he retired as vice commissioner of the Huaidong Pacification Commission with the rank of Regular Counselor. He died at seventy-one.
15
盧琦，字希韓，惠安人，登至正二年進士第。 十二年，稍遷至永春縣尹。 始至，賑饑饉，止橫斂，均賦役，減口鹽一百餘引，蠲包銀榷鐵之無徵者。 已而訟息民安，乃新學宮，延師儒課子弟，月書季考，文風翕然。 鄰邑仙遊盜發，琦適在邑境，盜遙見之，迎拜曰：「此永春大夫也。 為大夫百姓者，何幸之大乎！ 吾邑長乃以暴毒驅我，故至此耳。 」琦因立馬喻以禍福，眾皆投刃槊，請縛其酋以自新，琦許之。 酋至，琦械送帥府，自是威惠行於境外。 十三年，泉郡大饑，死者相枕籍。 其能行者，皆老幼扶攜，就食永春。 琦命分諸浮屠及大家使食之，所存活不可勝計。 十四年，安溪寇數万人來襲永春。 琦聞，召邑民喻之曰：「汝等能戰則與之戰，不能，則我當獨死之爾。 」眾皆感憤，曰：「使君何言也！ 使君父母，我民赤子，其忍以父母畀賊邪！ 且彼寇方將虜掠我妻子，焚毀我室廬，乃一邑深仇也。 今日之事，有進無退，使君其勿以為憂。 」因踴躍爭奮。 琦率以攻賊，大破之。 明日，賊复傾巢而至，又破之。 大小三十餘戰，斬獲一千二百餘人，而邑民無死傷者。 賊大衄，遂遁去。 時兵革四起，列郡皆洶洶不寧，獨永春晏然，無異承平時。 十六年，改調寧德縣尹而去。
Lu Qi, styled Xihan, was from Huian and passed the jinshi examination in the second year of Zhizheng. By the twelfth year he had risen to magistrate of Yongchun County. On arrival he relieved famine, halted illegal levies, equalized taxes and corvée, cut the per-capita salt quota by more than one hundred yin, and cancelled uncollected package-silver and iron monopoly dues. Soon lawsuits faded and the people settled; he rebuilt the school, hired teachers for the youth, held monthly essays and quarterly exams, and learning flourished. When bandits broke out in neighboring Xianyou, Qi happened to be on the border; seeing him from afar, they came forward and bowed, saying, "This is the magistrate of Yongchun. What fortune to be governed by such a magistrate! Our own magistrate drove us out with cruelty—that is why we are here. " Qi reined in his horse and reasoned with them about the consequences; they threw down their weapons, asked to bind their leader and submit, and Qi agreed. When the chieftain was brought in, Qi sent him in chains to the regional command; from then on his authority and benevolence reached beyond the county line. In the thirteenth year a great famine struck Quan Prefecture, and the dead lay stacked upon one another. Those who could still walk—old and young helping one another—streamed into Yongchun for food. Qi arranged for monasteries and wealthy households to feed them, saving countless lives. In the fourteenth year tens of thousands of bandits from Anxi attacked Yongchun. When he heard, Qi summoned the people and said: "If you can fight, fight; if not, I alone will die here. " The crowd was stirred and cried: "My lord, how can you say such a thing! You are our parent and we your children—would we hand our parent over to bandits! Besides, they come to seize our wives and children and burn our homes—this is a blood feud for the whole county. Today we advance, not retreat—my lord, do not worry. " They surged forward, eager to fight. Qi led them against the bandits and won a great victory. The next day the bandits returned in full force, and he defeated them again. In more than thirty engagements they killed or captured over twelve hundred enemies, yet not one townsman was killed or wounded. Badly beaten, the bandits fled. While war erupted everywhere and neighboring prefectures trembled, Yongchun alone remained calm, as peaceful as in better days. In 1356 he was reassigned as magistrate of Ningde County and departed.

鄒伯顏，字從吉，高唐人。 為建寧崇安縣尹。 崇安之為邑，區別其土田，名之曰都者五十，五十都之田上送官者，為糧六千石。 其大家以五十餘家，而兼五千石； 細民以四百餘家，而合一千石。 大家之田，連跨數都，而細民之糧，或僅升合。 有司常以四百之細民，配五十大家之役，故貧者受役旬日，而家已破。 伯顏曰：「貧弱之受困，一至此乎！ 」乃取其糧籍而分計從，有糧一石者，受一石之役，有糧升斗者，受升斗之役。 田多者受數都之役而不可辭，田少者稱其所出而無倖免。 貧因無告之民，始得以休息。 崇安賦役之均，遂為四方最。 邑有宋趙抃所鑿溝，溉民田數千畝。 歲久，溝湮而田廢。 伯顏修長溝十里，繞楓樹陂，累石以為固，溝悉复抃遺跡，而田為常稔，民賴其利。 安慶路嘗得造偽鈔者，遣卒械其囚至崇安，求其黨而執之，囚與卒結謀，望風入良民家肆虐。 伯顏捕訊得其狀，即執而歸諸安慶，自是偽造之連逮無濫及崇安者。 於是行省帥府、御史憲府咸舉其能。 選調漳州路判官。
Zou Boyan, whose courtesy name was Congji, came from Gaotang. He served as magistrate of Chong'an County in Jianning. Chong'an divided its land into fifty districts called du, whose combined tax yield to the government was six thousand shi of grain. More than fifty wealthy households shouldered five thousand shi among them; while more than four hundred poor households together owed only one thousand shi. The rich held fields spanning several districts, while some poor households owed only a few pints of grain. Officials routinely assigned the four hundred poor households to serve the corvée obligations of the fifty rich ones, so a peasant's family could be ruined within ten days of forced labor. Boyan exclaimed, "Must the poor and weak be driven to such straits!" " He took the tax registers and apportioned corvée by actual yield: one shi of grain meant one shi of labor, a few pints meant a few pints of labor. Large landowners bore corvée across several districts and could not refuse; smallholders paid in proportion to their output and could not evade. The poorest people, who had nowhere to turn, were finally able to breathe. Chong'an's fair apportionment of taxes and corvée became the best in the realm. The county had an irrigation ditch dug in Song times by Zhao Bian, watering several thousand mu of farmland. Over the years the ditch silted up and the fields lay fallow. Boyan rebuilt ten li of channel around Maple Tree Embankment, reinforced it with stone, fully restored Zhao Bian's work, and brought the fields back to steady harvest—the people prospered from it. Anqing Circuit once caught counterfeiters and sent soldiers escorting a prisoner to Chong'an to round up accomplices; the prisoner and guards conspired to raid innocent households at will. Boyan investigated, arrested them, and sent them back to Anqing, so Chong'an was no longer swept up in the counterfeiting dragnet. The Branch Secretariat, the marshal's office, and the surveillance commission all commended his competence. He was selected for transfer as assistant prefect of Zhangzhou Circuit.
17
劉秉直，字清臣，大都武清人。 至正八年，來為衛輝路總管​​，平徭役，興教化，敦四民之業，崇五土之利，養鰥寡，恤孤獨。 賊劫汲縣民張聚鈔一千二百錠而殺之，賊不獲，秉直具詞致禱城隍祠，而使人伺於死所，忽有村民阿蓮者，戰怖僕地，具言賊之姓名及所在，乃命尉襲之，果得賊於汴，遂正其罪。 秋七月，蟲螟生，民患之，秉直禱於八蜡祠，蟲皆自死。 歲大饑，人相食，死者過半，秉直出俸米，倡富民分粟，餒者食之，病者與藥，死者與棺以葬。 天不雨，禾且槁，秉直詣城北太行之蒼峪神祠，具詞祈祝，有青蛇蜿蜒而出，觀者異之。 辭神而還，行及數里，雷雨大至。 秩滿，以親老，去官侍養。
Liu Bingzhi, whose courtesy name was Qingchen, came from Wuqing near the capital. In 1348 he became circuit intendant of Weihui, equalized corvée, promoted education, encouraged all trades and agriculture, cared for widows and orphans, and relieved the destitute. Bandits robbed and murdered Zhang Ju of Jixian for twelve hundred ingots of paper money and escaped; Bingzhi prayed at the City God shrine and posted men at the murder site; a villager named Alian, shaking with fear, collapsed and named the culprits; Bingzhi sent constables who captured them at Bian and brought them to justice. In the seventh month locusts and caterpillars plagued the crops; Bingzhi prayed at the shrine of the Eight La, and the pests died away. Famine struck so hard that people ate the dead and more than half perished; Bingzhi gave his salary grain, urged the wealthy to share their stores, fed the hungry, tended the sick, and buried the dead in coffins he provided. When drought withered the crops, Bingzhi prayed at the Cangyu shrine on Mount Taihang north of the city; a green serpent emerged, to the wonder of all who saw it. He left the shrine and had gone only a few li when a thunderstorm broke. When his term ended he resigned to care for his aged parents.

許義夫，碭山人。 為夏邑縣尹，每親詣鄉社，教民稼穡。 見民勤謹者，出己俸賞之，怠惰者罰之。 三年之間，境內豐足。 後為封丘縣尹，值至正四年大饑，盜賊群起，抄掠州縣。 義夫聞賊至近境，乃單馬出郊十里外迎之，見賊數百人，義夫力言：「封丘縣小民貧，皆已驚惶逃竄，幸無入吾境也。 」言辭願款，賊遂他往。 封丘之民，得免於難。
Xu Yifu came from Dangshan. As magistrate of Xiayi he personally visited village communities to teach farming. He rewarded the diligent from his own salary and punished the idle. Within three years the district was prosperous. Later, as magistrate of Fengqiu during the great famine of 1344, bandits rose everywhere and raided the prefectures. Hearing bandits were near, Yifu rode out alone ten li beyond the town to meet several hundred of them and pleaded, "Fengqiu is small and poor; the people have already fled in terror—please do not enter our district." " His words were so earnest that the bandits turned away. The people of Fengqiu were spared the disaster.
