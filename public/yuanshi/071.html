禮樂五○樂服樂正副四人，舒腳襆頭，紫羅公服，烏角帶，木笏，皂靴。 照燭二人，服同前，無笏。
Rites and Music 5 — Music Attire: The four chief and vice directors of music wore spread-foot turbans, purple gauze official robes, black horn belts, wooden court tablets, and black boots. Two lamp attendants wore the same dress as above but carried no court tablets.
2
樂師二人，服緋，冠、笏同前。 運譜二人，服綠，冠、笏同前。
Two music masters wore scarlet robes, with caps and tablets as described above. Two score carriers wore green robes, with caps and tablets as before.
3
舞師二人，舒腳襆頭，黃羅繡抹額，紫服，金銅荔枝帶，皂靴，各執仗。 〈 （仗，牙仗也。）〉
Two dance masters wore spread-foot turbans, yellow gauze embroidered forehead bands, purple robes, gold-and-bronze lychee-pattern belts, and black boots, each bearing a ceremonial staff. ( (The staff is an ivory ceremonial staff.)
4
執旌二人，平冕，前後各九旒五就，青生色鸞袍，黃綾帶，黃絹袴，白絹襪，赤革履。 〈 （平冕鸞袍，皆仿金制，惟冕之旒數不同，詳見後至元二年博士議。）〉
Two banner bearers wore level caps with nine tassels and five clusters fore and aft, blue phoenix-pattern robes in the raw dye, yellow gauze belts, yellow silk trousers, white silk socks, and red leather shoes. ( (Both the level cap and the phoenix-pattern robe followed Jin practice, though the number of tassels on the cap differed; see the Erudites' ruling of the second year of Zhiyuan, below.)
5
執纛二人，青羅巾，余同執旌。
Two standard bearers wore blue gauze caps; all else matched the banner bearers.
6
樂工，介幘冠，緋羅生色鸞袍，黃綾帶，皂靴。 〈 （冠以皮為之，黑油如熊耳，亦金制也。）〉 歌工，服同樂工。 執麾，服同上，惟加平巾幘。 〈 （狀若籠金幘，以革為之。）〉
Musicians wore leveled caps, scarlet gauze phoenix-pattern robes in the raw dye, yellow gauze belts, and black boots. ( (The cap was of leather, blackened like bear ears; this too followed Jin practice.) Singers wore the same dress as the musicians. Banner conductors dressed as above but also wore a flat-cloth cap. ( (Shaped like a gilded cage-cap and made of leather.)
7
舞人，青羅生色義花鸞袍，緣以皂綾，平冕冠。 〈 （冠前後有旒，青白硝石珠相間。）〉
Dancers wore blue gauze phoenix-pattern robes patterned with righteousness flowers in the raw dye, trimmed in black gauze, and level caps. ( (The cap bore tassels fore and aft, strung with alternating blue and white saltpeter beads.)
8
執器二十人，服同樂工，綠油母追冠， 〈 （革為之，一名武弁。）〉 加紅抹額。
Twenty instrument bearers dressed like the musicians and wore green-oiled leather military caps, ( (Made of leather and also known as a martial cap.) With red forehead bands added.
9
至元二年閏五月，大樂署言，堂上下樂舞官員及樂工，合用衣服冠冕靴履等物，乞行制造。 太常寺下博士議定：樂正副四人、樂師二人、照燭二人、運譜二人，皆服紫羅公服，皂紗襆頭舒腳，紅鞓角帶，木笏，皂靴。 引舞色長四人，紫羅公服，皂紗襆頭展腳，黃羅繡南花抹額，金銅帶，皂靴。 樂工二百四十有六人，緋繡義花鸞袍，縣黃插口，介幘冠，紫羅帶，全黃羅抹帶，黃絹夾褲，白綾襪，朱履。 〈 （金太常寺掌故張珍所著《疊代世範》載金制：舞人服黑衫，皆四襖，有黃插口，左右垂之，黃綾抹帶，其衫以綢為之，胸背二答、兩肩二答，前後和一答，皆彩色，繡二鸞盤飛之狀，綴之於衫。 冠以平冕，亦有天板、口圈，天門納言以紫絹標背，銅裹邊圈，前後各五旒，以青白硝石珠相間。 《大備集》所載，二舞人皂繡義花鸞衫，縣紫插口，黃綾抹帶，朱履，平冕。 其冠有口圈，亦有天門納言系帶，口圈高一尺許，天板長二尺，闊一尺，前微高後低，裏外紫絹糊，銅楞道妝釘，無旒。）〉 執器二十人，緋繡義花鸞袍，縣黃插口，綠油革冠，黃羅抹帶，黃絹夾褲，白綾襪，朱履。 旌纛四人，青繡義花鸞袍，縣紫插口，平冕冠二，青包巾二，黃羅抹帶，黃絹夾褲，白綾襪，朱履。 七月，中書吏部再準太常博士議定，行下所司制造。 三年九月服成，緋鸞袍二百六十有七，青鸞袍一百三十二，黃絹褲一百五十二，紫羅公服一十四，黃綾帶三百九十七，介幘冠二百四十有四，平冕冠百三十，簪全，木笏十有六，襆頭十有四，平巾幘二，綠油革冠二十，荔枝銅帶四，角帶十，皂靴二百六十對，朱履百五十對。
In the intercalary fifth month of the second year of Zhiyuan, the Office of Great Music reported that the music and dance officers in the upper and lower halls and all the musicians needed robes, caps, crowns, boots, and shoes, and asked that these be made. The Erudites of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices ruled that the four chief and vice directors, two music masters, two lamp attendants, and two score carriers should all wear purple gauze official robes, black gauze spread-foot turbans, red-trim horn belts, wooden court tablets, and black boots. Four lead-dance section chiefs wore purple gauze official robes, black gauze extended-foot turbans, yellow gauze forehead bands embroidered with southern flowers, gold-and-bronze belts, and black boots. Two hundred forty-six musicians wore scarlet embroidered phoenix-pattern robes with righteousness flowers, yellow hanging insertion panels, leveled caps, purple gauze belts, full yellow gauze forehead bands, yellow silk lined trousers, white gauze socks, and red shoes. ( (In Zhang Zhen's Successive Ages as Models, an archivist of the Jin Court of Imperial Sacrifices records Jin practice: dancers wore black four-layer jackets with yellow insertion panels hanging at either side, yellow gauze forehead bands, and silk coats bearing colored panels on chest, back, and shoulders—seven panels in all—each embroidered with paired phoenixes in flight and sewn to the garment.) They wore level crowns with heaven boards and mouth rings; the Gate of Heaven and Reception of Words were marked on purple silk backing, the rim copper-clad, with five tassels fore and aft strung with alternating blue and white saltpeter beads. The Comprehensive Rites Collection records two dancers in black embroidered phoenix jackets with righteousness flowers, purple hanging insertion panels, yellow gauze forehead bands, red shoes, and level crowns. Their crowns had mouth rings and straps for the Gate of Heaven and Reception of Words; the mouth ring stood about a foot high, the heaven board two feet long and one foot wide, slightly higher in front, lined with purple silk inside and out, ribbed and studded with copper, and bore no tassels.) Twenty instrument bearers wore scarlet embroidered phoenix-pattern robes with righteousness flowers, yellow hanging insertion panels, green-oiled leather caps, yellow gauze forehead bands, yellow silk lined trousers, white gauze socks, and red shoes. Four banner and standard bearers wore blue embroidered phoenix-pattern robes with righteousness flowers and purple hanging insertion panels; two wore level crowns and two blue wrapped caps, with yellow gauze forehead bands, yellow silk lined trousers, white gauze socks, and red shoes. In the seventh month the Secretariat and Ministry of Personnel again ratified the Erudites' ruling and directed the appropriate offices to manufacture the items. By the ninth month of the third year the garments were finished: 267 scarlet phoenix-pattern robes, 132 blue phoenix-pattern robes, 152 yellow silk trousers, 14 purple gauze official robes, 397 yellow gauze belts, 244 leveled caps, 130 level crowns with hairpins, 16 wooden court tablets, 14 turbans, 2 flat-cloth caps, 20 green-oiled leather caps, 4 lychee-pattern bronze belts, 10 horn belts, 260 pairs of black boots, and 150 pairs of red shoes.

宣聖廟樂工，黑漆冠三十五，綠羅生色胸背花袍三十五，皂靴三十五對，黃絹囊三十五，黃絹夾袱三十五。 大樂職掌
For musicians at the Temple of the Exalted Sage there were 35 black lacquered caps, 35 green gauze robes with floral chest-and-back panels in the raw dye, 35 pairs of black boots, 35 yellow silk pouches, and 35 yellow silk lined wrappers. Duties of Great Music
11
大樂署，令一人，丞一人，掌郊社、宗廟之樂。 凡樂，郊社、宗廟則用宮縣，工四百六十有一人； 社稷，則用登歌，工五十有一人； 二樂用工三百一十有二人，代事故者五十人。 前祭之月，召工習樂及舞。 祀前一日，宿縣於庭中。 東方西方設十二镈鐘，各依辰位。 編鐘處其左，編磬處其右。 黃鐘之鐘起子位，在通街之西。 蕤賓之鐘居午位，在通街之東。 每辰三虡，謂之一肆，十有二辰，凡三十六虡。 樹建鞞應於四隅，左右，設縣中之北。 歌工次之， 〈 （三十二人，重行相向而坐。）〉 巢笙次之，簫次之，竽次之，籥次之，篪次之，塤次之，長笛又次之。 夾街之左右，瑟翼敔之東西，在前行。 路鼓、路鼗次之。 〈 （郊祀則雷鼓、雷鼗。）〉 閏余匏在簫之東，七星匏在西，九曜匏次之。 一弦琴列路鼓之東西， 〈 （東一，西二。）〉 三弦、五弦、七弦、九弦次之。 晉鼓一，處縣中之東南，以節樂。 〈 （一弦琴三，五弦以下皆六。 凡坐者，高以杌，地以氈。）〉 立四表於橫街之南，少東。 設舞位於縣北。 文郎左執籥，右秉翟； 武郎左執幹，右執戚； 皆六十有四人。 享日，與工人先入就位。 舞師二人，執纛二人，引文舞分立於表南。 武舞及執器者，俟立於宮縣之左右。 器鼗二，雙鐸二，單鐸二，鐃二，錞二， 〈 （二錞用六人。）〉 鉦二，相鼓二，雅鼓二，凡二十人。 文舞退，舞師二人、執旌二人，引武舞進，立其處，文舞還立於縣側。 又設登歌樂於殿之前楹， 〈 （殿陛之旁，設樂床二，樂工列於上。）〉 搏拊二，歌工六，一，一，在門內，相向而坐。 鐘一虡，在前楹之東。 一弦、三弦、五弦、七弦、九弦琴五，次之。 瑟二，在其東，笛一、籥一、篪一在琴之南，巢笙、和笙各二次之。 塤一，在笛之南。 閏余匏、排簫各一，次之，皆西上。 磬一虡，在前楹之西。 一弦、三弦、五弦、七弦、九弦琴五，次之。 塤一，在笛之南。 七星匏、九曜匏、排簫各一，次之，皆東上。 凡宗廟之樂九成，舞九變。 黃鐘之宮，三成，三變。 大呂之角，二成，二變。 太簇之徵，二成，二變。 應鐘之羽，二成，二變。 圜丘之樂六成，舞六變。 夾鐘之宮，三成，三變。 黃鐘之角，一成，一變。 太簇之徵，一成，一變。 姑洗之羽，一成，一變。 社稷之樂八成：林鐘之宮二成，太簇之角二成，姑洗之征二成，南呂之羽二成。 凡有事於宗廟，大樂令位於殿楹之東，西向； 丞位於縣北，通街之東，西向； 以肅樂舞。
The Office of Great Music had one director and one deputy, charged with music for suburban sacrifices and the ancestral temple. For suburban altars and the ancestral temple the palace suspended ensemble was used, with 461 musicians; for the altars of soil and grain, elevated singing, with 51 musicians; both forms together required 312 musicians, with 50 substitutes to cover vacancies. In the month before a sacrifice, musicians were summoned to rehearse the music and dances. On the eve of the rite the suspended ensemble was set up and kept overnight in the courtyard. Twelve bo bells were placed east and west, each aligned to its earthly-branch station. Serial bells stood to their left and serial chimes to their right. The Yellow Bell bell began at the zi station, west of the main thoroughfare. The Flourishing Guest bell stood at the wu station, east of the main thoroughfare. Each earthly-branch station had three stands, forming one array; across twelve stations there were thirty-six stands in all. Side drums and answering drums were set at the four corners; to left and right they stood north of the center of the suspended ensemble. Next came the singers, ( (Thirty-two singers seated in double rows facing one another.) Then came nest panpipes, vertical flutes, mouth organs, transverse flutes, bamboo flutes, globular flutes, and finally long flutes. Flanking the thoroughfare, zithers stood east and west of the clappers in the front rank. Road drums and road tambourines came next. ( (At suburban sacrifices, thunder drums and thunder tambourines were used.) The Intercalary Remainder gourd stood east of the vertical flute, the Seven Stars gourd to the west, then the Nine Luminaries gourd. One-string zithers were placed east and west of the road drums, ( (one on the east, two on the west.) Followed by three-, five-, seven-, and nine-string zithers. A single Jin drum stood southeast of the center of the ensemble to mark the beat. ( (Three one-string zithers; from the five-string type downward, six of each. Those who sat used stools for elevation and felt mats on the ground.) Four markers were set up south of the cross-street, slightly to the east. Dance positions were laid out north of the suspended ensemble. Civil dancers held flutes in the left hand and pheasant feathers in the right; Martial dancers held shields in the left hand and battle-axes in the right; sixty-four in all. On the day of the offering they entered ahead of the musicians and took their places. Two dance masters and two standard bearers led the civil dancers to stand in formation south of the markers. The martial dancers and instrument bearers waited on either side of the palace suspended ensemble. Two instrument tambourines, two paired bells, two single bells, two cymbals, and two gongs, ( (The two gongs required six players.) Two chimes, two phase drums, two elegant drums—twenty players in all. When the civil dance withdrew, two dance masters and two banner bearers led the martial dancers forward to take their place, while the civil dancers returned to stand beside the suspended ensemble. Elevated singing was also arranged before the front columns of the hall, ( (beside the hall steps, with two music platforms and musicians arrayed upon them.) With two clappers and six singers—three on each side inside the gate, seated facing one another. One bell stand stood east of the front columns. Followed by one-, three-, five-, seven-, and nine-string zithers, five in all. Two zithers stood to their east; a flute, transverse flute, and bamboo flute south of the zithers; then two nest panpipes and two harmony panpipes. A globular flute stood south of the flute. Then came one Intercalary Remainder gourd and one panpipe organ, all ranked ascending toward the west. One chime stand stood west of the front columns. Followed by one-, three-, five-, seven-, and nine-string zithers, five in all. A globular flute stood south of the flute. Then came one Seven Stars gourd, one Nine Luminaries gourd, and one panpipe organ, all ranked ascending toward the east. Ancestral temple music comprised nine movements, with nine dance transformations. The Yellow Bell mode: three movements and three dance transformations. The Great Regulator angle: two movements and two transformations. The Great Cluster mode: two movements and two transformations. The Responding Bell feather: two movements and two transformations. Music at the Round Mound comprised six movements and six dance transformations. The Bell Attached mode: three movements and three transformations. The Yellow Bell angle: one movement and one transformation. The Great Cluster mode: one movement and one transformation. The Maiden Wash feather: one movement and one transformation. Altars-of-soil-and-grain music had eight movements: two in Forest Bell mode, two in Great Cluster angle, two in Maiden Wash mode, and two in Southern Regulator feather. Whenever rites were performed at the ancestral temple, the Director of Great Music stood east of the hall columns, facing west; the deputy stood north of the suspended ensemble, east of the main thoroughfare, facing west; to keep order over the music and dance.
12
協律郎二人，掌和律呂，以合陰陽之聲。 陽律六：黃鐘子，太簇寅，姑洗辰，蕤賓午，夷則申，無射戌。 陰呂六：大呂醜，夾鐘卯，仲呂巳，林鐘未，南呂酉，應鐘亥。 文之以宮、商、角、徵、羽、變宮、變徵，播之以金、石、絲、竹、匏、土、革、木。 凡律管之數九，九九相乘，八十一以為宮； 三分去一，五十四以為徵； 三分益一，七十二以為商； 三分去一，四十八以為羽； 三分益一，六十四以為角。 如黃鐘為宮，則林鐘為徵，太簇為商，南呂為羽，姑洗為角，應鐘為變宮，蕤賓為變征，是為七聲十二律，還相為宮，為八十四調。 凡大祭祀皆法服，一人立於殿楹之西，東向； 一人立於縣北通街之西，東向； 以節樂。 〈 （堂上者主登歌，堂下者主宮縣。）〉 凡樂作，則跪，俯伏，舉麾以興，工鼓以奏； 樂止則偃麾，工戛而樂止。 〈 （今執麾者代執之，協律郎特拜而已。）〉
Two Pitch-Regulation Masters harmonized the pitch pipes so yin and yang tones might combine. The six yang pipes: Yellow Bell at zi, Great Cluster at yin, Maiden Wash at chen, Flourishing Guest at wu, Leveling Rule at shen, and Unending Shot at xu. The six yin tubes: Great Regulator at chou, Bell Attached at mao, Second Bell at si, Forest Bell at wei, Southern Regulator at you, and Responding Bell at hai. They were patterned with gong, shang, jue, zhi, yu, altered gong, and altered zhi, and sounded through metal, stone, silk, bamboo, gourd, earth, leather, and wood. Pitch-pipe numbers totaled nine; nine times nine yielded eighty-one for gong; one-third removed left fifty-four for zhi; one-third added yielded seventy-two for shang; one-third removed left forty-eight for yu; one-third added yielded sixty-four for jue. With Yellow Bell as gong, Forest Bell was zhi, Great Cluster shang, Southern Regulator yu, Maiden Wash jue, Responding Bell altered gong, and Flourishing Guest altered zhi—seven tones across twelve pipes, each cycling as gong to form eighty-four modes. At great sacrifices all wore ritual dress; one man stood west of the hall columns, facing east; another stood west of the main thoroughfare north of the suspended ensemble, facing east; to mark the beat. ( (Those in the upper hall directed elevated singing; those below directed the palace suspended ensemble.) When music began they knelt and prostrated themselves, raised the banner to start, and the musicians drummed to play; when it stopped they lowered the banner and the musicians struck to halt the music. ( (Today the banner conductor holds it in their stead; the Pitch-Regulation Master performs only the special bow.)
13
樂正二人，副二人，掌肄樂舞、展樂器、正樂位。 凡祭，二人立於殿內，二人立於縣間，以節樂。 殿內者視獻者奠獻用樂作止之節，以笏示照燭，照燭舉偃以示堂下。 若作登歌，則以笏示敔而已。 縣間者視堂上照燭。 及引初獻，照燭動，亦以笏示敔。
Two chief directors and two deputies rehearsed music and dance, set out instruments, and corrected positions. At every sacrifice two stood inside the hall and two between the suspended sets to mark the music. Those inside watched the offering officer's presentation and used their tablets to signal the lamp attendants, who raised or lowered their lamps to show those below. For elevated singing they signaled only the clapper player. Those between the sets watched the hall lamp attendants. When leading the first offering, as the lamps moved they also signaled the clapper player.
14
樂師一人，運譜一人，掌以樂教工人。 凡祭，立於縣間，皆北上，相向而立。
One music master and one score carrier taught the musicians. At every sacrifice they stood between the sets, all facing north, opposite one another.
15
舞師四人，皆執梃， 〈 （梃，牙仗也。）〉 執纛二人，執旌二人，祭則前舉以為舞容。 舞人從南表向第一表，為一成，則一變； 從第二至第三，為二成； 從第三至北第四表，為三成； 舞人各轉身南向於北表之北，還從第一至第二，為四成； 從第二至第三，為五成； 從第三至南第一表，為六成； 若八變者，更從南北向第二，為七成； 又從第二至第三，為八成； 若九變者，又從第三至北第一，為九變。
Four dance masters, each holding a staff, ( (The staff is an ivory ceremonial staff.) Two standard bearers and two banner bearers; at the sacrifice they advanced ahead to set the dance formation. From the southern marker to the first marker counted as one movement and one transformation; from the second to the third, two movements; from the third to the northern fourth marker, three movements; each dancer turned south beyond the northern marker, then returned from the first to the second for four movements; from the second to the third, five movements; from the third to the southern first marker, six movements; for eight transformations they went again from south to north to the second marker, seven movements; again from the second to the third, eight movements; for nine transformations, again from the third to the northern first marker, nine transformations.
16
執麾一人，從協律郎以麾舉偃而節樂。
One banner conductor followed the Pitch-Regulation Master, raising and lowering the banner to mark the music.

The Prosperity-Flourishing panpipes were of nanmu, shaped like a folding screen, sharp above and flat-faced, gilded with loquat, treasure-flower, peacock, bamboo, and cloud motifs; side panels stood on either flank, one-third of the back, with a hollow cabinet at center like a panpipe gourd. Ninety purple bamboo pipes stood on it, capped with lotus-bud wood. Outside the cabinet fifteen small pegs bore small pipes capped with bronze apricot leaves. Below, a stand ringed with lions and elephants; before it a carved panel; between panels two leather wind mouths; in use a vermilion frame stood before the stand with wind bags at the mouths, faces like pipa in vermilion floral lacquer with handles—one plucked small pipes, one pumped the bag, and reeds sounded to the tuning. During the Zhongtong period it was presented from the Muslim lands. Its reeds were bamboo; it sounded but had no standard pitch. Zheng Xiu of the Jade Hall Music Academy examined pitch, sorted clear and muddy tones, and revised it to the present form. In the hall, flanking the shield-heads, two carved wooden peacocks adorned with real feathers held a mechanism within. At each performance three workers—one pumped the wind bag, one held pitch, one worked the mechanism—and the peacocks danced in time.

The fifth troupe: five men in Three Dragons caps, red robes, each with a gold-inlaid correction axe, advanced like the preceding troupe and stood facing north. The sixth troupe: five boys with triple topknots in plain clothes, incense flowers in hand, danced forward; music stopped. The seventh troupe: eight musicians in bound-hair caps and brocade white robes; three dragon flutes, three suona, two zhang drums, joining the Great Music in "New Water Order" and "Water Immortal." The eighth troupe: twenty women in cage caps, purple robes, gold belts, tablets in hand, sang "New Water Order" with the music, advanced to the imperial presence in four rows facing north, bowed, rose, danced, kowtowed, shouted "ten thousand years," bowed again; then sang "Water Immortal" and "Green Mountain Pass" with the following troupe. The ninth troupe: twenty women in carriage-topknot caps, gold-brocade blue dress, cloud collars, sash pendants, peacock banners, dancing and singing with the preceding troupe. The tenth troupe: eight women in kingfisher floral Tang caps, brocade dress, treasure canopies, dancing and singing the preceding tune. Next eight men in phoenix-wing helmets, golden armor, golden halberds. Next one man in a Level Heaven cap and embroidered crane mantle, scepter in hand; all danced one stanza; music stopped. Next three women sang "New Water Order," "Buy Fine Wine," and "Peace Order"; after the slogans they danced and sang in harmony and withdrew in order.

說法隊：引隊禮官樂工大樂冠服，並同樂音王隊。 次二隊，婦女十人，冠僧伽帽，服紫禪衣，皂絳； 次婦女一人，服錦袈裟，余如前，持數珠，進至御前，北向立定，樂止，念致語畢，樂作，奏《長春柳》之曲。 次三隊，男子三人，冠、服、舞蹈，並同樂音王隊。 次四隊，男子一人，冠隱士冠，服白紗道袍，皂絳，執麈拂； 從者二人，冠黃包巾，服錦繡衣，執令字旗。 次五隊，男子五人，冠金冠，披金甲，錦袍，執戟，同前隊而進，北向立。 次六隊，男子五人，為金翅雕之像，舞蹈而進，樂止。 次七隊，樂工十有六人，冠五福冠，服錦繡衣，龍笛六，觱栗六，杖鼓四，與前大樂合奏《金字西番經》之曲。 次八隊，婦女二十人，冠珠子菩薩冠，服銷金黃衣，纓絡，佩綬，執金浮屠白傘蓋，舞唱前曲，與樂聲相和，進至御前，分為五重，重四人，曲終，再起，與後隊相和。 次九隊，婦女二十人，冠金翠菩薩冠，服銷金紅衣，執寶蓋，舞唱與前隊相和。 次十隊，婦女八人，冠青螺髻冠，服白銷金衣，執金蓮花。 次男子八人，披金甲，為八金剛像。 次一人，為文殊像，執如意； 一人為普賢像，執西番蓮花； 一人為如來像； 齊舞唱前曲一闋，樂止。 次婦女三人，歌《新水令》、《沽美酒》、《太平令》之曲終，念口號畢，舞唱相和，以次而出。
Dharma-Teaching Troupe: leading officers and Great Music dress matched the Music-King Troupe. The second troupe: ten women in Sangha caps, purple monastic robes, black borders; next one woman in a brocade kasaya as before, prayer beads in hand, advanced to the imperial presence facing north; music stopped; after the address music played "Long Spring Willow." The third troupe: three men in caps, dress, and dance as the Music-King Troupe. The fourth troupe: one man in a recluse cap, white gauze Daoist robe, black border, fly-whisk in hand; two followers in yellow wrapped caps and brocade dress holding command-character banners. The fifth troupe: five men in gold caps, golden armor, brocade robes, halberds in hand, advanced like the preceding troupe and stood facing north. The sixth troupe: five men as golden-winged eagles danced forward; music stopped. The seventh troupe: sixteen musicians in Five Blessings caps and brocade dress; six dragon flutes, six suona, four zhang drums, joining the Great Music in "Gold-Character Western Barbarian Scripture." The eighth troupe: twenty women in bead Bodhisattva caps, gold-brocade yellow robes, tassels, sash pendants, golden pagoda white umbrellas, danced and sang the preceding tune, advanced in five ranks of four; at the tune's end they rose to meet the following troupe. The ninth troupe: twenty women in gold-kingfisher Bodhisattva caps, gold-brocade red robes, treasure canopies, dancing and singing with the preceding troupe. The tenth troupe: eight women in blue-snail topknot caps, white gold-brocade robes, golden lotuses in hand. Next eight men in golden armor as the Eight Vajras. Next one man as Manjusri, ruyi in hand; one as Samantabhadra, Western lotus in hand; one as the Tathagata; all danced one stanza in unison; music stopped. Next three women sang "New Water Order," "Buy Fine Wine," and "Peace Order"; after the slogans they danced and sang in harmony and withdrew in order.
