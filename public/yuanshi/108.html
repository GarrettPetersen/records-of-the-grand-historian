昔周封列國七十，而同姓者五十三人； 漢申丹書之信，而外戚侯者恩寖廣矣。 詩曰：「大邦維屏，大宗維翰。」 其此之謂乎？ 元興，宗室駙馬，通稱諸王，歲賜之頒，分地之入，所以盡夫展親之義者，亦優且渥。 然初制簡朴，位號無稱，惟視印章，以為輕重。 厥後遂有國邑之名，而賜印之等猶前日也。 得諸掌故，具著于篇。 作諸王表。
In antiquity the Zhou enfeoffed seventy states, fifty-three of them bearing the royal surname; Under the Han the oath sworn in red ink still held, yet favor for maternal kinsmen enfeoffed as marquises grew ever wider. The Odes say: "Great states are bulwarks; great lineages are pillars." Is this not what is meant? When the Yuan rose, imperial clansmen and consorts alike were styled princes; the yearly largesse and allotted revenues with which kinship was honored were generous indeed. At first the system was spare: ranks went untitled, and only the seal marked precedence. Later came named states and fiefs, while the grades of seals bestowed stayed as before. Drawing on institutional records, the full account is set forth below. Hence this table of princes.
