古者三公之職，寅亮天地，燮理陰陽，以論道經邦者也。 元初，以太師、太傅、太保為三公，自木華黎國王始為太師，後凡為三公者，皆國之元勳，而漢人則惟劉秉忠嘗為太保，其後鮮有聞矣。 其制又有大司徒、司徒、太尉、司空之屬，然其置否不常，人品或混，故置者又或開府不開府焉。 若夫東宮，亦嘗置三師、三少，而不恒有也。 今固不得而悉著之，惟自木華黎而下，得拜三公者若干人，作三公表。 太祖皇帝
In ancient times the Three Ducal Ministers were charged with honoring Heaven and Earth, harmonizing yin and yang, and guiding the realm through discourse on the Way. Early in the Yuan dynasty the Grand Preceptor, Grand Tutor, and Grand Guardian constituted the Three Excellencies. Muqali, enfeoffed as king, was the first Grand Preceptor; thereafter every man raised to these three posts was a founding pillar of the realm. Among Han officials only Liu Bingzhong ever held Grand Guardian — and scarcely anyone among the Han after him. The institution also recognized Grand Minister of Education, Minister of Education, Grand Commandant, and Minister of Works, among others; but these offices were not consistently filled, and appointees varied in standing — so some appointees opened a formal bureau and others did not. The crown prince's establishment likewise sometimes included the Three Masters and Three Minor Mentors, though these posts were not kept permanently. A full record of every holder is impossible here; this table lists only those from Muqali onward who were appointed to the three great offices. Emperor Taizu
