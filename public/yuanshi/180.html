1
耶律希亮
Yelü Xiliang
2
耶律希亮，字明甫，楚材之孫，鑄之子也。 初，六皇后命以赤帖吉氏歸鑄，生希亮於和林南之涼樓，曰禿忽思，六皇后遂以其地名之。 憲宗嘗遣鑄核錢糧於燕，鑄曰：「臣先世皆讀儒書，儒生俱在中土，願攜諸子，至燕受業。 」憲宗從之，乃命希亮師事北平趙衍。 時方九歲，未浹旬，已能賦詩。 歲丙辰，憲宗召鑄還和林，希亮獨留燕。 歲戊午，憲宗在六盤山，希亮詣行在所。 已而鑄扈從南伐，希亮亦在行。 明年，憲宗崩於蜀，希亮將輜重北歸陝右。
Yelü Xiliang, whose courtesy name was Mingfu, was a grandson of Yelü Chucai and a son of Yelü Zhu. Earlier, Empress Dowager Töregene had given Chijiteji in marriage to Zhu. Xiliang was born at the Cool Tower south of Karakorum, a place called Tuhusi, and the empress dowager named him after it. Emperor Möngke once dispatched Zhu to Yan to audit revenue and supplies. Zhu said, "For generations my family has studied the Confucian classics, and the scholars are all in China proper. I ask permission to bring my sons to Yan for their education." The emperor agreed and appointed Xiliang to study under Zhao Yan of Beiping. He was only nine years old, yet within ten days he was already writing verse. In the bingchen year, Emperor Möngke recalled Zhu to Karakorum while Xiliang stayed behind in Yan. In the wuwu year the emperor was at Liupanshan, and Xiliang went to join the campaign headquarters. Soon afterward Zhu accompanied the southern expedition, and Xiliang marched with him. The following year Emperor Möngke died in Shu, and Xiliang led the supply train northward back into Shaanxi.
3
又明年，為中統元年，世祖即位，阿里不哥反，遣使召主將渾都海。 鑄說渾都海等入朝，皆不從，則棄其妻子，挺身來歸。 既而渾都海知鑄去，怒，遣百騎追之不及。 乃使百人監視希亮母子，迫脅使從行，自靈武過應吉里城，至西涼甘州。 阿里不哥遣大將阿藍答兒自和林帥師至焉支山，希亮見之。 阿藍答兒問：「而父安在？ 」希亮曰：「不知，與吾父同任事者宜知之。 」渾都海怒，詬曰：「我焉得知之，其父今亡命東見皇帝矣！ 」希亮曰：「若然，則何謂不知！ 」阿藍答兒熟視渾都海曰：「此言深有意焉。 」詰希亮甚急。 希亮曰：「使吾知之，亦從而去，安得獨留！ 」阿藍答兒以為實，免其監蒞。 既而阿藍答兒、渾都海為大兵所殺，其殘卒北走，眾推哈剌不花為帥。 希亮潛匿甘州北黑水東沙陀中。 殿兵已過十餘里，有尋馬者適至，老婢漏言，眾奄至，驅至肅州。 哈剌不花與鑄有婚姻之好，又哈剌不花在蜀時，嘗疾病，鑄召醫視之,遺以酒食，因釋希亮縛，謂曰：「我受恩於汝父，此圖報之秋也。 」及抵沙州北川，希亮與兄弟徒步負任，不火食者數日。 是冬，涉雪逾天山，至北庭都護府。 二年，至昌八里城。 夏，逾馬納思河，抵葉密里城，乃定宗潛邸湯沐之邑也。 時六皇后之妹主後位，與宗王火忽皆欲東覲。 希亮母密知其事，攜希亮入見，已而事不果。 冬，至於火孛之地。 三年，定宗幼子大名王閔其不能歸，遺以幣帛鞍馬，乃從大名王至忽只兒之地。 會宗王阿魯忽至，誅阿里不哥所用鎮守之人唆羅海，欲附世祖。 復從大名王及阿魯忽二王還至葉密里城。 王遺以耳環，其二珠大如榛，實價直千金，欲穿其耳使帶之。 希亮辭曰：「不敢因是以傷父母之遺體也。 且無功受賞，於禮尤不可。 」王又解金束帶遺之，且曰：「系此，於遺體宜無傷。 」五月，又為阿里不哥兵所驅，西行千五百里，至孛劣撒里之地。 六月，又西至換扎孫之地。 又從至不剌城。 又西行六百里，至徹徹里澤剌之山，后妃輜重皆留於此，希亮母及兄弟亦在焉。 希亮單騎從行二百餘里，至出布兒城。 又百里，至也裡虔城，而哈剌不花之兵奄至，希亮又從二王興師，還至不剌城，與哈剌不花戰，敗之，盡殲其眾。 二王乃函其頭，遣使報捷。 十月，至於亦思寬之地。 四年，至可失哈里城。 四月，阿里不哥兵復至，希亮又從征，至渾八升城。 時希亮母從後避暑於阿體八升山。 先是，鑄嘗言於世祖：「臣之妻子皆在北邊。 」至是，世祖遣不華出至二王所，因以璽書召希亮，馳驛赴闕。 六月，由苦先城至哈剌火州，出伊州，涉大漠以還。 八月，入覲世祖於上都之大安閣，備陳邊事，及羈旅困苦之狀。 世祖憐之，賜鈔千錠、金帶一、幣帛三十，命為速古兒赤、必阇赤。 至元八年，授奉訓大夫、符寶郎。
The next year was the first year of Zhongtong. Kublai took the throne, Ariq Böke rose in revolt, and sent envoys to summon the commander Hunduhai. Zhu urged Hunduhai and the others to present themselves at court, but they refused. He then left his wife and children behind and came alone to join Kublai. When Hunduhai learned that Zhu had fled, he flew into a rage and sent a hundred horsemen in pursuit, but they could not catch him. He then set a hundred men to guard Xiliang and his mother and forced them to march with the army from Lingwu past Yingjili City to Ganzhou in Xiliang. Ariq Böke sent the general Alandar from Karakorum at the head of an army to Yanzhi Mountain, where Xiliang was brought before him. Alandar asked, "Where is your father?" Xiliang replied, "I do not know. Those who served with my father should know." Hunduhai burst out angrily, "How should I know? His father has already fled east to join the emperor!" Xiliang said, "If that is so, what do you mean by saying you do not know?" Alandar fixed his gaze on Hunduhai and said, "Those words carry a great deal of meaning." He pressed Xiliang hard for an answer. Xiliang said, "If I had known, I would have gone with him. How could I have been left behind alone?" Alandar believed him and lifted the guard placed over him. Soon afterward Alandar and Hunduhai were killed by Kublai's main force. The survivors fled north and chose Harabuhua as their commander. Xiliang went into hiding in the Shatuo lands east of the Black River, north of Ganzhou. The rearguard had already marched more than ten li on when scouts came looking for horses. An old servant let the secret slip, the troops descended on them at once, and they were driven to Suzhou. Harabuhua was connected to Zhu by marriage, and when Harabuhua had fallen ill in Shu, Zhu had sent a physician and provided wine and food. He therefore released Xiliang from his bonds and said, "Your father once showed me kindness. Now is the time to repay it." When they reached the northern reaches of Shazhou, Xiliang and his brothers went on foot carrying their packs and went several days without a cooked meal. That winter they crossed the snow-covered Tianshan and reached the Protectorate General of Beiting. In the second year they reached Changbali. In summer they crossed the Manasi River and came to Yemili, the appanage town attached to Ögedei's princely establishment. At that time Töregene's younger sister held the empress's place, and she and the prince Huohu both wished to go east to pay homage at court. Xiliang's mother learned of the plan in secret and brought Xiliang in to see her, but in the end nothing came of it. That winter they reached the land of Huobie. In the third year Ögedei's youngest son, Prince Daming, took pity on their inability to return home and gave them silks, cloth, saddles, and horses. They then followed the prince to Huzhier. The prince Arigh Böke then arrived, executed Suoluo Hai, whom Ariq Böke had left as garrison commander, and sought to join Kublai. They then followed Princes Daming and Arigh Böke back to Yemili. The prince gave him a pair of earrings whose pearls were as large as hazelnuts and worth a thousand pieces of gold, and wanted to pierce his ears so that he could wear them. Xiliang declined, saying, "I dare not injure the body my parents gave me for such a reason." Besides, to accept a reward without having earned it is utterly contrary to propriety. The prince then took off a gold belt and gave it to him, saying, "Wear this instead—it should do no injury to the body your parents gave you." In the fifth month Ariq Böke's troops drove them west again for fifteen hundred li to Biliesali. In the sixth month they marched farther west to Huanzhasun. They continued on to Bula. They marched another six hundred li west to Mount Checheli Zelie, where the empresses' baggage was left behind along with Xiliang's mother and brothers. Xiliang rode alone with the column for more than two hundred li to Chubuer. Another hundred li brought them to Yeliqian, where Harabuhua's army suddenly appeared. Xiliang again followed the two princes as they raised troops, marched back to Bula, defeated Harabuhua, and wiped out his force to the last man. The two princes placed his head in a box and sent messengers to report the victory. In the tenth month they reached Yisikuan. In the fourth year they reached Keshahari. In the fourth month Ariq Böke's army returned, and Xiliang again joined the campaign as far as Hunbasheng. At that time Xiliang's mother had gone to the rear to escape the summer heat at Mount Atibasheng. Earlier Zhu had told Kublai, "Your servant's wife and children are all still in the north." Now the emperor sent Buhua to the two princes with a sealed summons for Xiliang, who raced by relay to court. In the sixth month he went from Kuxian to Karahuozhou, left by way of Yizhou, crossed the great desert, and made his way home. In the eighth month he had audience with Kublai in the Great Peace Pavilion at Shangdu and gave a full account of frontier affairs and the hardships of his years abroad. The emperor took pity on him, gave him a thousand ingots of paper money, a gold belt, and thirty pieces of silk and cloth, and appointed him attendant and scribe. In the eighth year of Zhiyuan he was made Grand Master for Court Instruction and Keeper of the Seals and Credentials.
4
十二年，既平宋，世祖命希亮問諸降將，日本可伐否。 夏貴、呂文煥、範文虎、陳奕等皆云可伐。 希亮奏曰：「宋與遼、金攻戰且三百年，干戈甫定，人得息肩，俟數年，興師未晚。 」世祖然之。 十三年，太府監令史盧贄言於監官：「各路所貢布長三丈，唯平陽加一丈，諸怯薛歹以故爭取平陽布。 苟截其長者，與他郡等，則無所爭，而以其所截者為髹漆宮殿器皿之用，甚便。 」監官從之。 適左右以其事聞，帝以詰監官，監官倉皇莫知所以對，歸罪於贄，帝命斬之。 希亮遇諸途，贄以冤告。 希亮命少緩，具以實入奏。 有旨令董文用讞之。 竟釋贄，而召御史大夫塔察兒等讓之曰：「此事言官當言而不言，向微禿忽思，不誤誅此人耶！ 」十四年，轉嘉議大夫、禮部尚書，尋遷吏部尚書。 帝駐蹕察納兒台之地，希亮至，奏對畢，董文用問大都近事。 希亮曰：「囹圄多囚耳。 」世祖方欹枕而臥，忽寤，問其故。 希亮奏曰：「近奉旨：漢人盜鈔六文者殺。 以是囚多。 」帝驚問：「孰傳此語？ 」省臣曰：「此旨實脫兒察所傳。 」脫兒察曰：「陛下在南坡，以語蒙古兒童。 」帝曰：「前言戲耳，曷嘗著為令式？ 」乃罪脫兒察。 希亮因奏曰：「令既出矣，必明其錯誤，以安民心。 」帝善其言，即命希亮至大都，諭旨中書。
In the twelfth year, after the conquest of the Song, Kublai ordered Xiliang to ask the surrendered generals whether Japan could be invaded. Xia Gui, Lü Wenhuan, Fan Wenhü, Chen Yi, and the others all said that it could. Xiliang memorialized, "The Song waged war against Liao and Jin for nearly three hundred years. Arms have only just been laid down and the people have barely had a moment's rest. Wait a few years before sending troops—it will not be too late." The emperor agreed. In the thirteenth year Lu Zan, a clerk of the Grand Provisionery, told his supervisor, "Each circuit presents cloth three zhang long, but Pingyang alone adds an extra zhang, and for that reason the imperial guards all scramble for Pingyang cloth." If the extra length were trimmed so that it matched the other prefectures, there would be no dispute, and the trimmed pieces could be used for lacquered palace vessels—a very practical arrangement. The supervisor agreed. Attendants happened to report the matter to the emperor, who questioned the supervisor. The man panicked and could not answer, blamed Zan, and the emperor ordered Zan executed. Xiliang met him on the road, and Zan pleaded that he had been wronged. Xiliang told them to wait and submitted a full report of the facts. An edict directed Dong Wenyong to review the case. Zan was released in the end, and the censor-in-chief Tachar and others were summoned and rebuked: "The remonstrance officials should have spoken up in this matter. Had it not been for Tuhusi, would we not have executed an innocent man by mistake?" In the fourteenth year he was promoted to Grand Master of Court Discussion and Minister of Rites, and soon afterward transferred to Minister of Personnel. While the emperor was encamped at Chanaiertai, Xiliang arrived and finished his audience. Dong Wenyong then asked him about recent affairs in Dadu. Xiliang said, "The prisons are simply full—that is all." Kublai had been dozing on his pillow, but he started awake and asked why. Xiliang replied, "A recent order says that any Han person who steals six cash of paper money is to be executed." That is why the prisons are so crowded. The emperor asked in alarm, "Who spread that saying?" A minister of the Secretariat said, "It was To'ercha who passed it along." To'ercha said, "Your Majesty said it in jest to some Mongol boys at Nanpo." The emperor said, "That was only a joke. When did I ever turn it into law?" He then punished To'ercha. Xiliang then said, "Once an order has gone out, its mistake must be made plain so that the people may be reassured." The emperor approved and immediately sent Xiliang to Dadu to announce the corrected edict to the Central Secretariat.
5
十七年，希亮以跋涉西土，足病痿攣，謝事而去，退居惣陽者二十餘年。 至大二年，武宗訪求先朝舊臣，特除翰林學士承旨、資善大夫，尋改授翰林學士承旨、知制誥兼修國史。 希亮以職在史官，乃類次世祖嘉言善行以進，英宗取其書，置禁中。 久之，閒居京師，四方之士多從之遊。 泰定四年卒，年八十一。
In the seventeenth year, worn out by years of travel in the west and crippled by paralysis of the legs, he resigned and withdrew to Zongyang, where he lived in retirement for more than twenty years. In the second year of Zhida, Emperor Wuzong sought out veterans of the previous reign and specially appointed him Academician Expositor-in-Chief and Grand Master of Excellent Virtue. Soon afterward he was given the additional posts of drafter of imperial edicts and compiler of the national history. As a historian, Xiliang compiled Kublai's memorable words and worthy deeds and presented the work to the throne. Emperor Yingzong took the book and kept it in the inner palace. In time he settled in the capital, and scholars from all directions came to study with him. He died in the fourth year of Taiding at the age of eighty-one.
6
希亮性至孝，困厄遐方，家貲散亡已盡，僅藏祖考畫像，四時就穹廬陳列致奠，盡誠盡敬。 朔漠之人，咸相聚來觀，歎曰：「此中土之禮也。 」雖疾病，不廢書史，或中夜起坐，取燭以書。 所著詩文及從軍紀行錄三十卷，目之曰《愫軒集》。 贈推忠輔義守正功臣、資善大夫、集賢學士、上護軍，追封漆水郡公，諡忠嘉。
Xiliang was profoundly filial. Though hardship in distant lands had stripped his family of everything, he kept only portraits of his ancestors and, at each season, set them out in his yurt to offer sacrifice with the utmost sincerity and respect. People of the northern steppe gathered to watch and marveled, "This is the ritual of China proper." Even when he was ill he never set aside his books. Sometimes he would rise in the middle of the night, light a candle, and write. His poetry, prose, and campaign diaries filled thirty juan, which he titled the 《Su Studio Collection》. He was posthumously honored as Meritous Subject Who Promotes Loyalty, Assists Righteousness, and Upholds Integrity, with the ranks of Grand Master of Excellent Virtue, Academician of the Hall of Gathered Talents, and Chief Commander of the Upper Army, enfeoffed as Duke of Qishui, and given the posthumous name Zhongjia.
7
趙世延
Zhao Shiyan
8
趙世延，字子敬，其先雍古族人，居雲中北邊。 曾祖公，為金群牧使，太祖得其所牧馬，公死之。 祖按竺邇，幼孤，鞠於外大父術要甲，訛為趙家，因氏為趙； 驍勇善騎射，從太祖征伐，有功，為蒙古漢軍徵行大元帥，鎮蜀，因家成都。 父黑梓，以門功襲父元帥職，兼文州吐蕃萬戶府達魯花赤。
Zhao Shiyan, whose courtesy name was Zijing, came from the Yonggu people, who lived on the northern border of Yunzhong. His great-grandfather, known as the Duke, was director of herds under the Jin. When Genghis Khan seized the horses under his charge, the Duke died defending them. His grandfather Anzhuer was orphaned in childhood and raised by his maternal grandfather Shuyao Jia. The family was mistakenly taken for the Zhao clan, and so they adopted Zhao as their surname. He was bold and skilled in horsemanship and archery, followed Genghis Khan on campaign with distinction, became Grand Marshal on Campaign of the Mongol Han Army, garrisoned Shu, and made his home in Chengdu. His father Heizi inherited the family marshal's post by hereditary merit and also served as darughachi of the Wenzou Tubo Ten-thousand Households Office.
9
世延天資秀發，喜讀書，究心儒者體用之學。 弱冠，世祖召見，俾入樞密院御史臺肄習官政。 至元二十一年，授承事郎、雲南諸路提刑按察司判官，時年二十有四。 烏蒙蠻酋叛，世延會省臣以軍討之，蠻兵大潰，即請降。 二十六年，擢監察御史，與同列五人劾丞相桑哥不法。 中丞趙國輔，桑哥黨也，抑不以聞，更以告桑哥。 於是五人者悉為其所擠，而世延獨倖免。 奉旨按平陽郡監也先忽都贓巨萬，鞫左司郎中董仲威殺人獄，皆明允。 二十九年，轉奉議大夫，出僉江南湖北道肅政廉訪司事。 敦儒學，立義倉，撤淫祠，修澧陽縣壞堤，嚴常、澧掠賣良民之禁，部內晏然。 元貞元年，除江南行御史臺都事，丁內艱，不赴。 大德元年，复除前官。 三年，移中臺都事，俄改中書左司都事。 臺臣奏，仍為都事中臺。 六年，由山東肅政廉訪副使改江南行臺治書侍御史。 十年，除安西路總管。 安西，故京兆省臺所治，號稱會府，前政壅滯者三千牘。 世延既至，不三月，剖決殆盡。 陝民飢，省臺議請於朝賑之，世延曰：「救荒如救火，願先發廩以賑，朝廷設不允，世延當傾家財若身以償。 」省臺從之，所活者眾。
Shiyan was gifted and quick-minded, loved reading, and devoted himself to the Confucian study of principle and practical application. When he came of age, Emperor Kublai summoned him and had him train in government at the Bureau of Military Affairs and the Censorate. In the twenty-first year of the Zhiyuan era (1284), he was appointed Gentleman for Managing Affairs and adjucator of the Yunnan Circuit Provincial Surveillance Commission, at the age of twenty-four. When the Wumeng chieftain rebelled, Shiyan joined the provincial officials in a military campaign against him. The tribal forces were routed and at once sued for surrender. In the twenty-sixth year, he was promoted to supervising censor. He and five colleagues jointly impeached Chancellor Sangge for unlawful conduct. Vice Censor-in-Chief Zhao Guofu, a partisan of Sangge, suppressed the memorial and never reported it to the throne, but instead told Sangge. All five of his colleagues were then forced out, but Shiyan alone narrowly escaped. By imperial order he investigated Ye Xianhudu, the Pingyang commandery supervisor, whose bribes ran to tens of thousands of cash. He also tried the murder case of Dong Zhongwei, director of the Left Department—all were judged clearly and fairly. In the twenty-ninth year, he was transferred to Gentleman for Discussion and dispatched as deputy commissioner of the Jiangnan-Hubei Circuit Commission for Rectifying Governance and Supervising Officials. He encouraged Confucian learning, established charity granaries, demolished illicit shrines, and repaired the broken dyke at Liyang County. He strictly enforced the prohibition on kidnapping and selling commoners in Chang and Li, and the region under his charge grew calm. In the first year of Yuanzhen, he was appointed chief clerk of the Jiangnan Branch Censorate, but when his mother died he did not take up the office. In the first year of Dade, he was again appointed to his former post. In the third year he was transferred to chief clerk of the Central Censorate, and soon after was reassigned as chief clerk of the Left Department of the Secretariat. The censorate officials memorialized the throne, and he remained chief clerk at the Central Censorate. In the sixth year he was moved from deputy commissioner of the Shandong Commission for Rectifying Governance to investigative attendant censor of the Jiangnan Branch Censorate. In the tenth year he was appointed prefect of Anxi Circuit. Anxi had formerly been governed by the Jingzhao provincial and censorial offices and was known as a metropolitan prefecture. The previous administration had left three thousand cases backlogged. Once Shiyan took office, he cleared nearly all of them within three months. When the people of Shaanxi were starving, the provincial and censorial offices debated asking the court for relief. Shiyan said, "Relieving famine is like fighting a fire. I ask permission to open the granaries at once. If the court refuses, I will repay the cost from my own family fortune and my own person." The provincial and censorial offices agreed, and many lives were saved.
10
至大元年，除紹興路總管，改四川肅政廉訪使。 蒙古軍士，科差繁重，而軍士就戍往來者多害人，且軍官或抑良為奴，世延皆除其弊而正其罪。 又修都江堰，民尤便之。 四年，升中奉大夫、陝西行臺侍御史。 先是，八百媳婦為邊患，右丞劉深往討之，兵敗而還，坐罪棄市。 及是，右丞阿忽台當繼行，世延言：「蠻夷事在羈縻，而重煩天討，致軍旅亡失，誅戮省臣，藉使盡得其地，何補於國？ 今窮兵黷武，實傷聖治。 朝廷第當選重臣知治體者，付以邊寄，兵宜止勿用。 」事聞，樞密院臣以為用兵國家大事，不宜以一人之言為興輟。 世延聞之，章再上，事卒罷。
In the first year of Zhida he was appointed prefect of Shaoxing Circuit, then transferred to commissioner of the Sichuan Commission for Rectifying Governance. Mongol soldiers were burdened with heavy levies, and troops traveling to and from garrison duty often preyed on the people. Some officers even forced commoners into slavery. Shiyan eliminated these abuses and punished the offenders. He also restored the Dujiangyan irrigation system, to the people's great benefit. In the fourth year he was promoted to grand master of palace attendance and appointed attendant censor of the Shaanxi Branch Censorate. Earlier the Babai Xifu people had troubled the frontier. Right Chancellor Liu Shen marched against them, but his army was defeated and he returned to face execution. When Right Chancellor Ahutai was about to take over the campaign, Shiyan said, "Barbarian affairs are best managed through loose reins. Yet we repeatedly send imperial armies against them, losing troops and executing provincial ministers. Even if we conquered every inch of their territory, what good would it do the state?" To exhaust the armies in endless war truly wounds the sage's rule. The court should choose senior ministers who understand governance, entrust the frontier to them, and halt the use of force. When the matter was reported, officials of the Bureau of Military Affairs argued that war is a grave affair of state and should not be launched or abandoned on the word of one man. Shiyan heard of this and submitted another memorial. In the end the campaign was abandoned.
11
皇慶二年，拜江浙行省參知政事，尋召還，拜侍御史。 延祐元年，省臣奏：「比奉詔漢人參政用儒者，趙世延其人也。 」帝曰：「世延誠可用，然雍古氏非漢人，其署宜居右。 」遂拜中書參知政事。 居中書二十月，遷御史中丞。 有旨省臣自平章以下，率送之官。 其禮前所無有，由是為權臣所忌，乃用皇太后旨，出世延為雲南行省右丞。 陛辭，帝特命仍還御史臺為中丞。 三年，世延劾奏權臣太師、右丞相帖木迭兒罪惡十有三，詔奪其官職。 尋升翰林學士承旨，兼御史中丞，世延固辭，乃解中丞。 五年，進光祿大夫、昭文館學士，守大都留守，乞補外，拜四川行省平章政事。 世延議即重慶路立屯田，物色江津、巴縣閒田七百八十三頃，摘軍千二百人墾之，歲得粟萬一千七百石。
In the second year of Huangqing he was appointed vice administrator of the Jiangzhe Branch Secretariat. He was soon recalled and made attendant censor. In the first year of Yanyou the provincial ministers memorialized, "By recent imperial decree, Han Chinese vice administrators should be men of Confucian learning. Zhao Shiyan is such a man." The emperor said, "Shiyan is indeed fit for office, but the Yonggu are not Han Chinese, so his appointment should be on the right." He was then appointed vice administrator of the Secretariat. After twenty months in the Secretariat, he was transferred to vice censor-in-chief. An edict required every provincial minister from the junior chancellor down to escort him to his new office. No such honor had ever been granted before. Powerful ministers resented him, and by the empress dowager's order Shiyan was sent out as right chancellor of the Yunnan Branch Secretariat. At his farewell audience the emperor specially ordered him back to the Censorate as vice censor-in-chief. In the third year Shiyan impeached the powerful minister Grand Preceptor and Right Chancellor Bayan on thirteen counts of misconduct. An edict stripped Bayan of his offices. He was soon promoted to academician chancellor of the Hanlin Academy while retaining the post of vice censor-in-chief. Shiyan firmly declined the dual appointment and was relieved of the censorate post. In the fifth year he was promoted to grand master of brilliant happiness and academician of the Zhaowen Hall, and served as acting metropolitan magistrate of Dadu. He asked for a provincial appointment and was made junior chancellor of the Sichuan Branch Secretariat. Shiyan proposed establishing military colonies at Chongqing Circuit. He located seven hundred eighty-three qing of land between Jiangjin and Ba Counties, assigned twelve hundred soldiers to farm it, and each year harvested eleven thousand seven hundred shi of grain.
12
明年，仁宗崩，帖木迭兒复居相位，銳意報復，屬其黨何志道，誘世延從弟胥益兒哈呼誣告世延罪，逮世延置對，至夔路，遇赦。 世延以疾抵荊門，留就醫。 帖木迭兒遣使督追至京師，俾其黨煅煉使成獄。 會有旨，事經赦原，勿復問。 帖木迭兒更以它事白帝，系之刑曹，逼令自裁，世延不為動，居囚再歲。 胥益兒哈呼自以所訴涉誣欺，亡去。 中書左丞相拜住屢言世延亡辜，得旨出獄，就舍以養疾。 先是，帝獵北涼亭，顧謂侍臣曰：「趙世延先帝所尊禮，而帖木迭兒妄入其罪，數請誅之，此殆報私怨耳，朕豈能從之。 」侍臣皆叩頭稱萬歲。 帖木迭兒在上京，聞世延出獄，索省牘視之，怒曰：「此左丞相罔上所為也。 」事聞，帝語之曰：「此朕意耳。 」未幾，帖木迭兒死，事乃釋。 世延出居於金陵。 泰定元年，召還朝，除集賢大學士。 明年，出為江南行臺御史中丞。 四年，入朝，復為御史中丞，又遷中書右丞。 明年，有旨：趙世延頃為權奸所誣，中書宜遍移天下，昭雪其非辜，仍加翰林學士承旨、光祿大夫。 經筵開，兼知經筵事，選揀勸講者，皆一時名流。 又加同知樞密院事。
The next year Emperor Renzong died. Bayan returned to power and set out to settle scores. He had his partisan He Zhidao induce Shiyan's cousin Xu Yierhahu to lodge a false accusation. Shiyan was arrested and brought for trial. On the road to Kuizhou Circuit he met with a general amnesty. Ill, Shiyan reached Jingmen and remained there for treatment. Bayan sent agents to hurry him to the capital and ordered his followers to fabricate a conviction. An edict had just been issued stating that cases covered by amnesty were not to be reopened. Bayan raised new charges before the emperor, had Shiyan imprisoned in the penal office, and pressed him to kill himself. Shiyan did not waver and remained in custody for two years. Xu Yierhahu, realizing his accusation was fraudulent, fled. Left Chancellor Baizhu repeatedly declared Shiyan innocent. An edict released him from prison, and he recuperated at home. Earlier, while hunting at Beiliang Pavilion, the emperor turned to his attendants and said, "Zhao Shiyan was honored by my predecessor, yet Bayan falsely charged him and repeatedly asked for his execution. That is plainly private revenge. How could I agree to it?" The attending ministers all kowtowed and shouted their congratulations. Bayan was at Shangdu. When he heard Shiyan had been released, he demanded the Secretariat documents and, after reading them, said angrily, "The left chancellor has deceived the emperor." When the remark reached the emperor, he told Bayan, "That was my own decision." Before long Bayan died, and the affair was finally laid to rest. Shiyan withdrew and made his home in Jinling. In the first year of Taiding he was recalled to court and appointed grand academician of the Hall of Gathered Talents. The following year he was sent out as vice censor-in-chief of the Jiangnan Branch Censorate. In the fourth year he returned to court, again became vice censor-in-chief, and was then promoted to right vice chancellor of the Secretariat. The next year an edict declared that Zhao Shiyan had been slandered by powerful traitors. The Secretariat was to circulate notices throughout the empire clearing his name, and he was further granted the titles of academician chancellor of the Hanlin Academy and grand master of brilliant happiness. When the imperial lectern was opened, he was put in charge of its affairs. The lecturers he chose were all leading figures of the day. He was also appointed vice director of the Bureau of Military Affairs.
13
泰定帝崩，燕鐵木兒與宗王大臣議：武宗二子周王、懷王，於法當立； 周王遠在朔漠，而懷王久居民間，備嘗艱險，民必歸之，天位不可久虛，不如先迎懷王，以從民望。 八月，即定策，迎之於江陵，懷王即位，是為文宗。 當是時，世延贊畫之功為多。 文宗即位，世延仍以御史中丞兼翰林學士承旨，以疾乞歸田里，詔不允。 天曆二年正月，复除江南行臺御史中丞； 行次濟州，三月，改集賢大學士； 六月，又加奎章閣大學士； 八月，拜中書平章政事。 冬，世延至京，固辭不允，詔以世延年高多疾，許乘小車入內。 至順元年，詔世延與虞集等纂修《皇朝經世大典》，世延屢奏：「臣衰老，乞解中書政務，專意纂修。 」帝曰：「老臣如卿者無幾，求退之言，後勿復陳。 」四月，仍加翰林學士承旨，封魯國公。 秋，以疾，移文中書致其事，明日即行，養疾於金陵之茅山。 詔徵還朝，不能行，二年，改封涼國公。 元統二年，詔賜世延錢凡四萬緡。 至元改元，仍除奎章閣大學士、翰林學士承旨、中書平章政事、魯國公。 明年五月，至成都，十一月卒，享年七十有七。 至正二年，贈世忠執法佐運翊亮功臣、太保、金紫光祿大夫、上柱國，追封魯國公，諡文忠。
When Emperor Taiding died, Yan Temur consulted with the imperial princes and senior ministers. By right, one of Emperor Wuzong's two sons—the Prince of Zhou and the Prince of Huai—should succeed; but the Prince of Zhou was far away in the northern steppe, whereas the Prince of Huai had long lived among the people and borne hardship. The people would surely rally to him. The throne could not remain empty, so it was better to welcome the Prince of Huai first and satisfy public expectation. In the eighth month they settled on this course, welcomed him at Jiangling, and the Prince of Huai ascended the throne as Emperor Wenzong. At that time Shiyan's contribution to the planning was the greatest. When Wenzong took the throne, Shiyan remained vice censor-in-chief while also serving as academician chancellor of the Hanlin Academy. He pleaded illness and asked to retire to his home, but the edict refused. In the first month of the second year of Tianli he was again appointed vice censor-in-chief of the Jiangnan Branch Censorate; while traveling he stopped at Jizhou, and in the third month was transferred to grand academician of the Hall of Gathered Talents; in the sixth month he was further made grand academician of the Kuizhang Pavilion; and in the eighth month he was appointed junior chancellor of the Secretariat. That winter Shiyan reached the capital. He tried again to decline the appointment, but was not permitted. An edict, noting his advanced age and frequent illness, allowed him to enter the palace in a small carriage. In the first year of Zhishun an edict ordered Shiyan, together with Yu Ji and others, to compile the "Records of Governance and Institutions." Shiyan repeatedly memorialized, "I am old and infirm. I beg to be relieved of Secretariat duties so that I may devote myself entirely to the compilation." The emperor said, "There are few old ministers like you. Do not speak of retirement again." In the fourth month he was again granted the title of academician chancellor of the Hanlin Academy and enfeoffed as Duke of Lu. That autumn, citing illness, he sent a letter to the Secretariat resigning his office. The next day he set out and went to convalesce on Mount Mao near Jinling. An edict summoned him back to court, but he was unable to travel. In the second year his title was changed to Duke of Liang. In the second year of Yuantong an edict granted Shiyan a total of forty thousand strings of cash. When the era name was changed to Zhiyuan, he was again appointed grand academician of the Kuizhang Pavilion, academician chancellor of the Hanlin Academy, junior chancellor of the Secretariat, and Duke of Lu. The following year, in the fifth month, he reached Chengdu. In the eleventh month he died at the age of seventy-seven. In the second year of Zhizheng he was posthumously honored as Meritous Subject Who Served the Age with Loyalty, Upheld the Law, Assisted the Destiny, and Illumined the Court, with the ranks of grand preceptor, grand master of splendid happiness with golden seal and purple robe, and pillar of the state of the upper rank. He was posthumously enfeoffed as Duke of Lu and given the posthumous name Wenzhong.
14
世延歷事凡九朝，揚歷省臺五十餘年，負經濟之資，而將之以忠義，守之以清介，飾之以文學，凡軍國利病，生民休戚，知無不言，而於儒者名教尤拳拳焉。 為文章波瀾浩瀚，一根於理。 嘗較定律令，匯次《風憲宏綱》，行於世。
Shiyan served nine reigns and spent more than fifty years in provincial and censorial office. He had the capacity for statecraft, yet tempered it with loyalty and righteousness, guarded it with integrity, and adorned it with learning. On every matter affecting army and state, and the people's welfare, he spoke without holding back; and he was especially devoted to Confucian moral teaching. His prose was broad and powerful, yet always rooted in principle. He once collated the statutes and compiled the "Comprehensive Statutes of Censorial Administration," which circulated widely.
15
五子，達者三人：野峻台，黃州路總管。 次月魯，江浙行省理問官。 伯忽，夔州路總管，天曆初，囊加台據蜀叛，死於難，特贈推忠秉義效節功臣、資善大夫、中書右丞、上護軍，追封蜀郡公，諡忠愍。
He had five sons, of whom three rose to distinction: Yejuntai, prefect of Huangzhou Circuit. The second, Yuelu, served as inquiry official of the Jiangzhe Branch Secretariat. Behu, prefect of Kuizhou Circuit. At the beginning of Tianli, when Nanggal Tai rebelled and seized Shu, Behu died in the upheaval. He was specially posthumously honored as Meritous Subject Who Promotes Loyalty, Upholds Righteousness, and Died in Service, with the ranks of grand master of excellent virtue, right vice chancellor of the Secretariat, and chief commander of the upper army. He was posthumously enfeoffed as Duke of Shu and given the posthumous name Zhongmin.
16
孔思晦
Kong Sihui
17
孔思晦，字明道，孔子五十四世孫也。 資質端重，而性簡默，童丱時，讀書已識大義。 及長，授業於導江張䇓，講求義理，於詞章之習，薄而弗為。 家貧，躬耕以為養，雖劇寒暑，而為學未嘗懈，遠近爭聘為子弟師。 大德中，遊京師，祭酒耶律有尚欲薦之，以母老，辭而歸。 母臥疾，躬進藥餌，衣不解帶。 居喪，勺水不入口者五日。 至大中，舉茂才，為范陽儒學教諭。 延祐初，調寧陽學。 先是，兩縣校官率以廩薄不能守職，而思晦以儉約自將，教養有法，比代去，學者皆不忍舍之。 於是孔氏族人相與議：思晦嫡長且賢，宜襲封爵，奉祠事。 狀上政府，事未決。 仁宗在位，雅崇尚儒道，一日，問：「孔子之裔今幾世，襲爵為誰？ 」廷臣具對曰：「未定。 」帝親取孔氏譜牒按之，曰：「以嫡應襲封者，思晦也，复奚疑！ 」特授中議大夫，襲封衍聖公，月俸百緡，加至五百緡，賜四品印。 泰定三年，山東廉訪副使王鵬南言：「襲爵上公，而階止四品，於格弗稱，且失尊崇意。 」明年，升嘉議大夫。 至順二年，改賜三品印。 思晦以宗祀責重，恆懼弗勝，每遇祭祀，必敬必慎。 初，廟毀於兵，後雖苟完，而角樓圍牆未備，思晦竭力營度，以復其舊。 金絲堂壞，又一新之，祭器禮服，悉加整飭。 又以尼山乃毓聖之地，故有廟，已毀，民冒耕祭田且百年，思晦復其田，且請置尼山書院，以列於學官，朝廷從之。 三氏學舊有田三千畝，佔於豪民，子思書院舊有營運錢萬緡，貸於民取子錢，以供祭祀，久之，民不輸子錢，並負其本，思晦皆理而復之。 聖父舊封齊國公，思晦言於朝曰：「宣聖封王，而父爵猶公，願加褒崇。 」乃詔加封聖父啟聖王，聖母王夫人。
Kong Sihui, whose courtesy name was Mingdao, was a fifty-fourth-generation descendant of Confucius. He was dignified and reserved by nature. Even as a child he read with understanding and grasped the larger meaning. When he came of age he studied under Zhang Yi of Daojiang, devoting himself to moral principle while giving little attention to literary composition. His family was poor, and he farmed with his own hands to support them. Through the harshest seasons he never slackened in his studies, and families near and far sought him out as a teacher for their sons. During the Dade era he went to the capital. Yelü Youshang, chancellor of the Directorate of Education, wished to recommend him, but he declined because his mother was elderly and returned home. When his mother fell ill, he personally tended her with medicine and food and never left her side. During mourning he went five days without taking even a spoonful of water. In the Zhida era he was recommended as a maocai and appointed instructor at the Fanyang Confucian school. At the beginning of Yanyou he was transferred to the Ningyang school. Previously, school officials in both counties had usually been unable to remain in office because their stipends were too small. Sihui lived frugally, taught with method, and when his term ended his students were loath to see him go. The Kong clansmen then agreed among themselves that Sihui, as the eldest legitimate son and a man of virtue, ought to inherit the enfeoffment and preside over the ancestral rites. They submitted a petition to the authorities, but the matter had not yet been settled. Emperor Renzong greatly honored Confucian learning. One day he asked, "Which generation of Confucius's descendants are we in now, and who holds the hereditary title?" The court ministers replied in full, "It has not yet been decided." The emperor personally examined the Kong clan genealogies and said, "The legitimate heir to the title is Sihui. What room for doubt remains?" He was specially appointed Grand Master of Palace Counsel and inherited the title Duke of Fulfilling the Sage, with a monthly stipend raised from one hundred to five hundred strings of cash and a seal of the fourth rank. In the third year of Taiding, Wang Pengnan, deputy surveillance commissioner of Shandong, said, "The hereditary holder of a supreme ducal title should not remain at only the fourth rank. That falls short of the regulations and of the respect due the sage's line." The following year he was promoted to Grand Master of Court Discussion. In the second year of Zhishun he was given a seal of the third rank. Because the duty of ancestral sacrifice weighed heavily upon him, Sihui constantly feared he might fall short. Whenever rites were performed, he was scrupulously reverent and careful. The temple had first been destroyed in war. Though it was later patched together, the corner towers and walls were still incomplete. Sihui threw himself into the work and restored it to its former condition. The Hall of Gold Threads had fallen into ruin, and he rebuilt it entirely. Sacrificial vessels and ritual garments were all put in proper order. Mount Ni was where the sage had been nurtured, and though a temple once stood there, it had been destroyed and local people had farmed the sacrificial lands for nearly a century. Sihui recovered those fields and petitioned to establish the Mount Ni Academy as an official school, which the court approved. The Three Clans School once held three thousand mu of land seized by powerful families, and the Zisi Academy had ten thousand strings of operating funds lent out at interest to finance sacrifices. In time the borrowers paid neither interest nor principal. Sihui investigated every case and recovered the assets. Confucius's father had long held the title Duke of Qi. Sihui said to the court, "The Exalted Sage has been enfeoffed as king, yet his father's rank remains that of duke. I ask that greater honor be granted." An edict then raised Confucius's father to King of Initiating the Sage and his mother to Lady of the Royal House.
18
五季時，孔末之後方盛，欲以偽滅真，害宣聖子孫幾盡，至是，其裔复欲冒稱宣聖後。 思晦以為：「不早辨則真偽久益不可明，彼與我不共戴天，乃列於族，與共拜殿庭，可乎？ 」遂會族人，稽典故斥之，既又重刻宗譜於石，而孔氏族裔益明矣。 元統元年卒，年六十七。 卒之日，有鶴百餘翔其屋上，又見神光自東南落其舍北。 至正中，朝廷加贈其官，而賜諡曰文肅。
During the Five Dynasties the descendants of Kong Mo had grown powerful and sought to supplant the true line, nearly wiping out Confucius's heirs. Now their descendants again tried to pass themselves off as heirs of the sage. Sihui said, "If we do not settle this early, truth and falsehood will only grow harder to tell apart. They are sworn enemies of our line. How can they be entered in the clan roll and worship with us in the temple hall?" He then assembled the clan, cited precedent to reject them, and had the genealogy carved anew in stone, so that the Kong lineage became still clearer. He died in the first year of Yuantong at the age of sixty-seven. On the day he died, more than a hundred cranes circled above his house, and a divine light was seen descending from the southeast to the north side of his dwelling. During the Zhizheng era the court added posthumous honors and gave him the posthumous name Wensu.
19
子曰克堅，襲封衍聖公，階嘉議大夫，既而進通奉大夫。 至正十五年，召為同知太常禮儀院事，拜陝西行臺侍御史，遷國子祭酒，擢山東肅政廉訪使，不赴。 孫希學，襲封衍聖公。
His son Kejian inherited the title Duke of Fulfilling the Sage at the rank of Grand Master of Court Discussion and was later promoted to Grand Master for Imperial Audience. In the fifteenth year of Zhizheng he was summoned as deputy director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, made attendant censor on the Shaanxi Branch Secretariat, transferred to chancellor of the Directorate of Education, and promoted to surveillance commissioner of Shandong, but he declined the final appointment. His grandson Xixue inherited the title Duke of Fulfilling the Sage.
