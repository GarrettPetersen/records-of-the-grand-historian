1
王庸，字伯常，雄州歸信人。 事母李氏以孝聞。 母有疾，庸夜禱北辰，至叩頭出血，母疾遂愈。 及母卒，哀毀幾絕，露處墓前，旦夕悲號。 一夕，雷雨暴至，鄰人持寢席往，欲蔽之，見庸所坐臥之地獨不沾濕，咸嘆異而去。 復有蜜蜂數十房，來止其家，歲得蜜蠟，以供祭祀。
Wang Yong, whose style was Bochang, came from Guixin in Xiongzhou. He cared for his mother, Lady Li, and his devotion earned wide renown. When his mother grew sick, Yong spent the nights praying to the Pole Star until his forehead bled from bowing; her illness then lifted. After his mother's death, grief and wasting nearly took his life; he kept vigil un sheltered before the grave, mourning from dawn to dusk. One night a sudden thunderstorm broke over him; neighbors brought bedding to cover him, yet the spot where Yong sat and slept alone stayed dry while everything around was soaked. They marveled and left. Bees also built several dozen hives at his home, and each year the wax and honey they yielded went to his ancestral rites.
2
黃贇，字止敬，臨江人。 父君道，延祐間求官京師，留贇江南。 時贇年幼，及既長，聞其父娶後妻居永平，乃往省之，則父歿已三年矣。 庶母聞贇來，盡挾其貲去，更嫁，拒不見贇。 贇號哭語人曰：「吾之來，為省吾父也。 今不幸吾父已歿，思奉其柩歸而窆之，莫知其墓。 苟得見庶母示以葬所，死不恨矣，尚忍利遺財邪！ 」久之，聞庶母居海濱，亟裹糧往。 庶母复拒之，三日不納。 庶母之弟憐之，與偕至永平屬縣樂亭求父墓，又弗得。 贇哭禱於神，一夕夢老父以杖指葬處曰：「見片磚即可得。 明日就其地求之，庶母之弟曰：「真是已，斂時有某物可驗。 」啟朽棺，得父骨以歸。
Huang Yun, styled Zhijing, came from Linjiang. His father Jundao went to the capital during the Yanyou period to seek an appointment, leaving Yun behind in the south. Yun was still a child then. When he came of age and learned that his father had remarried and was living at Yongping, he set out to visit him—only to find his father had been dead three years already. When the stepmother heard Yun had arrived, she seized everything of value, remarried elsewhere, and refused to see him. Yun wept aloud and told those around him: "I came here to see my father. Now, alas, he is gone. I long to bring his coffin home for burial, but I do not know where he lies. If only she would show me the grave, I could die content. How could I still covet whatever wealth he left behind! " After some time he learned she was living on the coast, packed food at once, and set out. She turned him away again and would not let him in for three days. Her younger brother took pity on him and went with him to Leting, a county under Yongping, to search for the grave, but they still could not find it. Yun wept and prayed to the spirits. One night he dreamed of his aged father pointing with a staff at a burial spot and saying: "When you see a broken tile, you will have found it. The next day he searched there. Her brother said: "This must be it—there was a particular object placed in the coffin when he was buried, by which we can be sure. " They opened the rotted coffin, took up his father's bones, and carried them home.
3
石明三者，與母居餘姚山中。 一日明三自外歸，覓母不見，見壁穿而臥內有三虎子，知母為虎所害。 乃盡殺虎子，礪巨斧立壁側，伺母虎至，斫其腦裂而死。 復往倚岩石傍，執斧伺候，斫殺牡虎。 明三亦立死不僕，張目如生，所執斧牢不可拔。
Shi Mingsan and his mother lived in the hills of Yuyao. One day he came back from outside and could not find her. He saw a breach in the wall and, inside the lair, three cubs—then knew a tiger had killed his mother. He killed every cub, sharpened a heavy axe, and stood by the wall until the mother tiger returned; he split its skull and killed it. He went back to wait beside the rocks, axe in hand, and struck down the male tiger as well. Mingsan too stood there dead without collapsing, eyes wide as in life, and the axe in his grip could not be pulled loose.
4
劉琦，岳州臨湘人。 生二歲而母劉氏遭亂陷於兵，琦獨事其父。 稍長，思其母不置，常歎曰：「人皆有母，而我獨無！ 」輒歔欷泣下。 及冠，請於父，往求其母。 遍歷河之南北、淮之東西，數歲不得。 後求得於池州之貴池，迎以歸養。 其後十五年而父歿，又三年而母歿，終喪猶蔬食。 有司上其事，旌表其門曰「孝義」。
Liu Qi came from Linxiang in Yuezhou. When he was two, his mother, Lady Liu, was swept up in the warfare; Qi grew up attending only his father. As he grew, he could not put his mother out of mind and would often sigh: "Every man has a mother—why do I alone have none! Then he would sigh and shed tears. When he reached manhood he asked his father's leave to go find her. He searched north and south of the Yellow River and east and west along the Huai for years without success. He finally found her at Guichi in Chizhou and brought her home to care for her. Fifteen years later his father died; three years after that his mother died. He kept to a plain vegetarian diet through the full mourning period. Officials reported his conduct to the court, which honored his household with the inscription "Filial and Righteous."
5
劉源，歸德中牟人。 母吳氏，年七十餘，病甚不能行。 適兵火起，且延至其家，鄰里俱逃，源力不能救，乃呼天號泣，趨入抱母，為火所焚而死。
Liu Yuan came from Zhongmou in Guide Prefecture. His mother, Lady Wu, was past seventy, gravely ill, and unable to walk. War fires broke out and soon reached their door; neighbors all fled. Yuan could not rescue her, so he cried to Heaven, ran in, and embraced his mother—and perished in the flames with her.
6
祝公榮，字大昌，處州麗水人。 隱居養親，事母甚孝。 母歿，居喪盡禮。 灶突失火，公榮力不能救，乃伏棺悲哭，其火自滅，鄉里異之。 塑二親像於堂，朝夕事之如事生焉。
Zhu Gongrong, styled Dachang, came from Lishui in Chuzhou. He lived in retirement to support his parents and served his mother with exceptional devotion. When she died, he observed the mourning rites to the letter. A fire broke out at the kitchen chimney; unable to fight it, he lay on the coffin and wept until the flames died away on their own. The whole village was astonished. He fashioned statues of both parents in the hall and tended them morning and evening as though they were still alive.
7
陸思孝，紹興山陰樵者，性至孝。 母老病痢，思孝醫禱久之，不效。 思孝方欲刲股肉為糜以進，忽夢寐間怳若有神人者授以藥劑，思孝得而異之，即以奉母，其疾遂愈。
Lu Sixiao was a woodcutter from Shanyin in Shaoxing, a man of the deepest filial feeling. His aged mother suffered from dysentery. Sixiao sought physicians and prayed for a long time, but nothing helped. He was about to cut flesh from his thigh to cook gruel for her when, in a dream, a figure like a god handed him a dose of medicine. He gave it to his mother at once, and she recovered.
8
姜兼，嚴州淳安人。 七歲而孤，與二兄養母至孝。 母死，兼哀慕幾絕。 既葬，獨居墓下，朝夕哭奠，寂焉荒山中，躬自樵爨，蔬食飲水，一衰麻寒暑不易。 同里陳氏、戴氏子不能事其父母，聞兼之行，慚感而悔，皆迎養焉。
Jiang Jian came from Chun'an in Yanzhou. Orphaned at seven, he and his two elder brothers raised their mother with devoted care. When she died, Jian's grief nearly destroyed him. After the burial he lived alone at the grave, mourning morning and night on a lonely mountain slope. He gathered his own firewood, cooked for himself, ate only vegetables and drank only water, and wore the same hemp mourning garments through winter and summer alike. Young men of the Chen and Dai families nearby had neglected their parents; when they heard of Jian's example they were ashamed, repented, and each brought his parents home to care for them.
9
胡伴侶，鈞州密縣人。 其父實嘗患心疾數月，幾死，更數醫俱莫能療。 伴侶乃齋沐焚香，泣告於天，以所佩小刀於右脅傍刲其皮膚，割脂一片，煎藥以進，父疾遂瘳，其傷亦旋愈。 朝廷旌表其門。
Hu Banlü came from Mi County in Junzhou. His father Shi had suffered a heart ailment for months and was near death; one physician after another failed to cure him. Banlü bathed, burned incense, and wept to Heaven, then took the small knife at his belt, cut the skin beside his right ribs, sliced out a piece of flesh, boiled it into medicine, and gave it to his father, who recovered. His own wound healed quickly as well. The court commended him and marked his gate with an honorific plaque.
10
王士弘，延安中部人。 父摶有疾，士弘傾家貲求醫，見醫即拜，遍禱諸神，叩額成瘡。 父歿，哀毀盡禮，廬墓三年，足未嘗至家。 墓廬上有奇鵲來巢，飛鳥翔集，與士弘親近，若相狎然，眾咸異之。 終喪，復建祠於塋前，朔望必往奠祭，雖風雨不廢也。 有司上其事於朝，旌表之。
Wang Shihong came from Zhongbu in Yan'an. When his father Tuohuo fell ill, Shihong spent the family fortune on doctors, bowing to every physician he met and praying to every god until his forehead was raw with sores from prostration. After his father's death he observed mourning in full and kept a hut by the grave for three years, never once setting foot inside his house. A remarkable magpie nested on the mourning hut; birds flew down and lingered close to Shihong as if tame, and onlookers were amazed. When the mourning period ended he built a shrine before the mound and, on the first and fifteenth of every month, went to offer sacrifice without fail, even in storm and rain. Local officials reported his conduct, and the court honored him.
11
何從義，延安洛川人。 祖良、祖母李氏偕亡，從義廬於墓側，旦夕哀慕，不脫絰帶，不食菜果，惟啖疏食而已。 事父世榮、母王氏，孝養尤至。 伯祖溫、伯祖母郝氏，叔祖恭、叔祖母賀氏，叔祖讓、叔祖母姜氏，叔父珍、叔母光氏，皆無子。 比其亡也，從義咸為治葬，築高墳，祭奠以禮，時人義之。
He Congyi came from Luochuan in Yan'an. When his grandfather Liang and grandmother Lady Li died together, Congyi kept a hut by their grave, grieving from dawn to dusk, never removing his mourning sash, eating no vegetables or fruit—only plain grain. He supported his father Shirong and his mother Lady Wang with still greater devotion. His great-uncle Wen and great-aunt Lady Hao, uncles Gong and Rang with their wives Ladies He and Jiang, and uncle Zhen and aunt Lady Guang—all were childless. When each of them died, Congyi arranged the funeral, raised a high mound, and performed the rites of sacrifice. People praised his sense of duty.
12
哈都赤，大都固安州人。 天性篤孝。 幼孤，養母，母嘗有疾，醫治不痊，哈都赤礪其所佩小刀，拜天泣曰：「慈母生我劬勞，今當捐身報之。 」乃割開左脅，取肉一片，作羹進母，母曰：「此何肉也？ 其甘如是！ 」數日而病癒。
Haduochi came from Gu'an Prefecture in the Dadu region. He was profoundly filial by nature. Orphaned early, he raised his mother. When she fell ill and doctors could not cure her, Haduochi sharpened the knife at his belt, bowed to Heaven, and wept: "My mother bore me with endless labor; now I must repay her with my own flesh. " He cut open his left side, took a slice of flesh, and cooked it into soup for her. His mother asked: "What meat is this? It tastes so sweet! " Within days she was well again.
13
高必達，建昌人。 五歲時，父明大忽棄家遠遊，莫知所適。 必達既長，晝夜哀慕，乃娶妻以養母，而歷往四方求其父。 十餘年不得見，心愈悲。 忽相傳黃州全真道院中有虛明子者，學道三十年矣，本姓高氏，建昌人也，匿姓名為道人云。 必達詢問，知為父，即往拜之，具言家世，及己之所生歲月，大父母之喪葬始末，因哀號叩頭不已。 虛明猶瞑坐不顧，久之，斥曰：「我非汝父，不去何為？ 」必達留侍左右不少懈，辭氣哀惻可矜。 其徒謂虛明曰：「師有子如此，忍弗歸乎？ 」虛明不得已，乃還家。 必達孝養篤至，鄉里稱之。
Gao Bida came from Jianchang. When he was five, his father Mingda suddenly left home and wandered far away; no one knew where he had gone. As he grew, he mourned his father day and night, took a wife to care for his mother, and traveled in every direction searching for him. More than ten years passed without a sight of him, and his sorrow only deepened. Word spread that at the Quanzhen monastery in Huangzhou there lived a recluse called Master Illuminated Void who had cultivated the Way for thirty years—originally a Gao from Jianchang, now hiding his identity as a Daoist. Bida made inquiries, recognized his father, went at once to bow before him, and recited the family history, his own birth date, and every detail of his grandparents' deaths and burials, weeping and kowtowing without cease. Illuminated Void kept his eyes closed and ignored him. At last he snapped: "I am not your father. Why will you not go? " Bida stayed at his side without rest, his pleas so pitiful they moved everyone who heard. His disciples said: "Master, you have a son like this—how can you refuse to go home? " At last Illuminated Void yielded and returned with him. Bida cared for him with unstinting devotion, and the neighborhood praised them both.
14
曾德，漁陽人，宗聖公五十七代孫。 母早亡，父仲祥再娶左氏。 仲祥遊襄陽，樂其土俗，因攜左氏家焉。 亂兵陷襄陽，遂失左氏。 德遍往南土求之，五年乃得於廣海間，奉迎以歸，孝養甚至。 有司以聞，詔旌復其家。
Zeng De came from Yuyang and was of the fifty-seventh generation after the Duke of the Sage. His mother died young; his father Zhongxiang married again, taking Lady Zuo. Zhongxiang went to Xiangyang, took to the place, and moved Lady Zuo's household there with him. When rebel troops overran the city, Lady Zuo was lost in the chaos. De searched the south for five years, found her along the Guang coast, brought her home with honor, and cared for her with the utmost devotion. Officials reported the matter, and an imperial edict commended and restored his household.
15
靳昺，字克昌，絳州曲沃人。 兄榮為奎章閣承製學士，奉母王氏官於朝。 母歿，昺與兄榮護喪還家。 至平定，大雷雨，流水驟至，昺伏柩上，榮呼之避水，昺不忍捨去，遂為水所漂沒。 後得王氏柩於三里外，得昺屍於五里外。 詔賜《孝子靳昺碑》。
Jin Bing, styled Kechang, came from Quwo in Jiangzhou. His elder brother Rong served as academician-attendant of the Kuizhang Pavilion and kept their mother Lady Wang with him at court. When she died, Bing and Rong escorted her coffin home. At Pingding a thunderstorm broke and a flash flood rushed in. Bing lay across the coffin; Rong shouted for him to flee, but Bing would not leave his mother and was swept away and drowned. Her coffin was recovered three li downstream; Bing's body was found five li away. The court granted him the inscription 《Stele of the Filial Son Jin Bing》.
16
黃道賢，泉州人。 嫡母唐無子，道賢在襁褓而生母蘇以疾去。 既長，思念生母，屢請於父，得召之歸。 道賢竭力養二母，得其歡心。 父病篤，道賢晝夜奉湯藥，不離膝下，遍求良醫，莫效。 乃夜禱於天，願減己一紀之算，以益父壽，其父遂愈。 至元統二年乃歿，果符一紀之數。 道賢居喪盡禮，負土築墳，廬於墓側，疏食終制。 至元二年，有司上其事，旌其門曰「孝子黃氏之門」。
Huang Daoxian came from Quanzhou. His father's principal wife, Lady Tang, had no children of her own; while Daoxian was still in swaddling clothes his birth mother Lady Su left the household because of illness. When he grew up, longing for Lady Su, he begged his father again and again until she was brought back. He devoted himself to both mothers and won their affection. When his father fell gravely ill, Daoxian tended him day and night at his bedside, bringing broth and medicine, and sought physicians far and wide, but none could help. He prayed to Heaven that his own life might be shortened by one twelve-year cycle in exchange for his father's, and his father recovered. He died in the second year of the Zhiyuantong reign—exactly one cycle later, as he had vowed. Daoxian mourned by the book, piled earth on the grave, kept a hut at the tomb, and ate only plain food until the mourning term ended. In the second year of Zhiyuan, officials reported his conduct and marked his gate "Gate of the Filial Son of the Huang Clan."
17
史彥斌，邳州人。 嗜學，有孝行。 至正十四年，河溢，金鄉、魚台墳墓多壞。 彥斌母卒，慮有後患，乃為厚棺，刻銘曰「邳州沙河店史彥斌母柩」，仍以四鐵環釘其上，然後葬。 明年，墓果為水所漂，彥斌縛草為人，置水中，仰天呼曰：「母棺被水，不知其處，願天矜憐哀子之心，假此芻靈，指示母棺。 」言訖，涕泣橫流，乃乘舟隨草人所之。 經十餘日，行三百餘里，草人止桑林中，視之，母柩在焉，載歸复葬之。
Shi Yanbin came from Pizhou. He was devoted to study and renowned for filial conduct. In Zhizheng 14 the Yellow River burst its banks, and graves throughout Jinxiang and Yutai were washed away. After his mother died, fearing what the flood might do, Yanbin commissioned a heavy coffin, had it inscribed "Coffin of the Mother of Shi Yanbin of Shahedian, Pizhou," nailed four iron rings to the lid, and buried her. The following year the grave was carried off by the flood. Yanbin fashioned a straw figure, set it afloat, and cried to Heaven: "My mother's coffin is lost in the waters. Pity a son's grief, and let this straw proxy show me where she lies. " Weeping, he boarded a boat and followed the figure downstream. For more than ten days he traveled over three hundred li until the figure halted in a mulberry grove—and there was his mother's coffin. He brought it home and buried her again.
18
張紹祖，字子讓，潁州人。 讀書力學，以孝行聞於朝，特授河南路儒學教授。 至正十五年，奉父避兵山間，賊至，執其父將殺之，紹祖泣曰：「吾父耆德善人，不當害，請殺我以代父死。 且若等非父母所生乎，何忍害人父也！ 」賊怒，以戈擊之，戈應手挫鈍，因感而相謂曰：「此真孝子，不可害。 」乃釋之。
Zhang Shaozu, styled Zirang, came from Yingzhou. A serious scholar known for filial piety, he was specially appointed professor at the Henan Circuit Confucian school. In Zhizheng 15 he took his father into the hills to escape the fighting. When bandits seized his father and raised their blades, Shaozu wept: "My father is an old man of virtue. Do not harm him—kill me instead. Were you not born of parents yourselves? How can you bear to kill another man's father! " The bandits struck him with a spear, but the blade turned blunt in their hands. Moved, they said to one another: "This is a true filial son—we must not harm him. They released them.
19
李明德，瑞州路上高縣人。 讀書有志操，孝行篤至。 至正十四年，亂兵陷袁州，因抄掠上高。 兵執其父欲殺之，明德泣告曰：「子豈不能代父乎，願勿害吾父也！ 」兵遂殺明德，而免其父，後以高壽終。
Li Mingde came from Shanggao in Ruizhou Circuit. He was a man of learning and principle, whose filial devotion was unwavering. In Zhizheng 14 rebel troops took Yuanzhou and swept through Shanggao. They seized his father to kill him. Mingde wept and pleaded: "Let me die in his place—spare my father! " The soldiers killed Mingde and spared his father, who lived to a ripe old age.
20
張緝，字士明，益都膠州人。 性孝友，能詩文。 至正七年，與兄紳、弟經同領鄉薦，由澤州儒學正轉泰州幕職，棄之，養親居揚州。 十五年，揚州亂，緝母姬氏方臥病，賊突入臥內，舉槍欲刺姬，緝以身蔽姬，槍中緝脅，三日而死。
Zhang Ji, styled Shiming, came from Jiaozhou in Yidu. He was filial toward his parents and devoted to his brothers, and wrote poetry and prose. In Zhizheng 7 he and his brothers Shen and Jing passed the provincial examination together. He served as director of the Zezhou school, then took a post on the Taizhou staff—but gave it up to care for his parents in Yangzhou. In the fifteenth year Yangzhou erupted in violence. His mother Lady Ji lay ill in bed when bandits burst in and leveled a spear at her. Ji threw himself across her body; the spear pierced his ribs, and he died three days later.
21
魏敬益，字士友，雄州容城人。 性至孝，居母喪，哀毀骨立。 素好施與​​，有男女失時者，出貲財為之嫁娶； 歲凶，老弱之飢者，為糜以食之。 敬益有田僅十六頃，一日語其子曰：「自吾買四莊村之田十頃，環其村之民皆不能自給，吾深憫焉。 今將以田歸其人，汝謹守餘田，可無餒也。 」乃呼四莊村民諭之曰：「吾買若等業，使若等貧不聊生，有親無以養，吾之不仁甚​​矣，請以田歸若等。 」眾聞，皆愕眙不敢受，強與之，乃受而言諸有司。 有司以聞於中書，請加旌表。 丞相賀太平歎曰：「世乃有斯人哉！」
Wei Jingyi, styled Shiyou, came from Rongcheng in Xiongzhou. Deeply filial by nature, he mourned his mother until grief had worn him to skin and bone. He was generous by habit: when men or women had no means to marry, he supplied the dowry or bride-price; in famine years he cooked gruel for the elderly and the weak who had nothing to eat. Jingyi owned only sixteen qing of land. One day he told his sons: "When I bought the ten qing at Sizhuang Village, the farmers there could no longer make a living. I have pitied them ever since. Today I mean to give those fields back. Guard the rest carefully and you will not want for food. " He summoned the villagers and said: "I bought your land and left you destitute, unable even to support your parents. That was a grave wrong. Take the fields back. " They were astonished and refused at first, but he pressed them until they accepted, then reported the gift to the authorities. Officials forwarded the matter to the Central Secretariat, asking that he be specially honored. Chief Councillor He Taiding exclaimed: "That such a man exists in our age!"

湯霖，字伯雨，龍興新建人。 早喪父，事母至孝。 母嘗病熱，更數醫弗能效。 母不肯飲藥，曰：「惟得冰，我疾乃可愈。 」爾時天氣甚燠，霖求冰不得，累日號哭於池上。 忽聞池中戛戛有聲，拭淚視之，乃冰澌也。 亟取以奉母，其疾果愈。
Tang Lin, styled Boyu, came from Xinjian in Longxing. His father died while he was young; he cared for his mother with the deepest devotion. When she fell ill with fever, physician after physician failed to help. She would not take their prescriptions and said: "Only ice can cure me. " It was the height of summer. Lin searched in vain for ice and for days wept beside a pond. Suddenly he heard ice cracking in the pond. He wiped his eyes—and saw ice forming. He gathered it at once for his mother, and she recovered.
23
孫抑，字希武，世居晉寧洪洞縣。 抑登進士第，歷仕至刑部郎中。 關保之變，挈父母妻子避兵平陽之柏村。 有亂兵至村剽掠，拔白刃嚇抑母，求財不得，舉刃欲斫之。 抑亟以身蔽母，請代受斫，母乃得釋。 而抑父被虜去，不知所之。 或語之曰：「汝父被驅而東矣，然東軍得所掠民皆殺之，汝慎無往就死也。 」抑曰：「吾可畏死而棄吾父乎？ 」遂往，出入死地，屢瀕危殆，卒得父以歸。
Sun Yi, styled Xiwu, came from a family long settled in Hongdong, Jinning. He passed the jinshi examination and rose to director in the Ministry of Justice. When the Guanbao uprising broke out, he fled with his parents, wife, and children to Baicun in Pingyang. Rebel troops raided the village. They brandished swords at Yi's mother, demanded money, found none, and raised a blade to strike her down. Yi threw himself in front of her and begged to take the blow himself. His mother was spared. His father, however, was seized and carried off—no one knew where. Someone warned him: "They drove your father east. The eastern army kills every captive it takes—do not throw your life away. " Yi replied: "How can I fear death and abandon my father? " He set out, passed again and again through mortal danger, and at last brought his father home.

石永，紹興新昌人。 性淳厚，事親至孝。 值亂兵掠鄉里，永父謙孫年八十，老不能行，永負父匿山谷中。 亂兵執其父，欲殺之，永亟前抱父請以身代，兵遂殺永而釋其父。
Shi Yong came from Xinchang in Shaoxing. Simple and steadfast by nature, he served his parents with complete devotion. When bandits swept the countryside, Yong's father Qiansun was eighty and could not walk. Yong carried him into the hills to hide. They seized the old man to kill him. Yong rushed forward, embraced his father, and begged to die in his place. The soldiers killed Yong and let his father go.

王克己，延安中部人。 父伯通歿，克己負土築墳，廬於墓側。 貊高縱兵暴掠，縣民皆逃竄，克己獨守墓不去。 家人呼之避兵，克己曰：「吾誓守墓三年，以報吾親，雖死不可棄也。 」遂不去。 俄而兵至，見其身衣衰絰，形容憔悴，曰：「此孝子也！ 」遂不忍害，竟終喪而歸。
Wang Keji came from Zhongbu in Yan'an. After his father Botong died, Keji piled earth on the grave and kept a mourning hut beside it. When Mo Gao's troops ravaged the district, everyone fled—yet Keji alone stayed at the tomb. His kin urged him to run. Keji said: "I vowed to keep vigil at this grave for three years to repay my parents. I will not leave, even if it costs my life. He would not leave. Soon the soldiers came. Seeing him in hemp mourning clothes, wasted and worn, they said: "This is a filial son! " They would not harm him. He finished the mourning period and went home.

劉思敬，延安宜君人。 事其繼母沙氏、杜氏，孝養之至，無異親母。 父年八十，兩目俱喪明，會亂兵剽掠其鄉，思敬負父避於巖穴中。 有兵至，欲殺思敬，思敬泣言曰：「我父老矣，又無目，我死不足惜，使我父何依乎？ 」兵憐其孝，不忍殺，父子皆免於難。
Liu Sijing came from Yijun in Yan'an. He cared for his stepmothers Lady Sha and Lady Du as devotedly as if they were his own mothers. His father was eighty and blind when rebel troops overran the countryside. Sijing carried him into a cave in the cliffs. Soldiers came to kill him. Sijing wept: "My father is old and sightless. My death means little—but what would become of him? " They pitied his devotion and spared them both.
27
呂祐，字伯通，晉安人。 至正二十六年，郡城破，有卒入其室，拔白刃脅其母林氏索財寶不得，揮刃欲斫母。 祐急以身蔽母，而奪其刃，手指盡裂，被傷僕地。 良久而蘇，開目視母曰：「母幸無恙，我死無憾矣。 」遂瞑目死。
Lü You, styled Botong, came from Jin'an. In Zhizheng 26 the prefectural city fell. A soldier entered his home, drew a sword, and threatened his mother Lady Lin for valuables. Finding none, he raised the blade to strike her. You threw himself between them, wrested the blade away, and had every finger split open before he collapsed, wounded. After a long while he came to and, opening his eyes, looked at his mother: "You are safe—that is enough. I die content. He closed his eyes and died.

周樂，溫州瑞安人。 宋狀元坦之後，父日成，通經能文。 海賊竊據溫州，拘日成置海舟上，樂隨往，事其父甚謹。 一日賊酋遣人沉日成於水，樂泣請曰：「我有祖母，幸留父侍養，請以己代父死。 」不聽，樂抱父不忍舍，遂同死焉。
Zhou Le came from Ruian in Wenzhou. He was descended from the Song dynasty top graduate Zhou Tan. His father Richeng was a classical scholar and a capable writer. Pirates seized Wenzhou, took Richeng aboard a ship at sea, and Le went with him, tending his father with meticulous care. One day the chief ordered Richeng thrown overboard. Le wept and begged: "I still have a grandmother at home. Let my father live to care for her—take my life instead. " They refused. Le clung to his father and would not let go, and both drowned.
