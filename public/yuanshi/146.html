1
耶律楚材子鑄附
Appended biography: Yelü Zhu, son of Yelü Chucai.
2
耶律楚材，字晉卿，遼東丹王突欲八世孫。 父履，以學行事金世宗，特見親任，終尚書右丞。 楚材生三歲而孤，母楊氏教之學。 及長，博極群書，旁通天文、地理、律曆、術數及釋老、醫卜之說，下筆為文，若宿構者。 金制，宰相子例試補省掾。 楚材欲試進士科，章宗詔如舊制。 問以疑獄數事，時同試者十七人，楚材所對獨優，遂辟為掾。 後仕為開州同知。 貞祐二年，宣宗遷汴，完顏福興行尚書事，留守燕，辟為左右司員外郎。 太祖定燕，聞其名，召見之。 楚材身長八尺，美髯宏聲。 帝偉之，曰：「遼、金世仇，朕為汝雪之。 」對曰：「臣父祖嘗委質事之，既為之臣，敢仇君耶！ 」帝重其言，處之左右，遂呼楚材曰吾圖撒合里而不名，吾圖撒合裡，蓋國語長髯人也。
Yelü Chucai, whose style was Jinqing, was an eighth-generation descendant of Tuyü, the Dongdan king of Liao. His father Lü won favor under Jin Emperor Shizong through learning and upright conduct, rose to unusual trust, and ended his career as right vice director of the Ministry of Works. Chucai lost his father at the age of three, and his mother, the Lady Yang, saw to his education. As he matured he mastered the classics and also astronomy, geography, calendrics, numerology, Buddhist and Daoist doctrine, medicine, and divination. When he wrote, the prose seemed already composed in his mind. Jin custom allowed sons of grand councilors to enter the secretariat by a special examination rather than the regular civil-service route. Chucai wanted to sit for the jinshi examination, and Emperor Zhangzong permitted him to follow the usual procedure. The examiners put several knotty legal cases to him. Among the seventeen candidates, only Chucai's answers stood out, and he was appointed a secretariat clerk. He later served as vice prefect of Kaizhou. In the second year of Zhenyou, when Emperor Xuanzong moved the court to Bian, Wanyan Fuxing stayed in Yan as acting director of the secretariat and recruited Chucai as an outer vice director of the left and right offices. After Taizu secured Yan, he heard of Chucai and summoned him to court. Chucai stood eight chi in height, with a splendid beard and a booming voice. The emperor was impressed and said, "Liao and Jin were hereditary enemies. I will avenge that wrong for you." Chucai answered, "My father and grandfather once pledged themselves to Jin. Once one has become a subject, how can one turn against one's lord?" The emperor respected this answer, kept him close, and thereafter addressed him as Uthu Sahalir instead of by name—Mongolian for "the long-bearded man."
3
己卯夏六月，帝西討回回國。 祃旗之日，雨雪三尺，帝疑之，楚材曰：「玄冥之氣，見於盛夏，克敵之徵也。 」庚辰冬，大雷，復問之，對曰：「回回國主當死於野。 」後皆驗。 夏人常八斤，以善造弓見知於帝，因每自矜曰：「國家方用武，耶律儒者何用。 」楚材曰：「治弓尚須用弓匠，為天下者豈可不用治天下匠耶？ 」帝聞之甚喜，日見親用。 西域歷人奏五月望夜月當蝕，楚材曰：「否。 」卒不蝕。 明年十月，楚材言月當蝕，西域人曰不蝕，至期果蝕八分。 壬午八月，長星見西方，楚材曰：「女直將易主矣。 」明年，金宣宗果死。 帝每征討，必命楚材卜，帝亦自灼羊胛，以相符應。 指楚材謂太宗曰：「此人天賜我家。 爾後軍國庶政，當悉委之。 」甲申，帝至東印度，駐鐵門關，有一角獸，形如鹿而馬尾，其色綠，作人言，謂侍衛者曰：「汝主宜早還。 」帝以問楚材，對曰：「此瑞獸也，其名角端，能言四方語，好生惡殺，此天降符以告陛下。 陛下天之元子，天下之人，皆陛下之子，願承天心，以全民命。 」帝即日班師。
In the sixth month of summer in the jimao year, the emperor marched west against the Muslim kingdoms. On the day the war banners were consecrated, snow piled three chi deep. When the emperor hesitated, Chucai said, "Yin force appearing in midsummer is a sign that the enemy will be overcome." In the winter of the gengchen year there was a great thunderclap. Asked again, Chucai said, "The Muslim king will die in the open country." Both predictions later came true. Chang Bajin, a man of Xia famed for bow-making, often boasted before the emperor, "The realm is at war—what use is a Khitan scholar?" Chucai replied, "Even bow-making needs a bow-maker. How can the ruler of all under heaven do without craftsmen who know how to govern?" The emperor was delighted and relied on him more closely every day. A calendar master from the Western Regions said the moon would eclipse on the night of the fifth-month full moon. Chucai said it would not. No eclipse occurred. The next year, in the tenth month, Chucai predicted an eclipse while the Westerners denied it; on the appointed day the moon was indeed eclipsed eight-tenths. In the eighth month of the renwu year a comet with a long tail appeared in the west. Chucai said, "The Jurchen throne is about to change hands." The following year Jin Emperor Xuanzong did in fact die. On every campaign the emperor had Chucai cast auguries, while he himself scorched sheep shoulder-blades to see whether the signs agreed. He pointed to Chucai and told Taizong, "Heaven has given this man to our house." After I am gone, you should entrust all military and civil affairs to him." In the jiashen year the emperor reached eastern India and camped at Iron Gate Pass. A horned beast shaped like a deer with a horse's tail and green in color spoke to the guards in human language: "Your lord should return soon." The emperor asked Chucai, who answered, "This is an auspicious beast called Jiaoduan. It speaks the languages of the four quarters, loves life and hates slaughter. Heaven has sent this sign to warn Your Majesty." Your Majesty is Heaven's eldest son, and all people under heaven are your children. I beg you to heed Heaven's will and spare the lives of the people." That very day the emperor turned the army homeward.
4
丙戌冬，從下靈武，諸將爭取子女金帛，楚材獨收遺書及大黃藥材。 既而士卒病疫，得大黃輒愈。 帝自經營西土，未暇定制，州郡長吏，生殺任情，至孥人妻女，取貨財，兼土田。 燕薊留後長官石抹咸得卜尤貪暴，殺人盈市。 楚材聞之泣下，即入奏，請禁州郡，非奉璽書，不得擅徵發，囚當大辟者必待報，違者罪死，於是貪暴之風稍戢。 燕多劇賊，未夕，輒曳牛車指富家，取其財物，不與則殺之。 時睿宗以皇子監國，事聞，遣中使偕楚材往窮治之。 楚材詢察得其姓名，皆留後親屬及勢家子，盡捕下獄。 其家賂中使，將緩之，楚材示以禍福，中使懼，從其言，獄具，戮十六人於市，燕民始安。
In the winter of the bingxu year, after the fall of Lingwu, the generals scrambled for women, children, gold, and silk while Chucai alone gathered abandoned books and stores of rhubarb. Soon afterward the troops fell sick with plague, and those who received rhubarb recovered at once. While the emperor was still securing the western territories he had no time to set laws in place. Prefectural officials killed or spared as they pleased, seized wives and daughters, plundered goods, and seized fields at will. Shimo Xiandebi, the acting commander of Yan and Ji, was especially greedy and brutal; corpses filled the markets. Chucai wept when he heard of it and immediately memorialized the throne. No prefecture might levy troops or execute capital sentences without an imperial sealed order; violators were to die. The worst abuses began to subside. Yan swarmed with violent robbers who, before nightfall, would drive ox-carts to rich houses, seize their goods, and kill anyone who resisted. Prince Ruizong was then regent. When the case came to his attention he sent a palace envoy with Chucai to investigate to the end. Chucai traced the culprits and found them all to be relatives of the acting commander or sons of powerful families. He had every one of them arrested. Their families bribed the palace envoy, who was ready to soften the case. Chucai showed him what fortune and ruin would follow. The envoy took fright, followed Chucai's advice, and when the trial ended sixteen men were executed in the market. Only then did the people of Yan feel safe.
5
中原甫定，民多誤觸禁網，而國法無赦令。 楚材議請肆宥，眾以為迂，楚材獨從容為帝言。 詔自庚寅正月朔日前事勿治。 且條便宜一十八事頒天下，其略言：「郡宜置長吏牧民，設萬戶總軍，使勢均力敵，以遏驕橫。 中原之地，財用所出，宜存卹其民，州縣非奉上命，敢擅行科差者罪之。 貿易借貸官物者罪之。 蒙古、回鶻、河西諸人，種地不納稅者死。 監主自盜官物者死。 應犯死罪者，具由申奏待報，然後行刑。 貢獻禮物，為害非輕，深宜禁斷。 」帝悉從之，唯貢獻一事不允，曰：「彼自願饋獻者，宜聽之。 」楚材曰：「蠹害之端，必由於此。 」帝曰：「凡卿所奏，無不從者，卿不能從朕一事耶？」
The Central Plains had only just been secured, and many people stumbled into the penal code by mistake, yet the state law allowed no general pardon. Chucai urged a general amnesty. Others called the idea impractical, but Chucai alone spoke to the emperor calmly and at length. An edict declared that crimes committed before the first day of the first month of the gengyin year would not be prosecuted. He also drafted eighteen administrative measures for promulgation nationwide. In brief they held that each commandery should have a civil magistrate to govern the people and a ten-thousand-household commander for military affairs, with their powers balanced so that arrogance could be checked. The Central Plains are the source of state revenue; the people there must be protected. Any prefecture or district that levies exactions without imperial command is to be punished. Trading in or borrowing government property on loan is to be punished. Any Mongol, Uighur, or Hexi cultivator who farms land but refuses taxes is to be put to death. Any supervisor who steals government property is to be put to death. Capital cases must be reported in full to the throne and may be carried out only after imperial approval. The custom of presenting tribute-gifts does grave harm and ought to be strictly forbidden. The emperor accepted everything except the ban on tribute-gifts. "If people wish to offer gifts of their own accord," he said, "they should be allowed." Chucai said, "This is where corruption always begins." The emperor said, "I have agreed to everything you proposed. Can you not yield to me on this one point?"
6
太祖之世，歲有事西域，未暇經理中原，官吏多聚斂自私，貲至巨萬，而官無儲彳侍。 近臣別迭等言：「漢人無補於國，可悉空其人以為牧地。 」楚材曰：「陛下將南伐，軍需宜有所資，誠均定中原地稅、商稅、鹽、酒、鐵冶、山澤之利，歲可得銀五十萬兩、帛八萬匹、粟四十餘萬石，足以供給，何謂無補哉？ 」帝曰：「卿試為朕行之。 」乃奏立燕京等十路徵收課稅使，凡長貳悉用士人，如陳時可、趙昉等，皆寬厚長者，極天下之選，參佐皆用省部舊人。 辛卯秋，帝至雲中，十路咸進廩籍及金帛陳於廷中，帝笑謂楚材曰：「汝不去朕左右，而能使國用充足，南國之臣，復有如卿者乎？ 「對曰：「在彼者皆賢於臣，臣不才，故留燕，為陛下用。 」帝嘉其謙，賜之酒。 即日拜中書令，事無鉅細，皆先白之。
Throughout Taizu's reign yearly campaigns in the west left no time to govern the Central Plains. Officials enriched themselves until their private fortunes reached millions, while the treasury had nothing in reserve. The intimate adviser Biedie and others said, "The Han are useless to the state. Drive them all out and turn the land into pasture." Chucai said, "Your Majesty is about to march south and will need supplies. If we regularize land tax, commercial tax, and revenues from salt, wine, ironworks, and mountain and marsh monopolies in the Central Plains, the state can draw five hundred thousand taels of silver, eighty thousand bolts of silk, and more than four hundred thousand shi of grain a year—enough for any campaign. How can the Han be called useless?" The emperor said, "Then put the scheme into practice for me." He memorialized to establish tax commissioners for ten circuits including Yanjing. Every chief and deputy post went to scholar-officials such as Chen Shike and Zhao Fang—men of generous and seasoned character, the best the realm could offer—with assistants drawn from former central-government staff. In the autumn of the xinmao year the emperor reached Yunzhong. All ten circuits presented granary registers together with gold and silk laid out in the hall. The emperor smiled at Chucai and said, "You never leave my side, yet you have filled the treasury. Are there any ministers in the southern states who can match you?" Chucai answered, "The men in the south are all more capable than I. I lacked talent, so I stayed in Yan to serve Your Majesty." The emperor praised his modesty and rewarded him with wine. That same day he was appointed director of the secretariat, and every matter, great or small, was referred to him first.
7
楚材奏：「凡州郡宜令長吏專理民事，萬戶總軍政，凡所掌課稅，權貴不得侵之。 」又舉鎮海、粘合，均與之同事，權貴不能平。 咸得卜以舊怨，尤疾之，譖於宗王曰：「耶律中書令率用親舊，必有二心，宜奏殺之。 」宗王遣使以聞，帝察其誣，責使者，罷遣之。 屬有訟咸得卜不法者，帝命楚材鞫之，奏曰：「此人倨傲，故易招謗。 今將有事南方，他日治之未晚也。 」帝私謂侍臣曰：「楚材不較私仇，真寬厚長者，汝曹當效之。 」中貴可思不花奏採金銀役夫及種田西域與栽蒲萄戶，帝令於西京宣德徙萬餘戶充之。 楚材曰：「先帝遺詔，山後民質樸，無異國人，緩急可用，不宜輕動。 今將徵河南，請無殘民以給此役。 」帝可其奏。
Chucai memorialized that in every prefecture the civil magistrate should handle civilian affairs alone, the ten-thousand-household commander military affairs alone, and that no powerful noble might seize the taxes under their charge. He also recommended Zhenhai and Nianhe to share power with him on equal footing, which the great families could not accept. Xiandebi, nursing an old grudge, hated him all the more and told a imperial prince, "The Khitan director of the secretariat fills his office with kin and friends. He must be disloyal. Memorializing for his death would be right." The prince sent an envoy to report this. The emperor saw the slander for what it was, rebuked the envoy, and sent him away. When a case was brought against Xiandebi for misconduct, the emperor ordered Chucai to try him. Chucai reported, "This man is arrogant and therefore invites slander." We are about to campaign in the south. Punishing him can wait until another day." The emperor told his attendants privately, "Chucai will not pursue a private grudge. He is truly a generous and mature man. You should take him as your model." The palace favorite Kesibuhua asked for laborers to mine gold and silver, farm the Western Regions, and plant vineyards. The emperor ordered more than ten thousand households moved from Xijing and Xuande to supply them. Chucai said, "The late emperor's testament warned that the people north of the Yan range are plain and loyal, no different from our own folk, and useful in any emergency. They should not be uprooted lightly." We are about to conquer Henan. I beg you not to ruin these people to supply this project." The emperor approved his request.
8
壬辰春，帝南征，將涉河，詔逃難之民，來降者免死。 或曰：「此輩急則降，緩則走，徒以資敵，不可宥。 」楚材請制旗數百，以給降民，使歸田裡，全活甚眾。 舊制，凡攻城邑，敵以矢石相加者，即為拒命，既克，必殺之。 汴梁將下，大將速不台遣使來言：「金人抗拒持久，師多死傷，城下之日，宜屠之。 」楚材馳入奏曰：「將士暴露數十年，所欲者土地人民耳。 得地無民，將焉用之！ 」帝猶豫未決，楚材曰：「奇巧之工，厚藏之家，皆萃於此，若盡殺之，將無所獲。 」帝然之，詔罪止完顏氏，餘皆勿問。 時避兵居汴者得百四十七萬人。 楚材又請遣人入城，求孔子後，得五十一代孫元措，奏襲封衍聖公，付以林廟地。 命收太常禮樂生，及召名儒梁陟、王萬慶、趙著等，使直釋九經，進講東宮。 又率大臣子孫，執經解義，俾知聖人之道。 置編修所於燕京、經籍所於平陽，由是文治興焉。
In the spring of the renchen year the emperor marched south. Before crossing the river he decreed that refugees who surrendered would be spared. Some argued, "They surrender when pressed and flee when the danger passes. They only feed the enemy and ought not to be spared." Chucai asked for several hundred safe-conduct banners for the surrendered people so they could return home. A great multitude was thus saved. By old custom any city that resisted with arrows and stones was treated as defiant, and once captured its people were always put to the sword. As Bianliang was about to fall, Subutai sent word: "The Jin held out so long that our army suffered heavily. When the city falls it should be slaughtered." Chucai rode in at once and said, "Your soldiers have campaigned in the open for decades. What they want is land and people." What use is land without people?" The emperor still hesitated. Chucai said, "The finest craftsmen and the wealthiest households in the realm are all here. If you kill everyone you will gain nothing." The emperor agreed and decreed that only the Wanyan clan would be punished; all others were to go unharmed. At that time 1,470,000 people who had taken refuge in Bian were spared. Chucai also sent men into the city to find a descendant of Confucius. They found Yuan Cuo, fifty-first in line, and memorialized that he inherit the title Duke Yansheng together with the temple estates. He gathered students of ritual music from the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and summoned eminent scholars such as Liang Zhi, Wang Wanqing, and Zhao Zhu to annotate the Nine Classics and lecture in the Eastern Palace. He also had the sons and grandsons of high ministers study the classics and expound their meaning so they might learn the way of the sages. He set up a compilation office in Yanjing and a classics repository in Pingyang, and from that point civil culture began to flourish.
9
時河南初破，俘獲甚眾，軍還，逃者十七八。 有旨：居停逃民及資給者，滅其家，鄉社亦連坐。 由是逃者莫敢舍，多殍死道路。 楚材從容進曰：「河南既平，民皆陛下赤子，走復何之！ 奈何因一俘囚，連死數十百人乎？ 」帝悟，命除其禁。 金之亡也，唯秦、鞏二十餘州久未下，楚材奏曰：「往年吾民逃罪，或萃於此，故以死拒戰，若許以不殺，將不攻自下矣。 」詔下，諸城皆降。 甲午，議籍中原民，大臣忽都虎等議，以丁為戶。 楚材曰：「不可。 丁逃，則賦無所出，當以戶定之。 」爭之再三，卒以戶定。 時將相大臣有所驅獲，往往寄留諸郡，楚材因括戶口，並令為民，匿佔者死。 乙未，朝議將四征不廷，若遣回回人徵江南，漢人征西域，深得制御之術，楚材曰：「不可。 中原、西域，相去遼遠，未至敵境，人馬疲乏，兼水土異宜，疾疫將生，宜各從其便。 」從之。
Henan had just fallen and captives were numerous, yet when the army marched home seven or eight in ten had escaped. An order went out that anyone who sheltered fugitives or aided them would have his household destroyed, and the local community would be punished as well. Fugitives dared accept no shelter, and many starved or died along the roads. Chucai spoke calmly: "Henan is already pacified. These people are all your children. Where else can they run?" Why should one escaped captive bring death to dozens or hundreds?" The emperor saw the point and revoked the order. As Jin was collapsing, more than twenty prefectures including Qin and Gong still held out. Chucai said, "In past years fugitives from our side gathered there and fought to the death because they feared slaughter. Promise them their lives and the cities will surrender without a fight." When the edict went out, every city submitted. In the jiawu year, when the court debated registering the people of the Central Plains, Huduhu and other ministers proposed using adult males as the unit of taxation. Chucai said, "That cannot be allowed." If you count only adult males, they will flee and revenue will vanish. Taxation must be based on households. He argued the point again and again until the court finally adopted the household unit. Generals and ministers often kept captives in various prefectures. Chucai ordered a census, registered them all as subjects, and decreed death for anyone who hid or seized people illegally. In the yiwei year the court debated campaigns against unsubmissive states. Some proposed sending Muslims against the Jiangnan and Han troops against the west, calling this a master stroke of control. Chucai said it would not work. The Central Plains and the Western Regions are immensely far apart. Troops would be exhausted before reaching the enemy, and different climates would breed plague. Each army should fight in its own region." The emperor agreed.
10
丙申春，諸王大集，帝親執觴賜楚材曰：「朕之所以推誠任卿者，先帝之命也。 非卿，則中原無今日。 朕所以得安枕者，卿之力也。 」西域諸國及宋、高麗使者來朝，語多不實，帝指楚材示之曰：「汝國有如此人乎？ 」皆謝曰：「無有。 殆神人也。 」帝曰：「汝等唯此言不妄，朕亦度必無此人。 」有於元者，奏行交鈔，楚材曰：「金章宗時初行交鈔，與錢通行，有司以出鈔為利，收鈔為諱，謂之老鈔，至以萬貫唯易一餅。 民力困竭，國用匱乏，當為鑑戒。 今印造交鈔，宜不過萬錠。 」從之。
In the spring of the bingshen year the princes assembled. The emperor personally raised a cup for Chucai and said, "I place my full trust in you because the late emperor commanded it." Without you the Central Plains would not be what they are today. It is through your efforts that I can sleep in peace. Envoys from the Western Regions, Song, and Goryeo came to court with speeches full of flattery. The emperor pointed to Chucai and asked, "Does your country have anyone like him?" They all bowed and said there was not. He is almost more than human. The emperor said, "That alone you speak truly. I too am sure your countries have no such man." Someone at court proposed issuing paper currency. Chucai said, "Jin introduced paper notes under Emperor Zhangzong. Officials profited by printing notes and refused to redeem them, calling them stale notes, until ten thousand strings bought only a single cake." The people were exhausted and the treasury empty. That history should be our warning. If notes are printed now, the issue should not exceed ten thousand ingots. The emperor agreed.
11
秋七月，忽都虎以民籍至，帝議裂州縣賜親王功臣。 楚材曰：「裂土分民，易生嫌隙，不如多以金帛與之。 」帝曰：「已許奈何？ 」楚材曰：「若朝廷置吏，收其貢賦，歲終頒之，使毋擅科徵，可也。 」帝然其計，遂定天下賦稅，每二戶出絲一斤，以給國用； 五戶出絲一斤，以給諸王功臣湯沐之資。 地稅，中田每畝二升又半，上田三升，下田二升，水田每畝五升； 商稅，三十分而一； 鹽價，銀一兩四十斤。 既定常賦，朝議以為太輕，楚材曰：「作法於涼，其弊猶貪，後將有以利進者，則今已重矣。 」時工匠製造，糜費官物，十私八九，楚材請皆考核之，以為定制。 時侍臣脫歡奏簡天下室女，詔下，楚材尼之不行，帝怒。 楚材進曰：「向擇美女二十有八人，足備使令。 今復選拔，臣恐擾民，欲覆奏耳。 」帝良久曰：「可罷之。 」又欲收民牝馬，楚材曰：「田蠶之地，非馬所產，今若行之，後必為人害。 」又從之。 丁酉，楚材奏曰：「制器者必用良工，守成者必用儒臣。 儒臣之事業，非積數十年，殆未易成也。 」帝曰：「果爾，可官其人。 」楚材曰：「請校試之。 」乃命宣德州宣課使劉中隨郡考試，以經義、詞賦、論分為三科，儒人被俘為奴者，亦令就試，其主匿弗遣者死。 得士凡四千三十人，免為奴者四之一。 先是，州郡長吏，多藉賈人銀以償官，息累數倍，曰羊羔兒利，至奴其妻子猶不足償。 楚材奏令本利相侔而止，永為定制，民間所負者，官為代價之。 至一衡量，給符印，立鈔法，定均輸，布遞傳，明驛券，庶政略備，民稍蘇息焉。
In the seventh month Huduhu delivered the household registers. The emperor discussed dividing prefectures among princes and meritorious officials. Chucai said, "Dividing land and people breeds suspicion. It would be better to reward them with gold and silk." The emperor said, "I have already promised. What can be done now?" Chucai said, "Let the court appoint officials to collect the taxes and distribute them at year's end, so the princes cannot levy on their own. That will suffice." The emperor approved and fixed taxes for the realm: every two households owed one jin of silk for state revenue; and every five households one jin of silk for the princes' and meritorious officials' maintenance allotments. Land tax was set at two and a half sheng per mu for medium fields, three for upper fields, two for lower fields, and five for paddy; commercial tax at one-thirtieth; and salt at forty jin per tael of silver. When the regular taxes were fixed, the court called them too light. Chucai said, "Even lenient laws breed greed. When later men press for profit, today's light burden will already seem heavy." Craftsmen were wasting government materials, keeping eight or nine parts in ten for themselves. Chucai ordered audits and made fixed regulations. The attendant Tuohuan proposed selecting maidens empire-wide. When the edict was issued Chucai blocked it, and the emperor grew angry. Chucai said, "Twenty-eight women have already been chosen, enough for the palace." Another selection would trouble the people. I only wished to report back for reconsideration. After a long pause the emperor said, "Let it be canceled." The emperor also wanted to seize the people's brood mares. Chucai said, "This is farming and sericulture country, not horse country. Do this now and later generations will suffer for it." Again the emperor agreed. In the dingyou year Chucai memorialized, "Making tools requires skilled craftsmen; preserving a realm requires Confucian ministers." Their work cannot be mastered in a few decades and is not easily done. The emperor said, "If that is so, then give them office." Chucai said, "Let them be examined first." He ordered Liu Zhong, tax commissioner of Xuande, to examine candidates in each commandery in three fields: classical interpretation, rhapsody, and discourse. Captured scholars enslaved were also required to sit for the exam; masters who hid them were put to death. Four thousand thirty scholars qualified, and one in four was freed from slavery. Prefectural officials had often borrowed silver from merchants to meet government quotas. Interest multiplied several times over in what was called "lamb-kid interest," until even selling wives and children into slavery could not repay the debt. Chucai ordered that interest cease once it equaled principal, made the rule permanent, and had the government pay private debts on the people's behalf. He standardized weights and measures, issued seals and tallies, established paper currency, regulated transport, set up courier posts, and clarified relay vouchers. Civil administration was largely in order, and the people began to recover.
12
有二道士爭長，互立黨與，其一誣其仇之黨二人為逃軍，結中貴及通事楊惟忠，執而虐殺之。 楚材按收惟忠。 中貴复訴楚材違制，帝怒，系楚材； 既而自悔，命釋之。 楚材不肯解縛，進曰：「臣備位公輔，國政所屬。 陛下初令系臣，以有罪也，當明示百官，罪在不赦。 今釋臣，是無罪也，豈宜輕易反覆，如戲小兒？ 國有大事，何以行焉！ 」眾皆失色。 帝曰：「朕雖為帝，寧無過舉耶？ 」乃溫言以慰之。 楚材因陳時務十策，曰：「信賞罰，正名分，給俸祿，官功臣，考殿最，均科差，選工匠，務農桑，定土貢，制漕運。 皆切於時務，悉施行之。
Two Daoist priests vied for precedence and formed factions. One accused two members of his rival's faction of desertion, enlisted a palace favorite and the interpreter Yang Weizhong, seized the men, and tortured them to death. Chucai investigated and arrested Weizhong. The palace favorite accused Chucai of violating regulations. The emperor was furious and had Chucai bound; but soon regretted it and ordered his release. Chucai refused to be unbound and said, "I hold the highest ministerial office. The state's affairs depend on me." When Your Majesty first bound me, it was because I was guilty. You should announce to the officials that my crime was unpardonable. To release me now is to declare me innocent. How can the throne turn so lightly back and forth, as if playing with a child? How can great affairs of state be conducted this way! Everyone in the hall turned pale. The emperor said, "Even an emperor can err, can he not?" He spoke gently to comfort him. Chucai then presented ten policies on state affairs: trust rewards and punishments, rectify ranks, pay salaries, appoint meritorious men, examine performance, equalize levies, select craftsmen, promote farming and sericulture, fix local tribute, and regulate grain transport. All were urgently relevant, and all were put into practice.
13
太原路轉運使呂振、副使劉子振，以贓抵罪。 帝責楚材曰：「卿言孔子之教可行，儒者為好人，何故乃有此輩？ 」對曰：「君父教臣子，亦不欲令陷不義。 三綱五常，聖人之名教，有國家者莫不由之，如天之有日月也。 豈得緣一夫之失，使萬世常行之道獨見廢於我朝乎！ 」帝意乃解。
Lü Zhen, transport commissioner of Taiyuan, and his deputy Liu Zizhen were punished for corruption. The emperor reproached Chucai: "You said Confucian teaching works and that scholars are good men. Why then do we have men like these?" Chucai answered, "Even fathers and rulers do not wish their sons and subjects to fall into wrongdoing." The Three Bonds and Five Constants are the sages' moral teaching. No state can do without them, any more than heaven can do without sun and moon. How can one man's failure justify abandoning in our dynasty alone the Way that all ages have followed! The emperor's anger subsided.
14
富人劉忽篤馬、涉獵發丁、劉廷玉等以銀一百四十萬兩撲買天下課稅，楚材曰：「此貪利之徒，罔上虐下，為害甚大。 」奏罷之。 常曰：「興一利不如除一害，生一事不如省一事。 任尚以班超之言為平平耳，千古之下，自有定論。 後之負譴者，方知吾言之不妄也。 」帝素嗜酒，日與大臣酣飲，楚材屢諫，不聽，乃持酒槽鐵口進曰：「曲蘗能腐物，鐵尚如此，況五臟乎！ 」帝悟，語近臣曰：「汝曹愛君憂國之心，豈有如吾圖撒合裡者耶？ 」賞以金帛，敕近臣日進酒三鐘而止。 自庚寅定課稅格，至甲午平河南，歲有增羨，至戊戌，課銀增至一百一十萬兩。 譯史安天合者，諂事鎮海，首引奧都剌合蠻撲買課稅，又增至二百二十萬兩。 楚材極力辨諫，至聲色俱厲，言與涕俱。 帝曰：「爾欲搏鬥耶？ 」又曰：「爾欲為百姓哭耶？ 姑令試行之。 」楚材力不能止，乃嘆息曰：「民之困窮，將自此始矣！」
The wealthy Liu Huduma, Shelie Fading, Liu Tingyu, and others offered 1,400,000 taels to monopolize tax collection empire-wide. Chucai said, "These greedy men deceive the throne and abuse the people. The harm would be immense." He memorialized and had the scheme stopped. He often said, "Creating one benefit is not as good as removing one harm; adding one task is not as good as cutting one task." Ren Shang thought Ban Chao's words merely ordinary, but after a thousand years history will judge them clearly. Later men who suffer for their warnings will know I spoke no falsehood. The emperor loved wine and drank heavily with his ministers every day. Chucai remonstrated repeatedly without success, then brought in the iron mouth of a wine vat and said, "Ferment can rot even iron. How much more the organs of the body!" The emperor understood and told his attendants, "Who among you loves his ruler and cares for the state as Uthu Sahalir does?" He rewarded him with gold and silk and ordered his attendants to limit his daily wine to three cups. From the gengyin year when tax rates were fixed until Henan was pacified in the jiawu year, revenue rose yearly. By the wuxu year tax silver reached 1,100,000 taels. The translator-clerk An Tianhe, who flattered Prince Zhenhai, introduced Odu Laheman to monopolize tax collection and raised the quota to 2,200,000 taels. Chucai argued against it with all his strength until his voice shook and tears ran with his words. The emperor said, "Do you want to fight me?" He added, "Do you want to weep for the people?" Let it be tried for now. Chucai could not stop it and sighed, "The people's suffering begins here!"
15
楚材嘗與諸王宴，醉臥車中，帝臨平野見之，直幸其營，登車，手撼之。 楚材熟睡未醒，方怒其擾己，忽開目視，始知帝至，驚起謝，帝曰：「有酒獨醉，不與朕同樂耶？ 」笑而去。 楚材不及冠帶，馳詣行宮，帝為置酒，極歡而罷。
Once at a feast with the princes Chucai drank until he fell asleep in his carriage. The emperor saw him on the plain, went straight to his camp, climbed into the carriage, and shook him awake. Still half asleep, Chucai was about to protest the disturbance when he opened his eyes and saw the emperor. He started up in alarm to apologize. The emperor said, "Drinking alone—will you not share the pleasure with me?" Laughing, he left. Chucai rode to the traveling palace without even fixing his cap and belt. The emperor set out wine, and they drank together in high spirits.
16
楚材當國日久，得祿分其親族，未嘗私以官。 行省劉敏從容言之，楚材曰：「睦親之義，但當資以金帛。 若使從政而違法，吾不能徇私恩也。」
Chucai had governed for many years. He shared his salary with his kin but never gave them office through private favor. The regional secretary Liu Min mentioned this casually. Chucai said, "Kin should be supported with gold and silk alone." If they enter government and break the law, I cannot favor them out of private affection."
17
歲辛丑二月三日，帝疾篤，醫言脈已絕。 皇后不知所為，召楚材問之，對曰：「今任使非人，賣官鬻獄，囚系非辜者多。 古人一言而善，熒惑退舍，請赦天下囚徒。 」後即欲行之，楚材曰：「非君命不可。 」俄頃，帝少蘇，因入奏，請肆赦，帝已不能言，首肯之。 是夜，醫者候脈復生，適宣讀赦書時也，翌日而瘳。 冬十一月四日，帝將出獵，楚材以太乙數推之，亟言其不可，左右皆曰：「不騎射，無以為樂。 」獵五日，帝崩於行在所。 皇后乃馬真氏稱制，崇信姦回，庶政多紊。 奧魯剌合蠻以貨得政柄，廷中悉畏附之。 楚材面折廷爭，言人所難言，人皆危之。
On the third day of the second month of the xinchou year the emperor fell gravely ill. The physicians said his pulse had stopped. The empress did not know what to do and summoned Chucai. He said, "Unworthy men hold office, offices and judgments are sold, and many innocents languish in prison." In antiquity one good deed made Mars withdraw from its course. I beg a general pardon for all prisoners. The empress wished to act at once. Chucai said, "It cannot be done without the emperor's command." Soon the emperor revived slightly. Chucai entered and asked for a general pardon. The emperor could not speak but nodded assent. That night the physicians found his pulse return—just as the amnesty was proclaimed—and the next day he recovered. On the fourth day of the eleventh month the emperor prepared to hunt. Chucai calculated by the Taiyi method and urgently warned against it. His attendants said, "Without riding and shooting, what pleasure is there?" After five days of hunting the emperor died at the traveling palace. The empress Töregene assumed regency. She favored wicked men, and government fell into disorder. Odu Laheman bought political power with wealth, and the whole court feared and followed him. Chucai confronted them openly in court and spoke what others dared not say. Everyone feared for his safety.
18
癸卯五月，熒惑犯房，楚材奏曰：「當有驚擾，然訖無事。 」居無何，朝廷用兵，事起倉卒，後遂令授甲選腹心，至欲西遷以避之。 楚材進曰：「朝廷天下根本，根本一搖，天下將亂。 臣觀天道，必無患也。 」後數日乃定。 後以御寶空紙付奧都剌合蠻，使自書填行之。 楚材曰：「天下者先帝之天下。 朝廷自有憲章，今欲紊之，臣不敢奉詔。 」事遂止。 又有旨：「凡奧都剌合蠻所建白，令史不為書者，斷其手。 」楚材曰：「國之典故，先帝悉委老臣，令史何與焉？ 事若合理，自當奉行，如不可行，死且不避，況截手乎！ 」後不悅。 楚材辨論不已，因大聲曰：「老臣事太祖、太宗三十餘年，無負于國，皇后亦豈能無罪殺臣也！ 」後雖憾之，亦以先朝舊勳，深敬憚焉。 甲辰夏五月，薨於位，年五十五。 皇后哀悼，賻贈甚厚。 後有譖楚材者，言其在相位日久，天下貢賦，半入其家。 後命近臣麻里扎覆視之，唯琴阮十餘，及古今書畫、金石、遺文數千卷。 至順元年，贈經國議制寅亮佐運功臣、太師、上柱國，追封廣寧王，諡文正。 子鉉、鑄。
In the fifth month of the guimao year Mars invaded the Room mansion. Chucai said, "There will be alarm, but in the end no harm." Before long the court mobilized for war in sudden haste. The regency ordered armor issued and trusted men chosen, and even considered moving the capital west to escape trouble. Chucai said, "The court is the root of the realm. Shake the root and the realm falls into chaos." I read Heaven's signs. There will be no real calamity." A few days later the crisis passed. Later the regency gave Odu Laheman blank sheets bearing the imperial seal so he could write edicts and execute them himself. Chucai said, "The realm belongs to the late emperor." The court has its own laws. To throw them into disorder now—I dare not obey." The matter was dropped. Another order declared that any clerk who refused to record Odu Laheman's proposals would have his hand cut off. Chucai said, "State precedent was wholly entrusted to me by the late emperor. What have clerks to do with it?" If a proposal is sound I will follow it. If it is not, I will not shrink from death—much less from losing a hand! The empress was displeased. Chucai argued on without stopping and finally cried out, "I served Taizu and Taizong for more than thirty years without failing the state. Even the empress cannot kill me without cause!" Though she resented him, she deeply respected and feared him as a veteran of the founding reigns. In the fifth month of summer in the jiachen year he died in office at the age of fifty-five. The empress mourned him and gave lavish funeral gifts. Later someone slandered him, claiming that during his long tenure half the empire's revenue had gone into his household. The empress ordered the intimate Maliqa to reinspect his estate. He found only a dozen musical instruments and several thousand scrolls of books, paintings, and rubbings. In the first year of Zhishun he was posthumously honored as grand preceptor and upper pillar of the state, enfeoffed as Prince of Guangning, and given the posthumous name Wenzheng. His sons were Xian and Zhu.
19
鑄字成仲，幼聰敏，善屬文，尤工騎射。 楚材薨，嗣領中書省事，時年二十三。 鑄上言宜疏禁網，遂採歷代德政合於時宜者八十一章以進。 戊午，憲宗徵蜀，詔鑄領侍衛驍果以從，屢出奇計，攻下城邑，賜以尚方金鎖甲及內厩驄馬。 乙未，憲宗崩，阿里不哥叛，鑄棄妻子，挺身自朔方來歸，世祖嘉其忠，即日召見，賞賜優厚。 中統二年，拜中書左丞相。 是年冬，詔將兵備禦北邊，後徵兵扈從，敗阿里不哥於上都之北。 至元元年，加光祿大夫。 奏定法令三十七章，吏民便之。 二年，行省山東。 未幾徵還。 初，清廟雅樂，止有登歌，詔鑄制宮懸八佾之舞。 四年春三月，樂舞成，表上之，仍請賜名《大成》，制曰「可」。 六月，改榮祿大夫、平章政事。 五年，復拜光祿大夫、中書左丞相。 十年，遷平章軍國重事。 十三年，詔監修國史。 朝廷有大事，必諮訪焉。 十九年，復拜中書左丞相。 二十年冬十月，坐不納職印、妄奏東平人聚謀為逆、間諜幕僚、及黨罪囚阿里沙，遂罷免，仍沒其家貲之半，徙居山後。 二十二年卒，年六十五。 子十一人：希徵，希勃，希亮，希寬，希素，希固，希周，希光，希逸淮東宣慰使，餘失其名。 至順元年，贈推忠保德宣力佐治功臣、太師、開府儀同三司、上柱國、懿寧王，諡文忠。
Zhu, style Chengzhong, was clever as a boy, wrote well, and was especially skilled at mounted archery. When Chucai died, Zhu succeeded him as head of the secretariat at the age of twenty-three. Zhu memorialized that the penal code should be relaxed and presented eighty-one examples of humane governance from past dynasties suited to the present age. In the wuwu year Emperor Xianzong campaigned against Shu. Zhu led the palace guard in the field, devised repeated stratagems, and captured cities. He was rewarded with imperial gold-linked armor and a piebald horse from the royal stables. In the yiwei year Emperor Xianzong died and Ariq Böke rebelled. Zhu abandoned his family and came alone from the north to submit. Kublai praised his loyalty, received him that same day, and rewarded him generously. In the second year of Zhongtong he was appointed left director of the secretariat. That winter he was ordered to guard the northern frontier. Later he joined the imperial campaign and defeated Ariq Böke north of Shangdu. In the first year of Zhiyuan he was promoted to grand master of splendid happiness. He drafted thirty-seven legal articles that officials and commoners found convenient. In the second year he served as regional commissioner in Shandong. He was soon recalled to court. At first the court ritual music had only ascending hymns. Zhu was ordered to compose full orchestral music and the eight-rows dance. In the third month of the fourth year the music and dance were finished. He presented them and asked that they be named Great Completion. The throne approved. In the sixth month he was made grand master for glorious blessing and co-director of the secretariat. In the fifth year he was again appointed grand master of splendid happiness and left director of the secretariat. In the tenth year he was made co-director of important military and civil affairs. In the thirteenth year he was ordered to supervise compilation of the national history. On every major matter the court consulted him. In the nineteenth year he was again appointed left director of the secretariat. In the tenth month of the twentieth year he was dismissed for refusing his seal of office, falsely reporting a Dongping conspiracy, employing spies among his staff, and shielding the prisoner Ali Sha. Half his property was confiscated and he was exiled north of the Yan range. He died in the twenty-second year at the age of sixty-five. He had eleven sons: Xizheng, Xibo, Xiliang, Xikuan, Xisu, Xigu, Xizhou, Xiguang, and Xiyi, who became Huaidong pacification commissioner; the names of the rest are lost. In the first year of Zhishun he was posthumously honored as grand preceptor and prince of Yining with the posthumous name Wenzhong.

○粘合重山子南合
Nianhe Chongshan; appended biography of his son Nanhe.

粘合重山，金源貴族也。 國初為質子，知金將亡，遂委質焉。 太祖賜畜馬四百匹，使為宿衛官必阇赤。 從平諸國有功。 圍涼州，執大旗指麾六軍，手中流矢，不動。 已而為侍從官，數得侍宴內廷。 因諫曰：「臣聞天子以天下為憂，憂之未有不治，忘憂未有能治者也。 置酒為樂，此忘憂之術也。 」帝深嘉納之。 立中書省，以重山有積勳，授左丞相。 時耶律楚材為右丞相，凡建官立法，任賢使能，與夫分郡邑，定課賦，通漕運，足國用，多出楚材，而重山佐成之。 太宗七年，從伐宋，詔軍前行中書省事，許以便宜。 師入宋境，江淮州邑望風款附，重山降其民三十餘萬，取定城、天長二邑，不誅一人。 復入中書視事，賜中厩馬十匹、貫珠袍一。 卒，贈太尉，封魏國公，諡忠武。
Nianhe Chongshan was a noble of the Jin frontier. In the founding years he was a hostage at the Mongol court. Seeing that Jin would fall, he pledged himself to the Mongols. Taizu gave him four hundred horses and made him a night-guard officer, a beköchi. He distinguished himself in the conquest of the various states. At the siege of Liangzhou he held the great banner and directed the armies. An arrow struck his hand, yet he did not stir. He later became an attendant and often joined banquets in the inner court. He remonstrated, "I have heard that a ruler who worries for the realm governs well, and one who forgets that worry cannot govern at all." Feasting for pleasure is a way to forget that worry." The emperor deeply approved and accepted his advice. When the secretariat was established, Chongshan was appointed left director for his accumulated merit. Yelü Chucai was right director at the time. Most of the work of establishing offices, appointing talent, dividing commanderies, fixing taxes, opening transport, and filling the treasury came from Chucai, with Chongshan assisting. In Taizong's seventh year he joined the campaign against Song and was ordered to handle secretariat affairs with the army, with discretionary authority. As the army entered Song territory, Yangzi and Huai prefectures submitted at once. Chongshan received the surrender of more than three hundred thousand people, took Dingcheng and Tianchang, and killed no one. He returned to the secretariat and was rewarded with ten horses from the central stables and a pearl-strung robe. At his death he was posthumously made grand commandant, enfeoffed as Duke of Wei, with the posthumous name Zhongwu.

○楊惟中
Yang Weizhong.
23
楊惟中，字彥誠，弘州人。 金末，以孤童子事太宗，知讀書，有膽略，太宗器之。 年二十，奉命使西域三十餘國，宣暢國威，敷布政條，俾皆籍戶口屬吏，乃歸，帝於是有大用意。 皇子闊出伐宋，命惟中於軍前行中書省事。 克宋棗陽、光化等軍，光、隨、郢、復等州，及襄陽、德安府，凡得名士數十人，收伊、洛諸書送燕都，立宋大儒周惇頤祠，建太極書院，延儒士趙復、王粹等講授其間，遂通聖賢學，慨然欲以道濟天下。 拜中書令，太宗崩，太后稱制，惟中以一相負任天下。
Yang Weizhong, style Yancheng, was from Hongzhou. At the end of Jin he entered Taizong's service as a fatherless boy. He could read, showed courage and resource, and Taizong valued him. At twenty he was sent as envoy to more than thirty Western Regions states to proclaim Mongol power, impose regulations, and register populations. When he returned the emperor formed great plans for the west. When Prince Kökechu campaigned against Song, Weizhong was ordered to handle secretariat affairs with the army. He captured Zaoyang, Guanghua, and other Song garrisons, together with Guang, Sui, Ying, Fu, Xiangyang, and De'an. He gathered several dozen eminent scholars, sent books from the Yi and Luo regions to Yan, built a shrine to Zhou Dunyi, founded the Taiji Academy, and invited Zhao Fu, Wang Cui, and other scholars to lecture there. He thus revived classical learning and hoped to save the world through the Way. He was appointed director of the secretariat. When Taizong died and the empress dowager assumed regency, Weizhong bore the weight of the realm alone as chief minister.

定宗即位，平陽道斷事官斜徹橫恣不法，詔惟中宣慰，惟中按誅之。 金亡，其將武仙潰於鄧州，餘黨散入太原、真定間，據大明川，用金開興年號，眾至數万，剽掠數千里，詔會諸道兵討之，不克。 惟中仗節開諭，降其渠帥，餘黨悉平。 憲宗即位，世祖以太弟鎮金蓮川，得開府，專封拜。 乃立河南道經略司於汴梁，奏惟中等為使，俾屯田唐、鄧、申、裕、嵩、汝、蔡、息、亳、潁諸州。 初，滅金時，以監河橋萬戶劉福為河南道總管，福貪鄙殘酷，虐害遺民二十餘年。 惟中至，召福聽約束，福稱疾不至，惟中設大梃於坐，復召之，使謂福曰：「汝不奉命，吾以軍法從事。 」福不得已，以數千人擁衛見惟中，惟中即握大梃擊僕之。 數日福死，河南大治。 遷陝右四川宣撫使。 時諸軍帥橫侈病民，郭千戶者尤甚，殺人之夫而奪其妻，惟中戮之以徇，關中肅然。 語人曰：「吾非好殺，國家綱紀不立，致此輩賊害良民，無所控告，雖慾不去，可乎！ 」歲己未，世祖總統東師，奏惟中為江淮京湖南北路宣撫使，俾建行台，以先啟行，宣布恩信，蒙古、漢軍諸帥並聽節制。 師還，卒於蔡州，年五十五。 中統二年，追諡曰忠肅公。
When Emperor Dingzong ascended, the Pingyang judge Sheche ruled lawlessly. Weizhong was sent as pacification commissioner and had him executed after investigation. After Jin fell, the general Wu Xian was routed at Dengzhou. Remnant bands scattered between Taiyuan and Zhending, held Damingchuan, used Jin's Kaixing reign title, and numbered tens of thousands, plundering for thousands of li. Troops from all circuits were summoned but could not defeat them. Weizhong went with imperial authority to persuade them. The chiefs submitted and the rest were pacified. When Emperor Xianzong ascended, Kublai as imperial younger brother guarded Jinlianchuan with an open office and exclusive authority to appoint officials. He established the Henan pacification commission at Bianliang and memorialized Weizhong as commissioner, ordering colonization in Tang, Deng, Shen, Yu, Song, Ru, Cai, Xi, Bo, and Ying. When Jin was destroyed, Liu Fu, commander of Jianhe Bridge, was made Henan chief commander. For more than twenty years he was greedy, cruel, and abusive toward the surviving population. When Weizhong arrived he summoned Fu to hear his orders. Fu claimed illness and stayed away. Weizhong placed a heavy staff by his seat and summoned him again, saying, "Disobey and I will deal with you by military law." Fu had no choice but to come with several thousand guards. Weizhong seized the staff and beat down his attendants. Within days Fu died, and Henan was well governed. He was transferred to pacification commissioner of Shaanxi-right and Sichuan. Army commanders were oppressing the people. One Commander Guo was especially vicious, killing a husband and seizing his wife. Weizhong executed him as a warning, and Guanzhong became orderly. He told others, "I do not love killing. When state discipline fails, such men prey on the innocent with no recourse. How can I leave them alone?" In the jiwei year Kublai commanded the eastern army and memorialized Weizhong as pacification commissioner for the Jianghuai, Jinghu, and Henan-north circuits. He was to establish a branch secretariat, advance ahead to proclaim imperial favor, and all Mongol and Han commanders were placed under his authority. When the army returned he died at Caizhou at the age of fifty-five. In the second year of Zhongtong he was posthumously titled Duke Zhongsu.
