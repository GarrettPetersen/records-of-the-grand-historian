1
烏古孫良楨
Wugusun Liangzhen
2
烏古孫良楨字幹卿，世次見父澤傳。 資器絕人，好讀書。 至治二年，蔭補江陰州判官，尋丁內艱，服除，調婺州武義縣尹，有惠政。 改漳州路推官，獄有疑者，悉平反之。 上言：「律，徒者不杖，今杖而又徒，非恤刑意，宜加徒減杖。 」遂定為令。 移泉州，益以能稱。 轉延平判官，拜陝西行臺監察御史，劾遼陽行省左丞相達識帖睦邇賣國不忠，援漢高帝斬丁公故事，以明人臣大義。 并劾御史中丞胡居祐奸邪，皆罷之，中外震懾。 陞都事，猶以言不盡行，解去。
Wugusun Liangzhen, styled Ganqing, is listed in the genealogical sequence in his father Ze's biography. He possessed exceptional talent and was devoted to study. In 1322 he entered office through hereditary privilege as assistant prefect of Jiangyin; shortly afterward his mother died and he withdrew for mourning. After the mourning period he was assigned magistrate of Wuyi in Wuzhou, where his policies brought real benefit to the people. He was moved to the post of judicial investigator in Zhangzhou Circuit, and every doubtful case in the prisons he set right. He memorialized the throne: "The code provides that convicts sentenced to penal servitude shall not also be flogged, yet now flogging is combined with servitude. That contradicts the intent of lenient punishment; the penalty of servitude should be increased in place of reducing the strokes of the rod." The proposal was adopted as a standing regulation. Transferred to Quanzhou, he grew still more celebrated for his ability. Promoted to assistant official of Yanping, he was appointed surveillance censor of the Shaanxi Branch Secretariat and impeached Toghuz Temür, left chancellor of the Liaoyang Branch Secretariat, for betraying the realm, invoking Emperor Gaozu of Han's execution of Duke Ding to illustrate a minister's paramount obligation. He also impeached Censor-in-Chief Hu Juyou as corrupt and treacherous; both men were removed from office, and the whole empire was awestruck. Promoted to secretariat supervisor, he resigned when he saw his recommendations were not being implemented in full.
3
復起為監察御史，良楨以帝方覽萬幾，不可不求賢自輔，於是連疏：「天曆數年間紀綱大壞，元氣傷夷。 天祐聖明，入膺大統，而西宮秉政，奸臣弄權，畜憾十有餘年。 天威一怒，陰晦開明，以正大名，以章大孝，此誠兢兢業業祈天永命之秋，其術在乎敬身修德而已。 今經筵多領以職事臣，數日一進講，不渝數刻已罷，而御小臣，恒侍左右，何益於盛德哉。 臣願招延儒臣若許衡者數人，置於禁密，常以唐、虞、三代之道，啟沃宸衷，日新其德，實萬世無疆之福也。 」又以國俗父死則妻其從母，兄弟死則收其妻，父母死無憂制，遂言：「綱常皆出於天而不可變，議法之吏，乃言國人不拘此例，諸國人各從本俗。 是漢、南人當守綱常，國人、諸國人不必守綱常也。 名曰優之，實則陷之，外若尊之，內實侮之，推其本心所以待國人者，不若漢、南人之厚也。 請下禮官有司及右科進士在朝者會議，自天子至於庶人，皆從禮制，以成列聖未遑之典，明萬世不易之道。 」又言：「隱士劉因，道學經術可比許文正公衡，從祀孔子廟庭。 」皆不報。 御史臺作新風憲，復疏其所當行者，以舉賢才為綱，而以厚風俗、均賦役、重審理、汰冗官、選守令、出奉使、均公田為目，指擿剴切，雖觸忌諱，亦不顧也。 宦者罕失嬖妾殺其妻，糜其肉飼犬，上疏乞正重刑，并論宦寺結廷臣撓政為害，可汰黜之。 憸佞側目。
Recalled as surveillance censor, Liangzhen judged that with the emperor now attending to every matter of government, he must seek worthy men to aid him. He therefore submitted memorial after memorial: "In the Tianshun period the institutions of state were gravely corrupted and the political order grievously depleted. Heaven blessed Your Sagely Enlightenment when you ascended the great succession, yet the western palace faction held power and corrupt ministers manipulated authority, harboring resentments for more than a decade. When Heaven's wrath flared once, darkness gave way to light: the great succession was set right and supreme filial duty was proclaimed. This is precisely the moment to serve Heaven with reverent care and pray for a lasting mandate—the means lie solely in cultivating the self and perfecting virtue. Today the lecture to the throne is largely entrusted to officials already burdened with office; they lecture only every few days and finish within a few moments, while favored attendants remain constantly at Your side. What profit does this bring to the cultivation of supreme virtue? I beg that several scholar-officials of Xu Heng's caliber be invited to reside within the inner palace, there to nourish Your Majesty's mind unceasingly with the traditions of Tang, Yu, and the Three Dynasties, day by day renewing Your virtue—a blessing without limit for ages to come. " He also addressed the national custom whereby a man marries his stepmother after his father's death and takes his brother's widow after a brother's death, with no mourning prescribed when parents die. He argued: "The bonds of human relations derive from Heaven and are immutable, yet the officials who draft law claim that Mongols need not observe these norms and that subjects of the various states may each follow their native customs. In effect this means that Han Chinese and southerners must uphold moral order, while Mongols and other subject peoples need not. What is called granting them privilege in fact ensnares them; outwardly they seem honored, yet inwardly they are despised. Judging by the government's true attitude, Mongols are treated less generously than Han Chinese and southerners. He asked that ritual officials, the relevant departments, and Right Branch jinshi at court deliberate together so that from the Son of Heaven to the common people all would follow the ritual code—thus fulfilling the canon successive sage emperors had not yet completed and affirming the eternal Way. " He also urged that the recluse Liu Yin, whose mastery of the Way and classical learning rivaled that of Xu Heng, Duke of Culture, be granted secondary sacrifice in the Confucian temple. None of these proposals received a reply. When the Censorate promulgated new surveillance regulations, he submitted another detailed memorial on what should be done: promoting worthy talent as the overarching principle, with enriching customs, equalizing taxes and labor service, strengthening judicial review, eliminating redundant officials, selecting prefects and magistrates, dispatching imperial envoys, and equalizing public fields as specific measures. His criticisms were sharp and forthright; even when they touched forbidden topics he did not hold back. When the eunuch Han Shi killed his wife, ground her flesh, and fed it to his dogs after losing a favored concubine, Liangzhen memorialized for severe punishment. He also argued that eunuchs who colluded with court officials to obstruct government should be purged. The sycophants and flatterers glared at him with hatred.
4
至正四年，召為刑部員外郎，轉御史臺都事。 五年，改中書左司都事，出為江東道肅政廉訪司副使。 上官一日，辭歸。 六年，授平江路緫管，不拜。 八年，復召為右司員外郎。 九年，陞郎中，尋遷廣東道肅政廉訪使，未行，還為郎中，遷福建道肅政廉訪使，中道召還，參議中書省事，兼經筵官。 十一年，拜治書侍御史，陞中書參知政事、同知經筵事。
In 1344 he was summoned as vice minister of the Ministry of Punishments and then transferred to secretariat supervisor of the Censorate. The following year he was reassigned as secretariat supervisor of the Left Secretariat Department and appointed deputy surveillance commissioner of the Jiangdong Surveillance Commission. After a single day in the post he resigned and went home. In 1346 he was offered the post of general manager of Pingjiang Circuit but declined it. In 1348 he was recalled as vice minister of the Right Secretariat Department. In 1349 he was promoted to director. He was soon appointed surveillance commissioner of Guangdong but before taking up the post was returned to his former rank as director, then appointed surveillance commissioner of Fujian. Recalled en route, he became associate administrator of Secretariat affairs and concurrently a lecturer at the classics lecture to the throne. In 1351 he was appointed attending censor-in-chief and promoted to associate administrator of the Secretariat and concurrent administrator of the classics lecture.
5
十三年，陞左丞，兼大司農卿，仍同知經筵事。 時中書參用非人，事多異同，不得一一如志。 會軍餉不給，請與右丞悟良哈台主屯田，歲入二十萬石。 東宮久未建，懇懇為言，車駕幸上都，始冊皇太子。 立詹事院，驛召為副詹事，每直端本堂，則進正心誠意之說、親君子遠小人之道，皇太子嘉納焉。 當時盜賊蜂起，帝聞，惡之，下詔分討，必盡誅而後已。 良楨言：「平賊在收人心，以回天意，多殺非道也。 」乃赦以安之。
In 1353 he was promoted to left chancellor, concurrently minister of the Grand Agrarian Office, while retaining his post as concurrent administrator of the classics lecture. At that time the Secretariat was staffed with unworthy men; policies often diverged and conflicted, and he could not always carry out his intentions. When military provisions ran short, he asked to oversee garrison farming jointly with Right Chancellor Ulabuqatai, producing two hundred thousand piculs per year. The heir apparent had long gone uninstalled; Liangzhen pleaded earnestly on the matter, and when the emperor traveled to Shangdu the crown prince was at last formally installed. When the Household of the Heir Apparent was established, he was summoned by express post as vice household master. Whenever he attended at the Hall of the Correct Root he expounded the doctrine of rectifying the mind and making the will sincere and the principle of drawing near to gentlemen while keeping petty men at a distance; the crown prince welcomed his teaching. Bandits and rebels were rising everywhere; when the emperor heard of it he was filled with loathing and issued an edict for their suppression on every front, vowing that none would be spared until all were exterminated. Liangzhen argued: "To pacify rebels one must win the people's hearts and turn Heaven's mind back to favor; indiscriminate slaughter is not the Way." An amnesty was issued to reassure the populace.
6
十四年，遷淮南行省左丞。 初，泰州賊張士誠既降復叛，殺淮南行省參知政事趙璉，進據高郵、六合，太師脫脫奉詔，緫諸王軍南征，而良楨洎參議龔伯璲、刑部主事廬山等從之。 既平六合，垂克高郵，會詔罷脫脫兵柄，遂有上變告伯璲等勸脫脫勒兵北向者，下其事逮問，詞連良楨，簿對無所驗。 即日還中書左丞，命分省彰德，主調軍食，居半歲，還中書。 十六年，進階榮祿大夫，賜玉帶一。
In 1354 he was transferred to left chancellor of the Huainan Branch Secretariat. Earlier the Taizhou rebel Zhang Shicheng had surrendered and then rebelled again, killing Zhao Lian, associate administrator of the Huainan Branch Secretariat, and seizing Gaoyou and Liuhe. Grand Preceptor Toghto received orders to command the princes' armies on a southern campaign, accompanied by Liangzhen, Associate Administrator Gong Bojiong, Ministry of Punishments registrar Lu Shan, and others. After Liuhe was pacified and Gaoyou was nearly taken, an edict arrived stripping Toghto of military command. Someone then denounced Gong Bojiong and others for urging Toghto to march the army northward. The case was sent down for arrest and interrogation; testimony implicated Liangzhen, but examination of the records found no evidence against him. That same day he was restored as left chancellor of the Secretariat and assigned to the Zhangde branch secretariat to oversee military provisions. After half a year he returned to the central Secretariat. In 1356 he was promoted to Grand Master of Glorious Blessing and granted a jade belt.
7
十七年，除大司農。 明年，陞右丞，兼大司農，辭，不允。 論罷陷賊延坐之令。 有惡少年誣知宜興州張復通賊之罪，中書將籍其孥，吏抱案請署。 良楨曰； 「手可斷，案不可署。 」同列變色，卒不署。
In 1357 he was appointed minister of the Grand Agrarian Office. The following year he was promoted to right chancellor while retaining the Grand Agrarian Office; he declined but was not permitted to withdraw. He argued successfully for abolishing the ordinance that extended punishment to the families of those implicated with rebels. A vicious youth falsely accused Zhang Fu, prefect of Yixing, of colluding with rebels; the Secretariat was about to register his family for confiscation, and a clerk brought the dossier for signature. Liangzhen said: "My hand may be cut off, but I will not sign this dossier." His colleagues blanched, but in the end he refused to sign.
8
良楨自左曹登政府，多所建白。 罷福建、山東食鹽，浙東、西長生牛租，瀕海被災圍田稅，民皆德之。 嘗論至正格輕重不倫，吏得並緣為奸，舉明律者數人，參酌古今，重定律書，書成而罷。 家居輒訓諸子曰：「吾無過人者，惟待人以誠，人亦以誠遇我，汝宜志之。 」晚歲病瘠，數謁告，病益侵，遂卒。 自號約齋。 有詩文奏議凡若干卷，藏于家。
From the time Liangzhen rose from the Left Secretariat Department into high office, he offered many constructive proposals. He abolished the salt monopoly in Fujian and Shandong, the long-life ox levy in eastern and western Zhejiang, and encircled-field taxes in coastal disaster zones; the people were deeply grateful. He once argued that the Zhizheng code was inconsistent in the severity of penalties, enabling clerks to exploit the gaps for corruption. He nominated several experts in Ming law to consult past and present precedents and redraft the legal code; when the work was completed the project was abandoned. At home he often admonished his sons: "I have no special gift beyond this: treat others with sincerity, and they will meet you with sincerity. Remember that." In his later years he grew ill and wasted away; he repeatedly requested leave, his illness worsened, and he died. He styled himself Yuezhai, Studio of Restraint. His collected poetry, prose, and memorials, totaling several juan, were kept in his family.
9
賈魯字友恒，河東高平人。 幼負志節，既長，謀略過人。 延祐、至治間，兩以明經領鄉貢。 泰定初，恩授東平路儒學教授，辟憲史，歷行省掾，除潞城縣尹，選丞相東曹掾，擢戶部主事，未上。 一日，覺心悸，尋得父書，筆勢顫縮，即辭歸，比至家，父已有風疾，未幾卒。
Jia Lu, styled Youheng, was a native of Gaoping in Hedong. From youth he showed strong character; as a man his strategic judgment surpassed others. During the Yanyou and Zhizhi reigns he twice led the provincial tribute selection through mastery of the classics. Early in the Taiding reign he received a grace appointment as professor of the Confucian school in Dongping Circuit, then served as surveillance clerk and branch-secretariat clerk, was appointed magistrate of Lucheng, selected as clerk in the chancellor's Eastern Department, and promoted to registrar of the Ministry of Revenue—but before he could take up the post, one day he felt his heart palpitate. Soon a letter arrived from his father, the handwriting trembling and uncertain; he resigned at once and hurried home, but by the time he arrived his father was already stricken with paralysis and died shortly afterward.
10
魯居喪服闋，起為太醫院都事。 會詔修遼、金、宋三史，召魯為宋史局官。 書成，選魯燕南山東道奉使宣撫幕官，考績居最，遷中書省檢校官。 上言：「十八河倉，近歲淪沒官糧百三十萬斛，其弊由富民兼并，貧民流亡，宜合先正經界，然事體重大，非處置盡善，不可輕發。 」書累數萬言，切中其弊。 俄拜監察御史，首言御史有封事，宜專達聖聰，不宜臺臣先有所可否。 陞臺都事，遷山北廉訪副使，復召為工部郎中，言考工一十九事。
After the mourning period Lu was recalled as secretariat supervisor of the Imperial Medical Academy. When an edict was issued to compile the histories of Liao, Jin, and Song, Lu was summoned as an official of the Song History Bureau. When the histories were completed, Lu was selected as staff officer on the Pacification Commission of the Yanshan Shandong East Circuit; his performance evaluation ranked first and he was transferred to reviewing officer of the Secretariat. He memorialized: "At the Eighteen River granaries, in recent years 1.3 million piculs of official grain have been lost to flooding. The root cause is wealthy households' land encroachment and the flight of the poor. Boundaries should first be restored according to precedent, but the matter is so large in scope that it must not be undertaken lightly unless every disposition is thoroughly sound." The memorial ran to tens of thousands of words and struck at the heart of the abuses. Soon he was appointed surveillance censor and argued first of all that sealed memorials from censors should reach the emperor directly, without prior approval or rejection by other censorate members. He was promoted to censorate secretariat supervisor, transferred to deputy surveillance commissioner of the Shanbei Commission, and again summoned as director of the Ministry of Works, where he presented nineteen proposals on public works.
11
至正四年，河決白茅堤，又決金堤，並河郡邑，民居昏墊，壯者流離。 帝甚患之，遣使體驗，仍督大臣訪求治河方略，特命魯行都水監。 魯循行河道，考察地形，往復數千里，備得要害，為圖上進二策：其一，議修築北堤，以制橫潰，則用工省； 其一，議疏塞並舉，挽河東行，使復故道，其功數倍。 會遷右司郎中，議未及竟。 其在右司，言時政二十一事，皆見舉行。 調都漕運使，復以漕事二十事言之，朝廷取其八事：一曰京畿和糴，二曰優恤漕司舊領漕戶，三曰接連委官，四曰通州緫治豫定委官，五曰船戶困於壩夫，海運壞於壩戶，六曰疏濬運河，七曰臨清運糧萬戶府當隸漕司，八曰宣忠船戶付本司節制。 事未盡行。 既而河水北侵安山，淪入運河，延袤濟南、河間，將隳兩漕司鹽場，實妨國計。
In 1344 the Yellow River burst the Baimao embankment and then the Jin embankment; towns along the river were inundated and the able-bodied fled as refugees. The emperor was deeply troubled, dispatched envoys to inspect the damage, and urged his ministers to seek a strategy for controlling the river; Lu was specially ordered to serve as acting director of the Directorate of Waterways. Lu traveled the river course, surveyed the terrain over thousands of li, mastered every critical point, and submitted a map with two strategies: the first proposed building the northern embankment to control lateral breaches, which would require less labor; the second proposed dredging and blocking together, drawing the river eastward to restore its old course, with labor several times greater. Just then he was transferred to director of the Right Secretariat Department, and the deliberation was left unfinished. While serving in the Right Department he presented twenty-one proposals on current policy, all of which were adopted. Transferred to director of the Capital Grain Transport, he presented twenty proposals on transport affairs; the court adopted eight: equitable grain purchase in the capital region; favorable treatment for transport households formerly under the transport office; continuous appointment of officials; unified governance at Tongzhou with pre-designated appointees; relief for boat households oppressed by dam laborers and for sea transport disrupted by dam households; dredging the Grand Canal; subordinating the Linqing Transport Grain Ten-Thousand-Households Office to the transport directorate; and placing the Xuanzhong boat households under its control. Not all of these measures were fully implemented. Before long the river invaded Anshan from the north and poured into the Grand Canal, spreading across Jinan and Hejian and threatening to destroy the salt fields of both transport commissions—a serious blow to state revenue.
12
九年，太傅、右丞相脫脫復相，論及河決，思拯民艱，以塞詔旨，乃集廷臣群議，言人人殊。 魯昌言：「河必當治。 」復以前二策進，丞相取其後策，與魯定議，且以其事屬魯。 魯固辭，丞相曰：「此事非子不可。 」乃入奏，大稱帝旨。 十一年四月，命魯以工部尚書、緫治河防使，進秩二品，授以銀章，領河南、北諸路軍民，發汴梁、大名十有三路民一十五萬，廬州等戍十有八翼軍二萬供役，一切從事大小軍民官，咸稟節度，便宜興繕。 是月鳩工，七月鑿河成，八月決水故河，九月舟楫通，十一月諸埽諸堤成，水土工畢，河復故道。 事見河渠志。 帝遣使報祭河伯，召魯還京師，魯以河平圖獻。 帝適覽臺臣奏疏，請褒脫脫治河之績，次論魯功，超拜榮祿大夫、集賢大學士，賞賚金帛，敕翰林丞旨歐陽玄製河平碑，以旌脫脫勞績，具載魯功，且宣付史館，并贈魯先臣三世。
In 1352 Grand Tutor and Right Chancellor Toghto returned to office. Discussing the river breach, he wished to relieve the people's suffering and fulfill the imperial mandate, and convened the court for deliberation—but opinions diverged widely. Lu spoke forthrightly: "The river must be brought under control." He again presented his two earlier strategies; the chancellor chose the second, settled the plan with Lu, and entrusted the entire project to him. Lu firmly declined, but the chancellor said: "This cannot be done without you." Lu then memorialized the throne, and the proposal met with the emperor's full approval. In the fourth month of 1351 Lu was appointed minister of works and commissioner-general for river control, promoted to second rank and granted a silver seal. He was put in charge of military and civilian personnel across all circuits north and south of the Yellow River, with 150,000 civilians from thirteen circuits including Bianliang and Daming and 20,000 troops from eighteen garrison wings mobilized for labor. All participating officials were placed under his command with discretionary authority to undertake construction. That month work began; by the seventh month the new channel was cut; in the eighth month water was released into the old course; by the ninth month boats could pass; by the eleventh month all revetments and embankments were complete. Earth and water works finished, the river had returned to its old course. The full account appears in the Treatise on Rivers and Canals. The emperor dispatched envoys to report the sacrifice to the River Lord and summoned Lu back to the capital, where Lu presented a map of the pacified river. The emperor was just then reading censorate memorials praising Toghto's achievements in controlling the river and commending Lu's contribution. Lu was exceptionally promoted to Grand Master of Glorious Blessing and Academician-Exceller of the Hall of Gathered Worthies and rewarded with gold and silks. Hanlin Academician Recipient of Edicts Ouyang Xuan was ordered to compose a stele commemorating the pacified river, honoring Toghto's labor while recording Lu's achievements in full; the text was proclaimed and sent to the History Office, and Lu's ancestors for three generations were enfeoffed.
13
尋拜中書左丞，從脫脫平徐州，脫脫既旋師，命魯追餘黨，分攻濠州，同緫兵官平章月可察兒督戰，魯誓師曰：「吾奉旨統八衛漢軍，頓兵于濠七日矣。 爾諸將同心協力，必以今日巳、午時取城池，然後食。 」魯上馬麾進，抵城下，忽頭眩下馬，且戒兵馬弗散，病愈亟，却藥不肯汗，竟卒于軍中，年五十七。 十三年五月壬午也。 月可察兒躬為治喪，選士護柩還高平，有旨賜交鈔五百錠以給葬事。 子稹。

15
逯魯曾字善止，修武人。 性剛介，通經術，中天曆二年進士第，授翰林國史院編修官，辟御史臺掾，掌機密。 監察御史劾中丞史顯夫簡傲，魯曾開實封於大夫前曰：「中丞素持重，不能與人周旋，御史以人情劾之，非公論。 」由是皆知其直。
Lu Luceng, styled Shanzhi, was a native of Xiuwu. By nature he was stern and upright and well versed in the classics. In 1329 he passed the jinshi examination, was appointed compiler of the Hanlin National History Academy, recruited as a censorate clerk, and entrusted with confidential affairs. When a surveillance censor impeached Censor-in-Chief Shi Xianfu for arrogance, Luceng opened the sealed memorial before the assembled ministers and said: "The censor-in-chief has always been grave and reserved and is not skilled at social maneuvering. To impeach him on personal grounds is not impartial judgment." From this everyone knew his integrity.

He was appointed erudite of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. For Emperor Wuzong's temple no principal consort had yet been designated for joint sacrifice, and the ministers were summoned to deliberate at court. Luceng spoke forthrightly: "The previous court, because Empress Zhenge of Wuzong had no son, did not install her as principal consort. At that time Bayan was right chancellor and argued that Emperor Mingzong's mother, also of the Qigilie clan, could be installed for joint sacrifice. The Bureau of Palace Governance transmitted the empress dowager's order that Emperor Wenzong's mother, of the Tangut clan, could be installed for joint sacrifice. Bayan asked Luceng: "Since the previous court did not install a principal consort because Empress Zhenge had no son, whom should we install now—Mingzong's mother? Or Wenzong's mother?" He replied: "Empress Zhenge had already received the jade book in the Wuzong reign and was thus Wuzong's empress, while the mothers of Mingzong and Wenzong were by status concubines. To refuse a principal consort because she had no son, yet elevate a concubine to the principal palace, is for a subject to displace a former sovereign's empress and for a son to ennoble his father's concubine posthumously—this is impermissible by ritual. Moreover, when the Prince of Yan, Murong Chui, took the throne he deposed his mother's empress status and installed his birth mother as empress for joint sacrifice—a laughingstock for all ages. How can we repeat that error? Academician-Exceller Chen Hao, who had long resented Luceng, objected: "Emperor Taizong of Tang ennobled the mother of Prince of Cao Li Ming as empress—there were two empresses then as well. Why should it not be permissible?" Luceng replied: "Yao's mother was a secondary consort of Emperor Ku; when Yao became emperor, we never hear of her being enfeoffed as empress to share sacrifice with Ku. Your Majesty is Son of Heaven of the Great Yuan—will you not take Yao and Shun as your model but emulate Emperor Taizong of Tang?" The assembly was persuaded by his argument, Bayan approved, and Empress Zhenge was installed for joint sacrifice.

貢師泰
Gong Shitai
21
貢師泰字泰甫，寧國之宣城人。 父奎，以文學名家，延祐、至治間，官京師，為集賢直學士，卒，諡文靖。
Gong Shitai, styled Taifu, was a native of Xuancheng in Ningguo. His father Kui was renowned for literary learning; during the Yanyou and Zhizhi reigns he served in the capital as academician of the Hall of Gathered Worthies, died, and was posthumously titled Duke of Culture.
22
師泰早肄業國子學為諸生。 泰定四年，釋褐出身，授從仕郎、太和州判官。 丁外艱，改徽州路歙縣丞，江浙行省辟為掾，尋以土著，自劾去。 大臣有以其名聞者，擢應奉翰林文字。 丁內艱，服闋，除紹興路緫管府推官，郡有疑獄，悉為詳讞而剖決之。
Shitai studied early at the Imperial Academy as a student. In 1327 he left student robes for office, appointed gentleman for attendance and assistant prefect of Taihe Prefecture. After his father's death he was appointed assistant magistrate of She County in Huizhou Circuit and recruited as a clerk by the Jiangzhe Branch Secretariat, but soon resigned on the ground that as a native of the region he should not hold the post. When a great minister brought his name to the court's attention, he was promoted to attendant drafter of Hanlin documents. After mourning for his mother he was appointed judicial investigator of the Shaoxing Circuit General Administration; in every doubtful case in the prefecture he reviewed the verdicts in detail and rendered judgment.
23
山陰白洋港有大船飄近岸，史甲二十人，適取鹵海濱，見其無主，因取其篙櫓，而船中有二死人。 有徐乙者，怪其無物而有死人，稱為史等所劫。 史傭作富民高丙家，事遂連高。 史既誣服，高亦就逮。 師泰密詢之，則里中沈丁載物抵杭而回，漁者張網海中，因盜網中魚，為漁者所殺，史實未嘗殺人奪物，高亦弗知情，其冤皆白。
At Baiyang Harbor in Shanyin a large ship drifted ashore. Twenty men surnamed Shi, who happened to be gathering brine by the sea, saw no owner aboard and took its poles and oars—but the ship held two dead men. A man named Xu Yi found it strange that the ship held no goods but two corpses, and accused Shi and the others of robbery and murder. Shi was hired labor in the household of the wealthy man Gao Bing, and the case thus implicated Gao as well. Shi had already confessed under coercion, and Gao was arrested as well. Shitai made secret inquiries and learned that Shen Ding of the village, returning from carrying goods to Hangzhou, had been fishing at sea; he stole fish from another's net and was killed by the fisherman. Shi had never killed or robbed anyone, and Gao knew nothing of the matter—their wrongful convictions were all cleared.

One day he met a merchant from Zhuji, seized his money, beat him to death, threw the body into the water, and reported to the county: "I captured a private-salt offender who, fearing punishment, drowned himself." Officials examined the body, found wounds, and grew suspicious. The case was therefore left unresolved as doubtful. Sun Guobin of Yuyao, while pursuing thieves, captured Yao Jia counterfeiting paper money, accepted a bribe and released him, then seized Gao Yi and Lu Bing and brought them to the authorities on false charges of joint counterfeiting. Gao had once circulated Yao's notes but had not counterfeited them himself; since Sun had already released Yao, he shifted the blame onto Gao. Because Lu had a grudge against Sun, he was implicated as well—though Lu and Gao had never even met. Shitai doubted that Gao and the others could have repeatedly counterfeited as charged; he interrogated Sun, whose testimony collapsed and the truth emerged. He released Lu immediately and imposed on Gao only his proper offense; Yao was executed, and Sun was punished by law as well. His clarity in reviewing wrongful convictions was mostly of this kind. Shitai was ordered to purchase grain equitably in western Zhejiang, obtained one million piculs, and supplied the capital. He was transferred to vice minister of the Ministry of War. Powerful families deeply resented him because the reforms harmed their interests, yet could not find any way to injure him. When the court wished again to purchase grain equitably in western Zhejiang, Shitai was appointed commissioner of water control and abandoned fields.

Pingjiang then lacked a prefect; the court could find no suitable candidate, and Shitai was again selected as general manager of Pingjiang Circuit. That winter, just as he took office, Zhang Shicheng led his forces from Gaoyou across the Yangtze, advanced straight to the city walls, and laid siege with great urgency. The following spring the defending general could not hold out, cut through the gate and fled. Shitai led militia out to fight but was overpowered; he too took his seal and sash, abandoned the city, and hid by the seashore for a long time.

Zhou Boqi
33
周伯琦字伯溫，饒州人。 父應極，至大間，仁宗為皇太子，召見，獻皇元頌，為言于武宗，以為翰林待制。 後為皇太子說書，日侍英邸。 仁宗即位，遷集賢待制，終池州路同知緫管府事。 伯琦自幼從宦，游京師，入國學，為上舍生，積分及高等。 去，以蔭授將仕郎、南海縣主簿，三轉為翰林修撰。
Zhou Boqi, styled Bowen, was a native of Raozhou. His father Yingji, during the Zhide period when Renzong was crown prince, was summoned to audience, presented an ode on the founding of the Yuan, spoke on his behalf to Wuzong, and was made Hanlin attendant drafter. Later he served as lecturer to the crown prince and attended the princely residence daily. When Renzong took the throne he was transferred to Hanlin attendant drafter of the Hall of Gathered Worthies and ended his career as associate manager of the Chizhou Circuit General Administration. Boqi from youth followed his father's official career, traveled to the capital, entered the Imperial Academy as an upper-hall student, and accumulated credits to the highest grade. He left the academy, received a yin appointment as gentleman for service and registrar of Nanhai County, and after three promotions became Hanlin compiler.
34
至正元年，改奎章閣為宣文閣、藝文監為崇文監，伯琦為宣文閣授經郎，教戚里大臣子弟，每進講，輒稱旨，且日被顧問。 帝以伯琦工書法，命篆「宣文閣寶」，仍題扁宣文閣； 及摹王羲之所書蘭亭序、智永所書千文，刻石閣中。 自是累轉官，皆宣文、崇文之間，而眷遇益隆矣。 帝嘗呼其字伯溫而不名。 會御史奏風憲宜用近臣，特命僉廣東廉訪司事。 八年，召入為翰林待制，預修后妃、功臣列傳，累陞直學士。
In 1341 the Kuizhang Pavilion was renamed the Hall of Propagating Culture and the Directorate of Literary Arts the Directorate of Honoring Culture. Boqi was appointed lecturer there, teaching the sons of imperial affines and great ministers; every lecture pleased the emperor, and he was consulted daily. Because Boqi was skilled in calligraphy, the emperor ordered him to cut the seal "Treasure of the Hall of Propagating Culture" and inscribe the hall plaque; and to copy Wang Xizhi's Lanting Preface and Zhiyong's Thousand-Character Essay and carve them in stone within the hall. Thereafter his successive appointments were all within the Hall of Propagating Culture and the Directorate of Honoring Culture, and imperial favor toward him grew ever greater. The emperor sometimes addressed him by his style Bowen rather than by his personal name. When censors memorialized that surveillance posts should be filled by close ministers of the throne, he was specially appointed associate surveillance commissioner of Guangdong. In 1348 he was summoned as Hanlin attendant drafter, participated in compiling the biographies of empresses and meritorious ministers, and was promoted to academician.
35
十二年，有旨令南士皆得居省臺。 除伯琦兵部侍郎，遂與貢師泰同擢監察御史。 兩人皆南士之望，一時榮之。 時御史大夫也先帖木兒以大軍南討，而失律喪師，陝西行臺監察御史劉希曾等十人共劾奏之。 伯琦乃劾希曾等越分干譽，希曾等皆坐左遷，補郡判官，由是不為公論所與。
In 1352 an edict declared that southerners might again hold Secretariat and Censorate posts. Boqi was appointed vice minister of the Ministry of War and then, together with Gong Shitai, was promoted to surveillance censor. Both men were leading figures among southern scholars, and for a time their appointments were celebrated as a great honor. At that time Censor-in-Chief Yesian Timur led a great army on a southern campaign but violated discipline and lost his forces; ten surveillance censors of the Shaanxi Branch Secretariat, including Liu Xizeng, jointly impeached him. Boqi then impeached Xizeng and the others for overstepping their authority to seek reputation; they were all demoted to assistant prefects, and from this Boqi lost public support.

In 1353 he was transferred to director of the Directorate of Honoring Culture, concurrently a classics-lecture official, and performed sacrifice to the Heavenly Consort by proxy. He entered mourning for his mother. In 1354 he was recalled from mourning as surveillance commissioner of Jiangdong. Changqiang Suonanban seized Ningguo; Boqi and his staff went out in panic to meet the rebels, then fled to Hangzhou, where he was appointed minister of war but did not take up the post, and was reassigned surveillance commissioner of western Zhejiang. Surveillance Censor Yu Guan of the Jiangnan Branch Secretariat impeached Boqi for losing Ningguo and argued that he should be punished.
37
十七年，江浙行省丞相達識帖睦爾承制假伯琦參知政事，招諭平江張士誠。
In 1357 Chancellor Toghuz Temür of the Jiangzhe Branch Secretariat, by imperial commission, provisionally appointed Boqi associate administrator to negotiate Zhang Shicheng's surrender at Pingjiang.
38
士誠既降，江南行臺監察御史亦辯釋伯琦罪，除同知太常禮儀院事，士誠留之，未行，拜資政大夫、江浙行省左丞。 於是留平江者十餘年。 士誠既滅，伯琦乃得歸鄱陽，尋卒。
After Zhang Shicheng submitted, Jiangnan surveillance censors also argued to clear Boqi's guilt; he was appointed associate director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, but Zhang Shicheng detained him and he did not depart. He was then appointed Grand Master for Governance and left chancellor of the Jiangzhe Branch Secretariat. He remained at Pingjiang for more than ten years. After Zhang Shicheng was destroyed, Boqi was able to return to Poyang and died shortly afterward.
39
伯琦儀觀溫雅，粹然如玉，雖遭時多艱，而善於自保。 博學工文章，而尤以篆、隸、真、草擅名當時。 嘗著六書正訛、說文字原二書，又有詩文稿若干卷。
Boqi's bearing was mild and elegant, pure as jade; though he lived through turbulent times, he was skilled at preserving himself. Broadly learned and skilled in literary composition, he was especially renowned in his time for seal, clerical, regular, and cursive calligraphy. He authored two works, Corrections to the Six Scripts and Origins of the Explanation of Characters, and left poetry and prose drafts in several juan.
40
吳當字伯尚，澄之孫也。 當幼承祖訓，以穎悟篤實稱。 長精通經史百家言，侍其祖至京，補國子生。 久之，澄既捐館，四方學子從澄遊者，悉就當卒業焉。
Wu Dang, styled Boshang, was the grandson of Wu Cheng. From youth Dang received his grandfather's instruction and was acclaimed for keen intelligence and solid integrity. As a man he mastered the classics, histories, and the teachings of the hundred schools; he accompanied his grandfather to the capital and was enrolled as an Imperial Academy student. After a time Cheng died, and scholars from all directions who had studied with him all came to Dang to complete their training.
41
至正五年，以父文蔭，授萬億四庫照磨，未上，用薦者改國子助教。 勤講解，嚴肄習，諸生皆樂從之。 會詔修遼、金、宋三史，當預編纂。 書成，除翰林修撰。 七年，遷國子博士。 明年，陞監丞。 十年，陞司業。 明年，遷翰林待制。 又明年，改禮部員外郎。 十三年，擢監察御史，尋復為國子司業。 明年，遷禮部郎中。 又明年，除翰林直學士。
In 1345, through his father Wen's yin privilege, he was appointed reviewing clerk of the Treasury of Ten Thousand Myriads but did not take up the post; on recommendation he was appointed assistant instructor of the Imperial Academy. He lectured diligently and supervised study strictly; all the students gladly followed him. When an edict was issued to compile the histories of Liao, Jin, and Song, Dang participated in the compilation. When the histories were completed he was appointed Hanlin compiler. In 1347 he was transferred to erudite of the Imperial Academy. The following year he was promoted to supervisor. In 1350 he was promoted to vice director. The following year he was transferred to Hanlin attendant drafter. The year after that he was reassigned vice minister of the Ministry of Rites. In 1353 he was promoted to surveillance censor and soon restored as vice director of the Imperial Academy. The following year he was transferred to director of the Ministry of Rites. The year after that he was appointed Hanlin academician.
42
時江南兵起且五年，大臣有薦當世居江西，習知江西民俗，且其才可任政事者，詔特授江西肅政廉訪使，偕江西行省參政火你赤、兵部尚書黃昭，招捕江西諸郡，便宜行事。 當以朝廷兵力不給，既受命至江南，即召募民兵，由浙入閩。 至江西境建昌界，招安新城孫塔，擒殄李三。 道路既通，乃進攻南豐，渠凶鄭天瑞遁，鄭原自刎死。 十六年，調檢校章迪率本部兵，與黃昭夾攻撫州，勦殺首寇胡志學，進兵復崇仁、宜黃。 於是建、撫兩郡悉定。
Warfare in the Jiangnan region had been raging for nearly five years when a great minister recommended Dang, who had long lived in Jiangxi and knew its customs well, as capable of government affairs. An edict specially appointed him surveillance commissioner of Jiangxi, together with Associate Administrator Huonichi of the Jiangxi Branch Secretariat and Minister of War Huang Zhao, to pacify the prefectures of Jiangxi with discretionary authority. Judging that the court's military strength was insufficient, once he received his commission and reached the Jiangnan region Dang immediately recruited militia and entered Fujian from Zhejiang. Reaching the Jianchang border in Jiangxi, he won over Sun Ta of Xincheng and captured and killed Li San. Once the roads were secure he advanced on Nanfeng; the ringleader Zheng Tianrui fled, and Zheng Yuan committed suicide. In 1356 he ordered Reviewing Officer Zhang Di to lead his troops and, together with Huang Zhao, attacked Fuzhou from both sides, killed the chief bandit Hu Zhixue, and advanced to recover Chongren and Yihuang. Thereupon the Jian and Fu regions were entirely pacified.
43
是時，參知政事朶歹緫兵撫、建，積年無功。 因忌當屢捷，功在己上，又以為南人不宜緫兵，則搆為飛語，謂當與黃昭皆與寇通。 有旨解二人兵柄，除當撫州路緫管，昭臨江路緫管，並供億平章火你赤軍。 火你赤殺當從事官范淳及章迪，將士皆憤怒不平，當諭之曰：「上命不可違也。 」而火你赤又上章言：「二人者，難任牧民。 」尋有旨當與昭皆罷緫管，除名。
At that time Associate Administrator Duodai had commanded troops in Fu and Jian for years without success. Resenting Dang's repeated victories and his own lesser merit, and believing that southerners should not command troops, he fabricated slanderous rumors claiming that Dang and Huang Zhao were both colluding with the rebels. An edict stripped them of military command, appointed Dang general manager of Fuzhou Circuit and Zhao general manager of Linjiang Circuit, and ordered them both to supply Grand Councillor Huonichi's army. Huonichi killed Dang's staff officers Fan Chun and Zhang Di; the officers and soldiers were furious. Dang told them: "The imperial command cannot be disobeyed." Huonichi also memorialized: "These two men are unfit to govern the people." Soon an edict dismissed Dang and Zhao from their posts as general managers and struck their names from the rolls.
44
十八年，火你赤自瑞州還龍興，當、昭皆隨軍不敢去。 先是，當與昭平賊功狀，自廣東由海道未達京師，而朶歹、火你赤等公牘乃先至，故朝廷責當、昭，皆左遷。 及得當、昭功狀，乃始知其誣，詔拜當中奉大夫、江西行省參知政事，昭湖廣行省參知政事。 命未下，而陳友諒已陷江西諸郡。 火你赤棄城遁，當乃戴黃冠，著道士服，杜門不出，日以著書為事。 友諒遣人辟之，當卧床不食，以死自誓，乃舁床載之舟，送江州，拘留一年，終不為屈。 遂隱居廬陵吉水之谷坪。 逾年，以疾卒，年六十五。 所著書，有周禮纂言及學言稿。
In 1358 Huonichi returned from Ruizhou to Longxing; Dang and Zhao both followed the army and dared not leave. Earlier, Dang's and Zhao's reports on pacifying the rebels, sent from Guangdong by sea, had not reached the capital, while the documents of Duodai, Huonichi, and others arrived first; the court therefore blamed Dang and Zhao and demoted them both. When Dang's and Zhao's merit reports arrived, the court learned of the false accusations and issued an edict appointing Dang Grand Master of the Palace Attendant and associate administrator of the Jiangxi Branch Secretariat, and Zhao associate administrator of the Huguang Branch Secretariat. Before the orders were issued, Chen Youliang had already seized the prefectures of Jiangxi. Huonichi abandoned the city and fled; Dang then put on a yellow cap and Daoist robes, shut his door, and devoted himself daily to writing. Youliang sent men to recruit him; Dang lay in bed and refused to eat, vowing to die rather than submit. They carried his bed onto a boat and sent him to Jiangzhou, where he was detained for a year, but in the end he would not yield. He then went into seclusion at Guping in Jishui, Luling. A little more than a year later he died of illness at the age of sixty-five. His works included Collected Explanations of the Rites of Zhou and Drafts of Learning.
