昔者史臣有言，婦人內夫家，雖天姬之貴，史氏猶外而弗詳。 然元室之制，非勳臣世族及封國之君，則莫得尚主，是以世聯戚畹者，親視諸王，其藩翰屏垣之寄，蓋亦重矣。 則其世次，顧可以弗之著耶？ 且秦漢以來，惟帝姬得號公主，而元則諸王之女亦概稱焉，是又不可不知也。 惜乎記載弗備，所可見者，僅此而已。 作諸公主表。
Historians of old observed that once a woman married into her husband's clan, even an emperor's daughter was regarded as outside the lineage and seldom recorded in full. Under Yuan custom, however, only families of merit—hereditary nobles and enfeoffed lords—might marry imperial princesses. Those bound to the throne by such marriages were treated as kin to the princes themselves, and the trust placed in them as bulwarks of the dynasty was considerable. Surely their generations ought to be recorded? Since Qin and Han times only an emperor's daughter had been called "princess," but under the Yuan the daughters of every prince were styled so as well—a distinction readers should bear in mind. Regrettably the records are fragmentary, and only what follows survives. Hence this table of princesses.
