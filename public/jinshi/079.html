列傳第十七○酈瓊李成孔彥舟徐文施宜生張中孚張中彥宇文虛中王倫
Biography 17 — Li Qiong, Li Cheng, Kong Yanzhou, Xu Wen, Shi Yisheng, Zhang Zhongfu, Zhang Zhongyan, Yu Wenxuzhong, and Wang Lun
2
酈瓊，字國寶，相州臨漳人。 補州學生。 宋宣和間，盜賊起，瓊乃更學擊刺挽強，試弓馬，隸宗澤軍，駐於磁州。 未幾告歸，括集義軍七百人，複從澤，澤署瓊為七百人長。 澤死，調戍滑州。 時宗望伐宋，將渡河。 戍軍亂，殺其統制趙世彥，而推瓊為主。 瓊因誘眾，號為勤王，行且收兵。 比渡淮，有眾萬餘。 康王以為楚州安撫使、淮南東路兵馬鈐轄，累遷武泰軍承宣使。 未幾，率所領步騎十余萬附于齊，授靜難軍節度使，知拱州。 齊國廢，以為博州防禦使。 用廉，遷驃騎上將軍。 宗弼複河南，以瓊為山東路弩手千戶，知亳州事。 丁母憂，去官。
Li Qiong, styled Guobao, came from Linzhang in Xiang Prefecture. He entered the prefectural school as a student. When banditry broke out in the Xuanhe era, Qiong turned instead to training in arms—swordplay, archery, and horsemanship—and joined Zong Ze's army, which was stationed at Ci Prefecture. Before long he went home on leave, raised a volunteer force of seven hundred men, and rejoined Zong Ze, who made him their commander. After Zong Ze's death, his unit was reassigned to garrison Hua Prefecture. Zongwang was then advancing against the Song and preparing to cross the Yellow River. The garrison mutinied, killed their commander Zhao Shiyan, and proclaimed Qiong their chief. Qiong rallied the men under the banner of rescuing the court and marched south, recruiting troops as he went. By the time he crossed the Huai, his force numbered more than ten thousand. Prince Kang made him pacification commissioner of Chuzhou and military intendant of Huainan East Circuit, and he rose in time to honorary commissioner of the Wutai Army. Before long he brought his force of more than a hundred thousand foot and horse to defect to Qi, receiving appointment as military governor of the Jingnan Army and prefect of Gong Prefecture. When the Qi puppet state was abolished, he was made defense commissioner of Bo Prefecture. Recognized for integrity in office, he was promoted to senior general of the Flying Cavalry. When Zongbi reconquered Henan, Qiong was made commander of a thousand crossbowmen on the Shandong Circuit and put in charge of Bozhou. He left office to observe mourning for his mother.
3
宗弼再伐江南，以瓊素知南方山川險易，召至軍與計事。 從容語同列曰：「瓊嘗從大軍南伐，每見元帥國王親臨陣督戰，矢石交集，而王免胄，指麾三軍，意氣自若，用兵制勝，皆與孫、吳合，可謂命世雄材矣。 至於親冒鋒鏑，進不避難，將士視之，孰敢愛死乎。 宜其所向無前，日辟國千里也。 江南諸帥，才能不及中人。 每當出兵，必身居數百裏外，謂之持重。 或督召軍旅，易置將校，僅以一介之士持虛文諭之，謂之調發。 制敵決勝委之偏裨，是以智者解體，愚者喪師。 幸一小捷，則露布飛馳，增加俘級以為己功，斂怨將士。 縱或親臨，亦必先遁。 而又國政不綱，才有微功，已加厚賞，或有大罪，乃置而不誅。 不即覆亡，已為天幸，何能振起耶？ 」眾以為確論。 元帥，謂宗弼也。
When Zongbi campaigned again against the south, he summoned Qiong—who knew the southern terrain well—to the army for counsel. He remarked casually to his colleagues: "When I marched south with the main army, I often saw the prince marshal take the field in person. Arrows and stones rained down, yet he would doff his helmet, command the three armies, and remain perfectly composed. The way he deployed troops to win matched Sunzi and Wuzi alike—truly a hero of the age." When he himself braved the front rank and advanced without flinching, what soldier watching him would hold back his life? Small wonder that his armies swept all before them and opened a thousand li of territory in a day. The southern commanders were men of no more than middling ability. Whenever they took the field they stayed hundreds of li behind the front, calling it prudent command. To move troops or replace officers they would send a lone courier with a formal order—this they called mobilization. They left the decision of battles to junior officers, so the able lost heart and the foolish lost their armies. After the slightest success they rushed out bulletins of victory, inflated the count of prisoners and heads to claim credit, and earned the hatred of their own troops. Even when they appeared in person, they were the first to run. Statecraft was equally lax: the smallest deed won lavish reward, while grave crimes went unpunished. That they had not already fallen was mere fortune—how could they ever recover? His listeners judged this exact. The "marshal" here means Zongbi.
4
及宗弼問瓊以江南成敗，誰敢相拒者。 瓊曰：「江南軍勢怯弱，皆敗亡之餘，又無良帥，何以禦我。 頗聞秦檜當國用事。 檜，老儒，所謂亡國之大夫，兢兢自守，惟顛覆是懼。 吾以大軍臨之，彼之君臣，方且心破膽裂，將哀鳴不暇，蓋傷弓之鳥可以虛弦下也。 」既而江南果稱臣，宗弼喜瓊為知言。
When Zongbi asked Qiong about the prospects south of the Yangtze—who there could dare resist them— "The southern armies are feeble—remnants of defeat—and lack able commanders. How could they withstand us? I hear that Qin Hui holds the reins of government. Hui is an aging scholar-official of the sort that ruins states—cautious, self-protective, and terrified of any overturn. When our great army advances, their court will be heartsick and terror-stricken, with no spirit even to lament—like a bird once struck by an arrow, they will fall at the twang of an empty bow. Soon afterward the south did indeed submit as a vassal, and Zongbi was delighted that Qiong had spoken so shrewdly.
5
李成，字伯友，雄州歸信人。 勇力絕倫，能挽弓三百斤。 宋宣和初，試弓手，挽強異等。 累官淮南招捉使。 成乃聚眾為盜，鈔掠江南，宋遣兵破之，成遂歸齊，累除知開德府，從大軍伐宋。 齊廢，再除安武軍節度使。
Li Cheng, styled Boyou, came from Guixin in Xiong Prefecture. His strength was unmatched—he could draw a three-hundred-jin bow. Early in the Xuanhe era he passed the archer examination with the highest rating for drawing the strong bow. He rose to become recruitment-and-suppression commissioner of Huainan. Cheng then gathered followers and turned bandit, raiding the south until Song forces defeated him. He defected to Qi, served successively as prefect of Kaide, and marched with the main army against Song. After Qi was abolished, he was again made military governor of the Anwu Army.
6
成在降附諸將中最勇鷙，號令甚嚴，眾莫敢犯。 臨陣身先諸將。 士卒未食不先食，有病者親視之。 不持雨具，雖沾濕自如也。 有告成反者，宗弼察其誣，使成自治，成杖而釋之，其不校如此。 以此士樂為用，所至克捷。
Among the defecting generals Cheng was the fiercest; his discipline was severe and none dared disobey. In battle he led from the front. He would not eat until his men had eaten, and he personally tended the sick. He carried no rain gear and was untroubled even when drenched. When someone accused him of rebellion, Zongbi saw the charge was false and let Cheng handle it himself; Cheng had the accuser beaten and released him—so little did he trouble himself over slights. Men were glad to follow him, and wherever he went he won.
7
宗弼再取河南，宋李興據河南府。 成引軍入孟津。 興率眾薄城，鼓噪請戰，成不應。 日下昃，興士卒倦且饑，成開門急擊，大破之。 興走漢南，成遂取洛陽、嵩、汝等。 河南平，宗弼奏成為河南尹，都管押本路兵馬。 嘗取官羨粟充公費，坐奪兩官，解職。 正隆間，起為真定尹，封郡王，例封濟國公。 卒，年六十九。
When Zongbi reconquered Henan, the Song general Li Xing held Luoyang. Cheng led his army to Meng Ford. Xing brought his force up to the walls, beating drums and clamoring for battle, but Cheng would not engage. As the afternoon waned and Xing's men grew weary and hungry, Cheng sallied out and routed them completely. Xing fled south of the Han, and Cheng took Luoyang, Songshan, Ruzhou, and the surrounding region. With Henan pacified, Zongbi recommended Cheng as intendant of Henan and overall commander of the circuit's forces. He once diverted official surplus grain to cover public expenses, for which he was demoted two ranks and dismissed. In the Zhenglong era he was recalled as intendant of Zhending, enfeoffed as a commandery prince, and by precedent as Duke of Ji. He died at sixty-nine.
8
孔彥舟
Kong Yanzhou
9
孔彥舟，字巨濟，相州林慮人。 亡賴，不事生產，避罪之汴，占籍軍中。 坐事系獄，說守者解其縛，乘夜逾城遁去。 已而殺人，亡命為盜。 宋靖康初，應募，累官京東西路兵馬鈐轄。 聞大軍將至山東，遂率所部，劫殺居民，燒廬舍，掠財物，渡河南去。 宋人複招之，以為沿江招捉使。 彥舟暴橫，不奉約束，宋人將以兵執之，彥舟走之齊，從劉麟伐宋，為行軍都統，改行營左總管。
Kong Yanzhou, styled Juji, came from Linlu in Xiang Prefecture. A wastrel who shunned honest work, he fled to Bian to escape the law and enrolled in the army. Imprisoned for an offense, he persuaded his jailer to loosen his bonds and escaped over the wall by night. He soon killed a man and took to the hills as a bandit. At the start of the Jingkang era he enlisted and rose to military intendant of the Jingdong and Jingxi Circuits. Learning that the Jin army was advancing into Shandong, he led his men south of the river, killing and plundering civilians, burning homes, and seizing goods as they went. The Song court recruited him again and made him riverine recruitment commissioner. Yanzhou was brutal and insubordinate; when the Song moved to arrest him by force, he fled to Qi. He followed Liu Lin against Song as overall field commander, later becoming left chief commander of the mobile camp.
10
齊國廢，累知淄州。 從宗弼取河南，克鄭州，擒其守劉政，破孟邦傑于登封，授鄭州防禦使。 討平太行車轅嶺賊。 從征江南，渡淮，破孫暉兵萬餘人，下安豐、霍丘。 及攻濠州，以彥舟為先鋒，順流薄城，擒其水軍統制邵青，遂克濠州。 師還，累官工、兵部尚書，河南尹，封廣平郡王。 正隆例降金紫光祿大夫，改西京留守。
After Qi was abolished, he served repeatedly as prefect of Zi Prefecture. He followed Zongbi in the reconquest of Henan, took Zhengzhou, captured its defender Liu Zheng, defeated Meng Bangjie at Dengfeng, and was made defense commissioner of Zhengzhou. He suppressed the bandits at Cheyuan Ridge in the Taihang range. In the southern campaign he crossed the Huai, routed more than ten thousand of Sun Hui's men, and captured Anfeng and Huoqiu. At the assault on Haozhou he led the vanguard downstream to the walls, captured the Song naval commander Shao Qing, and took the city. On the army's return he rose to minister of works and war, intendant of Henan, and was enfeoffed as Prince of Guangping. Under the Zhenglong demotion of ranks he was reduced to Grand Mentor of the Golden Purple Radiance and made defender of the Western Capital.
11
彥舟荒於色，有禽獸行。 妾生女姿麗，彥舟苦虐其母，使自陳非己女，遂納為妾。 其官屬負官錢，私其妻與折券。 惟破濠州時，諸軍凡系獲皆殺之，彥舟號令毋輒殺，免者數千人，人頗以此稱之。 然自幼至老常在行伍，習兵事，知利鈍。 海陵欲以為征南將佐，正隆五年，除南京留守。
Yanzhou was debauched and behaved like an animal. When a concubine bore a beautiful daughter, Yanzhou tortured the mother until she swore the child was not his, then took the girl as his own concubine. When a subordinate owed official funds, he seized the man's wife and applied her value against the debt. Only at the fall of Haozhou, when other armies slaughtered every prisoner, did Yanzhou order that none be killed without cause, sparing several thousand—an act for which people gave him some credit. Yet from youth to old age he lived in the ranks, knew warfare, and understood when to press and when to hold back. Hailing intended him as a commander for the southern campaign; in the fifth year of Zhenglong he was made defender of the Southern Capital.
12
彥舟有疾，朝臣有傳彥舟死者，而彥舟尚無恙，海陵盡杖妄傳彥舟死者，以激勵之。 無何竟死於汴，年五十五。 遺表言「伐宋當先取淮南」雲。
When Yanzhou fell ill, court rumor declared him dead though he was still alive; Hailing had everyone who spread the false report beaten, to rouse Yanzhou's spirit. Before long he died at Bian, aged fifty-five. His deathbed memorial advised that any campaign against Song should begin by seizing Huainan.
13
徐文，字彥武，萊州掖縣人，徙膠水。 少時販鹽為業，往來瀕海數州，剛勇尚氣，儕輩皆憚之。 宋季盜起，募戰士，為密州板橋左十將。 勇力過人，揮巨刀重五十斤，所向無前，人呼為「徐大刀」。 後隸王龍圖麾下，與夏人戰，生擒一將，補進武校尉。 東還，破群賊楊進等，轉承信郎。
Xu Wen, styled Yanwu, was born in Ye County, Laizhou, and later moved to Jiaoshui. In youth he traded salt along the coast, bold and quick to take offense; his companions all feared him. When banditry broke out late in the Song, he enlisted and became left tenth commander at Banqiao in Mizhou. His strength was extraordinary: he swung a fifty-jin great blade and cut through every foe—men called him "Xu the Great Blade." He later served under Wang the Dragon Diagram, captured a Xia general alive in battle, and was appointed probationary military training commissioner. On his return east he defeated the bandit Yang Jin and others and was promoted to gentleman for extending trust.
14
東海縣人徐元、張旺作亂，縣人房真等三人走海州，及走總管府，上變。 州、府皆遣使效隨真等詣東海觀賊形勢，皆為賊所害。 州、府合兵攻之，累月不下。 海陵且欲伐宋，惡聞其事，詔文與步軍指揮使張弘信、同知大興尹李惟忠、宿直將軍蕭阿窊率舟師九百浮海討之，謂文等曰：「朕意不在一邑，將以試舟師耳。 」文等至東海，與賊戰，敗之，斬首五千餘級，獲徐元、張旺，餘眾請降。 是役也，張弘信行至萊州，稱疾留止，日與妓樂飲酒。 海陵聞之，師還，杖弘信二百。 文遷定海軍節度使。 房真三人官賞有差。 死賊者皆贈官三級，以銀百兩、絹百匹賜其家。
When Xu Yuan and Zhang Wang rebelled in Donghai County, three locals including Fang Zhen fled first to Haizhou and then to the regional headquarters to report the uprising. Both Haizhou and the regional command sent envoys after Fang Zhen to scout the rebels at Donghai; the rebels killed them all. Joint forces from prefecture and region besieged them for months without success. Hailing was preparing to campaign against Song and disliked the distraction; he ordered Wen, infantry commander Zhang Hongxin, vice-director of Daxing Li Weizhong, and palace-duty general Xiao A'ao to take nine hundred ships by sea against the rebels, telling them: "My aim is not one county alone—I mean to test the fleet." Wen and his force reached Donghai, defeated the rebels, took more than five thousand heads, captured Xu Yuan and Zhang Wang, and the rest surrendered. During the campaign Zhang Hongxin stopped at Laizhou on pretext of illness and spent his days drinking with entertainers. When Hailing learned of this after the army's return, he had Hongxin beaten two hundred strokes. Wen was promoted to military governor of the Dinghai Army. Fang Zhen and his two companions received official rewards in varying degrees. Those who died fighting the rebels were posthumously promoted three ranks, and their families received one hundred taels of silver and one hundred bolts of silk.
15
大定二年，詣闕自陳年老目昏，懇求致仕。 許之。 以覃恩遷龍虎衛上將軍，卒于家。
In the second year of Dading he came to court, citing age and failing eyesight, and begged to retire. The request was granted. On a grace-bestowal promotion he was made senior general of the Dragon-Tiger Guard and died at home.
16
施宜生
Shi Yisheng
17
四年冬，為宋國正旦使。 宜生自以得罪北走，恥見宋人，力辭，不許。 宋命張燾館之都亭，因間以首丘風之。 宜生顧其介不在旁，為廋語曰：「今日北風甚勁。 」又取幾間筆扣之曰：「筆來，筆來。 」於是宋始警。 其副使耶律辟離剌使還以聞，坐是烹死。
In the winter of the fourth year he was appointed New Year's envoy to Song. Yisheng felt he had disgraced himself by defecting north and was ashamed to face his countrymen; he pleaded hard to be excused, but the court refused. Song assigned Zhang Tao to host him at the Duting Pavilion and, when he could, gently evoked the old longing for home—the fox that dies facing its mound. Seeing his attendant out of earshot, Yisheng spoke in veiled words: "The north wind blows very hard today." Then he took a brush from the table and tapped it, crying, "The brush comes—the brush comes!"—a homophone for "troops are coming." Only then did Song take warning. When his deputy Yelü Pilici returned and reported the warning, he was executed by boiling for it.
18
初，宜生困於場屋，遇僧善風鑒，謂之曰：「子面有權骨，可公可卿。 而視子身之毛，皆逆上，且覆腕，必有以合乎此而後可貴也。 」宜生聞其言，大喜，竟從范汝為於建、劍。 已而汝為敗，變服為傭泰之吳翁家三年。 翁異之，一日屏人詰其姓名，宜生曰：「我服傭事惟謹，主人乃亦置疑邪？ 」翁固詰之，則請其故。 翁曰：「日者燕客，執事咸餕，而汝獨孫諸儕，且撤器有歎聲，是以識汝非真傭也。 」宜生遂告之故。 翁贐之金，夜濟淮以歸。 試《一日獲熊三十六賦》擢第一，其後竟如僧言。
Long before, Yisheng had languished in the civil examinations when a monk skilled in physiognomy told him, "Your face shows the bone of command—you are fit to become a duke or a minister." But the hair on your body grows against the grain, and when you bend your wrist it curls back over it—you must find a path that fits these signs before you can rise to greatness." Yisheng was delighted by this prophecy and eventually joined Fan Ruwei's rebellion in Jian and Jian Prefectures. When Ruwei was defeated, Yisheng disguised himself and hired out as a servant to Old Man Wu in Taizhou for three years. The old man found him odd; one day he sent the others away and pressed him for his real name. Yisheng said, "I have served you faithfully—surely you do not doubt me as well?" When the old man persisted, Yisheng asked why. The old man said, "When I entertained guests the other day, every servant ate his fill while you alone hung back; and when you cleared the dishes I heard you sigh. That is how I knew you were no ordinary laborer." Yisheng then told him the whole story. The old man gave him money for the journey, and Yisheng crossed the Huai by night to go home. He entered the examination with the rhapsody "Thirty-Six Bears Taken in a Single Day" and placed first; in time everything came to pass just as the monk had foretold.
19
張中孚
Zhang Zhongfu
20
張中孚，字信甫，其先自安定徙居張義堡。 父達，仕宋至太師，封慶國公。 中孚以父任補承節郎。 宗翰圍太原，其父戰歿，中孚泣涕請跡父屍，乃獨率部曲十余人入大軍中，竟得其屍以還。 累官知鎮戎軍兼安撫使，屢從吳玠、張浚以兵拒大軍。 浚走巴蜀，中孚權帥事。 天會八年，睿宗以左副元帥次涇州，中孚率其將吏來降，睿宗以為鎮洮軍節度使知渭州，兼涇原路經略安撫使。
Zhang Zhongfu, styled Xinfu, came from a family that had moved from Anding to Zhangyi Fortress. His father Da had served Song as Grand Preceptor and was enfeoffed as Duke of Qing. Zhongfu entered office by virtue of his father's rank, receiving appointment as gentleman for extending propriety. When Zonghan besieged Taiyuan, his father fell in battle. Zhongfu wept and pleaded to recover the body, then led a dozen personal followers alone into the Jin host and brought his father's corpse back. He rose to prefect of Zhenrong Army and pacification commissioner, and repeatedly joined Wu Jie and Zhang Jun in resisting the Jin armies. When Zhang Jun fled to Ba-Shu, Zhongfu took temporary command. In the eighth year of Tianhui, Prince Ruizong encamped at Jing Prefecture as left deputy grand marshal; Zhongfu surrendered with his officers and was made military governor of the Zhentao Army and prefect of Wei, with concurrent charge of Jingyuan frontier pacification.
21
齊國建，以什一法括民田，籍丁壯為鄉軍。 中孚以為涇原地瘠無良田，且保甲之法行之已習，今遽紛更，人必逃徙，只見其害，未見其利也。 竟執不行。 時齊政甚急，莫敢違，人為中孚懼，而中孚不之顧。 未幾齊國廢，一路獨免掊克之患。
When the puppet Qi state was set up, it confiscated private land under a tithe law and registered able-bodied men as village militia. Zhongfu argued that Jingyuan was poor land with few good fields, and that the baojia system was already working—abrupt change would only drive people to flee, with harm and no gain. He held firm and refused to carry it out. Qi's rule was harsh and no one else dared resist; many feared for Zhongfu, but he ignored them. When Qi was abolished not long after, his circuit alone had been spared the worst exactions.
22
中孚天性孝友剛毅，與弟中彥居，未嘗有間言。 喜讀書，頗能書翰。 其禦士卒嚴而有恩，西人尤畏愛之。 葬之日，老稚扶柩流涕蓋數萬人，至為罷市，其得西人之望如此。 正隆例封崇進、原國公。
Filial, brotherly, and resolute by nature, Zhongfu lived with his younger brother Zhongyan without ever a word of discord between them. He loved books and wrote a capable hand. He commanded his troops with strictness and kindness, and the western frontier people both feared and loved him. On the day of his funeral, tens of thousands of old and young followed the bier in tears and markets closed in mourning—such was the regard he commanded in the west. Under the Zhenglong enfeoffment rules he was posthumously made Duke who Honors Advance and Duke of Yuan.
23
張中彥
Zhang Zhongyan
24
張中彥，字才甫，中孚弟。 少以父任仕宋，為涇原副將，知德順軍事。 睿宗經略陝西，中彥降，除招撫使。 從下熙、河、階、成州，授彰武軍承宣使，為本路兵馬鈐轄，遷都總管。
Zhang Zhongyan, styled Caifu, was Zhongfu's younger brother. He entered Song service young through his father's rank, serving as deputy general of Jingyuan and military administrator of Deshun. When Prince Ruizong secured Shaanxi, Zhongyan defected and was made pacification commissioner. After the fall of Xi, He, Jie, and Cheng, he received honorary commissioner of the Zhangwu Army, became military intendant of the circuit, and was promoted to overall commander.
25
宋將關師古圍鞏州，與秦鳳李彥琦會兵攻之。 王師下饒風關，得金、洋諸州，以中彥領興元尹，撫輯新附。 師還，代彥琦為秦鳳經略使。 秦州當要衝而城不可守，中彥徙治北山，因險為壘，今秦州是也。 築臘家諸城，以扼蜀道。 帥秦凡十年，改涇原路經略使知平涼府。
When the Song general Guan Shigu besieged Gong Prefecture, Zhongyan joined Li Yanqi of Qin-Feng in the assault. When the imperial army broke through Raofeng Pass and took Jin, Yang, and neighboring prefectures, Zhongyan was made governor of Xingyuan to pacify the newly submitted territories. After the army withdrew, he succeeded Li Yanqi as frontier commissioner of Qin-Feng. Qin Prefecture lay on a vital route but its old city was indefensible; Zhongyan moved the seat to North Mountain and built fortifications along the heights—the present Qin Prefecture stands there still. He fortified Lajia and other towns to block the road into Shu. He governed Qin for ten years, then became frontier commissioner of Jingyuan and prefect of Pingliang.
26
朝廷以河南、陝西賜宋，中孚以官守隨例當留關中。 熙河經略使慕洧謀入夏，將窺關、陝，中彥與環慶趙彬會兩路兵討之，洧敗入于夏。 中彥與兄中孚俱至臨安，被留，以為龍神衛四廂都指揮使，清遠軍承宣使，提舉佑神觀，靖海軍節度使。
When the court ceded Henan and Shaanxi to Song, Zhongfu by precedent was expected to remain in Guanzhong with his post. Mu Wei, frontier commissioner of Xi-He, plotted to defect to Xia and threaten Guanzhong and Shaanxi; Zhongyan and Zhao Bin of Huanqing combined their forces to crush him, and Mu Wei fled into Xia after defeat. Zhongyan and his brother Zhongfu both reached Lin'an and were detained, receiving nominal posts as commander of the Dragon Spirit Guard, honorary commissioner of the Qingyuan Army, director of the You Spirit Abbey, and military governor of the Jinghai Army.
27
皇統初，恢復河南，詔征中彥兄弟北歸，為靜難軍節度使，曆彰化軍、鳳翔尹，改尹慶陽，兼慶原路兵馬都總管、甯州刺史。 宗室宗淵毆死僚佐梁鬱。 鬱遠人，家貧無能赴告者。 中彥力為正其罪，竟置於法。 改彰德軍節度使，均賦調法，奸豪無所蔽匿，人服其明。
When Henan was recovered at the start of Huangtong, the brothers were recalled north; Zhongyan became military governor of the Jingnan Army, later governor of Zhanghua and Fengxiang, then governor of Qingyang with concurrent command of Qingyuan forces and the prefecture of Ning. The imperial clansman Zongyuan beat his aide Liang Yu to death. Yu was an outsider, poor, with no one who could afford to seek justice for him. Zhongyan pressed the case until Zongyuan was punished according to law. As military governor of the Zhangde Army he reformed tax and levy assessments so that local bullies could no longer conceal their holdings, and the people respected his fairness.
28
正隆營汴京新宮，中彥采運關中材木。 青峰山巨木最多，而高深阻絕，唐、宋以來不能致。 中彥使構崖駕壑，起長橋十數裏，以車運木，若行平地，開六盤山水洛之路，遂通汴梁。 明年，作河上浮梁，複領其役。 舟之始制，匠者未得其法，中彥手制小舟才數寸許，不假膠漆而首尾自相鉤帶，謂之「鼓子卯」，諸匠無不駭服，其智巧如此。 浮梁巨艦畢功，將發旁郡民曳之就水。 中彥召役夫數十人，治地勢順下傾瀉於河，取新秫秸密佈於地，複以大木限其旁，淩晨督眾乘霜滑曳之，殊不勞力而致諸水。
When the Zhenglong emperor built the new palace at Bianjing, Zhongyan was charged with supplying timber from Guanzhong. Qingfeng Mountain held the finest timber, but its slopes were steep and inaccessible—since Tang and Song times no one had managed to haul logs from there. Zhongyan built paths across cliffs and gorges and raised bridges more than ten li long so that carts could haul timber as on flat ground; he opened the route through Liupan and Shuiluo, and timber reached Bianliang. The next year, when a floating bridge was built across the river, he again directed the work. When shipbuilding began, the craftsmen could not get the joints right; Zhongyan carved a model boat only a few inches long whose bow and stern locked together without glue or lacquer—a joint he called the "drum mortise-tenon." Every craftsman was astonished; such was his ingenuity. When the floating bridge and great ships were finished, laborers from neighboring prefectures were to be drafted to drag them to the water. Zhongyan called up a few dozen workers, graded the ground to slope toward the river, laid fresh sorghum stalks thick on the path, and bound the sides with heavy beams; at dawn he had the men haul the ships over the frost-slick track, and with little strain they reached the water.
29
逾年，除南京留守。 時淮楚用兵，土民與戍兵雜居，訟牒紛紜，所司皆依違不決。 中彥得戍兵為盜者，悉論如法，帥府怒其專決，劾奏之，朝廷置而不問。 秩滿，轉真定尹兼河北西路兵馬都總管。 未幾，致仕，西歸京兆。 明年，起為臨洮尹兼熙秦路兵馬都總管。 鞏州劉海構亂，既敗，籍民之從亂者數千人，中彥惟論為首者戮之。
A year later he was made military governor of the Southern Capital. War was then raging in Huai and Chu; locals and garrison troops lived side by side, lawsuits multiplied, and officials everywhere hedged and delayed judgment. When he caught garrison soldiers stealing, he punished every case by the book; the marshal's headquarters resented his independent rulings and impeached him, but the court took no notice. At the end of his term he became governor of Zhending with concurrent command of Hebei West forces. Before long he retired and went west to Jingzhao. The next year he was recalled as governor of Lintao with concurrent command of Xi-Qin forces. After Liu Hai's rebellion in Gong Prefecture was crushed, thousands who had joined him were listed for punishment, but Zhongyan executed only the ringleaders.
30
西羌吹折、密臧、隴逋、龐拜四族恃險不服，使侍御史沙醇之就中彥論方略，中彥曰：「此羌服叛不常，若非中彥自行，勢必不可。 」即至積石達南寺，酋長四人來，與之約降，事遂定，賞而遣之。 還奏，上大悅，遣張汝玉馳驛勞之，賜以球文金帶，用郊恩加儀同三司。 以疾卒官，年七十五。 百姓哀號輟市，立像祀之。
Four western Qiang tribes—Chuizhe, Mizang, Longbu, and Pangbai—held the high country and refused submission; the court sent investigating censor Sha Chunzhi to consult Zhongyan. He said, "These tribes shift between loyalty and rebellion at will—unless I go myself, nothing can be done." He went at once to Jishi and Nan Temple at Dasi; the four chieftains came to meet him, agreed to submit, and after rewards were given the matter was settled. On his report the emperor was delighted, sent Zhang Ruyu by relay horse to congratulate him, granted a patterned gold belt, and on a suburban grace-bestowal promoted him to honorary three masters. He died in office of illness at seventy-five. The people mourned with such grief that markets closed, and they set up images in his honor.
31
贊曰：自古健將武夫，其不才者，遭世變遷，賣降恐後。 此其常態，君子之所不責也，酈瓊、徐文是已。 施宜生反覆壬人，李成盜賊之靡，孔彥舟漁色親出，自絕人類，又何責也。 張中孚、中彥雖有小惠足稱，然以宋大臣之子，父戰沒于金，若金若齊，義皆不共戴天之仇。 金以地與齊則甘心臣齊，以地歸宋則忍恥臣宋，金取其地則又比肩臣金，若趨市然，唯利所在，于斯時也，豈複知所謂綱常也哉。 籲！
Commentary: Strong generals and fighting men without real ability have always, when the times turned, scrambled to surrender lest they be left behind. That is the common pattern, and gentlemen do not much blame them for it—Li Qiong and Xu Wen are cases in point. Shi Yisheng was a fickle schemer, Li Cheng a bandit chieftain, Kong Yanzhou a man who preyed on women even in his own family—they had already cut themselves off from humanity, and what further blame could matter? Zhang Zhongfu and Zhongyan had minor virtues worth noting, yet as sons of a Song grand minister whose father had fallen fighting the Jin, both Jin and Qi were by every moral reckoning irreconcilable foes. When Jin ceded territory to Qi they gladly submitted to Qi; when it went back to Song they swallowed their shame and served Song; when Jin seized it again they stood beside Jin's ministers as equals—like men rushing to market wherever profit led. In such an age, could they still have known what moral duty meant? Alas!
32
宇文虛中
Yu Wenxuzhong
33
虛中恃才輕肆，好譏訕，凡見女直人輒以礦鹵目之，貴人達官往往積不能平。 虛中嘗撰宮殿榜署，本皆嘉美之名，惡虛中者擿其字以為謗訕朝廷，由是媒糵以成其罪矣。 六年二月，唐括酬斡家奴杜天佛留告虛中謀反，詔有司鞫治無狀，乃羅織虛中家圖書為反具，虛中曰：「死自吾分。 至於圖籍，南來士大夫家家有之，高士談圖書尤多於我家，豈亦反耶？ 」有司承順風旨並殺士談，至今冤之。
Xuzhong was talented, arrogant, and fond of mockery; he called every Jurchen he met a crude barbarian, and many high officials nursed lasting grudges against him. Xuzhong had once written inscription plaques for palace halls—all originally fine, auspicious names—but his enemies parsed the characters as slander of the court and fabricated a case against him. In the second month of the sixth year, Tangguo Chouwo's house slave Du Tianfoliu accused Xuzhong of plotting rebellion; though the investigation found nothing, officials fabricated a treason case from the books in his home. Xuzhong said, "Death is my portion." As for books, every southern scholar who came north keeps them; Gao Shitan's library is larger than mine—is he a rebel as well?" The officials, reading the court's mood, killed Shitan too—a wrong mourned to this day.
34
士談字季默，高瓊之後。 宣和末，為忻州戶曹參軍。 入朝，官至翰林直學士。 虛中、士談俱有文集行於世。
Shitan, styled Jimo, was a descendant of Gao Qiong. At the end of the Xuanhe era he served as registrar of households in Xin Prefecture. He entered court service and rose to Hanlin academician. Collected works by both Xuzhong and Shitan circulated in their day.
35
王倫，字正道，故宋宰相王旦弟王勉玄孫。 俠邪無賴，年四十余尚與市井惡少群游汴中。 天會五年，宋人以倫為假刑部侍郎，與閣門舍人硃弁充通問使。 是時，方議伐宋，凡宋使者如倫及宇文虛中、魏行可、顧縱、張邵等，皆留之不遣。 居數年，倫久困，乃唱為和議求歸。 元帥府使人謂之曰：「此非江南情實，特汝自為此言耳。 」倫曰：「使事有指，不然何為來哉。 惟元帥察之。」
Wang Lun, styled Zhengdao, was the great-great-grandson of Wang Mian, younger brother of the former Song chief minister Wang Dan. A rakish wastrel, he was past forty and still roaming Bianjing with street thugs. In the fifth year of Tianhui, Song appointed Lun acting vice minister of punishments and, with gatehouse attendant Zhu Bian, sent them as envoys to open communications. An invasion of Song was then under discussion, and every Song envoy—Lun, Yu Wenxuzhong, Wei Xingke, Gu Zong, Zhang Shao, and the rest—was detained and not allowed to return. After years of confinement, Lun, worn down by captivity, began urging peace talks as a way home. An envoy from the marshal's headquarters told him, "That is not how the south truly feels—you are saying this on your own." Lun replied, "An envoy's mission has its purpose—otherwise why would I have come?" "Let the grand marshal judge for himself."
36
天會十年，劉豫連歲出師皆無功，撻懶為元帥左監軍經略南邊，密主和議，乃遣倫歸。 先此，宋已遣使乞和，朝廷未之許也。 倫見康王言和議事，康王大喜，遷倫官，並官其子弟。 宋方與齊用兵，未可和。
In the tenth year of Tianhui, Liu Yu's repeated campaigns had failed; Tadun, left supervising general of the marshal's headquarters and overseer of the southern frontier, secretly favored peace and sent Lun home. Before this, Song had already sent envoys suing for peace, but the Jin court had refused. Lun met Prince Kang and spoke of peace; the prince was delighted, promoted Lun, and enfeoffed his sons and brothers as well. Song was still at war with Qi, so peace was not yet possible.
37
明年，宋以倫為端明殿學士，簽書樞密院事，進金器千兩、銀器萬兩，複來請天水郡王喪柩，及請母韋氏兄弟宗族等。 保信軍節度使藍公佐副之。 是歲，宗磐、宗雋、撻懶皆以謀反屬吏，熙宗誅宗磐、宗雋，以撻懶屬尊，赦其死，以為行台尚書省事左丞相，奪其兵權。 右副元帥宗弼奏曰：「撻懶、宗磐陰與宋人交通，遂以河南、陝西地與宋人。 」會撻懶複謀反，捕而殺之于祁州。 倫至上京，有司詳讀康王表文，不書年，閱進奉狀，稱禮物不言職貢，上使宰相責問倫曰：「汝但知有元帥，豈知有上國耶。 」遂留不遣，遣其副藍公佐歸。
The following year Song appointed Lun academician of the Duanming Hall and deputy director of the Bureau of Military Affairs, sent one thousand taels of gold vessels and ten thousand of silver, and again asked for the coffin of the Prince of Tianshui, his mother Lady Wei, his brothers, and the rest of the imperial clan held captive. Lan Gongzuo, military governor of the Baoxin Army, accompanied him as deputy envoy. That year Zongpan, Zongjun, and Tadun were all implicated in treason; Emperor Xizong executed Zongpan and Zongjun, but because Tadun was of the imperial line he spared his life, made him left chief minister of the Branch Secretariat, and stripped him of military command. Deputy marshal Zongbi memorialized: "Tadun and Zongpan secretly colluded with Song and ceded Henan and Shaanxi to them." When Tadun plotted rebellion again, he was captured and executed at Qizhou. When Lun reached the Supreme Capital, officials scrutinized Prince Kang's memorial and found no reign year; the tribute list called the items "gifts" rather than tribute. The emperor had his ministers rebuke Lun: "You know only the marshal—do you know there is an emperor above?" Lun was detained; only his deputy Lan Gongzuo was sent home.
38
四年，以倫為平州路轉運使，倫已受命，複辭遜，上曰：「此反復之人也。 」遂殺之於上京，年六十一。
In the fourth year Lun was appointed transport commissioner of Pingzhou; having accepted the post, he declined again with false modesty. The emperor said: "This man blows with every wind." He was executed at the Supreme Capital, aged sixty-one.
39
贊曰：孔子雲：「行己有恥，使于四方不辱君命，可謂士矣」。 宇文虛中朝至上京，夕受官爵。 王倫紈袴之子，市井為徒。 此豈「行己有恥」之士，可以專使者耶？ 二子之死雖冤，其自取亦多矣。
The historian comments: Confucius said, "If in conduct he has a sense of shame, and abroad he does not disgrace his prince's commission, he may be called a true gentleman." Yu Wenxuzhong reached the Supreme Capital in the morning and accepted Jin office by evening. Wang Lun was born to the silk-trousered elite yet kept company with market thugs. Were these men of "conduct with a sense of shame" fit to bear their prince's commission alone? Their deaths were unjust, yet they had largely brought their fates upon themselves.
