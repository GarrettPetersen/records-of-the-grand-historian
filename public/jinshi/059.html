古者太史掌敘邦國之世次，辨其姓氏，別其昭穆，尚失。 金人初起完毅十二部，其後皆以部為氏，史臣記錄有稱「宗室」者，有稱完顏者。 稱完顏者。 稱完顏者亦有二焉，有同姓完顏，蓋疏族，若石土門、迪古乃是也； 有異姓完顏，蓋部人，若歡都是也。 大定以前稱「宗室」，明昌以後避睿宗諱稱「內族」，其實一而已，書名不書氏，其制如此。 宣宗詔宗室皆稱完顏，不復識別焉。 大定、泰和之間，袒免以上親皆有屬籍，以敘授官，大功以上，薨卒輟朝，親親之道行焉。 貞祐以後，譜牒散失，大概僅存，不可殫悉，今掇其可次第者著於篇。 其上無所系、下無所承者，不能盡錄也。
Even in ancient times the Grand Historian who ordered the ages of the realms, sorted clan names, and marked senior from junior lines could not keep the record whole. The Jin first gathered in the twelve Wanyi tribes, then took each tribe as a clan name. Court historians sometimes wrote "Imperial Clan", sometimes Wanyan. As for those called Wanyan: "Wanyan" itself split two ways: same-surname Wanyan were really distant kin, men like Shitumen and Digunai; different-surname Wanyan were really tribesmen, men like Huandu. Before Dading they were called "Imperial Clan"; after Mingchang, to avoid the taboo on Ruizong, "Inner Kin"—the same group in fact. Registers record personal names, not clan names; that was the rule. Emperor Xuanzong ordered that all imperial kin take the name Wanyan, and the old distinctions ceased. From Dading through Taihe, relatives within tan-mian degree and nearer were entered in the clan rolls and ranked for appointment; when one within da-gong degree or nearer died, court paused in mourning—kinship was honored in deed. After Zhenyou the pedigree books were lost; a broken outline alone remains, too fragmentary to exhaust. Here I collect what can still be sequenced and place it in these pages. Lines that attach to nothing above and hand down to nothing below cannot be entered in full.
