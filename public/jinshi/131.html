1
古之宦者皆出於刑人，刑余不可列於士庶，故掌宮寺之事，謂之「婦寺」焉。 東漢以來，宦者養子以繼世。 唐世，繼者皆為閹人，其初進也，性多巧慧便僻、善固恩寵，及其得志，黨比糾結不可制。 東漢以宦者亡，唐又甚焉。 世儒論宦者之害，如毒藥猛虎之不可拯也。 金法置近侍局，嘗與政事，而宦者少與焉。 惟海陵時有梁珫，章宗時有梁道、李新喜幹政，二君為所誤多矣。 世傳梁道勸章宗納李妃後宮，金史不載梁道始末，弗得而論次之。 惟宋珪、潘守恆頗能諷諫宣、哀，時有裨益，蓋傭之佼佼、鐵之錚錚者也。 作《宦者傳》。
In ancient times every court eunuch sprang from the penal system—men mutilated under criminal sentence could not stand among scholars or commoners, so they were charged with palace and temple affairs and were known as "inner-chamber attendants." From the Eastern Han on, eunuchs took adopted sons to carry on their households. Under the Tang, every successor was a castrated man. On first entering court they tended to be clever, nimble, and adept at holding royal favor; once they rose to power, their cliques and entanglements proved impossible to curb. The Eastern Han dynasty fell victim to its eunuchs; the Tang suffered worse still. Contemporary literati spoke of the damage done by eunuchs as an evil no more remediable than poison or a ravening tiger. Under Jin law the Bureau of Personal Attendants was set up and sometimes took part in state affairs, yet eunuchs figured in that role only rarely. Only in Hailing's reign did Liang Chong wield power; only under Zhangzong did Liang Dao and Li Xinxi meddle in government—and both emperors were led astray by them again and again. Tradition holds that Liang Dao urged Zhangzong to install Consort Li in the inner palace, but the Jin History does not record his career from beginning to end, so he cannot be assessed here in due order. Only Song Gui and Pan Shouheng were able to offer pointed, indirect remonstrance to Emperors Xuan and Ai and at times did real good—outstanding among hirelings, ringing true as tempered iron. Hence the compilation of the Biographies of Eunuchs.
2
梁珫，本大抃家奴，隨元妃入宮，以閹豎事海陵。 珫性便佞，善迎合，特見寵信，舊制，宦者惟掌掖廷宮闈之事。 天德三年，始以王光道為內藏庫使，衛愈、梁安仁皆以宦官領內藏。 海陵謂光道等曰：「人言宦者不可用，朕以為不然。 後唐莊宗委張承業以軍，竟立大功，此中豈無人乎。 卿等宜悉此意。 帑藏之物皆出民力，費十致一，當糾察奸弊，犯者必罰無赦。 」宦者始與政事，而珫委任尤甚，累官近侍局使。 及營建南京宮室，海陵數數使珫往視工役。 是時，一殿之費已不可勝計，珫或言其未善，即盡撒去。 雖丞相張浩亦曲意事之，與之均禮。
Liang Chong had begun as a slave in the Dabian household. He entered the palace with the primary consort and, as a castrated attendant, served Emperor Hailing. Chong was smooth and obsequious, expert at currying favor, and won exceptional trust. Under the old rules eunuchs handled only the inner palace and women's quarters. In the third year of Tiande (1151), Wang Guangdao became the first Director of the Inner Treasury; Wei Yu and Liang Anren likewise took charge of the inner treasury as eunuchs. Hailing told Guangdao and the others, "People say eunuchs are useless. I disagree. Emperor Zhuangzong of Later Tang entrusted Zhang Chengye with his armies, and Zhang won great distinction—surely capable men can be found among you as well. Take this to heart. Everything in the treasury comes from the people's labor—ten parts spent may yield only one part stored. Root out fraud and abuse; offenders must be punished without mercy. " From then on eunuchs took part in government, and Chong's authority was greater than any other's; he rose step by step to head the Bureau of Personal Attendants. While the Southern Capital palace was under construction, Hailing sent Chong again and again to inspect the work. By then a single hall's cost was beyond reckoning; if Chong said the work was not good enough, the whole structure was torn down at once. Even Chancellor Zhang Hao deferred to him and accorded him equal ceremonial standing.
3
海陵欲伐宋，珫因極言宋劉貴妃絕色傾國。 海陵大喜，及南征將行，命縣君高師姑兒貯衾褥之新潔者俟得劉貴妃用之。 議者言珫與宋通謀，勸帝伐宋，征天下兵以疲弊中國。
When Hailing planned to attack the Song, Chong dwelt at length on the peerless beauty of the Song Honored Consort Liu, beauty said to be enough to topple a realm. Hailing was delighted. As the southern campaign was about to set out, he ordered the county lady Gao Shigu'er to keep fresh bedding ready for the day he might take Honored Consort Liu. Some charged that Chong was in league with the Song—that he had urged the emperor to invade the south and levy troops empire-wide in order to wear down the Jin state.
4
海陵至和州，聞珫與宋人交通有狀，謂珫曰：「聞汝與宋國交通，傳泄事情。 汝本奴隸，朕拔擢至此，乃敢爾耶。 若至江南詢得實跡，殺汝亦未晚也。 」又謂校書郎田與信曰：「爾面目亦可疑，必與珫同謀者。 」皆命執於軍中。 海陵遇弑，珫、與信皆為亂軍所殺。
When Hailing reached Hezhou he learned there was proof of Chong's traffic with the Song. He told Chong, "I hear you have been dealing with the Song and leaking state business. You were a slave; I raised you to this height—how dare you do such a thing? If we reach the south and inquiry confirms it, your execution will not be too late. " He also told the collator Tian Yuxin, "Your face looks suspicious too—you must be Chong's accomplice. " Both were ordered taken into military custody. When Hailing was assassinated, Chong and Yuxin were both killed by the mutinous soldiers.
5
宋珪，本名乞奴，燕人也。 為內侍殿頭。 宣宗嘗以元夕欲觀燈戲，命乞奴監作，乞奴誶語雲：「社稷棄之中都，南京作燈戲有何看耶。 」宣宗微聞之，杖之二十，既而悔之，有旨宣諭。
Song Gui, whose original name was Qinu, came from the Yan region. He held the post of head attendant in the inner service. On one Lantern Festival Emperor Xuan wished to watch lantern plays and put Qinu in charge of staging them. Qinu swore aloud: "The realm was abandoned at Zhongdu—what is there to watch in lantern plays at the Southern Capital? " Xuan caught wind of it and had him beaten twenty strokes, then regretted it and sent an edict to reassure him.
6
哀宗放鷂後苑，鷂逸去，敕近侍追訪之，市中一農民臂此鷂，近侍不敢言宮中所逸者，百方索之，農民不與，與之物直，僅乃得。 事聞，哀宗欲送其人於有司，乞奴從旁諫曰：「貴畜賤人，豈可宣示四方。 」哀宗惡其大訐，又仗之，尋亦悔，賜物慰遣之。
Emperor Ai released a goshawk in the rear garden and it escaped. He ordered his personal attendants to recover it. In the market a farmer was carrying the bird on his arm; the attendants did not dare say it was the palace falcon and tried every means to get it back. The farmer refused until they paid him its value in goods, and only then did they secure it. When the matter was reported, Ai wanted to hand the farmer over to the authorities. Qinu remonstrated beside him: "To prize a bird and slight a man—how can we display that to the empire? " Ai took offense at his bluntness and had him beaten again, but soon regretted it and sent gifts to console him.
7
及哀宗至歸德，馬軍元帥蒲察官奴為變，殺左丞李蹊、參政石盞女魯歡以下從官三百餘人。 倉皇之際，哀宗不得已，以官奴權參知政事，既為所制，含恨欲誅之未能也。 及官奴往亳州，珪陰與奉禦吾古孫愛實、納蘭忔答，護衛女奚烈完出、范陳僧、王山兒等謀誅之。 官奴自亳還，哀宗禦臨漪亭，召參政張天綱及官奴議事。 官奴入見，珪等即從旁殺之，及其党阿裏合、白進、習顯。 及蔡城破，哀宗自縊于幽蘭軒，珪與完顏斜烈、焦春和等皆從死。
When Ai reached Guide, the cavalry commander Pucha Guannu rose in mutiny and killed the Left Vice Chancellor Li Xi, the Councilor Shizhan Nülühuan, and more than three hundred officials in their train. In the crisis Ai had no choice but to appoint Guannu acting Associate Director of the Secretariat. Once under his control, Ai nursed hatred and wished to kill him but could not. When Guannu went to Bozhou, Gui secretly joined the palace attendant Wugusun Aishi, Nalan Yida, the guardswoman Xilie Wanchu, Fan Chenseng, Wang Shan'er, and others in a plot to kill him. When Guannu returned from Bo, Ai held audience at the Yiting Pavilion and summoned Councilor Zhang Tiangang and Guannu to discuss affairs. As Guannu entered for audience, Gui and the others struck him down on the spot, along with his partisans Alihe, Bai Jin, and Xi Xian. When Caicheng fell, Ai hanged himself in the Youlan Pavilion; Gui, Wanyan Xielie, Jiao Chunhe, and others died with him.

潘守恆
Pan Shouheng
9
有潘守恆者亦內侍也，素稱知書，南遷後規益甚多。 及哀宗自蒲城走歸德，道次民家，守恆進櫛，曰：「願陛下還宮之日無忘此草廬中，更加儉素，以濟大業。 」上聞其言，淒惋咨嗟久之。
There was also Pan Shouheng, another inner attendant, long known as a man of learning; after the court moved south his counsel proved of great benefit. When Ai fled from Pucheng toward Guide and lodged at a commoner's house along the way, Shouheng brought him a comb and said, "When Your Majesty returns to the palace, may you not forget this thatched dwelling—practice still greater frugality to carry through the great cause. " The emperor heard him and grieved aloud, sighing for a long while.
10
太史公敘九流，述《日者》、《龜策》、《扁鵲倉公列傳》。 劉歆校中秘書，以術數、方伎載之《七略》。 後世史官作《方伎傳》，蓋祖其意焉。 或曰《素問》、《內經》言天道消長、氣運贏縮，假醫術，托岐黃，以傳其秘奧耳。 秦人至以《周易》列之卜筮，斯豈易言哉！ 第古之為術，以吉凶導人而為善，後世術者，或以休咎導人為不善，古之為醫，以活人為功，後世醫者，或因以為利而誤殺人。 故為政於天下，雖方伎之事，亦必慎其所職掌，而務旌別其賢否焉。 金世，如武禎、武亢之信而不誣，劉完素、張元素之治療通變，學其術者皆師尊之，不可不記雲。
Grand Historian Sima surveyed the Nine Schools and wrote the biographies of day-selectors, tortoise-and-yarrow diviners, and the physicians Bian Que and the Lord of Cang. Liu Xin collated the imperial library and entered numerology and technical arts in his Seven Summaries. Later historians wrote Biographies of Technical Arts in much the same spirit. Some hold that the Plain Questions and Inner Canon really treat the waxing and waning of heaven's way and the ebb and flow of cosmic qi—that they merely borrow the language of medicine and the names of Qi Bo and the Yellow Emperor to convey deeper mysteries. The Qin even classed the Book of Changes under divination—such matters are not lightly judged! In antiquity practitioners used omens of fortune and misfortune to steer people toward the good; later ones sometimes used such signs to lead them astray. Ancient physicians took saving lives as their merit; later ones sometimes turned medicine to profit and killed patients by mistake. Whoever governs the realm must therefore be careful even in technical arts—watch closely what offices they hold and clearly mark the worthy apart from the unworthy. Under the Jin, men such as Wu Zhen and Wu Kang proved trustworthy and free of deceit; Liu Wansu and Zhang Yuansu mastered treatment that adapted to changing conditions—all who studied their arts revered them as teachers, and they must be recorded.

劉完素
Liu Wansu
12
劉完素，字守真，河間人。 嘗遇異人陳先生，以酒飲守真，大醉，及寤洞達醫術，若有授之者。 乃撰《運氣要旨論》、《精要宣明論》，慮庸醫或出妄說，又著《素問玄機原病式》，特舉二百八十八字，注二萬餘言。 然好用涼劑，以降心火、益腎水為主。 自號「通元處士」雲。
Liu Wansu, whose style was Shouzhen, came from Hejian. He once met a remarkable man, Master Chen, who plied Shouzhen with wine until he was deeply drunk; when he woke he had mastered medicine as though it had been handed to him whole. He wrote Essentials of Qi Cycles and Clarified Essentials of the Essential, fearing that mediocre physicians might spread reckless theories. He also wrote Patterns of Original Disease from the Plain Questions' Mysteries, taking two hundred eighty-eight key characters from the canon and annotating them in more than twenty thousand words. He favored cooling medicines, chiefly to reduce heart fire and strengthen kidney water. He took for himself the sobriquet "Retired Scholar Who Penetrates the Origin."

張從正
Zhang Congzheng
14
張從正，字子和，睢州考城人。 精於醫，貫穿《難》、《素》之學，其法宗劉守真，用藥多寒涼，然起疾救死多取效。 古醫書有《汗下吐法》，亦有不當汗者汗之則死，不當下者下之則死，不當吐者吐之則死，各有經絡脈理，世傳黃帝、岐伯所為書也。 從正用之最精，號「張子和汗下吐法」。 妄庸淺術習其方劑，不知察脈原病，往往殺人，此庸醫所以失其傳之過也。 其所著有「六門、二法」之目，存於世雲。
Zhang Congzheng, whose style was Zihe, came from Kaocheng in Suizhou. He was a master physician who had mastered the Difficult Issues and Plain Questions. His method followed Liu Shouzhen and relied heavily on cold and cooling drugs, yet he often succeeded in reviving the gravely ill. Ancient medical texts teach the methods of sweating, purging, and vomiting—but to sweat when one should not, purge when one should not, or vomit when one should not is to kill the patient. Each case has its proper channels, collaterals, and pulse logic. Tradition attributes these books to the Yellow Emperor and Qi Bo. Congzheng wielded these methods with supreme skill and was known as "Master Zhang Zihe's sweating, purging, and vomiting therapy." Mediocre practitioners who copied his formulas without learning to read the pulse or trace the root of disease often killed their patients—that is how the art was betrayed by poor physicians. His writings, including the categories "Six Gates" and "Two Methods," survive to this day.
15
李慶嗣
Li Qingsi
16
李慶嗣，洺人。 少舉進士不第，棄而學醫，讀《素問》諸書，洞曉其義。 天德間，歲大疫，廣平尤甚，貧者往往闔門臥病。 廣嗣攜藥與米分遺之，全活者眾。 慶嗣年八十餘，無疾而終。 所著《傷寒纂類》四卷、《改證活人書》三卷、《傷寒論》三卷、《針經》一卷，傳於世。
Li Qingsi came from Mo. In youth he failed the jinshi examination, turned to medicine, studied the Plain Questions and related texts, and mastered their meaning. During the Tiande era a great epidemic struck; Guangping was hit hardest, and poor families often saw every member of the household fall ill. Qingsi went about distributing medicine and rice; he saved a great many lives. Qingsi lived past eighty and died without illness. His works—Collected Categories of Cold Damage (four scrolls), Revised Evidence for the Book that Saves Lives (three scrolls), Treatise on Cold Damage (three scrolls), and Classic of Acupuncture (one scroll)—circulate to this day.
17
紀天錫
Ji Tianxi
18
紀天錫，字齊卿，泰安人。 早棄進士業，學醫，精於其技，遂以醫名世。 集注《難經》五卷，大定十五年上其書，授醫學博士。
Ji Tianxi, whose style was Qiqing, came from Tai'an. He abandoned the jinshi path early, took up medicine, mastered the art, and won fame throughout the realm as a physician. He compiled a five-scroll commentary on the Difficult Classic. In the fifteenth year of Dading (1155) he presented it to the throne and was appointed Doctor of Medicine.

張元素
Zhang Yuansu
20
張元素，字潔古，易州人。 八歲試童子舉。 二十七試經義進士，犯廟諱下第。 乃去學醫，無所知名，夜夢有人用大斧長鑿鑿心開竅，納書數卷於其中，自是洞徹其術。 河間劉完素病傷寒八日，頭痛脈緊，嘔逆不食，不和所為。 元素往候，完素面壁不顧，元素曰：「何見待之卑如此哉。 」既為診脈，謂之曰脈病云云，曰：「然。 」「初服某藥，用某味乎？ 」曰：「然。 」元素曰：「子誤矣。 某味性寒，下降走太陰，陽亡汗不能出。 今脈如此，當服某藥則效矣。 」完素大服，如其言遂愈，元素自此顯名。 平素治病不用古方，其說曰：「運氣不齊，古今異軌，古方新病不相能也。 」自為家法雲。
Zhang Yuansu, whose style was Jiegu, came from Yizhou. At eight he sat for the childhood examination. At twenty-seven he took the jinshi examination in classical exegesis but failed because his composition violated the imperial temple taboo. He then turned to medicine without yet making a name. One night he dreamed that a man opened his chest with a great axe and long chisel, placed several scrolls inside, and from that moment he mastered the art completely. Liu Wansu of Hejian had been ill with cold damage for eight days—headache, a tight pulse, vomiting, and inability to eat—and did not know what course to take. Yuansu went to call on him, but Wansu turned to the wall and ignored him. Yuansu said, "Why do you treat me with such contempt? " After taking his pulse he described the condition in detail. Wansu said, "Exactly so. " "Did you first take such-and-such medicine, using such-and-such ingredient? " "Yes," he said. " Yuansu said, "You are in error. That ingredient is cold in nature; it drives downward along the greater yin channel, yang is depleted, and sweat cannot break through. With the pulse as it is now, you should take such-and-such medicine and you will recover. " Wansu was deeply impressed. He followed the prescription and recovered; from that day Yuansu's name spread far and wide. In practice he did not rely on ancient formulas. His teaching ran: "Qi cycles are never uniform; ancient and modern times follow different courses—ancient formulas cannot meet new diseases. " This became his own school of practice.
21
馬貴中
Ma Guizhong
22
馬貴中，天德中，為司天提點。 與校書郎高守元奏天象災異忤旨，海陵皆杖之，黜貴中為大同府判官。 久之，遷司天監。 正隆三年三月辛酉朔，日當食。 是日，候之不食，海陵謂貴中曰：「自今凡遇日食皆面奏，不須頒示內外。」
During the Tiande era Ma Guizhong served as Supervisor of the Directorate of Astronomy. He and the collator Gao Shouyuan memorialized on celestial omens and disasters in a manner that displeased the throne. Hailing had them both beaten and demoted Guizhong to judge of Datong Prefecture. After some time he was promoted to Director of the Directorate of Astronomy. In the third year of Zhenglong (1158), on the first day of the third month (a xinyou day), a solar eclipse was expected. That day they waited, but the sun was not eclipsed. Hailing told Guizhong, "From now on, report every eclipse to me in person at court—do not issue proclamations inside and outside the palace."
23
海陵伐宋，問曰：「朕欲自將伐宋，天道何如？ 」貴中對曰：「去年十月甲戌，熒惑順入太微，至屏星，留、退、西出。 《占書》，熒惑常以十月入太微庭，受制出伺無道之國。 十二月，太白晝見經天，占為兵喪、為不臣、為更主，又主有兵兵罷、無兵兵起。 」鎮戎軍地震大風，海陵以問，貴中對曰：「伏陰逼陽，所以震也。 」又問曰：「當震，大風何也？ 」對曰：「土失其性則地震，風為號令，人君命令嚴急，則有烈風及物之災。 」六年二月甲辰朔，日有暈珥戴背，海陵問：「近日天道何如？ 」貴中對曰：「前年八月二十九日，太白入太微右掖門，九月二日，至端門，九日，至左掖門出，並曆左右執法。 太微為天子南宮，太白兵將之象，其占，兵入天子之廷。 」海陵曰：「今將征伐而兵將出入太微，正其事也。 」貴中又曰； 「當端門而出，其占為受制，曆左右執法為受事，此當有出使者，或為兵，或為賊。 」海陵曰：「兵興之際，小盜固不能無也。 」及被害於揚州，貴中之言皆驗。
When Hailing prepared to attack the Song, he asked, "I intend to lead the campaign against the Song in person—what do the heavens indicate? " Guizhong answered, "Last year, in the tenth month on a jiaxu day, Mars moved direct into the Supreme Palace Enclosure, reached the Screen Star, lingered, retreated, and departed westward." According to the books of divination, Mars regularly enters the Supreme Palace courtyard in the tenth month, is constrained, and then goes forth to observe states that have lost the Way. In the twelfth month Venus appeared in daylight and crossed the sky—omens of war and mourning, of disloyal ministers, and of a change of ruler; it also means that if war is underway it will end, and if there is no war war will break out. " At Zhenrong Army there was an earthquake and a great wind. Hailing inquired, and Guizhong answered, "Yin forces pent up are pressing yang—that is why the earth quakes." " He asked again, "If an earthquake is due, why is there a great wind?" " He replied, "When earth loses its proper nature the ground quakes. Wind stands for command—when the ruler's orders are harsh and urgent, fierce winds and disasters upon things follow." " In the sixth year, on the first day of the second month (a jiachen day), the sun showed a halo, ear-rings, a canopy, and a back-arc. Hailing asked, "What have the heavens shown of late?" " Guizhong answered, "Two years ago, on the twenty-ninth day of the eighth month, Venus entered the right side gate of the Supreme Palace Enclosure; on the second day of the ninth month it reached the central gate; on the ninth it passed out through the left side gate—each time crossing the left and right law-enforcer stars." The Supreme Palace Enclosure is the Son of Heaven's southern palace; Venus is the sign of armies and generals—its omen is that armed forces enter the imperial court. " Hailing said, "We are about to campaign, and armies and generals are passing in and out of the Supreme Palace—that suits the occasion exactly." " Guizhong added: "When it exits at the central gate the omen is one of constraint; passing the left and right law-enforcer stars means receiving assignments—envoys will go forth, whether as armies or as bandits." " Hailing said, "When armies are on the march, petty bandits are bound to appear." " When Hailing was killed at Yangzhou, every one of Guizhong's predictions was borne out.
24
大定八年，世宗擊球于常武殿，貴中上疏諫曰：「陛下為天下主，守宗廟社稷之重，圍獵擊球皆危事也。 前日皇太子墜馬，可以為戒，臣願一切罷之。 」上曰：「祖宗以武定天下，豈以承平遽忘之邪。 皇統嘗罷此事，當時之人皆以為非，朕所親見，故示天下以習武耳。」
In the eighth year of Dading (1168), Shizong played cuju at the Changwu Hall. Guizhong submitted a memorial of remonstrance: "Your Majesty is sovereign of the realm and bears the weight of the ancestral temples and the altars of soil and grain—hunting and ball games are perilous pursuits." The crown prince fell from his horse the other day—that should be warning enough. I beg Your Majesty to abandon such pastimes altogether." " The emperor replied, "Our ancestors settled the realm by force of arms—how could we forget that simply because we now enjoy peace? In the Huangtong era this practice was abolished, and everyone at the time thought that a mistake. I saw it with my own eyes, which is why I mean to show the realm that we still train in arms."
25
十年十一月，皇太子生日，世宗宴百官於東宮。 上飲歡甚，貴中被酒，前跪欲言事，錯亂失次，上不之罪，但令扶出。
In the tenth year, eleventh month, on the crown prince's birthday, Shizong gave a feast for the officials at the Eastern Palace. The emperor drank heartily. Guizhong, drunk, knelt forward to speak on some matter but stumbled over his words. The emperor did not punish him—he merely had attendants help him out.
26
武禎，宿州臨渙人。 祖官太史，靖康後業農，後畫界屬金。 禎深數學。 貞祐間，行樞密院僕散安貞聞其名，召至徐州，以上客禮之，每出師必資焉。 其占如響。 正大初，征至汴京，待詔東華門。 其友王鉉問禎曰：「朝廷若問國祚修短，子何以對？ 」禎曰：「當以實告之，但更言周過其曆，秦不及期，亦在修德耳。 」時久旱祈禱不應，朝廷為憂，禎忽謂鉉曰：「足下今日早歸，恐為雨阻。 」鉉曰：「萬里無雲，赤日如此，安得有雨？ 」禎笑曰：「若是，則天不誠也。 天何嘗不誠。 」既而東南有雲氣，須臾蔽天，平地雨注二尺，眾皆驚歎。 尋除司天臺管勾。
Wu Zhen came from Linhuan in Suzhou. His grandfather had been a grand astrologer. After the Jingkang debacle the family turned to farming; when the border was later redrawn their district fell under Jin rule. Zhen was deeply versed in numerology and the calendrical arts. During the Zhenyou era (1213–1217), the acting Privy Councilor Pushe San Anzhen heard of him, summoned him to Xuzhou, received him as an honored guest, and consulted him before every campaign. His divinations proved as reliable as an echo. Early in the Zhengda era (1224) he was summoned to Bianjing and attended court at the Donghua Gate. His friend Wang Xuan asked Zhen, "If the court asks how long the dynasty will endure, what will you say? " Zhen said, "I must tell the truth—but add that Zhou outlasted its allotted span while Qin fell short of its term, and that fate likewise depends on cultivating virtue." " A long drought had resisted every prayer, and the court was deeply worried. Zhen suddenly told Xuan, "You should go home early today—I fear rain may hinder your journey." " Xuan said, "The sky is clear for a thousand li and the sun blazes down—how could it rain? " Zhen laughed. "If that were so, heaven would not be sincere." When has heaven ever failed to be sincere? " Soon clouds rose in the southeast; in a moment they blotted out the sky, and rain fell two chi deep on the plain. Everyone gasped in astonishment. He was soon appointed manager of the Astronomy Bureau.
27
子亢
His son Kang
28
子亢，寡言笑，不妄交。 嘗與一學生終日相對，握籌布畫，目炯炯若有所營，見者莫測也。 哀宗至蔡州，右丞完顏仲德薦其術。 召至，屏人與語，大悅，除司天長行，賞賚甚厚。 上書曰：「比者有星變于周、楚之分，彗星起於大角西，掃軫之左軸，蓋除舊佈新之象。 」又言：「鄭、楚、週三分野當赤地千里，兵凶大起，王者不可居也。 」又曰：「蔡城有兵喪之兆，楚有亡國之征，三軍苦戰於西垣前後有日矣。 城壁傾頹，內無見糧，外無應兵，君臣數盡之年也。 」聞者悚然奪氣，哀宗惟嗟歎良久，不以此罪。 性頗倨傲，朝士以此非之。
His son Kang was sparing of speech and laughter and did not make friends lightly. Once he sat facing a student all day long, counting rods in hand and charts spread before him, his eyes bright as though engaged in some deep calculation—onlookers could not guess what he was about. When Emperor Ai reached Caizhou, the Right Vice Chancellor Wanyan Zhongde recommended his arts to the throne. Kang was summoned, attendants were dismissed, and the emperor spoke with him privately. Delighted, Ai appointed him Senior Astronomer and lavished rewards upon him. He submitted a memorial: "Stars have lately shifted in the Zhou and Chu astral divisions; a comet has risen west of the Great Horn and swept the left axle of the Chariot constellation—an omen of casting off the old and setting up the new." " He also said, "The astral territories of Zheng, Chu, and Zhou will become a thousand li of scorched earth; war omens are rising sharply—a ruler cannot remain there." " He added, "Caicheng bears omens of armies and mourning; Chu shows the signs of a state about to perish; our three armies have been fighting desperately before and behind the western wall for days." The walls are crumbling; within there is no grain in sight; without there are no armies to answer our call—it is the year in which the fated spans of ruler and ministers are spent." " Those who heard him were shaken and breathless. Emperor Ai only sighed for a long while and did not punish him. He was by nature rather arrogant, and court officials faulted him for it.
29
天興二年九月，蔡州被圍，亢奏曰：「十二月三日必攻城。 」及期果然。 末帝問曰：「解圍當在何日？ 」對曰：「明年正月十三日，城下無一人一騎矣。 」帝不知其由，乃喜圍解有期，日但密計糧草，使可給至其日不闕者。 明年甲午正月十日，蔡州破，十三日，大元兵退。 是日，亢赴水死雲。
In the ninth month of the second year of Tianxing (1233), when Caizhou was besieged, Kang memorialized, "On the third day of the twelfth month the enemy will certainly assault the city." " When the day arrived, it proved true. The Last Emperor asked, "When will the siege be lifted? " He answered, "On the thirteenth day of the first month next year there will not be a single man or horse below the walls." " The emperor did not grasp his meaning and rejoiced that relief was at hand. Day after day he secretly calculated grain and fodder so that supplies would last until that date. The next year, on the tenth day of the first month of the jiawu year (1234), Caizhou fell; on the thirteenth day the armies of the Great Yuan withdrew. That day Kang drowned himself.
30
李懋，不知何許人。 有異術。 正大間，遊京兆，行省完顏合達愛其術，與俱至汴京，薦于哀宗。 遣近侍密問國運否泰，言無忌避。 居之繁台寺，朝士日走問之，或能道隱事及吉凶之變，人以為神。 帝惡其言太泄，遣使者殺之。 使者乃持酒肴入寺，懋出迎，笑曰：「是矣。 」使者曰：「何謂也？ 」懋曰：「我數當盡今日，尚複何言。 」遂索酒，痛飲就死。
Li Mao—his place of origin is unknown. He possessed extraordinary arts. During the Zhengda era he traveled in the Jingzhao region. The branch secretariat official Wanyan Heda admired his arts, accompanied him to Bianjing, and recommended him to Emperor Ai. The emperor sent personal attendants to ask him privately about the dynasty's fortunes, and Mao spoke without reserve or evasion. He lodged at the Fantai Temple, and court officials flocked to him daily. He could sometimes reveal hidden affairs and foretell changes of fortune and misfortune, and people regarded him as supernatural. The emperor resented how freely he spoke and sent an envoy to kill him. The envoy arrived at the temple bearing wine and food. Mao came out to greet him and smiled. "So it has come to this." " The envoy asked, "What do you mean?" " Mao said, "My fated span ends today—what more is there to say?" " He called for wine, drank deeply, and died.

胡德新
Hu Dexin
32
胡德新，河北士族也。 寓居南陽，往來宛、葉間，嗜酒落魄不羈，言禍福有奇驗。 正大七年夏，與燕人王鉉邂逅於葉縣村落中。 與鉉初不相識，坐中謬以兵官對，胡曰：「此公在吾法中當登科甲，何以謂之兵官。 」眾愕然，遂以實告。 二人相得甚歡，即命家人具雞酒以待，酒酣、舉大白相屬曰：「君此去事業甚遠，不必置問。 某有所見，久不敢對人言，今欲告子。 」遂邀至野田，密謂曰：「某自去年來，行宛、葉道中，見往來者十且八九有死氣。 今春至陳、許間，見其人亦有大半當死者。 若吾目可用，則時事可知矣。 」鉉驚問應驗遲速，曰：「不過歲月間耳，某亦不逃此厄，請密志之。 」明年，大元兵由金、房入，取峭石灘渡漢，所過廬舍蕭然，胡亦舉家及難，其精驗如此。
Hu Dexin came from a gentry family of Hebei. He lived at Nanyang and traveled between Wan and Ye. Fond of wine, he was down-at-heel and unbridled, yet when he spoke of fortune and calamity his words were uncannily borne out. In the summer of the seventh year of Zhengda (1230) he happened upon Wang Xuan of Yan in a village of Ye county. They were strangers at first. Someone at the gathering mistakenly addressed Xuan as a military officer. Hu said, "By my art this gentleman is destined to pass the jinshi examination—why call him a soldier? " Everyone was astonished, and they then told him the truth. The two took to each other at once. Hu had his household prepare chicken and wine. When they were well into their cups he raised a full goblet and said, "Your career from here will be a long one—do not trouble yourself with questions." I have seen something I have long dared not tell anyone—now I wish to confide it to you." " He then led Xuan into the open fields and said in secret, "Since last year, on the roads between Wan and Ye, I have seen that nine travelers in ten bear the aura of death." This spring, in the region between Chen and Xu, I see that more than half the people there are likewise marked for death." If my eyes can be trusted, the course of events is plain to see." " Xuan asked in alarm how soon this would come to pass. Hu said, "Within a few months—and I shall not escape the calamity either. Please keep this to yourself." " The next year the armies of the Great Yuan entered through Jin and Fang, crossed the Han at the Qiaoshi ford, and left every dwelling they passed desolate. Hu and his whole family perished in the disaster—his foresight verified just so.
