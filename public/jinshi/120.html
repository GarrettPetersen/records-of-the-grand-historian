列傳第五十八世戚○石家奴裴滿達忽睹徒單恭烏古論蒲魯虎唐括德溫烏古論粘沒曷蒲察阿虎迭烏林答暉蒲察鼎壽徒單思忠徒單繹烏林答復烏古論元忠子誼唐括貢烏林答琳徒單公弼徒單銘徒單四喜
Biography 58: Imperial Affines — Shi Jianu, Pei Manda, Hu Du, Tudan Gong, Wugulun Puluhu, Tangkuo Dewen, Wugulun Zhanmeihe, Pucha Ahudie, Wulin Dahui, Pucha Dingshou, Tudan Sizhong, Tudan Yi, Wulin Dafu, Yi (son of Wugulun Yuanzhong), Tangkuo Gong, Wulin Dalin, Tudan Gongbi, Tudan Ming, and Tudan Sixi
2
金昭祖娶徒單氏，后妃之族，自此始見。 世祖時，烏春為難，世祖欲求昏以結其歡心，烏春曰：「女直與胡里改豈可為昏。 」世宗時，賜夾穀清臣族同國人。 清臣，胡里改人也。 然則四十七部之中亦有不通昏因者矣，其故則莫能詰也。 有國家者，昏因有恆族，能使風氣淳固，親義不渝，而貴賤等威有別焉，蓋良法也歟。 作《世戚傳》。
When the Jin dynastic founder Zhaozu took a wife from the Tudan clan, the kin of empresses and imperial consorts first entered the historical record. In the reign of Shizu, Wuchun stirred up trouble, and Shizu sought a marriage tie to win him over. Wuchun replied, "How could Jurchens and the Holi people ever marry one another?" Under Shizong, the Jiagu Qingchen clan was granted the same standing as native Jin subjects. Qingchen himself came from the Holi people. So even among the forty-seven tribes there were groups that did not intermarry, though the reason can no longer be traced. For any polity, fixed marriage clans can keep customs wholesome and kinship bonds steady, while preserving distinctions of rank and dignity — surely that is sound policy. Hence this "Biographies of Imperial Affines" was written.
3
石家奴
Shi Jianu
4
石家奴，蒲察部人，世居案出虎水。 祖斛魯短，世祖外孫。 桓赧、散達之亂，昭肅皇后父母兄弟皆在敵境，斛魯短以計迎還之。 石家奴自幼時撫養于太祖家，及長，太祖以女妻之。 年十五，從攻甯江州，敗遼主親軍，攻臨潢府皆有功，襲謀克。 。 其後，自山西護齊國王謀良虎之喪歸上京，道由興中。 是時，方攻興中未下，石家奴置柩于驛，率其所領猛安兵助王師，遂破其城。
Shi Jianu belonged to the Pucha tribe and his lineage had long settled on the Ansuchu River. His grandfather Huluhan was Shizu's grandson on the maternal side. In the upheavals of Huanzan and Sandai, Empress Zhaosu's parents and brothers were all trapped in hostile territory, and Huluhan contrived to bring them safely back. Shi Jianu was raised in Taizu's household from boyhood, and when he came of age Taizu gave him a princess in marriage. At fifteen he joined the assault on Ningjiang, routed the Liao emperor's personal guard, distinguished himself at Linhuang, and inherited his father's meng'an command. . Later, while escorting Prince Qi Guowang Moulianghu's coffin from Shanxi back to Shangjing, he traveled by way of Xingzhong. The siege of Xingzhong was still unresolved; Shi Jianu left the bier at the post station, led his meng'an soldiers to reinforce the imperial forces, and took the city.
5
從宗望討張覺。 再從宗翰伐宋。 宗翰聞宗望軍已圍汴，遣石家奴計事，抵平定軍遇敵兵數萬，敗之，遂見宗望。 已還報，宗翰聞其平定之戰，甚嘉之。 明年，復伐宋，石家奴隸婁室軍。 婁室討陝西未下，石家奴領所部兵援之。 既而，以本部屯戍西京，會契丹大石出奔，以余睹為元帥，石家奴為副，襲諸部族以還。 未幾，有疾，退居鄉里。
He campaigned under Zongwang against Zhang Jue. He again followed Zonghan in the campaign against Song. Learning that Zongwang's forces had already invested Bianjing, Zonghan dispatched Shi Jianu to coordinate plans. At Pingding Army he met a Song force tens of thousands strong, routed it, and then joined Zongwang. When he returned with his report, Zonghan heard of the victory at Pingding and commended him highly. The following year, in another Song campaign, Shi Jianu served under Lou Shi. Lou Shi had not yet pacified Shaanxi, and Shi Jianu marched his command to his aid. He then garrisoned his unit at the Western Capital. When the Khitan prince Dashi fled, Yuduo was made supreme commander with Shi Jianu as his deputy; they raided the tribal domains and withdrew. Before long he fell ill and retired to his native place.
6
天眷間，授侍中、駙馬都尉。 再以都統定邊部，熙宗賜御書嘉獎之。 封蘭陵郡王。 除東京留守，以病致仕。 卒，年六十三，加贈鄖王。 正隆奪王爵，封魯國公。
In the Tianjuan period he was made Director of Palace Affairs and Commandant of the Imperial Son-in-Law's Household. He was again appointed supreme commander to settle the frontier tribes, and Xizong rewarded him with an imperial commendation in the emperor's own hand. He was enfeoffed as Prince of Lanling. He was appointed military governor of the Eastern Capital but retired on grounds of illness. He died at sixty-three and was posthumously enfeoffed as Prince of Yun. Under Zhenglong his princely rank was stripped and he was posthumously enfeoffed as Duke of Lu.
7
裴滿達
Pei Manda
8
忽睹，天眷三年權猛安，皇統元年為行軍猛安。 曆橫海、崇義軍節度使，以後戚怙勢贓汙不法。 其在橫海，拜富人為父，及死，為之行服而分其資。 在崇義，諷寺僧設齋而受其施。 及留守中京，益驕恣，苟可以得財無不為者。 選諸猛安富人子弟為紮野，規取財物，時號「閑郎君」。 朝廷以忽睹與徒單恭等汙濫至甚，命秉德黜陟天下官吏，忽睹以贓罷。 海陵以忽睹所至縱家奴擾民，乃定禁外官任所閒雜人條約。 天德三年，復起為鄭州防禦使，改安國軍節度使。 卒，年三十九。
Hu Du served as acting meng'an commander in the third year of Tianjuan and as marching meng'an commander in the first year of Huangtong. He held successive posts as military governor of Henghai and Chongyi, and as a maternal kinsman of the throne he abused his power with brazen corruption. At Henghai he took a wealthy man as his adoptive father; when the man died he donned mourning garb and carved up his property. At Chongyi he induced temple monks to hold banquets and pocketed their donations. As military governor of the Central Capital he grew still more arrogant, stopping at nothing that might turn a profit. He recruited sons of wealthy meng'an families as personal attendants to squeeze them for goods and cash, and contemporaries dubbed them the "Idle Young Lords." The court judged Hu Du, Tudan Gong, and others to have sunk to extreme corruption and charged Bingde with reviewing officials empire-wide; Hu Du was removed for graft. Because Hu Du had allowed his household slaves to harass the populace wherever he served, Hailing promulgated rules barring superfluous retainers from provincial official residences. In the third year of Tiande he was recalled as Defender of Zhengzhou and later transferred to military governor of Anguo. He died at the age of thirty-nine.
9
徒單恭
Tudan Gong
10
徒單恭，本名斜也。 天眷二年，為奉國上將軍。 以告吳十反事，超授龍虎衛上將軍。 為戶部侍郎，出為濟南尹，遷會甯牧，封譚國公。 復出為太原尹。 斜也貪鄙，使工繪一佛像，自稱嘗見佛，其像如此，當以金鑄之。 遂賦屬縣金，而未嘗鑄佛，盡入其家，百姓號為「金總管」。 秉德廉訪官吏，斜也以贓免。
Tudan Gong, whose original name was Xieye. In the second year of Tianjuan he was appointed General-in-Chief Who Supports the State. For exposing Wu Shi's conspiracy he was abruptly promoted to General-in-Chief of the Dragon-Tiger Guard. He served as Vice Minister of Revenue, was posted as Prefect of Jinan, then transferred to Chief of Huining and enfeoffed as Duke of Tan. He was again sent out as Prefect of Taiyuan. Xieye was grasping and mean. He had craftsmen paint a Buddha image, claiming he had once seen the Buddha and that it should be cast in gold exactly as depicted. He levied gold from the counties under him but never cast the statue; everything went into his own coffers, and the people nicknamed him the "Gold Intendant." During Bingde's integrity review of officials, Xieye was removed for corruption.
11
海陵篡立，海陵後徒單氏，斜也女，由是復用為會甯牧，封王。 未幾，拜平章政事，海陵獵于胡剌渾水，斜也編列圍場，凡平日不相能者輒杖之。 海陵謂宰相曰：「斜也為相，朕非私之。 今聞軍國大事凡斜也所言，卿等一無取，豈千慮無一得乎？ 」他宰相無以對，溫都思忠舉數事對曰：「某事本當如此，斜也輒以為如彼，皆妄生異議，不達事宜。 臣逮事康宗，累朝宰相未嘗有如斜也專恣者。 」海陵默然。 斜也於都堂脊杖令史馮仲尹，御史臺劾之，海陵杖之二十。 斜也猛安部人撒合出者，言斜也強率取部人財物。 海陵命侍御史保魯鞫之。 保魯鞫不以實，海陵杖保魯，而以撒合出為符寶祗候，改隸合紮猛安。
After Hailing usurped the throne, his empress Tudan was Xieye's daughter, and on that account he was restored as Chief of Huining and enfeoffed as a prince. Soon he was made Grand Councilor. When Hailing hunted on the Hulahun River, Xieye laid out the beat and had anyone he disliked flogged on the spot. Hailing told the chief ministers, "I did not make Xieye a councilor out of private favor. Yet I hear that on matters of war and state you reject everything he says. Can a man who deliberates a thousand times never once be right?" The other chief ministers had no answer. Wendusi Zhong cited several cases: "This matter should have been handled one way, yet Xieye always insisted on another — empty objections that showed he did not understand affairs at all. I have served since the reign of Kangzong, and through successive reigns I have never seen a chief minister as domineering as Xieye." Hailing said nothing more. Xieye had the clerk Feng Zhongyin flogged across the back in the chief council hall. The Censorate impeached him, and Hailing ordered twenty strokes of the rod. Sahechu, a tribesman of Xieye's meng'an, accused him of forcibly seizing his clansmen's property. Hailing ordered Attending Censor Baolu to conduct the inquiry. Baolu failed to investigate honestly, so Hailing had him beaten and appointed Sahechu Keeper of Seals and Credentials, reassigning him to the Hezha meng'an.
12
斜也兄定哥尚太祖長女兀魯，定哥死無子，以季弟之子查剌為後。 斜也謀取其兄家財，強納兀魯為室而不相能，兀魯嘗怨詈斜也。 斜也妾忽撻與兀魯不葉，乃譖兀魯於海陵後徒單氏曰：「兀魯怨上殺其兄宗敏，有怨望語。 」會韓王亨改廣甯尹，諸公主宗婦往賀其母，兀魯以言慰亨母，忽撻亦以怨望指斥誣兀魯。 海陵使蕭裕鞫之，忽撻得幸于徒單後，左驗皆不敢言，遂殺兀魯，斜也因而盡奪查剌家財。 大定間皆追正之。 海陵以兀魯有怨望語，斜也不奏，遂杖斜也，免所居官。 俄，復為司徒，進拜太保，領三省事，兼勸農使。 再進太師，封梁晉國王。
Xieye's elder brother Dingge had married Taizu's eldest daughter Wulu. When Dingge died childless, the son of their youngest brother, Zhala, was made his heir. Xieye schemed to seize his brother's estate, forced Wulu to marry him though they were incompatible, and Wulu often reviled him in anger. Xieye's concubine Hutà quarreled with Wulu and slandered her to Empress Tudan, claiming that Wulu resented the emperor for executing her brother Zongmin and had uttered disloyal words. When Prince Han Xian was reassigned as Prefect of Guangning, the imperial princesses and clanswomen went to congratulate his mother. Wulu offered words of comfort to the prince's mother, but Hutà again accused her of disloyalty and slander. Hailing had Xiao Yu conduct the inquiry. Hutà enjoyed the empress's favor, witnesses dared not testify honestly, and Wulu was put to death. Xieye then seized the whole of Zhala's estate. In the Dading era all these wrongs were posthumously set right. Because Wulu had uttered disloyal words and Xieye had failed to report them, Hailing had him beaten and dismissed from office. Soon he was restored as Minister of Education, promoted to Grand Guardian, placed in charge of the Three Departments, and also named Commissioner for Encouraging Agriculture. He was further promoted to Grand Preceptor and enfeoffed as Prince of Liang and Jin.
13
其妻先斜也卒，海陵嘗至其葬所致祭，起復其子率府率吾裏補為諫議大夫。 大定間，海陵降為庶人，徒單氏為庶人妻，斜也降特進鞏國公。
His wife predeceased him. Hailing once attended her funeral rites in person and recalled his son Wulibu, a colonel of the rate-furnishing office, from mourning to appoint him Remonstrating Censor. In the Dading period Hailing was posthumously reduced to commoner rank, Empress Tudan to a commoner's wife, and Xieye demoted to Special Advancement Duke of Gong.

烏古論蒲魯虎
Wugulun Puluhu
15
烏古論蒲魯虎，父當海，國初有功。 蒲魯虎通契丹大小字，娶宋王宗望女昭甯公主什古。 熙宗初，為護衛，改牌印，常侍左右。 轉通進。 襲父謀克，再遷臨海軍節度使，改衛州防禦使。 海陵賜食內殿，謂之曰：「衛州風土甚佳，勿以防禦為降也。 」對曰：「頗聞衛州官署不利守者。 」即日改汾陽軍節度使，賜衣服、佩玉、帶劍。 入為太子詹事，卒，年四十一。 海陵親臨哭之，后妃皆弔祭，賻贈甚厚。 有司給喪事，贈特進駙馬都尉。 正隆例贈光祿大夫。
Wugulun Puluhu was the son of Danghai, who had rendered distinguished service in the founding of the Jin state. Puluhu was fluent in both Khitan scripts and married Princess Zhaoning Shigu, daughter of Prince Song Zongwang. Early in Xizong's reign he served in the guard, then became Keeper of Seals and attended the emperor constantly. He was promoted to Senior Remonstrator. He inherited his father's meng'an command, was twice promoted to military governor of Linhai, and then appointed Defender of Weizhou. Hailing entertained him in the inner palace and said, "Weizhou enjoys excellent climate and soil — do not take the Defender's post as a demotion." He replied, "I have often heard that Weizhou's official residence brings ill fortune to its occupants." That very day he was transferred to military governor of Fenyang and bestowed robes, girdle jade, and a sword. He was recalled to the capital as Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent and died at forty-one. Hailing came in person to mourn him; the empress and consorts all paid their respects; and the funeral gifts were lavish. The relevant offices arranged the funeral; he was posthumously enfeoffed as Special Advancement Commandant of the Imperial Son-in-Law's Household. Under Zhenglong the standard posthumous rank of Grand Master of Splendid Happiness was conferred.
16
唐括德溫
Tangkuo Dewen
17
唐括德溫，本名阿里，上京率河人也。 曾祖石古，從太祖平臘醅麻產，領謀克。 祖脫孛魯，領其父謀克，從太祖伐遼，攻甯江、泰州戰有功。 父撻懶，尚康宗女，從宋王宗望以軍二萬收平州，至城東十里許遇敵兵甚眾，戰敗之，太祖賞賚甚厚，授行軍猛安。 皇統初，遷龍虎衛上將軍，歷興平、臨海等軍節度使。
Tangkuo Dewen, whose original name was Ali, came from Shuaihe in the Upper Capital. His great-grandfather Shigu followed Taizu in the pacification of Lapimai and held a meng'an command. His grandfather Tuobolu inherited the family meng'an command, campaigned with Taizu against Liao, and distinguished himself at Ningjiang and Taizhou. His father Talan married a daughter of Kangzong and followed Prince Song Zongwang with twenty thousand men to recover Pingzhou. Some ten li east of the city they met a large enemy host, routed it in battle, and Taizu rewarded him handsomely with appointment as marching meng'an commander. Early in Huangtong he was promoted to General-in-Chief of the Dragon-Tiger Guard and served in turn as military governor of Xingping, Linhai, and other circuits.
18
十八年，追錄其父撻懶並德溫前後功，授其長子駙馬都尉鼎世襲西北路沒裏山猛安，徙隸泰州。
In the eighteenth year the court posthumously recorded Talan's merits together with Dewen's services. His eldest son Dingshi, Commandant of the Imperial Son-in-Law's Household, was granted hereditary command of the Meilishan meng'an on the Northwestern Circuit and reassigned to Taizhou.

蒲察鼎壽
Pucha Dingshou
23
蒲察鼎壽，本名和尚，上京曷速河人，欽懷皇后父也。 賦性沉厚有明鑒，通契丹、漢字，長於吏事。 尚熙宗女鄭國公主。 貞元三年，以海陵女弟慶宜公主子加定遠大將軍，為尚衣局使，累官器物局使。 大定二年，加駙馬都尉，職如故。 曆符寶郎、蠡州刺史、浚州防禦使，有惠政，兩州百姓刻石紀之。 遷泰甯軍節度使，歷東平府、橫海軍，入為右宣徽使，改左宣徽，授中都路昏得渾山猛安曷速木單世襲謀克。 改河間尹。 號令必行，豪右屏跡。 有宗室居河間，侵削居民，鼎壽奏徙其族于平州，郡內大治。 卒官。 上聞之深加悼惜。 喪至香山，皇太子往奠，百官致祭，賻銀彩絹。 明昌三年，以皇后父贈太尉、越國公。
Pucha Dingshou, whose original name was Heshang, came from Yisuhe in the Upper Capital and was the father of Empress Qinhuai. Steady and perceptive by nature, he was fluent in Khitan and Chinese and excelled at government business. He married Princess Zheng, a daughter of Xizong. In the third year of Zhenyuan, as son-in-law of Princess Qingyi, Hailing's younger sister, he was appointed General Who Pacifies the Far, then Commissioner of the Wardrobe Bureau, and eventually Commissioner of the Implements Bureau. In the second year of Dading he was also named Commandant of the Imperial Son-in-Law's Household while retaining his existing duties. He served in turn as Keeper of Seals, Prefect of Li, and Defender of Jun, winning such goodwill that the people of both jurisdictions set up stone monuments in his honor. He became military governor of Taining, served at Dongping and on the Henghai frontier, entered court as Right then Left Commissioner of the Palace Attendants, and received hereditary command of the Yisumu dan meng'an at Hundeshan in the Central Capital circuit. He was appointed Prefect of Hejian. His orders were enforced without fail, and local magnates vanished from public view. When an imperial clansman in Hejian preyed on local residents, Dingshou memorialized to relocate the clan to Pingzhou, and the prefecture was brought to good order. He died in office. The emperor, on hearing the news, mourned him deeply. When the funeral procession reached Xiangshan, the crown prince came to offer sacrifice, officials paid their respects, and lavish gifts of silver and silk were sent. In the third year of Mingchang he was posthumously enfeoffed as Grand Preceptor and Duke of Yue in his capacity as the empress's father.
24
鼎壽既世連姻戚，女為皇后，長子辭不失凡三尚定國、景國、道國公主。 其寵遇如此，未嘗以富貴驕人，當時以為外戚之冠云。
The Dingshou family had long been tied to the throne by marriage; his daughter became empress, and his eldest son Cibushi married in turn the princesses of Ding, Jing, and Dao. Though favored to this degree, he never lorded his wealth and rank over others, and his contemporaries hailed him as the model among imperial affines.
25
徒單思忠
Tudan Sizhong
26
徒單思忠，字良弼，本名甯慶。 曾祖賽補，尚景祖女。 從太祖伐遼，戰歿於臨潢之渾河。 父賽一，尚熙宗妹。 正隆末，為颭碗群牧使，契丹賊窩斡擾北邊，賽一與戰死之。 大定初，贈金吾衛上將軍。
Tudan Sizhong, courtesy name Liangbi, whose original name was Ningqing. His great-grandfather Saibu married a daughter of Jingzu. He followed Taizu against Liao and fell in battle on the Hun River near Linhuang. His father Saiyi married a younger sister of Xizong. Late in Zhenglong he was Commissioner of the Zhanwan Horse Herds. When the Khitan rebel Wowo raided the northern frontier, Saiyi died fighting him. Early in Dading he was posthumously appointed General-in-Chief of the Golden Crow Guard.

贊曰：天子娶后，王姬下嫁，豈不重哉。 秦、漢以來，無世世甥舅之家。 《關雎》之道缺，外戚驕盈，《何彼穠矣》不作，王姬肅雝之義幾希矣。 蓋古者異姓世爵公侯與天子為昏因，他姓不得參焉。 女為王后，己尚王姬，而自貴其貴，富厚不加焉，寵榮不與焉。 使漢、唐行此道，則無呂氏、王氏、武氏之難，公主下嫁各安其分、各得其所矣。 金之徒單、拿懶、唐括、蒲察、裴滿、紇石烈、僕散皆貴族也，天子娶後必於是，公主下嫁必於是，與周之齊、紀無異，此昏禮之最得宜者，盛于漢、唐矣。
The encomium reads: When the Son of Heaven takes a queen and royal daughters marry downward in rank, how weighty a matter that is. Since Qin and Han there had been no dynasty in which the same maternal clans served as affines generation after generation. The spirit of the "Ospreys" was lost, maternal kinsmen grew arrogant, the "How Luxuriant" was no longer heard, and the solemn dignity owed to royal daughters had nearly vanished. In antiquity only hereditary noble houses of other surnames formed marriage alliances with the throne; no other clans could enter that circle. When a daughter became empress, one had already married a royal princess; the clan rested on its own nobility without piling on further wealth or sharing in imperial favor. Had Han and Tang followed this practice, there would have been no catastrophes of the Lü, Wang, and Wu clans, and princesses marrying down would each have kept their proper place. Jin's Tudan, Nalan, Tangkuo, Pucha, Peiman, Hesheli, and Pusan were all noble clans. Emperors took empresses only from them, and princesses married only among them — no different from Zhou's Qi and Ji. This was the soundest marriage practice, surpassing even Han and Tang.

Anyone who says it cannot be done will be punished by separate edict." The two chief ministers fell silent, and the departure was decided. Apart from the two palaces, Gentle Consort Peiman, Lady-in-Waiting Zhang Xiurui, directors, attendants, medicine officers, the imperial wet nurse Lady of Gong, and a dozen others named in the order, everyone else was dismissed. Palace treasures were also taken: four hundred ingots of horse-hoof gold, seven thousand large chestnut-yellow pearls, one raw-gold mountain, two boards of borneol, and the imperial seals of trust and auspice. Half of the two palaces' traveling goods were granted to the Loyal and Filial Army.
48
壬寅，太后御仁安殿，出錠金及七寶金洗分賜忠孝軍。 是夜，兩宮騎而出，至陳留，見城外二三處火起，疑有兵，遲回間，奴申初不欲行，即承太后旨馳還。 癸卯，入京頓四喜家，少頃，還宮。 復議以是夜再往，太后憊于鞍馬不能動，遂止。
On renyin the empress dowager appeared at Ren'an Hall, distributed ingot gold and seven-jewel gold basins among the Loyal and Filial Army. That night the two palaces rode out. At Chenliu they saw fires outside the walls and suspected enemy troops. While they hesitated, Nushen—who had opposed the move from the start—received the empress dowager's order and galloped back. On guimao they entered the capital and stayed briefly at Sixi's house before returning to the palace. They debated setting out again that night, but the empress dowager was too exhausted from riding to continue, and the plan was abandoned.
49
明日，崔立變。 四喜、術甲塔失不及塔失不之父咬住、四喜妻完顏氏，以忠孝卒九十七騎奪曹門而出，將往歸德，不得出，轉陳州門，亦為門卒所止。 門帥裕州防禦使阿不罕斜合已遁去，經歷官完顏合住權帥職，麾門卒放塔失不等去，且曰：「罪在我，非汝等之過。 」明日，立以數十騎召合住，合住自分必死，易衣冠而往。 立左右扼腕欲加刃。 立遙見，問：「汝是放忠孝軍出門者耶？ 」合住曰：「然。 天子使命，某實放之，罪在某。 」立忽若有所省，顧群卒言：「此官人我識之，前築裏城時與我同事。 我所部十餘卒盜官木罪當死，此官人不之問，但笞數十而已。 此家能殺人，能救人。 」因好謂合住曰：「業已放出，吾不汝罪也。」
The next day Cui Li staged his mutiny. Sixi, Shujia Tashi, Tashi's father Yaozhu, Sixi's Wanyan wife, and ninety-seven Loyal and Filial horsemen forced the Cao Gate and tried to reach Guide but could not get out. They turned to the Chenzhou Gate and were stopped there as well. The gate commander, Defender of Yuzhou Abuhan Xiehe, had fled. Administrative officer Wanyan Hezhu took temporary command, ordered the guards to release Tashi and the others, and said, "The fault is mine, not yours." The next day Li summoned Hezhu with several dozen horsemen. Expecting death, Hezhu changed his clothes and went. Li's men seized him, blades ready. Li called from a distance, "Are you the man who let the Loyal and Filial Army out the gate?" Hezhu said, "Yes. By the emperor's order I released them. The guilt is mine." Li seemed to remember and told his men, "I know this official. When we built the inner wall he worked with me. A dozen of my men stole official timber, a capital offense, but he did not prosecute them—only gave them a few dozen strokes. This man can kill, and he can save lives." He then said kindly to Hezhu, "They are already out. I will not punish you."
50
四喜等至歸德，上驚問「兩宮何如？ 」二人奏「京城軍變，不及入宮。 」上曰：「汝父汝妻獨得出耶？ 」下之獄，皆斬於市。
When Sixi and the others reached Guide, the emperor asked in alarm, "How are the two palaces? They reported, "There was a military mutiny in the capital. We could not enter the palace." The emperor said, "Only your father and your wife got out?" They were thrown into prison and beheaded in the marketplace.
51
贊曰：四喜奉迎兩宮，而值崔立之變，智者居此，與兩宮周旋兵間，以俟事變之定而徐圖之。 萬一不然，以一死徇之耳，他無策也。 四喜奉其私親以歸，而望人主貸其死，豈非愚乎！
The encomium reads: Sixi was sent to welcome the two palaces but met Cui Li's mutiny. A wise man would have stayed with them amid the troops, waited for events to settle, and then planned carefully. If that failed, the only course was to die for them. There was no other strategy. Sixi brought his own kin home and still hoped the emperor would spare him — was that not folly!
