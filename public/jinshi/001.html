金之先，出靺鞨氏。 靺鞨本號勿吉。 勿吉，古肅慎地也。 元魏時，勿吉有七部：曰粟末部、曰伯咄部、曰安車骨部、曰拂涅部、曰號室部、曰黑水部、曰白山部。 隋稱靺鞨，而七部並同。 唐初，有黑水靺鞨、粟末靺鞨，其五部無聞。 粟末靺鞨始附高麗，姓大氏。 李績破高麗，粟末靺鞨保東牟山。 後爲渤海，稱王，傳十餘世。 有文字、禮樂、官府、制度。 有五京、十五府、六十二州。 黑水靺鞨居肅慎地，東瀕海，南接高麗，亦附於高麗。 嘗以兵十五萬衆助高麗拒唐太宗，敗于安市。 開元中，來朝，置黑水府，以部長爲都督、刺史，置長史監之。 賜都督姓李氏，名獻誠，領黑水經略使。 其後渤海盛強，黑水役屬之，朝貢遂絕。 五代時，契丹盡取渤海地，而黑水靺鞨附屬於契丹。 其在南者籍契丹，號熟女直； 其在北者不在契丹籍，號生女直。 生女直地有混同江、長白山，混同江亦號黑龍江，所謂「白山黑水」是也。
The Jin traced their origins to the Mohe people. The Mohe had formerly been known as the Wuji. The Wuji inhabited the territory of the ancient Sushen. Under the Northern Wei the Wuji were divided into seven tribes: Sumo, Boduo, Anchegu, Funie, Haoshi, Heishui, and Baishan. The Sui called them Mohe, and the seven divisions were treated as one people. Early in the Tang only the Heishui and Sumo Mohe are recorded; the other five divisions vanish from the sources. The Sumo Mohe first submitted to Goryeo and took the clan name Da. After Li Ji’s conquest of Goryeo, the Sumo Mohe withdrew to hold Dongmou Mountain. They later founded Parhae, took royal titles, and ruled for more than ten generations. They possessed a script, court ritual and music, offices, and a full administrative order. Their realm comprised five capitals, fifteen prefectures, and sixty-two districts. The Heishui Mohe lived in the old Sushen lands, with the sea to the east and Goryeo to the south, and they likewise owed allegiance to Goryeo. They once fielded a hundred and fifty thousand men to aid Goryeo against Tang Taizong and were routed at Anshi. During the Kaiyuan reign they presented tribute; the court set up the Heishui prefecture, appointed the tribal leader Protector-General and prefect, and posted a chief secretary to supervise him. The Protector-General received the Tang surname Li and the name Xiancheng, and was named Commissioner for Heishui Military Affairs. When Parhae rose in strength, the Heishui were reduced to vassalage and their tribute to the Tang ended. In the Five Dynasties the Khitan annexed Parhae’s lands, and the Heishui Mohe fell under Khitan authority. The southern groups were entered on Khitan registers and called the “civilized Jurchen”; those in the north lay outside Khitan registration and were called the “wild Jurchen.” The wild Jurchen homeland held the Huntong and Changbai Mountains; the Huntong was also known as the Black Dragon River—the “white mountain and black water” of their lore.

金之始祖諱函普，初從高麗來，年已六十餘矣。 兄阿古乃好佛，留高麗不肯從，曰：「後世子孫必有能相聚者，吾不能去也。 」獨與弟保活里俱。 始祖居完顏部僕幹水之涯，保活里居耶懶。 其後胡十門以曷蘇館歸太祖，自言其祖兄弟三人相別而去，蓋自謂阿古乃之後。 石土門、迪古乃，保活里之裔也。 及太祖敗遼兵於境上，獲耶律謝十，乃使梁福、斡荅刺招諭渤海人曰：「女直、渤海本同一家。 」蓋其初皆勿吉之七部也。 始祖至完顏部，居久之，其部人嘗殺它族之人，由是兩族交惡，哄鬥不能解。 完顏部人謂始祖曰：「若能爲部人解此怨，使兩族不相殺，部有賢女，年六十而未嫁，當以相配，仍爲同部。 」始祖曰：「諾。 」乃自往諭之曰：「殺一人而鬥不解，損傷益多。 曷若止誅首亂者一人，部內以物納償汝，可以無鬥，而且獲利焉。 」怨家從之。 乃爲約曰：「凡有殺傷人者，征其家人口一、馬十偶、牸牛十、黃金六兩，與所殺傷之家，即兩解，不得私鬥。 」曰：「謹如約。 」女直之俗，殺人償馬牛三十，自此始。 既備償如約，部衆信服之，謝以青牛一，並許歸六十之婦。 始祖乃以青牛爲聘禮而納之，並得其貲產。 後生二男，長曰烏魯，次曰斡魯，一女曰注思板，遂爲完顏部人。 天會十四年，追諡景元皇帝，廟號始祖。 皇統四年，號其藏曰光陵。 五年，增諡始祖懿憲景元皇帝。
The Jin founding ancestor, whose name was tabooed as Huanpu, first arrived from Goryeo already past sixty. His elder brother Aguna, a devout Buddhist, stayed in Goryeo and refused to go, saying, “Our descendants will surely meet again one day—I cannot leave.” He set out with only his younger brother Baohuoli. The founding ancestor settled on the Pugan River in Wanyan territory; Baohuoli made his home at Yelan. Later Hutumen brought the Hesuguan into Taizu’s service and declared that his grandfather and two brothers had split apart long ago—he held himself to be Aguna’s descendant. Shitumen and Digunai were of Baohuoli’s line. After Taizu routed the Liao on the border and took Yelü Xieyu prisoner, he sent Liang Fu and Wodaci to win over the Parhae, saying, “Jurchen and Parhae spring from one house.” In the beginning they had all been among Wuji’s seven divisions. The founding ancestor had long lived among the Wanyan when tribesmen killed a man of another clan; the two sides turned on each other in a feud that would not end. The Wanyan told the founding ancestor, “Settle this blood-feud so the clans stop killing each other, and we will give you a worthy woman of the tribe—sixty and still unwed—to marry, and you shall count as one of us.” The founding ancestor said, “Very well.” He went himself to reason with them: “One death has already set you at each other’s throats; if you go on, the wounded will only multiply.” “Execute the ringleader alone and let the tribe pay you in goods—you avoid further bloodshed and profit by it.” The aggrieved clan agreed. He drew up a pact: “Whoever kills or injures another shall pay the victim’s kin one household member, ten pairs of horses, ten cattle, and six liang of gold; the matter is then closed, and private vendetta is forbidden.” They answered, “We will abide by the pact.” From this settlement dates the Jurchen rule that a life is paid for with thirty horses and cattle. Once the payment was made as agreed, the tribe trusted him; they gave him a black ox in gratitude and betrothed him the sixty-year-old woman. He took the black ox as bride-price, married her, and with her came her estate. She later bore two sons, Wulu and Wolu, and a daughter, Zhushiban; he was thereby enrolled among the Wanyan. In Tianhui year fourteen he was posthumously titled Emperor Jingyuan, with the temple name Founding Ancestor. In Huangtong year four his tomb was named Guangling. In the fifth year his posthumous title was raised to Founding Ancestor Emperor Yixian Jingyuan.

子獻祖，諱綏可。 黑水舊俗無室廬，負山水坎地，梁木其上，覆以土，夏則出隨水草以居，冬則入處其中，遷徙不常。 獻祖乃徙居海古水，耕墾樹藝，始築室，有棟宇之制，人呼其地爲納葛里。 納葛里者，漢語居室也。 自此遂定居于安出虎水之側矣。 天會十四年，追諡定昭皇帝，廟號獻祖。 皇統四年，號其藏曰輝陵。 五年，增諡獻祖純烈定昭皇帝。
His son was Xianzu (Founding Ancestor), whose taboo name was Suikede. The Black Water Jurchen once had no fixed houses. They chose ravine ground among mountains and streams, laid beams, and roofed them with earth. In summer they moved with pasture and water; in winter they lived inside these shelters, always shifting and never settled. Xianzu then moved to the Haigu River, opened fields, planted crops, and for the first time built framed houses with rafters and beams. The place was called Nageli. Nageli means "dwelling place" in Chinese. From then on they settled permanently on the banks of the Anchuhu River. In the fourteenth year of Tianhui he was posthumously honoured as Emperor Dingzhao, with the temple name Xianzu (Founding Ancestor). In the fourth year of Huangtong his burial mound was named Huiling (Splendid Mound). In the fifth year additional titles were conferred: Xianzu, Pure and Ardent Emperor Dingzhao.
6
子昭祖，諱石魯，剛毅質直。 生女直無書契，無約束，不可檢制。 昭祖欲稍立條教，諸父、部人皆不悅，欲坑殺之。 已被執，叔父謝里忽知部衆將殺昭祖，曰：「吾兄子，賢人也，必能承家，安輯部衆，此輩奈何輒欲坑殺之！ 」亟往，彎弓注矢射於衆中，劫執者皆散走，昭祖乃得免。 昭祖稍以條教爲治，部落浸強。 遼以惕隱官之。 諸部猶以舊俗，不肯用條教。 昭祖耀武至於青嶺、白山，順者撫之，不從者討伐之，入于蘇濱、耶懶之地，所至克捷，還經僕燕水。 僕燕，漢語惡瘡也。 昭祖惡其地名，雖已困憊，不肯止。 行至姑里甸，得疾。 迨夜，寢於村舍。 有盜至，遂中夜啟行，至逼剌紀村止焉。 是夕，卒。 載柩而行，遇賊于路，奪柩去。 部衆追賊與戰，復得柩。 加古部人蒲虎復來襲之，垂及，蒲虎問諸路人曰：「石魯柩去此幾何？ 」其人曰：「遠矣，追之不及也。 」蒲虎遂止。 於是乃得歸葬焉。 生女直之俗，至昭祖時稍用條教，民頗聽從，尚未有文字，無官府，不知歲月晦朔，是以年壽修短莫得而考焉。 天會十五年，追諡成襄皇帝，廟號昭祖。 皇統四年，藏號安陵。 五年，增諡昭祖武惠成襄皇帝。
His son was Zhaozu (Illustrious Ancestor), taboo name Shilu — resolute, steadfast, and plain in character. The uncooked Jurchen kept no writing or tallies, no laws or restraints; they could not be governed or checked. Zhaozu wished to introduce regulations little by little. His paternal uncles and tribesmen were displeased and plotted to bury him alive. He had already been seized. His uncle Shelilie, learning that the tribe meant to kill Zhaozu, cried, "My brother's son is a worthy man who can sustain our house and settle the tribe. How can you people so rashly try to bury him alive! " He rushed over, drew his bow, nocked an arrow, and shot into the crowd. Those holding Zhaozu scattered and fled, and Zhaozu escaped. Zhaozu gradually ruled by regulations, and the tribe grew steadily stronger. The Liao appointed him tiyi (imperial son-in-law commissioner). The other tribes still clung to the old ways and refused to accept the regulations. Zhaozu showed force as far as the Green Mountains and White Mountains: he soothed those who submitted and attacked those who refused. He penetrated Subin and Yelan, winning wherever he went, and returned by the Puyan River. Puyan means "malignant sore" in Chinese. Zhaozu loathed the name of the place; though already spent, he would not halt. When he reached Guli Meadow he fell ill. At nightfall he lodged in a village house. Thieves appeared, so he set out again in the middle of the night and stopped at Bilaqi Village. That same night he died. As they carried the coffin along the road, bandits seized it and made off with it. The tribesmen pursued the bandits, fought them, and recovered the coffin. Puhu of the Jagü tribe attacked again. As he was about to overtake them, Puhu asked travellers on the road, "How far from here is Shilu's coffin? " They answered, "It is far off; you cannot catch up. " Puhu then gave up the pursuit. Only then could they return and bury him. By Zhaozu's day the uncooked Jurchen had begun to follow regulations and the people largely obeyed, but they still had no writing, no offices, and no reckoning of months, years, or new and full moons — so no one could tell how long anyone had lived. In the fifteenth year of Tianhui he was posthumously honoured as Emperor Chengxiao, with the temple name Zhaozu (Illustrious Ancestor). In the fourth year of Huangtong the burial mound was named Anling (Peace Mound). In the fifth year additional titles were conferred: Zhaozu, Martial and Gracious Emperor Chengxiao.

子景祖，諱烏古乃。 遼太平元年辛酉歲生。 自始祖至此，已六世矣。 景祖稍役屬諸部，自白山、耶悔、統門、耶懶、土骨論之屬，以至五國之長，皆聽命。 是時，遼之邊民有逃而歸者。 及遼以兵徙鐵勒、烏惹之民，鐵勒、烏惹多不肯徙，亦逃而來歸。 遼使曷魯林牙將兵來索逋逃之民。 景祖恐遼兵深入，盡得山川道路險易，或將圖之，乃以計止之曰：「兵若深入，諸部必驚擾，變生不測，逋戶亦不可得，非計也。 」曷魯以爲然，遂止其軍，與曷魯自行索之。 是時，鄰部雖稍從，孩懶水烏林答部石顯尚拒阻不服。 攻之，不克。 景祖以計告於遼主，遼主遣使責讓石顯。 石顯乃遣其子婆諸刊入朝，遼主厚賜遣還。 其後石顯與婆諸刊入見遼主於春蒐。 遼主乃留石顯於邊地，而遣婆諸刊還所部。 景祖之謀也。 既而五國蒲聶部節度使拔乙門叛遼，鷹路不通。 遼人將討之，先遣同幹來諭旨。 景祖曰：「可以計取。 若用兵，彼將走保險阻，非歲月可平也。 」遼人從之。 蓋景祖終畏遼兵之入其境也，故自以爲功。 於是景祖陽與拔乙門爲好，而以妻子爲質，襲而擒之，獻於遼主。 遼主召見於寢殿，燕賜加等，以爲生女直部族節度使。 遼人呼節度使爲太師，金人稱都太師者自此始。 遼主將刻印與之，景祖不肯系遼籍，辭曰：「請俟他日。 」遼主終欲與之，遣使來。 景祖詭使部人揚言曰：「主公若受印系籍，部人必殺之！ 」用是以拒之，遼使乃還。 既爲節度使，有官屬，紀綱漸立矣。 生女直舊無鐵，鄰國有以甲胄來鬻者，傾貲厚賈以與貿易，亦令昆弟族人皆售之。 得鐵既多，因之以修弓矢，備器械，兵勢稍振，前後願附者衆。 斡泯水蒲察部、泰神忒保水完顏部、統門水溫蒂痕部、神隱水完顏部，皆相繼來附。
His son was Jingzu (Resplendent Ancestor), taboo name Wugunai. He was born in the xinyou year, the first year of the Liao Taiping reign. From the founding ancestor to this point there had been six generations. Jingzu gradually brought the tribes under his command — from the White Mountains, Yehei, Tongmen, Yelan, and Tugulun, down to the chiefs of the Five States, all obeyed him. At this time Liao border people fled and came over to him. When the Liao moved the Tiele and Wunie peoples by force, many refused to relocate and fled to join him as well. The Liao sent Commissioner Helu with troops to recover the fugitives. Jingzu feared that if Liao troops marched deep into his country they would learn every mountain, river, road, and pass — and might then turn against him. He therefore stopped them with a stratagem, saying, "If your army goes deep, the tribes will be alarmed and unforeseen trouble may arise; you will not recover the fugitives either. That is no good plan. " Helu agreed, halted the army, and he and Jingzu went in person to search for the fugitives. By then neighbouring tribes had largely submitted, but Shixian of the Wulin'a tribe on the Hailan River still resisted. They attacked him but could not defeat him. Jingzu laid his plan before the Liao sovereign, who sent envoys to rebuke Shixian. Shixian then sent his son Pozhuokan to court; the Liao sovereign richly rewarded him and sent him home. Later Shixian and Pozhuokan attended the Liao sovereign at the spring hunt. The Liao sovereign kept Shixian at the frontier and sent Pozhuokan back to his tribe. This was Jingzu's stratagem. Before long Ba Yimen, commissioner of the Five States’ Punic division, rebelled against Liao and cut the hawk-road tribute route. The Liao prepared a punitive expedition and first sent Tonggan to deliver the sovereign’s orders. Jingzu said, “Take him by stratagem. Send an army and he will flee into the passes; you will not finish him in months.” The Liao agreed. Jingzu had always dreaded a Liao march into his lands, and so he claimed the plan as his own success. He feigned friendship with Ba Yimen, left wife and children as hostages, then struck and captured him and sent him to the Liao court. The Liao sovereign received him in the private hall, feasted him with exceptional gifts, and appointed him military commissioner of the uncooked Jurchen tribes. The Liao called commissioners “Grand Preceptor”; from this time the Jin title Chief Grand Preceptor dates. The Liao sovereign meant to engrave a seal for him; Jingzu refused Liao registration, saying, “Let that wait for another day.” The sovereign still insisted and sent envoys. Jingzu secretly had his people proclaim, “If our lord takes the seal and enters the register, the tribe will kill him!” On that pretext he refused, and the Liao envoys withdrew. As commissioner he had officers and retainers, and government by degrees took shape. The uncooked Jurchen had no iron of their own; when neighbors offered armor for sale he paid lavishly to buy it and had kinsmen trade as well. With iron in hand he had bows and gear made ready; his forces strengthened, and clans came over in growing numbers. The Pucha on the Wenmin, the Wanyan on the Taishen Tebao, the Wentihen on the Tongmen, and the Wanyan on the Shenyin rivers all submitted in turn.

景祖爲人寬恕，能容物，平生不見喜慍。 推財與人，分食解衣，無所吝惜。 人或忤之，亦不念。 先時，有叛去者，遣人諭誘之。 叛者曰：「汝主，活羅也。 活羅，吾能獲之，吾豈能爲活羅屈哉！ 」活羅，漢語慈烏也。 北方有之，狀如大雞，善啄物，見馬牛橐駝脊間有瘡，啄其脊間食之，馬牛輒死，若饑不得食，雖砂石亦食之。 景祖嗜酒好色，飲啖過人，時人呼曰活羅，故彼以此訕之，亦不以介意。 其後訕者力屈來降，厚賜遣還。 曷懶水有率衆降者，錄其歲月姓名，即遣去，俾復其故。 人以此益信服之。 遼鹹雍八年，五國沒拈部謝野勃堇叛遼，鷹路不通。 景祖伐之，謝野來禦。 景祖被重鎧，率衆力戰。 謝野兵敗，走拔里邁濼。 時方十月，冰忽解，謝野不能軍，衆皆潰去，乃旋師。 道中遇逋亡，要遮險阻，晝夜拒戰，比至部已憊。 即往見遼邊將達魯骨，自陳敗謝野功。 行次來流水，未見達魯骨，疾作而復，卒於家，年五十四。 天會十四年，追諡惠桓皇帝，廟號景祖。 皇統四年，藏號定陵。 五年，增諡景祖英烈惠桓皇帝。
Jingzu was magnanimous and forbearing; through his life he never showed pleasure or wrath. He gave away goods, shared his food, and stripped off his own clothes for others—he spared nothing. Those who crossed him he did not bear grudges against. When men had deserted him before, he sent envoys to coax them home. The deserters replied, “Your master is a huoluo.” “A huoluo—we can snare one anytime. Why should we bow to a huoluo?” Huoluo means “kindly crow” in Chinese. It lives in the north, large as a fowl and fierce at pecking. It spots sores on horses, cattle, and camels, pecks the wound, and the beast dies; starving, it will eat even grit and stone. Jingzu drank and womanized beyond measure, and men nicknamed him Huoluo; they taunted him with the bird, and he took no offense. When the taunters were spent and came in, he gave them rich gifts and sent them away again. On the Helan some arrived leading their bands; he noted their names and dates, then dismissed them to their old lives. The people trusted him all the more for it. In Liao Xianyong year eight, Xieye, bojin of the Five States’ Mojie division, rebelled and again blocked the hawk-road. Jingzu marched against him; Xieye met him in the field. Jingzu put on heavy mail and led his men in a hard fight. Xieye was beaten and fled toward the Bali Mai marsh. It was only the tenth month when the ice broke early; Xieye could not hold his host, his men scattered, and Jingzu turned homeward. Fugitives ambushed him in the passes and fought day and night; he reached his tribe worn out. He went at once to the Liao frontier commander Dalugu to report his victory over Xieye. On the Laishui, before he could see Dalugu, his old illness returned; he died at home, aged fifty-four. In Tianhui year fourteen he was posthumously titled Emperor Huihuan, with the temple name Jingzu (Resplendent Ancestor). In Huangtong year four his tomb was named Dingling. In the fifth year his posthumous title was raised to Jingzu, Heroic and Illustrious Emperor Huihuan.

If I slit your throat to pay him back—what then? ” Months later, when his brother’s bier came home, he turned on Xibuchu in fury; Xibuchu ran. He stormed Suzong’s house. An arrow pierced a side room’s curtain and stood quivering in the doorframe. He next attacked Huandu, who armored inside and barred the door. Foiled, Huoluo snatched Huandu’s door pennant and fled to Beinai. Beinai drew Wuchun’s men over the ridge. Shizu met them on the Susu Sea meadow. Shizu said, “I dreamed an omen once—today I must not fight myself. If someone on the left will strike hard, the day is won! ” He sent Suzong, Xielie, and Cibushi against them. Suzong dismounted, hailed Shizu by name, then his own, and vowed, “If Heaven makes me chief of all the tribes, let the spirits witness this day. ” He bowed twice and rose. Then he lit a fire and bound bundles of dry grass. Soon a strong wind rose at their backs and the flames leapt higher. It was the eighth month; the blaze took the standing grass and smoke filled the sky. Our men charged through the smoke and broke them completely. Beinai was taken, bound, and sent up to the Liao court. Huoluo was taken too. Suzong pardoned him, kept him at his side, and in time won his loyal service.
16
大安八年，自國相襲位。 是時，麻產尚據直屋鎧水，繕完營堡，誘納亡命。 招之，不聽，遣康宗伐之。 太祖別軍取麻產家屬，錡釜無遺。 既獲麻產，殺之，獻馘於遼。 陶溫水民來附。 二年癸酉，遣太祖以偏師伐泥厖古部帥水抹離海村跋黑、播立開，平之，自是寇賊皆息。 三年八月，肅宗卒。 天會十五年，追諡穆憲皇帝。 皇統四年，藏號泰陵。 五年，增諡肅宗明睿穆憲皇帝。
In Da’an year eight (1092) he inherited rule from his post as Guoxiang. Machan still held Zhiwu on the Kai River, rebuilt his forts, and sheltered outlaws. Suzong called him in; he refused. Kangzong was sent against him. Taizu (Grand Ancestor) took a detachment and seized Machan’s family—every kettle carried off. Machan was taken, executed, and his ears sent to the Liao as trophy. The Tao Wen River clans came over in submission. In the second year, guiyou (1093), Taizu was sent with a detachment against the Nimogu chief Shuimoli, Haicun Bagehei, and Bolikai. They were subdued, and raiding ceased from then on. In the third year, in the eighth month, Suzong died. In Tianhui year fifteen (1137) he was posthumously named Emperor Muzhen (Solemn and Virtuous). In Huangtong year four (1144) his tomb was named Tailing. In the fifth year his title was raised to Suzong, Bright and Sage Solemn and Virtuous Emperor.
17
母弟穆宗，諱盈歌，字烏魯完，景祖第五子也。 南人稱揚割太師，又曰揚割追諡孝平皇帝，號穆宗，又曰揚割號仁祖。 金代無號仁祖者，穆宗諱盈歌，諡孝平，「盈」近「揚」，「歌」近「割」，南北音訛。 遼人呼節度使爲太師，自景祖至太祖皆有是稱。 凡《叢言》、《松漠記》、張棣《金志》等書皆無足取。 穆宗，遼重熙二十二年癸巳歲生。 肅宗時擒麻產，遼命穆宗爲詳穩。 大安十年甲戌，襲節度使，年四十二。 以兄劾者子撒改爲國相。
His younger brother Muzong (Tranquil Ancestor), taboo name Yingge, style Wuluwan, was Jingzu’s fifth son. Southern writers call him Grand Preceptor Yangge, posthumous Emperor Xiaoping, reign title Muzong—or even Renzu (Benevolent Ancestor). No Jin ruler was ever called Renzu. Muzong was Yingge, posthumously Xiaoping; “ying” sounds like “yang” and “ge” like “ge” (to cut)—a southern mishearing. The Liao styled the military commissioner “Grand Preceptor”; every chief from Jingzu to Taizu used it. Works like Congyan, Songmo ji, and Zhang Di’s Jin zhi are not to be trusted. Muzong was born in Liao Chongxi year twenty-two, guisi (1053). After Suzong took Machan, the Liao made Muzong a xiangwen (detachment commander). In Da’an year ten, jiaxu (1094), he inherited the commissionership, aged forty-two. He installed Hezhe’s son Sagai as Guoxiang (State Minister).
18
三年丙子，唐括部跋葛勃堇與溫都部人跋忒有舊，跋葛以事往，跋忒殺跋葛。 使太祖率師伐跋忒，跋忒亡去，追及，殺之星顯水，紇石烈部阿疏、毛睹祿阻兵爲難，穆宗自將伐阿疏，撒改以偏師攻鈍恩城，拔之。 阿疏初聞來伐，乃自訴於遼。 遂留劾者守阿疏城，穆宗乃還。 會陶溫水、徒籠古水紇石烈部阿閣版及石魯阻五國鷹路，執殺遼捕鷹使者。 遼詔穆宗討之，阿閣版等據險立柵。 方大寒，乃募善射者操勁弓利矢攻之。 數日，入其城，出遼使存者數人，俾之歸。 統門、渾蠢水之交烏古論部留可、詐都與蘇濱水烏古論敵庫德，起兵于米里迷石罕城，納根涅之子鈍恩亦亡去，於是兩黨作難。
In the third year, bingzi (1096), Bagehejin of the Tangguo and Bate of the Wendu were old friends. Bagehejin went on a visit; Bate killed him. Taizu was sent against Bate, who fled and was run down and killed at Xingxian River. Ashu and Maoduilu of the Heishilie barred the way with force. Muzong marched in person on Ashu while Sagai took a detachment, stormed Dun’en, and carried the town. When Ashu heard they were coming, he fled to the Liao court to plead his case. Muzong left Hezhe to hold Ashu’s stronghold and withdrew. At the same time Akeban and Shilu of the Tao Wen and Tulungu Heishilie cut the Five States hawk-road and murdered Liao falconers. The Liao ordered Muzong against them. Akeban’s men held the passes and threw up stockades. In deep winter he raised marksmen with heavy bows and drove the attack. Within days the town fell. A few Liao envoys still alive were found and sent back. Where Tongmen and Hunchun meet, Liuke and Zhadou of the Wugulun, with Dikude of the Supin Wugulun, rose at Mili Mishihan. Nagenie’s son Dun’en fled too, and two rebel camps formed.
19
八月，撒改爲都統，辭不失、阿里合懣、斡帶副之，以伐留可、詐都、塢塔等。 謾都訶、石土門伐敵庫德。 撒改欲先平邊地城堡，或欲先取留可，莫能決，乃命太祖往。 鈍恩將援留可，乘謾都訶兵未集而攻之。 石土門軍既與謾都訶會，迎擊鈍恩，大敗之，降米里迷石罕城，獲鈍恩、敵庫德，釋弗殺。 太祖度盆搦嶺，與撒改會，攻破留可城，留可已先往遼矣，盡殺其城中渠長。 還圍塢塔城，塢塔先已亡在外，城降於軍。 詐都亦降于蒲家奴，於是撫甯諸路如舊時。 太師因致穆宗，教統門、渾蠢、耶悔、星顯四路及嶺東諸部自今勿復稱都部長。 命勝官、醜阿等撫定乙離骨嶺注阿門水之西諸部居民，又命斡帶及偏裨悉平二涅囊虎、二蠢出等路寇盜而還。
In the eighth month Sagai took overall command, with Cibushi, Ali Hemun, and Wodai as lieutenants, to crush Liuke, Zhadou, Wuta, and their allies. Manduhu and Shitumen moved against Dikude. Sagai could not choose between clearing the border forts or striking Liuke first and sent Taizu to settle the matter. Dun’en meant to relieve Liuke and struck before Manduhu’s men had gathered. Shitumen linked with Manduhu, met Dun’en, and broke him. Mili Mishihan fell; Dun’en and Dikude were taken alive and spared. Taizu crossed Pennao ridge, united with Sagai, and took Liuke’s town. Liuke had already fled to the Liao; every chief in the place was put to death. Turning back, they invested Wuta’s stronghold. Wuta was gone; the garrison surrendered. Zhadou yielded to Pujianu, and the frontier was quiet again. The Grand Preceptor wrote Muzong: the Tongmen, Hunchun, Yehui, and Xingxian routes and the eastern-ridge tribes were never again to call themselves dubuzhang (tribal chiefs). Shengguan, Chou’a, and others were sent to settle the peoples west of Yiligu ridge on the Zhu’amen River; Wodai and his captains cleared the Ernie’nanghu and Erchun roads of raiders and came home.
20
七年庚辰，劾者尚守阿疏城，毛睹祿來降。 阿疏猶在遼，遼使使來罷兵。 未到，穆宗使烏林答石魯往佐劾者，戒之曰：「遼使來罷兵，但換我軍衣服旗幟與阿疏城中無辨，勿令遼使知之。 」因戒劾者曰：「遼使可以計卻，勿聽其言遽罷兵也。 」遼使果來罷兵，穆宗使蒲察部胡魯勃堇、邈遜孛堇與俱至阿疏城。 劾者見遼使，詭謂胡魯、邈遜曰：「我部族自相攻擊，幹汝等何事？ 誰識汝之太師？ 」乃援創刺殺胡魯、邈遜所乘馬。 遼使驚駭遽走，不敢回顧，徑歸。 居數日，破其城。 狄故保還自遼。 在城中，執而殺之。 阿疏復訴於遼。 遼遣奚節度使乙烈來。 穆宗至來流水興和村，見乙烈。 問阿疏城事，命穆宗曰：「凡攻城所獲，存者復與之，不存者備償。 」且征馬數百匹。 穆宗與僚佐謀曰：「若償阿疏，則諸部不復可號令任使也。 」乃令主隈、禿答兩水之民陽爲阻絕鷹路，復使鱉故德部節度使言于遼曰：「欲開鷹路，非生女直節度使不可。 」遼不知其爲穆宗謀也，信之，命穆宗討阻絕鷹路者，而阿疏城事遂止。 穆宗聲言平鷹路，畋于土溫水而歸。 是歲，留可來降。 八年辛巳，遼使使持賜物來賞平鷹路之有功者。
In the seventh year, gengchen (1100), Hezhe still held Ashu; Maoduilu submitted. Ashu remained at the Liao court, and the Liao sent to halt the fighting. Before the envoy came, Muzong sent Wulin Da Shilu to help Hezhe with orders: “When the Liao man comes to stop the war, swap our men’s dress and flags for the garrison’s so he cannot tell us apart. ” He told Hezhe too, “You can trick the envoy away—do not obey him and lay down arms at once. ” The Liao envoy came as expected. Muzong sent Hulubojin of the Pucha and Miaosun Beijin with him to Ashu. Hezhe met the envoy and lied to Hulu and Miaosun: “We are fighting our own kin—what is that to you? Who ever heard of your Grand Preceptor? ” He drew his sword and lunged at their horses. The envoy bolted in terror without a backward glance and rode straight for the border. A few days later the town was taken by storm. Digubao came back from the Liao. He was inside; they seized him and killed him. Ashu appealed to the Liao once more. The Liao dispatched Xi frontier commissioner Yilie. Muzong went to Xinghe on the Laishui and received Yilie. Yilie asked about Ashu and commanded Muzong: “For everything seized in a siege, return the living; for the dead, pay indemnity. ” and demanded several hundred horses besides. Muzong told his counselors, “Pay Ashu off, and no tribe will take orders from us again.” ” He staged a blockade of the hawk road by Zhuwei and Tuda tribesmen and had the Bie Gude commissioner tell the Liao, “Only the commissioner of the uncooked Jurchen can reopen the hawk road.” ” The Liao court, duped, told Muzong to crush the blockaders—and the Ashu affair died away. Muzong proclaimed the hawk road secure, hunted along the Tuwen River, and came home. That year Liuke submitted. In the eighth year (1101) envoys from Liao arrived with gifts for those who had “pacified” the hawk road.
21
九年壬午，使蒲家奴以遼賜，給主隈、禿答之民，且修鷹路而歸。 冬，蕭海里叛，入於系案女直阿典部，遣其族人斡達剌來給結和，曰：「願與太師爲友，同往伐遼。 」穆宗執斡達剌。 會遼命穆宗捕討海里，穆宗送斡達剌於遼，募軍得甲千餘。 女直甲兵之數，始見於此，蓋未嘗滿千也。 軍次混同水，蕭海里再使人來，復執之。 既而與海里遇。 海里遙問曰：「我使者安在？ 」對曰：「與後人偕來。 」海里不信。 是時，遼追海里兵數千人，攻之不能克。 穆宗謂遼將曰：「退爾軍，我當獨取海里。 」遼將許之。 太祖策馬突戰，流矢中海里首，海里墮馬下，執而殺之，大破其軍。 使阿離合懣獻馘於遼。 金人自此知遼兵之易與也。 是役也，康宗最先登，於是以先登並有功者爲前行，次以諸軍護俘獲歸所部。 穆宗朝遼主於漁所，大被嘉賞，授以使相，錫予加等。
In the ninth year (1102) he sent Pujianu to pay Zhuwei and Tuda with Liao gifts, reopened the hawk road, and came back. That winter Xiao Haili rebelled and took refuge with the Adian Jurchen. His kinsman Wodala came offering alliance: “Let us be allies, Grand Master, and march on the Liao together.” ” Muzong took Wodala prisoner. Liao then ordered Muzong to pursue Haili. He handed Wodala over and mustered troops—over a thousand men in armor. This is the first record of Jurchen armored strength at such a scale—they had never mustered a full thousand before. The host camped on the Huntong. Haili sent another embassy; again Muzong took them prisoner. Soon the armies met. Haili shouted across the field, “Where are my messengers?” ” “They are coming with the rear guard,” came the reply. ” He did not believe them. Thousands of Liao pursuers had already failed to break him. Muzong told the Liao commander, “Pull your men back—I will take Haili myself.” ” The commander agreed. Taizu (Grand Ancestor) charged. A stray arrow struck Haili in the head; he fell from his horse, was seized and slain, and his host was shattered. Alihemen was sent to offer the trophies to the Liao court. From that day the Jurchen knew the Liao soldier was no match for them. Kangzong was first over the wall. Men who led the assault marched in the van; the rest guarded prisoners home to their camps. Muzong attended the Liao emperor at the autumn fishing hunt and came away laden with praise, the rank of envoy-chancellor, and lavish gifts.
22
十年癸未二月，穆宗還。 遼使使授從破海里者官賞。 高麗始來通好。 十月二十九日，穆宗卒，年五十有一。 初，諸部各有信牌，穆宗用太祖議，擅置牌號者置於法，自是號令乃一，民聽不疑矣。 自景祖以來，兩世四主，志業相因，卒定離析，一切治以本部法令，東南至於乙離骨、曷懶、耶懶、土骨論，東北至於五國、主隈、禿答，金蓋盛於此。 天會十五年，追諡孝平皇帝，廟號穆宗。 皇統四年，號其藏曰獻陵。 五年，增諡章順孝平皇帝。
In the tenth year (1104), second month, Muzong came home. Liao envoys arrived to reward every man who had fought at Haili. Goryeo opened diplomatic ties for the first time. On the twenty-ninth of the tenth month Muzong died, aged fifty-one. Each tribe had once carried its own tokens. At Taizu’s urging Muzong outlawed private tally-marks; from then on one law spoke, and the people obeyed without question. From Jingzu through four rulers in two generations, one policy built on the last until the tribes were one. Tribal law ruled from Yiligu, Halian, Yelang, and Tugulun in the southeast to the Five States, Zhuwei, and Tuda in the northeast—the Jurchen power stood at its height. In Tianhui year fifteen (1137) he was posthumously named Emperor Xiaoping, temple name Muzong. In Huangtong year four (1144) his tomb was named Xianling. In the fifth year his title was raised to Manifest Grace Filial Peace Emperor.
23
兄子康宗，諱烏雅束，字毛路完，世祖長子也。 遼清甯七年辛丑歲生。 乾統三年癸未，襲節度使，年四十三。 穆宗末年，阿疏使達紀誘扇邊民，曷懶甸人執送之。 穆宗使石適歡撫納曷懶甸，未行，穆宗卒，至是遣焉。 先是，高麗通好，既而頗有隙，高麗使來請議事，使者至高麗，拒而不納。 五水之民附於高麗，執團練使十四人。 語在《高麗傳》中。 二年甲申，高麗再來伐，石適歡再破之。 高麗復請和，前所執團練十四人皆遣歸，石適歡撫定邊民而還。 蘇濱水民不聽命，使斡帶至活羅海川，召諸官僚告諭之。 含國部蘇濱水居斡豁勃堇不至。 斡准部、職德部既至，復亡去。 塢塔遇二部于馬紀嶺，執之而來，遂伐斡豁，克之。 斡帶進至北琴海，攻拔泓忒城，乃還。 四年丙戌，高麗遣黑歡方石來賀襲位，遣杯魯報之。 高麗約還諸亡在彼者，乃使阿聒、勝昆往受之。 高麗背約，殺二使，築九城於曷懶甸，以兵數萬來攻。 斡賽敗之。 斡魯亦築九城，與高麗九城相對。 高麗復來攻，斡賽復敗之。 高麗約以還逋逃之人，退九城之軍。 復所侵故地。 九月，乃罷兵。 七年己丑，歲不登，減盜賊征償，振貧乏者。 十一年癸巳，康宗卒，年五十三。 天會十五年，追諡恭簡皇帝。 皇統四年，號其藏曰喬陵。 五年，增諡康宗獻敏恭簡皇帝。
His nephew Kangzong (Sagacious Ancestor), personal name Wuyashu, courtesy name Maoluwan, was Shizu’s eldest son. He was born in Liao Qingning year seven (1061). In Qiantong year three (1103) he inherited the commissionership, aged forty-three. Late in Muzong’s reign Ashu’s agent Daji stirred up the frontier tribes; Halian men seized him and delivered him up. Muzong had named Shishihuan to pacify Halian, but Muzong died before he marched; now Kangzong sent him. Diplomacy with Goryeo had soured. Goryeo asked to parley; when the Jurchen envoy reached the Goryeo court, he was turned away. Five Waters tribes went over to Goryeo and took fourteen regimental commissioners prisoner. The full account is in the Goryeo monograph. In the second year (1104) Goryeo invaded again; Shishihuan beat them back again. Goryeo sued for peace, returned the fourteen captives, and Shishihuan quieted the frontier before coming home. Subin River tribes defied orders. Wandai was sent to the Huoluo Sea to summon their chiefs and read them the law. Wohe, the Bojian of Subin in Hanguo, stayed away. Chiefs of Wozhun and Zhide appeared, then slipped away again. Wuta intercepted the defectors at Maji Ridge, took them prisoner, then marched on Wohe and overthrew him. Wandai pushed to the North Qin Sea, stormed Hongte fort, and turned home. In the fourth year (1106) Goryeo sent Heihuan Fangshi to hail the new rule; Kangzong sent Beilu in return. Goryeo promised to return everyone who had taken refuge in its territory, and Asuo and Shengkun were dispatched to take charge of the handover. Goryeo broke faith, executed both envoys, erected nine walled posts in Yilan Circuit, and attacked with an army numbering in the tens of thousands. Wosai defeated the Goryeo forces. Wulu likewise built nine fortresses, set directly opposite Goryeo’s nine posts. When Goryeo attacked once more, Wosai routed them again. Goryeo agreed to surrender the fugitives and pull its troops back from the nine fortresses. They also returned the lands they had invaded. In the ninth month the armies stood down. In the seventh year (jichou) crops failed; the court lowered theft penalties and provided relief to the destitute. In the eleventh year (guisi) Kangzong died, aged fifty-three. In the fifteenth year of Tianhui he received the posthumous title Emperor Gongjian. In the fourth year of Huangtong his tomb precinct was named Qiaoling. In the fifth year his posthumous title was expanded to Emperor Xianmin Gongjian of Kangzong.
24
贊曰：金之厥初，兄弟三人，亦微矣。 熙宗追帝祖宗，定著始祖、景祖、世祖廟，世世不祧。 始祖娶六十之婦而生二男一女，豈非天耶？ 景祖不受遼籍遼印，取雅達國相以與其子。 世祖既破桓赧、散達，遼政日衰，而以太祖屬之穆宗，其思慮豈不深遠矣夫！
The appraiser writes: In Jin’s earliest days the three brothers were humble indeed. Emperor Xizong traced the lineage of the imperial house and established the shrines of the Founding Ancestor, Jingzu, and Shizu, never to be displaced from the main line of sacrifice. The Founding Ancestor married a woman of sixty and fathered two sons and a daughter—could that have been anything but Heaven’s design? Jingzu refused to be registered by Liao or to accept Liao seals, took the Liao state chancellor Yada, and assigned him to his son. Once Shizu had broken Huanzan and Sandá and Liao power was waning day by day, he nevertheless placed Taizu under Muzong’s care—what long-range foresight that showed!
