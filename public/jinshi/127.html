1
孝友者，人之至行也，而恆性存焉。 有子者欲其孝，有弟者欲其友，豈非人之恆情乎？ 為子而孝，為弟而友，又豈非人之恆性乎？ 以人之恆情責人之恆性，而不副所欲者恆有焉。 有竭力於是，豈非難乎。 天生五穀以養人，五穀之有恆性也。 服田力穡以望有秋，農夫之有恆情也。 五穀熟，人民育，豈異事乎。 然以唐、虞之世，「黎民阻饑」不免以命稷，「百姓不親、五品不遜」不免以命契，以是知順成之不可必，猶孝友之不易得也。 是故「有年」、「大有年」以異書于聖人之經，孝友以至行傳于歷代之史，勸農興孝之教不廢於歷代之政，孝弟力田自漢以來有其科。 章宗嘗曰：「孝義之人，素行已備，雖有希覬，猶不失為行善。 」庶幾帝王之善訓矣。 夫金世孝友見於旌表、載於史冊者僅六人焉。 作《孝友傳》。
Filial devotion and brotherly regard are the highest virtues a person can practice, and they spring from our enduring human nature. Parents want their sons to be dutiful, and elder brothers want their juniors to be affectionate — is this not the enduring sentiment of humankind? To fulfill one's duty as a filial son and an affectionate younger brother — is this not likewise our constant nature? When we hold people to their innate virtues by the standards of what we always wish for them, there will always be those who fall short of our hopes. To devote one's utmost effort to this — is that not arduous? Heaven created the five grains to sustain humankind, and the grains themselves possess a fixed nature. Working the land and striving at cultivation in expectation of the autumn harvest — this is the farmer's enduring hope. When the grains ripen and the people thrive, are these not one and the same outcome? Yet even in the era of Yao and Shun, when "the common people were beset by famine," Yao had no choice but to appoint Ji; when "the people were estranged and the five ranks were in disorder," he had no choice but to appoint Xie. From this we know that a full harvest cannot be guaranteed any more than filial piety and brotherly love can be easily found. Thus "there shall be harvest" and "there shall be great harvest" receive special record in the sage's canonical texts; filial piety and fraternity as the highest conduct are handed down in histories dynasty after dynasty; policies encouraging farming and promoting filial devotion have never ceased across the ages; and since the Han, there has been an official category for filial sons, dutiful brothers, and those who vigorously farmed their fields. Emperor Zhangzong once remarked: "Those who embody filial righteousness have already perfected their conduct; even if they harbor some personal hope, they still cannot be said to have failed in doing good." This nearly amounts to the excellent counsel of sage rulers. Under the Jin, only six individuals whose filial and fraternal conduct earned public honor and a place in the historical records can be found. A "Biography of Filial Piety and Brotherly Regard" is hereby composed.
2
溫蒂罕斡魯補
Wendihan Wolubu
3
溫蒂罕斡魯補，西北路宋葛斜斯渾猛安人。 年十五，居父喪，不飲酒食肉，廬於墓側。 母疾，刲股肉療之，疾愈。 詔以為護衛。
Wendihan Wolubu came from Hunmeng'an in Songgexisi on the Northwestern Circuit. At fifteen, during his father's mourning period, he abstained from wine and meat and lived in a hut beside the grave. When his mother fell ill, he cut flesh from his own thigh to heal her, and she recovered. He was appointed to the Imperial Guard by imperial edict.
4
陳顏，衛州汲縣人。 世業農。 父光，宋季擢武舉第，調壽陽尉，未赴。 值金兵取汴，光病，圍城中。 顏間關渡河，往省其父，因扶疾北歸。 光家奴謀良不可，誣告光與賊殺人。 光系獄，榜掠不勝，因自誣服。 顏詣郡請代父死，太守徐某哀之，不敢決，適帥臣至郡，以其狀白，帥曰：「此真孝子也。 」遂並釋之。 天會七年，詔旌表其門閭。
Chen Yan was from Ji County in Weizhou. His family had farmed for generations. His father Guang, in the late Song, passed the military examination and was appointed magistrate of Shouyang, but never took up the post. When the Jin army captured Bianliang, Guang fell ill and was trapped inside the besieged city. Yan braved hardship to cross the river and visit his father, then escorted the ailing man back north. A household slave named Mou Liang, unable to carry out his scheme, falsely accused Guang of murdering people together with bandits. Guang was thrown into prison; unable to endure the torture, he falsely confessed to the crime. Yan went to the prefectural seat and asked to die in his father's place. Prefect Xu was moved with pity but dared not decide the matter. When the circuit commander happened to arrive, Xu reported the case to him. The commander said, "This is a true filial son." Both father and son were then released. In the seventh year of Tianhui, an edict ordered that his household gate be publicly honored.
5
劉瑜，棣州人。 家貧甚，母喪不能具葬，乃質其子以給喪事。 明昌三年，詔賜粟帛，複其終身。
Liu Yu was from Dizhou. His family was desperately poor. When his mother died, he could not afford a proper burial and had to pledge his son as collateral to pay for the funeral. In the third year of Mingchang, an edict granted him grain and cloth and exempted him from corvée labor for life.
6
孟興，蚤喪父，事母孝謹，母沒，喪葬盡禮。 事兄如事其父。 明昌三年，詔賜帛十匹、粟二十石。
Meng Xing lost his father at an early age. He served his mother with filial devotion and care. When she died, he conducted the mourning and burial with full propriety. He treated his elder brother with the same devotion he would have shown his father. In the third year of Mingchang, an edict granted him ten bolts of cloth and twenty piculs of grain.
7
王震，甯海州文登縣人。 為進士學。 母患風疾，刲股肉雜飲食中，疾遂愈。 母沒，哀泣過禮，目生翳。 服除，目不療而愈，皆以為孝感所致。 特賜同進士出身，詔尚書省擬注職任。
Wang Zhen was from Wendeng County in Ninghaizhou. He pursued the jinshi degree. When his mother suffered from a paralytic ailment, he cut flesh from his thigh and mixed it into her food and drink, and she recovered. When his mother died, his mourning exceeded all propriety in its grief, and cataracts formed in his eyes. When the mourning period ended, his eyes healed without any treatment, and everyone attributed this to the power of his filial devotion. He was specially granted jinshi status by imperial favor, and the Ministry of Personnel was ordered to propose an office for him.
8
劉政，洺州人。 性篤孝，母老喪明，政每以舌舐母目，逾旬母能視物。 母疾，晝夜侍側，衣不解帶，刲股肉啖之者再三。 母死，負土起墳，鄉鄰欲佐其勞，政謝之。 葬之日，飛鳥哀鳴，翔集丘木間。 廬於墓側者三年。 防禦使以聞，除太子掌飲丞。
Liu Zheng was from Mingzhou. Deeply filial by nature, when his aged mother went blind, Zheng licked her eyes with his tongue each day. After more than ten days, his mother could see again. When his mother fell ill, he attended her day and night without loosening his belt even to sleep, and several times cut flesh from his thigh for her to eat. When his mother died, he carried earth himself to build her tomb. Neighbors offered to help with the labor, but Zheng refused. On the day of the burial, birds cried mournfully and circled to perch in the trees on the mound. He lived in a hut beside the tomb for three years. The defense commissioner reported his conduct to the throne, and Zheng was appointed Director of Beverages to the Heir Apparent.
9
孔子稱逸民伯夷、叔齊、夷逸、硃張、柳下惠、少連，其立心造行之異同，各有所稱謂，而柳下惠則又嘗仕於當世者也。 長沮、桀溺之徒，則無所取焉。 後世，凡隱遁之士，其名皆列于史傳，何歟？ 蓋古之仕者，其志將以行道，其為貧而仕下列者，猶必先事而後食焉。 後世幹祿者多，其先人尚人之志與歎老嗟卑之心，能去是者鮮矣。 故君子于士之遠引高蹈者特稱述之，庶聞其風猶足以立懦廉頑也。 作《隱逸傳》。
Confucius praised the reclusive worthies Bo Yi, Shu Qi, Yi Yi, Zhu Zhang, Liuxia Hui, and Shaolian. Though each differed in purpose and conduct and received a distinct epithet, Liuxia Hui had also once held office in his own time. As for the likes of Changju and Jieni, he had nothing approving to say of them. In later ages, the names of all reclusive scholars are recorded in historical biographies — why is this? For in antiquity, those who took office intended to practice the Way; even those who accepted humble posts out of poverty still put service before salary. In later ages, office-seekers were legion, and few could shed the ambition of their forebears to win favor from superiors, or the habit of lamenting one's age and rank. Therefore the gentleman especially commends those scholars who withdraw from the world and hold themselves aloof, hoping that even hearing of their spirit may still embolden the timid, shame the greedy, and stiffen the obstinate. A "Biography of Reclusion" is hereby composed.
10
褚承亮
Chu Chengliang
11
褚承亮，字茂先，真定人。 宋蘇軾自定武謫官過真定，承亮以文謁之，大為稱賞。 宣和五年秋，應鄉試，同試者八百人，承亮為第一。 明年，登第。 調易州戶曹，未赴，會金兵南下。 天會六年，斡離不既破真定，拘籍境內進士試安國寺，承亮名亦在籍中，匿而不出。 軍中知其才，嚴令押赴，與諸生對策。 策問「上皇無道、少帝失信。 」舉人承風旨，極口詆毀。 承亮詣主文劉侍中曰：「君父之罪，豈臣子所得言耶？ 」長揖而出。 劉為之動容。 餘悉放第，凡七十二人，遂號七十二賢榜。 狀元許必仕為郎官，一日出左掖門，墮馬，首中閫石死，餘皆無顯者。 劉多承亮之誼，薦知槁城縣。 漫應之，即棄去。 年七十終，門人私諡曰「玄貞先生。」
Chu Chengliang, style name Maoxian, was from Zhending. When the Song poet Su Shi passed through Zhending on his way from demotion at Dingwu, Chengliang presented his writings to him and won his high praise. In the autumn of the fifth year of Xuanhe, he took the provincial examination. Among eight hundred candidates, Chengliang ranked first. The following year, he passed the metropolitan examination. He was assigned as registrar of households in Yizhou but had not yet taken up the post when the Jin army marched south. In the sixth year of Tianhui, after Wolibu captured Zhending, he registered all jinshi degree holders in the region and summoned them to an examination at Anguo Temple. Chengliang's name was on the register, but he hid himself and did not appear. The army knew of his talent and issued strict orders to bring him in. He answered the policy questions together with the other candidates. The policy question asked: "The Retired Emperor was without the Way, and the Young Emperor broke faith." The candidates followed the intended tone and vilified the Song emperors to the utmost. Chengliang went to the chief examiner, Vice Censor-in-Chief Liu, and said, "The crimes of one's sovereign and father — how can a subject presume to speak of them?" With a deep bow, he walked out. Liu was deeply moved. All the rest were passed and given degrees — seventy-two in all — and this came to be known as the list of the Seventy-Two Worthies. The top graduate Xu Bishi became a court gentleman. One day, as he went out the Left Yabo Gate, he fell from his horse; his head struck the gate stone and he died. None of the rest achieved any distinction. Liu, deeply impressed by Chengliang's integrity, recommended him as magistrate of Gaocheng County. He agreed perfunctorily, then immediately abandoned the post and left. He died at seventy. His disciples privately gave him the posthumous title Master Xuanzhen.
12
子席珍，正隆二年進士，官州縣有聲。
His son Xizhen passed the jinshi examination in the second year of Zhenglong and earned a fine reputation in prefectural and county office.

王去非
Wang Qufei
14
王去非，字廣道，平陰人。 嘗就舉，不得意即屏去，督妻孥耕織以給伏臘。 家居教授，束修有餘輒分惠人。 弟子班晄冘貧不能朝夕，一女及笄，去非為辦資裝嫁之。 北鄰有喪忌東出，西與北皆人居，南則去非家，去非壞蠶室使喪南出，遂得葬焉。 大定二十四年卒，年八十四。
Wang Qufei, style name Guangdao, was from Pingyin. He once attempted the examinations. When the results disappointed him, he withdrew at once and had his wife and children farm and weave to supply the household through the seasons. He taught students at home, and whenever his tuition fees exceeded his needs, he shared the surplus with others. His student Ban Zhao was desperately poor and could barely get by. When a daughter came of age, Qufei provided her dowry and saw her married. A northern neighbor needed to conduct a funeral but taboo forbade proceeding east. West and north were both occupied by other homes, while Qufei's house lay to the south. Qufei demolished his silkworm nursery so the funeral procession could go south, and the burial was accomplished. He died in the twenty-fourth year of Dading, at the age of eighty-four.
15
趙質，字景道，遼相思溫之裔。 大定末，舉進士不第，隱居燕城南，教授為業。 明昌間，章宗游春水過焉，聞弦誦聲，幸其齋舍，見壁間所題詩，諷詠久之，賞其志趣不凡。 召至行殿，命之官。 因辭曰：「臣僻性野逸，志在長林豐草，金鑣玉絡非所願也。 況聖明在上，可不容巢、由為外臣乎。 」上益奇之，賜田畝千，複之終身。 泰和二年卒，年八十五。
Zhao Zhi, style name Jingdao, was a descendant of the Liao's Sixian Wen clan. In the late Dading period, he failed the jinshi examination and retired to live south of Yanjing, supporting himself by teaching. During the Mingchang era, Emperor Zhangzong, while touring the spring waters, passed by and heard the sound of students reciting their lessons. He visited Zhi's study, saw the poems inscribed on the walls, and chanted them at length, admiring his extraordinary spirit. Zhi was summoned to the traveling palace and offered an official post. He declined, saying, "Your subject has a reclusive nature and loves the wild. My heart lies among deep forests and lush meadows; golden bridles and jade trappings are not what I desire." Moreover, with a sage ruler on the throne, surely Your Majesty can allow men like Chao Fu and Xu You to remain as recluses beyond the court? The emperor was all the more impressed and granted him a thousand mu of fields, exempting him from corvée labor for life. He died in the second year of Taihe, at the age of eighty-five.
16
杜時升
Du Shisheng
17
杜時升，字進之，霸州信安人。 博學知天文，不肯仕進。 承安、泰和間，宰相數薦時升可大用。 時升謂所親曰：「吾觀正北赤氣如血，東西亙天，天下當大亂，亂而南北當合為一。 消息盈虛，迴圈無端，察往考來，孰能違之。 」是時，風俗侈靡，紀綱大壞，世宗之業遂衰。 時升乃南渡河，隱居嵩、洛山中，從學者甚眾。 大抵以「伊洛之學」教人自時升始。 正大間，大元兵攻潼關，拒守甚堅，眾皆相賀，時升曰：「大兵皆在秦、鞏間，若假道于宋，出襄、漢入宛、葉，鐵騎長驅勢如風雨，無高山大川為之阻，土崩之勢也。 」頃之，大元兵果自饒峰關涉襄陽出南陽，金人敗績於三峰山，汴京不守，皆如時升所料雲。 正大末，卒。
Du Shisheng, style name Jinzhi, was from Xin'an in Bazhou. Broadly learned and versed in astronomy, he refused to pursue office. During the Cheng'an and Taihe periods, the chief ministers repeatedly recommended him for high office. Shisheng told those close to him, "I see crimson vapor like blood in the north, stretching east and west across the sky. The realm will fall into great disorder, and when the turmoil ends, north and south will become one." Growth and decline, fullness and emptiness — the cycle has no end. When one examines the past and considers what is to come, who can defy it? At that time, customs had grown extravagant, institutions were breaking down, and the achievements of Emperor Shizong were fading. Shisheng then crossed the river south and retired to the Song and Luo mountains, where he attracted a great many students. Broadly speaking, the teaching of Neo-Confucian learning to students in this region began with Shisheng. During the Zhengda period, the Great Yuan army attacked Tong Pass, and the defense held firm. Everyone congratulated one another, but Shisheng said, "The main force is all between Qin and Gong. If they borrow passage through Song territory, emerge from Xiang and Han into Wan and Ye, and drive their iron cavalry straight through like wind and rain, with no high mountains or great rivers to obstruct them, the Jin will collapse like a landslide." Before long, the Great Yuan army did indeed cross Raofeng Pass through Xiangyang and emerge at Nanyang. The Jin suffered defeat at Sanfeng Mountain, and Bianjing fell — all exactly as Shisheng had foreseen. He died in the late Zhengda period.

郝天挺
Hao Tianting
19
郝天挺，字晉卿，澤州陵川人。 早衰多疾，厭於科舉，遂不復充賦。 太原元好問嘗從學進士業，天挺曰：「今人賦學以速售為功，六經百家分磔緝綴，或篇章句讀不之知，幸而得之，不免為庸人。 」又曰：「讀書不為藝文，選官不為利養，唯通人能之。 」又曰：「今之仕多以貪敗，皆苦饑寒不能自持耳。 丈夫不耐饑寒，一事不可為。 子以吾言求之，科舉在其中矣。 」或曰：「以此學進士無乃戾乎？ 」天挺曰：「正欲渠不為舉子爾。 」貞祐中，居河南，往來淇衛間。 為人有崖岸，耿耿自信，寧落魄困窮，終不一至豪富之門。 年五十，終於舞陽。
Hao Tianting, style name Jinqing, was from Lingchuan in Zezhou. He declined early and was often ill. Weary of the civil examinations, he never again submitted his examination composition. Yuan Haowen of Taiyuan once studied for the jinshi under him. Tianting said, "Students today treat examination essays as a trade for quick success. They chop up the Six Classics and the hundred schools and stitch the pieces together — some cannot even parse a chapter or a sentence. If they luck into a degree, they are still mediocre." He also said, "Reading not for literary display, taking office not for profit — only a truly accomplished man can do that." He also said, "Officials today mostly ruin themselves through greed because they cannot endure hunger and cold." A man who cannot endure hunger and cold cannot accomplish anything worthwhile. Seek what you need through my teaching, and the examinations are already within it. Someone said, "Studying this way for the jinshi — is that not contrary to the examination?" Tianting said, "I precisely want you not to become a mere examination candidate." During the Zhenyou period he lived in Henan, traveling between Qi and Wei. He had a steep, unyielding character and trusted himself completely. He would rather remain poor and destitute than once visit a wealthy man's gate. He died at Wuyang at the age of fifty.

薛繼元
Xue Jiyuan
21
薛繼先，字曼卿。 南渡後，隱居洛西山中，課童子讀書。 事母孝，與人交謙遜和雅，所居化之。 子純孝，字方叔，有父風。 有詐為曼卿書就方叔取物者，曼卿年已老狀貌如少者，客不知其為曼卿而以為方叔也，而與之書，曼卿如所取付之。 監察禦史石玠行部過曼卿，曼卿不之見。 或言：「君何無鄉曲情。 」曼卿曰：「君未之思耳。 凡今時政未必皆善，禦史一有所劾，將謂自我發之。 同惡相庇，他日並鄉里必有受禍者。 」其畏慎皆此類。 壬辰之亂，病沒宜陽。
Xue Jixian, style name Manqing. After the southward migration, he retired to the western mountains of Luoyang and taught boys to read. He served his mother filially. In dealings with others he was modest and refined, and those around him were transformed by his example. His son Chunxiao, style name Fangshu, had his father's character. Someone forged a letter in Manqing's name and went to Fangshu to obtain goods. Manqing was old but still looked young; the visitor mistook him for Fangshu and handed him the letter. Manqing paid out what was requested without question. Supervising Censor Shi Jie passed through on an inspection tour, but Manqing refused to see him. Someone said, "Why show no regard for a fellow townsman?" Manqing said, "You have not thought it through." All policies of the day are not necessarily good. If the censor impeaches something, people will say I set it in motion. Those who shield one another in shared wrongdoing — someday our neighbors will suffer for it. His caution was all of this kind. In the disorder of the renchen year, he died of illness at Yiyang.
22
高仲振
Gao Zhongzhen
23
高仲振，字正之，遼東人。 其兄領開封鎮兵，仲振依之以居。 既而以家業付其兄，挈妻子入嵩山。 博極群書，尤深《易》《皇極經世》學。 安貧自樂，不入城市，山野小人亦知敬之。 嘗與其弟子張潛、王汝梅行山谷間，人望之翩然如仙。 或曰仲振嘗遇異人教以養生術，嘗終日燕坐，骨節戛戛有聲，所談皆世外事，有扣之者輒不復語雲。
Gao Zhongzhen, style name Zhengzhi, was from Liaodong. His elder brother commanded the Kaifeng garrison, and Zhongzhen lived with him. He then entrusted the family estate to his brother, took his wife and children, and retired to Mount Song. He read exhaustively among all books, especially mastering the Changes and the Huangji Jingshi. Content with poverty and delighting in his lot, he never entered the cities. Even mountain rustics knew to respect him. He once walked through mountain valleys with his disciples Zhang Qian and Wang Rumei. People who glimpsed them from afar thought them like immortals drifting along. Some say Zhongzhen once met an extraordinary man who taught him the art of nurturing life. He could sit in repose all day, his joints crackling audibly. What he discussed were matters beyond this world; if pressed, he would say no more.
24
張潛，字仲升，武清人。 幼有志節，慕荊軻、聶政為人，年三十始折節讀書。 時人高其行誼，目曰「張古人。 」後客崧山，從仲振受《易》。 年五十，始娶魯山孫氏，亦有賢行，夫婦相敬如賓，負薪拾穗，行歌自得，不知其貧也。 鄰里有為潛種瓜者，及熟讓潛，潛弗許，竟分而食之。 嘗行道中拾一斧，夫婦計度移時，乃持歸訪其主還之。 裏有兄弟分財者，其弟曰：「我家如此，獨不畏張先生知耶？ 」遂如初。 天興間，潛挈家避兵少室，乃不食七日死，孫氏亦投絕澗死焉。
Zhang Qian, style name Zhongsheng, was from Wuqing. In youth he aspired to heroic resolve and admired Jing Ke and Nie Zheng. Only at thirty did he restrain himself and take up reading. People of the time admired his conduct and called him "Zhang the Ancient." Later he lodged at Mount Song and studied the Changes under Zhongzhen. At fifty he first married a woman of the Sun clan of Lushan, who was likewise virtuous. Husband and wife respected each other as guests, carried firewood and gathered fallen grain, and walked along singing — unaware of their poverty. A neighbor once planted melons for Qian. When they ripened he offered them to Qian, who refused; in the end they divided and ate them together. Once on the road they picked up an axe. Husband and wife deliberated for some time, then took it home, found its owner, and returned it. In the village, brothers were dividing family property. The younger said, "Our household is like this — are we not afraid Mr. Zhang will find out?" They reverted to the original arrangement. During the Tianxing period, Qian led his family to Shaoshi to escape warfare. He fasted for seven days and died; Lady Sun also threw herself into a deep ravine and died.
25
王汝梅
Wang Rumei
26
王汝梅，字大用，大名人。 始由律學為伊陽簿，秩滿，遂隱居不仕。 性嗜書，動有禮法。 生徒以法經就學者，兼授以經學。 諸生服其教，無敢為非義者。 同業嘗憫其貧，時周之，皆謝不受。 後不知所終。
Wang Rumei, style name Dayong, was from Daming. He first entered office through legal studies as registrar of Yiyang. When his term ended, he retired and never took office again. He loved books by nature and observed ritual propriety in all his movements. Students who came to study the law canon with him he also instructed in the classical learning. His students submitted to his teaching, and none dared do what was not righteous. Colleagues pitied his poverty and from time to time gave him gifts, but he always declined. Later no one knew his end.
27
宋可，字予之，武陟人。 其姑適大族槁氏，貞祐之兵，夫及子皆死於難。 姑以白金五十笏遺可，可受不辭。 其後姑得槁氏疏族立為後，挈之省外家。 可乃置酒會鄉鄰，謂姑曰：「姑往時遺可以金，可以槁氏無子故受之。 今有子矣，此金槁氏物，非姑物也，可何名取之。 」因呼妻子舁金歸之，鄉里用是重之。 未幾，北兵駐山陽，軍中有聞可名者，訪知所在，質其子，使人招之曰：「從我者禍福共之，不然，汝子死矣。 」親舊競勸之往，可皆謝不從，曰：「吾有子無子，與吾兒死生，皆有命焉。 豈以一子故，並平生所守者亡之。 」後竟以無子。
Song Ke, style name Yuzhi, was from Wuzhi. His paternal aunt had married into the great Gao clan. During the warfare of Zhenyou, her husband and son all died in the calamity. The aunt left Ke fifty ingots of white gold. Ke accepted without declining. Later the aunt obtained a distant Gao clansman to establish as heir and took him to live outside her maiden family's home. Ke then set out wine and gathered the neighbors, telling his aunt, "When you left this gold with me, I accepted because the Gao clan had no heir. Now there is an heir. This gold belongs to the Gao clan, not to you — by what right should I keep it? He called his wife and children to carry the gold back. The neighborhood held him in high esteem. Before long, northern troops were stationed at Shanyang. Someone in the army who had heard Ke's name found where he lived, seized his son as a hostage, and sent a messenger saying, "Follow me and we shall share fortune and misfortune — otherwise your son dies." Relatives and friends urged him to go. Ke declined them all, saying, "Whether I have a son or not, my son's life and death, like my own, are matters of fate. How could I, for one son, cast away what I have upheld all my life? In the end he had no son.
28
辛願，字敬之，福昌人。 年二十五始知讀書，取《白氏諷諫集》自試，一日便能背誦。 乃聚書環堵中讀之，至《書伊訓》、《詩河廣》頗若有所省，欲罷不能，因更致力焉。 由是博極書史，作文有繩尺，詩律精嚴有自得之趣。 性野逸，不修威儀，貴人延客，麻衣草屨、足脛赤露坦然於其間，劇談豪飲，傍若無人。 嘗謂王鬱曰：「王侯將相，世所共嗜者，聖人有以得之亦不避。 得之不以道，與夫居之不能行己之志，是欲澡其身而伏於廁也。 是難與他人道，子宜保之。 」其志趣如此。
Xin Yuan, style name Jingzhi, was from Fuchang. At twenty-five he first learned to read. He took Bai Juyi's Collection of Admonitions and tested himself — in one day he could recite it from memory. He gathered books and read within his walled room. When he reached the Instructions of Yi in the Documents and the Broad River in the Odes, he seemed somewhat awakened, wished to stop but could not, and applied himself all the more. From this he read exhaustively in books and histories. His essays had measure and rule; his regulated verse was rigorous with a personal flavor. Wild and at ease by nature, he did not cultivate imposing bearing. When nobles invited him, he sat among them in hemp robe, straw sandals, and bare shins, talking freely and drinking boldly as though no one were present. He once said to Wang Yu, "Kings, marquises, generals, and ministers — what the age jointly craves — if the sage obtains them he does not avoid them. To obtain them not by the Way, or to dwell in them yet be unable to carry out one's own intent — that is wanting to bathe yet crouching in a privy. This is hard to speak of to others — you should keep it to yourself. His aspirations were of this kind.
29
後為河南府治中高廷玉客。 廷玉為府尹溫蒂罕福興所誣，願亦被訊掠，幾不得免，自是生事益狼狽。 願雅負高氣，不以從俗俯仰，迫以饑凍流離，往往見之於詩。 有詩數千首，常貯竹橐中。 正大末，歿洛下。 其詩有雲：「黃綺暫來為漢友，巢由終不是唐臣。 」真處士語也。
Later he became a retainer of Gao Tingyu, administrative supervisor of Henan Prefecture. Tingyu was framed by the prefect Wendihan Fuxing. Yuan was also interrogated under torture and nearly could not escape. From this his affairs grew ever more disordered. Yuan was proud by nature and would not bow to vulgar custom. Pressed by hunger, cold, and wandering exile, this often appeared in his poetry. He had several thousand poems, which he always stored in a bamboo bag. In the late Zhengda period, he died at Luoyang. Among his poems is the line: "Huang and Qi came for a time as friends of Han; Chao and You in the end were not ministers of Tang." Truly the words of a true recluse.
30
王予可
Wang Yuke
31
王予可，字南雲，河東吉州人。 父本軍校，予可亦嘗隸籍。 年三十許，大病後忽發狂，久之能把筆作詩文，及說世外恍惚事。 南渡後，居上蔡、遂平、郾城之間，遇文士則稱「大成將軍」，于佛前則稱「諦摩龍什」，於道則稱「騶天玄俊」，于貴遊則稱「威錦堂主人」。
Wang Yuke, style name Nanyun, was from Jizhou in Hedong. His father was originally a military school officer; Yuke was also once enrolled in the military registers. At about thirty, after a grave illness he suddenly went mad. After a long while he could take up the brush and compose poetry and prose, and speak of vague affairs beyond this world. After the southward migration he lived among Shangcai, Suiping, and Yancheng. Before literary gentlemen he called himself Great Accomplishment General; before the Buddha, Dharma Dragon Shiq; on the Way, Zou Tian Xuanjun; among noble travelers, Master of the Weijin Hall.
32
為人軀幹雄偉，貌奇古，戴青葛巾，項後垂雙帶若牛耳，一金鏤環在頂額之間，兩頰以青涅之為翠靨。 衣長不能掩脛。 落魄嗜酒，每入城，市人爭以酒食遺之。 夜宿土室中，夏月或屍穢在傍、蛆蟲狼籍不恤也。 人與之紙，落筆數百言，或詩或文，散漫碎雜，無句讀、無首尾，多六經中語及韻學家古文奇字，字畫峭勁，遇宋諱亦時避之。 或問以故事，其應如響，諸所引書，皆世所未見。 談說之際稍若有條貫，則又以誕幻語亂之。 麻九疇、張玨與之遊最狎，言其詩以百分為率，可曉者才二三耳。
Tall and imposing in person, his appearance was strangely ancient. He wore a green ge-cloth turban, with twin bands hanging behind his neck like ox ears and a gold-inlaid ring on his brow; on both cheeks indigo was painted as kingfisher dimples. His robes were long yet could not cover his shins. Down and out and addicted to wine, whenever he entered the city the townspeople vied to give him wine and food. At night he slept in earthen rooms. In summer corpses sometimes lay rotting beside him and maggots were scattered about — he paid no heed. When given paper, his brush produced several hundred words — poetry or prose, scattered and fragmentary, without punctuation or beginning or end, mostly phrases from the Six Classics and archaic odd characters of phonologists. The brushwork was steep and forceful; he sometimes avoided Song taboo names as well. Asked about historical matters, his responses were like an echo. All books he cited were unseen in the world. In discourse he would seem for a moment coherent, then confuse it again with fantastic words. Ma Jiuchou and Zhang Jue associated with him most intimately, saying that of his poetry, by percentage, only two or three parts in ten could be understood.
33
壬辰兵亂，為順天將領所得，知其名，竊議欲挈之北歸，館於州之瑞雲觀。 予可明日見將領自言曰：「我不能住君家瑞雲觀也。 」不數日卒。 後複有見於淮上者。
In the warfare of the renchen year he was taken by a Shuntian commander. Knowing his name, they privately discussed taking him north and lodged him at the prefecture's Ruixun Abbey. The next day Yuke went to see the commander and said, "I cannot stay at your household's Ruixun Abbey." Within a few days he died. Later someone again saw him on the Huai.
34
贊曰：金世隱逸不多見，今于簡冊所有，得十有二人焉。 其卓爾不群者三人。 褚承亮宋人，勒試進士，主司發策問宋徽、欽之罪，承亮長揖而去之。 方金人重舉業，杜時升居山中，首以「伊洛之學」教後進。 宋可不願仕，人執其子為質，寧棄而不就，遂以無子。 雖制行過中，豈不賢于殺妻以求大將者乎。 大夫士見善明、用心剛，故能為人所難為者如此。
The encomium says: Recluses of the Jin age are not often seen; those recorded in these annals amount to twelve persons. Among them, three stood out from the crowd. Chu Chengliang was a Song subject. Compelled to take the jinshi examination, when the chief examiner issued a policy question on the crimes of Huizong and Qinzong of Song, Chengliang made a deep bow and departed. Just when the Jin prized the examination curriculum, Du Shisheng lived in the mountains and was the first to teach the younger generation Neo-Confucian learning. Song Ke refused office. When men seized his son as a hostage, he would rather abandon the boy than submit, and thus ended without a son. Though his conduct exceeded the mean, is he not more worthy than one who killed his wife to win a general's command? When great officers and scholars see what is good with clarity and set their minds firmly, they can do what others find hard to do — as in cases such as these.
