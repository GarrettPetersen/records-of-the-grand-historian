【Han Records 46】 From the first year of the Qiangwu cycle through the last year of the Zhaoyang cycle—seven years in all.

In summer, in the fourth month, the rebels advanced against Jiuzhen, where Administrator Er Shi was killed in battle. An edict ordered Wei Lang, commandant of Jiuzhen, to crush the rebels.

7
或上言：「民之貧困以貨輕錢薄，宜改鑄大錢。 」事下四府群僚及太學能言之士議之。 太學生劉陶上議曰：「當今之憂，不在於貨，在乎民饑。 竊見比年已來，良苗盡於蝗螟之口，杼軸空於公私之求。 民所患者，豈謂錢貨之厚薄，銖兩之輕重哉！ 就使當今沙礫化為南金，瓦石變為和玉，使百姓渴無所飲，饑無所食，雖皇、羲之純德，唐、虞之文明，猶不能以保蕭牆之內也。 蓋民可百年無貨，不可一朝有饑，故食為至急也。 議者不達農殖之本，多言鑄冶之便。 蓋萬人鑄之，一人奪之，猶不能給； 況今一人鑄之，則萬人奪之乎！ 雖以陰陽為炭，萬物為銅，役不食之民，使不饑之士，猶不能足無厭之求也。 夫欲民殷財阜，要在止役禁奪，則百姓不勞而足。 陛下愍海內之憂戚，欲鑄錢齊貨以救其弊，猶養魚沸鼎之中。 棲鳥烈火之上； 水、木，本魚鳥之所生也，用之不時，必至焦爛。 願陛下寬鍥薄之禁，後冶鑄之議，聽民庶之謠吟，問路叟之所憂，瞰三光之文耀，視山河之分流，天下之心，國家大事，粲然皆見，無有遺惑者矣。 伏念當今地廣而不得耕，民眾而無所食，群小競進，秉國之位，鷹揚天下，鳥鈔求飽，吞肌及骨，並噬無厭。 誠恐卒有役夫、窮匠起於板築之間，投斤攘臂，登高遠呼，使怨之民響應雲合。 雖方尺之錢，何有能救其危也！ 」遂不改錢。
Someone submitted a memorial: "The people are impoverished because goods are cheap and coin is debased; the state should recoin larger currency. " The matter was referred to the officials of the four bureaus and to eloquent scholars of the Imperial Academy for deliberation. Liu Tao, a student of the Imperial Academy, submitted a proposal: "The pressing worry of our day lies not in currency but in famine among the people. I have seen that in recent years fine crops have been devoured by locusts and caterpillars, while looms stand empty under the demands of public and private exaction. What afflicts the people—can it truly be whether coin is thick or thin, heavy or light by the ounce! Even if gravel were turned to southern gold and tiles and stones to perfect jade, yet the people had nothing to drink when thirsty and nothing to eat when hungry—not even the pure virtue of the Yellow Emperor and Fu Xi, nor the civil brilliance of Yao and Shun, could keep peace within the palace walls. The people can do without goods for a hundred years but cannot endure hunger for a single morning—food is the supreme urgency. Those who debate the issue fail to grasp the foundations of agriculture and speak endlessly of the convenience of minting coin. If ten thousand men mint coin and one man seizes it all, it still would not suffice; how much less when one man mints the coin and ten thousand men seize it! Even if yin and yang were made charcoal and all creation copper, with unpaid laborers and unfed soldiers pressed into service, insatiable demands still could not be met. If the aim is to make the people prosperous and goods abundant, the essential thing is to halt corvée labor and forbid plunder—then the people will have enough without being worn down. Your Majesty, moved by the grief and distress throughout the realm, wishes to mint coin and equalize goods to remedy this ill—it is like raising fish in a boiling cauldron. Like birds perching upon blazing fire; Water and wood are what fish and birds naturally depend on; used at the wrong time, they will only end in scorching ruin. I pray Your Majesty will relax the prohibition on debased coin, set aside deliberation on minting, heed the ballads of the common people, ask what old men on the road worry over, look upon the brilliance of sun, moon, and stars, and gaze upon how mountains and rivers divide and flow—the heart of the realm and the great affairs of state will stand forth clearly, with nothing left obscure. I reflect that today the land is vast yet cannot be tilled, the people are many yet have nothing to eat; petty men vie for advancement and hold the reins of state, sweeping the realm like hawks and pecking like vultures for their fill, devouring flesh to the bone and gorging without end. I truly fear that suddenly corvée laborers and destitute craftsmen will rise from the earthworks, cast aside their tools and raise their arms, climb high and shout afar, and the resentful people will answer like clouds gathering. Even coin a foot square could not rescue such peril! " Thereupon the coinage was not changed.

In summer, in the fifth month, on jiaxu, the last day of the month, there was a solar eclipse. Director of Astronomy Chen Shou, through Junior Attendant of the Yellow Gates Xu Huang, reported that "the omen of the solar eclipse places the blame on Grand General Liang Ji." When Liang Ji heard of this, he had Luoyang officials arrest and examine Chen Shou, who died in prison. From this the emperor grew angry with Liang Ji.

17
十二月，南匈奴諸部並叛，與烏桓、鮮卑寇緣邊九郡。 帝以京兆尹陳龜為度遼將軍。 龜臨行，上疏曰：「臣聞三辰不軌，擢士為相； 蠻夷不恭，拔卒為將。 臣無文武之才，而忝鷹揚之任，雖歿軀體，無所云補。 今西州邊鄙，土地□脊□角，民數更寇虜，室家殘破，雖含生氣，實同枯朽。 往歲并州水雨，災螟互生，稼穡荒耗，租更空闕。 陛下以百姓為子，焉可不垂撫循之恩哉！ 古公、西伯天下歸仁，豈復輿金輦寶以為民惠乎！ 陛下繼中興之統，承光武之業，臨朝聽政而未留聖意。 且牧守不良，或出中官，懼逆上旨，取過目前。 呼嗟之聲，招致災害，胡虜凶悍，因衰緣隙； 而令倉庫單于豺狼之口，功業無銖兩之效，皆由將帥不忠，聚奸所致。 前涼州刺史祝良，初除到州，多所糾罰，太守令長，貶黜將半，政未逾時，功效卓然，實應賞異，以勸功能； 改任牧守，去斥奸殘； 又宜更選匈奴、烏桓護羌中郎將、校尉，簡練文下，授之法令； 除並、涼二州今年租、更，寬赦罪隸，掃除更始。 則善吏知奉公之祐，惡者覺營私之禍，胡馬可不窺長城，塞下無候望之患矣。 」帝乃更選幽、并刺史，自營、郡太守、都尉以下，多所革易。 下詔為陳將軍除並、涼一年租賦，以賜吏民。 龜到職，州郡重足震慄，省息經用，歲以億計。 詔拜安定屬國都尉張奐為北中郎將，以討匈奴、烏桓等。 匈奴、烏桓燒度遼將軍門，引屯赤阬，煙火相望。 兵眾大恐，各欲亡去。 奐安坐帷中，與弟子講誦自若，軍士稍安。 乃潛誘烏桓，陰與和通，遂使斬匈奴、屠各渠帥，襲破其眾，諸胡悉降。 奐以南單于車兒不能統理國事，乃拘之，奏立左谷蠡王為單于。 詔曰：「《春秋》大居正； 車兒一心向化，何罪而黜！ 其遣還庭！ 」
In the twelfth month, all divisions of the Southern Xiongnu rebelled together and, with the Wuhuan and Xianbei, raided nine border commanderies. The emperor appointed Chen Gui, governor of Jingzhao, as General Who Crosses the Liao. Before departing, Chen Gui submitted a memorial: "I have heard that when the three luminaries are irregular, a scholar is elevated to be chancellor; when the tribal peoples are insubordinate, a soldier is selected to be general. I have no civil or military talent, yet I disgracefully hold a commander's post; even if I gave my life, I could offer no real remedy. Now on the western frontier the land is barren and thin, the people have repeatedly suffered raids by enemy tribes, households lie in ruins, and though they still breathe, they are in truth like withered wood. In past years Bing province suffered floods and rain; disasters and caterpillars arose together; crops were wasted and depleted; and land tax and corvée dues went uncollected. Your Majesty treats the common people as your children—how can you not extend compassionate care and kindness! The Ancient Duke and the Lord of the West won the realm's allegiance through benevolence—did they need to load chariots with gold and treasures to benefit the people! Your Majesty has inherited the line of restoration and received the enterprise of Emperor Guangwu, yet in holding court and hearing governance you have not yet fixed your sage intent. Moreover, provincial overseers are unworthy; some come from palace eunuchs, fearing to go against the sovereign's intent and seeking only to pass the present moment. The sound of lamentation summons disaster; the barbarian enemies are fierce and bold, taking advantage of decline to exploit every opening; yet the storehouses are left to the wolves' mouths, and achievements show not the weight of an ounce—all because generals and commanders are disloyal and wicked men have gathered. Former Inspector of Liang province Zhu Liang, upon first arriving in office, punished many offenses; of prefects and magistrates nearly half were demoted and dismissed. Before his term had passed, his achievements stood forth conspicuously—he truly deserved reward and special recognition to encourage capable service; replace provincial overseers and remove the wicked and cruel; it is also fitting to newly select the commandants and colonels who protect the Qiang among the Xiongnu and Wuhuan, choose and train subordinate talent, and grant them laws and regulations; exempt Bing and Liang provinces from this year's land tax and corvée, broadly pardon convict laborers, and sweep away renewed beginnings of trouble. Then good officials will know the blessing of serving the public, the wicked will perceive the calamity of pursuing private gain, barbarian horses need not peer at the Long Wall, and below the passes there will be no worry of watch and alarm. " The emperor thereupon newly selected the inspectors of You and Bing provinces, and from commandants, commandery prefects, and commandants downward, changed many appointments. An edict exempted Bing and Liang provinces from one year's land tax and levies on account of General Chen, to be granted to officials and people. When Chen Gui took office, the provinces and commanderies stood on tiptoe in dread; he reduced routine expenses, saving tens of millions each year. An edict appointed Zhang Huan, commandant of the Dependent State of Anding, as Northern Commandant and ordered him to campaign against the Xiongnu, Wuhuan, and others. The Xiongnu and Wuhuan burned the gate of the General Who Crosses the Liao and encamped at Chikeng; signal fires stood face to face across the lines. The troops were greatly terrified and each wished to flee. Zhang Huan sat calmly within his tent, lecturing and reciting with his disciples as if nothing were amiss, and the soldiers gradually grew calm. He then secretly persuaded the Wuhuan and covertly made peace with them, had them behead the Xiongnu and Tuge chieftains, launch a surprise attack and break their forces, and all the tribes submitted. Zhang Huan, because Southern Chanyu Che'er could not govern the state's affairs, detained him and memorialized to install the Left Guli King as chanyu. An edict said: "The Spring and Autumn Annals greatly upholds the principle of proper succession; Che'er wholeheartedly turned toward civilization—what crime warrants his removal! Send him back to his court! "
18
大將軍冀與陳龜素有隙，譖其沮毀國威，挑取功譽，不為胡虜所畏，坐征還，以種暠為度遼將軍。 龜遂乞骸骨歸田裡，復征為尚書。 冀暴虐日甚，龜上疏言其罪狀，請誅之，帝不省。 龜自知必為冀所害，不食七日而死。 種暠到營所，先宣恩信，誘降諸胡，其有不服，然後加討； 羌虜先時有生見獲質於郡縣者，悉遣還之； 誠心懷撫，信賞分明，由是羌、胡皆來順服。 暠乃去烽燧，除候望，邊方晏然無警； 入為大司農。
Grand General Liang Ji and Chen Gui had long been at odds. Liang Ji slandered him for discouraging and damaging the state's prestige and contriving to seize credit for achievements, saying he was not feared by the barbarian enemies; Chen Gui was recalled on this charge, and Zhong Hao was made General Who Crosses the Liao. Chen Gui then requested to retire to his fields, but was again summoned as director of the masters of writing. Liang Ji's tyranny grew daily worse. Chen Gui submitted a memorial detailing his crimes and requesting his execution, but the emperor did not heed it. Chen Gui knew he would surely be harmed by Liang Ji; he fasted for seven days and died. When Zhong Hao arrived at the camp, he first proclaimed benevolence and trust, persuaded the various tribes to submit, and only then punished those who would not obey; among the Qiang enemies, those who had earlier been captured alive and held as hostages in the commanderies and counties were all sent back; with sincere care and comfort and clear rewards and punishments, from this the Qiang and tribal peoples all came to submit. Zhong Hao then removed beacon towers and watch posts; the border regions were tranquil and without alarm; He entered the capital as grand minister of agriculture.

梁皇后恃姊、兄廕勢，恣極奢靡，兼倍前世，專寵妒忌，六宮莫得進見。 及太后崩，恩寵頓衰。 後既無嗣，每宮人孕育，鮮得全者。 帝雖迫畏梁冀，不敢譴怒，然進御轉希，後益憂恚。 秋，七月，丙午，皇后梁氏崩。 乙丑，葬懿獻皇后於懿陵。 梁冀一門，前後七侯，三皇后，六貴人，二大將軍，夫人、女食邑稱君者七人，尚公主者三人，其餘卿、將、尹、校五十七人。 冀專擅威柄，凶恣日積，宮衛近侍，並樹所親，禁省起居，纖微必知。 其四方調發，歲時貢獻，皆先輸上第於冀，乘輿乃其次焉。 吏民繼貨求官、請罪者，道路相望。 百官遷召，皆先到冀門箋檄謝恩，然後敢詣尚書。 下邳吳樹為宛令，之官辭冀，冀賓客布在縣界，以情托樹，樹曰：「小人奸蠹，比屋可誅。 明將軍處上將之位，宜崇賢善以補朝闕。 自侍坐以來，未聞稱一長者，而多托非人，誠非敢聞！ 」冀嘿然不悅。 樹到縣，遂誅殺冀客為人害者數十人。 樹後為荊州刺史，辭冀，冀鴆之，出，死車上。 遼東太守侯猛初拜，不謁冀，冀托以它事腰斬之。 郎中汝南袁著，年十九，詣闕上書曰：「夫四時之運，功成則退，高爵厚寵，鮮不致災。 今大將軍位極功成，可為至戒，宜遵縣車之禮，高枕頤神。 傳曰：『木實繁者披枝害心。 』若不抑損盛權，將無以全其身矣！ 」冀聞而密遣掩捕，著乃變易姓名，托病偽死，結蒲為人，市棺殯送。 冀知其詐，求得，笞殺之。 太原郝絜、胡武，好危言高論，與著友善，絜、武嘗連名奏記三府，薦海內高士，而不詣冀。 冀追怒之，敕中都官稱檄禽捕，遂誅下家，死者六十餘人。 絜初逃亡，知不得免，因輿梓奏書冀門，書入，仰藥而死，家乃得全。 安帝嫡母耿貴人薨，冀從貴人從子林慮侯承求貴人珍玩，不能得，冀怒，並族其家十餘人。 涿郡崔琦以文章為冀所善，琦作《外戚箴》、《白鵠賦》以風，冀怒。 琦曰：「昔管仲相齊，樂聞譏諫之言； 蕭何佐漢，乃設書過之吏。 今將軍屢世台輔，任齊伊、周，而德政未聞，黎元塗炭，不能結納貞良以救禍敗，反欲鉗塞士口，杜蔽主聽，將使玄黃改色、馬鹿易形乎！ 」冀無以對，因遣琦歸。 琦懼而亡匿，冀捕得，殺之。
Empress Liang relied on the influence of her elder sister and brothers, indulging in extreme luxury exceeding the previous age, monopolizing favor with jealousy so that none in the six palaces could gain audience. When the empress dowager died, her favor suddenly declined. The empress had no heirs; whenever palace women conceived, few carried the pregnancy to term. Though the emperor was constrained and feared Liang Ji and dared not rebuke him in anger, his visits to the empress grew ever rarer, and the empress grew ever more aggrieved. In autumn, in the seventh month, on bingwu day, Empress Liang died. On yichou day, Empress Yixian was buried at Yiling. The Liang Ji clan, before and after, produced seven marquises, three empresses, six honored ladies, two grand generals, seven ladies and daughters with fiefs styled as lords, three who married princesses, and fifty-seven others among ministers, generals, governors, and colonels. Liang Ji monopolized authority, his violence and arrogance accumulating daily; palace guards and close attendants were all men he planted; within the forbidden precincts, the slightest movement was known to him. Tributes and seasonal gifts sent from all quarters were first delivered in the finest grade to Liang Ji; the imperial carriage received only what came second. Officials and common people bringing goods to seek office or plead for pardon filled the roads one after another. When officials were transferred or summoned, all first went to Liang Ji's gate with letters of thanks for the favor before daring to proceed to the masters of writing. Wu Shu of Xiapi was magistrate of Wan; on taking office he went to bid farewell to Liang Ji. Liang Ji's clients were spread throughout the county borders and asked Shu to show favor; Shu said: "Petty men are corrupt vermin—house by house they deserve execution. The enlightened general holds the position of a supreme commander and ought to honor the worthy and good to remedy the court's deficiencies. Since I have been in attendance, I have not heard you praise a single worthy man, yet you often entrust affairs to unworthy persons—this I truly dare not hear! " Liang Ji fell silent, displeased. When Wu Shu arrived at the county, he executed several dozen of Liang Ji's clients who had been harming people. Later Wu Shu became inspector of Jing province; on bidding farewell to Liang Ji, Liang Ji poisoned him, and he died in his carriage after leaving. Hou Meng, administrator of Liaodong, on first appointment did not pay a visit to Liang Ji; Liang Ji had him executed by waist-cutting on a pretext. Yuan Zhu of Runan, a gentleman of the palace aged nineteen, went to the palace gate and submitted a memorial: "The course of the four seasons is that when achievement is complete one withdraws; high rank and thick favor rarely fail to bring calamity. Now the grand general's position has reached its limit and his achievements are complete—this should be the greatest warning. He ought to follow the rite of hanging up his carriage, rest his head on a high pillow, and nourish his spirit. The tradition says: 'When a tree's fruit is abundant, its branches are stripped and its heart is harmed. ' If he does not restrain and reduce his overflowing power, he will have no means to preserve himself! " When Liang Ji heard of it, he secretly sent men to seize Zhu. Zhu then changed his name, feigned illness and pretended death, made a dummy of rushes, bought a coffin, and had a funeral procession. Liang Ji learned of the deception, found him, and had him beaten to death. Hao Jie and Hu Wu of Taiyuan loved bold words and lofty discourse and were friends with Zhu. Jie and Wu once jointly submitted memorials to the three bureaus recommending eminent men throughout the realm, but did not visit Liang Ji. Liang Ji pursued them in anger and ordered the Central Office to issue warrants for their arrest; he then executed their families to the last person, and more than sixty died. Hao Jie at first fled, knowing he could not escape; he then had a coffin brought and submitted a memorial at Liang Ji's gate. When the memorial was delivered, he took poison and died, and his family was spared. When Honored Lady Geng, empress dowager of Emperor An, died, Cheng, Marquis of Linlu and a nephew of the Honored Lady through her clan, asked Liang Ji for her precious objects but could not obtain them. Liang Ji was angry and exterminated his entire family of more than ten. Cui Qi of Zhuo commandery was favored by Liang Ji for his literary skill. Qi wrote "Admonition for Consort Clans" and "Ode to the White Swan" as indirect remonstrance, and Liang Ji was angry. Qi said: "In former times Guan Zhong served as chancellor of Qi and delighted in hearing words of criticism and remonstrance; Xiao He assisted the Han and even established officials to record faults. Now the general for generations has held the highest posts, his responsibility equal to Yi Yin and the Duke of Zhou, yet no virtuous governance is heard of and the common people are in misery. Formerly Emperor Gaozu endured Zhou Chang's blunt remonstrance, and Emperor Cheng pardoned Zhu Yun when he was to be beheaded at the waist—if Yun is killed today, I fear the reproach of 'cutting out the heart' will again be debated in the world!" Guan Ba reported on Yun's case and, kneeling, said: "Li Yun is a rustic fool of the wilds, and Du Zhong a petty clerk of the commandery—acting from reckless rashness, unworthy of punishment. " The emperor said to Ba: "'The emperor wishes not to examine closely'—what sort of words are these, and yet you, a regular attendant, wish to pardon them!" " He then had a junior attendant of the yellow gates approve Ba's report. Yun and Zhong both died in prison, and from this the emperor's favorites grew still more overbearing. Within the seas there is grief and fear, resentment ever more tightly bound; throughout court and countryside, loyalty has become a thing to be avoided. Director of the masters of writing Zhou Yong had long served Liang Ji and borrowed his authority; seeing Ji about to fall, he feigned denunciation to show loyalty, then by treacherous scheming also obtained enfeoffment as marquis. Moreover, the yellow gates harbor wickedness; factions band together. Since Ji's rise to power they have been intimate front and back, plotting day and night, jointly weaving treacherous schemes; When Ji was about to be executed and no trickery could be devised, they recorded his crimes to demand titles and rewards. Your Majesty does not conduct a clear investigation to distinguish true from false, but again enfeoffs them alongside loyal ministers at the same time, making crimson and purple one color and mixing powder with ink—what is called casting gold and jade among gravel and shattering scepters and disks in mud. When the four quarters hear of it, none fail to rage and sigh. Your servant's family has received the state's grace for generations; though my person is slight, my office is weighty. At this time enfeoffments and rewards exceeded regulation, and inner favorites were excessively numerous. Chen Fan submitted a memorial, saying: "Marquisates correspond above to the twenty-eight lunar mansions and serve as screens for the supreme state; Emperor Gaozu's covenant was that none but men of merit should be enfeoffed. Yet I hear that the slight merit of Deng Wanshi's father Zun, when intendant of Henan, is being traced and recorded, and that director of the masters of writing Huang Jun's ancestors' extinguished enfeoffment is being restored. Intimates receive fiefs without righteous cause; those at hand pass down rewards without merit—so that within a single household several may be marquises. Thus the cords of the heavens lose their measure and yin and yang fall into disorder. Your servant knows the enfeoffments are already done and speech comes too late; I truly wish Your Majesty would stop from here. Moreover, selected women number in the thousands—they eat meat and wear brocade; oils, powders, and cosmetics beyond reckoning. A vulgar saying runs: 'A thief does not pass a gate with five daughters'—because daughters impoverish a household; Are not the women of the rear palace impoverishing the state!" " The emperor took much of this to heart and released more than five hundred palace women—but he only granted Jun the title of marquis within the passes and enfeoffed Wanshi as marquis of Nanxiang.

" He replied: "When director of the masters of writing Chen Fan handles affairs, there is order; when regular attendants and the yellow gates share in government, there is chaos. From this I know Your Majesty can be led to do good or led to do wrong." " The emperor said: "In old times Zhu Yun broke the balustrade in court; now a palace attendant faces me and calls my conduct wrong—I respectfully accept the reproof of my failings." " He was appointed general of the five offices and in time was promoted to grand herald. When a guest star crossed the emperor's throne, the emperor secretly questioned Yan. I have heard that those at the emperor's side exist to consult on governance and virtue. Keep company with good men and each day you hear fine instruction; Keep company with wicked men and each day evil inclinations are born. If Your Majesty would keep slanderers and flatterers far off and take in upright, forthright men, then portents and anomalies can be removed." " The emperor could not adopt this. Yan pleaded illness and was dismissed to return home.

In spring, in the first month, on bingshen day, an amnesty was proclaimed throughout the realm and an edict sought descendants of Li Gu. Initially, after Gu was removed by imperial rescript, knowing he could not escape disaster, he sent his three sons Ji, Zi, and Xie back to their home district. Xie was then thirteen. His elder sister Wenji was the wife of Zhao Boying of the same commandery. Seeing the two elder brothers return, she fully understood the affair's root and, grieving silently alone, said: "The Li clan is destroyed! From the Grand Duke Taigong onward we have piled up virtue and heaped up kindness—how could we meet this!" " Secretly she plotted with the two elder brothers to hide Xie beforehand, falsely saying he had returned to the capital; everyone believed it. Before long disaster struck; the provinces and commanderies seized Ji and Zi, and both died in prison. Wenji then told her father's disciple Wang Cheng: "You have held to righteousness for my late father and have the integrity of the ancients; Now I entrust to you this orphan of six feet—the survival or extinction of the Li clan rests with you!" " Cheng then took Xie aboard a boat heading east down the river, entered the territory of Xu Province, changed their names, and made Xie a tavern hand while Cheng sold divinations in the market. Each appeared a stranger to the other while secretly keeping contact. After more than ten years, once Liang Ji had been executed, Xie told the tavern keeper the whole story. The innkeeper prepared a carriage and rich gifts to send him off, but Xie would accept none of them. He returned to his home district, resumed mourning garb, and when brother and sister met, their grief moved all who looked on. His elder sister admonished Xie: "Our family's sacrificial line was nearly cut off; you, younger brother, were spared by fortune—is this not Heaven's doing! You should cut yourself off from the crowd, make no rash comings and goings, and take care never to speak a word against the Liang clan! To speak against the Liangs would implicate the sovereign—disaster would come again. You need only take blame upon yourself." " Xie carefully followed her counsel. Later, when Wang Cheng died, Xie buried him with full rites and at each of the four seasonal festivals set out the seat of an honored guest and made offerings.

In Shanyin County there were five or six old men who came out from the valleys of Ruye. In winter, the Xianlian and Shendi Qiang together with the various Qiang tribes raided Bing and Liang provinces. Colonel Duan Jiong led volunteer auxiliaries from Huangzhong to attack them. Inspector of Liang Guo Hong, greedy to share in his achievements, detained and blocked Jiong's army so it could not advance; The volunteer auxiliaries, having served long and longing for home, all rebelled and returned. Guo Hong shifted blame onto Jiong; Jiong was arrested on charge, imprisoned, and sentenced to corvée labor at the Left Construction Office. Huo Hong, chancellor of Jinan, replaced him as colonel. Huo Hong had no authority or strategy; the Qiang then grew increasingly lawless, overran camps and fortresses, one after another joined forces, and burst upon the commanderies—the raids grew ever worse. Administrator of Taishan Huangfu Gui submitted a memorial, saying: "Now the cunning bandits are nearly exterminated and Taishan is largely pacified, yet I again hear that all the Qiang tribes have rebelled together. I grew up in Bin and Qi; I am fifty-nine years old. Formerly I was a commandery clerk and twice experienced Qiang rebellions; I prepared plans for these matters and had words that hit the mark by mistake. I have long suffered a chronic illness; I fear my dogs-and-horses years are ending and I will not repay the great grace. I wish to request a supernumerary post and serve as an envoy with a single carriage, to comfort and summon the Three Adjuncts, proclaim the state's might and favor, and with the terrain and military conditions I know assist the various armies. I have lived poor and isolated in peril, watching commandery generals for decades; from Wushu to Eastern Dai, the ailment is the same. To strive to seek fierce enemies is not as good as tranquility; Diligently mastering Sun and Wu is not equal to upholding the law. The recent upheaval is not far off; I am truly anxious, and therefore overstep my duty to offer my humble efforts. " An edict appointed Gui as general of the household and ordered him, bearing the staff of authority, to oversee the troops of the Pass West in attacking the Lingwu and others. In the eleventh month, Gui attacked the Qiang, defeated them, and beheaded eight hundred. The Xianlian and various Qiang tribes, admiring Gui's authority and faith, encouraged one another to surrender—more than one hundred thousand.

In the third month, the Shendi Qiang raided Zhangye and Jiuquan. Huangfu Gui dispatched the Xianlian and various Qiang tribes to jointly attack Longyou, but the roads were cut off and a great epidemic broke out in the army; three or four tenths died. Gui personally entered the tents and shelters to inspect the officers and troops; the three armies were moved and pleased. The Eastern Qiang then sent envoys begging to surrender, and Liang Province was reconnected. Earlier, administrator of Anding Sun Jun had taken bribes in disarray; commandant of the dependent state Li Xi and supervising army imperial secretary Zhang Bing had killed many surrendered Qiang; inspector of Liang Guo Hong and administrator of Hanyang Zhao Xi were both old and weak and unfit for office, yet all relied on powerful patrons and did not follow the law. When Gui arrived, he memorialized all their crimes in detail; some were dismissed and some executed. When the Qiang heard of this, they all at once turned to goodness; Dianchang and Jitian, great chiefs of the Shendi, together with more than one hundred thousand people again came to Gui to surrender.

Bandits of Ai County attacked the commanderies and counties of Changsha, killed the magistrate of Yiyang, and gathered to more than ten thousand; Palace attendant Ma Mu supervised Jing inspector Liu Du in attacking them; the army was defeated and Mu and Du fled. The tribes of Lingling also rebelled. In winter, in the tenth month, the tribes of Wuling rebelled and raided Jiangling. Administrator of Nanjun Li Su fled. Gui submitted a memorial in his own defense: "In the autumn of the fourth year, the western tribes grew rebellious; the old capital was seized with fear, and the court fixed its anxious gaze on the west. Your servant raised the state's authority; the Qiang and Rong bowed in submission, and more than a hundred million in expenses was saved. Believing that a loyal minister should not plead his hardships, I was ashamed to mention even in brief my modest achievements; yet when measured against what came before, I hoped at least to avoid guilt and remorse. When I first entered the province, I memorialized against Sun Jun, Li Xi, and Zhang Bing; When the army turned south on campaign, I again memorialized against Guo Hong and Zhao Xi, detailing their crimes and demanding the death penalty. These five officials and their factions commanded half the realm; from petty officials with black ribbons down to minor clerks, more than a hundred others were implicated as well. Officials seized on the general's grievances for revenge; sons sought to wipe away their fathers' shame. Bearing gifts they raced in chariots; with provisions in their packs they ran on foot; they colluded with powerful houses and competed to spread slander, claiming your servant privately paid off the Qiang with money and goods. If I used private funds, my household owns not a single measure of grain; If the goods came from public stores, the accounts are easily checked. Even setting aside my own dull confusion—if their accusations were true—in former ages the court sent palace women to the Xiongnu and stationed princesses to secure the Wusun; If all I did was spend ten million to win over rebellious Qiang, what crime against duty or reason is there in a capable minister's resourcefulness—the very quality military strategists prize! Since the Yongchu era, countless generals have been dispatched: five armies were wiped out, campaigns cost hundreds of millions; some returned with chariots intact and seals unbroken and traded their glory with powerful patrons, yet still won fame and success and received lavish enfeoffments. Now that I have returned to supervise my native region, investigated commanderies, severed ties and alienated kin, and shamed old associates—popular slander and covert attacks are only natural! "

Director of the masters of writing Zhu Mu, detesting the eunuchs' rampant arrogance, submitted a memorial: "According to Han precedent, regular attendants participated in selecting scholar-officials; after the Jianwu era, eunuchs alone were employed. Since the Yanping era they have grown ever more exalted, wearing sable and ring ornaments, occupying the roles of chief ministers—all affairs of the imperial court pass through their hands alone. Their power overshadows the realm; their favor and rank know no limit; sons, younger brothers, and kin alike bear honors and offices. Wanton and overflowing with pride, none can restrain them; they ruin the realm and drain the common people dry. Your humble servant believes they should all be abolished, the former practice restored, and upright men throughout the realm who understand statecraft chosen to fill their posts—then the myriad people would receive your sage transforming influence! " The emperor refused. Later, when Mu had an audience, he spoke again: "Your servant has heard that by Han household precedent, one attendant-in-ordinary and one regular attendant were appointed to oversee the masters of writing; one gentleman attendant at the Yellow Gates transmitted and dispatched memorials; All were drawn from the great clans. Since Empress Dowager Hexi held the regency as a woman ruler and refused to receive the high officials, eunuchs were made regular attendants, and junior attendants at the Yellow Gates carried messages between the two palaces. From that time power overshadowed the sovereign and exhausted the realm; all should be dismissed, and broadly chosen aged scholars of long-standing virtue should participate in governance. " The emperor was angered and made no reply. Mu prostrated himself and refused to rise; attendants relayed, "Withdraw! " After a long while, he at last hurried away. From this time the eunuchs repeatedly invoked imperial edicts on various pretexts to slander him. Mu was by nature stern; frustrated in his aims, he soon died of a carbuncle brought on by rage and vexation.
