漢靈帝中平末刺史王睿始治江陵，吳時西陵督鎮之。 晉太康元年平吳，以爲刺史治。 愍帝建興元年，刺史周顗避杜弢賊奔建康，陶侃爲刺史，治沌口。 王敦治武昌。 其後或還江陵，或在夏口。 桓溫平蜀，治江陵。 以臨沮西界，水陸紆險，行逕裁通，南通巴、巫，東南出州治，道帶蠻、蜑，田土肥美，立爲汶陽郡，以處流民。 屬氐陷襄陽，桓沖避居上明，頓陸遜樂鄉城上四十餘里，以田地肥良，可以爲軍民資實，又接近三峽，無西疆之虞，故重戍江南，輕戍江北。 苻堅敗後，復得襄陽。 太元十四年，王忱還江陵。 江陵去襄陽步道五百，勢同脣齒，無襄陽則江陵受敵，不立故也。 自忱以來，不復動移。 境域之內，含帶蠻、蜑，土地遼落，稱爲殷曠。 江左大鎮，莫過荊、揚。 弘農郡陝縣，周世二伯總諸侯，周公主陝東，召公主陝西，故稱荊州爲陝西也。 領郡如左：
Late in Han Lingdi's Zhongping reign, Inspector Wang Rui first took Jiangling as his seat; under Wu, the Xiling garrison commandant held it. In Jin Taikang 1, after the conquest of Wu, it became the provincial seat. In Emperor Min's Jianxing 1, Inspector Zhou Yi fled Du Tao's rebels to Jiankang; Tao Kan succeeded as inspector and sat at Zhunkou. Wang Dun governed from Wuchang. Afterward the seat shifted between Jiangling and Xiakou. Huan Wen pacified Shu and returned the seat to Jiangling. West of Linju the land and water routes were tortuous and dangerous, passable only with difficulty; south lay Ba and Wu, southeast the road out to the provincial seat ran through Man and Dan country, and the soil was rich—so Wenyang commandery was set up for displaced people. When the Di took Xiangyang, Huan Chong withdrew to Shangming and camped forty-odd li above Lu Xun's fortress at Lecheng; the fields were fertile enough to feed army and people, and the site lay near the Three Gorges with no threat from the west—so garrisons were weighted on the south bank and thinned on the north. After Fu Jian's defeat, Xiangyang was recovered. In Taixuan 14, Wang Chen returned to Jiangling. Jiangling lies five hundred li from Xiangyang on foot; the two stand like lips and teeth—without Xiangyang, Jiangling is exposed to attack, and for that reason the link was never abandoned. Since Chen's time the seat has not moved again. Inside its borders live Man and Dan peoples; the territory is broad and thinly settled, yet reckoned rich and spacious. Of the great strongholds south of the Yangtze, none match Jing and Yang. Shan county in Hongnong commandery: in Zhou times the Two Lords mustered the feudal lords; the Duke of Zhou held the east of Shan, the Duke of Shao the west—hence Jing province is called Shan-west. It administers the following commanderies:

三峽險隘，山蠻寇賊，宋泰始三年，議立三巴校尉以鎮之。 後省，昇明二年，復置。 建元二年，分荊州巴東、建平，益州巴郡爲州，立刺史，而領巴東太守，又割涪陵郡屬。 永明元年省，各還本屬焉。
The Three Gorges are perilous and mountain Man raiders abound; in the third year of Song Taishi a Commandant of the Three Ba was proposed to garrison the area. It was later abolished, then restored in the second year of Shengming. In the second year of Jianyuan, Badong and Jianping from Jing and Ba commandery from Yi were carved into a separate province with an inspector who also served as Administrator of Badong; Fuling commandery was detached to it. In the first year of Yongming it was abolished and each district returned to its original jurisdiction.

鎮夏口，舊要害也。 吳置督將爲魯口屯，對魯山岸，因爲名也。 晉永嘉中，荊州刺史都督山簡自襄陽避賊奔夏口，庾翼爲荊州，治夏口，並依地嶮也。 太元中，荊州刺史桓沖移鎮上明，上表言：「氐賊送死之日，舊郢以北，堅壁相望，待以不戰。 江州刺史桓嗣宜進屯夏口，據上下之中，於事爲便。」 義熙元年，冠軍將軍劉毅以爲夏口二州之中，地居形要，控接湘川，邊帶溳、沔，請幷州刺史劉道規鎮夏口。 夏口城據黃鵠磯，世傳仙人子安乘黃鵠過此上也。 邊江峻險，樓櫓高危，瞰臨沔、漢，應接司部，宋孝武置州於此，以分荊楚之勢。 領郡如左：
The seat lay at Xiakou, an old strategic chokepoint. Under Wu a garrison commandant was posted at Lukou encampment opposite the Lu Mountain bank, hence the name. During Jin Yongjia, Inspector-General Shan Jian fled bandits from Xiangyang to Xiakou; Yu Yi as inspector of Jing governed from Xiakou, relying on the terrain's barriers. In Taixuan, Inspector of Jing Huan Chong moved the seat to Shangming and memorialized: "When the Di enemy comes to their doom, north of old Ying our fortified walls will face each other, awaiting battle without engaging. Inspector of Jiang Huan Si should advance and encamp at Xiakou, holding the middle ground between upstream and downstream—it would be advantageous." In Yixi 1, Champion General Liu Yi argued that Xiakou lay between two provinces at a commanding point, linking Xiang province and bordering the Yu and Mian rivers; he asked Inspector of Bing Liu Daogui to garrison Xiakou. Xiakou city stands on Huanghe Rock; tradition says the immortal Zi'an once rode a yellow crane past this spot aloft. The river border is steep; watchtowers rise high over the Mian and Han, linking to the Si region. Emperor Xiaowu of Song established a province here to divide Jing and Chu power. It administers the following commanderies:

鎮襄陽，晉中朝荊州都督所治也。 元帝以魏該爲雍州，鎮酇城，襄陽別有重戍。 庾翼爲荊州，謀北伐，鎮襄陽。 自永嘉亂，襄陽民戶流荒。 咸康八年，尚書殷融言：「襄陽、石城，疆埸之地，對接荒寇。 諸荒殘寄治郡縣，民戶寡少，可幷合之。」 朱序爲雍州，於襄陽立僑郡縣，沒苻氐。 氐敗，復還南，復用朱序。 襄陽左右，田土肥良，桑梓野澤，處處而有。 郗恢爲雍州，于時舊民甚少，新戶稍多。 宋元嘉中，割荊州五郡屬，遂爲大鎮。 疆蠻帶沔，阻以重山，北接宛、洛，平塗直至，跨對樊、沔，爲鄢郢北門。 部領蠻左，故別置蠻府焉。 領郡如左：
The seat lay at Xiangyang; in the Jin central court it was where the Inspector-General of Jing sat. Emperor Yuan made Wei Gai inspector of Yong and sat at Zuo city; Xiangyang kept a separate strong garrison. Yu Yi held Jing province, planned a northern campaign, and garrisoned Xiangyang. After the Yongjia upheaval, Xiangyang's registered households were driven off and the land lay empty. In Xiankang 8, Minister of the Masters of Writing Yin Rong said: "Xiangyang and Shicheng are border posts hard against barbarian enemies. Refugee districts set up for the ruined borderlands have too few households; they should be consolidated." Zhu Xu became inspector of Yong, set up refugee commanderies and counties at Xiangyang, and the region fell to Fu Jian's Di. After the Di were beaten, the seat returned south, and Zhu Xu was again put in charge. On either side of Xiangyang the soil is rich; mulberry hamlets and marshland appear at every turn. Xi Hui held Yong; by then the long-settled people were few, while newer registrations were somewhat greater. In Song Yuanjia five Jing commanderies were detached and placed under it, and it became a major stronghold. The frontier runs with the Man; the Han River threads through it; great mountains bar the way; northward Wan and Luo lie on a straight level road; facing Fan and the Han across the water, it is the northern gate of Yan and Ying. It oversees the left-side Man peoples, and for that reason a separate Man headquarters was set up. It administers the following commanderies:

When the Central Plains fell into chaos, it was lost to the northerners. In Emperor Mu's Yonghe 8, Wang Zhuo, the Later Zhao puppet inspector of Qin, surrendered and was kept in post; soon Fu Jian defeated him. In year 11, Huan Wen made the Di king Yang Guo inspector of Qin, but he held no settled land or people. By Taixuan 14, Yongzhou inspector Zhu Xu first oversaw Qin—an arrangement of Emperor Xiaowu's reign. The seat lodged at Xiangyang with no separate inspector; thereafter the Yongzhou inspector regularly oversaw it. In Long'an 2, Guo Quan first became inspector of Liang and Southern Qin, with the provincial seat lodged at Hanzhong. In year 4, Huan Xuan oversaw seven provinces but named only Qin. In Yuanxing 1, Fu Jian's son Hong was made inspector of North Qin. From then on the Jingzhou overseer regularly oversaw Qin, while the Liang inspector regularly held the title of Southern Qin. In Yixi 3, the Di king Yang Guo was made inspector of North Qin. In year 14, East Qin was established and Liu Yizhen made inspector. Guo Gong was inspector of Liang; Yin Ya was inspector of Qin. 贊曰：郡國旣建，因州而部。 離過十三，合不踰九。 分城列邑，名號殷阜。 遷徙叛逆，代亡代有。 [1]
In praise: commanderies and states were founded, then grouped by province; split, they might run past thirteen; merged, they rarely held at nine. Towns and fiefs were carved out, names and ranks piled high. Through flight and revolt, one age died and another was born. [1] Endnote marker.
