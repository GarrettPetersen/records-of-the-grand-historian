南齊書卷五十三‧列傳第三十四良政
Book of Southern Qi, Volume 53, Biographies 34: Virtuous Officials
2
傅琰虞愿劉懷慰裴昭明沈憲李珪之孔琇之
Fu Yan, Yu Yuan, Liu Huaiwei, Pei Zhaoming, Shen Xian, Li Guizhi, and Kong Xiuzhi
3
太祖承宋氏奢縱，風移百城，輔立幼主，思振民瘼。 爲政未朞，擢山陰令傅琰爲益州刺史。 乃捐華反樸，恭己南面，導民以躬，意存勿擾。 以山陰大邑，獄訟繁滋，建元三年，別置獄丞，與建康爲比。 永明繼運，垂心治術。 杖威善斷，猶多漏網，長吏犯法，封刃行誅。 郡縣居職，以三周爲小滿。 水旱之災，輙加賑卹。 明帝自在布衣，曉達吏事，君臨億兆，專務刀筆，未嘗枉法申恩，守宰以之肅震。
The Founding Emperor took over after the Song court's extravagance had spread its influence through countless cities. He helped place a young ruler on the throne and set out to relieve the people's suffering. He had barely begun to govern when he promoted Fu Yan, the magistrate of Shanyin, to Governor of Yizhou. He cast off display for simplicity, held himself with restraint on the throne, led the people by his own example, and kept his mind set on leaving them undisturbed. Because Shanyin was a major prefecture where lawsuits multiplied, in the third year of Jianyuan a separate prison clerk was installed there, on the same footing as Jiankang. When the Yongming era began, he devoted himself to the craft of governing well. He ruled with stern authority and sharp judgment, yet many offenders still escaped; when senior officials broke the law, the executioner's blade was drawn and sentence was carried out. For service in prefectural and county office, three years counted as a completed term. Whenever flood or drought struck, relief and aid were promptly provided. Even before he took the throne, Emperor Ming understood how government worked. Once he ruled the realm he lived by the written law, never twisting it to show favor, and officials throughout the land were awed into discipline.
4
永明之世，十許年中，百姓無雞鳴犬吠之警，都邑之盛，士女富逸，歌聲舞節，袨服華粧，桃花綠水之間，秋月春風之下，蓋以百數。 及建武之興，虜難猋急，征役連歲，不遑啓居，軍國糜耗，從此衰矣。
For some ten years under Yongming the people knew no disturbance even of crowing cocks or barking dogs. The capital flourished: men and women lived in comfort and plenty, with song and dance, fine dress and ornament, amid peach blossoms and green waters, under autumn moons and spring breezes—such scenes numbered in the hundreds. When the Jianwu era began, northern raids grew fierce. Levies and corvée dragged on year after year; people had no peace even at home. State and army were drained, and from that point the dynasty declined.
5
齊世善政著名表績無幾焉，位次遷升，非直止乎城邑。 今取其清察有迹者，餘則隨以附焉。
Under the Qi there were few officials whose good governance won lasting fame. Many rose in rank beyond the level of city and county magistrate. Here we record those whose integrity and sharp judgment left a clear record; the others are included as they follow.
6
傅琰字季珪，北地靈州人也。 祖邵，員外郎。 父僧祐，安東錄事參軍。
Fu Yan, courtesy name Jigui, was a native of Lingzhou in Beidi commandery. His grandfather Shao served as a Director of Administration. His father Sengyou was Recorder under the Eastern Pacification command.
7
泰始六年，遷山陰令。 山陰，東土大縣，難爲長官，僧祐在縣有稱，琰尤明察，又著能名。 其年爵新亭侯。 元徽初，遷尚書右丞。
In the sixth year of Taishi he was appointed magistrate of Shanyin. Shanyin was a major county in the east and a hard post for any magistrate. Sengyou had already won praise there, and Yan was especially sharp in judgment and soon gained a reputation for ability. That same year he was enfeoffed as Marquis of Xinting. At the start of the Yuanhui era he was promoted to Right Vice Director of the Imperial Secretariat.
8
遭母喪，居南岸，隣家失火，延燒琰屋，琰抱柩不動，隣人競來赴救，乃得俱全。 琰股髀之閒，已被煙焰。 服闋，除邵陵王左軍諮議，江夏王錄事參軍。
When his mother died he lived on the south bank. A neighbor's fire spread to his house, but Yan held the coffin and would not move. Neighbors rushed to help, and both he and the coffin were saved unharmed. The flesh between his thigh and hip had already been scorched by smoke and flame. When mourning ended he was appointed consultative officer of the Left Army under the Prince of Shaoling and recorder under the Prince of Jiangxia.
9
太祖輔政，以山陰獄訟煩積，復以琰爲山陰令。 賣針賣糖老姥爭團絲，來詣琰，琰不辨覈，縳團絲於柱鞭之，密視有鐵屑，乃罰賣糖者。 二野父爭雞，琰各問「何以食雞」，一人云「粟」，一人云「豆」，乃破雞得粟，罪言豆者。 縣內稱神明，無敢復爲偷盜。 琰父子竝著奇績，江左鮮有。 世云「諸傅有《治縣譜》，子孫相傳，不以示人」。
While the Founding Emperor held power as regent, lawsuits in Shanyin had piled up, so Yan was again appointed magistrate of Shanyin. An old woman who sold needles and one who sold sugar quarreled over a ball of silk and brought the case to Yan. Without questioning them at once, he tied the silk to a post and had it beaten, watching secretly for iron filings. When he found them, he punished the sugar-seller. Two peasants disputed ownership of a chicken. Yan asked each what he fed it; one said millet, the other beans. He slaughtered the bird and found millet in its crop, then convicted the man who had said beans. The county hailed him as uncannily perceptive in judgment, and no one dared steal again. Father and son alike won extraordinary reputations as magistrates—rare in the lands south of the Yangzi. People said that the Fu family kept a Manual for Governing Counties, passed down from generation to generation and never shown to outsiders.
10
臨淮劉玄明亦有吏能，爲山陰令，大著名績。 琰子翽問之，玄明曰：「我臨去當告卿。 」將別，謂之曰：「作縣唯日食一升飰，而莫飲酒。」
Liu Xuanming of Linhuai also had real talent for administration; as magistrate of Shanyin he won great fame for his achievements. Yan's son Lou asked him for advice. Xuanming said, "When I am about to leave office I will tell you." " At parting he said to him, "As magistrate, eat only one sheng of rice a day—and do not drink wine."
11
虞愿字士恭，會稽餘姚人也。 祖賚，給事中，監利侯。 父望之，早卒。 賚中庭橘樹冬熟，子孫競來取之，愿年數歲，獨不取，賚及家人皆異之。
Yu Yuan, courtesy name Shigong, was a native of Yuyao in Kuaiji commandery. His grandfather Fen served as a Palace Attendant and was enfeoffed as Marquis of Jianli. His father Wangzhi died while still young. An orange tree in Fen's courtyard ripened in winter, and the grandchildren rushed to pick the fruit. Yuan was only a few years old and alone refused to take any. Fen and the whole family were struck by this.
12
元嘉末，爲國子生，再遷湘東王國常侍，轉潯陽王府墨曹參軍。 明帝立，以愿儒吏學涉，兼蕃國舊恩，意遇甚厚。 除太常丞，尚書祠部郎，通直散騎侍郎，領五郡中正，祠部郎如故。 帝性猜忌，體肥憎風，夏月常著皮小衣，拜左右二人爲司風令史，風起方面，輙先啓聞。 星文災變，不信太史，不聽外奏，勑靈臺知星二人給愿，常直內省，有異先啓，以相檢察。
Near the end of the Yuanjia era he entered the Imperial University, was twice promoted to regular attendant in the Principality of Xiangdong, and then became ink-bureau adjutant in the Principality of Xunyang. When Emperor Ming came to the throne, he favored Yuan greatly—for his learning and skill as a scholar-official, and for old ties to the imperial clan. He was appointed Vice Director of the Imperial Sacrifices, Director of the Sacrificial Affairs Section, Attendant-in-Ordinary of the Scattered Cavalry, and concurrently head of the Five-Commandery Rectification Bureau, while keeping his post in the Sacrificial Affairs Section. The emperor was suspicious by nature, heavy in build, and hated drafts. In summer he often wore a small leather vest. He appointed two attendants as Wind-Control Clerks who had to report at once whenever wind rose from any direction. For celestial signs and disasters he distrusted the Grand Astrologer and would not hear outside reports. He assigned Yuan two Observatory astronomers who stayed on duty in the inner palace, reported any anomaly at once, and kept watch on each other.
13
帝以故宅起湘宮寺，費極奢侈。 以孝武莊嚴剎七層，帝欲起十層，不可立，分爲兩剎，各五層。 新安太守巢尚之罷郡還，見帝，曰：「卿至湘宮寺未？ 我起此寺，是大功德。 」愿在側曰：「陛下起此寺，皆是百姓賣兒貼婦錢，佛若有知，當悲哭哀愍，罪高佛圖，有何功德？ 」尚書令袁粲在坐，爲之失色。 帝乃怒，使人驅下殿，愿徐去無異容。 以舊恩，少日中，已復召入。
The emperor built the Xiang Palace Temple on the site of his former home, at ruinous expense. Emperor Xiaowu's Zhuangyan Monastery had seven stories; the emperor wanted ten, but the structure would not bear it, so he built two monasteries of five stories each. Chao Shangzhi of Xin'an, having left office and returned to court, asked the emperor, "Have you been to the Xiang Palace Temple yet?" "I built this temple. It is a great act of merit." " Yuan, standing at his side, said, "Your Majesty, this temple was paid for with money wrung from people who sold their children and pawned their wives. If the Buddha could see it, he would weep in pity. The sin rises higher than the pagoda—where is the merit in that?" " Yuan Can, Director of the Imperial Secretariat, who was present, turned pale. The emperor flew into a rage and had him driven from the hall. Yuan withdrew slowly, his face unchanged. Thanks to their old bond, before noon he had been summoned back.
14
帝好圍碁，甚拙，去格七八道，物議共欺爲第三品。 與第一品王抗圍碁，依品賭戲，抗每饒借之，曰：「皇帝飛碁，臣抗不能斷。 」帝終不覺，以爲信然，好之愈篤。 愿又曰：「堯以此教丹朱，非人主所宜好也。 」雖數忤旨，而蒙賞賜，猶異餘人。 遷兼中書郎。
The emperor loved weiqi but played very badly, often leaving seven or eight lines of the board empty. Court opinion humored him into believing he ranked third class. He played weiqi with Wang Kang, ranked first class, wagering by rank. Kang always let him win, saying, "The emperor's stones fly like lightning; this servant cannot cut them off." " The emperor never caught on, took it all as truth, and grew even fonder of the game. Yuan also said, "Yao used this game to teach his son Danzhu. It is not a pastime a ruler should indulge in." " Though he often spoke against the emperor's wishes, he was still rewarded more generously than others. He was promoted to concurrent Attendant of the Central Secretariat.
15
帝寢疾，愿常侍醫藥。 帝素能食，尤好逐夷，以銀缽盛蜜漬之，一食數缽。 謂揚州刺史王景文曰：「此是奇味，卿頗足不？ 」景文曰：「臣夙好此物，貧素致之甚難。 」帝甚悅。 食逐夷積多，胷腹痞脹，氣將絕。 左右啓飲數升酢酒，乃消。 疾大困，一食汁滓猶至三升，水患積久，藥不復效。 大漸日，正坐，呼道人，合掌便絕。 愿以侍疾久，轉正員郎。
When the emperor fell gravely ill, Yuan stayed at his side to tend his medical care. The emperor had always been a hearty eater and was especially fond of zhuyi, a preserved fish delicacy kept in silver bowls steeped in honey; he could eat several bowls in one sitting. He asked Wang Jingwen, Inspector of Yangzhou, "This is a rare delicacy. Do you ever get enough of it?" " Jingwen replied, "I have always loved it, but in my humble circumstances it is very hard to come by." " The emperor was delighted. He ate so much zhuyi that his chest and belly swelled with blockage, and he was near death. His attendants had him drink several sheng of vinegar wine, and the swelling eased. As the illness worsened, he still took as much as three sheng of broth and sediment at a meal. Edema had built up over time, and medicine no longer helped. On the day he was failing fast he sat upright, summoned a Daoist priest, joined his palms, and died. Because Yuan had long attended him in illness, he was promoted to regular Attendant.
16
出爲晉平太守，在郡不治生產。 前政與民交關，質錄其兒婦，愿遣人於道奪取將還。 在郡立學堂教授。 郡舊出髯蛇膽，可爲藥，有餉愿蛇者，愿不忍殺，放二十里外山中，一夜蛇還牀下。 復送四十里外山，經宿，復還故處。 愿更令遠，乃不復歸，論者以爲仁心所致也。 海邊有越王石，常隱雲霧。 相傳云：「清廉太守乃得見。 」愿往觀視，清徹無隱蔽。 後琅邪王秀之爲郡，與朝士書曰：「此郡承虞公之後，善政猶存，遺風易遵，差得無事。 」以母老解職，除後軍將軍。 褚淵常詣愿，不在，見其眠牀上積塵埃，有書數袠。 淵歎曰：「虞君之清，一至於此。 」令人掃地拂牀而去。 遷中書郎，領東觀祭酒。 兄季，爲上虞令，卒。 愿從省步還家，不待詔便歸東。 除驍騎將軍，遷廷尉，祭酒如故。 愿嘗事宋明帝，齊初宋神主遷汝陰廟，愿拜辭流涕。 建元元年，卒。 年五十四。 愿著《五經論問》，撰《會稽記》，文翰數十篇。
He was sent out as Grand Administrator of Jinping and made no effort to enrich himself in office. His predecessor had taken a commoner's daughter-in-law as security for a debt. Yuan sent men to intercept her on the road and bring her back. In the commandery he founded a school and taught there. The commandery was known for bearded-snake gall used in medicine. When someone brought Yuan a live snake, he could not bear to kill it and released it twenty li into the hills—but overnight the snake was back under his bed. He released it again forty li away, but after another night it returned to the same spot. Yuan had it taken even farther off, and then it did not return. Commentators said this showed the power of his compassionate nature. On the coast stood a stone called the Yue King's Stone, usually shrouded in mist. Tradition held that only an incorrupt magistrate could see it clearly. " When Yuan went to look, the stone stood clear, with nothing hidden. Later, when Wang Xiuzhi of Langye took the post, he wrote to colleagues at court, "This commandery still bears the mark of Lord Yu's good rule. His legacy is easy to follow, and affairs are largely untroubled." " When his mother grew old he resigned and was appointed General of the Rear Army. Chu Yuan often visited Yuan. Once when Yuan was out, he saw dust thick on the bed and several bundles of books lying there. Chu sighed and said, "Lord Yu's integrity goes even this far." " He had someone sweep the floor and dust the bed, then left. He was promoted to Attendant of the Central Secretariat and concurrently Libationer of the Eastern Pavilion. His elder brother Ji, magistrate of Shangyu, died. Yuan left the Secretariat on foot for home, not waiting for imperial permission before returning east. He was appointed General of Valiant Cavalry, then promoted to Minister of Justice, while keeping his post as Libationer. Yuan had long served Emperor Ming of Song. When the Qi dynasty began and the Song imperial spirit tablets were moved to the temple at Ruyin, he bowed in farewell with tears streaming down his face. He died in the first year of Jianyuan. He was fifty-four years old. Yuan wrote Discussions and Questions on the Five Classics, compiled Records of Kuaiji, and left several dozen other literary works.
17
劉懷慰字彥泰，平原平原人也。 祖奉伯，元嘉中，爲冠軍長史。 父乘民，冀州刺史。 懷慰初爲桂陽王征北板行參軍。 乘民死於義嘉事難，懷慰持喪，不食醯醬，冬月不絮衣。 養孤弟妹，事寡叔母，皆有恩義。
Liu Huaiwei, courtesy name Yantai, was a native of Pingyuan in Pingyuan commandery. His grandfather Fengbo served during the Yuanjia era as chief clerk to the Champion General. His father Chengmin was Inspector of Jizhou. Huaiwei began as acting adjutant on the northern campaign staff of the Prince of Guiyang. Chengmin died in the troubles of the Yijia era. Huaiwei mourned him without eating pickled foods and, even in winter, wore no padded clothes. He raised his orphaned younger siblings and cared for his widowed aunt by marriage, showing kindness and duty in both.
18
復除邵陵王南中郎參軍，廣德令，尚書駕部郎。 懷慰宗從善明等，太祖心腹，懷慰亦豫焉。 沈攸之有舊，令爲書戒喻攸之，太祖省之稱善。 除步兵校尉。
He was again appointed adjutant of the Southern Central Army under the Prince of Shaoling, magistrate of Guangde, and Director of the Chariot Section. Huaiwei's kinsmen Shanming and others were trusted intimates of the Founding Emperor, and Huaiwei was counted among them as well. He had old ties with Shen Youzhi and was told to write a letter warning him. The Founding Emperor read it and praised it highly. He was appointed Commandant of Footsoldiers.
19
齊國建，上欲置齊郡於京邑，議者以江右土沃，流民所歸，乃治瓜步，以懷慰爲輔國將軍、齊郡太守。 上謂懷慰曰：「齊邦是王業所基，吾方以爲顯任。 經理之事，一以委卿。 」又手勑曰：「有文事者，必有武備。 今賜卿玉環刀一口。 」懷慰至郡，修治城郭，安集居民，墾廢田二百頃，決沈湖灌溉。 不受禮謁，民有餉其新米一斛者，懷慰出所食麥飯示之，曰：「旦食有餘，幸不煩此。 」因著《廉吏論》以達其意。 太祖聞之，手勑褒賞。 進督秦、沛二郡。 妻子在都，賜米三百斛。 兖州刺史柳世隆與懷慰書曰：「膠東淵化，潁川致美，以今方古，曾何足云。 」在郡二年，遷正員郎，領青冀二州中正。
When the Qi state was founded, the ruler wanted to place Qi commandery in the capital region. Advisers argued that the lands south of the Yangzi were fertile and drew displaced people, so the seat was established at Guabu, and Huaiwei was made General Who Supports the State and Grand Administrator of Qi commandery. The ruler said to Huaiwei, "The Qi domain is the foundation of our royal enterprise. I mean this as a post of real consequence." I entrust all matters of governance entirely to you. " He also wrote in his own hand, "Where there are civil affairs, there must be military preparedness as well." I now bestow on you a jade-ringed saber." " When Huaiwei took office he repaired the walls, settled the people, reclaimed two hundred qing of abandoned farmland, and opened the Shen Lake for irrigation. He accepted no gifts. When a commoner brought him a hu of new rice, Huaiwei showed him the barley meal he was eating and said, "I already have enough for breakfast—please do not trouble yourself." " He then wrote On Incorrupt Officials to make his views known. When the Founding Emperor heard of it, he wrote in his own hand to praise and reward him. He was promoted to oversee Qin and Pei commanderies as well. Because his wife and children remained in the capital, he was granted three hundred hu of grain. Liu Shilong, Inspector of Yanzhou, wrote to Huaiwei, "Jiaodong's deep civilizing influence and Yingchuan's celebrated excellence—set against your work today, what can the ancients even claim?" " After two years in office he was promoted to regular Attendant and made head of the Rectification Bureau for Qing and Ji provinces.
20
懷慰本名聞慰，世祖卽位，以與舅氏名同，勑改之。 出監東陽郡，爲吏民所安。 還兼安陸王北中郎司馬。 永明九年，卒。 年四十五。 明帝卽位，謂僕射徐孝嗣曰：「劉懷慰若在，朝廷不憂無清吏也。 」懷慰與濟陽江淹、陳郡袁彖善，亦著文翰。 永明初，獻《皇德論》云。
Huaiwei had originally been named Wenwei. When Emperor Wu came to the throne, the name matched that of his maternal uncle, so an edict changed it. He was sent out to supervise Dongyang commandery, where officials and commoners alike found security under his rule. On returning to court he concurrently served as marshal of the Northern Central Army under the Prince of Anlu. He died in the ninth year of Yongming. He was forty-five years old. When Emperor Ming came to the throne, he told Xu Xiaosi, Director of the Secretariat, "If Liu Huaiwei were still alive, the court would never lack incorrupt officials." " Huaiwei was close to Jiang Yan of Jiyang and Yuan Can of Chen commandery, and also left literary works. At the beginning of Yongming he presented his treatise On Imperial Virtue.
21
裴昭明，河東聞喜人，宋太中大夫松之孫也。 父駰，南中郎參軍。
Pei Zhaoming was a native of Wenxi in Hedong and grandson of Pei Songzhi, Grand Master of Palace Counsel under the Song. His father Yin served as adjutant of the Southern Central Army.
22
昭明少傳儒史之業。 泰始中，爲太學博士。 有司奏：「太子婚，納徵用玉璧虎皮，未詳何所准據。 」昭明議：「禮納徵，儷皮爲庭實，鹿皮也。 晉太子納妃注『以虎皮二』。 太元中，公主納徵，虎豹皮各一。 豈其謂婚禮不詳。 王公之差，故取虎豹文蔚以尊其事。 虎豹雖文，而徵禮所不言，熊羆雖古，而婚禮所不及，珪璋雖美，或爲用各異。 今宜准的經誥，凡諸僻謬，一皆詳正。 」於是有司參議，加珪璋，豹熊羆皮各二。
From youth Zhaoming inherited the family tradition of classical learning and historical scholarship. During the Taishi era he served as an Erudite of the Imperial University. The relevant offices reported, "For the crown prince's wedding, betrothal gifts include jade disks and tiger skins, but the authority for this is unclear." " Zhaoming argued, "According to ritual, betrothal gifts use paired hides as court offerings—and those hides should be deerskin." A Jin commentary on the crown prince taking a consort reads, "two tiger skins." In the Taiyuan era, when a princess received betrothal gifts, one tiger skin and one leopard skin were used. Surely this does not mean that marriage rites are left unspecified. For princes and dukes the distinction lies in using tiger and leopard skins, splendid in pattern, to honor the occasion. Tiger and leopard skins may be splendid, but betrothal ritual does not mention them; bear skins may be ancient precedents, but marriage rites do not include them; jade tablets may be beautiful, yet their uses differ. We should now take the classics and canonical edicts as our standard and examine and correct every irregular practice. " The relevant offices then deliberated and added jade tablets, plus two each of leopard, bear, and brown-bear skins.
23
元徽中，出爲長沙郡丞，罷任，刺史王蘊謂之曰：「卿清貧，必無還資。 湘中人士有須一禮之命者，我不愛也。 」昭明曰：「下官忝爲邦佐，不能光益上府，豈以鴻都之事仰累清風。 」歷祠部通直郎。
During Yuanhui he served as assistant administrator of Changsha. When he left office, Inspector Wang Yun told him, "You are incorrupt and poor—you surely have no wealth to take home." If any gentleman in the Xiang region needs a small gift from my office, I will not begrudge it." " Zhaoming replied, "I am unworthy to serve as your assistant and cannot bring credit to your prefecture. How could I burden your integrity with such favors?" " He later served as Attendant-in-Ordinary of the Sacrificial Affairs Section.
24
永明三年，使虜，世祖謂之曰：「以卿有將命之才，使還，當以一郡相賞。 」還爲始安內史。 郡民龔玄宣，云神人與其玉印玉板書，不須筆，吹紙便成字。 自稱「龔聖人」，以此惑衆。 前後郡守敬事之，昭明付獄治罪。 及還，甚貧罄。 世祖曰：「裴昭明罷郡還，遂無宅。 我不諳書，不知古人中誰比？ 」遷射聲校尉。 九年，復遣北使。
In the third year of Yongming he was sent as envoy to the northern court. Emperor Wu told him, "You have the talent to carry out a diplomatic mission. When you return, I shall reward you with a commandery." " On his return he was appointed Interior Secretary of Shi'an. A commandery resident named Gong Xuanyuan claimed that a spirit had given him jade seals and writing tablets; without a brush, he could blow on paper and characters would appear. He called himself "the Sage Gong" and used this to mislead the people. Previous administrators had honored him, but Zhaoming had him imprisoned and punished. When he returned to court he was utterly destitute. Emperor Wu remarked, "Pei Zhaoming left office and came back without even owning a house." I am no scholar—among the ancients, who does he resemble?" " He was promoted to Commandant of the Archers. In the ninth year he was again sent as envoy to the northern court.
25
建武初，爲王玄邈安北長史、廣陵太守。 明帝以其在事無所啓奏，代還，責之。 昭明曰：「臣不欲競執關楗故耳。 」昭明歷郡皆有勤績，常謂人曰：「人生何事須聚蓄，一身之外，亦復何須？ 子孫若不才，我聚彼散； 若能自立，則不如一經。 」故終身不治產業。 中興二年，卒。
At the beginning of Jianwu he served as chief clerk of the Northern Pacification under Wang Xuanmiao and as Grand Administrator of Guangling. Emperor Ming recalled and replaced him because he had filed no memorials while in office, and rebuked him. Zhaoming replied, "I simply did not wish to wrangle over who held the keys to power." " In every post Zhaoming achieved solid results. He often said, "What need has life of hoarded wealth? Beyond one's own person, what more is required?" If my descendants lack talent, what I gather they will squander; if they can stand on their own, one classic text is worth more than any fortune." " For that reason he never built up an estate in his lifetime. He died in the second year of Zhongxing.
26
從祖弟顗，字彥齊。 少有異操。 泰始中，於總明觀聽講，不讓劉秉席，秉用爲參軍。 昇明末，爲奉朝請。 齊臺建，世子裴妃須外戚譜，顗不與，遂分籍。 太祖受禪，上表誹謗，掛冠去，伏誅。
His paternal cousin Yan, courtesy name Yanqi. From youth he showed an unusual character. During Taishi, at lectures in the Zongming Observatory, he refused to yield his seat to Liu Bing, who then took him on as adjutant. Near the end of the Shengming era he served as a Court Gentleman in Attendance. When the Qi regime was established, the crown prince's Pei consort needed a genealogy of maternal kin. Yan refused to supply it, and the families split their registers. When the Founding Emperor accepted the abdication, Yan submitted a slanderous memorial, resigned his office, and was executed.
27
沈憲字彥璋，吳興武康人也。 祖說道，巴西梓潼二郡太守，父璞之，北中郎行參軍。
Shen Xian, courtesy name Yanzhang, was a native of Wukang in Wuxing commandery. His grandfather Shuodao served as Grand Administrator of Baxi and Zitong commanderies; his father Puzhi was acting adjutant of the Northern Central Army.
28
憲初應州辟，爲主簿。 少有幹局，歷臨首、餘杭令，巴陵王府佐，帶襄令，除駕部郎。 宋明帝與憲棋，謂憲曰：「卿，廣州刺史才也。 」補烏程令，甚著政績。 太守褚淵歎之曰：「此人方員可施。 」除通直郎，都水使者。 長於吏事，居官有績。 除正員郎，補吳令，尚書左丞。
Xian first answered a provincial summons and became chief clerk. From youth he showed administrative talent. He served as magistrate of Linshou and Yuhang, as staff officer to the Prince of Baling while concurrently magistrate of Xiang, and was appointed Director of the Chariot Section. Emperor Ming of Song played weiqi with Xian and told him, "You have the makings of a Governor of Guangzhou." " He was appointed magistrate of Wucheng and won a strong reputation for his achievements in office. Grand Administrator Chu Yuan sighed and said, "This man adapts to any shape required—he can be put to use anywhere." " He was appointed Attendant-in-Ordinary and Commissioner of Waterworks. He excelled in administrative work and achieved solid results in every post. He was appointed regular Attendant, then magistrate of Wu, and Left Vice Director of the Secretariat.
29
昇明二年，西中郎將晃爲豫州，太祖擢憲爲晃長史，南梁太守，行州事。 遷豫章王諮議，未拜，坐事免官。 復除安成王冠軍、武陵王征虜參軍，遷少府卿。 少府管掌市易，與民交關，有吏能者，皆更此職。 遷王儉鎮軍長史。
In the second year of Shengming, when General Huang of the Western Central Army held Yuzhou, the Founding Emperor made Xian his chief clerk, Grand Administrator of Nanliang, and acting head of provincial affairs. He was transferred to consultative officer under the Prince of Yuzhang, but before taking office he was dismissed for an offense. He was again appointed adjutant under the Prince of Ancheng and the Prince of Wuling, then promoted to Minister of the Palace Supplies. The Ministry of Palace Supplies oversaw market trade and dealings with the people, and officials known for administrative talent were routinely assigned there. He was transferred to serve as chief clerk of the Pacification Army under Wang Jian.
30
武陵王曄爲會稽，以憲爲左軍司馬。 太祖以山陰戶衆難治，欲分爲兩縣。 世祖啓曰：「縣豈不可治，但用不得其人耳。 」乃以憲帶山陰令，政聲大著。 孔稚珪請假東歸，謂人曰：「沈令料事特有天才。 」加寧朔將軍。 王敬則爲會稽，憲仍留爲鎮軍長史，令如故。
When the Prince of Wuling Ye governed Kuaiji, Xian was appointed marshal of the Left Army. Because Shanyin's population was large and hard to govern, the Founding Emperor wanted to split it into two counties. Emperor Wu replied, "A county can be governed—the problem is simply putting the wrong man in charge." " Xian was then made concurrent magistrate of Shanyin, and his reputation for good governance spread far and wide. Kong Zhigui took leave to return east and told people, "Magistrate Shen has a rare genius for judging affairs." " He was given the additional title General Who Pacifies the North. When Wang Jingze took over Kuaiji, Xian remained chief clerk of the Pacification Army while keeping his magistracy.
31
遷爲冠軍長史，行南豫州事，晉安王後軍長史、廣陵太守。 西陽王子明代爲南兖州，憲仍留爲冠軍長史，太守如故，頻行州府事。 永明八年，子明典籤劉道濟取府州五十人役自給，又役子明左右，及船仗贓私百萬，爲有司所奏，世祖怒，賜道濟死。 憲坐不糾，免官。 尋復爲長史、輔國將軍，以疾去官。 除散騎常侍，未拜，卒。 當世稱爲良吏。
He was transferred to chief clerk of the Champion General with acting authority over Southern Yuzhou, then served as chief clerk of the Rear Army under the Prince of Jin'an and as Grand Administrator of Guangling. When the Prince of Xiyang Ziming took Southern Yanzhou, Xian remained chief clerk of the Champion General and kept his post as grand administrator, often handling provincial and prefectural affairs. In the eighth year of Yongming, Ziming's registrar Liu Daoji impressed fifty men from the prefecture and commandery for his own use, also conscripted the prince's attendants, and embezzled a million in ships and arms. When the relevant offices reported this, Emperor Wu was furious and had Daoji put to death. Xian was dismissed for failing to investigate the matter. Soon he was reappointed chief clerk and General Who Supports the State, but then resigned because of illness. He was appointed Regular Attendant of the Scattered Cavalry but died before he could take up the post. His contemporaries hailed him as an excellent official.
32
憲同郡丘仲起，先是爲晉平郡，清廉自立。 褚淵歎曰：「見可欲心能不亂，此楊公所以遺子孫也。 」仲起字子震，少爲憲從伯領軍寅之所知。 宋元徽中，爲太子領軍長史，官至廷尉。 卒。
Qiu Zhongqi, a fellow townsman of Xian, had earlier governed Jinping commandery with incorrupt integrity. Chu Yuan sighed and said, "To see temptation yet keep one's heart steady—that is the lesson Lord Yang left his descendants." " Zhongqi, courtesy name Zizhen, was known from youth to Xian's father's cousin, Commander-in-Chief Yin. During the Song Yuanhui era he served as chief clerk of the crown prince's Pacification Army and rose to Minister of Justice. He died.
33
李珪之字孔璋，江夏鍾武人也。 父祖皆爲縣令。
Li Guizhi, courtesy name Kongzhang, was a native of Zhongwu in Jiangxia commandery. His father and grandfather had both served as county magistrates.
34
珪之少辟州從事。 宋泰始初，蔡興宗爲郢州，以珪之爲安西府佐，委以職事，清治見知。 遷鎮西中郎諮議，右軍將軍，兼都水使者。 珪之歷職稱爲清能，除游擊將軍，兼使者如故。 轉兼少府，卒。
In youth Guizhi was summoned to serve as a provincial aide. At the beginning of the Song Taishi era, when Cai Xingzong governed Yingzhou, he made Guizhi a staff officer of the Western Pacification headquarters, entrusted him with important duties, and his clean administration won wide notice. He was promoted to consultative officer of the Western Central Army, General of the Right Army, and concurrently Commissioner of Waterworks. In successive posts Guizhi won praise for integrity and ability. He was appointed General of the Mobile Guard while keeping his concurrent post as commissioner. He was transferred to concurrent Minister of the Palace Supplies and soon died.
35
先是，四年，滎陽毛惠素爲少府卿，吏才強而治事清刻。 勑市銅官碧青一千二百斤供御畫，用錢六十萬。 有讒惠素納利者，世祖怒，勑尚書評賈，貴二十八萬餘，有司奏之，伏誅。 死後家徒四壁，上甚悔恨。
Earlier, in the fourth year, Mao Huisu of Xingyang served as Minister of the Palace Supplies. He was a strong administrator but stern and exacting in his methods. An edict ordered twelve hundred jin of azure pigment from the copper office for imperial painting, at a cost of six hundred thousand cash. Someone accused Huisu of taking a kickback. Emperor Wu was furious and ordered the Secretariat to reassess the price, which came to more than two hundred eighty thousand. The relevant offices reported this, and Huisu was executed. After his death his home was left bare of possessions, and the emperor deeply regretted what had been done.
36
孔琇之，會稽山陰人也。 祖季恭，光祿大夫，父靈運，著作郎。
Kong Xiuzhi was a native of Shanyin in Kuaiji commandery. His grandfather Jigong was Grand Master of Splendid Happiness; his father Lingyun served as Gentleman of the Palace Library.
37
琇之初爲國子生，舉孝廉。 除衞軍行參軍，員外郎，尚書三公郎。 出爲烏程令，有吏能。 還遷通直郎，補吳令。 有小兒年十歲，偷刈隣家稻一束，琇之付獄治罪，或諫之，琇之曰：「十歲便能爲盜，長大何所不爲？ 」縣中皆震肅。
Xiuzhi first entered the Imperial University and was recommended as Filial and Incorrupt. He was appointed acting adjutant of the Guard Army, outside-the-capital attendant, and director of the Three Dukes Section. He was sent out as magistrate of Wucheng, where he showed real administrative talent. On returning to court he was promoted to Attendant-in-Ordinary and appointed magistrate of Wu. A ten-year-old boy stole a sheaf of rice from a neighbor's field. Xiuzhi had him imprisoned. When some urged leniency, Xiuzhi said, "If he steals at ten, what will he not do when he grows up?" " The whole county was awed into discipline.
38
遷尚書左丞，又以職事知名。 轉前軍將軍，兼少府。 遷驍騎將軍，少府如故。 出爲寧朔將軍、高宗冠軍征虜長史、江夏內史。 還爲正員常侍，兼左民尚書、廷尉卿。 出爲臨海太守，在任清約，罷郡還，獻乾薑二十斤，世祖嫌少，及知琇之清，乃歎息。 除武陵王前軍長史，未拜，仍出爲輔國將軍，監吳興郡，尋拜太守，治稱清嚴。
He was promoted to Left Vice Director of the Secretariat and again won fame for his work in office. He was transferred to General of the Forward Army and concurrently Minister of the Palace Supplies. He was promoted to General of Valiant Cavalry while keeping his post at the Ministry of Palace Supplies. He was sent out as General Who Pacifies the North, chief clerk of the Barbarian-quelling Champion General under the High Ancestor, and Interior Secretary of Jiangxia. On returning he was appointed regular Attendant-in-Ordinary and concurrently Director of the Left People Section and Minister of Justice. He served as Grand Administrator of Linhai, governing with austerity. When he left office he presented only twenty jin of dried ginger as a gift. Emperor Wu thought it too little, but when he learned how incorrupt Xiuzhi was, he sighed in admiration. He was appointed chief clerk of the Forward Army under the Prince of Wuling but never took up the post. He was then sent out as General Who Supports the State to supervise Wuxing commandery and soon became grand administrator, winning praise for pure and stern governance.
39
史臣曰：琴瑟不調，必解而更張也。 魏晉爲吏，稍與漢乖，苛猛之風雖衰，而仁愛之情亦減。 局以峻法，限以常條，以必世之仁未及宣理，而朞月之望已求治術。 先公後私，在己未易，割民奉國，於物非難，期之救過，所利苟免。 且目見可欲，嗜好方流，貪以敗官，取與違義，吏之不臧，罔非由此。 擿姦辯偽，誠俟異識，垂名著績，唯有廉平。 今世之治民，未有出於此也。
The historian writes: When the lute and zither are out of tune, they must be restrung. From Wei and Jin onward, official service gradually diverged from Han practice. The harsh and severe style faded, but humane feeling diminished as well. Officials were hemmed in by harsh laws and routine statutes. The benevolence that should last generations had not yet been put into practice, yet within a single term in office they were already expected to show results. Putting the public good before private interest was hard for oneself, but cutting the people to serve the state was easy enough toward others. What was expected was merely to remedy faults, and the profit sought was only to escape blame. Moreover, when the eye sees temptation, desire takes hold. Greed ruins office; taking and giving violate right principle. There is scarcely an instance of official corruption that does not spring from this. Exposing crime and distinguishing falsehood truly require exceptional insight, but to leave a lasting name and manifest achievement, only integrity and fairness will do. In governing the people today, nothing goes beyond this principle.
40
贊曰：蒸蒸小民，吏職長親。 棼亂須理，卹隱歸仁。 枉直交瞀，寬猛代陳。 伊何導物，貴在清身。 [1]
The encomium says: The teeming common people look to officials as elders and kin. Tangled disorder must be set right; comforting the suffering leads back to benevolence. Right and wrong mingle in confusion; lenience and severity take turns in office. What guides the people? The precious thing is integrity in the official himself. [1] Endnote marker.
41
