南齊書卷第五十六列傳第三十七
Book of Southern Qi, Volume 56, Biography 37
2
紀僧真劉係宗茹法亮呂文顯呂文度
Ji Sengzhen; Liu Zongzong; Ru Faliang; Lü Wenxian; and Lü Wendu
3
有天象，必有人事焉。 倖臣一星，列于帝座。 經禮立教，亦著近臣之服。 親倖之義，其來已久。 爰自衰周，侯伯專命，桓、文霸主，至于戰國，寵用近習，不乏於時矣。 漢文幸鄧通，雖錢徧天下，位止郎中。 孝武韓嫣、霍去病，遂至侍中大司馬。 迄于魏、晉，世任權重，才位稍爽，而信倖唯均。
When signs appear in the heavens, corresponding events unfold among men. The star of favored officials stands ranked beside the imperial seat. The classical rites that ground moral instruction likewise set down garb for those who serve close at hand. The institution of intimate favor at court reaches deep into antiquity. From late Zhou, when regional lords held unchecked authority, through Duke Huan and Duke Wen as hegemons and on into the Warring States, rulers who pampered men at their side were never absent from the age. Emperor Wen of Han favored Deng Tong; though his coin filled the realm, his office rose no higher than Palace Gentleman. Under Emperor Wu, Han Yan and Huo Qubing rose to Attendant-in-Ordinary and Grand Commander-in-Chief. Through Wei and Jin the times gave them heavy authority; talent and rank grew slightly mismatched, yet reliance on favorites alone held steady.
4
中書之職，舊掌機務。 漢元以令僕用事，魏明以監令專權，及在中朝，猶爲重寄。 陳准歸任上司，荀勗恨於失職。 晉令舍人位居九品，江左置通事郎，管司詔誥。 其後郎還爲侍郎，而舍人亦稱通事。 元帝用琅邪劉超，以謹慎居職。 宋文世，秋當、周糾竝出寒門。 孝武以來，士庶雜選，如東海鮑照，以才學知名。 又用魯郡巢尚之，江夏王義恭以爲非選。 帝遣尚書二十餘牒，宣敕論辯，義恭乃歎曰：「人主誠知人。 」及明帝世，胡母顥、阮佃夫之徒，專爲佞倖矣。
The Secretariat had long held charge of confidential state affairs. Emperor Yuan of Han had the Director and Deputy Director run affairs; Emperor Ming of Wei had the Supervisor and Director monopolize authority; even at the central court they remained a weighty trust. Chen Huai yielded his post to his superiors; Xun Xu nursed resentment at losing his office. In Jin the Secretariat clerk held ninth-rank standing; east of the Yangtze they set up the Communicator Gentleman to oversee edicts and proclamations. Later the Gentleman post reverted to Deputy Director, while the clerk was also styled Communicator. Emperor Yuan of Jin used Liu Chao of Langye, who served with scrupulous care. In the reign of Emperor Wen of Song, Qiu Dang and Zhou Jiu both came from humble households. From Emperor Xiaowu onward, noble and common birth were mixed in selection; such as Bao Zhao of Donghai, famed for literary talent. They also employed Chao Shangzhi of Lu commandery; the Prince of Jiangxia, Liu Yigong, deemed this an improper choice. The emperor sent more than twenty Secretariat documents to proclaim edicts and argue the point; Yigong sighed and said, "The ruler truly knows men." In the reign of Emperor Ming, Hu Mugao, Ruan Tianfu, and their like were flatterers and favorites through and through.
5
齊初亦用久勞，及以親信。 關讞表啓，發署詔敕。 頗涉辭翰者，亦爲詔文，侍郎之局，復見侵矣。 建武世，詔命殆不關中書，專出舍人。 省內舍人四人，所直四省，其下有主書令史，舊用武官，宋改文吏，人數無員。 莫非左右要密，天下文簿板籍，入副其省，萬機嚴祕，有如尚書外司。 領武官，有制局監，領器仗兵役，亦用寒人被恩幸者。 今立《倖臣篇》，以繼前史之末云。
At the founding of Qi they likewise used men of long service and personal intimates. They controlled memorials and reports and issued and countersigned edicts and commands. Those with some skill in letters also drafted edicts; the Deputy Director's domain was encroached upon once more. In the Jianwu era, edicts and commands scarcely touched the Secretariat and issued solely from the clerks. Inside the Secretariat were four clerks, each in charge of one of the four bureaus; below them were chief clerks and registrars; formerly military officers were used, but the Song changed them to civil clerks, with no fixed quota. All were indispensable intimates at the ruler's side; the realm's documents and registers were copied into their offices, and state secrets were guarded as closely as in an outer arm of the Secretariat. For military affairs there was the Bureau of Arms Supervisor, commanding weapons and troops; they too used men of humble birth who had won imperial favor. This "Favored Officials" chapter is now set down to continue where earlier histories leave off.
6
紀僧真，丹陽建康人也。 僧真少隨逐征西將軍蕭思話及子惠開，皆被賞遇。 惠開性苛，僧真以微過見罰，旣而委任如舊。 及罷益州還都，不得志，僧真事之愈謹。 惠開臨終歎曰：「紀僧真方當富貴，我不見也。 」乃以僧真託劉秉、周顒。 初，惠開在益州，土反，被圍危急，有道人謂之曰：「城圍尋解。 檀越貴門後方大興，無憂外賊也。 」惠開密謂僧真曰：「我子弟見在者，竝無異才。 政是道成耳。 」僧真憶其言，乃請事太祖。 隨從在淮陰，以閑書題，令荅遠近書疏。 自寒官歷至太祖冠軍府參軍、主簿。 僧真夢蒿艾生滿江，驚而白之。 太祖曰：「詩人採蕭，蕭卽艾也。 蕭生斷流，卿勿廣言。 」其見親如此。
Ji Sengzhen was a native of Jianye in Danyang commandery. In youth Sengzhen followed the western campaign general Xiao Sihua and his son Huikai, and at every turn was shown favor. Huikai was harsh by nature; Sengzhen was punished for a minor offense, yet afterward was entrusted as before. When Huikai left Yizhou and returned to the capital, frustrated in his ambitions, Sengzhen served him with still greater devotion. At the end of his life Huikai sighed: "Ji Sengzhen is about to grow rich and noble—I shall not live to see it." With that he entrusted Sengzhen to Liu Bing and Zhou Yong. Earlier, when Huikai was in Yizhou, the native tribes rebelled and he was besieged in dire peril; a Daoist told him, "The siege will soon be lifted." "Patron, your noble house will flourish hereafter—have no fear of outside enemies." Huikai secretly told Sengzhen, "My sons and younger brothers now living all lack unusual talent." "None but Daocheng." Sengzhen remembered his words and thereupon asked to enter Emperor Gao's service. While following the army at Huaiyin, because he was skilled at informal brushwork he was ordered to answer letters from near and far. From a humble post he rose through service to staff officer and chief clerk in Emperor Gao's Champion-general's office. Sengzhen dreamed mugwort and artemisia filled the river; alarmed, he reported the dream. Emperor Gao said, "The poets gather mugwort—'mugwort' is also ai." "'Ai grows across the broken stream'—do not speak broadly of this." Such was how close he was to him.
7
元徽初，從太祖頓新亭，拒桂陽賊。 蕭惠朗突入東門，僧真與左右共拒戰。 賊退，太祖命僧真領親兵，遊邏城中。 事寧，除南臺御史、太祖領軍功曹。 上將廢立，謀之袁粲、褚淵，僧真啓上曰：「今朝廷猖狂，人不自保，天下之望，不在袁、褚。 明公豈得默己，坐受夷滅。 存亡之機，仰希熟慮。 」太祖納之。
At the start of the Yuanhui era he followed Emperor Gao to camp at Xinting and resist the Guiyang rebels. Xiao Huilang burst through the eastern gate; Sengzhen and the guards fought them off together. When the rebels retreated, Emperor Gao ordered Sengzhen to lead the personal guard on patrol within the walls. When the crisis passed he was appointed Censor of the Southern Bureau and merit officer on Emperor Gao's General-in-Chief staff. When Emperor Gao was about to depose and enthrone, consulting Yuan Can and Chu Yuan, Sengzhen memorialized: "Today the court is reckless and men cannot protect themselves; the hope of the realm does not rest on Yuan and Chu." "How can you, my lord, keep silent and sit by while you are destroyed root and branch?" "The moment of survival or extinction—I beg you to ponder it deeply." Emperor Gao accepted the advice.
8
太祖欲度廣陵起兵，僧真又啓曰：「主上雖復狂釁，虐加萬民，而累世皇基，猶固盤石。 今百口北度，何必得俱。 縱得廣陵城，天子居深宮施號令，目明公爲逆，何以避此？ 如其不勝，則應北走胡中，竊謂此非萬全策也。 」上曰：「卿顧家，豈能逐我行耶。 」僧真頓首稱無貳。 昇明元年，除員外郎，帶東武城令。 尋除給事中、邵陵王參軍。
Emperor Gao planned to cross to Guangling and raise troops; Sengzhen again urged: "Though the sovereign is still reckless and cruel to the myriad people, generations of imperial foundation remain firm as bedrock." "Now a hundred mouths cross north—must all go together?" "Even if you take Guangling city, the Son of Heaven sits deep in the palace issuing commands and marks you as rebel—how would you escape that?" "If you do not prevail, you must flee north among the barbarians—I venture this is not a plan without flaw." Emperor Gao said, "You think of your family—could you really follow me in going?" Sengzhen kowtowed and declared he would not be of two minds. In the first year of Shengming he was appointed Outer Gentleman with concurrent magistrate of Dongwucheng. Soon he was appointed Giver of Counsel and staff officer to the Prince of Shaoling.
9
太祖坐東府高樓，望石頭城，僧真在側。 上曰：「諸將勸我誅袁、劉，我意不欲便爾。 」及沈攸之事起，從太祖入朝堂。 石頭反夜，太祖遣衆軍掩討。 宮城中望石頭火光及呌聲甚盛，人懷不測。 僧真謂衆曰：「呌聲不絕，是必官軍所攻。 火光起者，賊不容自燒其城，此必官軍勝也。 」尋而啓石頭平。 上出頓新亭，使僧真領千人在帳內。 初，上在領軍府，令僧真學上手迹下名，至是報荅書疏，皆付僧真，上觀之，笑曰：「我亦不復能別也。 」初，上在淮陰治城，得一錫趺，大數尺，下有篆文，莫能識者。 僧真曰：「何須辨此文字，此自久遠之物，九錫之徵也。 」太祖曰：「卿勿妄言。 」及上將拜齊公，已剋日，有楊袓之謀於臨軒作難。 僧真更請上選吉辰，尋而祖之事覺。 上曰：「無卿言，亦當致小狼狽，此亦何異呼遝之冰。 」轉齊國中書舍人。
Emperor Gao sat in the high tower of the Eastern Residence looking toward Stone Fortress city, with Sengzhen at his side. Emperor Gao said, "The generals urge me to kill Yuan and Liu; I am not yet willing to do so immediately." When Shen Youzhi rebelled he followed Emperor Gao into the audience hall. On the night Stone Fortress rose in revolt, Emperor Gao sent the armies to take it by surprise. Inside the palace they saw fire and cries from Stone Fortress very loud; everyone was filled with uncertainty. Sengzhen told the crowd, "The cries do not cease—that must be the government troops attacking." "Where fire rises, the rebels would not burn their own city—the government troops must have won." Soon word came that Stone Fortress had been pacified. Emperor Gao went out to camp at Xinting and ordered Sengzhen to command a thousand men inside the tents. Earlier, at the General-in-Chief's office Emperor Gao had Sengzhen learn his hand in signing at the bottom; now replies to letters were all entrusted to Sengzhen; the Prince looked and laughed, "I could not tell the difference myself anymore." Earlier, while building the city at Huaiyin, he found a bronze pedestal several feet across with seal script below that no one could read. Sengzhen said, "Why bother to decipher these characters? This is a thing of great antiquity—a sign of the Nine Bestowals." Emperor Gao said, "Do not speak rashly." When Emperor Gao was about to receive title as Duke of Qi, the day was already set, and Yang Zuzhi plotted to make trouble at the audience. Sengzhen again asked Emperor Gao to choose an auspicious day; soon Zuzhi's plot was exposed. Emperor Gao said, "Without your words we would still have met a small embarrassment—how is this different from Huzi Guang's warning about the ice?" He was moved to Secretariat Clerk of the State of Qi.
10
宋世道人楊法持，與太祖有舊。 元徽末，宣傳密謀。 昇明中，以爲僧正。 建元初，罷道，爲寧朔將軍，封州陵縣男，三百戶。 二年，虜圍朐山，遣法持爲軍主，領支軍救援。 永明四年，坐役使將客，奪其鮭稟，削封。 卒。
In Song times the Daoist Yang Fachi had long-standing ties with Emperor Gao. At the end of Yuanhui he circulated word of secret plots. During Shengming he was made chief of the Buddhist clergy. At the opening of Jianyuan he left the priesthood, became General Pacifying the North, and was enfeoffed as Baron of Zhouling with three hundred households. In the second year, when the barbarians besieged Qushan, he sent Fachi as army commander leading a detached force to relieve it. In the fourth year of Yongming, for employing and robbing guests of their salmon provisions, he lost his enfeoffment. He passed away.
11
劉係宗，丹陽人也。 少便書畫，爲宋竟陵王誕子景粹侍書。 誕舉兵廣陵，城內皆死，敕沈慶之赦係宗，以爲東宮侍書。 泰始中，爲主書。 以寒官累遷至勳品。 元徽初，爲奉朝請，兼中書通事舍人，員外郎。 封始興南亭侯，食邑三百七十戶。 帶秣陵令。
Liu Zongzong was a man of Danyang. As a youth he was skilled at calligraphy and painting and served as attendant scribe to Jingchun, son of the Prince of Jingling, Liu Dan. When Dan raised troops at Guangling and everyone in the city was executed, an edict had Shen Qingzhi pardon Zongzong and name him attendant scribe to the Eastern Palace. In the Taishi era he served as chief clerk. Rising from humble office step by step, he reached merit-grade rank. At the opening of Yuanhui he was a Court Gentleman, serving concurrently as Communicator Clerk of the Secretariat and as Outer Gentleman. He was enfeoffed as Marquis of Nanting in Shixing with a fief of three hundred seventy households. He held concurrent office as magistrate of Moling.
12
太祖廢蒼梧，明旦，呼正直舍人虞整，醉不能起，係宗歡喜奉命。 太祖曰：「今天地重開，是卿盡力之日。 」使寫諸處分敕令，及四方書疏。 使主書十人書吏二十人配之，事皆稱旨。 除羽林監，轉步兵校尉。 仍除龍驤將軍，出爲海鹽令。 太祖卽位，除龍驤將軍、建康令。 永明元年，除寧朔將軍，令如故。 尋轉右軍將軍、淮陵太守，兼中書通事舍人。 母喪自解，起爲寧朔將軍，復本職。
When Emperor Gao deposed Emperor Cangwu, at dawn he summoned the upright-duty clerk Yu Zheng, who was drunk and could not rise; Zongzong gladly took the command. Emperor Gao said, "Heaven and earth are newly opened—this is the day for you to exert yourself to the utmost." He had him draft all orders and edicts of assignment and letters to the four directions. He gave him ten chief clerks and twenty clerks to assist; everything met the emperor's intent. He was appointed Supervisor of the Feathered Forest, then transferred to Commandant of Footsoldiers. He was next made General of Dragon Cavalry and sent out as magistrate of Haiyan. When Emperor Gao took the throne, he was appointed General of Dragon Cavalry and magistrate of Jiankang. In the first year of Yongming he was made General Pacifying the North, with his duties unchanged. Soon he was transferred to General of the Right Army and administrator of Huailing, while retaining his post as Communicator Clerk of the Secretariat. On his mother's death he resigned; when mourning ended he was restored as General Pacifying the North and resumed his former duties.
13
四年，白賊唐宇之起，宿衞兵東討，遣係宗隨軍慰勞，徧至遭賊郡縣。 百姓被驅逼者，悉無所問，還復民伍。 係宗還，上曰：「此段有征無戰，以時平蕩，百姓安怗，甚快也。 」賜係宗錢帛。 上欲脩治白下城，難於動役。 係宗啓謫役東民丁隨宇之爲逆者，上從之。 後車駕講武，上履行白下城，曰：「劉係宗爲國家得此一城。」
In the fourth year the White Bandit Tang Yuzhi rose; the palace guard marched east to suppress him; Zongzong was sent with the army to comfort and reward troops, reaching every commandery and county that had suffered the rebels. Commoners who had been driven into service were all left unpunished and restored to civilian rolls. When Zongzong returned, the emperor said, "This campaign had expeditions without battles; it was pacified in good time and the people are settled—very satisfying." The emperor bestowed money and silks on Zongzong. The emperor wanted to repair the White Fortress walls but found it hard to mobilize labor. Zongzong memorialized to conscript eastern commoner laborers who had followed Yuzhi in rebellion; the emperor approved. Later when the emperor reviewed troops and walked the White Fortress walls, he said, "Liu Zongzong won this city for the state."
14
永明中，虜使書常令係宗題答，祕書書局皆隷之。 再爲少府，遷游擊將軍、魯郡太守。 鬱林卽位，除驍騎將軍，仍除寧朔將軍、宣城太守。 係宗久在朝省，閑於職事。 明帝曰：「學士不堪治國，唯大讀書耳。 一劉係宗足持如此輩五百人。 」其重吏事如此。 建武二年，卒官，年七十七。
In Yongming, barbarian envoys' letters were regularly entrusted to Zongzong for inscription and reply; the Secretariat script offices were all placed under him. He again headed the Privy Purse, then became General Who Patrols and administrator of Lu. When Emperor Deposed of Lin ascended, Zongzong was made General of Valiant Cavalry, then again General Pacifying the North and administrator of Xuancheng. Zongzong had long served in the central offices and knew administrative work well. Emperor Ming said, "Scholars cannot govern the state—they only read many books." A single Liu Zongzong is enough to manage five hundred such men. Such was the weight he placed on practical administration. In the second year of Jianwu he died in office at the age of seventy-seven.
15
茹法亮，吳興武康人也。 宋大明世，出身爲小史，歷齋幹扶。 孝武末年，作酒法，鞭罰過度，校獵江右，選白衣左右百八十人，皆面首富室，從至南州，得鞭者過半。 法亮憂懼，因緣啓出家得爲道人。 明帝初，罷道，結事阮佃夫，用爲兖州刺史孟次陽典籤。 累至太祖冠軍府行參軍。 元徽初，除殿中將軍，爲晉熙王郢州典籤，除長兼殿中御史。
Ru Faliang was a native of Wukang in Wuxing commandery. Under Song in the Daming era he started as a petty clerk and worked through orderly and attendant posts. At the end of Emperor Xiaowu's reign he made wine regulations with excessive flogging; on hunts in the lands south of the Yangtze he selected one hundred eighty white-robed attendants, all from wealthy households; going south with him, more than half received the lash. Faliang was fearful and anxious and seized the occasion to request leaving home and becoming a Daoist priest. At the start of Emperor Ming's reign he left the priesthood and attached himself to Ruan Tianfu, who used him as chief clerk to Meng Ciyang, inspector of Yanzhou. He climbed step by step to acting staff officer in Emperor Gao's Champion-general's office. At the start of Yuanhui he was appointed General of the Palace and made chief clerk to the Prince of Jinxi in Yingzhou; he was also appointed chief concurrent Censor of the Palace.
16
巴東王子響於荊州殺僚佐，上遣軍西上，使法亮宣旨慰勞，安撫子響。 法亮至江津，子響呼法亮，法亮疑畏不肯往。 又求見傳詔，法亮又不遣。 故子響怒，遣兵破尹略軍。 事平，法亮至江陵，刑賞處分，皆稱敕斷決。 軍還，上悔誅子響，法亮被責。 少時，親任如舊。
When the Prince of Badong, Zixiang, killed his staff in Jingzhou, the court sent armies west and ordered Faliang to proclaim the imperial will and reassure Zixiang. Faliang reached Jiangjin; Zixiang summoned him, but Faliang, suspicious and afraid, would not go. Zixiang again asked to see the edict-bearer; Faliang again refused to send him. So Zixiang was enraged and sent troops to defeat Yin Lue's army. When affairs were settled Faliang reached Jiangling; punishments and rewards were all decided in the emperor's name. After the army returned the emperor regretted killing Zixiang, and Faliang was blamed. Before long he enjoyed the same trust as before.
17
鬱林卽位，除步兵校尉。 延興元年，爲前軍將軍。 延昌殿爲世祖陰室，藏諸御服。 二少帝竝居西殿，高宗卽位住東齋，開陰室出世祖白紗帽防身刀，法亮歔欷流涕。 除游擊將軍。 高武舊人鮮有存者，法亮以主署文事，故不見疑，位任如故。 永泰元年，王敬則事平，法亮復受敕宣慰。 出法亮爲大司農，中書勢利之職，法亮不樂去，固辭不受，旣而代人已致，法亮垂涕而出。 年六十四，卒官。
When Emperor Deposed of Lin took power he was named Commandant of Footsoldiers. In Yanxing's first year he became General of the Vanguard. Yanchang Hall was Emperor Wu's private chamber, where all imperial vestments were stored. The two young emperors both dwelt in the western hall; when Emperor Ming took the throne he lived in the eastern quarters; opening the private chamber they brought out Emperor Wu's white gauze cap and body-guard knife, and Faliang sobbed with tears. He was named General Who Patrols. Few of Emperor Gao's old intimates survived; because Faliang managed documentary affairs he was not suspected, and his rank and duties remained as before. In Yongtai's first year, once Wang Jingze's revolt was crushed, Faliang again received orders to proclaim comfort. Faliang was sent out as Grand Minister of Agriculture; the Secretariat was a post of power and profit, and Faliang was unwilling to leave; he firmly declined, yet before long his replacement had arrived, and Faliang left in tears. He died in office at the age of sixty-four.
18
文顯治事以刻覈被知。 三年，帶南清河太守。 與茹法亮等迭出入爲舍人，竝見親倖。 四方餉遺，歲各數百萬，竝造大宅，聚山開池。 五年，爲建康令，轉長水校尉，歷帶南泰山、南譙太守，尋爲司徒中兵參軍，淮南太守，直舍人省。 累遷左中郎將，南東莞太守，右軍將軍。 高宗輔政，以文顯守少府，見任使。 歷建武、永元之世，尚書右丞，少府卿。 卒。
Lü Wenxian was known for handling affairs with harsh scrutiny. In his third year in office he also served as administrator of Southern Qinghe. He and Ru Faliang and others took turns serving as clerks, all receiving intimate favor. Gifts from the four directions came to each in millions every year; all built great mansions, heaped hills, and opened pools. In the fifth year he was magistrate of Jiankang, transferred to Commandant of the Long River, and successively held concurrent posts as administrator of Southern Taishan and Southern Qiao, then staff officer for military affairs to the Minister of State, administrator of Huainan, and duty clerk in the Secretariat. He rose through Left Commandant of the Center, administrator of Southern Dongguan, and General of the Right Army. While Emperor Ming held the regency, Wenxian was placed over the Privy Purse and given real responsibility. Through Jianwu and Yongyuan he was Right Director of the Secretariat and Minister of the Privy Purse. He passed away.
19
呂文度，會稽人。 宋世爲細作金銀庫吏，竹局匠。 元徽中，爲射雉典事，隨監莫脩宗上郢。 世祖鎮盆城拒沈攸之，文度仍留伏事，知軍隊雜役，以此見親。 從還都，爲石頭城監，仍度東宮。 世祖卽位，爲制局監，位至員外郎，帶南濮陽太守。 殿內軍隊及發遣外鎮人，悉關之，甚有要勢。 故世傳越州嘗缺，上覔一直事人往越州，文度啓其所知費延宗合旨，上卽以爲刺史。 永明中，敕親近不得輙有申薦，人士免官，寒人鞭一百。
Lü Wendu came from Kuaiji. During Song he was clerk in the fine crafts gold and silver treasury and a bamboo-works artisan. In Yuanhui he was officer in charge of pheasant shooting and followed Supervisor Mo Xiuzong up to Ying. When Emperor Wu garrisoned Pencheng to resist Shen Youzhi, Wendu remained to serve in secret, knowing military miscellaneous duties, and for this won favor. After returning to the capital he was supervisor of Stone Fortress city and also passed through the Eastern Palace. When Emperor Wu took the throne he became Bureau of Arms Supervisor, rising to Outer Gentleman with concurrent administrator of Southern Puyang. Palace troops and men dispatched to outer garrisons all passed through him, and he held very great influence. Hence it was said that Yuezhou was once vacant; the emperor sought a straight-duty man to go to Yuezhou; Wendu memorialized that Fei Yanzong, whom he knew, suited intent, and the emperor immediately made him inspector. In Yongming the emperor ordered that intimates must not casually make recommendations; gentry who lost office were flogged one hundred strokes, and commoners likewise.
20
上性尊嚴，呂文顯嘗在殿側咳聲高，上使茹法亮訓詰之，以爲不敬，故左右畏威承意，非所隷莫敢有言也。 時茹法亮掌雜驅使簿，及宣通密敕； 呂文顯掌穀帛事； 其餘舍人無別任。 虎賁中郎將潘敞掌監功作。 上使造禪靈寺新成，車駕臨視，甚悅。 敞喜，要呂文顯私登寺南門樓，上知之，繫敞上方，而出文顯爲南譙郡，久之乃復。
The emperor was severe; when Lü Wenxian once coughed loudly beside the throne, the emperor had Ru Faliang rebuke him for disrespect, so those around him feared his authority, followed his wishes, and none outside their branch dared speak up. Ru Faliang then held the miscellaneous service registers and relayed secret edicts; while Lü Wenxian managed grain and cloth affairs; the other clerks had no separate duties of their own. Pan Chang of the Tiger Guard Commandant of the Center oversaw construction projects. The emperor had the new Chanling Temple built; the imperial carriage visited and was very pleased. Chang in delight invited Lü Wenxian privately to climb the temple's southern gate tower; the emperor learned of it, imprisoned Chang in the palace storehouse, and sent Wenxian out as administrator of Southern Qiao; only after a long time was he restored.
21
濟陽江瞿曇、吳興沈徽孚等，以士流舍人通事而已，無權利。 徽孚粗有筆札。 建武中文詔，多其辭也。 官至黃門郎。
Men such as Jiang Qutan of Jiyang and Shen Huifu of Wuxing were gentry clerks for communication only, without power or profit. Huifu had modest skill with the brush. In Jianwu, literary edicts often bore his wording. He rose to Gentleman at the Yellow Gate.
22
史臣曰：中世已來，宰御天下，萬機碎密，不關外司。 尚書八座五曹，各有恒任，係以九卿六府，事存副職。 咸皆冠冕搢紳，任疏人貴，伏奏之務旣寢，趨走之勞亦息。 關宣所寄，屬當有歸，通驛內外，切自音旨。 若夫環纓斂笏，俯仰晨昏，贍幄座而竦躬，陪蘭檻而高眄，探求恩色，習睹威顏，遷蘭變鮑，久而彌信，因城社之固，執開壅之機。 長主君世，振裘持領，賞罰事殷，能不踰漏，宮省咳唾，義必先知。 故能窺盈縮於望景，獲驪珠於龍睡。 坐歸聲勢，卧震都鄙。 賄賂日積，苞苴歲通，富擬公侯，威行州郡。 制局小司，專典兵力，雲陛天居，亙設蘭錡，羽林精卒，重屯廣衞。 至于元戎啓轍，式候還麾，遮迾清道，神行案轡，督察來往，馳騖輦轂，驅役分部，親承几案，領護所攝，示總成規。 若徵兵動衆，大興民役，行留之儀，請託在手，斷割牢稟，賣弄文符，捕叛追亡，長戍遠謪，軍有千齡之壽，室無百年之鬼，害政傷民，於此爲蠹。 況乎主幼時昏，其爲讒慝，亦何可勝紀也！
The historian says: From the middle ages onward, those who held the realm governed minute and numerous affairs that did not pass through outer offices. The Secretariat's eight seats and five bureaus each had standing duties, together with the nine ministers and six offices, where deputy posts carried the work. All were crowned gentry and silk-clad nobles; their duties were remote and their persons exalted; memorials to the throne had already ceased, and the labor of running about had also ended. Whatever was entrusted for proclamation had to land somewhere; couriers inside and outside the palace carried the ruler's own voice. As for those with ring and tassel who gathered their tablets, bowing morning and evening, attending the imperial seat and straightening their bodies, standing by the orchid balustrade and looking down from on high, probing for signs of favor, accustomed to the august countenance—stored with orchid long enough one smells of abalone, and over time trust deepens; relying on the solidity of wall and altar, they hold the mechanism that opens and closes the barrier. When the ruler was long on the throne, they shook the fur robe and held the collar; rewards and punishments were numerous, yet nothing leaked past them—a cough or spit in the palace, they were sure to know first. Therefore they could read expansion and contraction from a glance at the sun's shadow and seize the black pearl while the dragon slept. Seated, they gathered sonic power; reclining, they shook the capital and its outskirts. Bribes piled day by day, gifts passed year by year; their wealth rivaled marquises and dukes, and their authority ran through provinces and commanderies. The Bureau of Arms was a petty office that held military strength; cloud stairs and heaven's dwelling were lined with orchid halberds; elite Feathered Forest troops heavily garrisoned the broad guard. When the grand army set out and returned with banners, screening the clear road, reins on the writing table at divine speed, they inspected comings and goings, galloped at the chariot hub, drove and assigned sections, personally received the writing desk, led what they oversaw, and showed the overall rule. When troops were levied and masses mobilized, with great labor conscripted, rules of movement and stay—all entreaties were in their hands; they carved prison provisions and trafficked in documents; capturing rebels and pursuing fugitives, long garrison and distant exile—armies had men who lived a thousand years, households without ghosts of a hundred years: here government was harmed and the people injured. All the more when the throne was young and the age benighted—how could their slander and villainy even be counted!
23
贊曰：恩澤而侯，親倖爲舊。 便煩左右，旣貴且富。 [1]
The encomium says: Favored with grace and enfeoffed as lords, they were intimate favorites of old. They took on troubles at the ruler's side and became both exalted and rich. See note 1.
