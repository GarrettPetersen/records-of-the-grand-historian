三載考績之法，昉自唐、虞。 清沿明制，而品式略殊。 京官曰京察，外官曰大計，吏部考功司掌之。 京察以子卯午酉歲，部院司員由長官考覈，校以四格，懸「才、守、政、年」為鵠。 分稱職、勤職、供職三等。 列一等者，加級記名，則加考引見備外用。 糾以六法，不謹、罷輭者革職，浮躁、才力不及者降調，年老、有疾者休致，註考送部。 自翰、詹、科、道外，依次過堂。 三品京堂由部開列事實，四、五品由王、大臣分別等第，具奏引見取上裁。 大計以寅已申亥歲，先期籓、臬、道、府遞察其屬賢否，申之督、撫，督、撫覈其事狀，註考繕冊送部覆覈。 才守俱優者，舉以卓異。 劣者，劾以六法。 不入舉劾者為平等。 卓異官自知縣而上，皆引見候旨。 六法處分如京察，貪酷者特參。
The practice of evaluating officials every three years began in the age of Yao and Shun. The Qing adopted the Ming framework, though the ranks and procedures differed in detail. Capital officials underwent the capital inspection; provincial officials underwent the grand assessment. Both were managed by the Merit Evaluation Department of the Ministry of Personnel. The capital inspection was conducted in the years of the Rat, Rabbit, Horse, and Rooster. Department and bureau staff were rated by their superiors against four standards—talent, integrity, governance, and age. Ratings were divided into three grades: competent, diligent, and dutiful service. Those placed in the first grade received a step in rank and were registered by name; after further review and imperial audience, they were held ready for appointment outside the capital. Misconduct was judged under the six penalty categories: the careless and ineffectual were dismissed; the impetuous and those lacking ability were demoted or transferred; the elderly and infirm were retired; and evaluation records were forwarded to the ministry. Except for the Hanlin Academy, the Imperial Academy, the Censorate, and circuit intendants, officials were examined in turn before the evaluation hall. Third-rank capital officials were evaluated on factual reports submitted by their ministries. Fourth- and fifth-rank officials were graded by princes and grand ministers, presented in memorial for imperial audience, and left to the emperor's final judgment. The grand assessment was conducted in the years of the Tiger, Snake, Monkey, and Boar. In advance, provincial commissioners, circuit intendants, prefects, and magistrates successively reviewed their subordinates and reported upward to the governor-general and governor, who verified the facts, recorded evaluations, compiled registers, and sent them to the ministry for final review. Those distinguished in both talent and integrity were recommended as outstanding performers. The deficient were impeached under the six penalty categories. Those neither commended nor impeached were rated as ordinary. Outstanding officials from the rank of magistrate upward were all presented for imperial audience to await the throne's decision. Penalties under the six categories followed the same rules as the capital inspection, while the greedy and cruel were specially impeached.
5
凡京察一等、大計卓異有定額，京官七而一，筆帖式八而一，道、府、廳、州、縣十五而一，佐雜、教官百三十而一，以是為率。 非歷俸滿者，未及年限者，革職留任或錢糧未完者，滿官不射布靶、不諳清語者，均不得膺上考。 其大較也。 順治八年，京察始著為令，以六年為期。 十三年，吏部奏定則例，三品以上自陳，四品等官吏部、都察院察考議奏，親定去留。 筆帖式照有職官例一體考察。 遇京察時，各官暫停升轉。 尋復定考滿議敘例，三年考滿與六年察典並行。 十七年，從左都御史魏裔介請，行糾拾之法，以補甄別所未及。 唐熙元年罷京察，專用三年考滿例。 三品以上仍自陳。 餘官分五等：一等稱職者紀錄，二等稱職者賞賚，平常者留任，不及者降調，不稱職者革職。 三年，御史季振宜請停考滿三疏，極言徇情鑽營，章奏繁擾，無裨勸懲。 因停考滿自陳例。 六年，復行京察。 明年，甄別不及官三十七員。 嗣以各部、院甄別司員，類多末職，二十三年，嚴諭指名題參，復甄汰王三省等三十六人。 明年，京察又停。 雍正元年復舉行，改為三年，自是為定制。
Quotas were fixed for first-grade capital inspection and for outstanding grand-assessment ratings: one in seven capital officials, one in eight clerks, one in fifteen circuit, prefectural, department, subprefectural, and county officials, and one in one hundred thirty assistant and educational officers—ratios that served as the general rule. Those who had not completed a full term of service, those not yet eligible by seniority, those dismissed but retained in post or with tax accounts still unsettled, and Manchu officials who could not pass the archery test or did not know Manchu were all barred from top ratings. Such was the general framework. In the eighth year of Shunzhi (1651), the capital inspection was first codified, with a six-year cycle. In the thirteenth year (1656), the Ministry of Personnel submitted fixed regulations: officials of third rank and above offered self-criticism; fourth rank and below were examined by the Ministry of Personnel and the Censorate, deliberated in memorial, and the emperor personally decided who would stay or go. Clerks were examined under the same rules as regular officials with substantive posts. During the capital inspection, promotions and transfers were suspended for all officials. Regulations on triennial completion and merit deliberation were soon restored, so triennial completion reviews ran alongside the six-year inspection cycle. In the seventeenth year (1660), at the request of Left Censor-in-Chief Wei Yijie, a supplementary impeachment procedure was introduced to catch cases formal screening had missed. In the first year of the Kangxi reign, the capital inspection was abolished and only the triennial completion rule was used. Officials of third rank and above still submitted self-criticism. Other officials were divided into five grades: first-grade competent officers were recorded for merit; second-grade competent officers received rewards; ordinary performers remained in post; those falling short were demoted or transferred; and the incompetent were dismissed. In the third year, Censor Ji Zhenyi submitted three memorials urging an end to triennial completion, arguing forcefully that favoritism and careerist scheming dominated the process, that paperwork was excessive and disruptive, and that the system did nothing to reward merit or punish failure. The self-criticism procedure tied to triennial completion was therefore abolished. In the sixth year, the capital inspection was restored. The following year, thirty-seven officials who failed screening were removed from office. Thereafter, when departments and bureaus screened their clerks, they mostly targeted junior posts. In the twenty-third year, a stern edict required officials to be named and impeached by name, and Wang Sansheng and thirty-five others were screened out again. The following year, the capital inspection was suspended again. It was restored in the first year of Yongzheng (1723), the cycle was shortened to three years, and this thereafter became the permanent rule.
6
初，京察一等無定額，康熙三年，御史張沖翼疏請以部、院員數之多寡定一、二等名數，以息奔競，從之。 乾隆間，部、院保送一等，或浮濫溢舊額，詔停兼部行走，仍歸本衙門另班聲敘，暨到任未滿半年，仍由原衙門註考等例。 又罷未授職庶吉士保列一等之例，以示限制。 四十二年，命部、院保送一等人數，毋庸過泥上屆成例，遞行裁減，以防溢額。 應將上兩次數目比較，酌中定制。 既無慮濫膺保薦，亦不至屈抑人才。 五十年，定例保送一等人數，以不溢四十八年原額為準。 後世踵行，間有增損，無甚懸殊也。 向例部、院司官由吏部、都察院考覈，雍正四年，命內閣大學士同閱。 乾隆九年，帝慮部、院堂官有瞻徇情面濫列一等者，敕大學士驗看，慎重甄別，不稱一等者裁去。 十一年諭曰：「前命大學士分別去留，亦權宜辦理之道。 察覈司員，惟堂官最為親切。 要在平日留心體察，臨時舉措公平。 如上次定一等者，三年中行走平常，當改為二、三等。 上次原列二、三等者，三年中知所奮勉，即改為一等。 庶察典肅而人知勸懲。 」厥後考察權責，悉屬吏部，驗看特奉行故事而已。
Initially there was no fixed quota for first-grade capital inspection. In the third year of Kangxi (1664), Censor Zhang Chongyi proposed setting first- and second-grade numbers according to each department's and bureau's staff size, in order to curb frantic competition for ratings; the court approved his proposal. During the Qianlong reign, departments and bureaus sometimes recommended so many first-grade candidates that quotas were exceeded. An edict ended concurrent service in another ministry, required officials to return to their home office for separate review, and ruled that those who had served less than half a year were still evaluated by their original office. The practice of recommending unassigned Hanlin bachelors for first grade was also abolished as a further limit. In the forty-second year (1777), departments and bureaus were told not to treat the previous cycle's numbers as a rigid precedent and to reduce first-grade recommendations progressively to prevent quota inflation. The numbers from the last two cycles were to be compared and a balanced fixed quota adopted. This would guard against indiscriminate recommendations without unjustly suppressing able men. In the fiftieth year (1785), the number of first-grade recommendations was fixed by rule, with the forty-eighth year's original quota as the ceiling. Later reigns followed the same practice, with occasional adjustments but no major departure. By former practice, department and bureau clerks were examined by the Ministry of Personnel and the Censorate. In the fourth year of Yongzheng (1726), Grand Secretaries were ordered to review them jointly. In the ninth year of Qianlong (1744), fearing that department and bureau heads were showing favoritism and listing unworthy men as first grade, the emperor ordered grand ministers to inspect the lists, screen candidates carefully, and remove those unfit for first grade. In the eleventh year (1746) an edict stated: "The earlier order for grand ministers to decide who should stay or go was only an expedient measure. In examining clerks, no one is closer to the facts than the department head. What matters is attentive observation in daily work and fair decisions when evaluations are due. If someone rated first grade last time performed only ordinarily over the next three years, he should be moved to the second or third grade. If someone previously rated second or third grade showed real improvement over three years, he should be promoted to first grade. Only then will the inspection system be rigorous and officials understand what reward and punishment mean." Thereafter examination authority rested entirely with the Ministry of Personnel, while review by grand ministers became a mere formality observed by precedent.
7
大臣循例自陳求斥罷，候旨照舊供職，國初以來行之。 乾隆八年，曾諭大臣自陳罷斥者舉賢自代。 嗣以所舉不得其人，或樹黨營私，行不久即罷。 十七年，帝以「內、外大臣親自簡擢，隨時黜陟，奚待三年？ 自陳繁文，相率為偽，甚無謂也」。 詔罷其例。
Grand ministers routinely submitted self-criticism asking to be dismissed, then awaited the imperial reply and continued in office as before—a practice followed since the founding of the dynasty. In the eighth year of Qianlong (1743), ministers who submitted self-criticism asking to be dismissed were once ordered to recommend worthy successors. The practice was soon abandoned because recommended successors were often unsuitable or were promoted to build factions for private gain. In the seventeenth year (1752) the emperor said: "Inner and outer grand ministers personally select and promote officials and dismiss or advance them at any time—why wait three years? These self-criticism memorials are empty formalities; everyone follows the same pretense. The whole thing is pointless." An edict abolished the practice.

先是京堂官無甄敘例，乾隆十五年，帝以三品以上堂官，具本自陳，部、院司員，皆令引見，而四、五品京堂不在自陳之列，亦無引見之例，吏部、都察院考語無實，龍鍾庸劣者得姑容，才具優長者無由見。 特派王、大臣分別等第，奏聞引見。 十八年，敕吏部開列三品京堂事實，親為裁奪。 四十八年，以三品京堂不便派大臣驗看，令吏部帶額引見。 嘉慶十二年，以三、四品京堂，向來京察但有降黜無甄敘，既與內、外大臣辦理兩歧，並不得與部、院司員同邀加級。 於是予太常少卿色克精額等議敘，而予陳鍾琛等休致。 自後三品以下京堂始有甄敘之例矣。
Capital officials had previously had no rule for merit deliberation. In the fifteenth year of Qianlong (1750), the emperor observed that third-rank capital officials and above submitted formal self-criticism and that department and bureau clerks were presented for audience, while fourth- and fifth-rank capital officials were excluded from both procedures. Ministry and Censorate evaluations were hollow: doddering mediocrities were tolerated, while able men had no way to be recognized. Princes and grand ministers were specially assigned to grade them separately and present the results in memorial for imperial audience. In the eighteenth year (1753), the Ministry of Personnel was ordered to submit factual reports on third-rank capital officials for the emperor to decide personally. In the forty-eighth year (1783), because it was awkward to send grand ministers to inspect third-rank capital officials, the Ministry of Personnel was ordered to present them for audience within the fixed quota. In the twelfth year of Jiaqing (1807), because third- and fourth-rank capital officials in the capital inspection could only be demoted and never receive merit deliberation—unlike inner and outer grand ministers in procedure and unlike department and bureau clerks in rank advancement— the court granted merit deliberation to Vice Minister of Rites Sekesengge and others, while Chen Zhongchen and others were retired. From then on, capital officials below third rank finally had a rule for merit deliberation.

年老休致，例有明文。 乾隆二十二年，定部、院屬官五十五歲以上，堂官詳加甄別。 三十三年，改定京察二、三等留任各官，六十五歲以上引見。 嘉慶三年，命京察二、三等官引見，以年逾七十為限。 尋復舊例。 六法處分綦嚴，長官往往博寬大之名，每屆京察，祗黜退數人，虛應故事，餘概優容，而被劾者又不免屈抑。 雍正中，汪景祺、查嗣庭輩論列時政，以部員壅滯為言，有「十年不調、白首為郎」等語。 帝責以怨望誹謗，而事實不得謂誣。 蓋部員冗濫，康、雍時已然矣。
Retirement on grounds of age was clearly regulated. In the twenty-second year of Qianlong (1757), department and bureau subordinates aged fifty-five and above were to be carefully screened by their department heads. In the thirty-third year (1768), officials retained in post after second- or third-grade capital inspection who were sixty-five or older were required to be presented for audience. In the third year of Jiaqing (1798), second- and third-grade capital inspection officials were to be presented for audience only if over seventy. The former rule was soon restored. Penalties under the six categories were strict on paper, yet superiors often cultivated a reputation for leniency. Each capital inspection dismissed only a handful of officials in perfunctory compliance, indulged nearly everyone else, and still allowed those impeached to be unfairly suppressed. During the Yongzheng reign, Wang Jingqi, Zha Siting, and others discussed current affairs and complained of congestion in the ministries, using phrases such as "ten years without transfer, white-haired yet still a gentleman-attendant." The emperor condemned them for resentful slander, yet the facts could hardly be called false. Ministry staffs were already bloated in the Kangxi and Yongzheng periods.
10
乾隆三年，鴻臚少卿查斯海疏言：「京官被劾，不無以嫌隙入吏議者。 京察六法官，應援大計例送部引見。 」從之。 乾隆末，士夫習為諂諛，堂官拔識司員，率以逢迎巧捷為曉事，察典懈弛。 仁宗初，銳意求治，頗思以崇實黜華，獎勵氣節，風示天下。 嘉慶五年，詔部、院堂官慎重選舉，猷守兼優者膺首薦，餘寧取資格較久、謹愿樸實之員，其少年浮薄、才華髮越者，應令深其經練，下屆保列。 尚書、侍郎各備冊密識賢否，公議同覽。 十一年，大學士、尚書等議奏京察事宜：「捐納人員，限以年資，軍機處司員能兼部務者，方列上考，不許濫保充數。 」報可。
In the third year of Qianlong (1738), Vice Minister of Rites Zha Sihai memorialized: "When capital officials are impeached, personal grudges sometimes shape the personnel review. Officials penalized under the six capital-inspection categories should follow the grand-assessment precedent and be sent to the ministry for audience. " The request was approved. By the end of the Qianlong reign, officials had grown accustomed to flattery. Department heads mostly favored clerks who were ingratiating and quick-witted, treating such traits as practical competence, and the inspection system grew lax. Early in the Jiaqing reign, the emperor was determined to improve governance and sought to elevate substance over display, reward moral integrity, and set an example for the empire. In the fifth year of Jiaqing (1800), department and bureau heads were ordered to evaluate carefully: those outstanding in both policy and integrity should receive first recommendation; otherwise prefer officers of longer seniority who were cautious and solid; youthful flighty men and those of flashy talent should gain more experience and wait for the next cycle. Ministers and vice ministers were each to keep private registers noting who was capable or not, to be reviewed jointly in consultation. In the eleventh year (1806), grand ministers and ministers memorialized on capital inspection: "Purchased-office holders were to be limited by seniority; Grand Council clerks could receive top ratings only if they could handle ministry work; indiscriminate recommendations to fill quotas were forbidden. " The memorial was approved.
11
道光四年，候際清贖罪舞弊一案，刑部司員恩德等朋謀撞騙堂官，以謬登薦牘，保列一等，下部議處。 諭嗣後京察有冒濫徇私者連坐。 七年，給事中吳傑奏：「大計、軍政，皆有舉有劾。 近年六部辦理京察，除保舉一等外，不問賢否，概列二等。 間有三等數人，仍予留任。 六法不施，有勸無懲。 應申明舊章，舉劾並用。 」帝韙其言，降諭飭行。 十五年，令於京察外隨時糾參，以為補救。 咸豐十年，刑部堂官濫保不諳例案之員，朝廷務循寬大，輒以相習成風，不獨刑部為然，多為原宥。 僅予大學士桂良等鐫級留任，出考堂官罰俸而已。 穆宗即位，大難未平，厲精澄敘。 同治五年，詔部、院堂官謹遵嘉慶五年備冊密識賢否、公議同覽之諭，並常川進署，與司員講求公事，藉覘其屬賢否。 八年，又諭京察不得有舉無劾，冀湔滌舊習，一新庶政。 然積重之勢，不能復返。 光緒七年，禮部侍郎寶廷疏陳京察積弊，言之痛切，謂：「瞻徇情面之弊，不專在部、院堂官，當責樞臣考察，必公必嚴。 樞臣果精白乃心，破除情面，不特能考察部、院司員之賢否，並能考察內、外大臣之賢否。 而考察樞臣功過，在聖明獨斷。 若朝廷先以京察為故事具文，何責乎樞臣，更何責乎部、院堂官！ 」論雖切中而難實行，徒託空言而已。 宣統二年，吏部設立憲政籌備處，改考功司為考績科，主文職功過應行變通事宜。 其時浮議紛紜，新舊雜糅，吏部等於贅疣矣。
In the fourth year of Daoguang (1824), in the Hou Jiqing ransom-fraud case, Ministry of Justice clerks Ende and others conspired to deceive their department head and were falsely listed as first grade on recommendation documents; the case was sent down for disciplinary deliberation. An edict ruled that thereafter anyone who abused capital inspection quotas or showed favoritism would be punished together with subordinates. In the seventh year (1827), Supervising Secretary Wu Jie memorialized: "The grand assessment and military evaluations both combine commendation and impeachment. In recent years the six ministries, apart from first-grade recommendations, listed nearly everyone second grade regardless of merit. A few third-grade ratings appeared occasionally, yet even those officers were usually retained. The six penalty categories were not enforced; there was reward without punishment. The old regulations should be reaffirmed so commendation and impeachment are used together. " The emperor approved and issued an edict ordering enforcement. In the fifteenth year (1835), officials were allowed to impeach wrongdoers at any time outside the capital inspection cycle as a corrective measure. In the tenth year of Xianfeng (1860), Ministry of Justice heads indiscriminately recommended officials ignorant of case precedents. The court preferred leniency, and the practice spread beyond the Ministry of Justice; most offenders were pardoned. Only Grand Secretary Gui Liang and others received a one-step demotion while remaining in post; the department heads who conducted the evaluation were merely fined. When the Tongzhi Emperor took the throne, with great calamity still unresolved, he strove to clarify personnel evaluation. In the fifth year of Tongzhi (1866), department and bureau heads were ordered to follow the Jiaqing fifth-year edict on private registers and joint review, to attend office regularly, discuss official business with clerks, and thereby observe their subordinates' merit. In the eighth year (1869), the court again ordered that capital inspection must include impeachment as well as commendation, hoping to wash away old habits and renew governance. Yet the weight of long-standing habit could not be reversed. In the seventh year of Guangxu (1881), Vice Minister of Rites Baoting memorialized on the accumulated abuses of capital inspection, speaking with painful force: "The abuse of favoritism lies not only with department and bureau heads; the Grand Council should be charged with examination, and that examination must be fair and strict. If the Grand Council were truly pure in heart and broke through personal ties, they could examine not only department and bureau clerks but also inner and outer grand ministers. Examining the Grand Council itself, however, rested in the emperor's sole judgment. If the court itself treated capital inspection as empty precedent, how could one blame the Grand Council, much less department and bureau heads! " His argument, though penetrating, was hard to put into practice and remained empty words. In the second year of Xuantong (1910), the Ministry of Personnel established a Constitutional Preparation Office, renamed the Merit Evaluation Department the Performance Evaluation Section, and took charge of reforms needed in civil performance evaluation. By then debate was rampant, old and new practices were tangled together, and the Ministry of Personnel had become little more than a vestigial organ.

The grand assessment began in the second year of Shunzhi (1645), when Censor Zhang Huo proposed that local administrators be ranked with integrity and sincere care for the people as the highest standard. The ministry approved the proposal. The following year court-audience evaluation was codified. Five-colored registers were issued, and governors-general and governors were ordered to record evaluations under the four criteria. By precedent, apart from formal assessment impeachment, the Censorate also conducted supplementary impeachments. That year, in assessing officials, dismissals and promotions relied solely on reports from governors and provincial censors. The Censorate proposed following precedent, but inner grand ministers were displeased. Grand Secretary Chen Mingxia strongly supported the Censorate; Supervising Secretary Wei Xiangshu also petitioned for it. The throne ruled that supplementary impeachment officials who acted from private motives would be punished under grand-assessment rules. Thereafter the Censorate minded favoritism, and supplementary impeachments became rare. The practice was soon abolished, and the power of governors-general and governors grew steadily heavier. In the fourth year, the grand assessment was fixed at once every three years, and officials penalized in the assessment were not allowed to return to office. Officials attending court audience were instructed: "The greedy and cruel shall be heavily punished; the incompetent shall not be pardoned. For the present you are allowed to remain in post; wash away your former faults and strive for better results hereafter. " Thereafter, each court-audience year brought stern admonitions as routine. Under the old court-audience assessment rule, provincial commissioners, circuit intendants, prefects, subprefects, and magistrates all attended court in person. In the ninth year of Shunzhi (1652), only one provincial commissioner and one circuit intendant per province, and one prefectural assistant per prefecture, were sent to attend in proxy. In the eighteenth year (1661), Supervising Secretary Lei Yilong memorialized: "In the triennial grand assessment, do not overlook senior officials while picking on minor clerks, or punish those already out of office while indulging those still in post. Let provincial commissioners and circuit intendants come to the ministry, examine the facts face to face, and scrutinize the registers in detail. " The memorial was sent down to the ministries for deliberation and implementation. In the first year of Kangxi, provincial commissioners and circuit intendants ceased attending court; administrative commissioners and vice commissioners attended in their place. In the twelfth year, provincial commissioners and circuit intendants were again ordered to attend court. In the twenty-fifth year (1686), because court audience was used as a pretext for harsh levies and corruption flourished, the rules requiring provincial commissioners, circuit intendants, and prefectural assistants to attend court were abolished. Whether officials stayed or went depended on the written registers of governors-general and governors; the parallel registers of the provincial administration and surveillance commissioners were discontinued. Early in the dynasty the grand assessment and triennial completion ran in parallel. In the first year of Kangxi the grand assessment was abolished and only triennial completion remained. Circuit intendants and intendants served two years on interior salary and one and a half on frontier salary; local administrators served two years on frontier salary and three on interior salary. Those who completed tax accounts in full were eligible for triennial completion. Localities were classified as devastated, heavily burdened, prosperous, or simple, and grades were set according to administrative achievement under each condition. In the fourth year, triennial completion was stopped and the grand assessment was restored as the permanent rule. Grand-assessment recommendations, impeachments, and evaluation records were by rule submitted by magistrates and subprefects to prefectures and circuits for examination; educational officials by the educational circuit, and salt administrators by their supervising regular official; then forwarded to provincial administration and surveillance commissioners for re-examination, finalized by governors-general and governors, and reported to the ministries and bureaus. River officials who also bore criminal and fiscal duties were examined separately by the Director-General of Rivers and by governors-general and governors. Those who managed river affairs exclusively were examined and memorialized by the Director-General of Rivers himself.

In the twenty-third year of Kangxi (1684), because provincial commissioners and circuit intendants were close to governors-general and governors, they were no longer eligible for outstanding ratings. Outstanding officials were recorded for merit and promoted immediately, often out of regular seniority order. Every dynasty valued this selection highly, and those who recommended unworthy men from favoritism were punished. Early in Kangxi, Censor Zhang Chongyi urged strict enforcement of outstanding quotas, detailed verification of facts, and a match between reputation and reality. The matter was sent down to the ministries for deliberation. In the sixth year, following Censor Tian Liushan's request, outstanding officials were to be judged above all on integrity. Circuit intendants and intendants had to certify that they levied no festival gifts or demanded presents; magistrates and subprefects had to certify that they imposed no miscellaneous corvée, excessive melt fees, losses on merchant houses, or forced loans on wealthy households. The merit of governors-general and governors was judged by whether their subordinates were honest officials. At that time honest officials emerged in succession; Lingshou Magistrate Lu Longqi and others were promoted to judicial posts, and local administration flourished to a degree later called the high point of the age. In the forty-fourth year, outstanding recommendations were ordered to aim for no extra levies, no abusive punishment, no theft cases, no long tax arrears or granary deficits, a settled livelihood for the people, and steady improvement in local conditions. Other empty phrases need not be entered. In the eighth year of Qianlong (1743), governors-general and governors were ordered to evaluate subordinates with agriculture as the fundamental task. Commentators said that making encouragement of farming the key to encouraging officials grasped the root of governance and matched the spirit of Han edicts. Earlier, in the sixth year of Yongzheng (1728), penalties were fixed for false outstanding recommendations, though those who reported their own errors were exempted. If an outstanding official was later found greedy, cruel, or lawless, or with tax or theft cases unsettled, the recommending governor-general or governor was punished more lightly than the circuit intendant, intendant, or prefect who had forwarded the recommendation. In the forty-eighth year of Qianlong (1783), it was revised that when an outstanding official was found guilty of graft, the date of the offense relative to his recommender's departure would determine how each official was punished. Surveillance commissioners, circuit intendants, and prefects received one grade less punishment than governors-general and governors; administration commissioners were punished like governors-general and governors, since circuit intendants and prefects forwarded reports for them to verify personally. In the fiftieth year, the emperor noted that outstanding recommendations had been divided into principal and supplementary lists without clear limits, easily opening the way to opportunism. The ministry was ordered to examine each province's size and number of posts, set a balanced fixed quota, and abolish the supplementary recommendation category. Thereafter each province had fixed outstanding quotas, which changed little for the rest of the dynasty.

The eight penalty categories had long been in force, yet superiors sometimes treated them as empty form, filling registers with minor clerks' trifling faults merely to meet obligation. Dynasty after dynasty issued earnest admonitions against favoritism, while still guarding against wrongful suppression. In the first year of Yongzheng, officials demoted or fined in the grand assessment were normally barred from outstanding ratings, but honest and capable men who had erred through public duty could still receive them. Because outstanding ratings and eight-category recommendations and impeachments involved only a few dozen men, ordinary officials from magistrates upward who were neither commended nor impeached were to be evaluated by governors-general and governors and reported to the ministry. In the fourth year, the court noted that impeached officials might suffer wrongful suppression or that superiors might avoid serious charges and stress minor ones. Apart from the greedy and cruel, who need not be presented, impeached officials for carelessness, flightiness, or incompetence were to be sent to the ministry for audience. In the twenty-fourth year of Qianlong (1759), the emperor noted that eight-category impeachment memorials often failed to state exactly what made an official careless or flighty. Some had committed no error in public business but were broad in procedure; others had talent but rough temperaments. Superiors, from personal incompatibility, often listed them for dismissal—a waste the emperor regretted. Some who had ruined conduct were merely charged with the lighter offense of avoiding serious impeachment—none of this served the intent to rectify official discipline. Officials were ordered to record detailed facts and not impeach in vague generalities. In the eighth year of Jiaqing (1803), governors-general and governors were allowed to impeach at any time for incompetence or mediocrity. If facts were not fully stated and the impeached official wished to appeal at the ministry, the six-category grand-assessment precedent applied. Here examination was deliberately detailed, hoping to uncover overlooked talent and supplement what the formal assessment cycle could not reach. After the Jiaqing and Daoguang reigns, the assessment cycle followed old precedent, and governors-general and governors observed form without reviving its substance. In the eighth year of Daoguang (1828), in Shandong's grand assessment, Acting Governor He Changling had described Xincheng Magistrate Rong Bing as sincere, kindly, and gentle. An edict ruled that mere broad kindness could not earn a top rating and ordered provinces to recommend men who combined substance and practical ability and knew how to govern. During the Xianfeng and Tongzhi military emergencies, some regions had only just been recovered and needed pacification, while frontiers faced rebel threats and urgently needed defense. Governors-general and governors were ordered to note honest and capable men, rate them highly, and hold them ready for promotion. At that time governors-general and governors acted expediently, employed men without regard to seniority, and took measures as circumstances required; they could hardly be bound afterward by the ordinary rules of the grand assessment.

During the Guangxu reign, memorialists often listed the accumulated abuses of the assessment cycle and urged frontier officials to examine subordinates earnestly. Edicts repeatedly admonished them to do so. Yet talent had declined, administration worsened daily, and the law in the end could not be enforced. In the twenty-eighth year, each province was ordered to establish an official evaluation hall and submit memorials every six months. In the thirty-first year, evaluation of magistrates and subprefects was fixed in four grades: highest, superior, ordinary, and inferior. Yet evaluation relied on a single day's written test, and examination on only one year's record—to demand fairness was indeed difficult. In the second year of Xuantong, the Constitutional Compilation Office proposed evaluating magistrates and subprefects separately on schools, police, crafts, planting, homicides and thefts, lawsuits, detention, and grain transport. Each supervising office was to draw up separate evaluation regulations. Categories multiplied, tables and registers were false, and the whole system merely adorned the record without serving reward or punishment. The old rule of the Hanlin and Imperial Academy great examinations, distinguishing superior and inferior with differences in promotion, transfer, demotion, and dismissal, was a special performance-evaluation method. Provincial intendants and circuit intendants underwent confidential year-end examinations. Magistrates and subprefects were screened at the end of their one-year terms; educational and assistant officials at the end of six years of service. These were methods of evaluation at any time, outside the two cycles of capital inspection and grand assessment.

At the founding no limit was set. In the ninth year of Shunzhi (1652), a six-year cycle was fixed—the beginning of military evaluation. In the eleventh year, the cycle was shortened to five years. In the thirteenth year, at Supervising Secretary Zhang Wenguang's request, outstanding military ratings first received robes and commendation like civil officials, later changed to a one-step rank advancement. In the first year of Kangxi, military evaluation was stopped and only triennial completion was used. Soon the Ministry of War memorialized that provincial military posts should follow civil rules, with governors-general and provincial military commanders jointly recommending and impeaching by term of service. Censor Ji Zhenyi memorialized: "Military triennial completion has become a matter of scheming for superior grades, deducting military pay, and harming the frontier. Promotions should follow salary history and merit order. " In the sixth year military evaluation was codified. Capital and provincial military commanders were rated under four criteria, with service records and military merit listed in detail, retention and removal distinguished, and reported to the ministry. Recommendations required explicit evaluation phrases such as upright conduct, skilled archery and horsemanship, strict command, diligent service, and no harm to the locality. Impeachment required factual evidence under the eight penalty categories. Provincial military commanders and regional commanders were again ordered to submit self-criticism. Provincial commanders were evaluated by governors-general; regional commanders by governors-general and provincial commanders. In provinces without a governor-general, the governor conducted the evaluation. Later, war in Yunnan disturbed the realm, urgent dispatches were unceasing, and military evaluation was neglected for ten years. By the twenty-first year the Yunnan rebels were pacified. At Supervising Secretary Shuomuke's request, the great military evaluation was held, with each officer's service record counted from after the eleventh year of Kangxi's military evaluation. Battalion commanders of the Nine Gates and the like were evaluated by the Nine Gates commander. Candidate regional commanders were also ordered to submit self-criticism. Brigadiers and below awaiting vacancies were examined under the old rule. In the sixty-first year, capital military officials including leading imperial bodyguard grand ministers, Eight Banner commanders-in-chief, vanguard, guard, and infantry commanders-in-chief, and deputy commanders-in-chief were exempted from self-criticism. During military evaluation, subordinates were evaluated following the provincial recommendation and impeachment rules. Garrison generals and deputy commanders-in-chief in each province submitted self-criticism following provincial and regional commander precedent. Their subordinates followed the capital rule. Garrison commandants and assistant commanders at Dezhou and elsewhere were examined by dispatched grand ministers, who jointly screened their subordinates and reported the evaluations. In the first year of Yongzheng, ordinary officers from garrison commanders upward were evaluated by governors-general, governors, provincial commanders, and regional commanders. That winter an edict said: "At the first military evaluation, those who had served in campaign, were old with long seniority, and could still manage affairs were to be retained. Those unfit to remain were separately memorialized for special favor. Even those who had not served in the field but had long service were to be carefully examined. " This showed concern for seniority and long service and specially issued a lenient rule—not the ordinary precedent. In the second year, recommended brigadiers, colonels, and regimental commanders from each province were presented in turn to observe talent and martial skill; governors-general, governors, provincial commanders, and regional commanders secretly graded them by integrity and training. In the sixth year, Shanxi Taiyuan Regional Commander Yuan Lisong memorialized that Pingyuan Garrison Commander Liang Yu was honest, alert, and skilled, yet was impeached for age. The emperor said seasoned talent was hard to find and ordered him considered for appointment as regimental commander—a special grace. That year it was fixed that if an outstanding official proved greedy or lawless in his original post, or was transferred and later committed graft elsewhere, the original recommender would be punished separately.
18
乾隆二年，部議出兵效力人員，年老休致，令子弟一人入伍食糧，無子弟亦給守糧養贍。 從之。 時直省保題員弁，類以明白勤敏、才堪辦事列上選。 十一年，諭嗣後保題，務重弓馬漢仗。 十五年，以各省所保總兵官鮮當意，諭曰：「年滿千總一項，類多猥瑣。 國家擢用武職，營伍為正途，拔補將弁，必選之若輩。 緣次而升，皆自年滿千總始。 折衝禦侮之用，豫籌於昇平無事之日，不可視為緩圖。 」二十四年，以大臣自陳例既罷，敕兵部于軍政年，將在京都統、副都統，在外駐防將軍、都統、副都統，各省提督、總兵官，分別三本，條舉事實候鑒裁，以重考績。 四十二年，定衛、所綠營武職薦舉卓異尚未升轉，再遇軍政列平等者，將上次卓異註銷。 嘉慶四年，定侍衛軍政考試，向例軍政年不許告病乞休，以杜規避。 八年，申諭查閱營伍年分，事關考覈，照軍政例，不得告病、乞休。 咸、同軍興，百度稍弛，軍政大典，相沿不廢。 咸豐二年，黑龍江將軍英隆以俄兵窺伺，派將弁扼守要隘，疏請本年軍政展限舉行。 不允。 嗣湖廣總督程矞采等以軍務未竣，疏請展限，令凱撤後再行補考。 並諭年老力衰者，隨時參辦。 沿及德宗，雖加意振飭，勢成弩末，展限之舉，史不絕書。
In the second year of Qianlong, the ministry ruled that campaign veterans who retired on age should have one son or brother enter the ranks for rations, or receive guard-ration support if they had no suitable relative. The proposal was approved. At that time recommended provincial officers were mostly listed for top selection as clear, diligent, alert, and capable of handling affairs. In the eleventh year, it was ordered that future recommendations must emphasize archery, horsemanship, and Chinese weapons. In the fifteenth year, because recommended regional commanders rarely pleased the emperor, an edict said: "The pool of battalion commanders completing their terms is mostly petty and trivial. The state promotes military officers through the camps as the regular path, and in selecting generals and commanders it must draw from such men. Promotion by seniority all begins with battalion commanders completing their terms. The capacity to repel enemies must be prepared in days of peace—it cannot be treated as a matter to defer. " In the twenty-fourth year, because grand ministers no longer submitted self-criticism, the Ministry of War was ordered that in military evaluation years, capital and provincial commanders-in-chief, garrison generals, provincial commanders, and regional commanders should each submit three memorials listing facts for imperial judgment, to strengthen performance evaluation. In the forty-second year, it was fixed that Green Standard officers recommended as outstanding but not yet promoted would have that rating cancelled if listed as ordinary in the next military evaluation. In the fourth year of Jiaqing, bodyguard military evaluation examinations were fixed. By precedent, reporting illness or requesting retirement in military evaluation years was forbidden to prevent evasion. In the eighth year, it was reiterated that in years of camp inspection, which concerned evaluation, illness reports and retirement requests were forbidden as in military evaluation years. During the Xianfeng and Tongzhi military emergencies, many regulations grew lax, yet the great military evaluation ceremony continued. In the second year of Xianfeng, Heilongjiang General Yinglong, with Russian troops watching the frontier, dispatched officers to hold key passes and memorialized to postpone that year's military evaluation. The request was denied. Later Huguang Governor-General Cheng Yucai and others, because military affairs were unfinished, memorialized for extension and ordered re-examination after victory and withdrawal. The aged and weak were also to be impeached at any time. Down to the Guangxu Emperor, though effort was made to revive discipline, momentum was spent and extensions appear endlessly in the histories.
19
光緒十四年，編定北洋海軍，由海軍衙門司黜陟。 甲午以後，力鑒覆轍，裁綠營，練新軍，別訂考覈章程。 三十二年，改兵部為陸軍部，其考覈隸軍衡司。 宣統二年，設海軍部，其考覈隸軍制司。 朝廷銳意革新，軍紀宜可少振。 無如積習已深，時艱日棘，卒歸罔濟雲。
In the fourteenth year of Guangxu, the Beiyang Navy was organized, with promotions and dismissals managed by the Naval Yamen. After the Sino-Japanese War of 1894–1895, taking hard lesson from repeated failure, the court cut the Green Standard, trained new armies, and drew up separate evaluation regulations. In the thirty-second year, the Ministry of War became the Ministry of the Army, with evaluation under the Military Standards Department. In the second year of Xuantong, the Ministry of the Navy was established, with evaluation under the Military Organization Department. The court was keen to reform, and military discipline ought to have revived somewhat. Yet habits were deeply entrenched, the times grew daily more difficult, and in the end it came to nothing.
