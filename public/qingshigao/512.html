1
西南諸省，水復山重，草木蒙昧，雲霧晦冥，人生其間，叢叢虱虱，言語飲食，迥殊華風，曰苗、曰蠻，史冊屢紀，顧略有區別。 無君長不相統屬之謂苗，各長其部割據一方之謂蠻。 若粵之僮、之黎，黔、楚之瑤，四川之倮儸、之生番，雲南之野人，皆苗之類。 若漢書：「南夷君長以十數，夜郎最大。 其西，靡莫之屬以十數，滇最大。 自滇以北，君長以十數，卭都最大。 」在宋為羈縻州。 在元為宣慰、宣撫、招討、安撫、長官等土司。 湖廣之田、彭，四川之謝、向、冉，廣西之岑、韋，貴州之安、楊，雲南之刀、思，遠者自漢、唐，近亦自宋、元，各君其君，各子其子，根柢深固，族姻互結。 假我爵祿，寵之名號，乃易為統攝，故奔走惟命，皆蠻之類。
The southwestern provinces are a country of interlaced rivers and stacked ranges, where vegetation grows rank beneath perpetual cloud and mist. The peoples who live there are countless and densely packed; their languages and diets are wholly unlike those of the Central Plains. Historians have long called them Miao or Man, yet the two categories are not quite the same. Groups with no paramount chief and no mutual subordination are termed Miao; groups whose leaders each command a separate domain and hold territory on their own are termed Man. The Zhuang and Li of Guangdong, the Yao of Guizhou and Huguang, the Luoluo and uncivilized tribes of Sichuan, and the hill peoples of Yunnan all belong to the Miao category. The Book of Han records: "The southern barbarians have chieftains by the dozen; Yelang is the largest." West of them, the Mo tribes and their kind likewise number in the dozens; Dian is foremost among them. North of Dian there are again chieftains by the dozen; Qiongdu ranks largest. " In Song times these became jimi prefectures—border districts left under native rule. Under the Yuan they were organized as native offices—Pacification, Comforting, Punitive, Soothing, and Chief Official commissions, among others. The Tian and Peng clans of Huguang, the Xie, Xiang, and Ran of Sichuan, the Cen and Wei of Guangxi, the An and Yang of Guizhou, and the Dao and Si of Yunnan—some lineages reaching back to Han and Tang, others no earlier than Song and Yuan—each acknowledged only its own chief and passed succession only within its own house. Their foundations were deep and their kin networks tightly interwoven. Once the court lent them titles and salaries and honored them with official designations, they could readily be brought under centralized control, and so they hurried to obey every order. These are all of the Man type.
2
明代播州、藺州、水西、麓川，皆勤大軍數十萬，殫天下力而後剷平之。 故云、貴、川、廣恆視土司為治亂。
Under the Ming, Bozhou, Linzhou, Shuixi, and Luchuan each demanded armies in the hundreds of thousands and the full weight of the realm before they could finally be suppressed. For this reason Yunnan, Guizhou, Sichuan, and Guangdong always took the native chieftains as the barometer of regional stability.
3
清初因明制，屬平西、定南諸籓鎮撫之。 康熙三年，吳三桂督雲、貴兵兩路討水西宣慰安坤之叛，平其地，設黔西、平遠、大定、威寧四府。 三籓之亂，重啗土司兵為助。 及叛籓戡定，餘威震於殊俗。
Early in the Qing the court retained the Ming arrangement and left these territories under the pacification of the Pingxi, Dingnan, and other feudatory princes. In Kangxi year 3, Wu Sangui led Yunnan and Guizhou forces along two routes against the rebellion of An Kun, the Shuixi Pacification Commissioner. After pacifying the territory, the court established the four prefectures of Qianxi, Pingyuan, Dading, and Weining. During the Rebellion of the Three Feudatories, the rebels once again recruited native chieftain forces as allies. Once the rebel princes had been crushed, the lingering prestige of the dynasty awed the frontier peoples.
4
至雍正初，而有改土歸流之議。 四年春，以鄂爾泰巡撫雲南兼總督事，奏言：「雲貴大患，無如苗蠻。 欲安民必先制夷，欲制夷必改土歸流。 而苗疆多與鄰省犬牙相錯，又必歸併事權，始可一勞永逸。 即如東川、烏蒙、鎮雄，皆四川土府。 東川與滇一嶺之隔，至滇省城四百餘里，而距四川成都千有八百里。 去冬，烏蒙土府攻掠東川，滇兵擊退，而川省令箭方至。 烏蒙至滇省城亦僅六百餘里。 自康熙五十三年土官祿鼎乾不法，欽差、督、撫會審畢節，以流官交質始出，益無忌憚。 其錢糧不過三百餘兩，而取於下者百倍。 一年四小派，三年一大派。 小派計錢，大派計兩。 土司一取子婦，則土民三載不敢婚。 土民有罪被殺，其親族尚出墊刀數十金，終身無見天日之期。 東川已改流三十載，仍為土目盤踞，文武長寓省城，膏腴四百里，無人敢墾。 若東川、烏蒙、鎮雄改隸雲南，俾臣得相機改流，可設三府一鎮，永靖邊氛。 此事連四川者也。 廣西土府州縣峒寨等司五十餘員，分隸南寧、太平、思恩、慶遠四府，多狄青征儂智高、王守仁征田州時所留設。 其邊患，除泗城土府外，餘皆土目，橫於土司。 且黔、粵向以牂牁江為界，而粵之西隆州與黔之普安州逾江互相鬥入，苗寨寥闊，文武動輒推諉。 應以江北歸黔，江南歸粵，增州設營，形格勢禁。 此事連廣西者也。 滇邊西南界以瀾滄江，江外為車里、緬甸、老撾諸土司。 其江內之滇沅、威遠、元江、新平、普洱、茶山諸夷，巢穴深邃，出沒魯魁、哀牢間，無事近患腹心，有事遠通外國，自元迨明，代為邊害。 論者謂江外宜土不宜流，江內宜流不宜土。 此雲南宜治之邊夷也。 貴州土司向無鉗束群苗之責，苗患甚於土司。 而苗疆四周幾三千餘里，千有三百餘寨，古州踞其中，群砦環其外。 左有清江可北達楚，右有都江可南通粵，皆為頑苗蟠據，梗隔三省，遂成化外。 如欲開江路以通黔、粵，非勒兵深入，遍加剿撫不可。 此貴州宜治之邊夷也。 臣思前明流土之分，原因煙瘴新疆，未習風土，故因地制宜，使之鄉導彈壓。 今歷數百載，相沿以夷治夷，遂至以盜治盜，苗、倮無追贓抵命之憂，土司無革職削地之罰，直至事大上聞，行賄詳結，上司亦不深求，以為鎮靜邊民無所控訴； 若不剷蔓塞源，縱兵刑財賦事事整飭，皆治標而非治本。 其改流之法，計擒為上，兵剿次之。 令其自首為上，勒獻次之。 惟制夷必先練兵，練兵必先選將。 誠能賞罰嚴明，將士用命，先治內，後攘外，必能所向奏效，實雲貴邊防百世之利。 」世宗知鄂爾泰才，必能辦寇，即詔以東川、烏蒙、鎮雄三土府改隸雲南。 六年，復鑄三省總督印，令鄂爾泰兼制廣西。
Early in the Yongzheng reign the court began debating the policy of abolishing native chieftaincies and appointing regular officials in their place. In the spring of his fourth year on the throne, the emperor appointed Ortai Grand Coordinator of Yunnan with concurrent governorship and received his memorial: "The gravest trouble in Yunnan and Guizhou is none other than the Miao and Man peoples." To secure the populace one must first subdue the frontier peoples; to subdue them one must abolish native chieftaincies and install regular officials. Miao territories moreover zigzag across provincial borders; only by merging administrative authority can the problem be settled once and for all. Dongchuan, Wumeng, and Zhenxiong, for instance, were all native prefectures subordinate to Sichuan. Dongchuan lies just one mountain range from Yunnan—only four hundred-odd li from the Yunnan capital—yet more than eighteen hundred li from Chengdu in Sichuan. Last winter the Wumeng native prefect attacked Dongchuan. Yunnan troops had already driven him off before Sichuan's orders even reached the scene. Wumeng itself is only about six hundred li from the Yunnan capital. Since Kangxi year 53, when the native official Lu Dingqian broke the law, imperial commissioners and provincial governors tried him jointly at Bijie. He surrendered only after a regular official was held as hostage, and thereafter grew ever bolder in his defiance. Their registered tax was barely three hundred taels, yet what they collected from the people was a hundred times that amount. They imposed four minor exactions every year and one major exaction every three years. Minor levies were counted in copper cash; major levies in ounces of silver. Whenever a chieftain seized a subject's bride, the people would not dare wed for three years afterward. When a guilty native was put to death, his kin still had to pay dozens of taels as "blade-padding" compensation, and his family might never again see the light of day in public life. Though Dongchuan had been under regular administration for thirty years, native headmen still controlled it. Civil and military officials lodged permanently at the provincial capital, and four hundred li of rich farmland lay untilled because no one dared reclaim it. If Dongchuan, Wumeng, and Zhenxiong were reassigned to Yunnan so that I might replace native rule as circumstances allow, we could establish three prefectures and one garrison and secure the frontier for good. This proposal also involves Sichuan. Guangxi has more than fifty native prefectures, districts, stockade offices, and the like, distributed among Nanning, Taiping, Si'en, and Qingyuan—most of them remnants of Di Qing's war against Nong Zhigao and Wang Shouren's expedition against Tianzhou. Border troubles there arise, except at the Sicheng native prefecture, chiefly from native headmen who bully the recognized chieftains. Guizhou and Guangdong have long taken the Zangge River as their border, yet Xilong in Guangdong and Pu'an in Guizhou raid across the river into each other's territory. Miao villages lie scattered across a vast wilderness, and officials on both sides habitually shift blame to one another. The north bank should go to Guizhou and the south bank to Guangdong; new prefectures and garrisons should be added so that each side can restrain the other by geography and military presence. This proposal likewise involves Guangxi. Yunnan's southwestern frontier is defined by the Lancang River. Beyond it lie the native domains of Cheli, Burma, Laos, and others. Inside the river, the tribes of Dianyuan, Weiyuan, Yuanjiang, Xinping, Pu'er, and Chashan nest in remote fastnesses and raid between Luokui and Ailao. In peacetime they threaten the interior; in crisis they reach out to foreign powers. From Yuan through Ming they have been a perennial frontier menace. Policy makers have long held that beyond the river native rule is appropriate, while within it regular administration is required. These are the frontier peoples of Yunnan that most need effective governance. Guizhou's native chieftains were never charged with controlling the Miao, and Miao unrest has proved a greater problem than the chieftains themselves. The Miao region spans nearly three thousand li on every side and contains more than thirteen hundred stockades. Guzhou sits at the center, surrounded by a ring of fortified villages. The Qing River on the left could link north to Huguang; the Du River on the right could link south to Guangdong. Both waterways are held by obstinate Miao bands that cut off three provinces and leave the region effectively outside the empire. To open the river routes between Guizhou and Guangdong, the court must send troops deep into the region and carry out thorough suppression and pacification—there is no other way. These are the frontier peoples of Guizhou that most urgently require governance. I believe the Ming distinction between regular and native administration arose because the new southwestern territories were malarial and unfamiliar. The court therefore adapted policy to local conditions and relied on natives as guides and enforcers. Centuries later, the policy of letting natives govern natives has devolved into letting bandits govern bandits. Miao and Luoluo need not fear pursuit for stolen goods or capital punishment; chieftains need not fear dismissal or loss of territory. Only when a case grows too large to hide do they bribe officials to close the file. Superiors rarely probe deeply, satisfied so long as the frontier appears calm and no one petitions. Unless we cut away the overgrowth and stop it at the source, even perfect discipline over troops, law, and taxation will treat only symptoms, not the disease itself. The best method for conversion is stratagem and capture; military suppression comes second. Inducing voluntary surrender is best; forced submission is second best. To control the frontier peoples one must first train troops, and to train troops one must first choose the right commanders. With strict rewards and punishments, obedient officers and men, pacification of the interior before expansion outward, every campaign will succeed. This would secure Yunnan and Guizhou's frontiers for generations to come. " Recognizing Ortai's ability and confidence that he could handle the frontier troubles, the Yongzheng Emperor immediately ordered Dongchuan, Wumeng, and Zhenxiong transferred to Yunnan's jurisdiction. In the sixth year a new seal was cast for the three-province governorship, and Ortai was given concurrent authority over Guangxi.
5
於是自四年至九年，蠻悉改流，苗亦歸化，間有叛逆，旋即平定。 其間如雍正朝古州苗疆之蕩平，乾隆朝四川大小金川之誅鋤，光緒朝西藏瞻對之征伐，皆事之钜者，分見於篇。
From the fourth through the ninth year of his reign, Man domains were converted to regular administration and Miao groups submitted. Occasional revolts broke out but were quickly suppressed. Major campaigns such as the Yongzheng pacification of the Guzhou Miao frontier, the Qianlong suppression of Great and Little Jinchuan in Sichuan, and the Guangxu expedition against Zhandui in Tibet are each recounted in their own chapters.
6
其土官銜號，曰宣慰司，曰宣撫司，曰招討司，曰安撫司，曰長官司。 以勞績之多寡，分尊卑之等差，而府、州、縣之名亦往往有之。
Native officials bore titles such as Pacification Commissioner, Comforting Commissioner, Punitive Commissioner, Soothing Commissioner, and Chief Official. Rank reflected the extent of service rendered, and many offices also bore the designations of prefecture, department, or county.

Native chieftaincies not yet converted to regular administration today include two Sichuan Comforting Commissioners: Qiongdu and Shama. Five Pacification Commissioners: Muping, Mingzheng, Badi, Bawang, and De'ergete. There are twenty-one Soothing Commissioners, including Changning, Wori, Wasi, Suomo, Guabie, Muli, Gebuxizha, Badi, Chuosijiabu, Lagun, Washuyuke, and the various Huo'er and Chunke offices, as well as Lower Zhaundui and Upper Naduo. Twenty-nine Chief Officials include Jingzhou, Longmu, Yuexi, Songgang, Zhuokeji, Weilongzhou, Yangdi Pass, Dangba, Hedong, Aduzheng, Pujizhou, Changzhou, Shenbian, Lengbian, the Washu offices, the Huo'er offices, Chunke Gaori, Upper Zhaundui, Menggejie, Nixi, Pingyi, Manyi, Muchuan, and Jiuxing.

Yunnan has one Pacification Commissioner: Cheli. Five Comforting Commissioners: Gengma, Longchuan, Ganya, Nandian, and Menglian. Two Vice Comforting Commissioners: Zhefang and Zhanda. Three Soothing Commissioners: Lujiang, Mangshi, and Mengmao. Three Vice Chief Officials—Nalou, Kuirongdian, and Shierguan—and four native prefectures: Menghua, Jingdong, Mengding, and Yongning. Four native departments: Fuzhou, Wandian, Zhenkang, and Beisheng.

Guangxi has twenty-six native departments, including Zhongzhou, Guide, Guohua, Xialei, Xiashixi, Siling, Pingxiang, Jiangzhou, Sizhou, Wancheng, Taiping, Anping, Longying, Dujie, Jie'an, Shangxia Dong, Jielun, Mingzhou, Mingying, Zhenyuan, Nadi, Nanzhou, Tianzhou, Xiangwu, Dukang, and Shangying. 11
凡宣慰、宣撫、安撫、長官等司之承襲隸兵部，土府、土州之承襲隸吏部。 凡土司貢賦，或比年一貢，或三年一貢，各因其土產，穀米、牛馬、皮、布，皆折以銀，而會計於戶部。
Succession to Pacification, Comforting, Soothing, and Chief Official posts was handled by the Ministry of War; succession to native prefectures and departments by the Ministry of Personnel. Native chieftains paid tribute either annually or every three years, according to local products such as grain, livestock, hides, and cloth, all commuted to silver and recorded by the Ministry of Revenue.
12
雍正七年，川陝總督岳鍾琪奏四川巴塘、里塘等處請授宣撫司三員、安撫司九員、長官司十二員，給與印結號紙，副土官四員、千戶三員、百戶二十四員，給以職銜，以分職守。 內巴塘、裏塘正副土官原無世代頭目承襲，請照流官例。 如有事故，開缺題補，與他土司不同。
In Yongzheng year 7, Governor-General Yue Zhongqi of Sichuan-Shaanxi requested appointment of three Comforting Commissioners, nine Soothing Commissioners, and twelve Chief Officials for Batang, Litang, and other Sichuan districts, with seals and credentials issued. Four vice chiefs, three chiliarchs, and twenty-four centurions received titles to divide administrative duties. The principal and vice chiefs of Batang and Litang had no hereditary succession; he asked that appointments follow the regular official model instead. Vacancies would be filled by imperial appointment, unlike other hereditary chieftaincies.

Huguang's southwestern corner was in Warring States times the land of Wu Commandery and Qianzhong. Shinan and Rongmei in Hubei and Yongshun, Baojing, and Sangzhi in Hunan form a contiguous region between Yueyang, Chenzhou, Changde, and Yichang. It borders Ba and Kui in eastern Sichuan, opens south to Guizhou, and west to Sichuan. The Pacification, Soothing, and Chief Official offices established under the Yuan were retained under the Ming. The Yong and Bao Pacification Commissioners were long regarded as powerful hereditary houses with bold troops who marched at the van of every campaign. The state depended on them heavily. By the Qing conquest only three Comforting Commissioners remained—Shinan, Sanmao, and Rongmei—along with two Pacification Commissioners, Yongshun and Baojing. During the Yongzheng reign Shinan, Rongmei, Yongshun, and Baojing surrendered in turn. The court established Shinan Prefecture under the Hubei administration and Yongshun Prefecture under the Hunan administration. After the two prefectures were created, native chieftain titles disappeared throughout the region. Later Miao rebellions are recorded in other biographies and are not included here.

Shinan: the ancient territory of Ba. Under the Qin and Han it was the land of the Nan Commandery tribes. Under the Tang it was Shi Prefecture. The Yuan established the Shinan Comforting Commission and the Zhongxiao Soothing Commission. Under Ming Yuzhen the Zhonglu Comforting Commission was restored. In Ming Xuande year 3 the Jiannan Chief Official Commission was restored and Shi Guard established, governing one battalion, four Comforting Commissions, nine Soothing Commissions, thirteen Chief Officials, and five Manyi offices. In Qing Kangxi year 3 Shi Prefecture first submitted to the dynasty. In the fourth year Shaxi was downgraded from Pacification to Comforting Commissioner, Jiannan Chief Official was renamed Jiannan (Ji'an) Chief Official, while Shinan Comforting, Zhongxiao Soothing, and Zhonglu Soothing Commissions remained unchanged. In Yongzheng year 6, at Governor-General Mai Zhu's request, Shi Guard was abolished and Enshi County created. Guizhou was made a directly governed department, and all fifteen former native chieftaincies were placed under Enshi. In the twelfth year Zhongxiao Soothing Commissioner Tian Zhang surrendered his territory, which was incorporated into Enshi County. In the thirteenth year Shinan Comforting Commissioner Qin Yuding was stripped of his post for misconduct and converted to regular administration. Zhongdong chieftain Tian Guangzu and others then petitioned to surrender as well. The fifteen chieftaincies and Enshi County were combined into the new Shinan Prefecture governing six counties. Rongmei became Hefeng Department, placed under Yichang Prefecture and the Xunjing surveillance circuit. Under the Ming, Shi Guard governed three li districts, five battalions, and thirty-one native chieftaincies—the li of Shiguo, Duting, and Chongning; the Left, Right, and Center battalions attached to the county seat; the Datian Military-Civilian Thousand-Household Battalion; and the Zhi Luo Garrison Hundred-Household Battalion.

The Rongmei chieftaincy: in Tang Yuanhe year 1, Tian Xinggao joined Gao Chongwen's campaign to suppress Liu Pi and was appointed punitive commissioner over Shi, Qin, Rong, and Wan, while continuing to govern four prefectures. Under the Song the line was held by Tian Zhengzhi. Under the Yuan it passed to Tian Qianheng. In Ming Hongwu year 3, Tian Guangbao traveled to the imperial camp with his Yuan-era patent to request reconfirmation; the court appointed him Pacification Commissioner anew. The line descended to Tian Jilin, who submitted during the Shunzhi reign and was confirmed as Pacification Commissioner. His son Ganlin succeeded to the post. Ganlin, courtesy name Tieyun, wrote the Hepu ji collection. Ganlin's son Shunian, courtesy name Jiufeng, had once accepted a spurious patent from the Wu pretender making him "Earl Who Receives Grace"; he later turned it in to the authorities. Called to military service by imperial order, he earned distinction in the field. He also gathered literary men to study the classics and history, and published a printed compendium of the Twenty-One Histories. He kept a daily reading schedule, noting in the margins of his books which classic he had read or which history he had studied and how far he had progressed, stamping each entry with a small personal seal. He authored the Bailutang ji and the Rongyang shishu lu. His son Mingru succeeded to the office. Zhao Shenqiao impeached him for misconduct, but the throne issued an edict granting him pardon. In Yongzheng year 11, Governor Mai Zhu submitted another stern denunciation. Mingru shifted his headquarters to Pingshan Stockade as if preparing to resist, but when Zhang Tong, chief of Shiliang, stationed there and pressed him to comply, Mingru took his own life. The domain was converted to regular administration: the commission became Hefeng Prefecture under Yichang Prefecture.

Yongshun: in Han times it was Wuling; under the Sui it belonged to Chenzhou; under the Tang it was Xizhou territory. Under the Song it was Yongshun Prefecture. During the Yuan dynasty, Peng Wanqian reorganized the territory as the Military and Civil Pacification Commission of Yongshun and adjacent areas. In the fifth year of the Hongwu reign (1372), the office was reclassified as Pacification Commissioner. In the fourth year of the Shunzhi reign (1647), Prince Gongshun Kong Youde reached Chenzhou, and Pacification Commissioner Peng Hongpeng brought three native prefects, six native chiefs, and three hundred eighty Miao and Man communities in submission. In the fourteenth year (1657), the court issued the Pacification Commissioner's seal and appointed one regular administrative director. In the tenth year of the Kangxi reign (1671), Wu Sangui rebelled and seized Chenlong Pass, granting a counterfeit seal to Yongshun Pacification Commissioner Peng Tingchun, who promptly returned it to the court. By imperial decree, his son Honghai was given the rank of regional commander and ordered to lead native troops in the joint campaign. For his service, he received the Pacification Commission seal. In the sixth year of the Yongzheng reign (1728), Pacification Commissioner Peng Zhaohuai surrendered his territory and asked to return to his ancestral home in Jiangxi. The emperor commended him, appointed him brigade vice-commander with a hereditary Tuosha Lahafan rank, granted ten thousand taels of silver, and allowed him to establish an estate and settle in Jiangxi. Yongshun Commission was converted into a prefecture; the seat became Yongshun County, and land from Yongshun's Baiya Dong was carved off to form Longshan County.

施溶州土知州，在永順司東南。 元會溪、施溶等處長官司。 明洪武二年，改州，以田建霸為土知州。 傳至田茂年，清順治四年，歸附。 雍正五年，田永豐納土。
The native prefect of Shirong Prefecture lay southeast of the Yongshun Pacification Commission. Under the Yuan, it was the native chief office of Huixi, Shirong, and neighboring districts. In the second year of the Hongwu reign (1369), the office was elevated to a prefecture, with Tian Jianba appointed native prefect. The line passed to Tian Maonian, who submitted in the fourth year of the Shunzhi reign (1647). In the fifth year of the Yongzheng reign (1727), Tian Yongfeng surrendered his territory.
40
上溪州土知州，屬永順司。 明洪武二年，以張義保為土知州。 傳至張漢卿，清順治四年，歸附。 雍正五年，張漢儒納土。
The native prefect of Shangxi Prefecture was under the Yongshun Pacification Commission. In the second year of the Hongwu reign (1369), Zhang Yibao was appointed native prefect. The line passed to Zhang Hanqing, who submitted in the fourth year of the Shunzhi reign (1647). In the fifth year of the Yongzheng reign (1727), Zhang Hanru surrendered his territory.
41
臘惹峒長官司，元屬思州，以向孛爍為總管。 明洪武五年，改屬永順司，以田世貴為長官司。 傳至田仕朝，清順治四年，歸附。 雍正五年，田中和納土。
The Lara Dong native chief office had belonged to Sizhou under the Yuan, with Xiang Boshuo as superintendent. In the fifth year of the Hongwu reign (1372), it was transferred to Yongshun's jurisdiction, with Tian Shigui appointed native chief. The line passed to Tian Shichao, who submitted in the fourth year of the Shunzhi reign (1647). In the fifth year of the Yongzheng reign (1727), Tian Zhonghe surrendered his territory.
42
麥著黃峒長官司，元曰麥著土村，屬思州。 明洪武五年，改屬永順司，以黃穀踵為長官司。 傳至黃甲，清順治四年，歸附。 雍正五年，黃正乾納土。
The Maizhu Huang Dong native chief office was known under the Yuan as the Maizhu native village and fell under Sizhou. In the fifth year of the Hongwu reign (1372), it was transferred to Yongshun's jurisdiction, with Huang Guzhong appointed native chief. The line passed to Huang Jia, who submitted in the fourth year of the Shunzhi reign (1647). In the fifth year of the Yongzheng reign (1727), Huang Zhengqian surrendered his territory.
43
驢遲峒長官司，元屬思州。 明洪武五年，改屬永順司，以向迪踵為長官司。 傳至向光胄，清順治四年，歸附。 雍正五年，向錫爵納土。
The Lüchi Dong native chief office was under Sizhou during the Yuan. In the fifth year of the Hongwu reign (1372), it was transferred to Yongshun's jurisdiction, with Xiang Dizhong appointed native chief. The line passed to Xiang Guangzhou, who submitted in the fourth year of the Shunzhi reign (1647). In the fifth year of the Yongzheng reign (1727), Xiang Xijue surrendered his territory.
44
施溶溪長官司，元屬思州。 明初，改屬永順司，以汪良為長官司。 傳至汪世忠，清順治四年，歸附。 雍正五年，汪文珂納土。
The Shirong Creek native chief office was under Sizhou during the Yuan. In the early Ming, it was transferred to Yongshun's jurisdiction, with Wang Liang appointed native chief. The line passed to Wang Shizhong, who submitted in the fourth year of the Shunzhi reign (1647). In the fifth year of the Yongzheng reign (1727), Wang Wenke surrendered his territory.
45
白岩峒長官司，元屬葛蠻安撫司。 明初，改屬永順司，以張那律為長官司。 傳至張四教，清順治四年，歸附。 雍正五年，張宗略納土。
The Baiyan Dong native chief office was under the Geman Pacification Commission during the Yuan. In the early Ming, it was transferred to Yongshun's jurisdiction, with Zhang Nalü appointed native chief. The line passed to Zhang Sijiao, who submitted in the fourth year of the Shunzhi reign (1647). In the fifth year of the Yongzheng reign (1727), Zhang Zonglüe surrendered his territory.
46
田家峒長官司，明洪武三年，以田勝祖為長官司。 傳至田興祿，清順治四年，歸附。 雍正五年，田藎臣納土。
The Tianjia Dong native chief office was established in the third year of the Hongwu reign (1370), with Tian Shengzu appointed native chief. The line passed to Tian Xinglu, who submitted in the fourth year of the Shunzhi reign (1647). In the fifth year of the Yongzheng reign (1727), Tian Jinchen surrendered his territory.
47
保靖宣慰司，亦唐溪州地。 宋曰保靜州。 元為保靖州安撫司。 明仍為安撫使。 清順治四年，明宣慰司彭象乾之子彭朝柱歸附。 象乾曾孫澤虹病廢，其妻彭氏用事。 漢奸高倫、張為任二人結連其舍把長官彭澤蛟、彭祖裕等，相與樹黨，以劫殺為事。 雍正元年，澤虹死，子御彬幼，澤蛟欲奪其職，為禦彬所遏。 迨御彬襲職，肆為淫凶，澤蛟與其弟澤虬合謀，互相劫殺。 二年，御彬以追緝澤蛟為名，潛結容美土司田旻如、桑植土司向國棟，率土兵搶虜保靖民財。 七年，御彬安置遼陽，以其地為保靖縣。
The Baojing Pacification Commission occupied the same territory as Tang-era Xizhou. Under the Song it was known as Baojing Prefecture. Under the Yuan it became the Baojing Prefecture Pacification Commission. The Ming continued the office as Pacification Commissioner. In the fourth year of the Shunzhi reign (1647), Peng Chaochu, son of the former Ming Pacification Commissioner Peng Xiangqian, submitted. Xiangqian's great-grandson Zehong was incapacitated by illness, and his wife, Lady Peng, held the real power. Two Han collaborators, Gao Lun and Zhang Weiren, allied with his subordinate chiefs Peng Zejiao, Peng Zuyu, and others, forming rival factions that lived by robbery and murder. In the first year of the Yongzheng reign (1723), Zehong died, leaving a young heir, Yubin. Zejiao sought to seize the office but was thwarted by Yubin. Once Yubin inherited the office, he ruled with unrestrained brutality. Zejiao and his younger brother Zeqiu plotted together, and the two sides raided and killed one another. In the second year (1724), Yubin, claiming he was hunting Zejiao, secretly allied with Rongmei chieftain Tian Minru and Sangzhi chieftain Xiang Guodong, leading native troops to loot the people of Baojing. In the seventh year of the Yongzheng reign (1729), Yubin was resettled in Liaoyang, and his territory was reorganized as Baojing County.
48
大喇司，在龍山縣，屬保靖司。 明正德十五年，以土舍彭惠協理巡檢事。 傳至彭禦佶，雍正十三年，納土。
The Dala office lay in Longshan County under the Baojing Pacification Commission. In the fifteenth year of the Zhengde reign (1520), the native sub-official Peng Hui was appointed to assist in patrol and inspection duties. The line passed to Peng Yuji, who surrendered his territory in the thirteenth year of the Yongzheng reign (1735).
49
桑植宣慰司，本慈利縣地。 元有上桑植、下桑植宣慰司。 明置安撫司。 清順治四年，宣慰司向鼎歸附，授原職。 鼎子長庚調鎮古州八萬。 長庚子向國棟殘虐，與容美、永順、茅岡各土司相仇殺，民不堪命。 雍正四年，土經歷唐宗聖與國棟弟國柄等相率赴愬，總督傅敏入奏，乃繳追印篆，國棟安置河南，以其地為桑植縣。
The Sangzhi Pacification Commission occupied land that had once belonged to Cili County. Under the Yuan there had been separate Upper and Lower Sangzhi pacification commissions. The Ming consolidated the office into a single pacification commission. In the fourth year of the Shunzhi reign (1647), Pacification Commissioner Xiang Ding submitted and was confirmed in his former office. Ding's son Chang Geng was transferred to command the garrison at Guzhou and Bawan. Chang Geng's son Xiang Guodong ruled with vicious cruelty, feuding and killing with the chieftains of Rongmei, Yongshun, Maogang, and others until the people could endure no more. In the fourth year of the Yongzheng reign (1726), the native director Tang Zongsheng led Guodong's younger brother Guobing and others to lodge a collective complaint. Governor-General Fu Min memorialized the court, the commission seal was confiscated, Guodong was resettled in Henan, and the territory was reorganized as Sangzhi County.
50
上下峒長官司，明置宣撫司，復改為長官司，而分其地為二。 清康熙二年，向九鸞、向日葵歸附。 二十一年，給九鸞上峒長官司印，日葵下峒長官司印。 雍正十三年，上峒司向玉衡、下峒司向良佐納土，以其地屬桑植縣。
The upper and lower dong native chief offices had been a single pacification commission under the Ming, later reduced to chief offices with the land split in two. In the second year of the Kangxi reign (1663), Xiang Jiuluan and Xiang Rikui submitted. In the twenty-first year (1682), Jiuluan received the seal of upper-dong native chief, and Rikui the seal of lower-dong native chief. In the thirteenth year of the Yongzheng reign (1735), Upper Dong chief Xiang Yuheng and Lower Dong chief Xiang Liangzuo surrendered their territories, which were placed under Sangzhi County.
51
茅岡長官司，明改天平千戶所。 清順治四年，石門天平所千戶覃祚昌、茅岡長官覃廕祚等相繼歸附，給與印信。 雍正十二年，茅岡土司覃純一納土，石門天平所、慈利麻寮所相繼請設流官，分其地屬石門、慈利、安福三縣。
The Maogang native chief office had been reorganized under the Ming as the Tianping Guard post. In the fourth year of the Shunzhi reign (1647), Qin Zuochang, commander of the Tianping Guard at Shimen, and Maogang chief Qin Yinzuo submitted in turn and received official seals. In the twelfth year of the Yongzheng reign (1734), Maogang chieftain Qin Chunyi surrendered his territory. The Tianping Guard at Shimen and the Maliao Guard in Cili then petitioned for regular officials, and the land was divided among Shimen, Cili, and Anfu counties.
