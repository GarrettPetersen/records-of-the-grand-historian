1
王杰，字偉人，陝西韓城人。 以拔貢考銓藍田教諭，未任，遭父喪，貧甚，為書記以養母。 歷佐兩江總督尹繼善、江蘇巡撫陳宏謀幕，皆重之。 初從武功孫景烈遊，講濂、洛、關、閩之學； 及見宏謀，學益進，自謂生平行己居官得力於此。
Wang Jie, whose courtesy name was Weiren, came from Hancheng in Shaanxi. Selected as a tribute scholar, he qualified in examination for the Lantian district tutorship but never took up the post. After his father's death left him destitute, he earned a living as a secretary to support his mother. He served in turn on the staffs of Liangjiang Governor-General Yin Jishan and Jiangsu Governor Chen Hongmou, and both men valued him highly. Early on he studied under Sun Jinglie of Wugong, taking up the Neo-Confucian traditions associated with Zhou Dunyi, the Cheng brothers, Zhang Zai, and Zhu Xi; Once he came under Chen Hongmou's influence his learning deepened further, and he credited this training with whatever strength he showed in personal conduct and public service.
2
乾隆二十六年，成進士，殿試進呈卷列第三。 高宗熟視字體如素識，以昔為尹繼善繕疏，曾邀宸賞，詢知人品，即拔置第一。 及引見，風度凝然，上益喜。 又以陝人入本朝百餘年無大魁者，時值西陲戡定，魁選適得西人，御製詩以紀其事。 尋直南書房，屢司文柄。 五遷至內閣學士。 三十九年，授刑部侍郎，調吏部，擢左都御史。 四十八年，丁母憂，即家擢兵部尚書。 車駕南巡，傑赴行在謝，上曰：「汝來甚好。 君臣久別，應知朕念汝。 然汝儒者，不欲奪汝情，歸終制可也。 」服闋，還朝。 五十一年，命為軍機大臣、上書房總師傅。 次年，拜東閣大學士，管理禮部。 台灣、廓爾喀先後平，兩次圖形紫光閣，加太子太保。
In 1761 he passed the metropolitan examination and took his jinshi degree; the palace examination paper presented to the throne placed him third. Emperor Qianlong studied the script at length as though it were familiar, remembered that Jie had once drafted memorials for Yin Jishan to imperial acclaim, asked about his character, and at once promoted him to first place. At his audience his bearing was grave and steady, and the emperor was all the more delighted. Moreover, in the century and more since the dynasty's rise no Shaanxi native had taken first place in the palace examination; with the western campaigns just concluded and the top honor falling by chance to a man from the west, the emperor wrote an imperial poem to commemorate it. He was soon assigned to the Southern Study and repeatedly put in charge of civil examinations. Five promotions later he had risen to grand secretary of the Grand Secretariat. In 1774 he was made vice minister of punishments, transferred to the Board of Personnel, and promoted to left censor-in-chief. In 1783, while at home in mourning for his mother, he was promoted to minister of war without returning to the capital. During the southern tour Jie came to the temporary palace to express his gratitude; the emperor said, "It is very good that you have come. Monarch and subject have been long parted—you should know that I have been thinking of you. But you are a scholar; I do not wish to tear you from your filial duty—go home and finish your mourning." When his mourning was complete, he returned to the capital. In 1786 he was appointed a grand councilor and chief tutor of the Upper Study. The next year he was made grand secretary of the Eastern Pavilion and given charge of the Ministry of Rites. After the successive pacification of Taiwan and the Gurkhas, his portrait was twice placed in the Hall of Purple Glaze, and he received the additional title of grand guardian of the heir apparent.
3
傑在樞廷十餘年，事有可否，未嘗不委曲陳奏。 和珅勢方赫，事多擅決，同列隱忍不言，傑遇有不可，輒力爭。 上知之深，和珅雖厭之而不能去。 傑每議政畢，默然獨坐。 一日，和珅執其手戲曰：「何柔荑乃爾！ 」傑正色曰：「王杰手雖好，但不能要錢耳！ 」和珅赧然。 嘉慶元年，以足疾乞免軍機、書房及管理部事，允之。 有大事，上必諮詢，傑亦不時入告。
Jie served on the Grand Council for more than ten years; on every matter that admitted approval or objection, he invariably laid out his views with careful tact in memorial. Heshen's influence was then at its zenith and he often decided affairs on his own; his colleagues swallowed their objections, but whenever Jie found a matter unacceptable he would argue it out. The emperor understood this well; though Heshen resented him, he could not drive him out. After each council session Jie would sit apart in silence. One day Heshen seized his hand in jest and said, "What delicate hands these are! " Jie replied gravely, "Wang Jie's hands may be fair, but they cannot take bribes!" " Heshen flushed with embarrassment. In 1796, pleading foot trouble, he asked to be excused from the Grand Council, the study, and his ministerial duties; the request was granted. On weighty affairs the emperor still sought his counsel, and Jie would also come in from time to time to report.
4
時教匪方熾，傑疏言：「賊匪剿滅稽遲，由被賊災民窮無倚賴，地方官不能勞來安輯，以致脅從日眾，兵力日單而賊焰日熾。 此時當安良民以解從賊之心，撫官兵以勵行間之氣。 三年之內，川、楚、秦、豫四省殺傷不下數百萬，其倖存而不從賊者，亦皆鋒鏑之餘，男不暇耕，女不暇織。 若再計畝徵輸，甚至分外加派，胥吏因緣勒索，艱苦情形無由上達聖主之前。 祈將被賊地方錢糧蠲免，不令官吏舞弊重徵，有來歸者概勿窮治，賊勢或可漸孤矣。 至於用兵三載未即成功，實由將帥有所依恃，怠玩因循，非盡士卒之不用命也。 乞頒發諭旨，曲加憐恤，有驕惰不馴者，令經略概行撤回，或就近更調召募，申明紀律，鼓行勵戎，庶幾人有挾纊之歡，眾有成城之志。 」又言：「教匪之蔓延，其弊有二：一由統領之有名無實。 勒保雖為統領，而統兵大員名位相等，人人得專摺奏事，於是賊至則畏避不前，賊去則捏稱得勝。 即如前歲賊竄興安，領兵大員有'匪已渡江五日，地方官並不禀報'之奏，此其畏避情形顯而易見。 又如去歲賊擾西安城南，殺傷數万，官兵既不近賊，撫臣一無設施； 探知賊去已遠，然後虛張聲勢，名為追賊，實未見賊。 近聞張漢潮蔓延商、雒，高均德屯據洋縣，往來衝突，如入無人之境。 秦省如此，川省可知。 實由統領不專、賞罰不明之所致也。 一由領兵大員專恃鄉勇。 鄉勇陣亡，無庸報部，人數可以虛捏； 藉鄉勇為前陣，既可免官兵之傷亡，又可為異日之開銷，此所以耗國帑而無可稽核也。 臣以為軍務緊要，莫急於去鄉勇之名而為召募之實，蓋有五利：一，民窮無依，多半從賊，苟延性命，募而為兵，即有口糧，多一為兵之人，即少一從賊之人； 一，隔省徵調，曠日持久，就近召募，則旬日可得； 一，徵兵遠來，筋力已疲，召募之人，不須跋涉； 一，隔省之兵，水土不習，路徑不諳，就近之人，則不慮此； 一，鄉勇勢不能敵，則逃散無從懲治，召募之兵退避，則有軍法。 具此五利，何不增募，一鼓而殲賊？ 如謂兵多費多，獨不思一萬兵食十月之糧，與十萬兵食一月之糧，其費相等而功可早奏也。 」疏入，並被採用。
While the White Lotus rebels were at their height, Jie submitted a memorial: "The slow progress in wiping out the rebels stems from the fact that people ruined by the rebellion are destitute and without refuge, while local officials fail to relieve and settle them, so that those coerced into joining the rebels grow daily, the army grows thinner day by day, and rebel power burns brighter. At this juncture the government should reassure law-abiding people so as to loosen their attachment to the rebels, and care for the troops so as to raise fighting spirit in the field. Within three years in Sichuan, Huguang, Shaanxi, and Henan the dead and wounded numbered no less than several million; even survivors who had not joined the rebels were left shattered by war—men had no time to farm, women no time to weave. If land tax is again assessed acre by acre, with surcharges piled on by quota, and clerks use the chance to squeeze them, their misery will never reach Your Majesty's ears. I beg that taxes and grain levies in rebel-ravaged districts be remitted, that officials be barred from fraudulent over-collection, and that deserters who return home be left unpunished—then the rebels may gradually be cut off. As for three years of war without decisive victory, the real cause is that commanders have patrons to shield them and grow slack and dilatory—it is not simply that the rank and file refuse to fight. I ask that an edict go forth showing them special kindness: let the commander-in-chief recall every arrogant, idle, or insubordinate officer, replace them with local recruits if need be, enforce discipline, and rouse the army—then the men may feel the comfort of a winter coat and the host the spirit to stand as one wall. " He went on: "The rebels' spread has two root causes. First, the supreme commander holds a title without real command. Though Lebao was named commander-in-chief, the field generals were his equals in rank, and each could memorialize the throne on his own; when the rebels appeared they hung back in fear, and when the rebels withdrew they invented victories. Last year, for instance, when rebels broke into Xing'an, one field commander reported that the bandits had crossed the river five days earlier while local officials had not notified him—how plainly this shows their timidity and evasion. Again, last year when rebels ravaged the country south of Xi'an, tens of thousands were killed or wounded; the troops never closed with the enemy, and the provincial governor did nothing at all; only after scouts reported the rebels far away did they raise a great clamor in the name of pursuit, though in truth they never sighted the enemy. Lately Zhang Hanchao has spread through Shangzhou and Luozhou, while Gao Junde holds Yang County; they strike back and forth as though no one stood in their way. If Shaanxi is in this state, one can imagine what Sichuan must be like. This truly comes of a commander without sole authority and of rewards and punishments left unclear. Second, field commanders rely exclusively on local militia. Militia casualties need not be reported to the Board of War, so head counts can be invented; using militia as the vanguard both keeps regular troops out of harm's way and pads future expense accounts—hence the treasury bleeds dry with nothing to audit. I hold that in the present crisis nothing is more urgent than to end the militia fiction and recruit real soldiers. There are five advantages. First, the destitute, with nowhere to turn, mostly join the rebels merely to survive; recruit them as soldiers and they receive rations—every man enlisted is one fewer rebel. Second, troops summoned from distant provinces take months to arrive, whereas local recruits can be raised in ten days; Third, conscripts marched in from afar arrive exhausted, while local recruits need make no long march; Fourth, out-of-province soldiers are unaccustomed to climate and terrain and ignorant of the roads, whereas local men face no such handicap; Fifth, when militia cannot stand against the enemy they scatter without penalty, but regular recruits who flee can be punished under military law. With these five advantages in view, why not expand recruitment and crush the rebels at a blow? If the objection is that more troops mean more expense, consider that ten thousand men fed for ten months and one hundred thousand men fed for one month cost the same, yet victory may be won far sooner. " When the memorial reached the throne, its recommendations were all adopted.
5
二年，復召直軍機，隨扈熱河。 未幾，因腿疾，詔毋庸入直，先行回京。 三年秋，川匪王三槐就擒，封賞樞臣，詔：「傑現雖未直軍機，軍興曾有贊畫功，並予優敘。 」
In 1797 he was recalled to regular Grand Council duty and accompanied the court to Rehe. Before long, on account of leg trouble, an edict excused him from regular attendance and he went back to Beijing ahead of the court. In the autumn of 1798 the Sichuan rebel Wang Sanhuai was captured; the grand councilors were ennobled and rewarded. An edict read: "Although Jie is not now on regular council duty, he rendered planning service when the war began and shall receive preferential recognition as well. "
6
洎仁宗親政，傑為首輔，遇事持大體，竭誠進諫，上優禮之。 五年，以衰病乞休，溫詔慰留，許扶杖入朝。 七年，固請致仕，晉太子太傅，在籍食俸。 八年春，瀕行上疏，略謂：「各省虧空之弊，起於乾隆四十年以後，州縣營求餽送，以國帑為夤緣，上司受其挾制，彌補無期。 至嘉慶四年以後，大吏知尚廉節，州縣仍形拮据，由於苦樂不均，賢否不分，宜求整飭之法。 又，舊制，驛丞專司驛站，無可誅求。 自裁歸州縣，濫支苛派，官民俱病。 宜先清驛站，以杜虧空。 今當軍務告竣，朝廷勤求治理，無大於此二者。 請睿裁獨斷，以挽積重之勢。 」所言切中時弊，上嘉納之。 陛辭日，賜高宗御用玉鳩杖、御製詩二章，以寵其行，有云：「直道一身立廊廟，清風兩袖返韓城。 」時論謂足盡其生平。 既歸，歲時頒賞不絕，每有陳奏，上輒親批答，語如家人。
Once the Jiaqing Emperor took personal rule, Jie became chief minister; he kept to broad principle in state affairs, remonstrated with wholehearted sincerity, and the emperor honored him with exceptional courtesy. In 1800, pleading age and infirmity, he asked to retire; a gracious edict urged him to stay and permitted him to attend court with a walking staff. In 1802 he pressed his request to retire; he was promoted to grand tutor of the heir apparent and granted salary while living at home. In the spring of 1803, on the eve of his departure, he submitted a farewell memorial stating in essence: "Provincial deficits began after 1775, when magistrates scrambled for gifts and used the public purse to buy favor; their superiors were held hostage to them, and the holes could never be filled. After 1799 the high officials learned to prize integrity, yet the magistrates remained hard pressed, because hardship and ease were unevenly shared and the capable were not distinguished from the corrupt—remedies for this must be found. Moreover, under the old system post commissioners managed the stations alone and had no opportunity for extortion. Since those posts were abolished and their duties folded into the magistracies, reckless spending and harsh levies have afflicted officials and commoners alike. The post system should be put in order first, as the surest way to stop the deficits. Now that the war is over and the court is earnestly seeking good government, nothing matters more than these two reforms. I beg Your Majesty to decide these matters yourself and turn back this deeply entrenched decline. " His words went straight to the abuses of the day, and the emperor commended and accepted them. On the day he took leave of the throne he received the jade dove staff Qianlong had used in person and two imperial poems to honor his departure, including the lines: "Upright in office he stood alone in the hall of state; with empty sleeves and a clear name he returns to Hancheng." " Contemporary opinion held that these lines summed up his life. After his return home, seasonal gifts from the court never ceased; whenever he wrote in, the emperor answered in his own hand in tones as familiar as family speech.
7
九年，傑與妻程並年八十，命巡撫方維甸齎御​​製詩、額、珍物，於生日就賜其家。 傑詣闕謝，明年正月，卒於京邸。 上悼惜，賜金治喪，贈太子太師，祀賢良祠，諡文端。
In 1804 Jie and his wife, née Cheng, both turned eighty; the emperor ordered Governor Fang Weidian to bring imperial poems, a commemorative plaque, and precious gifts and present them at their home on their birthday. Jie came to the capital to express his thanks; in the first month of the following year he died at his Beijing residence. The emperor grieved his loss, granted gold for the funeral, posthumously appointed him grand preceptor of the heir apparent, had him enshrined in the Shrine of Worthy Officials, and gave him the posthumous title Wenduan (Cultured and Upright).
8
傑體不逾中人，和靄近情，而持守剛正，歷事兩朝，以忠直結主知。 當致仕未行，會有陳德於禁城驚犯乘輿，急趨朝請對曰：「德庖廚賤役，安敢妄蓄逆謀？ 此必有元姦大憝主使行明張差之事，當除肘腋之患。 」至十八年林清逆黨之變，上思其言，特賜祭焉。
Jie was no taller than an average man, affable and warm in manner, yet unyielding in principle; serving two emperors, he won their trust through loyal, forthright service. Though he had retired but not yet left the capital, Chen De suddenly attacked the imperial carriage within the Forbidden City. Jie rushed to court and begged an audience, saying: "Chen De is a low kitchen servant—how could he dare harbor treason on his own? Some great villain behind the scenes must be directing this, as in the notorious Zhang Cha affair—we must root out the danger at the emperor's very side." " When the Lin Qing rebellion broke out in 1813, the emperor recalled his warning and ordered a special sacrifice in his honor.
9
孫篤，道光二年進士，歷編修、御史，出為汀州知府、廣東督糧道，署鹽運使。 時林則徐為按察使，治海防，甚倚之。 募廣州遊手精壯者備守禦，以機敏稱。 擢山東布政使，署巡撫。 失察家人、屬官受賂，連降罷職歸，襄理西安城工。 卒，贈布政使銜。
His grandson Du took his jinshi degree in 1822, rose through compiler and censor, and served outside the capital as prefect of Tingzhou, Guangdong grain intendant, and acting salt commissioner. At the time Lin Zexu was provincial judicial commissioner in charge of coastal defense and relied on him heavily. He recruited able-bodied Guangzhou street men for coastal defense and was noted for his resourcefulness. He was promoted to Shandong provincial treasurer and served as acting governor. He was punished for failing to detect bribery by his household and subordinates, was demoted and dismissed, returned home, and helped supervise the Xi'an city wall project. He died and was posthumously granted the rank of provincial treasurer.
10
董誥，字蔗林，浙江富陽人，尚書邦達子。 乾隆二十八年進士，殿試進呈卷列第三，高宗因大臣子，改二甲第一。 選庶吉士，即預修國史、三通、皇朝禮器圖。 散館，授編修。 三十二年，命入懋勤殿寫金字經為皇太后祝嘏。 次年，大考翰詹，因寫經未與試，特加一級。 尋擢中允，丁父憂。 三十六年，服闋，入直南書房。 初，邦達善畫，受高宗知。 誥承家學，繼為侍從，書畫亦被宸賞，尤以奉職恪勤為上所眷注。 累遷內閣學士。 四十年，擢工部侍郎，調戶部，歷署吏、刑兩部侍郎，兼管樂部。 充四庫館副總裁，接辦全書薈要，命輯滿洲源流考。 四十四年，命為軍機大臣。 五十二年，加太子少保，擢戶部尚書。 台灣、廓爾喀先後底定，並列功臣，圖形紫光閣。
Dong Gao, whose courtesy name was Zhelin, came from Fuyang in Zhejiang and was the son of Minister Dong Bada. He took his jinshi degree in 1763; his palace examination paper ranked third, but because he was a minister's son Qianlong moved him to first place in the second class. Selected as a Hanlin bachelor, he immediately joined work on the national history, the Three Comprehensive Works, and the Illustrated Rites Implements of the Dynasty. After completing his Hanlin probation he was appointed a compiler. In 1767 he was ordered to the Hall of Cultivated Diligence to copy the golden-letter sutra in celebration of the empress dowager's birthday. The next year, in the triennial examination of Hanlin and academy officials, he was excused from testing because of the sutra work and was specially promoted one rank. He was soon promoted to middle secretary of the heir apparent, then entered mourning for his father. In 1771, when his mourning was complete, he was assigned to regular duty in the Southern Study. Earlier, Bada had been a skilled painter and had won Qianlong's favor. Gao inherited his father's art, continued as a court attendant, and his calligraphy and painting likewise won imperial praise; above all, the emperor favored him for his scrupulous diligence in office. He rose through repeated promotions to grand secretary of the Grand Secretariat. In 1775 he was made vice minister of works, transferred to revenue, served in turn as acting vice minister of personnel and punishments, and also oversaw the Board of Music. He served as deputy director of the Siku Library project, took charge of the Essentials of the Complete Library, and was ordered to compile the Study of Manchu Origins. In 1779 he was appointed a grand councilor. In 1787 he received the additional title of junior guardian of the heir apparent and was promoted to minister of revenue. After Taiwan and the Gurkhas were pacified in turn, he was named among the meritorious officials and his portrait was placed in the Hall of Purple Glaze.
11
嘉慶元年，授受禮成，詔硃珪來京，將畀以閣務，仁宗賀以詩。 屬稿未竟，和珅取白高宗曰：「嗣皇帝欲市恩於師傅。 」高宗色動，顧誥曰：「汝在軍機、刑部久，是於律意云何？ 」誥叩頭曰：「聖主無過言。 」高宗默然良久，曰：「汝大臣也，善為朕輔導之。 」乃以他事罷珪之召。 時大學士懸缺久，難其人。 高宗謂劉墉、紀昀、彭元瑞三人皆資深，墉遇事模棱，元瑞以不檢獲愆，昀讀書多而不明理，惟誥在直勤勉，超拜東閣大學士，明詔宣示，俾三人加愧勵焉。 命總理禮部，仍兼管戶部事。 二年，丁生母憂，特賜陀羅經被，遣御前侍衛、額駙丰紳殷德奠醊。
In 1796, when the succession rites were complete, an edict summoned Zhu Gui to the capital for a grand secretariat post; the Jiaqing Emperor congratulated him with a poem. Before the draft was finished, Heshen took it to Qianlong and said, "The heir apparent means to curry favor with his tutor." " Qianlong's expression changed. Turning to Gao he said, "You have long served on the Grand Council and at the Board of Punishments—what does the law say of this?" " Gao kowtowed and said, "The sage sovereign's words are never in error." " Qianlong was silent a long while, then said, "You are a senior minister—guide him well for Us." " Zhu Gui's summons was then cancelled on another pretext. A grand secretary post had long stood vacant, and no suitable appointee could be found. Qianlong said Liu Yong, Ji Yun, and Peng Yuanrui were all senior: Liu was evasive in office, Peng had been censured for misconduct, and Ji read widely but lacked sound judgment—only Gao was diligent in regular attendance. He was therefore promoted over them to grand secretary of the Eastern Pavilion, with an explicit edict meant to shame and spur the three. He was put in charge of the Ministry of Rites while continuing to oversee revenue affairs. In 1797, while mourning his birth mother, he was specially granted a Buddhist sutra quilt, and imperial guards including the emperor's son-in-law Fengshen Yinde were sent to offer libations.
12
誥既以喪歸，川、楚兵事方亟，高宗欲召之，每見大臣，數問； 「董誥何時來？ 」逾年，葬母畢，詣京師，和珅遏不上聞。 會駕出，誥於道旁謝恩，高宗見之，喜甚，命暫署刑部尚書，素服視事，不預典禮，專辦秋讞及軍營紀略，且曰：「誥守制已逾小祥，不得已用人之苦心，眾當共諒。 」尋以王三槐就擒，與軍機大臣同被議敘。 四年春，高宗崩，和珅伏誅，命誥复直軍機，晉太子太保。 既，服闋，授文華殿大學士，兼刑部尚書如故。 高宗山陵禮成，命題神主，晉太子太傅。 七年，三省教匪平，予騎都尉世職。 十二年，高宗實錄告成，詔以誥在館八年，始終其事，特加優獎，賜其父邦達入祀賢良祠。 十四年，萬壽慶典，晉太子太師。 充上書房總師傅。 十七年，晉太保。
After Gao went home to mourn, the Sichuan and Huguang campaigns were pressing; Qianlong wished to recall him and whenever he met senior ministers asked repeatedly, "When will Dong Gao return?" " A year later, when his mother's burial was finished, he came to Beijing, but Heshen prevented word from reaching the throne. When the imperial procession passed, Gao thanked him from the roadside. Qianlong saw him and was delighted, ordered him to act temporarily as minister of punishments, handle affairs in plain mourning garb without attending ceremonies, and focus on autumn judicial reviews and military chronicles, saying, "Gao's mourning has passed the lesser anniversary—I employ him from state necessity; all should understand." " Soon after Wang Sanhuai was captured, he received preferential recognition along with the grand councilors. In the spring of 1799 Qianlong died and Heshen was executed; Gao was restored to regular Grand Council duty and promoted to grand guardian of the heir apparent. When his mourning ended he was appointed grand secretary of the Hall of Literary Glory while retaining the ministry of punishments. When Qianlong's mausoleum rites were complete, he was ordered to compose the spirit-tablet inscription and was promoted to grand tutor of the heir apparent. In 1802, when the sect rebels in the three provinces were pacified, he was granted the hereditary rank of cavalry commandant. In 1807, when Qianlong's veritable records were completed, an edict noted that Gao had spent eight years in the directorate and saw the project through; he received special reward, and his father Bada was admitted to the Shrine of Worthy Officials. In 1809, at the longevity celebration, he was promoted to grand preceptor of the heir apparent. He was appointed chief tutor of the Upper Study. In 1812 he was promoted to grand mentor.
13
十八年，扈從秋狝。 林清逆黨突入禁城，時回鑾，中途聞變，有議俟調大兵成列而後進者，誥曰：「是滋亂也，獻俘者行至矣！ 」即日扈駕進次，人心乃定。 窮治邪教，誥謂：「燒香祈福，愚民無知，率所常有。 惟從逆者不可貸。 」凡論上，皆以是定讞。 林清既誅，滑縣逆匪尋平，論功，迭被優敘，賜子淳為郎中。 二十年，因病請致政，溫詔慰留，改管兵部。 未幾，復命管刑部。 二十三年，再疏乞休，許致仕食全俸。 是年十月，卒，贈太傅。 上親奠，入祀賢良祠，賜金治喪，御製詩輓之，嘉其父子歷事三朝，未嘗增置一畝之田、一椽之屋，命刻詩於墓，以彰忠藎。 諡文恭。
In 1813 he accompanied the autumn hunt at Mulan. Lin Qing's rebels broke into the Forbidden City. The court was returning from the hunt and heard the news en route; some urged waiting until large forces were drawn up in formation before advancing. Gao said, "That would only spread panic—the men bringing captives are already on their way!" " That same day he accompanied the emperor forward, and public alarm subsided. In prosecuting the heterodox sects, Gao said, "Burning incense to pray for blessings is something ignorant common people commonly do. Only those who took part in the rebellion must not be spared." " In every sentencing recommendation this principle governed the final judgment. After Lin Qing was executed the Huaxian rebels were soon suppressed; for his merit he received repeated preferential rewards, and his son Chun was granted the rank of director. In 1815, pleading illness, he asked to retire; a gracious edict urged him to remain and transferred his portfolio to the Ministry of War. Before long he was again put in charge of the Ministry of Punishments. In 1818 he memorialized again to retire and was permitted to leave office on full salary. In the tenth month of that year he died and was posthumously made grand tutor. The emperor offered libation in person, had him enshrined in the Shrine of Worthy Officials, granted gold for the funeral, and composed an elegiac poem praising father and son for serving three reigns without acquiring an extra mu of land or a single extra rafter of roof, ordering the poem carved on the tomb to honor their loyal service. He was given the posthumous title Wengong (Cultured and Respectful).
14
誥直軍機先後四十年，熟於朝章故事，有以諮者，無不悉。 凡所獻納皆面陳，未嘗用奏牘。 當和珅用事，與王杰支柱其間，獨居深念，行處幾失常度，卒贊仁宗殲除大憝。 及林清之變，獨持鎮定，尤為時稱雲。
Gao served on regular Grand Council duty for forty years in all, thoroughly versed in court regulations and precedent; whatever he was asked, he knew completely. Whatever he offered in counsel he stated face to face and never put in written memorials. While Heshen held power he and Wang Jie bore up the government between them; alone he brooded deeply and at times nearly lost his composure, yet in the end helped the Jiaqing Emperor destroy the great villain. At the Lin Qing disturbance he alone kept his composure, which contemporaries especially praised.
15
硃珪，字石君，順天大興人。 先世居蕭山，自父文炳始遷籍。 文炳官盩厔知縣，曾受經於大學士硃軾。 珪少傳軾學，與兄筠同鄉舉，並負時譽。 乾隆十三年成進士，年甫十八，選庶吉士，散館授編修。 數遇典禮，撰進文冊。 高宗重其學行，累遷侍讀學士。 二十五年，出為福建糧驛道。 擢按察使，治獄平恕，以父憂去。 三十二年，補湖北按察使。 會緬甸用兵，以部署驛務詳慎，被褒獎。
Zhu Gui, whose courtesy name was Shijun, came from Daxing in Shuntian prefecture. His family had lived in Xiaoshan; his father Wenbing was the first to transfer their household registration. Wenbing served as magistrate of Zhouzhi and had studied the classics under Grand Secretary Zhu Shi. Gui in youth inherited Shi's learning; he and his elder brother Yun passed the provincial examination together, and both enjoyed high repute. In 1748 he took his jinshi degree at only eighteen, was selected as a Hanlin bachelor, and after completing his probation was appointed a compiler. He took part in many state ceremonies and drafted the memorial documents presented on those occasions. Qianlong valued his learning and character and repeatedly promoted him to reader of the heir apparent. In 1760 he was posted as Fujian grain and courier intendant. He was promoted to provincial judicial commissioner, where he judged cases with fairness and leniency, then left office to mourn his father. In 1767 he was appointed Hubei provincial judicial commissioner. During the Burma campaign his careful organization of courier services won praise.
16
調山西，就遷布政使，署巡撫。 疏請歸化、綏遠二城穀二萬餘石搭放兵糧，以省採買、免紅朽； 又免土默特蒙古私墾罪，以所墾牧地三千餘頃，許附近兵民認耕納租，歲六千餘兩，增官兵公費； 又太僕寺牧地苦寒，改徵折色，以便民除弊； 皆下部議行。 珪方正，為同僚所不便，按察使黃檢奏劾讀書廢事。
Transferred to Shanxi, he was soon promoted provincial treasurer and served as acting governor. He memorialized to lend out more than twenty thousand dan of grain stored at Guihua and Suiyuan for military rations, saving purchase costs and preventing spoilage; also pardoned Tumed Mongols for unauthorized cultivation and allowed nearby soldiers and civilians to claim and farm more than three thousand qing of reclaimed pasture, paying more than six thousand taels in annual rent to augment official expenses; also, because the imperial stud pastures were bitterly cold, he changed collection to cash payment for the people's convenience and to remove abuses; all were referred to the ministries for deliberation and implementation. Gui was upright and awkward for his colleagues; provincial judicial commissioner Huang Jian memorialized that he neglected his duties through excessive reading.
17
四十年，召入覲，改授侍講學士，直上書房，侍仁宗學。 四十四年，典福建鄉試。 次年，督福建學政。 瀕行，上五箴於仁宗：曰養心，曰敬身，曰勤業，曰虛己，曰致誠。 仁宗力行之，後親政，嘗置左右。 五十一年，擢禮部侍郎，典江南鄉試，督浙江學政。 還朝，調兵部。 五十五年，典會試。 出為安徽巡撫。 皖北水災，馳驛往賑，攜僕數人，與村民同舟渡，賑宿州、泗州、碭山、靈壁、五河、盱眙餘災，輕者貸以糧種。 築決堤，展春賑，並躬蒞其事，民無流亡。 五十九年，調廣東。 尋署兩廣總督，授左都御史、兵部尚書，仍留巡撫任。 嘉慶元年，授總督，兼署巡撫。 珪初以文學受知，洎出任疆寄，負時望，將大用。 和珅忌之，授受禮成，珪進頌冊，因加指摘，高宗曰：「陳善納誨，師傅之職宜爾，非汝所知也。 」會大學士缺，詔召珪，卒為和珅所沮。 以廣東艇匪擾劫閩、浙，責珪不能緝捕，寢前命，左遷安徽巡撫。 皖北复災，親治賑，官吏無侵蝕。 三省教匪起，安徽亦多伏莽。 珪曰：「疑而索之，是激之變。 」親駐界上籌防禦，遍蒞潁、亳所屬，集鄉老教誡之，民感化，境內迄無事。 明年，授兵部尚書，調吏部，仍留巡撫任。
In 1775 he was summoned to audience, appointed expositor, and assigned directly to the Upper Study to tutor the future Jiaqing Emperor. In 1779 he presided over the Fujian provincial examination. The following year he was appointed Fujian education intendant. On leaving he presented five admonitions to the heir apparent: cultivate the mind, respect the person, be diligent in duty, empty the self, and practice utmost sincerity. The future emperor practiced them earnestly; after taking personal rule he often kept them at hand. In 1786 he was promoted to vice minister of rites, presided over the Jiangnan provincial examination, and supervised Zhejiang education. On returning to court he was transferred to the Ministry of War. In 1790 he presided over the metropolitan examination. He was posted as governor of Anhui. When northern Anhui was flooded he rode post haste to relieve the victims, taking only a few servants and crossing in the same boats as the villagers; he relieved lingering disaster in Suzhou, Sizhou, Dangshan, Lingbi, Wuhe, and Xuyi, lending seed grain to the less severely affected. He repaired breached dikes, extended spring relief, and personally oversaw the work, so that the people did not flee. In 1794 he was transferred to Guangdong. Soon he served as acting Liangguang governor-general, was appointed left censor-in-chief and minister of war, and retained the governorship. In 1796 he was appointed governor-general while also acting as governor. Gui had first won notice through his scholarship; when he took up frontier duty he bore high expectations and seemed destined for the highest office. Heshen envied him. When the succession rites were complete, Gui presented a congratulatory scroll, and Heshen seized the chance to criticize him. Qianlong said, "To offer good counsel and accept remonstrance is a tutor's proper duty—not something you would understand." " When a grand secretary post fell vacant, an edict summoned Gui, but Heshen blocked the appointment in the end. Because Guangdong boat bandits raided Fujian and Zhejiang, Gui was blamed for failing to capture them; the earlier appointment was shelved, and he was demoted to governor of Anhui. When northern Anhui was flooded again, he personally managed relief, and officials did not embezzle funds. When sect rebels rose in three provinces, Anhui also harbored many hidden bandits. Gui said, "To suspect people and hunt them down is to provoke rebellion." " He personally stationed on the border to plan defense, toured every district under Yingzhou and Bozhou, gathered village elders to instruct them, and the people were won over; within the province there was no trouble. The following year he was appointed minister of war, transferred to the Board of Personnel, and retained the governorship.
18
四年正月，高宗崩，仁宗即馳驛召珪，聞命奔赴。 途中上疏，略曰：「天子之孝，以繼志述事為大。 親政伊始，遠聽近瞻，默運乾綱，雱施渙號。 陽剛之氣，如日重光，惻怛之仁，無幽不浹。 修身則嚴誠欺之界，觀人則辨義利之防。 君心正而四維張，朝廷清而九牧肅。 身先節儉，崇獎清廉，自然盜賊不足平，財用不足阜。 惟原皇上無忘堯、舜自​​任之心，臣敢不勉行義事君之道。 」至京哭臨，上執珪手哭失聲。 命直南書房，管戶部三庫，加太子少保，賜第西華門外。 時召獨對，用人行政悉以諮之。 珪造膝密陳，不關白軍機大臣，不沽恩市直，上傾心一聽，初政之美，多出贊助。
In the first month of 1799 Qianlong died; the Jiaqing Emperor immediately sent post to summon Gui, who hurried to the capital on receiving the order. En route he submitted a memorial stating in essence: "The Son of Heaven's filial piety consists chiefly in continuing the late emperor's will and carrying forward his work. At the start of your personal rule, listen far and near, silently wield the imperial authority, and issue broad, sustaining edicts. Let your yang force shine like the sun redoubled in light; let compassionate benevolence reach every hidden corner. In self-cultivation draw a strict line against deceit; in judging men distinguish the boundary between righteousness and profit. When the ruler's mind is upright the four pillars of state stand firm; when the court is pure the nine provinces are orderly. Take the lead in frugality and honor upright officials, and bandits will be subdued and revenues enriched without extraordinary effort. Only pray Your Majesty never forget the resolve to measure yourself against Yao and Shun; your servant dares not fail to practice righteous service to the throne." " At the capital mourning audience the emperor took Gui's hand and wept aloud. He was assigned to regular duty in the Southern Study, put in charge of the three revenue treasuries, granted the title of junior guardian of the heir apparent, and given a residence outside Xihua Gate. He was often summoned for private audiences; appointments and administration were all referred to him. Gui spoke confidentially at the emperor's knee, did not inform the grand councilors, and did not court favor by direct appeal; the emperor listened with full trust, and much of the excellence of the early reign owed to his counsel.
19
尋充上書房總師傅，調戶部尚書。 詔清漕政，禁浮收​​。 疆吏以運丁苦累，仰給州縣，州縣不得不取諸民，於是安徽加贈銀，江蘇加耗米，珪謂小民未見清漕之益，先受其害，力爭罷之，令曹司凡事近加賦者皆議駁。 長蘆鹽政請加增鹽價，駁曰：「蘆東因錢價賤，已三加價矣，且免積欠三百六十萬兩，餘欠展三年，商力已寬，無庸再議加價。 」廣東請濱海沙地升賦，駁曰：「海沙淤地，坍漲靡常，故照下則減半賦之。 今視上、中田增賦，是與民計微利，非政體。 且民苦加賦，別有漲地，將不敢報墾，不可行。 」倉場請預納錢糧四五十倍，準作義監生，駁曰：「國家正供有常經，名實關體要。 於名不正，實必傷，斷不可行。 」凡駁議每自屬稿，奏上，皆韙之。 五年，兼署吏部尚書。
Soon he was made chief tutor of the Upper Study and transferred to minister of revenue. An edict ordered reform of grain transport administration and forbade excessive collection. Frontier officials said transport laborers were overburdened and depended on the districts and counties, which in turn had to levy on the people; Anhui therefore added gift silver and Jiangsu added waste-rice surcharges. Gui argued that the common people had not yet seen any benefit from transport reform but already felt the harm; he fought vigorously to abolish these levies and ordered every office to reject proposals for new surcharges. The Changlu salt administration asked to raise the salt price again; he rejected it: "East of the Pass, because cash was cheap, the price has already been raised three times, and 3.6 million taels of arrears were remitted with the remainder extended three years—the merchants' resources are already eased; there is no need to discuss another price increase." " Guangdong asked to raise the land tax on coastal reclaimed fields; he rejected it: "Sea-silt land shifts with erosion and accretion, so it is assessed at half the lower rate." " To raise the assessment to the level of upper and middle-grade fields is to squeeze petty profit from the people, not sound government." " Moreover, the people already suffer from higher levies; if other reclaimed land exists, they will not dare report it for taxation—this must not be done." " The granary administration asked that grain tax be prepaid forty or fifty times over in exchange for charitable-student status; he rejected it: "The state's regular revenues follow fixed rules; name and reality touch the foundations of government. If the name is not correct, the reality must suffer—this absolutely cannot be done." " He usually drafted these rejections himself; when they were submitted, the emperor approved them all. In 1800 he also served as acting minister of personnel.
20
先是彭元瑞於西華門內墜馬，珪呼其輿入舁之，為御史週栻所劾。 尋有珪輿人毆傷禁門兵，忌者嗾護軍統領訐之。 詔：「珪素恪謹，造次不檢，特申戒。 」坐褫宮銜，解三庫事，鐫級留任。 七年，協辦大學士，复太子少保。 尋兼翰林院掌院學士，晉太子少傅。 九年，上幸翰林院，聯句賜宴，御書「天祿儲才」額刻懸院中，以墨書賜珪家。 十年，拜體仁閣大學士，管理工部。 上以是命遵高宗諭，遣詣裕陵謝。 逾歲，年七十六，以老乞休，溫詔慰留，賜玉鳩杖； 命天寒，間二三日入直。
Earlier, Peng Yuanrui fell from his horse inside Xihua Gate; Gui had his sedan brought in to carry him, for which Censor Zhou Shi impeached him. Soon Gui's sedan bearers beat and injured gate guards; his enemies incited the guard commander to denounce him. An edict read: "Gui is always careful and respectful; in a moment of carelessness he was not restrained—he is specially admonished." " He was stripped of his palace rank, relieved of the three treasuries, reduced in rank, but kept in office. In 1802 he was made assisting grand secretary and again received the title of junior guardian of the heir apparent. Soon he also served as chancellor of the Hanlin Academy and was promoted to junior tutor of the heir apparent. In 1804 the emperor visited the Hanlin Academy, held a joint-verse banquet, and inscribed the plaque "Heaven's Blessing Stores Talent" to be carved and hung in the academy; an ink copy was given to Gui's family. In 1805 he was made grand secretary of the Tiren Pavilion and put in charge of the Ministry of Works. Because this appointment followed Qianlong's instruction, the emperor sent him to the Yu Mausoleum to give thanks. A year later, at seventy-six, he asked to retire on account of age; a gracious edict urged him to stay and granted him a jade dove staff; and ordered that in cold weather he need attend regular duty only every two or three days.
21
未幾，召對乾清宮，眩暈，扶歸第，數日卒。 上親奠，哭之慟。 贈太傅，祀賢良祠，賜金治喪。 詔：「珪自為師傅，凡所陳說，無非唐、虞、三代之言，稍涉時趨者不出諸口，啟沃至多。 揆諸諡法，足當'正'字而無愧，特諡文正。 又見其門庭卑隘，清寒之況，不減儒素。 」命內府備筵，遣皇子加奠。 啟殯日，遣慶郡王永璘祖奠目送。 逾年，上謁西陵，珪墓近蹕路，遣官賜奠。 高宗實錄成，特賜祭，擢長子錫經為四品京堂。 二十年，復因謁陵回鑾，親奠其墓，恩禮始終無與比。
Before long he was summoned to audience at the Palace of Heavenly Purity, grew dizzy, was helped home, and died a few days later. The emperor offered libation in person and wept bitterly. He was posthumously made grand tutor, enshrined in the Shrine of Worthy Officials, and granted gold for the funeral. An edict read: "Since Gui served as tutor, whatever he said was nothing but the language of the age of Yao, Shun, and the Three Dynasties; nothing touching mere fashion ever left his lips, and his counsel was abundant. Measured by the standards for posthumous titles, he fully merits the character for 'upright' without shame—he is specially given the posthumous title Wenzheng (Cultured and Upright)." The emperor also saw that his gate and courtyard were humble and his circumstances of austere poverty no less than those of a plain scholar. " He ordered the inner court to prepare a feast and sent princes to offer additional libations. On the day the coffin was opened, he sent Prince Qing Yonglin to offer libation as chief mourner and see him off. A year later, when the emperor visited the western tombs, Gui's tomb lay near the imperial route, and an official was sent to offer libation. When Qianlong's veritable records were completed, a special sacrifice was granted, and his eldest son Xijing was promoted to fourth-rank capital office. In 1820, again on returning from a tomb visit, the emperor personally offered libation at his tomb—favor from beginning to end unmatched.
22
珪文章奧博，取士重經策，銳意求才。 嘉慶四年典會試，阮元佐之，一時名流搜拔殆盡，為士林宗仰者數十年。 學無不通，亦喜道家，嘗曰：「硃子注參同契，非空言也。 」
Gui's writings were profound and learned; in selecting officials he valued classical examination essays and earnestly sought talent. In 1799 he presided over the metropolitan examination with Ruan Yuan assisting; nearly all the leading men of the day were selected, and the scholarly world revered him for decades. There was no branch of learning he did not master; he also took an interest in Daoism and once said, "Zhu Xi's commentary on the Cantong qi is not empty talk. "
23
論曰：君子小人消長之機，國運系焉。 王杰、董誥、硃珪皆高宗拔擢信任之臣，和珅一再間沮，卒不屈撓。 一旦共、驩伏法，眾正盈朝，攄其忠誠，啟沃新主，殄寇息民，苞桑永固。 天留數人，弼成仁宗初政之盛，可謂大臣矣。
The historian comments: The rise and fall of gentlemen and petty men is a matter on which the nation's fortune depends. Wang Jie, Dong Gao, and Zhu Gui were all ministers whom Qianlong had selected and trusted; Heshen repeatedly obstructed them, yet in the end they did not bend. Once Heshen was executed, upright men filled the court; they poured out their loyalty, counseled the new emperor, crushed the rebels and brought peace to the people, and the dynasty stood firm as a rooted mulberry. Heaven preserved these few men to help shape the splendor of the Jiaqing Emperor's early reign—they may truly be called great ministers.
