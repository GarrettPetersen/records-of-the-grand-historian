哈攀龍子國興任舉冶大雄馬良柱本進忠劉順
Ha Panlong, Guo Xing, Ren Ju, Ye Daxiong, Ma Liangzhu, Ben Jinzhong, and Liu Shun
3
哈攀龍，直隸河間人，其先出回部。 乾隆二年一甲一名武進士，授頭等侍衛。 以副將發福建，除興化城守副將。 遷總兵，歷河南南陽，福建海壇、漳州諸鎮。 以母喪去官。 十三年，高宗東巡，攀龍迎鑾，命往金川，隸總督張廣泗軍，署松潘鎮總兵。 出美諾溝，取撒臥山、大松林、噶達諸寨。 分兵出馬溝右梁，察形勢，得其險要，搜截松林，賊蔽松設卡。 毀其二，徑左梁山溝，砲斃賊數十。 進克渴足寨，焚碉寨四、水城一，殺賊二十餘。 尋與署重慶鎮總兵任舉合兵攻色爾力石城，舉沒於陣。 攀龍入林，殪賊三十餘，奪舉屍回。 复偕都統班第、署重慶鎮總兵段起賢、侍衛富成分道夜襲色爾力，焚木卡三，殺賊五十餘。 進破石梁、雙溝諸壘。 經略訥親、總督張廣泗劾攀龍攻色爾力不能下，兵部議左遷。 上責攀龍自陳，攀龍言屢克卡殺賊報廣泗，廣泗不以入告。 會訥親、廣泗皆得罪去，上知攀龍枉，命罷議。 尋從經略大學士傅恆夜攻色爾力，先登，拔石卡，殪賊數十。 十四年，金川事定，命署固原提督。 十六年，移湖廣提督，陳整飭弁兵諸事，上嘉勉之。 尋命真除。 复移貴州提督。 入陛見，病留京師，卒。
Ha Panlong was from Hejian in Zhili; his family originally came from the Muslim communities. In 1737 he ranked first among the top-tier military graduates and was appointed a First-Class Bodyguard. He was sent to Fujian as a brigade deputy commander and made deputy commander of the Xinghua garrison. Promoted to regional commander, he served in turn at Nanyang in Henan and at Haicheng and Zhangzhou in Fujian. He left office to observe mourning for his mother. In 1748, when the emperor toured the east, Panlong welcomed the imperial procession and was ordered to Jinchuan under Zhang Guangsi, with acting command of the Songpan garrison. Advancing from Meinuo Ravine, he took Sawo Mountain, Great Pine Forest, Gada, and other rebel strongholds. He detached troops along the right ridge of Magou Ravine to scout the ground, seized the key positions, and swept the pine woods where rebels had hidden and set up barriers. He destroyed two barriers, pushed up Zuoliang Mountain Ravine, and killed dozens of rebels with cannon fire. He then took Kezu stockade, burning four blockhouses, one water fort, and killing more than twenty rebels. Soon afterward he joined Acting Chongqing Commander Ren Ju in assaulting the stone fortress at Serli; Ju was killed in action. Panlong fought into the woods, killed more than thirty rebels, and brought Ju's body back. He again joined Ban Di, Duan Qixian, and Fu Cheng in a night assault on Serli by separate routes, burning three wooden barriers and killing more than fifty rebels. He pressed forward and broke the fortifications at Shiliang and Shuanggou. Commissioner Neqin and Zhang Guangsi accused Panlong of failing to capture Serli; the Ministry of War recommended his demotion. The emperor required Panlong to explain himself; Panlong said he had repeatedly reported captures and kills to Guangsi, who had not passed those reports upward. When Neqin and Guangsi were both removed for misconduct, the emperor saw that Panlong had been wronged and halted the demotion proceedings. Soon he joined Grand Secretary Fu Heng in a night assault on Serli, was first over the wall, took the stone barrier, and killed dozens of rebels. In 1749, after the Jinchuan campaign ended, he was appointed acting provincial commander at Guyuan. In 1751 he was transferred to Huguang as provincial commander and memorialized on tightening discipline among officers and men; the emperor commended him. He was soon confirmed in the post. He was then transferred again to Guizhou as provincial commander. He came to court for audience, fell ill in the capital, and died there.
4
子國興，乾隆十七年武進士，授三等侍衛。 出為雲南督標右營游擊，遷東川營參將。 緬甸頭人召散據孟艮為亂。 總督楊應琚檄國興佐軍，戰楞木，進克猛卯，督戰被槍，創右輔及臂。 應琚以聞，賜孔雀翎。 尋署騰越營副將。 時副將趙宏榜以偏師深入，與緬人戰於新街，師敗績。 國興師至蠻暮，诇新街無備，督兵潛入，緬人乃引退。 從將軍明瑞進克木邦，戰於蠻暮，大破之。 复偕侍衛莽克察擊斬守隘賊六十餘。 擢楚姚鎮總兵。 入陛見，命在乾清門行走，賚銀幣。 還軍，移普洱鎮總兵，遷貴州提督。 經略傅恆議用水師，令國興赴銅壁關外野人山督造船。 移雲南提督，加太子少保。 船成，從傅恆出猛拱、孟養、南豐、猛烈、猛壩，次老官屯。 緬人水陸備甚固，攻之不時下。 頭人諾爾塔以其酋懵駁命，遣使得魯蘊詣軍乞解兵。 傅恆令國興出見，曉以利害，令具約十年一貢，毋更擾邊，歸所掠內地人。 緬人誓奉約。 時傅恆方病，將軍阿桂召從征諸大臣議，皆言許之便，遂與定約解兵。 既而貢弗至，總督彰寶遣都司蘇爾相諭意，留不遣，揚言國興許以木邦、猛拱、蠻暮三土司予緬人，請如議。 彰寶劾國興與緬人議具約不以實，上召國興至京師，詰國興，國興自陳未嘗有此議。 上責國興遷就畢事，奪太子少保，左授貴州古州鎮總兵。 移雲南臨元鎮。 後二年，得魯蘊復至老官屯，請如前誓三事。
His son Guo Xing passed the military metropolitan examination in 1752 and was appointed a Third-Class Bodyguard. He was posted to Yunnan as colonel of the governor-general's right battalion and later promoted to garrison colonel at Dongchuan. The Burmese chieftain Zhao San seized Menggen and rose in revolt. Governor-General Yang Yingju ordered Guo Xing to join the campaign; at Lengmu and in the capture of Mengmeng he led from the front, was shot, and wounded in the right temple and arm. Yingju reported the feat, and Guo Xing was awarded the peacock feather. He was soon made acting deputy commander of the Tengyue garrison. At that time Deputy Commander Zhao Hongbang led a detached force deep into enemy country and was routed by the Burmese at Xinjie. Guo Xing marched to Manmu, learned that Xinjie was undefended, led his men in by stealth, and the Burmese withdrew. Under General Ming Rui he helped capture Mubang and won a major victory at Manmu. He again joined Bodyguard Mangkecha in storming the passes and killing more than sixty defenders. He was promoted to regional commander at Chuyao. At court audience he was ordered to serve at the Gate of Celestial Purity and was granted silver and coin. Returning to the field, he was made commander at Pu'er and then provincial commander of Guizhou. Commissioner Fu Heng decided on a naval campaign and sent Guo Xing beyond Tongbi Pass to the Wild Man Mountains to supervise construction of the fleet. He was transferred to Yunnan as provincial commander and given the title Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent. When the fleet was ready he followed Fu Heng through Menggong, Mengyang, Nanfeng, Menglie, and Mengban, and camped at Laoguantun. Burmese defenses on land and water were formidable, and the positions could not be taken quickly. Chieftain Nuerta, acting for his lord Meng Ba, sent the envoy Deluyun to the army to ask for withdrawal of the troops. Fu Heng had Guo Xing meet the envoy, explain the stakes, and require a treaty pledging tribute every ten years, an end to border raids, and the return of captives taken from Qing territory. The Burmese swore to uphold the agreement. Fu Heng was then ill; General Agui called the ministers on campaign to council, and all agreed acceptance was expedient; they concluded the treaty and withdrew. When tribute failed to arrive, Governor Zhang Bao sent Company Commander Su Erxiang to state Qing demands; Su was detained, and the Burmese claimed Guo Xing had promised them the three chieftaincies of Mubang, Menggong, and Manmu and asked that the bargain be honored. Zhang Bao accused Guo Xing of misrepresenting the treaty negotiations; the emperor summoned him to Beijing and questioned him; Guo Xing denied ever having made such a promise. The emperor blamed Guo Xing for settling the affair too readily; he was stripped of Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent and demoted to regional commander at Guzhou in Guizhou. He was transferred to command the Linyuan garrison in Yunnan. Two years later Deluyun returned to Laoguantun and asked again for the three pledges of the original oath.
5
時師徵金川，上命國興從將軍溫福進討。 三十七年，遷西安提督，命盡護陝西、甘肅從征諸軍。 尋令偕總兵董天弼自曾頭溝取底木達、布朗郭宗。 溫福以國興能軍，令自策卜丹徑取美諾當一面。 國興自阿喀木雅山溝紆道徑瑪爾迪克山寨，察策卜丹地勢，林深徑狹，不宜於行師，乃將二千人佐海蘭察攻瑪爾迪克。 溫福再疏聞上。 金川賊千餘屯貢噶山左，謀劫糧，國興馳擊，賊敗匿。 師還，經瑪爾迪克，賊自林中出，复擊敗之，上賚荷包四。 進攻貢噶山，設伏，斬賊百餘，搜箐奪碉卡。 九月，金川酋索諾木使詣國興，請獻鄂克什地以降。 國興令並割南北兩山美美卡、木蘭壩及瑪爾迪克。 越日，賊盡撤諸柵。 國興以兵入鄂克什舊寨，賊退守路頂宗。 十月，使歸墨壟溝師敗時所掠外委臧儒，且言嘗勸僧格桑同降。 溫福以聞，上令國興檄諭索諾木聲其罪。 時國興及海蘭察將五千人屯貢噶山，謀攻策卜丹，阻冰雪未進。 上命還師攻路頂宗。 路頂宗山麓有巨溝，溝源出南山。 海蘭察紆道出山後，侍衛額森特自小徑為應，國興前越溝攻碉。 師繼進，遂克路頂宗，破卡五十餘、碉三百餘，俘獲甚眾。 復自喀木色爾北山攻穆拉斯郭大寨，進據兜烏山巔，與總兵馬彪軍合，奪附近碉卡，克額爾奔木柵。 復將千人渡水，自南山鄂爾濟仰攻，克諸寨，與大軍會，進攻明郭宗。 別以兵襲擊公雅山，克木爾古魯寨，並奪據嘉巴山麓。 廷議既定小金川，分命將帥三道進討金川。 上曰：「國興雖綠營漢員，熟軍事； 又嘗為乾清門侍衛，與滿洲大臣無異。 」授參贊大臣，佐副將軍豐升額。 是月克明郭宗，焚念經樓。 整兵進取日果爾烏谷山麓，攻美諾。 上嘉國興功，官其子文虎守備。 攻克布朗郭宗，僧格桑遁金川。 我軍直抵底木達，僧格桑父澤旺出降。 小金川平。
The army was then campaigning in Jinchuan; the emperor ordered Guo Xing to join General Wen Fu in the advance. In 1772 he was made provincial commander at Xi'an and placed in overall charge of the Shaanxi and Gansu forces on campaign. He was soon ordered to join Regional Commander Dong Tianbi in taking Dimuda and Bulangguozong via Zengtougou Ravine. Wen Fu, judging Guo Xing an able field commander, ordered him to strike Meinuo from Zebudan on an independent axis. Guo Xing took a detour through Akemuya Mountain Ravine to Ma'erdike stockade, reconnoitered Zebudan, found the woods too dense and the paths too narrow for a main column, and sent two thousand men to support Hailancha against Ma'erdike. Wen Fu reported this again to the throne. More than a thousand Jinchuan rebels massed on the left flank of Gongga Mountain to ambush supply trains; Guo Xing rode out to attack and drove them into hiding. On the return march past Ma'erdike rebels sprang from the woods; he beat them back again, and the emperor granted him four reward purses. He advanced on Gongga Mountain, laid an ambush, killed more than a hundred rebels, swept the ravines, and seized blockhouses and barriers. In the ninth month the Jinchuan chief Suonuomu sent envoys to Guo Xing offering to surrender the territory of E'keshi. Guo Xing demanded as well the cession of Meimeika, Mulanba, and Ma'erdike on the northern and southern ranges. The next day the rebels pulled down all their palisades. Guo Xing marched into the old E'keshi stockade; the rebels fell back to Ludingzong. In the tenth month they returned Deputy Commissioner Zang Ru, captured when the army was beaten at Molong Ravine, and said they had urged Senggesang to surrender with them. Wen Fu reported this; the emperor ordered Guo Xing to send a proclamation listing Suonuomu's crimes. Guo Xing and Hailancha then had five thousand men at Gongga Mountain planning an attack on Zebudan, but ice and snow held them back. The emperor ordered them to turn back and assault Ludingzong. At the foot of Ludingzong stood a great ravine fed from the southern heights. Hailancha circled behind the mountain; Bodyguard Esente took a side path in support; Guo Xing crossed the ravine in front to storm the blockhouse. The main force pressed on, took Ludingzong, broke more than fifty barriers and three hundred blockhouses, and captured a great number of prisoners. He again attacked the great stockade at Mulasiguo from the Kamise'er north ridge, seized the summit of Douwu Mountain, joined Regional Commander Ma Biao, took nearby blockhouses, and captured the E'erbenmu palisade. He led a thousand men across the river, struck from E'erjiyang on the southern slope, took the outlying stockades, rejoined the main army, and advanced on Mingguozong. In a separate column he raided Gongya Mountain, took Mu'ergulu stockade, and seized the foothills of Jiaba Mountain. Once the court had settled Lesser Jinchuan, commanders were assigned on three routes to advance against Great Jinchuan. The emperor said, "Though Guo Xing is a Han officer of the Green Standard Army, he knows warfare well; and he once served at the Gate of Celestial Purity as a bodyguard, no different in standing from a Manchu grand minister." He was appointed assistant commissioner to aid Deputy General Feng Sheng'e. That month they took Mingguozong and burned the scripture hall. They regrouped and advanced along the foothills of Rigu'erwu Valley against Meinuo. The emperor commended Guo Xing's achievements and appointed his son Wenhu as garrison commander. They took Bulangguozong, and Senggesang fled into Great Jinchuan. Our forces pressed to Dimuda, where Senggesang's father Zewang surrendered. Lesser Jinchuan was pacified.
6
國興卒於軍，賜白金千，存卹其家，加贈太子太保，諡壯武。 祀昭忠祠，圖形紫光閣。 文虎授陝西提標右營守備，從軍攻木果木，陣沒，從祀昭忠祠。 复官次子文彪千總。
Guo Xing died in camp; the court granted a thousand taels of silver for his family, posthumously raised him to Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent, and gave him the posthumous name Zhuangwu (Stalwart and Martial). He was enshrined in the Hall of Loyalty and Merit, and his portrait was hung in the Hall of Purple Splendor. Wenhu was made garrison commander of the Shaanxi provincial right battalion; he fell at Muguo Woods and was granted joint enshrinement in the Hall of Loyalty and Merit. His second son Wenbiao was also given an appointment as company commander.
7
任舉，山西大同人。 雍正二年武進士。 以守備發陝西。 累遷固原提標左營游擊，署城守營參將。 乾隆十一年十二月，固原兵變，夜攻提督許仕盛，毀轅門將入。 舉聞亂，單騎詣鼓樓鳴角，招營兵未變者才五十人，部勒使成列。 變兵懼，退掠市廛。 舉追及，手刃十餘人，擒四十餘人。 變兵出城南門，還攻東西二門。 舉守東門，右營游擊鐵保守西門，禦戰，變兵潰。 事定，總督慶復以聞，擢中軍參將。
Ren Ju was from Datong in Shanxi. He passed the military metropolitan examination in 1724. He was posted to Shaanxi as a garrison commander. He rose to colonel of the Guyuan provincial left battalion and acting garrison colonel of the city command. In December 1746 the Guyuan garrison mutinied, attacked Provincial Commander Xu Shisheng by night, smashed the yamen gate, and were on the point of breaking in. Hearing of the riot, Ju rode alone to the drum tower, sounded the horn, gathered the fifty men who had not joined the mutiny, and formed them into ranks. The mutineers lost heart, fell back, and looted the market streets. Ju overtook them, killed more than ten with his own hand, and took more than forty prisoner. The mutineers went out the south gate and turned back to assault the east and west gates. Ju held the east gate while Right Battalion Colonel Tie Bao held the west; they beat off the assault and the mutineers broke. When order was restored, Governor-General Qing Fu reported the affair and Ju was promoted to central-battalion colonel.
8
十二年，命徵金川，隸總督張廣泗軍。 尋授西鳳協副將。 舉至軍，與總兵許應虎、副將高宗瑾、參將買國良攻色底賊碉，擊以砲二百餘發，碉一角圮，垣鑿孔發砲，密如鱗比。 舉度我軍砲小不能下，將移軍退守，賊出戰，再設伏敗之。 十三年，上諭謂：「在軍諸將狃於瞻對之役，庸懦欺矇，已成夙習。 今別用舉等，皆未從征瞻對，無所掣肘，宜鼓勵勇往。 」廣泗亦奏在川鎮將，忠誠勇幹無出舉右者，令率漢、土兵三千取道攻昔嶺。 尋又奏令署重慶鎮總兵。
In 1747 he was ordered to the Jinchuan campaign under Governor-General Zhang Guangsi. He was soon made deputy commander of the Xifeng garrison. At the front Ju joined Regional Commander Xu Yinghu, Deputy Commander Gao Zongjin, and Colonel Mai Guoliang in assaulting the rebel blockhouse at Sedi; after more than two hundred rounds one corner gave way, and they bored holes in the wall to fire back until the embrasures were packed tight as scales. Judging that Qing cannon were too light to breach the works, Ju was about to pull back to a defensive line when the rebels sallied; he laid another ambush and routed them. In 1748 the emperor said, "The generals on this campaign are still mired in the habits of Zhandui—timid, deceitful conduct that has become second nature. I am now using Ju and men like him, none of whom served at Zhandui and none of whom are shackled by that record; they should be urged to press forward boldly." Zhang Guangsi also reported that among Sichuan commanders none matched Ju in loyalty and martial ability, and ordered him to lead three thousand Han and native troops against Xiling. He soon memorialized again appointing Ju acting regional commander at Chongqing.
9
舉與參將王愷自牛廠至素可尼山。 時五月，遇大雪，闢道以行。 經撒烏山，至昔嶺山梁，山北曰木岡，孤峰當道，賊置城卡守隘。 舉督兵攻卡，憑高發砲洞其垣，令土兵緣溝潛進，毀賊碉。 師循出山腰，克賊卡，遂陟中峰，以千人駐守，進攻木岡。 時總兵哈攀龍師至馬溝右梁，阻松林不得進。 廣泗令自納喇溝出昔嶺右，與舉合攻木岡賊所署城卡，力戰未即下。 舉察昔嶺左有道通卡撒，中經得思東、木達溝，賊皆置碉焉。 總兵冶​​大雄方自卡撒進，舉與合軍，焚木達溝諸碉，圍得思東，斷其汲道，督兵挾斧斫賊，賊墮岩遁，得大小碉三。 進攻色爾力石城，分兵為三道：舉督兵直攻石城，攀龍出其右，副將唐開中及國良出其左。 越溝度林，攻賊所置木城，國良戰死。 六月己巳，舉與攀龍、開中合攻石城，城堅甚。 我師方力攻，賊三百餘自西南林內出，舉督兵與戰，被創； 戰益力，槍復中要害，遂卒。 攀龍入林殺賊，以其屍還。
Ju and Colonel Wang Kai marched from Niuchang to Sukeni Mountain. It was the fifth month; they met deep snow and had to cut a road through it. They crossed Sawo Mountain to the Xiling ridge; to the north stood Mugang, a lone peak athwart the road where rebels had built a walled barrier. Ju stormed the barrier, battered its wall with cannon from the heights, and sent native troops along the ravine to destroy the rebel blockhouses. The column wound along the mountainside, took the barrier, seized the central peak with a thousand men, and pressed on against Mugang. Regional Commander Ha Panlong had reached the right ridge of Magou but was held up in the pine woods. Zhang Guangsi ordered Panlong out through Nala Ravine to Xiling's right to join Ju against the Mugang barrier; despite fierce fighting it did not fall at once. Ju found a road on Xiling's left to Kasa through Desidong and Muda Ravine, both lined with rebel blockhouses. Regional Commander Ye Daxiong was advancing from Kasa; Ju joined him, burned the Muda Ravine blockhouses, besieged Desidong and cut its water, and drove the rebels over the cliffs with axmen—taking three blockhouses in all. In the assault on the Serli stone fortress they split three ways: Ju struck the fortress head-on, Panlong on the right, and Deputy Commander Tang Kaizhong and Guoliang on the left. Crossing ravines and woods they stormed the rebels' wooden fort; Guoliang was killed in action. On the jisi day of the sixth month Ju, Panlong, and Kaizhong attacked the stone fortress together; it was exceedingly strong. While the assault was at its height more than three hundred rebels burst from the southwest woods; Ju led the fight and was wounded; he fought on harder still until a musket ball found a vital spot and he fell dead. Panlong fought into the woods, killed rebels, and brought Ju's body back.

The emperor had just confirmed Ju's appointment when Grand Secretary Neqin reported his death in battle; reading the memorial the emperor wept and said, "Ju met death gladly out of loyal rage, yet I sent a fine officer into peril over these petty rebels—the thought pierces me to the heart!" He was granted funeral honors on the provincial-commander scale, posthumously raised to Vice Commissioner-in-Chief, given the posthumous name Yonglie, enshrined in the Hall of Loyalty and Merit, and his sons Chengen and Chenxu were made company commander and thousand-man commander. When Chengen came to court after mourning, the emperor said he was still young and told his mother through an envoy to raise him carefully. In 1759 he was appointed Third-Class Bodyguard. He rose to provincial commander of Fujian on land. In 1787 Lin Shuangwen rebelled in Taiwan; Chengen volunteered to suppress him, but the campaign failed; brought to Beijing he deserved death, yet the emperor spared him. In 1788 he was pardoned and released from prison. In 1790 he was again made patrol-battalion colonel and then deputy commander. He died. Chenxu was patrol-battalion colonel; when fire broke out in the market he rushed to help, was wounded, and died. In pardoning Chengen the emperor said he had no son and Chenxu had died in the line of duty—Ju's line could not be allowed to end.
11

Ye Daxiong was from Chengdu in Sichuan. In the late Kangxi era he enlisted and campaigned in Tibet at Keri and Batang, reducing several stockades and capturing five rebellious lamas. Early in the Yongzheng reign he marched from Songpan through Huangsheng Pass and pacified the twelve Redang tribes. At Guolong Monastery he took three ridges and fifteen stockades and pursued the Kangbu lama to Qinghai, where he was killed. He also fought at Zhuozi and Qizi mountains and killed tribal chiefs. Pursuing Lobzang Danjin he captured Danjin Huitaiji. Governor-General Yue Zhongqi recommended him; received at court, he was specially made a Blue-Lapel Bodyguard. He rose to garrison colonel at Zhuanglang in Shaanxi. Given deputy-commander rank and the peacock feather, he was sent to the Balikun army and by dispatch made acting deputy commander of the Sichuan-Shaanxi central column.
12

The Dzungars attacked the frontier barriers at Kashi and Ekunji. With Regional Commander Fan Yan, Daxiong held twenty thousand rebels at bay with two thousand men for seven days and nights and brought the garrison out of the besieged barriers. Joining Zhang Yuanzuo and others, they fought fiercely and killed many of the enemy. He was granted the hereditary rank of Baitalabulehafan and five thousand taels of silver. He was soon made deputy commander of the Shanyong garrison in Zhili. He was appointed acting regional commander at Yiling in Hubei. He memorialized, "Yiling lies more than a thousand li from the provincial capital; troop pay is issued quarterly—please change to two disbursements, in summer-autumn and winter-spring." The proposal was sent to the governor-general and governor for approval and implementation. He was soon transferred to acting regional commander at Datong in Shanxi. He and the former commander Li Rubai accused each other; both were dismissed. In 1736 he was posted to Huguang as a deputy commander and soon made deputy commander at Hengzhou. When the Chengsui Miao and Yao rebelled, Daxiong garrisoned Chang'an Fort, burned rebel stockades, executed the ringleaders, and the rest submitted. He was promoted to regional commander at Zhenjiang. Governor-General Sun Jiagan accused him of greed and lax discipline, and he was dismissed. Hunan Governor Jiang Pu reported that the case showed no proof of greed or indulgence; received at court, he was again made regional commander at Zhaotong in Yunnan. In 1748 he joined the Jinchuan campaign, reached Kasa, and led the Yunnan and Guizhou forces against Sedi, Guangduo, and other stockades. He led troops west of Xiling's central peak, joined Ha Panlong and Ren Ju, took ten blockhouses and one stone fortress, and destroyed one hundred thirty more. Together they captured the stone fortress and water barrier at the foot of Xiling Ravine. Grand Secretary Fu Heng reported Daxiong's long combat experience and put him in charge of camp arrangements; he was granted the peacock feather. The Jinchuan chieftains including Saloben sued for peace and the army withdrew. He was made provincial commander of Yunnan with the rank of Left Assistant Commissioner-in-Chief. At court his son Jijun was made Blue-Lapel Bodyguard and ordered to escort Daxiong to his post. He memorialized, "Places such as Kalawusu in Tibet border the Dzungars, and banditry there is an old habit. Now Tibet's northern frontier is our border; guarding it will itself suppress banditry. I ask that the resident commissioner keep strong garrisons and post stations along the main road for defense." The emperor praised his attentiveness.
14

Jijun met the family at Changde; en route he borrowed money from a Hui merchant, and Daxiong reported it. The emperor said Daxiong had waited until exposure was inevitable before reporting it; he was demoted to regional commander at Hami. He was made acting provincial commander of Anxi and sent to Balikun to inspect horses and camels, reporting more than four thousand. When Governor-General Fang Guancheng verified that Colonel Zhong Shijie and others received only nineteen hundred-odd horses at Balikun and many died en route, they were punished. The emperor found Daxiong's report false and referred the case to the ministry; Governor-General Huang Tinggui accused him again; he was stripped of office and brought to Beijing for trial. In the fourth month of 1756 he died on reaching Xi'an. In 1767 the emperor noted that Green Standard hereditary posts could not pass in perpetuity and ordered the Ministry of War to review meritorious officers; when succession quotas were filled they would receive perpetual Ensign rank—and Daxiong was included.

馬良柱，甘肅張掖人，其先本回部。 康熙季年，從軍徵吐魯蕃； 雍正初，將軍阿爾邦檄赴插漢麥里幹討賊：皆有功。 復從安西鎮總兵孫繼宗攻羅卜藏丹津，降台吉三十三。 戰於哈馬兒打布罕噶斯，擒其渠，授藍翎侍衛，賜白金百，遷三等侍衛。 外授四川提標游擊，賜貂皮、數珠。 命將兵屯西藏。 旋以兵擾民，左降，聽四川巡撫、提督調遣。
Ma Liangzhu was from Zhangye in Gansu; his family originally came from the Muslim communities. In the late Kangxi era he campaigned against Turfan; and early in Yongzheng General Alabang sent him to Chaohanmailigan to suppress rebels—in each campaign he distinguished himself. He again followed Anxi Commander Sun Jizong against Lobzang Danjin and received the surrender of thirty-three taiji. At Hamar'dabuhangasi he captured the rebel chief, was made Blue-Lapel Bodyguard, granted a hundred taels of silver, and promoted to Third-Class Bodyguard. He was posted to Sichuan as provincial battalion colonel and granted sable fur and prayer beads. He was ordered to garrison troops in Tibet. Soon his troops were found harassing civilians; he was demoted and placed under the Sichuan governor and provincial commander.
16
八年，瞻對土司為亂，提督黃廷桂檄良柱討之。 賊堅守石碉，督兵仰攻，槍殪所乘馬，易馬進，再殪，乃步行督戰。 碉上投石如雨，傷面，搏賊益奮，火其碉，並焚擦馬、擦牙諸寨，殲賊無算。 側冷邦諸頭人皆降。 复授松潘鎮左營游擊。 三遷夔州協副將。
In 1730 the Zhandui chieftain rebelled; Provincial Commander Huang Tinggui ordered Liangzhu to suppress him. The rebels held stone blockhouses; he led uphill assaults; musket fire killed his horse twice, and he walked on foot to direct the fighting. Stones rained down and wounded his face; he fought on with greater fury, burned the blockhouses, and also burned the Camar and Caya stockades, destroying countless rebels. The chieftains of Cilenbang all submitted. He was again made left-battalion colonel of the Songpan garrison. He was transferred three times to deputy commander at Kuizhou.
17

In 1745 the army campaigned again against Zhandui, breaking Zhida and Songduo stockades and taking more than seventy blockhouses. Advancing on the Xiamizuo ridge they captured chieftain Galong Danping. Pressing forward they took more than a hundred stockades including Xiami and captured chieftain Taba Sijiao. Crossing the Yalu River they broke Zhandui and burned its stockades. Their chieftain Jiangcuotai died in the flames. In 1747 Great Jinchuan chief Saloben attacked the Gebushizan chieftain and raided Lumu, Zhanggu, and other lands of the Mingzheng chieftain. Governor Ji Shan moved Liangzhu from Weimao to deputy commander and put him in charge of the defense. Saloben enlisted Lesser Jinchuan chief Zewang to invade the Wo Day stockades; Major Ma Guangzu went to reinforce but was surrounded at Relong when the rebels came in force. Liangzhu led light cavalry to the rescue, routed the rebels at Bana Mountain, and took two hundred twenty-three stone barriers. Guangzu and others sallied forth; the rebels broke and the siege was lifted. Zewang surrendered and returned the three Wo Day stockades he had seized. The court commended his valor and made him regional commander at Chongqing. He advanced again, retook Sunkezong, attacked Jiangka, won battle after battle, took more than a hundred blockhouses and stockades, and received the surrender of more than twenty strongholds. He took Danga Mountain, detached troops to burn seven stockades including Salong, and the Gagu stockades submitted one after another. The rebels held the great blockhouse at Shida; Liangzhu pressed forward in the rain through dozens of fights; when rebels stormed the camp by night he ambushed and destroyed them. Mabang chieftain Sicuo had submitted, but Regional Commander Xu Yinghu mishandled him and he rebelled again, besieging Yinghu at Dijiao; Liangzhu rode to the rescue. The rebels fell back into Rongbu stockade, which could not be taken. Soon they raided Mabang again and Deputy Commander Zhang Xing was surrounded. Liangzhu asked to shift the Rongbu column to reinforce Zhang Xing, but Zhang Guangsi refused, and Xing was lost to the rebels. At Gagu the garrison mutinied to the rebels' side and seven frontier barriers were lost. Guangsi ordered Liangzhu to attack; despite fierce fighting the rebels held. Snow fell for more than twenty days; provisions ran out and they boiled armor and crossbow parts for food. Unable to hold on, Guangsi ordered a withdrawal. In the hasty withdrawal, cannon and arms fell into rebel hands.

Liangzhu had a broad brow, large eyes, and a curling beard; frontier people feared him and called him Lion's Head. A skilled fighter, in battle he wielded an iron whip that he spun on horseback like a wheel. At Gagu, Guangsi gave no support and pay arrived late; the emperor knew Guangsi resented his success, and therefore lightened his punishment. His son Yingzhao was deputy commander at Hejian in Zhili. His grandson Yu has a separate biography.

In the Yongzheng era he followed General Zhang Guangsi to relieve Turfan and garrisoned Lukqin. When the Dzungars attacked he intercepted them and took seven prisoners. He died; posthumously raised to Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent with the posthumous name Qinyi.
22
劉順，順天人。 雍正五年武進士，授藍翎侍衛。 以守備發陝西。 累遷至金塔協副將。 乾隆十三年，令將千五百人赴金川，偕副將高雄自甲索攻囊得，道松林。 賊百餘出戰，擊之遁，毀賊碉。 從大軍自卡撒左山梁進，諸碉以次皆下。 惟普瞻雙、單二碉守甚堅。 日暮，將收兵，順潛率所部逼單碉，縱火攻之，賊潰，並奪雙碉。 師繼進，遂克色底。 普瞻西有山曰阿利，賊碉林立。 順冒雨奮攻，奪山樑木卡，破碉。 發砲，殪賊數十，复破大碉一、石卡四。
Liu Shun was from the capital district. He passed the military metropolitan examination in 1727 and was made a Blue-Lapel Bodyguard. He was posted to Shaanxi as a garrison commander. He rose to deputy commander at Jinta. In 1748 he led fifteen hundred men to Jinchuan and, with Deputy Commander Gao Xiong, attacked Nangde from Jiasuo through the pine woods. More than a hundred rebels sallied; he drove them off and destroyed their blockhouses. With the main army from Kasa's left ridge, the blockhouses fell in succession. Only the twin and single blockhouses at Puzhan held out stubbornly. At dusk, as troops were pulling back, Shun secretly led his men against the single blockhouse, set it afire, routed the defenders, and took the twin blockhouses as well. The main force pressed on and took Sedi. West of Puzhan stood Mount Ali, thick with rebel blockhouses. Shun attacked through the rain, took the ridge barrier, and broke the blockhouses. With cannon he killed dozens of rebels and took one great blockhouse and four stone barriers.
23
經略訥親屢奏順奮勇。 金川平，擢貴州威寧鎮總兵。 上以順熟邊情，移甘肅西寧鎮總兵。 入見，賜孔雀翎。 擢安西提督。 病，乞罷。 卒，加太子太保，諡壯靖。
Commissioner Neqin repeatedly reported Shun's valor. After Jinchuan was pacified he was made regional commander at Weining in Guizhou. The emperor judged him expert in frontier affairs and transferred him to regional commander at Xining in Gansu. At court he was granted the peacock feather. He was promoted to provincial commander of Anxi. Falling ill, he asked to retire. He died; posthumously raised to Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent with the posthumous name Zhuangjing.
24
論曰：初徵金川，攀龍、舉、大雄皆以勇略著。 舉尤驍桀為軍鋒，訥親、張廣泗督戰急，鼓銳攻堅，遂以身殉，傷已！ 良柱善戰，又以廣泗牽制，不能盡其材。 進忠、順力戰破堅碉，亦攀龍輩之亞也。
Commentary: In the first Jinchuan campaign, Panlong, Ju, and Daxiong were all famed for courage and strategy. Ju was the fiercest vanguard of the army; Neqin and Zhang Guangsi drove the fighting hard, hurling crack troops against strongholds until Ju fell—a grievous loss! Liangzhu was a skilled fighter, but Guangsi's interference kept him from showing his full ability. Jinzhong and Shun fought hard to break stubborn blockhouses and stood only a step below men like Panlong.
