2
米思翰子李榮保顧八代瑪爾漢田六善杜臻薩穆哈
Mi Sihan; his sons; Li Rongbao; Gu Badai; Ma Erhan; Tian Liushan; Du Zhen; and Sa Muha
3
米思翰，富察氏，滿洲鑲黃旗人。 先世居沙濟。 曾祖旺吉努，當太祖時，率族來歸，授牛錄額真。 父哈什屯，事太宗，以侍衛襲管牛錄。 擢禮部參政，改副理事官。 討瓦爾喀，招明總兵沈志祥。 從攻錦州，明總兵曹變蛟夜襲御營，先眾扞禦，被創，力戰卻之。 順治初，授內大臣、議政大臣，世職屢進一等阿達哈哈番兼拖沙喇哈番。 睿親王多爾袞攝政，諸大臣鞏阿岱等並附之，哈什屯獨持正，忤睿親王，降世職拜他喇布勒哈番。 肅親王豪格以非罪死，鞏阿岱等議殺其子富綬，哈什屯與巴哈力持，事乃已。 世祖親政，累進世職一等阿思哈尼哈番加拖沙喇哈番。 十二年，獎奉職恪勤諸大臣，加太子太保。 康熙初卒，諡恪僖。
Mi Sihan, of the Fuca clan, belonged to the Bordered Yellow Banner of the Manchu. His family had long lived at Shaji. His great-grandfather Wangjinu brought the clan over to the founding emperor and was made a company captain. His father Hashitun served Emperor Taizong and, as an imperial guardsman, inherited command of the family company. He rose to vice minister of the Board of Rites and was later reassigned as associate director. While campaigning against the Walgaha, he persuaded the Ming regional commander Shen Zhixiang to come over. At the siege of Jinzhou, when Ming commander Cao Bianjiao struck the imperial camp by night, he was the first to meet the assault, took wounds, and fought the enemy off. Early in the Shunzhi reign he became a grand secretary and deliberative minister, and his hereditary rank was repeatedly raised to first-class baron with an additional junior rank. While Prince Regent Dorgon ruled, ministers such as Gongadai sided with him, but Hashitun alone stood firm, crossed the prince, and was demoted in hereditary rank. After Prince Su Haoge was put to death on trumped-up charges, Gongadai and others wanted his son Fushou killed, but Hashitun and Bahali objected until the proposal was abandoned. Once the Shunzhi Emperor assumed personal rule, his hereditary rank was again and again raised to first-class viscount with an additional junior title. In the twelfth year he was honored among the dutiful ministers in office and made Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent. He died early in the Kangxi reign and was posthumously titled Kexi.
4
米思翰，其長子也，襲世職，兼管牛錄，授內務府總管。 輔政大臣從假尚方器物，力拒之。 聖祖親政，知其守正，授禮部侍郎。 八年，擢戶部尚書，列議政大臣。 是時各直省歲賦，聽布政使存留司庫，蠹弊相仍，米思翰疏請通飭各直省俸餉諸經費，所餘悉解部，由是勾稽出納權盡屬戶部。
Mi Sihan, his eldest son, inherited the family rank, also commanded the company, and was appointed head of the Imperial Household Department. When the regent ministers sought to borrow imperial regalia, he firmly refused. Once the Kangxi Emperor assumed personal rule, recognizing his integrity, he was made vice minister of the Board of Rites. In the eighth year he was raised to minister of revenue and made a deliberative minister. At that time each province's annual taxes were left in local treasuries, and corruption went on unchecked; Mi Sihan memorialized that salaries and routine expenses should be met locally while all surpluses were sent to the capital, so that fiscal control passed entirely to the Board of Revenue.
5
十二年，尚可喜疏請撤籓，吳三桂、耿精忠疏繼入，下戶、兵二部議。 米思翰與兵部尚書明珠議三籓並撤，有言吳三桂不可撤者，以兩議入奏。 复集諸大臣廷議，米思翰堅持宜並撤，議乃定。 既而吳三桂反，上命王貝勒等率八旗兵討之，議者謂軍需浩繁，宜就近調兵禦守。 米思翰言：「賊勢猖獗，非綠旗兵所能製，宜以八旗勁旅會剿。 軍需內外協濟，足支十年，可無他慮。 」於是請以內府所儲分年發給，复綜覈各直省庫金、倉粟，以時撥運，悉稱旨。 又疏言：「師行所至，屢奉明詔以正賦給軍需，恐有司尚多藉端私派，請敕各督撫嚴察所屬，供應糧餉薪芻，一切動官帑，毋許苛派； 其購自民間者，務視時價支給，勿纖毫累民。 」上命如議速行。
In the twelfth year Shang Kexi asked to surrender his fief, and memorials from Wu Sangui and Geng Jingzhong followed; the case went to the ministries of revenue and war. Mi Sihan and War Minister Mingzhu argued that all three feudatories should be abolished at once, while others held that Wu Sangui's fief could not be touched; both views were reported to the throne. At a further court conference Mi Sihan insisted that all three must go, and the policy was fixed. When Wu Sangui rose in rebellion, the emperor ordered princes and banner lords to march against him, but some argued that the war would be costly and that nearby troops should be used for defense alone. Mi Sihan said, "The rebels are too strong for the Green Standard Army; we should send the best of the Eight Banners in a combined campaign." "With resources pooled from court and provinces, we can supply the war for ten years and need not worry on that score." He then proposed issuing supplies from the imperial storehouses year by year, audited provincial silver and grain reserves, and arranged timely shipments—all of which the emperor approved. He also wrote, "Though repeated edicts say that regular taxes should feed the armies, local officials may still levy illegal surcharges; let every governor-general and governor see that grain, pay, fuel, and fodder come from public funds and that the people are not squeezed." "Whatever must be bought from civilians should be paid at fair market prices, without the least added burden." The emperor ordered these measures put into effect at once.
6
米思翰尋卒，年甫四十三，上深惜之，予祭葬，諡敏果。 時三桂勢方張，精忠及可喜子之信皆叛，議者追咎撤籓主議諸臣，上曰：「朕自少時，以三籓勢日熾，不可不撤。 豈因其叛，諉過於人耶？ 」及事定，上追憶主議諸臣，猶稱米思翰不置。
Mi Sihan died soon after, at only forty-three; the emperor mourned him deeply, granted state funeral honors, and posthumously titled him Minguo. While Wu Sangui was at the height of his power and Geng Jingzhong and Shang Zhixin had also rebelled, critics blamed the ministers who had urged abolishing the fiefs; the emperor said, "Even as a young man I saw the Three Feudatories growing too strong to be left in place." Am I to blame others because they turned rebel? When the rebellion was over, the emperor still spoke again and again of Mi Sihan and the other ministers who had pressed for abolition.

米思翰子馬斯喀、馬齊、馬武，皆自有傳。
Mi Sihan's sons Maskha, Maqi, and Mawu are each treated in separate biographies.
8
李榮保，襲世職，兼管牛錄，累遷至察哈爾總管，卒。 乾隆二年，冊李榮保女為皇后，追封一等公。 十三年，冊諡孝賢皇后，推恩先世，進封米思翰一等公。 十四年，以李榮保子大學士傅恆經略金川功，敕建宗祠，祀哈什屯、米思翰、李榮保，並追諡李榮保曰莊愨。
Li Rongbao inherited the family rank, commanded the company as well, rose to Chahar commandant, and died in office. In Qianlong 2 his daughter was made empress, and he was posthumously created a first-class duke. In the thirteenth year the empress was posthumously titled Xiaoxian; honors reached back to earlier generations, and Mi Sihan was raised to first-class duke. In the fourteenth year, because Li Rongbao's son Grand Secretary Fu Heng had directed the Jinchuan campaign, the court ordered an ancestral hall for Hashitun, Mi Sihan, and Li Rongbao, and Li Rongbao was posthumously titled Zhuangque.
9
顧八代，字文起，伊爾根覺羅氏，滿洲鑲黃旗人。 父顧納禪，事太宗，從伐明，次大同，攻小石城，先登，賜號「巴圖魯」，予世職牛錄章京。 旋授甲喇額真。 順治初，從入關，定陝西、湖南、江南、浙江，皆在行間，進三等阿達哈哈番。 子顧蘇，襲，進二等。
Gu Badai, whose style was Wenqi, belonged to the Irgen Gioro clan of the Bordered Yellow Banner. His father Gunazan served Taizong, marched against the Ming, and at Little Stone City near Datong was first up the wall; the emperor named him a Baturu and granted him the hereditary rank of company colonel. He was soon made a battalion captain. Early in Shunzhi he crossed into China proper and fought through Shaanxi, Hunan, the lower Yangtze, and Zhejiang, earning promotion to third-class baron. His son Gusu inherited the rank and was raised to second class.
10
顧八代，其次子也。 任俠重義，好讀書，善射。 以廕生充護軍。 順治十六年，從征雲南有功，授戶部筆帖式。 旋以顧蘇及子佛岳相繼卒，無嗣，顧八代襲世職，遷吏部郎中。 康熙十四年，聖祖試旗員第一，擢翰林院侍讀學士。
Gu Badai was his second son. He was bold and loyal by nature, loved books, and excelled at archery. Through hereditary privilege he entered the guards. In Shunzhi 16 he distinguished himself in the Yunnan campaign and was made a clerk in the Board of Revenue. When Gusu and his son Foyue died in turn without heirs, Gu Badai inherited the family rank and was moved to director in the Board of Personnel. In Kangxi 14 he topped the emperor's examination of banner officers and was made a Hanlin reader.
11
吳三桂陷湖南，遣其將掠兩廣。 鎮南將軍莽依圖自江西下廣東，駐韶州。 十六年，上命顧八代傳諭莽依圖規复廣西，即留軍，從征廣西。 巡撫傅弘烈為三桂將吳世琮所敗，莽依圖引兵與相合。 顧八代按行諸軍，謂結營散亂，敵至慮不相應。 世琮兵至，師复敗，還駐梧州。 世琮來追，擊卻之。 顧八代策世琮且復至，益誡備。 會除夕，世琮以三萬人奄至，又擊敗之。 十七年，師進次盤江，與世琮軍遇，莽依圖病甚，以軍事屬顧八代； 偕副都統勒貝等渡江，與世琮戰，分兵出敵後，破其左而合擊其右。 世琮潰​​圍出，遣精騎追之，自殺。 師進克南寧，叛將馬承廕與三桂軍合，可十萬，拒戰。 諸將或難之，顧八代奮入陣，諸將皆力戰，遂破敵。
After Wu Sangui took Hunan, he sent generals to ravage Guangdong and Guangxi. General Mangyitu marched from Jiangxi into Guangdong and camped at Shaozhou. In the sixteenth year the emperor sent Gu Badai to tell Mangyitu to recover Guangxi; he stayed with the army and took part in the campaign. Governor Fu Honglie was beaten by Wu Sangui's general Wu Shijiong, and Mangyitu marched to his relief. Gu Badai inspected the camps and warned that their scattered dispositions would leave them unable to support one another if attacked. When Shijiong attacked, the army was beaten again and fell back to Wuzhou. Shijiong pursued, but they drove him off. Gu Badai expected Shijiong to return and pressed for tighter defenses. On New Year's Eve Shijiong surprised them with thirty thousand men, and they defeated him again. In the seventeenth year the army reached the Pan River and met Shijiong; Mangyitu was seriously ill and put Gu Badai in command; he crossed with Vice Commander Lebei and others, sent a detachment around the rebel rear, broke their left, and struck their right in concert. Shijiong broke out and fled; pursuing cavalry closed in, and he took his own life. The army took Nanning, where the rebel Ma Chengbian joined Wu Sangui's forces in a host of some one hundred thousand and offered battle. When some generals hung back, Gu Badai charged into the line and roused them all to fight until the enemy broke.
12
十八年，京察，掌院學士拉薩里、葉方藹以顧八代從征有績效，注上考； 大學士索額圖改注「浮躁」，坐奪官。 莽依圖疏言顧八代從征三載，竭誠奮勉，運籌決勝，請留軍委署副都統，參贊軍務，上命以原銜從征。 十九年，莽依圖卒於軍，顧八代從平南大將軍賚塔下雲南，攻會城。 顧八代議當先取銀錠山，俯瞰城內，攻得勢。 及勇略將軍趙良棟師至，用顧八代策，先取銀錠山，克會城，雲南平。 師還，授侍講學士。
In the eighteenth year's metropolitan review, Academicians Lasali and Ye Fangai rated Gu Badai for top honors on account of his campaign record; but Grand Secretary Songgotu changed the rating to "rash," and Gu Badai lost his post. Mangyitu reported that for three years Gu Badai had served the campaign with tireless zeal and sound strategy, and asked that he stay on as acting vice commander; the emperor let him continue under his former title. In the nineteenth year Mangyitu died in camp; Gu Badai followed General Jaisang into Yunnan to besiege the provincial capital. Gu Badai urged taking Silver Ingot Hill first, which overlooked the city and would give the attack its advantage. When General Zhao Liangdong arrived, they followed Gu Badai's plan, seized the hill, took the capital, and pacified Yunnan. After the army returned, he was made a Hanlin expositor.
13
二十三年，命直尚書房，累遷禮部侍郎。 二十八年，授尚書。 三十二年，坐事，上責其不稱職，奪官，留世職，仍直尚書房。 三十七年，以病乞休。 四十七年，卒。
In the twenty-third year he entered the Imperial Study and rose to vice minister of rites. In the twenty-eighth year he was made a ministry director. In the thirty-second year he was punished; the emperor said he had failed in office, stripped him of his post but kept his hereditary rank, and still kept him in the Imperial Study. In the thirty-seventh year he retired on grounds of illness. He died in the forty-seventh year.
14
顧八代直尚書房時，世宗從受學； 及卒，貧無以斂，世宗親臨奠，為經紀其喪。 雍正四年，詔复官，加太傅，予祭葬，諡文端，又以其貧，賜其家​​白金萬。 八年，建賢良祠京師，諭滿洲大臣當入祀者五人，大學士圖海、都統賚塔，次即顧八代，及尚書瑪爾漢、齊蘇勒。
While Gu Badai served in the Imperial Study, the future Yongzheng Emperor studied under him; and when he died too poor for a proper burial, the prince came in person to mourn and arranged the funeral. In Yongzheng 4 his offices were restored, he was made Grand Tutor, granted state funeral honors, and posthumously titled Wenduan; because he had died poor, the court also gave his family ten thousand taels of silver. In the eighth year the Worthy Ministers Shrine was built in the capital; five Manchu statesmen were named for enshrinement—Grand Secretary Tuhai, Commander Jaisang, then Gu Badai, Minister Ma Erhan, and Qisule.
15
子顧儼，襲世職，自參領官至副都統。 孫顧琮，自有傳。
His son Gu Yan inherited the family rank and rose from company adjutant to vice commander. His grandson Gu Cong has a separate biography.
16
瑪爾漢，兆佳氏，滿洲正白旗人。 順治十一年，繙譯舉人，授工部七品筆帖式，累遷刑部員外郎。
Ma Erhan, of the Zhaojia clan, belonged to the Plain White Banner. In Shunzhi 11 he passed the translator examination, entered the Board of Works as a seventh-rank clerk, and rose to vice director in the Board of Punishments.
17
康熙十三年，陝西提督王輔臣叛應吳三桂，上命揚威將軍阿密達自江寧移師討之，瑪爾漢以署驍騎參領從。 十四年，與副都統鄂克濟哈、穆舒琿等自涇州進兵，屢破壘，斬級數百，克寧州。 十五年，大將軍圖海督兵圍平涼，輔臣降，瑪爾漢還京師。 圖海請調涼州、寧夏、固原諸鎮兵進攻興安、漢中，上命副都統吳丹及瑪爾漢赴諸鎮料理徵發，兼詢緩急機宜。 甘肅提督張勇請緩師，上命圖海固守鳳翔、秦州諸要隘，分兵授征南將軍穆佔下湖廣，命瑪爾漢從。 十七年，授御史。
In Kangxi 13, when Shaanxi commander Wang Fuchen joined Wu Sangui's rebellion, General Amida was ordered from Nanjing to suppress him, and Ma Erhan went as acting cavalry adjutant. In the fourteenth year he marched from Jingzhou with Vice Commanders Ekjeha and Mushuhuan, stormed several rebel forts, took hundreds of heads, and captured Ningzhou. In the fifteenth year Grand General Tuhai besieged Pingliang until Fuchen surrendered; Ma Erhan then returned to the capital. Tuhai asked to move troops from Liangzhou, Ningxia, Guyuan, and other garrisons against Xing'an and Hanzhong; the emperor sent Vice Commander Wudan and Ma Erhan to arrange levies and report on strategy. When Gansu commander Zhang Yong asked to slow the advance, the emperor told Tuhai to hold Fengxiang and Qinzhou while General Muzhan marched into Huguang, with Ma Erhan in his train. In the seventeenth year he was made a censor.
18
十九年，穆佔師進貴州，二十年，師進雲南，瑪爾漢皆在行間，得功牌十二。 雲南平，師還，追論徵湖南不力援永興，致損將士，奪功牌九。 二十一年，命巡視河東鹽政。 御史許承宣、羅秉倫劾山西巡撫圖克善令平陽屬十三州縣增報鹽丁加課累民，下巡撫穆爾賽會瑪爾漢覈實，請免虛報一萬七千餘丁。 二十五年，以按治歸化城都統固穆德不實，吏議左遷。 二十六年，授理籓院司務。 從大學士索額圖等使鄂羅斯定邊界，辭辨明析，鄂羅斯人折服。 事聞，聖祖嘉其能。 尋遷戶部郎中。 三十三年，遷翰林院侍講學士，再遷兵部侍郎。 三十五年，上親征噶爾丹，命瑪爾漢駐土木董理驛站，以送軍馬羸，吏議奪官，命寬之。
In the nineteenth year Muzhan entered Guizhou, and in the twentieth the army entered Yunnan; Ma Erhan was with them throughout and earned twelve merit plaques. After Yunnan was pacified, a review blamed him for failing to relieve Yongxing during the Hunan campaign, costing many lives, and nine plaques were stripped away. In the twenty-first year he was sent to inspect salt administration in eastern Shanxi. Censors Xu Chengxuan and Luo Binglun accused Shanxi governor Tukeshan of making thirteen counties under Pingyang inflate salt-labor rolls and overtax the people; Governor Muerse and Ma Erhan investigated and asked to strike more than seventeen thousand false entries. In the twenty-fifth year an investigation of the Guihuacheng commandant Gumude was found unsound, and the personnel office recommended demotion. In the twenty-sixth year he was made secretary of the Court of Colonial Affairs. On the border mission to Russia with Grand Secretary Songgotu, his clear reasoning won over the Russians. When the report reached court, the Kangxi Emperor praised his talent. He was soon promoted to director in the Board of Revenue. In the thirty-third year he became a Hanlin reader and then vice minister of war. In Kangxi 35 the emperor marched against Galdan and left Ma Erhan at Tumu to manage the relay stations. Because the army horses he supplied were in poor condition, the personnel office recommended stripping him of rank, but the emperor showed mercy.
19
三十八年，遷左都御史。 再遷兵部尚書，充經筵講官、議政大臣。 四十三年，歲饑，流民就食京師。 命與內大臣佟國維、明珠、阿密達等監賑。 四十六年，調吏部。 四十八年，以老病乞休。 五十七年，卒，年八十五。 上遣內大臣臨奠，賜祭葬。 雍正八年，世宗諭獎瑪爾漢謹慎忠厚，事聖祖宣力多年，完名引退，贈太子太傅。 賢良祠成，命入祀。 乾隆元年，高宗命追諡恭勤。
In the thirty-eighth year he became Left Censor-in-Chief. He was then made minister of war, lecturer at the imperial lectures, and a deliberative minister. In the forty-third year famine drove refugees to the capital for relief. He was ordered to oversee famine relief with inner court ministers Tong Guowei, Mingzhu, Amida, and others. In the forty-sixth year he was moved to the Board of Personnel. In the forty-eighth year he retired on grounds of age and illness. He died in the fifty-seventh year, at eighty-five. The emperor sent an inner court minister to mourn him and granted state funeral honors. In Yongzheng 8 the Yongzheng Emperor praised Ma Erhan as prudent and loyal, noted his long service to the Kangxi Emperor and honorable retirement, and posthumously made him Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent. When the Worthy Ministers Shrine was completed, he was ordered enshrined there. In Qianlong 1 the Qianlong Emperor granted the posthumous title Gongqin.
20
田六善，字兼山，山西陽城人。 順治三年進士，授河南太康知縣，時當兵後，勞來安集。 九年，巡撫吳景道疏薦才守兼優，遷戶部主事，監臨清關，复監鳳陽倉兼臨淮關。 罷濫徵，革奇羨，商民稱便。 累遷郎中。 十五年，授江南道御史。 兵部議禁民乘馬，六善疏言其不便，下廷臣集議，弛禁。 十六年，疏言：「欲安民在勸清吏，乞敕各督撫實行薦舉，吏部於各督撫蒞任一二年後，列奏薦舉何人，能否察吏安民，即可以是鑑別。 議者或謂舉薦清吏，無以處乎不在清吏之列者，一難也； 恐督撫依舊受賄徇私，二難也； 徵糧緝逃處分罣礙，三難也。 然臣謂清吏果得薦舉，則為清吏者見公道尚存，益堅其持守，一便也； 群吏以不著清名為愧，力自濯磨，二便也； 某省有清吏幾人，以驗政治修廢，三便也； 天下曉然知有能必先有守，風俗丕變，四便也； 向日督撫厭憎清吏無益於己，今必且卵翼而親愛之，五便也。 不惑於三難，力致其五便，將循良興起，不讓前古矣。 」下部議行。 尋命巡視長蘆鹽政。 十七年，還掌江南道事。
Tian Liushan, whose style was Jianshan, came from Yangcheng in Shanxi. A Shunzhi 3 jinshi, he became magistrate of Taikang in Henan and, in the aftermath of war, worked to restore and settle the district. In the ninth year Governor Wu Jingdao recommended him for combined talent and integrity; he was made a revenue secretary, supervised Linqing Pass, and later Fengyang Granary together with Linhuai Pass. He ended abusive levies and irregular surcharges, to the relief of merchants and townspeople. He rose through successive promotions to director. In the fifteenth year he was made censor of the Jiangnan circuit. When the Board of War proposed banning commoners from riding horses, Liushan argued against it; the court debated the matter and lifted the ban. In the sixteenth year he wrote, "To secure the people we must encourage honest officials. I ask that every governor-general and governor be ordered to recommend them in earnest, and that after one or two years in office the Board of Personnel report whom each has recommended and whether he can judge officials and settle the people — by that we may tell who is fit." "Some object that recommending the honest leaves no place for those not on the list — the first difficulty;" "that governors-general and governors may still take bribes and show favoritism — the second difficulty;" "and that grain collection and fugitive-hunting penalties will get in the way — the third difficulty." "Yet I hold that if honest men are truly recommended, the honest will see justice still lives and hold firmer to their standards — the first advantage;" "that other officials will be ashamed to lack a clean name and will strive to reform — the second advantage;" "that the number of honest officials in a province will show whether its government thrives or fails — the third advantage;" "that the empire will learn that capacity must rest on integrity, and morals will change — the fourth advantage;" "and that governors-general and governors who once scorned honest men as useless to themselves will now protect and favor them — the fifth advantage." "Ignore the three difficulties, pursue the five advantages, and worthy officials will rise again, not inferior to those of old." The proposal was referred to the ministries and put into effect. He was soon sent to inspect the Changlu salt administration. In the seventeenth year he resumed charge of the Jiangnan circuit.

康熙元年，乞假歸。 三年，補貴州道御史。 四年，疏言：「兵部議裁山西、陝西、河南等處兵額，三營裁一營。 遇裁之兵，挾久練之技，處坐困之時，窮無所歸，遂為賊盜。 請諭總督、提督諸臣，察已裁之兵，如弓馬嫻熟、膂力精強，仍收入伍。 自後老弱必斥，逃亡不補。 所漸去者疲卒，不慮其為非； 所招回者勁兵，可資其實用。 」下部議，令各營汰去老弱，其年力精壯者仍留充伍。 又疏言：「吏部於往日曾行之事，率皆援以為例，惟意所彼此，莫窮其弊。 請敕部以上所裁定及有旨著為例者，彙為一冊，敬謹遵守，餘仍循舊章。 」得旨，如所請。 七年，命巡視京、通倉，還掌山東道事，得旨內升，回籍待缺。
In Kangxi 1 he asked for leave to go home. In the third year he was appointed censor of the Guizhou circuit. In the fourth year he wrote, "The Board of War proposes to cut troop quotas in Shanxi, Shaanxi, Henan, and elsewhere, disbanding one camp in three. Soldiers thus discharged, skilled from long service and stranded in desperate times with nowhere to go, turn to banditry. I ask that governors-general and provincial commanders review discharged men and re-enlist those still adept with bow and horse and strong in body. Thereafter only the old and weak should be removed, and deserters should not be replaced. Those gradually let go will be worn-out men, and we need not fear they will turn to crime; those brought back will be strong soldiers of real use." The memorial was referred to the ministries, which ordered every camp to weed out the old and weak while keeping vigorous men in the ranks. He also wrote, "The Board of Personnel cites almost every past action as precedent, choosing whichever side suits it, so the abuses never end. I ask that all rulings from above and all matters expressly made precedent be compiled in one volume and strictly followed, while everything else remains under the old rules." The emperor approved the request. In the seventh year he inspected the Beijing and Tongzhou granaries, resumed charge of the Shandong circuit, received approval for promotion from within the ranks, and returned home to await appointment.

十一年，授刑科給事中，秩視正四品。 疏言：「臣里居讀上諭，以蘇克薩哈為鼇拜仇陷，殺其子孫，連坐族人白爾赫圖，恩予昭雪。 臣思法律為天下共者也，以滿洲勞苦功高之人，因與執政諸臣意見相左，輒牽連興大獄，恐尤而效之，報復相尋，藉端推刃。 周禮有八議，罪大可減，罪小可赦。 請特製昭示，滿洲犯罪非反叛有實蹟者，一準於律，勿妄議株連。 儲人才，固國本，於是乎在。 」上韙其言，下王大臣議，從之。 又疏言：「聖學宜先讀史。 史者，古帝王得失之林也。 其君寬仁明斷，崇儉納諫，則其民必安，其事必治，其世必興必平。 若夫苛察因循，惡聞過，樂逞欲，其民必不安，其事必不治，其世必衰必亂。 乞諭日講諸臣，以通鑑與經史並進。 」得旨俞允。 尋轉戶科掌印給事中。 三遷至右僉都御史。
In the eleventh year he was made supervising secretary of the Penalties Section, with the rank of a regular fourth-grade official. He wrote, "At home I read Your Majesty's edict clearing Suksaha, whom Oboi's faction had framed, whose descendants were killed, and whose clansman Bierhetu was implicated along with him. Law belongs to all the realm alike. When meritorious Manchus who had borne hardship were dragged into great cases because they differed with those in power, others may copy the example, revenge may run on, and any pretext may become a blade. The Rites of Zhou provides the Eight Deliberations: great crimes may be mitigated and lesser ones pardoned. I ask for a special edict declaring that Manchus guilty of crimes short of proven rebellion shall be judged by law alone, without reckless talk of implicating others. On this depends the preservation of talent and the security of the state." The emperor praised the memorial, referred it to princes and ministers, and agreed. He also wrote, "Imperial study should begin with history. History is the record of what ancient rulers gained and lost. When a ruler is generous, clear-minded, frugal, and open to remonstrance, the people are secure, affairs are well ordered, and the age flourishes. When he is harsh, nitpicking, resentful of criticism, and given to his desires, the people are uneasy, affairs go untended, and the age declines into disorder. I ask that the daily lecturers be instructed to teach the Comprehensive Mirror alongside the classics and histories." The emperor approved. He was soon made chief supervising secretary of the Revenue Section. After three further promotions he became Right Vice Censor-in-Chief.

十三年，疏言：「吳三桂負恩叛逆，處必滅之勢。 綠旗月餉，步兵一兩有奇，馬兵二兩有奇，甲胄不必堅強，弓刀不必精利，登山涉水，資以先驅。 臣謂綠旗力雖弱，善用之則強； 心雖渙，善收之則聚。 供給宜足，勞逸宜均。 至先登破陣，無分滿、漢，賞賚公平。 斯忠勇自奮，克佐勁旅以奏膚功，今日所宜急計者也。 」下部議鼓勵綠旗官兵敘給爵賞例。 遷順天府尹。 未幾，复遷左副都御史。 十四年，疏言：「臣昔為河南知縣時，孫可望、李定國尚據云、貴、四川，其勢不減於吳三桂。 金聲桓叛江西，姜瓖叛大同，亦不異耿精忠、王輔臣。 而當日民心未若今之驚惶疑懼者，由其時督撫有孟喬芳、張存仁、吳景道諸臣，敦行儉樸，慎守廉隅，吏治肅清，民生樂遂也。 宜特頒嚴諭，令各督撫禁雜派，覈軍實。 有司或剝民敗檢，立行糾劾，以省民力、安眾心。 師行所至，更宜審酌剿禦。 近見江西、浙江報捷諸疏，屢言殺賊累萬。 然必待殺盡而後入閩，恐愚頑之民無盡，草竊之賊亦無盡。 臣謂先取精忠，則群賊自息。 昔姜瓖乍叛，土寇群起，瓖滅，土寇亦盡，其明驗也。 至三桂狡謀，覬以一隅之地困天下全力，我即以天下全力困此一隅。 三桂授首，則四川、廣西不煩兵而自定。 」又疏言：「臣籍山西，與陝西接壤。 黃河自邊外折入內地，至蒲州一千餘裡。 蒲州上至禹門，為平陽府屬，河西為西安，有提督、總兵重兵駐守。 自此以北，永寧州、臨縣為汾州府屬，渡口有孟門鎮、高家塔諸處； 更北保德州為太原府屬，渡口有黑田溝、窮狼窩諸處。 河西為延安，素稱荒野，河東為交城，路險山深，草竊潛匿。 請敕巡撫、提督分兵駐防。 」又疏言：「師已抵平涼，輔臣迫於必死，困獸猶鬥，殺賊百不償失兵一。 宜駐軍城下，以逸待勞，急攻固原，絕其糧道。 平涼地瘠，非比湖南地廣米多，可以持久。 糧道不通，人心自散，必有斬輔臣獻軍門者。 若賊東出則東應，賊西出則西應，疲我師徒，分我威力，固原圍解，賊氣貫通，此斷斷不可者也。 」諸疏並下王大臣議行。
In the thirteenth year he wrote, "Wu Sangui has betrayed imperial grace and rebelled; he stands in a position that must be destroyed. Green Standard troops receive a little over one tael a month for infantry and two for cavalry; their armor need not be the finest nor their weapons the sharpest — they are meant to climb hills, ford streams, and lead the way. I hold that though the Green Standard Army is weak in itself, used well it can be strong; though their spirit is scattered, gathered well it can unite. Their supplies should be adequate and their burdens fairly shared. For leading assaults and breaking enemy lines, Manchu and Han alike should receive fair rewards. Then loyalty and courage will rise of themselves, and they can help the main forces win real victories — this is what must be planned at once." The memorial was referred to the ministries, which drafted rules for rewarding Green Standard officers and soldiers with titles and honors. He was made Intendant of Shuntian Prefecture. Soon afterward he was again made Left Vice Censor-in-Chief. In the fourteenth year he wrote, "When I was magistrate in Henan, Sun Kewang and Li Dingguo still held Yunnan, Guizhou, and Sichuan — a threat no smaller than Wu Sangui's today. Jin Shengheng rebelled in Jiangxi and Jiang Xiang in Datong — no different from Geng Jingzhong and Wang Fuchen. Yet the people then were not so panic-stricken as now, because governors such as Meng Qiaofang, Zhang Cunren, and Wu Jingdao were frugal and upright, kept administration clean, and let the people live in peace. A stern edict should go out ordering every governor-general and governor to stop irregular levies and verify troop strength. Any official who squeezes the people or breaks discipline should be impeached at once, to spare the people and steady their hearts. Wherever the army marches, commanders should weigh carefully how to suppress rebels and protect the people. Recent victory reports from Jiangxi and Zhejiang repeatedly claim tens of thousands of rebels killed. Yet if we must kill every last man before entering Fujian, the foolish and the petty bandits will never run out. I hold that if we take Geng Jingzhong first, the other rebels will subside of themselves. When Jiang Xiang first rebelled, local bandits rose everywhere; when he fell, they vanished too — clear proof of the point. As for Sangui's scheme to tie down the empire with one corner of the realm, we should tie down that corner with the full strength of the empire. Once Sangui is dead, Sichuan and Guangxi will settle themselves without further fighting." He also wrote, "My native province is Shanxi, which borders Shaanxi. The Yellow River enters the interior from beyond the frontier and runs more than a thousand li to Puzhou. From Puzhou up to Yumen, in Pingyang Prefecture, the west bank faces Xi'an, where provincial and regional commanders keep heavy garrisons. North of that, Yongning Prefecture and Lin County belong to Fenzhou Prefecture, with crossings at Mengmen, Gaojia Tower, and elsewhere; farther north, Baode Prefecture belongs to Taiyuan Prefecture, with crossings at Heitiangou, Qionglangwo, and elsewhere. West of the river lies wild Yan'an; east of it lies Jiaocheng, where steep roads and deep hills hide petty bandits. I ask that governors and provincial commanders be ordered to post troops at these crossings." He also wrote, "The army has reached Pingliang. Fuchen, with no way out, fights like a cornered beast; killing a hundred rebels is not worth the loss of one of our men. We should camp beneath the walls, wait at ease for his exhaustion, strike Guyuan hard, and cut his supply lines. Pingliang is poor country, unlike Hunan with its broad fields and abundant grain; the enemy cannot hold out long. Once supplies fail, his followers will scatter, and someone will surely cut off Fuchen's head and bring it to camp. If we chase east when the enemy moves east and west when he moves west, we will exhaust our troops, split our strength, lift the siege of Guyuan, and let rebel morale revive — that must not be allowed." All these memorials were referred to princes and ministers and put into effect.

十六年，擢工部侍郎。 十七年，以夏旱求言，疏言：「今日官至督撫，居莫敢誰何之勢，自非大賢，鮮不縱恣。 道府歲納規禮，加之以搜括，則道府所轄官民，不啻鬻之道府矣。 州縣歲納規禮，重之以勒索，則州縣所屬士民，不啻鬻之州縣矣。 世祖朝，山東巡按程衡劾巡撫耿焞，江南巡按秦世禎劾土國寶，皆置重典，天下肅然。 今巡按久停，雖欲議复，恐一時難得多人。 惟有出自上意，欲清一省，則選一人遣往，不必一時俱發。 出其不意，示以不測，使天下奸惡吏不敢恃督撫而肆志，即有不肖之督撫，亦莫敢庇貪而害民。 」疏入，報聞。
In the sixteenth year he was promoted to vice minister of works. In the seventeenth year, when drought prompted a call for advice, he wrote, "Governors-general and governors now stand where no one dares challenge them; unless a man is truly exceptional, few resist excess. Circuit intendants and prefects pay yearly gifts upward, and on top of that squeeze their jurisdictions — so the officials and people under them are virtually sold to their superiors. District magistrates pay gifts to prefects and add extortion of their own — so the gentry and common people under them are virtually sold to their magistrates. Under the Shunzhi Emperor, Shandong surveillance commissioner Cheng Heng impeached Governor Geng Tun and Jiangnan commissioner Qin Shizhen impeached Tu Guobao; both offenders received severe punishment, and the empire was awed into order. The surveillance commissioners have been suspended for so long that even if we debated restoring them, we could hardly find enough qualified men at once. Only when the emperor himself wishes to cleanse a province should a single man be chosen and sent; there is no need to launch them all together. Strike without warning and keep officials guessing, so that corrupt clerks everywhere dare not lean on their governors for impunity — and even unworthy governors dare not shelter the greedy at the people's expense. The memorial was submitted and acknowledged.

調戶部。 十八年，疏言：「國家有錢法以通有無、利民用，自秦、漢及唐、宋，公私皆悉用錢； 至金、元，以銀與錢鈔並行； 至明中葉，乃專資於銀。 闖逆之亂，或沉江河，或埋山谷，又以貪吏厚藏，銀益少，民益困。 今欲救天下之窮，惟有多鑄錢。 鑄錢所資，銅六鉛四，而可采之山，所司每深諱之，蓋恐時有時無，貽累償稅。 且上官聞其地開採，此挾彼制，誅求甚多也。 臣謂宜令天下產銅鉛之地，任民採取，有則以十分二輸稅於官，無則聽之州縣自行稽察，毋使多官旁撓。 報採多者予議敘，則官與民皆樂為，資以鼓鑄，錢不可勝用矣。 」下九卿詳議，擬例以上，得旨：「採銅關係國計，其令各督撫率屬殫力奉行。 」
He was transferred to the Board of Revenue. In the eighteenth year he wrote, "The state maintains coinage to move goods and serve the people. From Qin and Han through Tang and Song, public and private alike used cash; under Jin and Yuan, silver circulated with notes and coin; by mid-Ming, the empire relied on silver alone. In the turmoil of Li Zicheng's rebellion, silver was lost to rivers and buried in hills, while grasping officials hoarded still more — supply shrank and the people grew poorer. To relieve the empire's want today, nothing will serve but to mint more coin. Coinage needs six parts copper to four of lead, yet the offices that oversee mineable hills conceal them, fearing that when yields fluctuate they will be held liable for tax shortfalls. When superiors hear that a place has opened mines, they play one office against another and squeeze the region dry. I propose that wherever copper and lead occur, the people be free to mine; when ore is found, two-tenths should go to the state, and when none is found, let prefectures and districts inspect on their own — without a swarm of officials meddling from the side. Reward those who report rich yields with promotion, and officials and commoners alike will gladly supply the mint — coin will be more than enough." The memorial went to the Nine Ministers; after deliberation a regulation was submitted, and the emperor replied, "Copper mining bears on the national economy — let every governor and governor-general lead his subordinates to enforce this with all diligence. "

He passed the jinshi examination in the fifteenth year of Shunzhi, entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor, completed his probation, and was appointed a compiler. 國初以海上多事，下令遷東南各省沿海居民於內地，畫界而設之禁。 界外皆棄地，流民無所歸，去為盜。 及師定金門、廈門，總督姚啟聖請以界外地按籍還民，弛海禁，收魚鹽之利給軍食，廷臣持不可。 康熙二十二年，台灣平，上命以界外地還民。 會給事中傅感丁請以江、浙、閩、粵濱海界外地招徠開墾，乃命臻及內閣學士席柱赴福建、廣東察視展界，進臻工部尚書。 臻與席柱如廣東，自欽州防城始，遵海以東而北，歷府七、州三、縣二十九、衛六、所十七、巡檢司十六、台城堡砦二十一，還民地二萬八千一百九十二頃，復業丁口三萬一千三百。 復如福建，自福寧州西分水關始，遵海以北，歷府四、州一、縣二十四、衛四、所五、巡檢司三、關城鎮砦五十五，還民地二萬一千一十八頃，復業丁口四萬八百。 於是兩省濱海居民咸得復業。 別遣使察視江南、浙江展界復業，同時畢事。 臻以母喪還裡，席柱復命，奏陳濱海居民還鄉安業。 上曰：「民樂處海濱，以可出海經商捕魚，爾等知其故，前此何以不准議行？ 邊疆大臣當以國計民生為念，曩禁令雖嚴，私出海貿易初未嘗斷絕。 凡議出海貿易不可行者，皆總督、巡撫自圖射利故也。 」
Early in the dynasty, with the coast in turmoil, the court ordered coastal populations of the southeastern provinces moved inland, drew a boundary, and imposed strict prohibitions. Land beyond the line was left empty; displaced people with nowhere to go turned to banditry. After Kinmen and Xiamen were taken, Governor-General Yao Qisheng asked that abandoned coastal land be returned to its registered owners, sea bans eased, and profits from fish and salt applied to army rations — but court ministers opposed the plan. In Kangxi 22, once Taiwan was pacified, the emperor ordered coastal land beyond the boundary returned to the people. When Supervising Secretary Fu Ganding proposed opening abandoned coastal land in Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Fujian, and Guangdong, the emperor sent Zhen and Grand Secretary Xi Zhu to Fujian and Guangdong to inspect the boundary extension and promoted Zhen to minister of works. Zhen and Xi Zhu began in Guangdong at Fangcheng in Qinzhou and traced the coast east and north through seven prefectures, three subprefectures, twenty-nine counties, six garrisons, seventeen posts, sixteen inspection stations, and twenty-one forts and stockades — restoring 28,192 qing of land and 31,300 registered persons to their livelihoods. They then went to Fujian, starting west of Funing at Shuifeng Pass and following the coast north through four prefectures, one subprefecture, twenty-four counties, four garrisons, five posts, three inspection stations, and fifty-five passes, towns, forts, and stockades — restoring 21,018 qing and 40,800 registered persons. Coastal people in both provinces were thus able to resume their livelihoods. Other envoys were sent to inspect boundary extension and resettlement in Jiangnan and Zhejiang; all the work was finished at once. Zhen went home to mourn his mother; Xi Zhu returned and reported that coastal residents had gone back to their villages and resumed their trades. The emperor said, "People like living on the coast because they can trade and fish at sea — you knew that. Why did you not approve this before? Frontier ministers must think of the national interest and the people's livelihood. However strict the old bans, private overseas trade never really stopped. Whenever anyone says overseas trade must be forbidden, it is because governors and governors-general are lining their own pockets. "
29
臻喪終，起刑部尚書。 舊制，方冬獄囚月給煤，獄吏率乾沒，囚多以寒疾死，臻力禁之。 調兵部。 時議裁各省駐防及督、撫、提、鎮標兵，臻謂：「兵冗可裁而不宜驟行，請自今老弱、物故、額缺概不補，數歲額自減。 」從之。 再調禮部。 以疾告歸，尋卒於家。 上南巡，書「眷懷舊德」額追賜之。
When his mourning ended, he was recalled as minister of justice. Under the old rule, prisoners received monthly coal in winter, but jailers routinely stole it and many died of cold; Zhen forbade this firmly. He was transferred to the Board of War. When the court debated cutting provincial garrisons and the banner detachments of governors, governors-general, circuit intendants, and brigade commanders, Zhen argued, "Redundant troops can be cut, but not all at once — from now on do not fill posts left by the old, weak, dead, or vacant; in a few years the rolls will shrink on their own. The court agreed. He was transferred again to the Board of Rites. He asked leave on grounds of illness and went home; he died there soon after. On a southern tour the emperor wrote and posthumously bestowed a plaque reading "Cherishing an old worthy."
30
臻少貧力學，事祖母及父母孝，宏獎人才，詩文剴切中條理。
Zhen was poor as a youth but studied hard, served his grandmother and parents filially, encouraged talent generously, and wrote prose and verse that cut cleanly to the point.
31
薩穆哈，吳雅氏，滿洲正黃旗人。 順治十二年進士，授戶部主事，遷員外郎。
Sa Muha, of the Wuya clan, belonged to the Plain Yellow Banner. He passed the jinshi examination in Shunzhi 12, was appointed a principal clerk in the Board of Revenue, and rose to assistant department director.
32
康熙十二年，聖祖允吳三桂疏請撤籓，遣薩穆哈偕郎中黨務禮、席蘭泰，主事辛珠，筆帖式薩爾圖如貴州，具舟及芻粟，諭以毋騷擾，毋遲悮。 既至，三桂謀反，提督李本深與謀，書招貴州巡撫曹申吉，總督甘文焜得之，告薩穆哈等，趣詣京師告變，並請兵赴援。 薩穆哈與黨務禮、席蘭泰行至鎮遠，三桂已舉兵，鎮遠將吏得三桂檄，不給驛馬。 薩穆哈、黨務禮得馬二，馳至沅州。 乃乘驛，十一晝夜至京師，詣兵部，下馬喘急，抱柱不能言，久之始蘇，上三桂反狀。 席蘭泰自鎮遠乘小舟至常德，乃乘驛，後七日至。 辛珠、薩爾圖不及行，死之。 十三年，擢薩穆哈刑部郎中。 十四年，敘告變功，薩穆哈、黨務禮、席蘭泰並應升光祿、太僕諸卿。
In Kangxi 12 the emperor granted Wu Sangui's request to withdraw his fief and sent Sa Muha with Department Director Dang Wuli, Xilan Tai, Principal Clerk Xin Zhu, and Clerk Sa'ertu to Guizhou, supplying boats and fodder and ordering them to cause no disturbance and suffer no delay. On arrival they found Sangui plotting rebellion; Regional Commander Li Benshen joined him and wrote to summon Guizhou Governor Cao Shenji. Governor-General Gan Wenkun learned of it, told Sa Muha and the others, and urged them to race to the capital to report the mutiny and request troops. Sa Muha, Dang Wuli, and Xilan Tai reached Zhenyuan after Sangui had already risen; the local officers, having received his proclamation, refused them post horses. Sa Muha and Dang Wuli secured two horses and galloped to Yuanzhou. By relay they reached the capital in eleven days and nights, went straight to the Board of War, dismounted gasping, clutched a pillar speechless, and only after a long while recovered enough to report Sangui's rebellion. Xilan Tai left Zhenyuan by small boat for Changde, then by relay — arriving seven days later. Xin Zhu and Sa'ertu could not escape in time and were killed. In the thirteenth year Sa Muha was promoted to director in the Board of Punishments. In the fourteenth year, for reporting the mutiny, Sa Muha, Dang Wuli, and Xilan Tai were all eligible for promotion to director of the Court of Imperial Entertainments, minister of the imperial stud, and comparable posts.
33
十五年，授太僕寺卿。 十六年，再遷戶部侍郎。 命監賑山東。 十七年，還京師。 疏言：「臣屢奉使命，所過州縣，間有藉差科派民財，深滋擾累。 請嗣後有大事，特遣部院官，餘並責督撫料理。 」上為下廷臣會議，定州縣科斂俱視貪吏治罪。 調吏部。 二十年，再遷工部尚書。 二十一年，命察視石景山至盧溝橋石隄，疏言：「堤內本官地，康熙初招民墾荒，致侵損堤根。 請敕部免其賦，罷勿復耕。 」從之。 二十二年，命察視山西地震，疏請被災最重州縣發帑治賑。
In the fifteenth year he was appointed director of the Court of the Imperial Stud. In the sixteenth year he was again promoted to vice minister of revenue. He was ordered to supervise famine relief in Shandong. In the seventeenth year he returned to the capital. He memorialized, "I have repeatedly carried out imperial commissions, and in prefectures and districts along the way I have sometimes seen unlawful levies on the people's wealth under color of official business — a serious burden. I ask that hereafter major affairs alone warrant special envoys from the ministries and courts; everything else should be left to governors and governors-general." The emperor referred the memorial to court ministers, who decided that district and prefectural exactions would be punished like those of corrupt officials. He was transferred to the Board of Civil Appointments. In the twentieth year he was again promoted to minister of works. In the twenty-first year he was ordered to inspect the stone dike from Shijingshan to Lugou Bridge and wrote, "Land within the dike was originally government property; in early Kangxi settlers were recruited to open wasteland there, eroding the dike's base. I ask that the ministry remit its taxes and forbid further cultivation." The court agreed. In the twenty-second year he was sent to inspect the Shanxi earthquake and memorialized that the treasury be opened for relief in the hardest-hit prefectures and districts.
34
二十四年，河道總督靳輔請於高郵、寶應諸州縣築堤，束黃河注海，按察使於成龍主濬海口，下廷臣議，用輔策。 上詢日講官籍江南者，侍讀喬萊力請用成龍策。 上曰：「鄉官議如此，未知民意如何？ 」令薩穆哈與學士穆成額，會漕運總督徐旭齡、巡撫湯斌，詳察民間利害。 薩穆哈等行歷海口諸州縣，諸州縣民陳狀參差不一； 檄諸州縣，令各擇通達事體者十人詢利害，皆言濬海口不便。 二十五年，薩穆哈還奏，謂詳問居民，從成龍議； 積水不能施工，從輔議； 水中亦不能取土，請兩罷之。 是時成龍召詣京師，上命廷臣及薩穆哈、成龍再議。 成龍言濬海口當兼治串場河，費至百餘萬。 廷臣以為費鉅，疏請停。 未幾，斌入為尚書，奏言：「海口不急濬，再遇水，下游諸州縣悉付巨浸。 」上召問薩穆哈，薩穆哈不堅執前奏。 復下廷臣議，始定用成龍策。 上責薩穆哈前覆奏不實，奪官。 尋授步軍翼尉。
In the twenty-fourth year Yellow River Director Jin Fu proposed building dikes in Gaoyou, Baoying, and other counties to confine the Yellow River and send it to the sea, while Surveillance Commissioner Yu Chenglong urged dredging the estuary; court ministers deliberated and adopted Fu's plan. The emperor asked Daily Lecture officials from Jiangnan, and Reader-in-Waiting Qiao Lai strongly pressed Chenglong's plan. The emperor said, "Home-province officials argue this way — but what do the people think? " He ordered Sa Muha and Academician Mu Cheng'e, together with Grain Transport Director-General Xu Xuling and Governor Tang Bin, to investigate the matter thoroughly among the people. Sa Muha and the others toured the estuary counties; petitions from the people were inconsistent; they ordered each county to choose ten knowledgeable locals to weigh costs and benefits — all said dredging the estuary was impracticable. In the twenty-fifth year Sa Muha returned and reported that, after questioning residents in detail, he favored Chenglong's plan; with standing water, construction was impossible, so he also favored Fu's plan; and since earth could not be taken from the water, he asked that both be abandoned. Chenglong was then summoned to the capital, and the emperor ordered court ministers, Sa Muha, and Chenglong to debate the matter again. Chenglong said dredging the estuary would also require treating the Chuan Chang River, at a cost of more than a million taels. Court ministers judged the expense too great and memorialized to stop. Before long Tang Bin entered the capital as minister and wrote, "If the estuary is not dredged soon and floods return, every downstream county will be drowned. " The emperor summoned Sa Muha and questioned him; Sa Muha did not stand by his earlier report. The matter went back to court ministers, and Chenglong's plan was finally adopted. The emperor rebuked Sa Muha for his earlier reply and stripped him of rank. He was soon appointed wing commander of the Metropolitan Infantry.
35
三十二年，仍授工部尚書。 三十九年，上察知工部積弊，河工糜帑，受請託，發​​銀多侵蝕，詰責薩穆哈等。 薩穆哈尋以老疾乞罷，上斥其偽詐，命奪官，仍留任，察工部積弊，一一自列。 四十三年，以疏濬京師內外河道侵蝕帑銀，薩穆哈得賕，逮治擬絞。 卒於獄。
In the thirty-second year he was again appointed minister of works. In the thirty-ninth year the emperor uncovered long-standing abuses in the Board of Works — river works wasting treasury funds, favors traded for bribes, disbursed silver largely stolen — and rebuked Sa Muha and the rest. Sa Muha soon asked to retire on grounds of age and illness; the emperor called him deceitful, stripped his rank, yet kept him in office to investigate the Board of Works' accumulated abuses and list every one himself. In the forty-third year, because dredging rivers inside and outside the capital involved embezzling treasury silver, Sa Muha was found to have taken bribes; he was arrested, tried, and sentenced to strangulation. He died in prison.
36
論曰：米思翰贊撤籓之議，綢繆軍食，足以支十年，知定謀有由也。 顧八代、瑪爾漢皆文臣，能克敵，復以廉勤建績。 六善於軍事有建白，收綠旗之用，其效著於後矣。 臻巡复海疆，兵後一大政也。 薩穆哈以告變受賞，亦附著於斯篇。
The commentators say: Mi Sihan backed withdrawal of the fiefs and laid in army provisions enough for ten years — proof that the settled plan rested on solid grounds. Gu Badai and Ma Erhan were civil officials who could beat the enemy and built their reputations on integrity and hard work. Liushan offered sound military counsel and put Green Banner troops to effective use — with results that showed later. Zhen's tour to restore the maritime frontier was a major postwar policy. Sa Muha was rewarded for reporting the mutiny and is therefore included here as well.
