1
法蘭西一名佛郎機，在歐羅巴之西。 清來廣東互巿，廣東總督佟養甲疏言：「佛郎機國人寓居濠境澳門，與粵商互巿，仍禁深入省會。 」法人素崇天主教，康熙以來，屢禁漢人入教。
France, also known by the older name Folanggi, lies in western Europe. Under the Qing, the French began reciprocal trade at Guangdong. Governor Tong Yangjia of Guangdong reported: "Folanggi nationals live at Macau in the Haojing quarter and trade with Cantonese merchants, but they are still barred from penetrating inland to the provincial capital." " The French were devoted to Catholicism, and since the Kangxi era the court had repeatedly forbidden Han Chinese to convert.
2
九年五月，法公使布林布隆以進京換約為名，隨英公使普魯斯赴天津，拒不納，致傷敗數百人，折回上海，聲言調兵復仇。 未幾，法人復北駛，分擾登、青等處。 十年六月，隨英來攻，連陷新河、唐兒沽北岸砲台，遂入天津。 先是遣西寧辦事大臣文俊、武備院卿恆祺往議，不報。 至是，又遣桂良、恆福為欽差大臣，往津會議，冀緩師，而法與英益恣要求。 初，津約原許補法軍費二百萬，英四百萬。 至是，英索倍加，法欲照英數，復要求天津通商、京師長駐。 朝旨不許。 乃隨英督兵北上，進逼通州，京師戒嚴。 怡親王載垣等再議和，不就。 進薄京師。 八月，恭親王奕訢留守，再議和。 九月，和議成，所得通商、軍費、權利與英等，而傳教、建堂初無限制。 十月，始定傳教之人須薙須服中國衣冠，其入內地，預領中、法合同護照，向所過地方官鈐印，以為信據。 法人以江南為新許商埠，欲早通商，請助剿粵賊，不許。 十一年二月，法公使布林布隆偕英使普魯斯由津如京，此為各國公使駐京之始。 先是條約有還清軍費始行退出廣東省城之議。 至是，法人哥士耆來言，原先撤兵退出粵城，並求廣東籓署賃作領事署，又索還京城及各省天主堂舊基，均許之。 九月，交還廣東省城。
In the fifth month of the ninth year of Xianfeng, the French minister Bourboulon, claiming he was going to Beijing to exchange treaties, accompanied the British minister Bruce to Tianjin. When China refused them entry, several hundred men were killed or wounded; they withdrew to Shanghai and threatened to bring troops for revenge. Soon afterward French forces sailed north again and harried Dengzhou, Qingdao, and other points along the coast. In the sixth month of the tenth year of Xianfeng they joined the British attack, took the north-bank batteries at Xinhe and Tanggu in succession, and advanced into Tianjin. Earlier the court had sent the Xining commissioner Wen Jun and Wubeiyuan director Hengqi to negotiate, but received no response. The court then appointed Guiliang and Hengfu imperial commissioners to confer at Tianjin in hopes of easing military pressure, but the French and British only pressed their demands more aggressively. The Tianjin treaty had originally set indemnities at two million taels for France and four million for Britain. Britain now demanded double the original sum; France wanted parity with Britain and also insisted on opening Tianjin to trade and establishing permanent legations in the capital. The court refused. They then marched north with British forces, pressing toward Tongzhou, and Beijing was placed under martial law. Prince Yi Zaiyuan and others tried to reopen peace talks, without success. The allied armies closed on Beijing. In the eighth month Prince Gong Yixin stayed behind in the capital and reopened negotiations. In the ninth month peace was concluded. France gained trade concessions, indemnities, and privileges on par with Britain, and at first there were no limits on missionary activity or church construction. In the tenth month regulations were set: missionaries had to shave their heads and wear Chinese dress; to enter the interior they had to carry a joint Sino-French passport stamped by each local official they passed. With the lower Yangtze newly opened to trade, the French sought early commercial access and asked China to help suppress the Taiping rebels in Guangdong; the court refused. In the second month of the eleventh year of Xianfeng, Bourboulon and Bruce traveled from Tianjin to Beijing—marking the start of permanent foreign legations in the capital. The treaty had stipulated that French troops would leave Guangzhou only after the indemnity was paid in full. Gros then announced that France would withdraw from Guangzhou first, asked to lease the Guangdong provincial yamen as a consulate, and demanded the restoration of former Catholic church properties in Beijing and the provinces—all of which was granted. In the ninth month Guangzhou was returned to Chinese control.
3
九年夏五月，天津民擊殺法領事豐大業。 初，天津喧傳天主教堂迷拐幼孩，抉眼割心為藥料，人情洶洶。 三口通商大臣崇厚等詣法領事豐大業赴堂同訊，觀者麕集。 偶與教堂人違言，磚石相拋擊，豐大業怒，徑至崇厚署忿詈，至擬以洋槍。 出遇劉傑，復以槍擊傷某僕，遂群起毆斃豐大業，鳴鑼集眾，焚毀教堂、洋房數處，教民及洋人死者數十人。 事聞，命大學士直隸總督曾國籓赴津查辦。 國籓至津，示諭士民，宣佈懷柔外國、息事安民之意。 法公使羅淑亞來見，以四事相要：曰賠修教堂； 曰埋葬豐大業； 曰查辦地方官； 曰懲究兇手。 尋牒請將府、縣官及提督陳國瑞抵罪，國籓拒之。 與崇厚會奏，稱：「仁慈堂查出男女，訊無被拐情事，懇降諭各省，俾士民咸知謠傳多系虛誣，請將道、府、縣三員均撤任查辦。 」奏入，報可。 遂於八月擬結，辦為首十數人，天津府、縣減戍黑龍江。
In the fifth month of summer in the ninth year of Tongzhi, Tianjin residents killed the French consul Henri Fontanier. Rumors had spread through Tianjin that Catholic churches were kidnapping children and using their eyes and hearts for medicine, and public anger was running high. Trade commissioner Chonghou and others went with Fontanier to the church to investigate together, while a dense crowd of onlookers gathered. A quarrel broke out with church staff and stones began flying. Fontanier stormed into Chonghou's office, cursed him furiously, and reached for his pistol. Outside he encountered Liu Jie, shot and wounded a servant, and the mob then beat him to death. Gongs summoned more rioters, who burned several churches and foreign buildings; dozens of converts and foreigners were killed. On report of the incident, Grand Secretary and Zhili governor Zeng Guofan was sent to Tianjin to investigate. Zeng Guofan reached Tianjin and issued proclamations urging restraint toward foreigners and calm among the populace. French minister Rochechouart came to negotiate and demanded four things: compensation to rebuild the churches; a proper burial for Fontanier; punishment of local officials; and prosecution of the rioters. France soon demanded that the prefect, county magistrate, and provincial commander Chen Guorui be punished; Zeng Guofan refused. Zeng and Chonghou jointly reported that orphans at the Benevolent Hall showed no sign of kidnapping, asked the throne to inform every province that the rumors were largely false, and recommended removing the circuit intendant, prefect, and county magistrate pending investigation. " The memorial was approved. In the eighth month a settlement was reached: over a dozen ringleaders were punished, and the Tianjin prefect and county magistrate were sentenced to exile in Heilongjiang.
4
十一年，法遣全權大臣熱福里如京換約，並進書籍。 十二年，穆宗親政，各國請覲見，法與焉。 是年法人侵越南，入河內省城。
In the eleventh year of Tongzhi, France sent plenipotentiary Gerofilo to Beijing to exchange treaties and presented books as gifts. In the twelfth year, after the Tongzhi Emperor began personal rule, foreign powers requested audiences, France included. That year French forces invaded Vietnam and occupied Hanoi.
5
越南向隸籓屬，自法據西貢，脅越人訂約，許于紅江通舟。 曾紀澤與法外部言：「法、越私立之約，中國不能認。 」不省。 八年二月，法兵船由西貢駛至海防進口。 三月，陷河內省。 朝議始遣提督黃桂蘭等軍出關。 既而法公使寶海向北洋大臣李鴻章要求中國退兵，及通商保勝，驅逐盜賊，畫紅江南北為界。 朝廷下各督撫議。 法人見不允所求，遂欲增軍撤使以相恫喝。
Vietnam had long been a Qing tributary. After France seized Saigon, it forced treaties on Vietnam allowing navigation on the Red River. Zeng Jize told the French foreign ministry: "China cannot recognize treaties concluded privately between France and Vietnam." " France paid no heed. In the second month of the eighth year of Guangxu, French warships sailed from Saigon into Haiphong. In the third month they captured Hanoi. The court finally ordered Provincial Commander Huang Guilan and other units across the border. French minister Bourée then demanded of Li Hongzhang that China withdraw its troops, open Baosheng to trade, expel bandits, and fix the boundary along both banks of the Red River. The court referred the matter to provincial governors for discussion. When China refused, France threatened to reinforce its army and recall its minister.
6
九年三月，戰事起。 法據南定，旋為劉永福所敗。 會越王薨，法以兵脅嗣王立新約二十七條，盡攘其兵權、利權、政權，並申明越境全歸保護，中國不得干預。 中國聞之，乃命唐炯、徐延旭出關，彭玉麟辦粵防，張佩綸會辦軍務。 會山西、北寧連陷，官軍退守太原，法乘勢擾浙、閩，陷基隆、澎湖，至是始宣戰。 十年二月，諒山大捷，法忽請和，帝命吳大澂、陳寶琛、張佩綸會辦海防，以議和全權任李鴻章。 先是福祿諾所擬五條，僅允不索兵費，不入滇境，而要脅中國不再與聞越事。 議久不決。 五月，法兵以巡防為名，忽攻諒山，敗走。 藉口中國不能如約退師，責賠費，不允。 法使巴德諾出京。 六月，攻臺北基隆，為劉銘傳所敗。 秋七月，法水師提督孤拔等率兵船入閩，泊馬尾等處，迫交船廠，欲據為質。 時張佩綸以會辦海防兼船政大臣，漫不設備，法遂開砲毀船廠。 復分兵擾東京、臺灣，陷基隆，窺諒山。 十一年春正月，犯鎮南關，楊玉科戰沒。 旋收復，大創之，並砲斃孤拔於南洋。 法人乃請和，原照天津原約，不索償款。 李鴻章與議新約十條：一，法自行弭亂，華不派兵赴北圻； 二，法與越自立約，或已定或續立，中、越往來，不礙中國威望體面，亦不違此次約； 三，六個月會勘界，北圻界處或稍改正，以期兩益； 四，法保護人民欲過界入中國，邊員給照，華人入越，請法給照； 五，保勝以西、諒山以北通商，華設關，法設領事，北圻亦可駐華領事； 六，三個月內會定商款，法運越貨稅照他處較減； 七，法在北圻造鐵路，中國若造鐵路，雇法工； 八，此約十年再修； 九，法即退基隆，二月內臺灣、澎湖全退，中、法前約照舊等語。 旋法派戈可當代為駐華公使，欲改前約，出所擬二十四條。 鴻章以與原約不符，不許。 戈使又欲辦滇、粵礦務，及製造土貨，運越南食鹽，復拒之。 又欲於雲南省城及廣西內地設領事。 時正遣鄧承修、周德潤與法勘界，鴻章謂宜俟邊界勘明，方能指定通商碼頭。 戈使又要求稅則減半，鴻章祗允五分減一。 又另擬通商章程十八款，並將互交逃犯、洋藥進出口各條亦擬在內。 法使復援咸豐八年約內第七款有「工作」二字，仍要求增入在口製造，許之。
In the third month of the ninth year of Guangxu, fighting broke out. France took Nam Dinh but was soon defeated by Liu Yongfu's Black Flag forces. When the Vietnamese king died, France forced the new king to sign a twenty-seven-article treaty stripping Vietnam of military, fiscal, and political authority, placing the whole country under French protection and barring Chinese interference. China then ordered Tang Jiong and Xu Yanxu across the border, put Peng Yulin in charge of Guangdong defenses, and assigned Zhang Peilun to joint military planning. After Son Tay and Bac Ninh fell, Chinese forces retreated to Taiyuan. France then raided Zhejiang and Fujian, captured Keelung and Penghu, and only then did China declare war. In the second month of the tenth year of Guangxu, a major victory at Lang Son led France to sue for peace. The emperor put Wu Dacheng, Chen Baochen, and Zhang Peilun in charge of coastal defense and gave Li Hongzhang full authority to negotiate. Fournier's earlier five-point draft offered only to waive indemnities and refrain from entering Yunnan, while demanding that China cease all involvement in Vietnam. Talks dragged on without agreement. In the fifth month French troops, claiming they were on patrol, suddenly attacked Lang Son and were driven back in defeat. France claimed China had failed to withdraw as agreed and demanded compensation; China refused. French minister Patenôtre left Beijing. In the sixth month they attacked Keelung in northern Taiwan and were repulsed by Liu Mingchuan. In the seventh month Courbet led the French fleet into Fujian waters, anchored at Mawei, and demanded the Foochow Navy Yard as security. Zhang Peilun, serving as both joint coastal-defense commissioner and shipyard superintendent, had made no preparations, and the French opened fire and destroyed the yard. French forces then raided Tonkin and Taiwan again, reoccupied Keelung, and threatened Lang Son. In the first month of the eleventh year of Guangxu they attacked Zhennan Pass, where Yang Yuke was killed in action. Chinese forces soon retook the pass and inflicted a crushing defeat; Courbet was killed by shellfire off Taiwan. France then sued for peace on the basis of the earlier Tianjin terms, without demanding an indemnity. Li Hongzhang negotiated a new ten-article treaty. First, France would pacify Tonkin on its own and China would not send troops there; second, treaties between France and Vietnam, existing or future, and Sino-Vietnamese relations would not impair China's prestige or violate this agreement; third, within six months commissioners would survey the border and might adjust the Tonkin line where both sides benefited; fourth, French protected persons crossing into China would carry passes issued by border officials, and Chinese entering Vietnam would obtain French passes; fifth, trade would open west of Baosheng and north of Lang Son; China would establish customs posts and France consulates, and China might station consuls in Tonkin as well; sixth, tariffs would be fixed within three months, with reduced duties on French goods transshipped through Vietnam; seventh, France might build railways in Tonkin, and if China built railways it would hire French engineers; eighth, the treaty would be revised after ten years; ninth, France would evacuate Keelung immediately and leave Taiwan and Penghu within two months, with earlier Sino-French agreements left in force. France soon appointed Cogordan minister to China; he sought to revise the treaty and submitted twenty-four new articles. Li Hongzhang refused, saying they conflicted with the original agreement. Cogordan also sought mining rights in Yunnan and Guangdong, permission to manufacture local goods, and to import Vietnamese salt—all refused. He also asked to open consulates in the Yunnan capital and in inland Guangxi. Deng Chengxiu and Zhou Derun were then surveying the border with France; Li Hongzhang said trading ports could be designated only after the boundary was settled. Cogordan demanded a fifty-percent tariff cut; Li Hongzhang agreed only to a twenty-percent reduction. He also drafted eighteen commercial regulations, including provisions on extradition and the import and export of opium. The French minister cited the word "work" in Article 7 of the 1858 treaty to demand the right to manufacture in treaty ports, and China conceded.
7
時雲南界務，周德潤會商岑毓英後，出關與法使狄隆晤商，擬先勘保勝上游一二段，並同擬全局辦法八條：一，中、法兩國勘界大臣等說明所應勘之界，俱是現在之界； 一，勘現界後，或有改正之處，兩國勘界大臣公同商酌，如彼此意見不合，各請旨商辦； 一，續開勘雲、越交界，中國大臣等意欲一律勘完，所以照會法國請旨； 一，各大臣等商議先由老街勘到龍膊河，及龍膊河鄰近地方，復回老街，再勘老街鄰近地方； 一，勘老街至龍膊河之界，中、法繪圖各官從紅江南岸歸，一路同走，中國繪圖官歸法國保護，自老街起至龍膊河止，兩國勘界大臣等各走雲、越邊界； 一，紅河自北河岸之老鏊至南岸之龍膊，以河中為界； 一，雲、越之界，遇有以河為界，均以河中為界，如有全河現在歸中國界者，仍歸中國，現在歸越南界者，仍歸越南； 一，勘界時隨處開節略圖說，均由兩國大臣等畫押。 以上節略，彼此畫押遵守。 德潤與狄隆各按地圖校改，互有爭執，而於大小賭呪河、猛援、猛賴兩段，爭執尤力。 會法勘路弁兵在者蘭被越遊勇所戕，法指為雲南提督散勇，中國不承，狄隆欲緩勘，但就圖定界。 粵東、粵西界務，鄧承修與張之洞、李秉衡等會商，其與法使浦理燮在關門文淵會議。 承修執約內「北圻邊界必要更正，以期兩國有益」之語，欲以諒山迤西自艽葑、高平省至保樂州，東自祿平、那陽、先妥州至海寧府劃歸中界。 浦使以據約不過於兩邊界址略為更改，不能及諒山及東西地。 旋允請示本國，卒不行。 十二年復議界，會浦理燮病，僅由鎮南起勘至平關而止，東西不過三百餘里，餘未履勘。 浦理燮旋回國，法改派狄隆由滇赴粵，與鄧承修等議界。
For the Yunnan border, Zhou Derun consulted Cen Yuying, crossed the frontier to meet French commissioner Dilhonne, agreed to survey first one or two sections upstream of Baosheng, and drafted eight general principles: first, both sides would survey only the existing boundary; second, after surveying, any corrections would be negotiated jointly, with disputes referred to both governments; third, China wished to complete the entire Yunnan-Vietnam border survey and so notified France to seek instructions; fourth, they agreed to survey from Laokai to the Longbo River and its vicinity, return to Laokai, then survey the Laokai area; fifth, for the Laokai–Longbo sector, Chinese and French surveyors would travel together along the south bank of the Red River under French escort, while commissioners walked the Chinese and Vietnamese sides respectively; sixth, from Lao'ao on the north bank to Longbo on the south, the river's mid-channel would be the boundary; seventh, wherever a river formed the border, the mid-channel would divide it; rivers wholly on the Chinese side would remain Chinese, and those wholly on the Vietnamese side would remain Vietnamese; eighth, provisional maps and notes made during the survey would be signed by commissioners of both sides. Both parties signed these provisions and pledged to abide by them. Zhou Derun and Dilhonne revised maps against each other and clashed repeatedly, especially over the Greater and Lesser Duzhou Rivers and the Mengyuan and Menglai sectors. When French survey troops were killed by Vietnamese irregulars at Zhelan, France blamed Yunnan's provincial commander; China denied it, and Dilhonne proposed suspending the survey and fixing the line from maps alone. For the Guangdong border, Deng Chengxiu consulted Zhang Zhidong and Li Bingheng, then met French commissioner Pouliche at Guanmen and Wenyuan. Chengxiu cited the treaty's clause on correcting the Tonkin border for mutual benefit and sought to bring within China's line the territory from Mangcang and Gaoping west of Lang Son to Baole, and from Luping, Nayang, and Xiantuo east to Haining. Pouliche replied that the treaty allowed only minor adjustments along the existing line, not changes reaching Lang Son or the eastern and western regions. He agreed to refer the matter to Paris, but the proposal was ultimately rejected. In the twelfth year boundary talks resumed, but Pouliche fell ill; survey ran only from Zhennan Pass to Pingguan—about three hundred li east to west—with the rest unsurveyed. Pouliche soon returned to France; France reassigned Dilhonne from Yunnan to Guangdong to negotiate the border with Deng Chengxiu and others.
8
先是鴻章欲先議界，後議商約，法使不從，乃復議商約。 至是議成十九款：一，保勝以上某處、諒山以北某處，中國設關通商，許法設立領事； 二，中國可在河內、海防二處設立領事，並可商酌在北圻他處設領事，惟須後日； 三，兩國領事駐紮及商民通商，均須優待； 四，中國人在越置地建屋，及官商往來公文、書信、電報，法允保護遞送； 五，兩國遊歷人過界，各發給護照； 六、七，出口貨照稅則三分減一，進口貨照稅則五分減一，估價之貨為稅則所未載者，進出口仍照值百抽五徵收，至洋土各貨赴內地買賣，應完子口稅，不在減徵之列； 八、九，載明洋、土各貨在邊關已完稅，復轉運通商各海關者，均照海關稅則另收正稅，不以邊關單作抵，其在邊關所領存票，亦只准在邊關抵稅，概不發還現銀； 十至十二，嚴防詐偽偷漏之法； 十三，定洋人自用雜物免稅之法； 十四，定洋、土各藥不准販運買賣； 十五，米穀等糧不准販運出中國邊關，進關准免稅，違禁物各禁； 十六，中國商民僑居越南，所有命案、賦稅、詞訟等件，法國應優待； 十七，中國人犯罪，照中律，法領事宜拘送，不得庇匿； 十八、十九，定條約續修期限及互換遵守各事。 是為滇粵邊界通商約。
Li Hongzhang had wanted to settle the boundary before the commercial treaty, but the French minister refused, so talks returned to the commercial treaty. Nineteen articles were then agreed: first, at designated points above Baosheng and north of Lang Son, China would open customs stations for trade and allow France to appoint consuls; second, China might open consulates at Hanoi and Haiphong and consult on consulates elsewhere in Tonkin, but only at a later date; third, consuls of both states and their merchants were to receive preferential treatment in residence and trade; fourth, Chinese in Vietnam might acquire land and build houses; France would protect and forward official and commercial documents, letters, and telegrams; fifth, travelers crossing the frontier would each be issued passports by their own government; sixth and seventh, export duties were cut by one-third and import duties by one-fifth under the tariff; unlisted goods were still taxed at five percent ad valorem; foreign and native goods sold inland remained subject to transit dues and were excluded from the reductions; eighth and ninth, goods taxed at border stations and re-shipped to treaty ports paid full customs duties there without credit for border payments; deposit tickets issued at the border could offset duties only at the border and were never redeemed in silver; articles ten through twelve set strict rules against fraud and smuggling; thirteenth, fixed rules exempting foreigners' personal effects from duty; fourteenth, prohibited the transport and sale of foreign and native drugs; fifteenth, grain might not be exported through China's border passes but entered duty-free; other contraband was likewise banned; sixteenth, France would treat Chinese merchants residing in Vietnam preferentially in criminal cases, taxation, and litigation; seventeenth, Chinese offenders were to be punished under Chinese law; French consuls must detain and surrender them and not harbor fugitives; eighteenth and nineteenth, fixed the period for treaty revision and procedures for exchange and mutual compliance. This became the Yunnan–Guangdong border trade treaty.

商約既定，鄧承修即赴欽州之東興與狄隆議勘東界。 狄隆以中國所屬江平、黃竹、白龍尾為越境。 鄧承修以數地皆內地，有圖可據，不許。 辯論不洽。 狄隆又約履勘，承修欲照雲南分途履勘辦法，並請先撤江平法兵。 越日，復議請旨立約三條：一，大段相合； 二，較圖不合，作為未定，各請示本國； 三，勒其去江平之兵及辦事官員。 又令以後未定界內，不得再派兵及官員前往。 狄隆不允，轉要承修不得於未定界內駐兵。 時張之洞所派道員王之春、李興銳亦與會議。 議界將及一年，中國屢請撤兵，法兵分屯江平、黃竹、石角、句冬、白龍尾等處如故。 會總署允承修所定三條，承修命王之春往議，狄隆執不允，而法人突以兵踞白龍尾，驅害汛兵。 華民築營壘，承修詰令撤退，狄隆諉之。 時桂界已校竣，欽界南自嘉隆河、北抵北侖十萬山分茅嶺、西至峒中墟北，亦允歸中國，而白龍、江平，狄隆謂須以商務抵換。 又以九頭山未議，及之春與議，亦無效。 狄隆又欲議海界，以津約所無，未奉旨議海界，卻之。 法又欲以白龍、江平抵換龍州通商。 初恭思當來華也，即有求改商約之請，總署以界務方殷，且商約既經畫押，何能議改？ 拒之。 至是復以為請，並以商務苟可通融，界務亦可稍讓。 稱已奉本國訓，准令在京商辦。 總署以狄隆與鄧承修議界久不決，允與商辦。 恭思當始允中國廣東邊界除現在勘界大臣劃定外，所有白龍尾及江平、黃竹一帶地方，並雲南邊界前歸另議之南丹山以北、西至狗頭寨、東至清水河一帶地方，均歸中國管轄。 又議減稅，總署以俄國通商章程辦有成案，滇、桂邊界皆為陸路，不得不酌議減稅，以歸平允。 於是議進口稅減十分之三，出口稅減十分之四，滇土藥每百斤定稅釐各二十兩，必完釐者，方准法商完稅接買，並不准法、越商人往入內地販運，高平、諒山往來之船隻免徵稅，仍納船錢，惟運販食鹽、接辦鐵路及越南與滇、粵通商進出口稅則，均請減半，運中國土貨往中國各海口，稅則減三分之一各節，均拒絕刪節。 計訂商務續約十條，界務續約四條。 又照會緩設領事，及法在龍、蒙等處之領事等官，不得設立租界二端。 是為與法勘界通商續約。
Once the commercial treaty was signed, Deng Chengxiu went to Dongxing in Qinzhou to survey the eastern border with Dilhonne. Dilhonne claimed Jiangping, Huangzhu, and Bailongwei—Chinese territory—as Vietnamese land. Deng Chengxiu refused, citing maps showing all were interior Chinese territory. Talks broke down. Dilhonne proposed an on-site survey; Chengxiu wanted the Yunnan practice of separate routes and demanded French troops withdraw from Jiangping first. The next day they again sought imperial approval for a three-article pact: first, where the line largely agreed; second, disputed stretches would remain undecided pending instructions from both governments; third, French troops and officials must leave Jiangping. They also barred further troop or official deployments in undemarcated areas. Dilhonne refused and instead demanded that Chengxiu not garrison troops in disputed territory. Circuit intendants Wang Zhichun and Li Xingrui, sent by Zhang Zhidong, also joined the talks. After nearly a year of talks China repeatedly asked France to withdraw, but French troops still held Jiangping, Huangzhu, Shijiao, Goudong, Bailongwei, and other posts. When the Zongli Yamen approved Chengxiu's three articles, he sent Wang Zhichun to negotiate; Dilhonne still refused, and French troops suddenly seized Bailongwei and attacked Chinese garrison troops. Chinese civilians built fortifications; Chengxiu demanded their removal, but Dilhonne disclaimed responsibility. The Guangxi line was already settled; from the Jialong River south to Fenmaoling on Beilun's Shiwanshan ridge and west to north of Dongzhong market was conceded to China, but Dilhonne insisted Bailongwei and Jiangping must be traded for commercial concessions. Jiutoushan remained unsettled; even Wang Zhichun's participation changed nothing. Dilhonne also sought to negotiate the maritime boundary; China refused, citing the Tianjin treaty and the lack of imperial authorization. France also offered to trade Bailongwei and Jiangping for opening Longzhou to commerce. When Gongsin first arrived in China he had already asked to revise the commercial treaty; the Yamen refused, citing urgent border affairs and the treaty already having been initialed. The request was refused. He renewed the request, saying that if commercial terms could be adjusted, border concessions might follow. He said he had received instructions from Paris authorizing negotiations in Beijing. Because Dilhonne and Deng Chengxiu had deadlocked on the border, the Yamen agreed to commercial talks. Gongsin initially conceded that beyond what the commissioners were then surveying, Bailongwei, Jiangping, Huangzhu, and the Yunnan tract north of Nandanshan between Goutouzhai and the Qingshui River would remain under Chinese jurisdiction. On tariff reductions the Yamen cited Russian precedents and held that overland routes on the Yunnan and Guangxi frontiers required moderated duties for equity. Import duties were cut by thirty percent and export duties by forty; Yunnan native drugs were taxed at twenty taels of lijin per hundred jin before French merchants might buy them; French and Vietnamese traders were barred from the interior; vessels between Gaoping and Lang Son paid no duty but still paid tonnage fees. China rejected French demands to halve duties on salt, railways, and Vietnam–Yunnan–Guangdong trade, and to cut tariffs on native goods shipped to treaty ports by one-third. In all, ten commercial supplementary articles and four boundary supplementary articles were concluded. Notes also deferred consular establishments and barred French consuls at Longzhou, Mengzi, and elsewhere from creating concessions. This was the supplementary Sino-French treaty on boundary survey and trade.

In the fourteenth year of Guangxu (1888), the French consul, citing frequent Chinese visits to Haiphong, asked Lianzhou to order shipowners to obtain consular licenses on pain of detention. Zhang Zhidong replied that the treaty said nothing of the kind and that ships of all nations used Haiphong—why bar Chinese vessels alone? He later learned the consul had posted notices charging ship fees of several to several tens taels each, claiming authority from the French minister. Zhang Zhidong asked the Yamen to halt the levies. That year France asked to link its telegraph lines with Guangdong's, and permission was granted. French troops from Mang Cai also crossed the border to burn and plunder Nasha; Zhang Zhidong urged the Yamen to demand compensation from the French minister. In the fifteenth year of Guangxu (1889), French vessels entered Yulin Harbor, a hundred li east of Yazhou in Qiongzhou, to survey the channel and plant markers on shore; they were stopped. The French consul also levied license fees on fishing boats at Beihai; the government refused, holding this an infringement of sovereignty. In the tenth month boundary commissioner Li Shoutong and a French judge surveyed the river line near Dongxing, agreeing that future sandbars would belong to whichever side they lay nearer, that shifting channels would not alter navigation rights, and that both states might sail the river regardless of jurisdiction. That year the French minister protested Chinese troops stationed at Banbang in Vietnam. He also claimed Nasha market lay outside China, on the north bank of the Xian'an River opposite Hengmo commune in Tonkin, near Banbang. He further said that last winter's reception of routed bandits by Chinese troops referred to the north bank opposite Ningyang Temple, eight li from Mang Cai. An investigation was ordered. Li Shoutong later telegraphed: "The western prefectural boundary runs from Bazhuang through Banxing, Banshan, and Lengdong. A ditch three li from Vietnamese Dongzhong serves as the line. By compass bearing, Lengdong lies chou-gen-yin, Dongzhong wei-kun-shen, and Nasha northwest at xu-qian-hai. Dongzhong market stands between two ditches whose waters flow west into Xian'an province." By orientation, land southwest of the ditch is broadly Vietnamese and northwest broadly Chinese. By village jurisdiction, Nasha and Bandong belong to Jiayan commune, not to Hengmo commune centered at Dongzhong. By proximity to the border, north of Nasha through Nahuaai lies Beiyan, about twenty-five li away, in Guangxi's Shangsi prefecture. On the imperial commissioner's map Nahuaai is Chinese territory; Nasha adjoins it only three li away and had no market before. Last year in the first month Chinese from Dongzhong market first moved here. Before the eleventh month last year the French had not crossed the ditch; in the twelfth month they burned Nasha market, abducted women, then paid silver for their release. The French judge told the women themselves it had been a mistaken capture across the border. The border map further shows Banbang Pass to the northwest in Guangxi. Natives also said another Banbang sixty li southwest of Hengmo lies in Vietnamese territory. East of Dongzhong there is no Banbang, only Banben about nine li away on interior soil. Last autumn a Cuizhou garrison camped there but withdrew to Banxing because of plague; Banben now has no troops. Ningyang lies more than ten li from Mang Cai, southwest of Dongxing across a river reachable only by boat; even if troops were present they could hardly have sheltered bandits there, and none were. —and similar points." Feng Zicai's telegram agreed. Zhang Zhidong held that the dispute arose because Chinese took the ditch as the boundary while France took the north bank of the Xian'an River. The ditch was the river, but the original maps specified neither. Nasha was a market founded only last year in the first month, very near the line, which provoked the quarrel. Once it was clear that Banbang Pass was a separate place in Guangxi, the French claim collapsed.

In the ninth month of the sixteenth year of Guangxu (1890), eighteen fugitives including Wei Minggao were returned. In the eighth month of the seventeenth year of Guangxu (1891), French minister Lin Chun revised Article 14 of the New Caledonia labor-recruitment contract. China had proposed a "consular agent" under Article 14; Lin Chun refused and substituted "superintendent of Chinese labor," whose powers were limited to bringing suits in court and hiring counsel. Li Hongzhang objected that the revised title differed little from a labor broker and afforded no real power of protection. He refused. As Hunan was attacking foreign missions, the French consul sought to open a wharf and church in Changsha but was blocked. In the fourth month of the nineteenth year of Guangxu (1893), France asked to link telegraph lines between Dongxing and Mang Cai. The Guangdong governor replied that dozens of li of border from earlier cases remained unsettled; who would protect an undemarcated stretch if lines were joined hastily? It would only breed further disputes. He urged that the boundary be settled first. In the twentieth year of Guangxu (1894), French minister Nichigai presented his credentials. He also discussed reducing the poll tax on Chinese in Vietnam and raised the Siam frontier. Li Hongzhang cited Anglo-French talks on the Siam frontier and argued that a buffer strip should revert to China; Nichigai refused. In the third month China and France jointly surveyed the Qinzhou–Vietnam border. France had first sent Palardi and Franchin, who claimed Banxing, Linghuai, and other places the treaty assigned to China; the government refused. France then reassigned Cogordan and his chief officer Yuxi to continue the work. Guangdong governor Li Hanzhang sent Li Shoutong to join the survey and learned that the strategic points Palardi and Franchin had claimed were separated from Vietnam by deep ravines and steep ridges, with ravines especially numerous. They agreed that waterways would form the boundary where water lay and ridges where mountains stood, for a total length of four hundred li. The land boundary ran only fifty li along steep ridges; the rest followed ravines, except Pilao, about three li across, which was divided equally. As in the original survey map and treaty, Fenmaoling, Banxing, Bandian, Linghuai, and ten li around Dongzhong all remained Chinese. Yunnan and Vietnam were negotiating their border at the same time. Yunnan governor Wang Wenshao refused to reopen settled lines; he only delayed withdrawing Chinese troops from Huangshupi, Qingmen, and Menggang until French garrisons arrived. The border agreement was settled. In the twenty-first year of Guangxu (1895), after the Sino-Japanese treaty was concluded, France pressed to revise the commercial and border agreements; China then allowed the opening of Longzhou, Mengzi, and other treaty ports and ceded Mengwu and Wude within the Sino-Vietnamese border. China had held that these two districts belonged to the Cheli native chieftain under Ning'er county, but the French minister claimed they had long been Vietnamese, and they were awarded to France.

二十三年，法要求瓊州不割讓租借於他國，許之。 二十四年，法乘廣東雷州人殺其士民二人，以兵艦據廣州灣，來商租借，言為停船屯煤之所，無損中國主權，而所租借跨高、雷二府之間，由海岸以入內地，所得東海、匈洲各島，及赤坎、志滿、新墟等處，均歸入租界。 又得吳川之半島及通明港。 是年，又以兵強佔上海、寧波四明公所義地，寧人罷巿，幾激變。 久之始定。 時廣西永安有殺斃法教民之事，方議辦犯、劾官、賠償、建堂四條，適值北海鐵路造至南寧，援龍州鐵路案，中、法合辦，法使遂要求將鐵路歸併教案。 議久始允就案議結，不及他事。 又施南、宜昌、長沙均因教堂、教民啟釁未結。 二十六年春，拳匪亂，法人調兵與德、英、俄、美、日本聯軍入京，復督兵西進至廣昌，屢阻之。 二十七年，展漢口租界。 是年法遣鮑渥為駐華公使。 二十八年，外務部與法隆興公司總辦彌樂石訂雲南礦務章程。 先是彌樂石到滇，與礦務大臣唐炯議欲設中西礦務公司，唐炯入告，奉旨交雲貴總督魏光燾等與彌樂石議，曆七閱月始竣。 乃入奏，略謂：一，初議限制中國公司延聘礦師，貸用洋款，後亦不入別國洋股，專用英、法礦師，定議； 一，運礦自修鐵路，接通滇越幹路，訂明俟幹路成時再議，並禁售票搭載客貨，預存限制； 一，公司收買山地，按民間租價，公平租賃，地由滇官指交，價由公司照給，逾限三年不辦，原地歸還業主； 一，完納礦稅，議定按出井出爐礦質，每百抽五，抵納稅課，並派員分礦監收。 適彌樂石由滇入京，向外務部催訂合同，外務部告以礦地未定，未便先議章程，並不准攬辦全省。 彌樂石允指澂江、臨安、開化、雲南、楚雄等府及元江州、永北廳凡七處，載入章程第一款內，將原議「嗣後別國公司概不准來滇辦礦」，改為「嗣後別國公司概不准在公司所指之地勘采」，以清界限。 彌樂石以原議包辦全省礦利，故原歲給京銅一百五十萬斤，並津貼員弁兵勇護廠銀二萬兩。 今既改為七處，應請減議定繳京銅一百萬斤。 護廠費由公司給發，不拘定數。 招募土勇，改為稟請地方官招募，遴選武官一員管帶。 遂定議。 惟第一款內載有「公司尋出之金、銀、煤、鐵、五金、白銅、錫及火油、寶石、硃沙礦，允給公司承辦」等語，滇督魏光燾以礦類白金、白銅、錫三項為原章所無，因咨外務部，請照滇中前定原章，照會英、法公使，轉令彌樂石仍將三項刪除。
In the twenty-third year of Guangxu (1897), France demanded that Qiongzhou not be ceded or leased to any other power, and China agreed. In the twenty-fourth year of Guangxu (1898), after two French subjects were killed in Leizhou, Guangdong, French warships seized Guangzhou Bay (Kwangchow Wan) and opened lease talks, claiming the site was only for coaling and anchorage and would not impair Chinese sovereignty; the lease nonetheless straddled Gaozhou and Leizhou prefectures from the coast inland, and Donghai, Naozhou, and the mainland sites Chikan, Zhiman, and Xinxu were all folded into the concession. France also took the Wuchuan peninsula and Tongming Harbor. That same year French troops forcibly seized charitable land belonging to the Siming guild halls in Shanghai and Ningbo; Ningbo merchants shut the markets and nearly sparked a riot. Only after a long interval was the matter settled. Meanwhile in Yong'an, Guangxi, French converts had been killed, and officials were negotiating the usual four terms—punish the guilty, discipline officials, pay indemnity, and rebuild the church—when the Beihai railway reached Nanning; citing the Longzhou railway precedent for Sino-French joint operation, the French minister insisted the railway dispute be folded into the missionary case. After prolonged talks China agreed to settle only the case at hand, without conceding anything further. Missionary disputes likewise remained open at Shinan, Yichang, and Changsha. In the spring of the twenty-sixth year of Guangxu (1900), during the Boxer uprising, France sent troops with the German, British, Russian, American, and Japanese expeditionary forces into Beijing, then marched west as far as Guangchang, where Chinese forces repeatedly blocked them. In the twenty-seventh year of Guangxu (1901), the Hankou French concession was enlarged. That year France appointed Boutwell minister to China. In the twenty-eighth year of Guangxu (1902), the Foreign Ministry and Milliet, chief of the French Longxing Company, signed regulations for Yunnan mining. Milliet had earlier reached Yunnan and with mining commissioner Tang Jiong proposed a joint Chinese-Western mining company; Tang memorialized the throne, and the court ordered Yunnan-Guizhou governor Wei Guangtao and others to negotiate with Milliet—a process that took seven months to finish. They then memorialized the throne, in summary: first, an early proposal to limit Chinese firms in hiring foreign engineers and borrowing foreign capital was revised so that no other nation's capital would enter and only British and French engineers would be employed—this was agreed; second, ore would move on a company-built line to join the Yunnan-Vietnam trunk railway, with further terms deferred until the trunk was finished and with advance limits forbidding ticketed passenger or freight service; third, the company would lease mountain land at fair local rents, with Yunnan officials assigning parcels and the company paying; if work did not begin within three years, the land would revert to the owners; fourth, mining tax would be five percent of ore raised from shafts and smelters, with officials assigned to each mine to collect it. When Milliet came to Beijing from Yunnan and pressed the Foreign Ministry to sign a contract, the ministry replied that mining districts were still unsettled, that regulations could not be fixed first, and that a province-wide monopoly would not be allowed. Milliet agreed to limit operations to seven jurisdictions—Chengjiang, Lin'an, Kaihua, Yunnan, and Chuxiong prefectures plus Yuanjiang and Yongbei—and had them written into Article 1; the original ban on any foreign firm mining anywhere in Yunnan was narrowed to a ban on prospecting only within the company's designated districts. Under the original province-wide monopoly Milliet had agreed to deliver 1,500,000 jin of copper to the capital annually and subsidize garrison officers and guards with 20,000 taels. Now that the grant covered only seven districts, the annual copper tribute was reduced to 1,000,000 jin. Guard costs would be paid by the company without a fixed ceiling. Native guards would be recruited through local officials, with one military officer chosen to command them. The terms were then settled. Article 1, however, listed platinum, white copper, and tin among ores the company might work—minerals absent from the earlier Yunnan draft—so governor Wei Guangtao asked the Foreign Ministry to follow the original Yunnan terms, notify the British and French ministers, and have Milliet strike out those three categories.

二十九年，總理外務部慶親王奕劻與法使呂班訂滇越鐵路條約三十四條：:一，鐵路自河口抵蒙自，或由蒙自附近至雲南省城，日後擬改，須彼此商准； :二至四，勘路繪圖及交地購地各事； :五，各項廠棧同時開工； :六，鐵軌寬一邁當； :七，鐵路經過地方，不得損壞城垣公署； :八、九，購料及挖取沙石、採伐林木各事； :十，運路及暫時興工各地，用竣後即交還； :十一，幹路造成，商接支路； :十二，各執事凡須專門學者，可用外國人； :十三、四，工匠之招募管理及賞恤傷亡、懲辦犯罪各辦法； :十五，巡丁可募土民，不得請派西兵； :十六，洋員請給護照事； :十八，租賃房屋事； :十九，不得損及民人產業，有則賠償； :二十，火藥炸藥之運制及防險； :二十一、二，運貨納稅、免稅各例； :二十三，收費、減費、免費各例； :二十四，鐵路不准載運交鹽及西國兵械，如中國有戰事，悉聽調度； :二十八，設專門學堂； :二十九，設電線、電話； :三十一，滇省派員襄助公司； :三十二，定公司補償中國查看費，各員來往照料費； :三十四，此路十八年期滿，中國可與法國商議收回。
In the twenty-ninth year of Guangxu (1903), Prince Qing Yikuang, superintendent of the Foreign Ministry, and the French minister Jean-Baptiste Beau signed a thirty-four-article treaty on the Yunnan-Vietnam Railway: Article 1—the line would run from Hekou to Mengzi or from near Mengzi to the Yunnan provincial capital, with any later route change subject to mutual consent; Articles 2–4 covered surveying, mapping, land transfer, and purchase; Article 5 required workshops and depots to be built concurrently; Article 6 fixed track gauge at one meter; Article 7 forbade damage to city walls or yamens along the right-of-way; Articles 8 and 9 governed procurement of materials, quarrying, and timber cutting; Article 10 required haul roads and temporary worksites to be restored after use; Article 11 allowed branch lines to be negotiated once the trunk was finished; Article 12 permitted foreigners in posts requiring specialized training; Articles 13 and 14 set rules for hiring and supervising workers, compensating casualties, and punishing offenses; Article 15 allowed native patrolmen but barred foreign troops; Article 16 covered passports for foreign staff; Article 18 governed leasing of houses; Article 19 required compensation for damage to private property; Article 20 regulated transport, manufacture, and safe handling of explosives; Articles 21 and 22 set freight duties and exemptions; Article 23 defined standard, reduced, and free fares; Article 24 forbade carrying government salt or foreign munitions, and placed the line wholly under Chinese command in wartime; Article 28 provided for a technical school; Article 29 allowed telegraph and telephone lines; Article 31 required Yunnan to assign officers to assist the company; Article 32 set company payment for Chinese inspection and supervisory travel costs; Article 34 allowed China, after eighteen years, to negotiate recovery of the line with France.

是年，法人因吉林教案索賠償。 三十年秋七月，法使館交還欽天監觀象臺儀器二十八件。 三十一年春，法商欲自上海至紹興行輪，阻之。 是年與各國定值百抽五稅則，法有違言，久之始允。 三十二年春正月二十九日，南昌縣知縣江召棠被殺於天主堂。 先是召棠辦教案頗持正。 法教士王安之因上年荏港教案，有二教民鄧貴和、葛洪泰在南昌縣監禁，強請釋放，召棠向索縱囚，其一匿法教堂中，王安之不交，函約召棠會飲，被殺。 民情大憤，集眾毀法教堂，傷斃王安之及教習等數名，並波及英教堂，久之始定。 法人欲坐召棠自刎，及派兵船來贛責償。 命鄂督張之洞查辦，屢執仵傷單及醫憑單與爭，終徇其請，賠以法銀二十餘萬。 三十三年，法遣領事入滇商辦事。 六月，蒙自法郵局設代收遞人役，詰之。 九月，索還法人所占塘沽碼頭。
That year France demanded indemnity over a missionary case in Jilin. In the seventh month of autumn in the thirtieth year of Guangxu (1904), the French legation returned twenty-eight instruments from the Directorate of Astronomy's observatory. In the spring of the thirty-first year of Guangxu (1905), French merchants sought to run steamers from Shanghai to Shaoxing and were refused. That year China and the powers fixed the ad valorem five-percent tariff; France objected at first but eventually agreed. On the twenty-ninth day of the first month in the spring of the thirty-second year of Guangxu (1906), Nanchang county magistrate Jiang Zhaotang was murdered in the Catholic church. Jiang Zhaotang had earlier handled missionary cases with notable firmness. The French priest Wang Anzhi, over the previous year's Rengang missionary case, had demanded the release of converts Deng Guihe and Ge Hongtai, held in Nanchang jail; Jiang Zhaotang insisted they be surrendered, but one was hidden in the French church and Wang Anzhi refused to produce him; Wang Anzhi then invited Jiang to a meeting by letter and killed him. Public fury erupted; crowds wrecked the French church, killing Wang Anzhi and several church teachers, and violence spread to the British mission before order was slowly restored. France tried to claim Jiang Zhaotang had killed himself and sent gunboats to Jiangxi to demand indemnity. The court ordered Hubei governor Zhang Zhidong to investigate; he repeatedly cited the coroner's report and medical records disputing suicide, but in the end acceded and paid more than 200,000 French silver dollars. In the thirty-third year of Guangxu (1907), France sent a consul into Yunnan on commercial business. In the sixth month the French post office at Mengzi began using agents to collect and forward mail, which China protested. In the ninth month China demanded the return of the Taku wharf held by France.
