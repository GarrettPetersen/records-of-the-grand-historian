岳起荊道乾謝啟昆李殿圖張師誠王紹蘭
Yue Qi, Jing Daoqian, Xie Qikun, Li Diantu, Zhang Shicheng, and Wang Shaolan
3
李奕疇錢楷和舜武
Li Yichou, Qian Kai, and He Shunwu
4
岳起，鄂濟氏，滿洲鑲白旗人。 乾隆三十六年舉人，議敘，授筆帖式。 累擢戶部員外郎、翰林院侍講學士、詹事府少詹事。 五十六年，遷奉天府尹。 前官貪黷，岳起至，屋宇器用遍洗滌之，曰：「勿染其污跡也！ 」與將軍忤。 逾年，擢內閣學士，尋出為江西布政使。 殫心民事，值水災，行勘圩堤，落水致疾。 詔嘉其勤，許解任養疴。
Yue Qi, of the Eji clan, was a Manchu bannerman of the Bordered White Banner. In 1771 he passed the provincial examinations; after his qualifications were reviewed, he was appointed a clerical secretary. He rose through the ranks to become a vice commissioner in the Ministry of Revenue, a Hanlin reader-in-waiting, and junior vice commissioner of the Heir Apparent's Household. In 1791 he was appointed governor of Fengtian Prefecture. His predecessor had been corrupt; when Yue Qi took office, he had every room and piece of furniture scrubbed clean, saying, "Do not let their filth stain you! " He soon came into conflict with the garrison commander. A year later he was promoted to grand secretariat academician, then posted as Jiangxi provincial administration commissioner. He threw himself into local administration; during a flood he went to inspect dikes and polders, fell into the water, and took ill. The throne commended his diligence and allowed him to resign and recover his health.
5
嘉慶四年，特起授山東布政使。 未幾，擢江蘇巡撫。 清介自矢，僮僕僅數人，出屏騶從，禁遊船聲伎，無事不許宴賓演劇。 吳下奢俗為之一變。 疏陳漕弊，略曰：「京漕積習相因，惟弊是營。 米數之盈絀，米色之純雜，竟置不問。 旗丁領運，無處不以米為挾制，即無處不以賄為通融。 推原其故，沿途之抑勒，由旗丁之有幫費； 旗丁之索幫費，由州縣之浮收。 除弊當絕其源，嚴禁浮收，實絕弊源之首。 請下有漕各省，列款指明，嚴行禁革，俾旗丁及漕運倉場，無從更生觀望冀幸之心。 」詔嘉其實心除弊。 常州知府胡觀瀾結交鹽政徵瑞長隨高柏林，派捐修葺江陰廣福寺。 岳起疏言觀瀾、柏林雖罷逐，尚不足服眾心，請將錢二萬餘串責二人分償，以修蘇州官塘橋路。 丹徒知縣黎誕登諷士紳臚其政績保留，實不職，劾罷之。
In 1799 he was specially recalled from retirement and appointed Shandong provincial administration commissioner. Soon afterward he was promoted to governor of Jiangsu. He held himself to the strictest integrity, kept only a handful of servants, went out without a mounted escort, banned pleasure boats and musicians, and forbade banquets and theater except on official business. The region's extravagant ways changed overnight. In a memorial on abuses in the grain transport system, he wrote in part: "The tribute grain convoys to the capital are riddled with entrenched custom, and everyone profits from the abuses alone. Shortfalls in quantity and adulteration in quality go entirely unchecked. The banner transport crews use grain as leverage at every turn and bribes to smooth every obstacle. The root cause is clear: extortion along the route stems from the assistance fees demanded by the transport crews; and those fees exist because counties and prefectures levy illegal surcharges. To root out abuse you must cut off its source; a strict ban on illegal surcharges is the essential first step. He asked that every grain-transport province itemize prohibited charges and enforce the ban, so transport crews and granary officials would no longer hope to profit from the old ways. " The throne commended his earnest campaign against corruption. Changzhou prefect Hu Guanlan, in league with salt commissioner Zheng Rui's chief attendant Gao Bolin, imposed levies to restore Guangfu Temple in Jiangyin. Yue Qi argued that dismissing Hu and Gao was not enough to satisfy public outrage; he asked that they be ordered to repay jointly the more than twenty thousand strings of cash they had extorted, and that the money be used to repair Suzhou's official roads and bridges. Dantu magistrate Li Yandeng had local gentry praise his record so he could remain in office, but Yue Qi found him unfit and had him removed.
6
五年，署兩江總督。 劾南河工員莊剛、劉普等侵漁舞弊，莫澐於任所設店肆運貨至工居奇網利，並治如律。 揚州關溢額稅銀不入私，盡以報解； 覈減兩籓司耗羨閒款，實存銀數報部：並下部議行。 六年，疏請濬築毛城鋪以下河道堤岸、上游永城洪河、下游蕭、碭境內河堰，並藉帑舉工，分五年計畝徵還，允之。
In 1800 he served as acting governor-general of Liangjiang. He impeached Southern Canal engineers Zhuang Gang, Liu Pu, and others for graft; Mo Yun had run shops from his post, shipping goods to worksites to corner profits—all were punished by law. Surplus customs revenue at Yangzhou he kept none of for himself but remitted in full; he audited and cut surplus miscellaneous funds held by the two provincial treasuries and reported actual balances to the ministry—all referred to the ministries for implementation. In 1801 he requested dredging and dike work below Maochengpu, on the upper Yongcheng Hong River, and on interior waterways in Xia and Dang, funded from the treasury and repaid by land assessment over five years; the request was approved.
7
八年，入覲，以疾留京，署禮部侍郎。 會孝淑皇后奉移山陵，坐會疏措語不經，革職留任。 尋命解署職，遂卒。 帝深惜之，贈太子少保，賜卹如例。
In 1803 he came to Beijing for an audience, remained there because of illness, and served as acting vice minister of rites. During the transfer of Empress Xiaoshu's remains to the imperial tomb, he was demoted for improper wording in a joint memorial but kept at his post. He was soon relieved of his acting appointment and died shortly afterward. The emperor mourned him deeply, posthumously made him junior guardian of the heir apparent, and granted the usual funeral honors.
8
無子，詔問其家產，僅屋四間、田七十六畝。 故事，旗員歿無嗣者產入官。 以岳起家清貧，留贍其妻； 妻歿，官為管業，以為祭掃修墳之資。 異數也。 妻亦嚴正，岳起為巡撫時，一日親往籍畢沅家。 暮歸，飲酒微醺。 妻正色曰：「畢公躭於酒色，不保其家，君方畏戒之不暇，乃復效彼耶？ 」岳起謝之。 及至京，居無邸舍，病歿於僧寺，妻紡績以終。 吳民尤思其德，呼曰岳青天，演為歌謠，謂可繼湯斌雲。
He left no son; when the court inquired into his estate, it found only four rooms and seventy-six mu of land. By custom, when a bannerman died without heirs, his property reverted to the state. Because Yue Qi's household had been so poor and upright, the estate was left to support his wife; and after her death the government managed it to fund sacrifices and upkeep of his tomb. Such treatment was wholly out of the ordinary. His wife was equally stern; while he was governor of Jiangsu, he once went in person to inventory the household of Bi Yuan. He came home at dusk slightly drunk. His wife said sharply, "Bi Yuan drowned himself in wine and women and ruined his house—you ought to be on guard against that very example. Will you imitate him now? " Yue Qi apologized at once. In Beijing he had no house; he died of illness in a monastery, and his wife lived out her days spinning thread. The people of Wu cherished his memory, called him "Justice Yue," set his praises in ballads, and said he stood in the line of Tang Bin.
9
荊道乾，字健中，山西臨晉人。 乾隆二十四年舉人，大挑知縣，官湖南，歷麻陽、龍山、東安、永順、慈利、靖州。 所至有惠政，屏陋規，平冤獄。 在靖州賑饑，尤多全活，屢膺上考。 四十七年，遷甘肅寧夏同知，入覲，大學士劉墉曾官湖南巡撫，稱之曰：「第一清官也。 」名始著。 尋署石峰堡同知，時方用兵，治事不廢，修復水利，复薦卓異記名。 五十四年，擢安徽池州知府，屢署徽寧池太道，筦蕪湖關，贏餘不入己，以充賑恤。 調安慶，硃珪為巡撫，尤信任之，疏薦，擢山東登萊青道，攝布政使。 以激濁揚清為己任，薦廉吏崔映淮、李如珩等，而劾不飭者。
Jing Daoqian, courtesy name Jianzhong, was from Linjin in Shanxi. A provincial graduate of 1759, he was appointed county magistrate through the great assignment and served in Hunan at Mayang, Longshan, Dong'an, Yongshun, Cili, and Jingzhou. Everywhere he served he brought real benefit, abolished petty abuses, and cleared wrongful convictions. At Jingzhou his famine relief saved countless lives, and he repeatedly earned top merit ratings. In 1782 he was transferred to Ningxia subprefect in Gansu; at court Grand Secretary Liu Yong, who had once governed Hunan, called him "the foremost honest official in the land. " His fame dates from that moment. He soon acted as subprefect of Shifengbao; though the region was at war, he kept administration running, restored irrigation works, and was again marked for outstanding promotion. In 1789 he was promoted to prefect of Chizhou in Anhui, repeatedly acted as circuit intendant of Huai, Ning, Chi, and Tai, managed Wuhu customs, kept no surplus for himself, and devoted it all to relief. Transferred to Anqing, he won the deep trust of Governor Zhu Gui, who memorialized on his behalf; he was promoted to intendant of Deng, Lai, and Qing in Shandong and acted as provincial administration commissioner. He made it his mission to purge corruption and elevate the honest, recommending upright officials such as Cui Yinghuai and Li Ruheng while impeaching the undisciplined.
10
嘉慶二年，遷按察使。 四年，遷江蘇布政使。 先是州縣存留俸薪役食及驛站經費，改解籓庫，俟奏銷後請支，始則防吏侵挪，久之解有浮費，發有短平。 或勒抵前官虧空，佐雜教官不能得俸，驛傳領於臬司； 或苛駁案牘，因索餽遺，郵政日弛廢。 道乾入覲時，面陳其弊，請悉依定章，於州縣徵收時開支，省解領之繁。 仁宗俞可； 至是疏上施行，天下便之。 上方欲整飭漕政，以巡撫岳起及道乾皆有清名，責其肅清諸弊。 到官三閱月，擢安徽巡撫，疏請禁徵漕浮收舊耗米一斗，給運丁五升，加給二升。 運丁所得，有據可考； 其所用沿途浮費，採訪知之，應禁革。 詔下所奏於有漕各省永禁。 又言：「屯田所以贍運，每丁派田若干及應得租耔，新僉旗丁不能了然。 令糧道刊刻木榜，俾僉丁認田收租。 運船領款，刻易知單，由丁正身親領，以杜包領欺壓之弊。 田冊歸糧道收管，另造副冊發各衛以備查驗。 」並允行。 宿州、靈壁、泗州水災，道乾親往監視賑廠。 六年，以病乞罷，詔許解任調理，俟病痊來京候簡。 次年三月，詔詢道乾病狀，已先卒於安慶，帝悼惜，賜祭，賜其孫炆舉人。
In 1797 he was appointed provincial surveillance commissioner. In 1799 he became Jiangsu provincial administration commissioner. Previously counties had remitted retained funds for salaries, runners, and courier stations to the provincial treasury and drawn them back only after accounts cleared—a reform meant to stop clerks from skimming, but over time remittances swelled with surcharges and disbursements fell short. Funds were sometimes seized to cover predecessors' deficits; junior educational officials went unpaid; courier expenses were controlled by the surveillance commissioner; and documents were nitpicked to extort gifts, so the postal system decayed day by day. When Jing came to court he described these abuses in person and asked that disbursement follow the statutes at the county level, eliminating the round of remittance and reissue. Emperor Renzong approved the proposal; and when he now put the reform into effect by memorial, the whole empire benefited. The throne wished to reform the grain transport system and, because both Governor Yue Qi and Jing Daoqian were known for integrity, charged them to root out every abuse. Three months after taking office he was promoted to governor of Anhui and memorialized to ban illegal surcharges on tribute grain, fix the old wastage allowance at one dou, grant transport crews five sheng, and add two sheng more. What the transport crews received would be fixed and auditable; and every illegal fee along the route that investigation had uncovered should be abolished. An edict ordered his recommendations permanently enforced in every grain-transport province. He added: "Garrison fields support the transport system, but newly enlisted banner crews cannot tell how much land each man holds or what rent is due. Have the grain intendant post carved wooden registers so each crew can identify its fields and collect rent. Issue plain receipts for transport funds so each man collects payment in person, ending proxy collection and extortion. Keep the master land registers with the grain intendant and issue duplicate copies to each guard for inspection. " All were approved. When floods struck Suzhou, Lingbi, and Sizhou, Jing went in person to oversee the relief depots. In 1801 he resigned on grounds of illness; the throne allowed him to leave office to recover and return to Beijing for reassignment when well. The following March the court inquired after his health—he had already died at Anqing. The emperor mourned him, ordered sacrifices, and granted his grandson Wen the status of provincial graduate.
11
道乾由監司不三年擢至巡撫，求治益急，不避嫌怨，自處刻苦。 臨歿，呼舊僚至寢所，指床下金示之曰：「吾受重恩，積養廉數千兩，足以歸喪。 諸君素愛我，勿為斂賻。 」又呼其兄曰：「兄仁弱，勿聽人慫恿受賻，違吾意。 」兄如其言。
Jing rose from circuit commissioner to provincial governor in under three years, pressed ever harder for good government, shrank from no enmity, and lived in deliberate austerity. Near death he called old colleagues to his bedside, pointed to gold beneath it, and said, "I have received great favor from the throne and saved several thousand taels of my integrity stipend—enough to bear my coffin home. You have always cared for me—do not collect funeral gifts on my behalf. " He then called his elder brother: "Brother, you are kind but weak—do not let anyone persuade you to accept funeral gifts against my wishes. " His brother obeyed.
12
謝啟昆，字蘊山，江西南康人。 進士，朝考第一，選庶吉士，授編修。 典河南鄉試，分校禮闈，均得士。 三十七年，出為江蘇鎮江知府，調揚州。 明於吏事，所持堅正，上官異意不為奪。 治東台徐述夔詩詞悖逆獄遲緩，褫職戍軍台。 尋捐復原官，留江南。 父憂，奪情署安徽寧國知府； 复遭母憂，服闋，稱病久不出。 五十五年，特擢江南河庫道，遷浙江按察使。 六十年，遷山西布政使。 州縣倉庫積虧八十餘萬，不一歲悉補完。 高宗異其才，以浙江財賦地虧尤多，特調任。 歷三歲，亦彌補十之五。
Xie Qikun, courtesy name Yunshan, was from Nankang in Jiangxi. A jinshi who ranked first in the palace examination, he entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor and was appointed compiler. He conducted the Henan provincial examinations and assisted in the metropolitan civil examinations, selecting able scholars in both. In 1772 he was posted as prefect of Zhenjiang in Jiangsu, then transferred to Yangzhou. He was expert in administration, held his principles firmly, and would not bend when superiors disagreed. He was dismissed and sent to garrison duty for moving too slowly in the Xu Shukui literary-treason case at Dongtai. He soon bought his way back to his former rank and remained in Jiangnan. Recalled from mourning for his father, he acted as prefect of Ningguo in Anhui; then mourned his mother; after mourning ended he pleaded illness and stayed out of office for years. In 1790 he was specially promoted to intendant of the Jiangnan river treasury and then to Zhejiang surveillance commissioner. In 1795 he became Shanxi provincial administration commissioner. County treasuries had run up deficits of more than eight hundred thousand taels; he made them up entirely within a year. Emperor Gaozong admired his ability and, because Zhejiang's revenue deficits were especially severe, transferred him there specially. In three years he made up half of those deficits as well.
13
廣西土司四十有六，生計日絀，貸於客民，輒以田產準折。 啟昆請禁重利盤剝，違者治罪。 田產給還土司，其無力回贖者，俟收田租滿一本一利，田歸原主，五年為斷； 其不禁客民入苗地者，廉土民馴愚，物產稀少，藉販運以通有無也。 仿浙江海塘竹簍囊石之法，修築興安陡河石堤，以除水患。 河流深通，舊銅船過陡河必一月，至是三日而畢。 七年，卒於官，詔嘉其廉潔，於所節省潯、梧兩關盈餘項下賜銀三千兩治喪。 廣西士民請祀名宦祠。
Guangxi had forty-six native chieftaincies whose livelihoods were failing; they borrowed from Han settlers and routinely pledged their land as collateral. Xie memorialized to ban usurious lending and punish violators. Land was returned to the chieftains; those unable to redeem might recover it once rent collected equaled principal plus interest, with a five-year limit after which title reverted; He did not ban Han traders from Miao lands because the natives were poor in goods and needed commerce to supply their wants. He built stone dikes on the Dou River at Xing'an, using Zhejiang's wicker-basket revetment method, to control flooding. Once the channel was dredged, copper transport boats that had needed a month to pass the Dou River could finish the run in three days. In 1802 he died in office; the throne praised his integrity and granted three thousand taels from surpluses he had saved at the Xun and Wu customs posts for his funeral. The gentry and people of Guangxi petitioned to enshrine him in the local shrine of distinguished officials.
14
啟昆少以文學名，博聞強識，尤善為詩。 著《樹經堂集》、《西魏書》、《小學考》，晚成《廣西通志》，為世所稱。
Xie was known early for scholarship, possessed encyclopedic learning, and was an accomplished poet. His works include the Shujingtang Collection, History of Western Wei, and Studies in Elementary Learning; late in life he finished the Guangxi Gazetteer, all highly regarded.
15
李殿圖，字桓符，直隸高陽人。 乾隆三十一年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 典湖南鄉試，遷御史。 督廣西學政，遷給事中。
Li Diantu, courtesy name Huanfu, was from Gaoyang in Zhili. A jinshi of 1766, he entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor and was appointed compiler. He conducted the Hunan provincial examinations and was promoted to censor. He served as Guangxi education commissioner and was promoted to supervising secretary.
16
四十九年，甘肅回亂，從阿桂、福康安赴軍治糧餉、台站，授鞏秦階道。 軍事初竣，民、回相仇，焚掠報復，訛言時起。 殿圖處以鎮靜，叛黨緣坐，婦稚量情釋宥； 罹害戶口，隨宜賑恤，流亡漸安。 卓泥土司與四川松潘、漳臘各番爭噶噶固山界，殿圖輕騎履勘，歷小洮河、丈八嶺、鸚哥口，皆人跡罕到，群番導行，片語判決，立石達魚山頂而還。 高宗幾餘考涇、渭清濁源流，命殿圖親勘，自秦州溯流至鳥鼠、崆峒，繪圖附說以進，詔嘉其詳實。
In 1784, during the Gansu Muslim uprising, he joined Agui and Fuk'anggan's campaign to manage supplies and courier stations and was appointed intendant of Gong, Qin, and Jie. When fighting ended, Han and Muslim communities turned on each other in arson, looting, and rumor. Li kept the situation calm, released women and children implicated by association with rebels when circumstances warranted, and gave relief to victim households until refugees gradually settled. When the Zhuotu chieftain disputed the Gaga Gu boundary with Songpan and Zhangla tribes, Li rode out to survey Xiaotao River, Zhangba Ridge, and Parrot Pass—almost untraveled country—guided by tribal leaders, settled the case in a few words, set a boundary marker on Dayu Peak, and returned. Emperor Gaozong had studied the sources of the Jing and Wei in his leisure and ordered Li to survey them in person from Qinzhou upstream to Wushu and Kongtong; his maps and report won imperial praise for their thoroughness.
17
六十年，遷福建按察使，嘉慶三年，就遷布政使。 疏言：「乾隆中，業農家必畜騾馬三四以任耕種，嗣後官吏借用應差，漸形滋擾，應嚴行革除。 獄訟必速為審結，開釋無辜，小民始得安業。 常平倉穀積久弊生，民未受益，官倉已受其虧。 無災之年，不宜貸假。 吏役例有定額，近則人思託足，藉免役徭。 關津稅口，官署長隨，呼朋引類，並為奸藪，宜並禁止。 」詔下直省一體察禁。 閩俗售田，田面田根，糾纏不決。 蠹吏影射，佃戶頑抗，錢糧日多脫欠，徵收不敷，每以虛出通關而致虧缺，殿圖奏請嚴治。 在任逾年，庫儲大增。
In 1795 he became Fujian surveillance commissioner; in 1798 he was promoted to provincial administration commissioner. He memorialized: "Under Qianlong, farming families kept several mules for plowing, but officials later commandeered them for corvée until the practice became intolerable and must be abolished. Cases must be tried promptly and the innocent released so common people can live in peace. Ever-normal granaries had grown corrupt over time—the people gained nothing while official stores bled losses. In years without disaster, grain should not be lent out. Clerks and runners had fixed quotas, but lately people sought posts under them to escape corvée. At customs posts and ferries, yamen attendants formed gangs of extortionists—all should be banned. " An edict ordered every province to investigate and enforce the ban. In Fujian, land sales split surface rights from underlying title, breeding endless disputes. Corrupt clerks exploited the confusion, tenants resisted payment, tax arrears mounted, and false clearance receipts caused deficits—Li memorialized for strict enforcement. Within a year, provincial reserves grew substantially.

和舜武，伊拉里氏，滿洲鑲藍旗人。 官學生，考授太常寺筆帖式。 累遷步軍統領衙門員外郎。 以治獄明獲議敘，遷兵部郎中，兼公中佐領。 嘉慶十五年，出為江蘇鹽法道。 累遷山東布政使，整飭吏治，輿論歸之。 二十二年，擢山西巡撫，調河南。 會布政使吳邦慶疏請於漳、衛合流之處建閘壩，和舜武謂：「漳河盛漲湍悍，非一閘所能禦，越閘旁趨，且停蓄泥沙，塞衛水宣洩之路。 」疏請罷之，仍舊章每年挑濬竇公河以資鹽運，如所議行。 逾年，調山東。 仁宗聞其前為布政使有聲，故有此授。 山東民俗好訟，又近畿，輒走訴京師。 和舜武再蒞，訟頓減，特詔褒勉。 疏請清理京控積案，責巡撫、籓、臬分提鞫訊，月定課程，各自陳奏； 又請酌​​改竊盜窩匪條例，加重定擬，俟盜風稍戢，復舊：並從之。 至年終，審結積案千餘起，予優敘。 京察复予議敘。 二十四年，卒，上甚惜之，優詔賜卹，贈總督銜，諡恭慎。
He Shunwu, of the Ilari clan, was a Manchu bannerman of the Bordered Blue Banner. A government school graduate, he passed examination and was appointed a clerical secretary in the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. He rose to vice commissioner in the Metropolitan Infantry Command. Recognized for clear handling of cases, he became director in the Ministry of War and assistant commandant of a garrison company. In 1810 he was posted as Jiangsu salt administration intendant. He rose to Shandong provincial administration commissioner, rectified official conduct, and won public acclaim. In 1817 he was promoted to governor of Shanxi, then transferred to Henan. When Administration Commissioner Wu Bangqing proposed a sluice where the Zhang and Wei rivers meet, He argued: "The Zhang in flood is too fierce for a single sluice; water would bypass it, deposit silt, and block the Wei's drainage. " He memorialized to abandon the project and continue annual dredging of the Dougong River for salt transport, as adopted. A year later he was transferred to Shandong. Emperor Renzong appointed him because of his reputation as Shandong administration commissioner. Shandong was litigious and, being near the capital, people often took cases to Beijing. On his return lawsuits dropped sharply, and the emperor issued special commendation. He memorialized to clear the backlog of capital appeals, assigning governors, treasurers, and surveillance commissioners monthly quotas for investigation and reporting; and asked to stiffen penalties for theft and bandit dens until crime subsided, then restore former statutes—all approved. By year's end he had cleared more than a thousand backlog cases and received superior commendation. At the capital personnel review he again received commendation. He died in 1819; the emperor mourned him deeply, granted superior funeral honors, posthumously made him governor-general, and titled him Gongjin.

論曰：仁宗初政，特重廉吏。 岳起、荊道乾清操實政為之冠； 謝啟昆、張師誠才猷建樹，卓越一時：並專圻碩望矣。 李殿圖、李奕疇、錢楷亦各以明慎慈惠見稱，和舜武課最簿書，遂邀易名曠典； 王紹蘭一眚坐廢，晚成經學：殆有幸有不幸哉？
Commentary: At the start of Emperor Renzong's reign, honest officials were especially prized. Yue Qi and Jing Daoqian led them all in integrity and effective government; Xie Qikun and Zhang Shicheng were men of outstanding talent and achievement in their day—great provincial hopes every one. Li Diantu, Li Yichou, and Qian Kai were praised for judicial clarity and kindness; He Shunwu excelled in administrative metrics and won the rare honor of a posthumous title; Wang Shaolan was ruined by a single fault yet became a classical scholar in retirement—is that fortune within misfortune?
