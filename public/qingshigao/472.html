=陸潤庠=陸潤庠，字鳳石，江蘇元和人。 父懋修，精醫，見藝術傳。 潤庠，同治十三年一甲一名進士，授修撰。 光緒初，屢典試事，湖南、陝西皆再至。 入直南書房，洊擢侍讀。 出督山東學政。 父憂服闋，再遷祭酒，典試江西。 以母疾乞養歸。 二十四年，起補祭酒，擢內閣學士，署工部侍郎。 兩宮西巡，奔赴行在，授禮部侍郎，充經筵講官。 擢左都御史，管理醫局，典順天鄉試，充會試副總裁，署工部尚書。
Lu Runxiang, whose style was Fengshi, was a native of Yuanhe in Jiangsu. His father Maoxiu was an accomplished physician; he is treated in the Treatise on Arts. In the thirteenth year of Tongzhi, Runxiang took first place in the highest tier of the jinshi examination and was made a Hanlin Compiler. Early in the Guangxu era he was repeatedly put in charge of examinations, visiting Hunan and Shaanxi twice apiece. After entering the Southern Studio on palace duty, he was promoted in turn to Reader. He was sent out to serve as educational commissioner of Shandong. After his period of mourning for his father, he was again made Chancellor of the Directorate of Education and put in charge of the Jiangxi examinations. When his mother fell ill, he asked leave to care for her and went home. In the twenty-fourth year he was recalled as Chancellor, raised to Grand Secretary of the Inner Court, and served concurrently as acting Vice Minister of Works. When the two palaces went west on tour, he hurried to the traveling court, was made Vice Minister of Rites, and served as a lecturer at the imperial lecture sessions. He rose to Left Censor-in-Chief, took charge of the Medical Bureau, supervised the metropolitan provincial examination, served as associate chief examiner of the palace examination, and acted as Minister of Works.
2
三十二年，充釐訂官制大臣。 已而工部裁省，以尚書兼領順天府尹事。 明年，授吏部尚書、參預政務大臣，謂：「捐例開，仕途雜，膺民社者或不通曉文義，因訂道府以下考試章程，試不及格者停其分發，設仕學館教習之。 」潤庠為陸贄後，嘗奏進文集，參以時事，大意謂：「成規未可墨守，而新法亦須斟酌行之。 若不研求國內歷史，以為變通，必至窒礙難行，且有變本加厲之害。 」
In the thirty-second year he was appointed to the commission charged with revising the governmental system. Soon afterward the Ministry of Works was cut, and as its former minister he also took on the duties of Prefect of Shuntian. The following year he was made Minister of Personnel and a councilor participating in state affairs. He said: "Once the purchase-of-office quotas were opened, official careers became a jumble, and men entrusted with local government sometimes could not even grasp written meaning. He therefore drew up examination rules for appointments at the prefectural level and below; candidates who failed would not be assigned, and an Official Learning Academy was established to train them. " A descendant of Lu Zhi, Runxiang once memorialized with his collected writings, relating them to current affairs. His main point was that old regulations should not be followed blindly, yet new policies also had to be applied with careful judgment. Unless one studies the nation's own history to guide change, reforms will only meet obstruction and become unworkable, and the harm will grow worse still. "
3
宣統元年，協辦大學士，由體仁閣轉東閣大學士，充弼德院院長。 皇帝典學，充毓慶宮授讀，兼顧問大臣。 疏陳：「曲阜篤生聖人之地，今新建曲阜學堂，必須闡明經術，提倡正學。 若雜聘外人，異言異服，喧賓奪主，將來聖教澌滅，亦朝廷之憂。 」又陳：「釐訂官制，宜保存臺諫一職。 說者謂既有國會，不須復有言官。 豈知議員職在立法，言官職在擊邪。 議院開會，不過三月，臺諫則隨時可以陳言。 行政裁判，系定斷於事後，言官則舉發於事前。 朝廷欲開通耳目，則諫院不可裁； 諸臣欲鞏固君權，則亦不可言裁。 即使他時國會成立，亦宜使該院獨立，勿為邪說所淆。 」又言：「遊學諸生，於實業等事學成而歸者，寥寥可數，而又用非所學。 其最多者惟法政一科。 法政各國歧異，悉就其本國人情風俗以為製。 今諸生根柢未深，於前古聖賢經傳曾未誦習，道德風尚概未聞知，襲人皮毛，妄言改革； 甚且包藏禍心，倡民權革命之說，判國家與君主為兩途，布其黨徒，潛為謀主。 各部院大臣以為朝廷銳意變法，非重用學生不足以稱上旨，遂乃邪說诐行，遍播中外，久之必致根本動搖，民生塗炭。 」
In the first year of Xuantong he served as associate grand secretary, moved from the Hall of Embodied Humanity to the Eastern Pavilion as grand secretary, and became director of the Academy of Moral Cultivation. When the emperor began his formal studies, he served as reader in the Palace of Accumulated Celebration and also as a consulting minister. In a memorial he wrote: "Qufu is the land where the Sage was truly born. With a new Qufu Academy now established, the classics must be clarified and orthodox learning upheld. If foreigners are hired indiscriminately, with alien speech and dress, guests will drown out the host; in time the Sage's teaching will wither away, which would also be a grave concern for the court. " He also urged that in revising the governmental system, the censorate and remonstrance offices ought to be kept. Some argue that once a national parliament exists, remonstrance officials are no longer needed. They fail to see that parliamentarians are charged with legislation, whereas remonstrance officials are charged with striking down wrongdoing. Parliament sits for only three months at a time, but the censorate and remonstrance offices may speak whenever the need arises. Administrative review settles matters only after the fact, whereas remonstrance officials expose problems before they take hold. If the court wishes to keep its ears and eyes open, the Remonstrance Bureau must not be cut; and if the ministers wish to consolidate the sovereign's authority, they likewise must not speak of abolishing it. Even when a national parliament is later established, that bureau should remain independent and not be muddled by heterodox doctrines. " He also observed that of students sent abroad to study, only a handful return having truly mastered practical fields such as industry, and even they are not put to use in what they learned. By far the largest number study only law and government. Law and government differ from country to country; each nation fashions its system entirely according to its own customs and habits. Today these students' roots are still shallow; they have never studied the classics of the ancient sages, know nothing of moral standards or social customs, pick up foreign surface scraps, and rashly talk of reform; some even harbor malicious intent, preach popular rights and revolution, treat state and sovereign as two separate paths, spread their factions, and secretly act as ringleaders. Ministers of the boards and courts, believing the court was bent on reform and that only by heavily employing returned students could they satisfy the throne, allowed heterodox doctrines and crooked conduct to spread at home and abroad; in time this would shake the foundations and bring the people to ruin. "
4
又疏陳財用枯竭，請酌停新政，謂：「今日之害，先由於督撫無權，漸而至於朝廷無權。 庫儲之困難，寇賊之充斥，猶其顯而易見者也。 鎮兵之設也，所用皆未經歷練之學生，韜略則紙上空談，作用則徒取形式，甚至持不擊同胞之謬說。 一旦有事，督撫非但不能調遣，甚且反戈相向，其不可用明矣。 則莫如停辦鎮兵，仍取巡防隊而整理之。 審判之立也，所授皆未曾聽訟之法官，黑白混淆，是非倒置。 舊時諳練之老吏，督撫不得用之，散遣州縣捕役，以緝盜責之巡警。 巡警無能也，且不過省會及通商口岸有巡警，豈能分佈​​鄉閭？ 將來必至遍地皆盜，人民無可控訴。 則莫如停辦審判，仍以聽斷緝捕歸之州縣。 諮議局之設也，所舉皆不諳掌故之議員，逞臆狂談，箝制當道，督撫莫能禁之。 於是藉籌款之名，魚肉鄉里，竊自治之號，私樹黨援。 上年資政院開議，竟至戟手漫罵，藐視朝廷。 以辯給為通才，以橫議為輿論，蜩螗沸羹，莫可究詰。 則莫如停辦國會，仍以言事責之諫院。 學堂之設也，所聘皆未通經史之教員，其沿用教科書，僅足啟發顓蒙，廢五經而不讀，禍直等於秦焚。 暑假、星期，毫無拘束，彼血氣未定者，豈不結黨為非？ 又膳學費百倍於前，致使貧寒聰穎之士流，進身無路。 則莫如停辦中小學堂，仍用經策取士。 凡此皆於財政有關，而禍不僅在財政，使不早為之所，必至權柄下移，大局不可收拾。 」疏上，多不報。 時建設立憲內閣，宰輔擁虛名而已。
He also memorialized that revenues were exhausted and asked that new policies be suspended in measured steps, saying: "Today's harm begins with governors-general and governors stripped of authority and gradually extends until the court itself has none. The treasury's straits and the land's filling with bandits are only the most obvious signs. As for the new garrison armies, those employed are untrained students; strategy is empty talk on paper, operations are mere form, and some even hold the absurd notion that one must not strike fellow countrymen. When trouble comes, governors cannot deploy them and may even face mutiny — their uselessness is plain. It would be better to halt the garrison armies and instead reorganize the existing patrol forces. As for the new courts, those appointed are judges who have never heard a case; right and wrong are confused and inverted. Experienced old clerks can no longer be used by governors; county constables are scattered, and bandit-catching is left to the new police. The police are incompetent, and they exist only in provincial capitals and treaty ports — how can they cover the countryside? In time bandits will fill the land and the people will have nowhere to turn for justice. It would be better to halt the new courts and again leave trials and bandit-catching to the prefectures and counties. As for the deliberative assemblies, those elected are men ignorant of precedent; they indulge reckless talk, constrain those in authority, and governors cannot restrain them. Under the pretext of raising funds they prey on the countryside, seize the name of self-government, and privately build factional networks. When the Political Consultative Assembly met last year, members even pointed fingers and hurled abuse, showing contempt for the court. They take glibness for talent and reckless debate for public opinion; the clamor is like boiling soup, and nothing can be settled. It would be better to halt the national parliament and again charge the Remonstrance Bureau with speaking on affairs of state. As for the new schools, the teachers hired know neither classics nor histories; their textbooks can only instruct the ignorant; the Five Classics are cast aside unread — a calamity equal to the Qin burning of books. With summer vacation and weekly holidays wholly unrestrained, will hot-blooded youths not band together for mischief? Board and tuition now cost a hundred times what they once did, shutting poor but gifted scholars out of advancement altogether. It would be better to halt primary and secondary schools and again select officials through the classics and policy essays. All of this touches finances, yet the harm is not financial alone; unless dealt with early, authority will drain downward and the situation will become irremediable. " When the memorial was submitted, most of it went unanswered. At the time a constitutional cabinet was being formed, but the chief ministers held only empty titles.

武昌兵變，官軍既克漢陽，武昌旦夕下。 而新內閣又成立，總理大臣袁世凱議修和息戰禍，取隆裕太后懿旨，頒示天下，改建國體，於是遜位詔下矣。 潤庠以老瞶辭授讀差，奉懿旨仍照料毓慶宮，給月俸如故，授太保。 越二年，病卒，年七十五，贈太傅，諡文端。
When the Wuchang mutiny broke out, government troops had already taken Hanyang, and Wuchang was about to fall. A new cabinet was formed; Prime Minister Yuan Shikai proposed peace to end the war, obtained an edict from Empress Dowager Longyu, promulgated it throughout the realm, and changed the form of government — and so the abdication edict was issued. Runxiang, citing old age and failing sight, resigned his post as instructor; by edict he was still to oversee the Palace of Accumulated Celebration, his monthly salary continued unchanged, and he was made Grand Guardian. Two years later he died of illness at seventy-five; he was posthumously made Grand Tutor and given the posthumous name Wenduan.
6
潤庠性和易，接物無崖岸，雖貴，服用如為諸生時。 遇變憂鬱，內結於胸而外不露。 及病篤，竟日危坐，瞑目不言，亦不食，數日而逝。
Runxiang was easygoing by nature and unpretentious in dealing with others; though he rose high, his dress and habits remained those of a student. When upheaval came he was inwardly troubled, knotting grief within while showing nothing outwardly. When his illness grew grave he sat upright all day with eyes closed, neither speaking nor eating, and after several days he died.

=世續=世續，字伯軒，索勒豁金氏，隸內務府滿洲正黃旗。 光緒元年舉人，以議敘主事歷內務府郎中，擢武備院卿，授內閣學士。 二十二年，為總管內務府大臣，兼工部侍郎。 二十六年，各國聯軍入京，兩宮西狩，適遭父喪，命留京辦事。 即日縗墨詣聯軍請保護宮廷​​，日為宮中備飲饌，並保壇廟。 晉理籓院尚書，調禮部。 兩宮回鑾，賞黃馬褂，轉吏部，兼都統。 內務府三旗甲米向歸吏胥代領折價，名曰「米折」，所得甚微。 世續商之倉場，飭旗丁自領，眾感實惠。 纂呈四書圖說，特旨褒嘉。 三十年，以吏部尚書協辦大學士，尋授體仁閣大學士。 三十二年，命為軍機大臣。 歷轉文華殿大學士，充憲政編查館參預政務大臣。 念八旗生計日艱，奏設工藝廠，俾習工藝贍身家。 德宗崩，議繼體，世續獨言國事艱危，宜立長君，不能用。
Shi Xu, whose style was Boxuan, was of the Suoluohejin clan and registered with the Imperial Household under the Manchu Plain Yellow Banner. In the first year of Guangxu he passed the provincial examination; by seniority he served in the Imperial Household, rose to Director of the Armory, and was made Grand Secretary of the Inner Court. In the twenty-second year he became Superintendent of the Imperial Household and concurrently Vice Minister of Works. In the twenty-sixth year the allied armies entered Beijing; the two palaces went west on tour; he was mourning his father and was ordered to remain in the capital to handle affairs. That very day, still in mourning garb, he went to the allied armies to request protection for the palaces; each day he arranged food for the palace and also secured the altars and temples. He was promoted to Minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs and transferred to the Ministry of Rites. When the two palaces returned, he was rewarded with a yellow jacket, moved to the Ministry of Personnel, and concurrently served as banner general. Ration grain for the three banners under the Imperial Household had long been collected by clerks on the recipients' behalf and cashed out at a discount, called "grain conversion," yielding very little. Shi Xu negotiated with the granary officials and ordered banner soldiers to collect rations in person; the people were grateful for the real benefit. He compiled and presented Illustrated Explanations of the Four Books and received a special edict of praise. In the thirtieth year, as Minister of Personnel he served as associate grand secretary and soon after was made grand secretary of the Hall of Embodied Humanity. In the thirty-second year he was appointed to the Grand Council. He was transferred to grand secretary of the Hall of Literary Glory and served as a councilor on the Constitutional Research Bureau. Seeing that livelihoods in the Eight Banners grew harder by the day, he memorialized to establish craft workshops so bannermen could learn trades to support their families. When Emperor Guangxu died and succession was discussed, Shi Xu alone urged that the state was in peril and an older heir should be chosen; his view was not adopted.
8
宣統改元，以疾乞休。 三年，復起原官，仍兼總管內務府大臣。 及議遜位，世續首讚之。 太后令磋商優待條件，授太保。 接修崇陵工程，加太傅。 丁巳復辟，懼禍及，力阻之。 事變亟，入宿衛，並以殮服自隨。 頻年以經費拮据，支持尤苦，纂修德宗實錄，始終其事，及書成，已病不能起矣。 辛酉年，卒，年六十九。 贈太師，諡文端。
When the Xuantong reign began, he asked to retire on account of illness. In the third year he was recalled to his former posts and again served as Superintendent of the Imperial Household. When abdication was discussed, Shi Xu was the first to endorse it. The empress dowager ordered him to negotiate favorable terms of treatment and appointed him Grand Guardian. He took charge of the Chongling mausoleum repairs and was further made Grand Tutor. In the dingsi year, when restoration was attempted, fearing disaster would reach him, he strongly opposed it. When the crisis broke swiftly, he entered palace guard service and even carried burial garments with him. Year after year funds were tight and the strain was severe; he saw through the compilation of Emperor Guangxu's Veritable Records from start to finish, and when the work was done he was too ill to rise. In the xinyou year he died at sixty-nine. He was posthumously made Grand Preceptor and given the posthumous name Wenduan.

=伊克坦=伊克坦，字仲平，瓜爾佳氏，滿洲正白旗人，西安駐防。 光緒十二年進士，以編修歷至都察院副都​​御史，充滿蒙文學堂監督。 有請達海從祀文廟者，伊克坦以達海創定國書，繙譯經史，有功聖教，允宜附祀，即為代奏，略言：「學官立於漢京，而配享實始於唐代，宋、元以來，迭有增祀，大率以闡明聖學，有功經訓為斷。 漢儒許慎，特因說文解字，功在經籍，專隆升祔。 我太祖高皇帝、太宗文皇帝指授文臣創立國書，傳譯經史，宣布文教，尤極千古未有之盛。 夫國書字體，創自文臣額爾德尼及噶蓋等，而仰承聖意，匯集大成，詳定頒行者，實唯儒臣達海。 達海以肇造貞元之佐，擅閎通著述之才，歷相兩朝，瞻言百里。 其初奉命詳定國書，重加圈點，發明音義； 又以國書漢字對音未全，於十二字頭之外有所增加，而國書之用乃廣。 复定兩字切音之法，較之漢文切音，更為精當，而國書之制乃備。 繙譯經典，昭示群倫，功不在傳經諸儒下。 崇德十年，既蒙賜諡文成，康熙九年，复奉賜文立碑，隆德報功，永受恩澤。 旋有學士阿理瑚奏請從祀文廟，禮臣復奏，以為創造國書，一藝之長，不當從祀，未經議準。 查達海詳定國書字體，實禀太宗指示而成。 作者為聖，述者為明，非唯羽翼六經，抑且昭示百世。 部議謂僅一藝之長，實未深知大體。 達海於聖經有表章之力，於後學有津逮之功。 方今宗學、旗學兼重國書，並奉旨特設滿蒙文學堂於京師，奉省亦經奏立八旗滿蒙文中學堂。 揆諸古者釋奠祭師之誼，達海應得附祀，核與漢儒許慎從祀之例亦屬相符。 仰懇俯準達海附祀文廟，並請敕建專祠於盛京，以昭矜式。 查盛京東門外尚有達海塋墓，榛莽荒蕪，碣碑剝落，並請敕下所司修治看護，用示朝廷崇尚實學、藎念儒臣之至意。 」
Yi Ketan, whose style was Zhongping, was of the Guwalgiya clan, a Manchu of the Plain White Banner garrisoned at Xi'an. In the twelfth year of Guangxu he passed the jinshi examination; from Compiler he rose to Vice Censor-in-Chief and served as superintendent of the Manchu-Mongol Literary Academy. When someone petitioned that Dahai be granted accompanying sacrifice in the Confucian temple, Yi Ketan argued that Dahai had created and fixed the national script and translated the classics and histories, serving the Sage's teaching, and ought indeed to receive accompanying sacrifice; he memorialized on their behalf, stating in brief: "School officials were established in the Han capital, but temple accompanying sacrifice truly began in the Tang; since Song and Yuan sacrifices have been repeatedly expanded, generally on the criterion of clarifying the Sage's learning and meriting the classics. The Han scholar Xu Shen was specially elevated to accompanying sacrifice solely because Explaining Graphs and Analyzing Characters served the classics. Our Taizu and Taizong directed civil officials to create the national script, translate the classics and histories, and proclaim civil culture — a splendor unmatched in history. The forms of the national script were first created by civil officials such as Erdeni and Gagai, but it was the Confucian minister Dahai alone who, following the imperial intent, brought the work to completion, worked out the details, and promulgated it. Dahai, as an assistant at the dynasty's founding, possessed broad literary talent, served as chief minister through two reigns, and his counsel carried far. At first he was ordered to finalize the national script in detail, adding diacritical dots and clarifying sounds and meanings; Because phonetic correspondence between the national script and Chinese characters was incomplete, he made additions beyond the twelve syllabaries, and the script's use broadened. He further established the method of two-character phonetic spelling, more precise than Chinese fanqie spelling, and so the national script's system was completed. By translating the classics and making them known to the people, his merit was no less than that of the scholars who transmitted the classics. In the tenth year of Chongde he received the posthumous name Wencheng; in the ninth year of Kangxi a stele was again erected in his honor; virtue was exalted and merit rewarded, and his favor endured. Soon Scholar Alihu petitioned for accompanying sacrifice in the Confucian temple; the Ministry of Rites replied that creating the national script was mastery of a single art and unfit for temple sacrifice, and the request was not approved. Investigation shows that Dahai's finalization of the national script was in fact carried out under Taizong's direction. The author is the sage, the expounder brings clarity; he not only supported the Six Classics but proclaimed them for generations to come. The ministry held that this was only mastery of a single art and failed to grasp the larger principle. Dahai had the power to expound the sage classics and provided a bridge for later learners. Today the imperial clan schools and banner schools all emphasize the national script; by imperial order a Manchu-Mongol Literary Academy was established in the capital, and Fengtian has also set up banner Manchu-Mongol middle schools. By the ancient rite of honoring teachers at the libation sacrifice, Dahai ought to receive accompanying sacrifice; this also matches the precedent of the Han scholar Xu Shen. We humbly beg approval for Dahai's accompanying sacrifice in the Confucian temple and also request an order to build a dedicated shrine at Mukden as a model for admiration. He also memorialized on canonical learning, stating in brief: "I have read the instruction of Emperor Yongzheng in the third year of his reign: 'Emperors who rule the realm all rely on canonical learning. Our Sagely Ancestor the Kangxi Emperor was heaven-endowed with intelligence, yet loved antiquity and pursued learning keenly, for more than sixty years without slackening. ' Again in the twenty-fourth year of Jiaqing the Jiaqing Emperor said: 'The learning of emperors lies in penetrating heaven and man, clarifying principle and putting it into practice — unlike classicists who hunt through chapters and phrases. ' Thus one sees the successive sage emperors' deepest intent in valuing canonical learning. Our present emperor's sagely wisdom is innate and his intelligence heaven-bestowed; ascending the throne in tender years, the great enterprise was newly founded. At this time of canonical learning in his youth, it is truly the beginning of daily advance in sage reverence. The Regent Prince has cultivated his moral nature, nurtured the emperor's person, carefully chosen attendants, and guarded him on every side; he has already received instruction deeply in his tender years, with every model and warning preserved. Yet whether the emperor's single thought is disciplined or lax bears on the diligence or neglect of all affairs of state; the regulations of a single court bear on how all within the seas regard the throne. There are matters that demand the utmost caution; I respectfully set them forth in detail for our emperor: First, I request that sage learning be exalted. The Changes begin with nurturing the young, the Rites emphasize teachers, the Documents describe humility, the Odes praise harmony — sage learning is subtle and refined, beyond what ordinary branches of learning can encompass. Song Confucians said 'the learning of emperors differs in aim from that of scholars' — this matches exactly the intent of our Jiaqing Emperor's instruction on canonical learning. Now at the beginning of our emperor's studies, teaching subjects should be set; they should unite past and present and blend Chinese and Western learning, and cannot be bound by old precedents. I humbly beg that Confucian ministers be selected to plan in detail, taking established statutes as guide and weighing present needs, not merely offering empty lecture texts or vainly following old Tang court rituals, so as to open the emperor's ear, sharpen his intelligence, and lay the foundation of sage learning and sage rule. Second, I request selection of worthy tutors. By old regulation tutors were chosen from among great ministers, expected to be mature models who would complete the ruler's virtue; yet some entered office and pursued politics, and lecturing was not their strength. Venerable teachers and great scholars keep their virtue hidden; education is profound, and without long study one cannot truly benefit from them. It is proposed to order ministers within and without to recommend those they know, without regard to formal qualifications, roughly following the Qianlong fourteenth-year precedent for recommending classical scholars; men of upright character, pure learning, and outstanding teaching records should be summoned, honored as tutors, and assigned to teach by subject, while a great minister oversees the whole, so they may devote themselves daily to the imperial lectures without other official burdens. The more specialized the duty, the heavier the responsibility, the swifter the effect; let all under heaven understand the intent to honor teachers and exalt Confucian learning, so scholars may have a model to admire and sage virtue daily advance. Third, I request strict regulations. In antiquity when sage kings taught the imperial heirs, they chose upright men of broad learning in the Way to dwell with them; thus habit grew with wisdom and character formed with the heart. Our emperor's character is newly forming and the foundation should be made firm; the old system of eunuch reading companions seems insufficient to dignify the school system and broaden moral guidance. It is proposed to choose worthy sons of princes and great ministers to attend morning and evening, learning from each other, and with reference to school systems to build lecture halls, display books and ritual vessels for study and observation, to achieve reverent learning and fellowship. The three matters above present only the main outline. Our emperor's words, conduct, and daily life today are without irreverence; likewise in future establishing government and spreading teaching there will be nothing amiss — this is the fundamental plan, which must be carefully attended from the start. I reflect that the court broadly encourages talent and revives education; countless students strive to improve themselves; as our emperor's studies have just begun, it is all the more fitting to expand this far-reaching plan to satisfy the hopes of scholars and people throughout the realm. "

Lao Naixuan, whose style was Yuchu, was a native of Tongxiang in Zhejiang. In the tenth year of Tongzhi he passed the jinshi examination and was assigned as county magistrate in Zhili. Investigating the enclosed estates of the Prince of Li's mansion in Laishui, he strongly petitioned to reduce rent and relieve the people's hardship. Serving in Wan county, he purchased more than ten thousand volumes of books and stored them in the Hall of Honored Classics. His works include Records of Leaving Peace, Explanatory Studies of Ancient Counting Rods, Essentials of Treaty Compilation, and Collected Poems and Essays.

=沈曾植=沈曾植，字子培，浙江嘉興人。 光緒六年進士，用刑部主事。 事親孝，母多疾，醫藥必親嘗，終歲未嘗解衣安臥，遂通醫。 遷員外郎，擢郎中。 居刑曹十八年，專研古今律令書，由大明律、宋律統、唐律上溯漢、魏，於是有漢律輯補、晉書刑法志補之作。 曾植為學兼綜漢、宋，而尤深於史學掌故，後專治遼、金、元三史，及西北輿地，南洋貿遷沿革。 尋充總理衙門章京。 中日和議成，曾植請自借英款創辦東三省鐵路，時俄之韋特西比利亞鐵路尚未建議也，不果行。 母憂歸，兩湖總督張之洞聘主兩湖書院講席。
Shen Cengzhi, whose style was Zipei, was a native of Jiaxing in Zhejiang. In the sixth year of Guangxu he passed the jinshi examination and was appointed a director in the Ministry of Punishments. He was filial toward his parents; his mother was often ill; he always tasted her medicines himself; for a full year he never undressed to sleep peacefully, and thus came to master medicine. He was transferred to Vice Director and promoted to Director. He served in the Punishments Bureau for eighteen years, specializing in codes and statutes ancient and modern; from the Ming, Song, and Tang codes he traced back to Han and Wei; thus he produced Collected Supplements to the Han Code and Supplements to the Treatise on Punishments in the History of Jin. Cengzhi's learning combined Han and Song traditions, yet he was especially deep in historical precedents; later he specialized in the Liao, Jin, and Yuan histories, northwestern geography, and the evolution of Southeast Asian trade. Soon he served as a secretary in the Zongli Yamen. When the Sino-Japanese peace treaty was concluded, Cengzhi proposed borrowing British funds to build the Three Eastern Provinces railway; at the time Russia's Trans-Siberian railway had not yet been proposed, and the plan was not carried out. Returning home to mourn his mother, Huguang Governor-General Zhang Zhidong engaged him to head the lecture chair at the Two Lakes Academy.
21
拳亂啟釁，曾植與盛宣懷等密商保護長江之策，力疾走江、鄂，決大計於劉坤一、張之洞，而以李鴻章主其成，所謂「畫保東南約」也。 旋還京，調外交部。 出授江西廣信知府，曾植為政，知民情偽，而持之以忠恕，故事治而民親。 歷署督糧道、鹽法道，擢安徽提學使，赴日本考察學務。 三十二年，署布政使，尋護巡撫。 值江、鄂、皖三省軍會操太湖，而適遭國卹，群情忷忷，民一日數驚，城外砲馬兵又譁變。 曾植聞之，登城守禦，檄協統餘大鴻馳入江防，楚材兵艦擊毀東門外砲兵壁壘，黃鳳岐奪回菱湖嘴火藥局，一日而亂定。
When the Boxer disorder opened hostilities, Cengzhi with Sheng Xuanhuai and others secretly devised a plan to protect the Yangzi; forcing himself despite illness he hurried to Jiangsu and Hubei, settled the great plan with Liu Kunyi and Zhang Zhidong, and had Li Hongzhang bring it to completion — the so-called Southeast Mutual Protection Agreement. Soon he returned to the capital and was transferred to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Sent out as prefect of Guangxin in Jiangxi, Cengzhi in governing knew the people's true conditions, yet upheld loyalty and forbearance; thus affairs were well ordered and the people were close to him. He served in turn as grain superintendent and salt commissioner, was promoted to Educational Commissioner of Anhui, and went to Japan to investigate educational affairs. In the thirty-second year he acted as Financial Commissioner and soon after served as acting governor. It happened that the armies of Jiangsu, Hubei, and Anhui held joint maneuvers at Taihu, while the nation was in mourning; public sentiment was agitated, the people were alarmed repeatedly, and outside the city the artillery and cavalry troops mutinied. When Cengzhi heard of it he mounted the wall to defend; he ordered Assistant Commander Yu Dahong to hurry to the river defenses; the Chucai gunboat destroyed the artillery breastworks outside the eastern gate; Huang Fengqi recovered the Linghu Mouth powder magazine; in one day the disorder was settled.
22
曾植在皖五年，重治人而尚禮治，政無鉅細，皆以身先。 其任學使，廣教育，設存古學堂。 又興實業，創造紙諸廠。 會外人要我訂約開銅官山礦，曾植嚴拒之。 未幾，貝子載振出皖境，當道命籓庫支巨款供張，曾植不允，遂與當道忤。 宣統二年，移病歸。 遜位詔下，痛哭不能止。 丁巳復辟，授學部尚書。 事變歸，臥病海上，壬戌冬，卒，年七十三。 著有海日樓文詩集。
Cengzhi was in Anhui five years, emphasizing governing people and honoring ritual governance; in affairs great or small he always led by example. As educational commissioner he broadened education and established the Academy for Preserving Antiquity. He also promoted industry and founded paper mills and other factories. When foreigners demanded a treaty to open the Tongguanshan mines, Cengzhi sternly refused. Before long Prince Zai Zhen left Anhui; the authorities ordered the provincial treasury to disburse a huge sum for his entertainment; Cengzhi refused and thus clashed with the authorities. In the second year of Xuantong he cited illness and returned home. When the abdication edict was issued, he wept bitterly and could not stop. In the dingsi year, when restoration was attempted, he was appointed Minister of Education. When the crisis ended he returned home and lay ill by the sea; in the winter of the renxu year he died at seventy-three. His works include Collected Writings and Poems from the Sea-Sun Pavilion.

=【論】=論曰：辛壬之際，世變推移，莫之為而為，其中蓋有天焉。 潤庠、世續諸人非濟變才，而鞠躬盡瘁，始終如一，亦為人所難者也。 乃宣、曾植皆碩學有遠識，惓惓不忘，卒憂傷憔悴以死。 嗚呼，豈非天哉！
[Commentary] The commentary says: At the xin-ren turning point, the world shifted; no one made it happen yet it happened — within this there was surely heaven's hand. Runxiang, Shi Xu, and the others were not men who could meet the crisis of change, yet they gave their all from start to finish — that too is what others find hard to match. Naixuan and Cengzhi were both great scholars of far-sighted understanding, earnest and never forgetting, and in the end died worn away by grief. Alas, was this not heaven's doing!
