阮元汪廷珍湯金釗
Ruan Yuan, Wang Tingzhen, and Tang Jinzhao
3
阮元，字伯元，江蘇儀徵人。 祖玉堂，官湖南參將，從征苗，活降苗數千人，有陰德。
Ruan Yuan, courtesy name Boyuan, was from Yizheng in Jiangsu. His grandfather Yutang had been a deputy regional commander in Hunan; on Miao campaigns he spared and persuaded several thousand tribesmen to submit, building hidden merit for the family.
4
元，乾隆五十四年進士，選庶吉士，散館第一，授編修。 逾年大考，高宗親擢第一，超擢少詹事。 召對，上喜曰：「不意朕八旬外復得一人！ 」直南書房、懋勤殿，遷詹事。 五十八年，督山東學政，任滿，調浙江。 歷兵部、禮部、戶部侍郎。
Ruan passed the jinshi examination in 1789, entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor, topped the academy leaving examination, and received an appointment as compiler. In the next year's palace examination the Qianlong Emperor personally placed him first and leapfrogged him to junior vice-president of the Household Administration. Called to audience, the emperor said with delight, "Beyond my eightieth year I never expected to find another man like this! " He was assigned to the Southern Study and Maqin Hall, then promoted to grand tutor of the heir apparent. In 1793 he became educational commissioner of Shandong, and at the end of his term was moved to Zhejiang. He served in turn as vice-minister of War, Rites, and Revenue.
5
嘉慶四年，署浙江巡撫，尋實授。 海寇擾浙歷數年，安南夷艇最強，鳳尾、水澳、箬黃諸幫附之，沿海土匪勾結為患。 元徵集群議為弭盜之策，造船砲，練陸師，杜接濟。 五年春，令黃岩鎮總兵岳璽擊箬黃幫，滅之。 夏，寇大至，元赴台州督剿，請以定海鎮總兵李長庚總統三鎮水師，並調粵、閩兵會剿。 六月，夷艇糾鳳尾、水澳等賊共百餘艘，屯松門山下。 遣諜間水澳賊先退，會颶風大作，盜艇覆溺無算，餘眾登山，檄陸師搜捕，擒八百餘人。 安南四總兵溺斃者三，黃岩知縣孫鳳鳴獲其一，曰倫貴利，磔之。 九月，總兵岳璽、胡振聲會擊水澳幫，擒殲殆盡。 土匪亦次第殲撫。 浙洋漸清，而餘盜為蔡牽所並，閩師不能制，勢益熾，复時犯浙。 李長庚巳擢提督，元集貲與造霆船成，配巨砲，數破牽於海上。 八年，奏建昭忠祠，以歷年捕海盜傷亡將士從祀。 盜首黃葵集舟數十，號新興幫，令總兵岳璽、張成等追剿，逾年乃平之。 偕總督玉德奏請以李長庚總督兩省水師，數逐蔡牽幾獲，而玉德遇事仍掣肘。 十年，元丁父憂去職，長庚益無助，復與總督阿林保不協，久無成功，遂戰歿。
In 1799 he acted as governor of Zhejiang and soon received the substantive post. Pirates had harassed Zhejiang for years; the strongest force was Annamese pirate craft, with the Fengwei, Shui'ao, Ruohuang, and other gangs in their train, while coastal bandits joined in to compound the trouble. Ruan convened a council on suppressing piracy, ordered new warships and guns, trained land forces, and blocked outside support. In the spring of 1800 he had Huangyan garrison commander Yue Xi strike the Ruohuang gang and wipe it out. That summer, as pirates swarmed in, Ruan went to Taizhou to direct operations, asked that Dinghai commander Li Changgeng take overall command of the three naval squadrons, and brought in Guangdong and Fujian forces for a joint campaign. In the sixth month more than a hundred foreign craft and Fengwei and Shui'ao pirates gathered and anchored below Songmen Mountain. He used agents to split the Shui'ao gang so they pulled out first; a typhoon then wrecked countless pirate boats, and the survivors fled inland, where land troops rounded up more than eight hundred of them. Three of four Annamese commanders drowned; Huangyan magistrate Sun Fengming captured one, Lun Guili, and had him executed by dismemberment. In the ninth month commanders Yue Xi and Hu Zhensheng jointly attacked the Shui'ao gang and all but annihilated it. Land bandits were likewise wiped out or pacified in turn. Zhejiang waters grew calmer, but surviving pirates rallied under Cai Qian; Fujian troops could not contain him, his power swelled, and he again raided Zhejiang. Li Changgeng had been promoted to provincial commander-in-chief; Ruan raised funds, built "thunder" warships armed with heavy guns, and repeatedly defeated Cai Qian at sea. In 1803 he memorialized for a Zhaozhong Shrine to honor officers and men killed in years of anti-pirate fighting. Pirate leader Huang Kui mustered several dozen ships as the Xinxing gang; Ruan had commanders Yue Xi and Zhang Cheng pursue them, and more than a year passed before they were subdued. With Governor Yude he asked that Li Changgeng command both provinces' fleets; Li several times nearly took Cai Qian, but Yude still obstructed him at every turn. In 1805 Ruan left office to mourn his father; Li Changgeng was even less supported, fell out with Governor Alinbao, made no headway for years, and finally died in battle.
6
十一年，詔起元署福建巡撫，以病辭。 十二年，服闋，署戶部侍郎，赴河南按事。 授兵部侍郎，復命為浙江巡撫，暫署河南巡撫。 十三年，乃至浙，詔責其防海殄寇。 秋，蔡牽、硃濆合犯定海，親駐寧波督三鎮擊走之，牽复遁閩洋。 時用長庚部將王得祿、邱良功為兩省提督，協力勦賊，元議海戰分兵隔賊船之策，專攻蔡牽。 十四年秋，合擊​​於漁山外洋，竟殄牽，詳得祿等傳。 元兩治浙，多惠政，平寇功尤著雲。
In 1806 an edict recalled him to act as Fujian governor, but he declined on grounds of illness. In 1807, when mourning ended, he acted as vice-minister of Revenue and went to Henan on an investigative tour. He was appointed vice-minister of War, again named governor of Zhejiang, and briefly acted as governor of Henan. In 1808 he finally reached Zhejiang, under an edict charging him to guard the coast and destroy the pirates. That autumn Cai Qian and Zhu Fen jointly attacked Dinghai; he personally stationed at Ningbo to direct the three garrisons in driving them off, and Cai Qian again fled to Fujian waters. Li Changgeng's former subordinates Wang Delu and Qiu Lianggong were now provincial commanders of the two provinces; cooperating to suppress the pirates, Ruan devised a tactic of dividing fleets to cut off enemy ships and focused the attack on Cai Qian. In the autumn of 1809 they fought together on the open sea off Yushan and finally destroyed Cai Qian; see the biographies of Wang Delu and the others for details. Ruan governed Zhejiang twice, enacted many popular measures, and his success in pacifying piracy was especially notable.
7
方督師寧波時，奏請學政劉鳳誥代辦鄉試監臨，有聯號弊，為言官論劾，遣使鞫實，詔斥徇庇，褫職，予編修，在文穎館行走。 累遷內閣學士。 命赴山西、河南按事，遷工部侍郎，出為漕運總督。 十九年，調江西巡撫。 以捕治逆匪胡秉耀，加太子少保，賜花翎。 二十一年，調河南，擢湖廣總督。 修武昌江堤，建江陵范家堤、沔陽龍王廟石閘。
While directing operations at Ningbo he had educational commissioner Liu Fenggao supervise the provincial examination in his place; linked examination numbers were exposed, censors impeached him, investigators confirmed it, and an edict rebuked his cover-up, stripped his rank, and reduced him to compiler serving in the Wenyin Hall. He was promoted in stages to Grand Secretariat academician. Sent on investigative tours to Shanxi and Henan, he was moved to vice-minister of Works and then appointed director-general of grain transport. In 1814 he was transferred to governor of Jiangxi. For capturing the rebel Hu Bingyao he was made junior guardian of the heir apparent and awarded the peacock feather. In 1816 he was moved to Henan and promoted to governor-general of Huguang. He repaired the Yangtze dikes at Wuchang and built the Fan family dike at Jiangling and a stone sluice at the Dragon King Temple in Mianyang.
8
二十二年，調兩廣總督。 先一年，英吉利貢使入京，未成禮而回，遂漸跋扈。 元增建大黃、大虎山兩砲台，分兵駐守。 迭疏陳預防夷患，略曰：「英吉利恃強桀驁，性復貪利。 宜鎮以威，不可盡以德綏。 彼之船堅砲利，技長於水短於陸。 定例外國貨船不許擅入內洋，儻違例禁，即宜隨機應變，量加懲創。 各國知彼犯我禁，非我輕啟釁也。 」詔勗以德威相濟，勿孟浪，勿葸懦。 道光元年，兼署粵海關監督。 洋船夾帶鴉片煙，劾褫行商頂帶。 二年，英吉利護貨兵船泊伶丁外洋，與民鬥，互有傷斃，嚴飭交犯，英人揚言罷市歸國，即停其貿易。 久之拆閱多，託言兵船已歸，俟復來如命。 乃暫許貿易，與約船來不交犯乃停止。 終元任，兵船不至。 元在粵九年，兼署巡撫凡六次。
In 1817 he was transferred to governor-general of Guangdong and Guangxi. The year before, the English tribute embassy had come to Beijing but left without completing the rites, and thereafter grew increasingly arrogant. Ruan added batteries at Dahuangjiao and Dahushan and stationed troops at each. In repeated memorials on guarding against foreign trouble he wrote in summary: "England relies on force and is obstinate and unruly, and by nature greedy for profit as well. They should be checked by awe and cannot be wholly won by conciliation. Their ships are stout and their guns powerful; they excel on water and are weak on land. Foreign merchant ships are barred from the inner seas without permission; if they break the rule, respond as circumstances require and punish them proportionately. Other nations will see that they broke our rules and that we did not lightly pick a quarrel. " An edict urged him to balance conciliation and force, neither to be rash nor timid. In 1821 he also acted as superintendent of the Guangdong maritime customs. Foreign ships were smuggling opium; he impeached the licensed merchants and stripped their rank insignia. In 1822 English convoy warships anchored off Lintin; fighting with locals left casualties on both sides; he sternly demanded the culprits be handed over; when the English threatened to boycott trade and leave, he halted their commerce at once. After a long interval many dispatches were opened; they claimed the warships had left and would return when told. He then allowed trade temporarily on the condition that if ships came without surrendering the culprits commerce would stop. Throughout Ruan's tenure the warships never returned. Ruan spent nine years in Guangdong and acted as governor six times in all.
9
六年，調雲貴總督。 滇鹽久敝，歲絀課十餘萬，元劾罷蠹吏，力杜漏私； 鹽井衰旺不齊，調劑抵補，逾年課有溢銷，酌撥邊用。 騰越邊外野人時入內地劫掠，而保山等處邊夷曰枲僳，以墾山射獵為生，可用，乃募枲僳三百戶屯種山地，以御野人，即以溢課充費，歲有擴充。 野人畏威，漸有降附者。 十二年，協辦大學士，仍留總督任。 車里土司刀繩武與叔太康爭鬥，脅官求助，檄鎮道擊走之，另擇承襲乃安。 越南保樂州土官農文云內閧，嚴邊防勿使竄入，亦不越境生事，尋文云走死。 詔嘉其鎮靜得大體。 十五年，召拜體仁閣大學士，管理刑部，調兵部。 十八年，以老病請致仕，許之，給半俸，瀕行，加太子太保。 二十六年，鄉舉重逢，晉太傅，與鹿鳴宴。 二十九年，卒，年八十有六，優詔賜卹，諡文達。 入祀鄉賢祠、浙江名宦祠。
In 1826 he was transferred to governor-general of Yunnan and Guizhou. Yunnan salt revenue had long been corrupt, with annual shortfalls over a hundred thousand taels; Ruan impeached corrupt officials and worked hard to stop smuggling; as salt wells waxed and waned unevenly he adjusted accounts to offset shortfalls; after more than a year revenue showed a surplus, which he allotted for border expenses. Wild tribes beyond Tengyue often raided inland; Lisu tribesmen in Baoshan and elsewhere lived by hill farming and hunting and could be enlisted; he recruited three hundred Lisu households to farm border hills against the raiders, funding the settlements from surplus salt revenue, which grew year by year. The wild tribes feared his authority and some gradually submitted. In 1832 he was made assisting grand secretary while retaining the governorship. Cheli native official Dao Shengwu fought his uncle Taikang and pressured officials for help; Ruan ordered regional commanders to drive them off, chose another heir for succession, and restored order. Vietnam's Baole native official Nong Wenyun had an internal feud; Ruan tightened the border so he could not flee in but also did not provoke trouble across it; Wenyun soon fled and died. An edict praised his calm grasp of the larger situation. In 1835 he was summoned as grand secretary of the Tiren Hall, put in charge of the Ministry of Punishments, and transferred to the Ministry of War. In 1838, citing age and illness, he asked to retire; permission was granted with half salary, and just before he left he was made senior guardian of the heir apparent. In 1846 he again met the provincial examination year of his own jinshi cohort, was promoted to grand mentor, and attended the lu ming banquet. In 1849 he died at eighty-six; a gracious edict granted funeral honors and the posthumous title Wendá. He was enshrined in the local worthies hall and the Zhejiang eminent officials hall.
10
元博學淹通，早被知遇。 敕編石渠寶笈，校勘石經。 再入翰林，創編國史儒林、文苑傳，至為浙江巡撫，始手成之。 集四庫未收書一百七十二種，撰提要進禦，補中秘之闕。 嘉慶四年，偕大學士硃珪典會試，一時樸學高才搜羅殆盡。 道光十三年，由雲南入覲，特命典試，時稱異數。 與大學士曹振鏞共事意不合，元歉然。 以前次得人之盛不可復繼，歷官所至，振興文教。 在浙江立詁經精舍，祀許慎、鄭康成，選高才肄業； 在粵立學海堂亦如之，並延攬通儒：造士有家法，人才蔚起。 撰十三經校勘記、經籍篡詁、皇清經解百八十餘種，專宗漢學，治經者奉為科律。 集清代天文、律算諸家作疇人傳，以章絕學。 重修浙江通志、廣東通志，編輯山左金石志、兩浙金石志、積古齋鐘鼎款識、兩浙輶軒錄、淮海英靈集，刊當代名宿著述數十家為文選樓叢書。 自著曰揅經室集。 他紀事、談藝諸編，並為世重。 身歷乾、嘉文物鼎盛之時，主持風會數十年，海內學者奉為山斗焉。
Ruan was broadly learned and versatile and won early imperial favor. He was ordered to compile the Shiqu baoji imperial catalogue and collate the stone classics. On returning to the Hanlin he began the Ruilin and Wenyuan sections of the national history and only finished them by hand once he became governor of Zhejiang. He gathered 172 works omitted from the Siku Library, wrote summaries for the throne, and filled gaps in the imperial collection. In 1799, with Grand Secretary Zhu Gui, he presided over the metropolitan examination and nearly all the leading scholars of the day were selected. In 1833, while coming from Yunnan for audience, he was specially ordered to preside over the examination, which contemporaries called a rare honor. Working with Grand Secretary Cao Zhenyong he found their views at odds and felt regret. Knowing the brilliance of his earlier selections could not be repeated, wherever he served he revived literary education. In Zhejiang he founded the Gujing Academy, honoring Xu Shen and Zheng Xuan and enrolling talented students; in Guangdong he founded the Xuehai Hall on the same model, recruited leading scholars, and built a school of talent that produced a flowering of ability. He wrote Collation Notes on the Thirteen Classics and Jingji zuangu and collected more than 180 Qing commentaries, championing Han Learning as the standard for classical studies. He compiled Biographies of Calculators on Qing astronomers and mathematicians to honor supreme learning. He revised the Zhejiang and Guangdong gazetteers, edited Shandong and Two-Zhejiang epigraphic records, Jigu zhai inscriptions, a Two-Zhejiang examination anthology, and Huaihai yingling, and published dozens of contemporary masters' works as the Wenxuanlou congshu. His own collected writings are the Yanjingshi ji. His other chronicles and literary miscellanies were likewise prized in his day. He lived through the Qianlong and Jiaqing flowering of culture, shaped the scholarly world for decades, and scholars across the empire revered him as the undisputed authority.
11
汪廷珍，字瑟庵，江蘇山陽人。 少孤，母程撫之成立。 家中落，歲兇，饘粥或不給，不令人知。 母曰：「吾非恥貧，恥言貧，疑有求於人也。 」力學，困諸生十年，始舉於鄉。 成乾隆五十四年一甲二名進士，授編修。 大考，擢侍讀。 未幾，遷祭酒。 六十年，以事忤旨，降侍講。 嘉慶元年，直上書房。 大考，擢侍講學士。 母憂歸，服闋，補原官。 七年，督安徽學政。 任滿，复督江西學政。 累遷侍讀學士、太僕寺卿、內閣學士，皆留任。
Wang Tingzhen, courtesy name Se'an, was from Shanyang in Jiangsu. Orphaned young, his mother Cheng raised him to adulthood. The family fell on hard times; in famine years they sometimes lacked even thin gruel, yet she let no one know. His mother said, "I am not ashamed of being poor, only of talking about poverty, as if we were begging from others. " He studied hard, struggled as a licentiate for ten years, and only then passed the provincial examination. In 1789 he placed second in the first class of the jinshi examination and was appointed compiler. In the palace examination he was promoted to reader. Before long he was made libationer of the Imperial Academy. In 1795 an offense against the imperial will demoted him to expositor. In 1796 he was assigned to the Upper Study. In the palace examination he was promoted to expositor of the Hanlin Academy. He went home on his mother's death; when mourning ended he resumed his former post. In 1802 he became educational commissioner of Anhui. When his term ended he again became educational commissioner of Jiangxi. He rose in stages to Hanlin reader, minister of the Court of Imperial Studs, and Grand Secretariat academician, each time keeping his educational commission.
12
廷珍學有根底，初為祭酒，以師道自居，選成均課士錄，教學者立言以義法，力戒摹擬剽竊之習。 及官學政，為學約五則以訓士：曰辨塗，曰端本，曰敬業，曰裁偽，曰自立。 與士語，諄諄如父兄之於子弟。 所刻試牘，取易修辭之旨曰立誠編。 士風為之一變。 萬載棚民入籍，舊分學額，後裁之，土客訐訟久不決； 廷珍請復分額，爭端乃息。 十六年，授禮部侍郎。 复直上書房，侍宣宗學。 十八年，典浙江鄉試，留學政，任滿回京。 二十二年，署翰林院掌院學士，擢左都御史，充上書房總師傅。 二十三年，遷禮部尚書。 二十四年，仁宗六旬萬壽，慶賀期內遇孝慈高皇后忌辰，部臣未援故事疏請服色，坐率忽，降侍郎。 逾年，复授禮部尚書。
Wang's scholarship was deeply grounded; as libationer he held himself to the teacher's role, chose the Imperial Academy curriculum, taught students to write by principles of meaning and method, and strongly warned against imitation and plagiarism. As educational commissioner he issued five rules for students: discern the right path, rectify fundamentals, revere learning, cut off falsity, and stand on one's own. In speaking with students he was earnest and kindly, like a father or elder brother to his sons. His published examination essays, taking the Yi Xiu ci zhi as their theme, were titled the Licheng bian. Scholarly manners were transformed as a result. Shed-dwellers of Wanzai had been registered and once shared examination quotas; when quotas were later cut, natives and migrants sued for years without resolution; Wang asked that quotas be divided again, and the dispute ceased. In 1811 he was appointed vice-minister of Rites. He again served in the Upper Study, tutoring the future Xuanzong Emperor. In 1813 he presided over the Zhejiang provincial examination, stayed on as educational commissioner, and returned to Beijing when his term ended. In 1817 he acted as Hanlin chancellor, was promoted to left censor-in-chief, and became chief tutor of the Upper Study. In 1818 he was made minister of Rites. In 1819, during Renzong's sixtieth birthday celebrations, the anniversary of Empress Xiaoci Gao fell within the congratulatory period; ministry officials failed to cite precedent on mourning dress, and Wang was demoted to vice-minister for negligence. More than a year later he was again made minister of Rites.
13
道光二年，典會試，教習庶吉士。 車駕謁陵，命留京辦事。 三年，宣宗釋奠文廟禮成，臨幸辟雍，詔曰：「禮部尚書汪廷珍蒙皇考簡用上書房師傅，與朕朝夕講論，非法不道，使朕通經義，辨邪正，受益良多。 朕親政後，畀以尚書之任，盡心厥職，於師道、臣道可謂兼備。 今值臨雍，眷懷舊學，加太子太保。 子報原，以員外郎即補用，示崇儒重道之意。 」四年，仁宗實錄成，賜子報閏主事，孫承佑舉人。 南河高堰潰決阻運，上以廷珍生長淮、揚，命偕尚書文孚往勘，劾河督張文浩、總督孫玉庭，譴黜有差。 疏籌修濬事宜，交河督辦理。 五年，回京，協辦大學士。 七年，卒，上震悼，優詔賜卹，贈太子太師，入祀賢良祠，命大阿哥賜奠，賜銀千兩治喪，諡文端。 江蘇請祀鄉賢，特詔允之。
In 1822 he presided over the metropolitan examination and instructed Hanlin bachelors. When the emperor visited the imperial tombs he was ordered to remain in Beijing to handle affairs. In 1823, after the Xuanzong Emperor completed the Confucian temple sacrifice and visited the Imperial Academy, an edict said: "Minister of Rites Wang Tingzhen was chosen by my late father as Upper Study tutor; he discussed learning with me daily, would not speak of what was unlawful or improper, helped me master the classics and distinguish right from wrong, and benefited me greatly. After I took personal rule I gave him a ministry; he devoted himself to his duties and fulfilled both the teacher's and the minister's roles. Now, cherishing my old studies on this visit to the Academy, I make him senior guardian of the heir apparent. His son Baoyuan is given immediate appointment as a department director to show esteem for learning and the Way. " In 1824, when Renzong's veritable records were completed, his son was given bureau director rank with intercalary appointment and his grandson Chengyou the rank of provincial graduate. The Gaoyan dike on the South Canal burst and blocked transport; because Wang had grown up in the Huai and Yang regions, the emperor sent him with Minister Wen Fu to investigate, impeached canal director Zhang Wenhao and Governor Sun Yuting, and punished them in varying degrees. He memorialized repair and dredging plans and entrusted them to the canal director. In 1825 he returned to Beijing as assisting grand secretary. In 1827 he died; the emperor was deeply grieved, granted gracious funeral honors, posthumously made him grand mentor of the heir apparent, enshrined him in the Worthy Officials Shrine, ordered the eldest imperial son to offer sacrifices, granted a thousand taels for the funeral, and gave the posthumous title Wenduan. Jiangsu requested enshrinement in the local worthies hall, and a special edict granted it.
14
廷珍風裁嚴峻，立朝無所親附。 出入內廷，寮寀見之，莫不肅然。 自言生平力戒刻薄，凡貪冒諂諛有不忍為，皆守母教。 大學士阮元服其多聞淵博，勸著書，廷珍曰：「六經之奧，昔人先我言之，便何以長語相溷？ 讀書所以析義，要歸於中有所主而已。 」服用樸儉，或以公孫弘擬之，笑曰：「大丈夫不以曲學阿世為恥，而徒畏布被之譏乎？ 」後進以文謁，言不宗道，曰：「異日恐喪所守。 」屬官有例送御史者，持不可，曰：「斯人華而不實，何以立朝？ 」後皆如所言，人服其精鑑。
Wang's bearing was stern; in court he belonged to no faction. Entering and leaving the inner court, colleagues who saw him were uniformly awed into respect. He said that all his life he strove against harshness and refused greed, presumption, and flattery by keeping his mother's teaching. Grand Secretary Ruan Yuan admired his breadth of learning and urged him to write books; Wang said, "The profundities of the Six Classics were spoken of before me by men of old—how could I mingle long discourse with theirs? Reading is for analyzing meaning; what matters is having an inward master principle. " He dressed plainly; when some compared him to Gongsun Hong he laughed, "A great man is ashamed of crooked learning and currying favor with the age—would he only fear ridicule for a cloth quilt? " When juniors presented writings, if the words did not honor the Way he said, "Some day you may lose what you should hold to. " A subordinate routinely recommended someone for censor; he refused, saying, "This man is showy without substance—how could he stand in court? " Later events proved him right, and people admired his keen judgment.
15
湯金釗，字敦甫，浙江蕭山人。 嘉慶四年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 十三年，入直上書房。 金釗端謹自持，宣宗在潛邸，甚敬禮之。 母憂服闋，擢侍講，督湖南學政。 累遷內閣學士。 二十一年，复直上書房。 典江南鄉試，留學政，詔勉以訓士不患無才，務培德，經學為本，才藻次之。 金釗闡揚詔旨，通誡士子。 會匪以禍福煽惑鄉愚，金釗著福善辨，刊發曉諭。 徐州俗悍，武生不馴者，繩之以法。 遷禮部侍郎，任滿，仍直上書房。
Tang Jinzhao, courtesy name Dunfu, was from Xiaoshan in Zhejiang. A jinshi of 1799, he entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor and was appointed compiler. In 1808 he entered the Upper Study. Tang was upright and self-restrained; the future Xuanzong Emperor, while still heir apparent, greatly respected him. When mourning for his mother ended he was promoted to expositor and became educational commissioner of Hunan. He was promoted in stages to Grand Secretariat academician. In 1816 he again served in the Upper Study. He presided over the Jiangnan provincial examination and stayed on as educational commissioner; an edict urged him that in instructing scholars one need not worry about lack of talent but must cultivate virtue, with classical learning as foundation and literary ornament secondary. Tang expounded the edict and broadly admonished the scholars. Secret societies used fortune and misfortune to incite country folk; Tang wrote the Fushan bian and published it to enlighten them. Xuzhou custom was fierce; martial licentiates who would not submit he restrained by law. He was made vice-minister of Rites; when his term ended he still served in the Upper Study.
16
宣宗即位，調吏部，益鄉用。 時用尚書英和議，命各省查州縣陋規，明定限制。 金釗疏言：「陋規皆出於民，地方官未敢公然苛索者，畏上知之治其罪也。 今若明定章程，即為例所應得，勢必明目張膽，求多於例外，雖有嚴旨，不能禁矣。 況名目碎雜，所在不同，檢察難得真確，轉滋紛擾。 無論不當明定章程，亦不能妥立章程也。 吏治貴在得人，​​得其人，雖取於民而民愛戴之，不害其為清； 非其人，雖不取於民而民嫉仇之，何論其為清？ 有治人無治法，惟在督撫舉措公明，而非立法所能限制。 」會中外大臣亦多言其不便，金釗疏入，上手批答曰：「朝有諍臣，使朕胸中黑白分明，無傷於政體，不勝欣悅！ 」予議敘。
When the Xuanzong Emperor took the throne he was transferred to the Ministry of Personnel and increasingly favored men from his home region. Minister Yinghe's proposal was adopted, ordering each province to investigate county and prefectural improper fees and fix clear limits. Tang memorialized: "Improper fees all come from the people; local officials dare not openly extort because they fear the court learning of it and punishing them. If we now fix regulations openly, they become what precedent allows; officials will surely seek more than the exception in broad daylight, and even stern edicts cannot stop them. Moreover the items are numerous and differ from place to place; investigation can hardly obtain truth and will only increase disorder. Leaving aside that regulations ought not be fixed openly, they also cannot be properly established. Good official governance lies in obtaining the right men; with the right man, even if he takes from the people they love him, and it does not harm his integrity; without the right man, even if he does not take from the people they hate him—what talk is there of his integrity? There is governance of men but no governance by law alone; it lies in governors-general and governors acting fairly, and cannot be limited by legislation. " Ministers at court and abroad also said it was inconvenient; when Tang's memorial arrived, the emperor wrote in reply: "The court has remonstrating ministers, enabling me to distinguish right from wrong without harm to the polity—I am exceedingly pleased! " He was given merit consideration in evaluation.
17
道光元年，兼署戶部侍郎。 兩江總督孫玉庭以南漕浮收不能盡去，議請八折徵收，學政姚文田、御史王家相皆奏言不可。 金釗既同部臣議覆，复疏爭曰：「康熙中奉永不加賦之明詔，此大清億萬年培養國脈之至計也。 前有議加耗米及公費銀者，戶部以事近加賦議駁。 今准其略有浮收，不肖者益無顧忌，而浮收且多於往日，雖告以收逾八折即予嚴參，然前此逾額者何嘗不干嚴譴，卒不聞為之減少，獨於新定之額，恪遵而不敢逾，此臣之所不敢信也。 在督撫奏定之後，不慮控告浮收； 在州縣縱有發覺，又將巧脫其罪。 是限制仍同虛設，徒為盛朝開加賦之端，臣竊惜之！ 」疏入，下江、浙督撫妥議，事乃寢。 尋以吏部事繁，罷直上書房。 典江南鄉試，道經銅山，見運河支渠為黃流淤塞，歲苦潦，回京奏請疏濬，如議行。 二年，典會試，調戶部，父憂歸。 六年，服闋，署禮、工二部及倉場侍郎，仍直上書房，授皇長子奕緯讀。 實授戶部侍郎。 七年，連擢左都御史、禮部尚書、上方倚畀，迭命赴山西、直隸、四川、湖北、福建鞫獄按事，四年之中，凡奉使五次。 所至持法明慎，悉當上意。 充上書房總師傅，調吏部尚書。 十一年，皇長子遘疾不起，忌者因以激上怒，罷總師傅，降兵部侍郎。 逾兩年，復自左都御史授工部尚書，轉吏部。 連典江南、順天鄉試。 十六年，陝西巡撫楊名颺被劾，命偕侍郎文慶往按，暫署巡撫； 又往四川按事，名颺復與臬司互訐，得其冒工庇屬狀，劾罷。 會京察，以奉使公明，予議敘。 又赴張家口、太原鞫獄。 十八年，以戶部尚書協辦大學士，仍調吏部。
In 1821 he also acted as vice-minister of Revenue. Liangjiang Governor-General Sun Yuting, because surplus collection on southern tribute transport could not be wholly removed, proposed collecting at eighty percent; Educational Commissioner Yao Wentian and censor Wang Jiaxiang both said it could not be done. Tang had joined ministry colleagues in the reply memorial but protested again: "In the Kangxi reign came the edict that the land tax would never be increased—this is the supreme plan by which the Qing nourishes the nation's lifeblood for ten thousand years. Previously when surcharge grain and public-expense silver were proposed, the Ministry of Revenue rejected it as nearly equivalent to increasing the tax. If slight surplus collection is now permitted, the unscrupulous will be more reckless and surplus collection will exceed former levels; though told that collection beyond eighty percent will bring severe impeachment, past excesses have often drawn severe punishment yet never been reduced—only the newly fixed quota will be strictly obeyed: this I dare not believe. After governors-general and governors report the fixed quota, accusations of surplus collection need not be feared; if county and prefectural officials are caught, they will cleverly escape punishment. Thus the limit remains void, vainly opening for our flourishing dynasty a gateway to increasing the tax—I lament it! " When the memorial arrived, it was sent to Jiangsu and Zhejiang governors for deliberation, and the matter lapsed. Soon, because Ministry of Personnel affairs were heavy, he left the Upper Study. While presiding over the Jiangnan provincial examination he passed Tongshan, saw canal branches silted by the Yellow River and yearly flooding, and on returning to Beijing memorialized for dredging, which was carried out. In 1822 he presided over the metropolitan examination, was transferred to the Ministry of Revenue, and went home on his father's death. In 1826, when mourning ended, he acted as vice-minister of Rites and Works and of the granary depots, returned to the Upper Study, and taught the eldest imperial son Yizhi. He received substantive appointment as vice-minister of Revenue. In 1827 he was successively promoted to left censor-in-chief and minister of Rites; the emperor relied on him and sent him five times in four years to Shanxi, Zhili, Sichuan, Hubei, and Fujian to try cases and investigate affairs. Wherever he went he upheld the law with clarity and caution, all meeting the emperor's intent. He became chief tutor of the Upper Study and was made minister of Personnel. In 1831 the eldest imperial son fell ill and died; jealous persons stirred the emperor's anger; Tang was removed as chief tutor and demoted to vice-minister of War. More than two years later he was again appointed from left censor-in-chief to minister of Works and then to Personnel. He presided in succession over the Jiangnan and Shuntian provincial examinations. In 1836 Shaanxi Governor Yang Mingyang was impeached; Tang was ordered with Vice-Minister Wen Qing to investigate and briefly acted as governor; he again went to Sichuan on an investigative mission; Mingyang again quarreled with the provincial judicial commissioner; Tang obtained evidence of false reporting and shielding subordinates and impeached him. At the capital evaluation he was given merit consideration for fairness on his missions. He again went to Zhangjiakou and Taiyuan to try cases. In 1838, as minister of Revenue he became assisting grand secretary and was still transferred to Personnel.
18
十九年，命按事安徽、江蘇、浙江。 自禁煙議起，海疆久不靖。 林則徐既罷，琦善主撫，復不得要領。 金釗素不附和議，與穆彰阿等意齟《齒吾》。 一日召對，上從容問廣東事可付諸何人，金釗以林則徐對，上不悅。 至二十一年，事且益棘，詔予則徐四品卿銜赴浙江軍營，亦未果用之。 未幾，有吏部司員陳起詩規避倉差，金釗還其呈牘禁勿遞，為所訐，坐降四級調用。 逾年，授光祿寺卿。 以衰老乞罷，住京養疴，許以二品頂戴致仕。 久之，上仍眷念，二十九年，皇太后之喪，具疏上慰，賜頭品頂戴。 咸豐四年，重宴鹿鳴，加太子太保。 六年，卒，詔以尚書例賜卹，諡文端。
In 1839 he was sent on investigative missions to Anhui, Jiangsu, and Zhejiang. From the time the opium prohibition debate arose, the maritime frontier had long been unsettled. After Lin Zexu was dismissed, Qishan led the appeasement faction yet again failed to grasp essentials. Tang had never favored the appeasement faction and was at odds with Mu Zhang'a and others. One day at audience the emperor casually asked to whom Guangdong affairs might be entrusted; Tang answered Lin Zexu, and the emperor was displeased. By 1841 affairs grew still more urgent; an edict gave Lin Zexu fourth-rank commissioner rank to go to the Zhejiang military camp, yet he was still not successfully employed. Before long Ministry of Personnel clerk Chen Qishi sought to avoid granary duty; Tang returned his petition forbidding submission, was impeached by him, and was demoted four ranks and transferred. More than a year later he was appointed minister of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. Citing age and infirmity he requested retirement, remained in Beijing to nurse his health, and was permitted to retire with second-rank insignia. After a long interval the emperor still cherished him; in 1849, at the empress dowager's funeral, he submitted a memorial of condolence and was granted first-rank insignia. In 1854 he again attended the lu ming banquet and was made senior guardian of the heir apparent. In 1856 he died; an edict granted funeral honors by ministerial precedent and the posthumous title Wenduan.
19
金釗自為翰林，布衣脫粟，後常不改。 當官廉察，負一時清望，雖被排擠，卒以恩禮終。 子修，通政司副使。
From his Hanlin days Tang wore plain cloth and ate coarse grain, and seldom changed afterward. In office he was incorrupt and discerning and bore the age's reputation for integrity; though squeezed out, he ended with imperial favor and ritual honors. His son Xiu was vice commissioner of the Office of Transmission.
20
論曰：阮元由詞臣出膺疆寄，竟殄海寇； 開府粵、滇，綏邊之績，並有足稱； 晚登宰輔，與樞臣曹振鏞異趣，惟以文學裁成後進，世推耆碩。 汪廷珍、湯金釗正色立朝，清節並著； 金釗雖以直言被擯，宣宗終鑑其忠誠，易名曰「端」，胥無愧焉。
The commentator says: Ruan Yuan, rising from literary courtier to frontier command, finally destroyed the sea pirates; governing Guangdong and Yunnan, his achievements in pacifying the borders were likewise praiseworthy; In later years he reached the chief ministership, differed in outlook from Grand Councilor Cao Zhenyong, and shaped later generations through scholarship alone; the age acclaimed him as a venerable master. Wang Tingzhen and Tang Jinzhao stood upright in court, and their integrity was jointly renowned; Though Tang was cast out for blunt speech, the Xuanzong Emperor finally recognized his loyalty, changed his posthumous name to "Duan," and none of them need feel ashamed.
