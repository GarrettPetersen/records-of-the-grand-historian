雷正綰陶茂林曹克忠胡中和何勝必蕭慶高楊復東
Lei Zhengwan, Tao Maolin, Cao Kezhong, Hu Zhonghe, He Shengbi, Xiao Qinggao, and Yang Fudong
3
周達武李輝武唐友耕
Zhou Dawu, Li Huiwu, and Tang Yougeng
4
雷正綰，字偉堂，四川中江人。 由把總從軍湖北，積功至游擊，賜號直勇巴圖魯。 咸豐八年，從多隆阿援安徽石牌、潛山、太湖、桐城，諸戰皆功最，累擢副將，以總兵記名。 十一年，敗黃文金於蔣家山、項家河、江家河、麻子嶺，一月五捷，授陝安鎮總兵。 同治元年，克廬州，以提督記名。
Lei Zhengwan, whose courtesy name was Weitang, came from Zhongjiang in Sichuan. He entered service in Hubei as a platoon commander, rose through merit to brigade commander, and was awarded the title Zhiyong Batulu. In the eighth year of the Xianfeng reign (1858), he followed Duolong'a to Anhui, fighting at Shipai, Qianshan, Taihu, and Tongcheng; in each engagement his merit ranked first, and he was repeatedly promoted to deputy commander and placed on the register for major general. In the eleventh year (1861), he defeated Huang Wenjin at Jiangjiashan, Xiangjiahe, Jiangjiahe, and Maziling, winning five victories in a single month, and was appointed major general of the Shaan'an garrison. In the first year of the Tongzhi reign (1862), he captured Luzhou and was placed on the register for provincial commander-in-chief.
5
從多隆阿援陝西，詔正綰先赴本任，未至，擢陝西提督，幫辦軍務，駐西安。 二年，多隆阿既克東路，令正綰規三原，屢破賊。 會解鳳翔圍，進援甘肅，連戰靈臺、鎮原，皆捷。 三年，破賊崇仁、新城，進逼平原。 會多隆阿卒於軍，都興阿繼督師甘肅，正綰仍奉命為副。 克平涼，斬賊首鐵酉、羽輕林，賜黃馬褂。 於是固原踞賊悉竄龍山鎮，追擊敗之。 賊回竄，又陷固原。 正綰疾趨蓮花城，欲襲其巢穴，遇伏，受矛傷，部下亡千餘人，裹創攻蓮花城，克之，詔嘉其勇。 四年春，克固原，進攻黑城子，斬賊首黑虎。 克官橋、李旺二堡，擒賊首木棍等。 乘勝薄預望城，破下馬關、半​​角城賊壘，進規靈州，分兵解安定圍。
He followed Duolong'a to Shaanxi; the court ordered Zhengwan to take up his post first, but before he arrived he was promoted to provincial commander-in-chief of Shaanxi, assigned to assist in military affairs, and stationed at Xi'an. In the second year (1863), after Duolong'a had secured the eastern front, he ordered Zhengwan to advance on Sanyuan and repeatedly routed the rebels. He then helped raise the siege of Fengxiang, advanced into Gansu, and won successive victories at Lingtai and Zhenyuan. In the third year (1864), he defeated the rebels at Chongren and Xincheng and pressed toward Pingyuan. When Duolong'a died in camp, Duxing'a took command in Gansu, and Zhengwan continued to serve as his deputy. He captured Pingliang and beheaded the rebel leaders Tie You and Yu Qinglin, and was awarded a yellow riding jacket. The rebels holding Guyuan then fled to Longshan Town, and he pursued and defeated them. The rebels doubled back and seized Guyuan again. Zhengwan rushed to Lianhuacheng to strike their stronghold, walked into an ambush, and was speared; more than a thousand of his men fell. He bound his wounds, pressed the attack, and took the town; the court praised his courage. In the spring of the fourth year (1865), he recovered Guyuan, advanced on Heichengzi, and beheaded the rebel leader Heihu. He took the forts at Guanqiao and Liwang and captured the rebel leaders Mugun and others. Riding the momentum, he pressed toward Yuwangcheng, stormed the rebel camps at Xiamaguan and Banjiaocheng, advanced on Lingzhou, and detached a force to raise the siege of Anding.
6
七月，偕曹克忠攻金積堡，軍餉不繼，為賊所圍，飢潰。 正綰自劾，褫勇號、黃馬褂，黜幫辦，歸總督楊岳斌節制。 正綰弟總兵雷恆及副將李高啟等以主將失職，煽亂，犯涇州，正綰不能制止，憤欲自裁。 詔念前功，不加罪，責令整軍勦賊圖自贖。 命巡撫趙長齡會楊岳斌按訊，正綰縛送雷恆等置之法。 當事變初起，謠諑紛淆，詔斥劉蓉張皇妄奏，許正綰專摺奏事以慰之。 所部招集增募僅三千人。
In the seventh month, he joined Cao Kezhong in an assault on Jinjibao; supplies ran out, the rebels surrounded them, and the army collapsed from hunger. Zhengwan submitted a self-accusation; his Batulu title and yellow jacket were stripped, he was removed from his assisting post, and placed under Governor-General Yang Yuebin. Zhengwan's brother, Major General Lei Heng, and Deputy Commander Li Gaoqi and others, blaming the commander's failure, incited a mutiny and marched on Jingzhou; Zhengwan could not restrain them and in despair sought to kill himself. The court, mindful of his past service, imposed no punishment and ordered him to reorganize his troops, suppress the rebels, and redeem himself. The court ordered Governor Zhao Changling to investigate jointly with Yang Yuebin; Zhengwan bound Lei Heng and the others and handed them over for punishment. When the crisis first erupted, rumors flew in every direction; the court rebuked Liu Rong for alarmist false reports and allowed Zhengwan to file memorials directly, by way of reassurance. Through recruitment and re-enlistment, his command numbered only three thousand men.
7
五年，蘭州兵變，回匪窺伺，正綰支拄於平涼、固原之間，破賊於橫河川，克平涼，复黃馬褂、勇號。 六年，左宗​​棠入陝督師，正綰率軍助剿，援慶陽。 七年，兩破賊於長武，克黃家堡。 八年，會攻董志原，克之，晉號達春巴圖魯。 又破白彥虎於李旺堡。 會攻金積堡，當西路，屢克要隘，合圍。 及馬化隆伏誅，被優敘。
In the fifth year (1866), when the Lanzhou garrison mutinied and Muslim rebels pressed their advantage, Zhengwan held the line between Pingliang and Guyuan, routed the rebels at Henghechuan, retook Pingliang, and recovered his yellow jacket and Batulu title. In the sixth year (1867), Zuo Zongtang took command in Shaanxi; Zhengwan led his army to assist and relieved Qingyang. In the seventh year (1868), he twice defeated the rebels at Changwu and captured Huangjiabao. In the eighth year (1869), he joined the assault on Dongzhiyuan, took it, and was promoted to the title Dachun Batulu. He again defeated Bai Yanhu at Liwangbao. He joined the siege of Jinjibao on the western front, repeatedly took key passes, and helped complete the encirclement. After Ma Hualong was executed, he received special commendation for his service.
8
光緒十年，法越兵事起，命率甘軍駐鳳凰城，固邊防，事定回任。 兩遇萬壽慶典，加太子少保、尚書銜。 二十一年，循化撒回倡亂，督剿無功，革職留任。 二十三年，罷，卒於家，仍以前勞賜卹。
In the tenth year of the Guangxu reign (1884), when war broke out between France and Vietnam, he was ordered to lead Gansu troops to Fenghuangcheng to hold the frontier; when the crisis passed he returned to his post. On two imperial birthday celebrations he was granted the additional titles Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent and Minister. In the twenty-first year (1895), when the Salar Muslims of Xunhua rebelled, he directed suppression without success and was stripped of rank but kept at his post. In the twenty-third year (1897) he was dismissed, died at home, and posthumous honors were granted in recognition of his earlier service.
9
陶茂林，湖南長沙人。 以武童入湘軍，轉戰湖北、江西，積功至游擊。 咸豐八年，胡林翼調為楚軍營官，扼黃州，破賊霍山、舒城，克建德，擢參將。 十年，從多隆阿破賊於桐城掛車河，擢副將。 十一年，破賊施家山，擒其渠，及克安慶，賜號鍾勇巴圖魯。 同治元年，克廬州，先登，以總兵記名。
Tao Maolin came from Changsha in Hunan. He joined the Xiang Army as a military cadet, campaigned in Hubei and Jiangxi, and rose through merit to brigade commander. In the eighth year of the Xianfeng reign (1858), Hu Lin'yi assigned him as a Chu Army camp officer to hold Huangzhou; he routed rebels at Huoshan and Shucheng, took Jiande, and was promoted to colonel. In the tenth year (1860), he followed Duolong'a and defeated rebels at Guachehe near Tongcheng, and was promoted to deputy commander. In the eleventh year (1861), he defeated rebels at Shijiashan and captured their leader; when Anqing fell he was awarded the title Zhongyong Batulu. In the first year of the Tongzhi reign (1862), he captured Luzhou, was first over the wall, and was placed on the register for major general.
10
遂從多隆阿西征，破賊於武關。 從剿回匪，解同州圍。 克羌白鎮、王閣村賊巢，功皆最，授漢中鎮總兵。 鳳翔被圍久，茂林率三千人往援，連戰解圍，擢甘肅提督。 粵匪出寶雞山口，擾郿縣、盩厔，茂林要擊雨門鎮、二嶺關，迭敗之。 進克汧陽、隴州。 遂會雷正綰分道規平涼，陣斬賊首木仲沅訥三等，克之，賜黃馬褂。 進拔張家川賊巢，破龍山鎮、蓮花城援賊，解安定圍。 克金縣，破賊惠城，擒其渠黑牙古。 四年，克黑城賊巢，解靖遠圍。 進攻會寧，所部索餉譁潰五營，賊乘之，六營皆陷。 茂林調後路四營來援，突圍出，退駐安定。 巡撫劉蓉疏陳甘軍積弊，論茂林不職，茂林亦以兵潰自劾。 詔斥廢法營私，以致兵潰而叛，遂罷職，歸。
He then followed Duolong'a west and defeated the rebels at Wuguan. He joined the campaign against the Muslim rebels and raised the siege of Tongzhou. He took the rebel strongholds at Qiangbaizhen and Wanggecun, ranking first in merit each time, and was appointed major general of the Hanzhong garrison. Fengxiang had been under siege for a long time; Maolin led three thousand men to its relief, fought his way through in successive battles, and was promoted to provincial commander-in-chief of Gansu. Taiping forces came out through Baoji Pass and raided Mei County and Zhouzhi; Maolin intercepted them at Yumen Town and Erying Pass and defeated them repeatedly. He advanced and captured Qianyang and Longzhou. He then joined Lei Zhengwan in a pincer on Pingliang, killed the rebel leaders Muzhongyuenne and others in battle, took the city, and was awarded a yellow riding jacket. He advanced on the rebel stronghold at Zhangjiachuan, routed relief forces from Longshan Town and Lianhuacheng, and raised the siege of Anding. He took Jinxian, defeated the rebels at Huicheng, and captured their leader Heiyagu. In the fourth year (1865), he took the rebel stronghold at Heicheng and raised the siege of Jingyuan. As he advanced on Huining, five of his battalions mutinied over unpaid wages; the rebels pressed the advantage and all six battalions were overrun. Maolin brought up four rear battalions, fought his way out, and fell back to Anding. Governor Liu Rong reported on the chronic abuses of the Gansu army and judged Maolin unfit for command; Maolin submitted a self-accusation over the rout. The court rebuked him for corruption and lax discipline that had led to mutiny and rout; he was dismissed and sent home.
11
十年，貴州巡撫曾璧光調茂林赴黔協剿。 復新城，克安順賊巢，平古州、丹江苗，復原官。 光緒二年，收復下江、永從各城，破六峒賊巢，加頭品頂戴，晉號愛星阿巴圖魯。 十六年，署古州鎮總兵，卒於官。
In the tenth year (1871), Guizhou Governor Zeng Biguang summoned Maolin to Guizhou to assist in suppression. He recovered Xincheng, took the rebel stronghold at Anshun, pacified the Miao of Guzhou and Danjiang, and was restored to his former rank. In the second year of the Guangxu reign (1876), he recovered the cities of Xiajiang and Yongcong, destroyed the rebel stronghold at Liudong, was granted the first-rank hat ornament, and was promoted to the title Aixing'a Batulu. In the sixteenth year (1890) he served as acting major general of the Guzhou garrison and died in office.
12
曹克忠，直隸天津人。 初投效湘軍，嗣從多隆阿，積功至都司。 咸豐十年，令募五百人為忠字營，大破援賊於潛山、太湖，洊擢參將，賜號悍勇巴圖魯。 掛車河之捷，擢副將。 克桐城、宿松諸城，以總兵記名。 同治元年，克廬州。
Cao Kezhong came from Tianjin in Zhili. He first joined the Xiang Army, then served under Duolong'a, and rose through merit to battalion commander. In the tenth year of the Xianfeng reign (1860), he was ordered to raise five hundred men as the Zhongzi Battalion, routed relief forces at Qianshan and Taihu, was repeatedly promoted to colonel, and was awarded the title Hanyong Batulu. For the victory at Guachehe he was promoted to deputy commander. He captured Tongcheng, Susong, and other cities and was placed on the register for major general. In the first year of the Tongzhi reign (1862), he captured Luzhou.
13
後從多隆阿西征，武關、同州諸戰皆從。 二年，攻羌白鎮，克忠單騎往諭賊，賊請降，察其詐，潛師會攻，下之，乘勝奪王閣村，予一品封典。 尋率烏拉馬隊及楚勇七營屯長安、鄠縣之間。 光泰廟為入省要衝，賊踞之以扼糧路，克忠擊走之。 分隊清西路餘匪，省城始安。 以提督記名，授河州鎮總兵。 渡渭連破賊於白起營、馬家埠、白吉原，邠州平，陝回西趨。 三年，平麟游諸匪。 會援甘肅，連破賊於西河口、黑水峪，赴河州本任。 克秦安，解秦州圍，賜黃馬褂。
He later followed Duolong'a west and fought in every engagement at Wuguan, Tongzhou, and beyond. In the second year (1863), assaulting Qiangbaizhen, Kezhong rode out alone to parley with the rebels; they offered surrender, but he saw through the ruse, launched a coordinated surprise attack, took the town, and pressed on to seize Wanggecun; he was granted first-rank enfeoffment privileges. He soon led the Wula cavalry and seven Chu battalions to camp between Chang'an and Huxian. Guangtai Temple guarded the main route into the province; rebels held it to cut the grain supply, and Kezhong drove them off. He detached columns to clear remaining rebels on the western front, and the provincial capital was finally secure. He was placed on the register for provincial commander-in-chief and appointed major general of the Hezhou garrison. Crossing the Wei, he routed rebels at Baiqiying, Majiabu, and Baijiyuan in succession; Binzhou was pacified and the Shaanxi Muslim rebels fled west. In the third year (1864), he pacified the rebels around Linyou. He joined the relief of Gansu, won successive victories at Xihekou and Heishuiyu, and took up his post at Hezhou. He took Qin'an, raised the siege of Qinzhou, and was awarded a yellow riding jacket.
14
四年，攻蕭何城及馬定嘴，將台、隆德諸堡，悉平。 克海城，回匪並竄李旺堡、同心城，攻下之。 偕雷正綰規取金積堡，屯強家沙窩，數有斬獲。 輕進，為賊所包鈔，正綰軍先潰，克忠亦退。 因前功免罪，授甘肅提督。 時陶茂林、雷正綰軍相繼譁變，回氛益熾，自楊岳斌楚軍外，僅克忠一軍與之相持。 克忠援鞏昌，賊敗走，又毀董家堡賊巢。 五年，援洮州，次李岐山，回目馬芳乞降，誅其酋丁重選等而還。
In the fourth year (1865), he attacked Xiaohecheng, Madingzui, and the forts at Jiangtai, Longde, and elsewhere, pacifying them all. He took Haicheng; the Muslim rebels fled together to Liwangbao and Tongxincheng, and he stormed and captured both. With Lei Zhengwan he advanced on Jinjibao, encamped at Qiangjiashawo, and won repeated victories. He advanced too boldly, was surrounded by the rebels, Zhengwan's force broke first, and Kezhong fell back as well. On account of his earlier service he was spared punishment and appointed provincial commander-in-chief of Gansu. Tao Maolin's and Lei Zhengwan's armies mutinied in turn, Muslim rebel power surged, and apart from Yang Yuebin's Chu forces only Kezhong's army still held the line. Kezhong relieved Gongchang, routed the rebels, and destroyed the rebel stronghold at Dongjiabao. In the fifth year (1866), he relieved Taozhou and halted at Liqishan; the Muslim leader Ma Fang sued for peace, and Kezhong executed the chiefs Ding Zhongxuan and others before withdrawing.
15
蘭州標兵變，楊岳斌令克忠移軍鎮懾。 克忠至，人心稍定，然糧餉俱竭，乞病回籍。 十年，詔起赴陝接統淮軍，專防肅州。 十一年，所部有結會匪者，甘軍馬世俊騎兵亦變，降捻多叛應，克忠遣兵平之。 复乞病解軍事。 十一年，署甘肅提督，尋解職歸。
When the Lanzhou garrison mutinied, Yang Yuebin ordered Kezhong to march in and restore order. Kezhong's arrival steadied morale, but supplies and pay were exhausted; he requested sick leave and returned home. In the tenth year (1871), the court recalled him to Shaanxi to take over the Huai Army and hold Suzhou. In the eleventh year (1872), men in his command joined secret-society bandits; Ma Shijun's Gansu cavalry mutinied as well, and many surrendered Nian rebels rose in sympathy; Kezhong sent troops to put them down. He again requested sick leave and was relieved of military command. That same year he served as acting provincial commander-in-chief of Gansu, then resigned and went home.
16
光緒九年，命募六營防山海關。 十年，授廣東水師提督。 十一年，病罷，食全俸。 二十年，命治天津團練，統津勝軍。 二十二年，卒，賜卹。
In the ninth year of the Guangxu reign (1883), he was ordered to raise six battalions to defend Shanhaiguan. In the tenth year (1884), he was appointed naval commander-in-chief of Guangdong. In the eleventh year (1885) he was dismissed for illness and retained his full stipend. In the twentieth year (1894), he was ordered to organize Tianjin militia and command the Jinsheng Army. In the twenty-second year (1896) he died, and posthumous honors were granted.
17
胡中和，字元廷，湖南湘鄉人。 咸豐初，從湘軍剿粵匪，積功擢把總。 六年，從蕭啟江援江西，复袁州，超擢都司，賜花翎。 七年，從克臨安，中砲傷，以游擊留湖南補用。 八年，破賊上屯渡，乘勝复撫州，擢參將。 九年，复南安，擢副將。 石達開由寶慶竄廣西，陷興安，遣黨攻桂林，自率悍賊屯大溶江。 中和從蕭啟江往援，大破賊於大溶江，賊竄貴州境，加總兵銜，賜號伊德克勒巴圖魯。 十年，蕭啟江率軍援四川，中和從之。 啟江卒於軍，中和偕何勝必、蕭慶高等分領其眾。
Hu Zhonghe, whose courtesy name was Yuanting, came from Xiangxiang in Hunan. Early in the Xianfeng reign, he served with the Xiang Army against the Taiping rebels and rose through merit to platoon commander. In the sixth year (1856), he followed Xiao Qijiang to Jiangxi, recovered Yuanzhou, was promoted ahead of schedule to battalion commander, and was awarded the peacock feather. In the seventh year (1857), he took part in capturing Lin'an, was wounded by artillery, and was kept on in Hunan at the rank of brigade commander pending appointment. In the eighth year (1858), he routed the rebels at Shangtundu, pressed the advantage to retake Fuzhou, and was promoted to regimental commander. In the ninth year (1859), he retook Nan'an and was promoted to deputy commander. Shi Dakai broke out from Baqing into Guangxi, captured Xing'an, sent detachments against Guilin, and personally led elite rebel troops to camp on the Darong River. Zhonghe marched with Xiao Qijiang to the relief, crushed the rebels on the Darong River, and drove them into Guizhou; he received major-general rank and the title Yidekele Batulu. In the tenth year (1860), Xiao Qijiang marched into Sichuan with his forces, and Zhonghe went with him. When Qijiang died on campaign, Zhonghe, He Shengbi, Xiao Qinggao, and others jointly took over his command.
18
剿滇匪李永和於井研，連戰皆捷，賊解圍遁，以總兵記名。 尋授四川建昌鎮總兵。 十一年，永和竄踞富順牛腹渡，兩岸築堅壘，背水而陣。 中和選銳卒沿河設伏，自率羸師誘之，賊大出，伏發，截其歸路，俘斬無算，賊壘盡夷，進解大邑之圍，予二品封典。
He campaigned against the Yunnan rebel Li Yonghe at Jingyan, won a string of victories, and forced the rebels to abandon their siege and flee; he was placed on the register for major general. He was soon appointed major general of the Jianchang garrison in Sichuan. In the eleventh year (1861), Yonghe seized Niufudu in Fushun, threw up fortified camps on both riverbanks, and drew up his lines with the water at his back. Zhonghe posted elite troops in ambush along the river while leading a decoy force of weaker men; when the rebels poured out, the ambush struck and sealed their retreat, inflicting countless casualties and leveling their camps; he then relieved the siege of Dayi and was granted a second-rank hereditary privilege.
19

When Luo Bingzhang took command in Sichuan, he ordered Zhonghe to march with the relief column to Mianzhou. Of the Yunnan rebels, Lan Chaozhu was the craftiest and hardest fighter, and had kept Mianzhou under siege for a long time. After the relief force arrived, it beat them repeatedly until the siege was raised, then routed them again at Xishan Pass. Chaozhu withdrew to Danling and united with Li Yonghe to assault Meizhou. Zhonghe hurried to the rescue; the rebels struck from several directions; he broke through the formation, took a spear wound to the cheek that drenched his coat in blood, ignored it, and fought on until the enemy was shattered and Meizhou was saved. He pressed the attack on Danling; Chaozhu fled; the city was retaken; and he was placed on the register for provincial commander-in-chief. In the first year of the Tongzhi reign (1862), he was promoted to provincial commander-in-chief of Yunnan. After his defeat at Meizhou, Li Yonghe seized Qing Shen; government forces closed in, defeated him repeatedly, and drove him to Longchang in Qianwei, where he held out in rough country and resisted to the last. Zhonghe invested the position with stone walls, wooden palisades, outer trenches, and dense fields of caltrop stakes. Seeing no escape, the rebels stormed Xiao Qinggao's camp; Zhonghe cut them off and drove them back; the fighting lasted seven days. When the rebels refused to show themselves, he sent the defector Tan Renqu with a surrender offer and a rendezvous at Zhushipo, while hiding troops beside their stronghold. Yonghe appeared with Mao Dexing and a few dozen horsemen; the ambush sprang up on every side and burned out their lair. Yonghe and Dexing fled in terror, were run down and captured, and five thousand of their men surrendered. The court commended Zhonghe for his strategy, decisive victory, and capture of the rebel chief alive, and awarded him the yellow riding jacket.

When Shi Dakai raided the Sichuan frontier, Zhonghe, Xiao Qinggao, and He Shengbi combined at Hengjiang and drove him away. In the spring of the second year (1863), Dakai again split his army to invade Sichuan; he personally led tens of thousands across the Jinsha at Miliangba. Zhonghe blocked Huaping and Luding Bridge, broke the rebel column, and chased them into the Gongbu chieftain's hills; Dakai was captured soon after. He was transferred to provincial commander-in-chief of Sichuan. In the third year (1864), he defeated Yunnan rebels in the Xuyong district. After Li Yonghe's execution, his surviving followers had fled into Shaanxi; they now crossed into Gansu and seized Jiezhou. In the fourth year (1865), Zhonghe and Major General Zhou Dawu campaigned against them, stormed the camps at Longwang Temple and Sanguan Hall, pushed to the walls of Jiezhou, mined the fortifications, took the city, executed the rebel chief Cai Changling, and wiped out his followers. With Jiezhou pacified, he received imperial gifts.

He Shengbi came from Xiangxiang in Hunan. During the Xianfeng era, Shengbi enlisted in the Xiang Army, campaigned with Xiao Qijiang in Jiangxi and Guangxi, and rose through merit to deputy commander. He entered Sichuan with the army, commanded the right wing of the Xiang Guo Corps, beat Li Yonghe at Jingyan and again at Zizhou, killed the rebel chief Wang Erguan in the field, and received the title Yuyong Batulu. In the eleventh year (1861), he helped rout Lan Chaozhu at Xishan Pass, beat him again at Qingyi Dam, saved Meizhou, pursued to Qing Shen, and killed or captured a great number before being appointed major general of the Suzhou garrison in Gansu. In the first year of Tongzhi (1862), he helped take Qing Shen, chased the rebels to Yibin, and captured the rebel officer Zhou Tingguang. With Hu Zhonghe he trapped and captured Li Yonghe at Longchang in Qianwei; in the second year (1863) he marched with Xiao Qinggao to Hanzhong, was beaten at Youfang Street, and after Hanzhong and Chenggu fell in turn was dismissed from office but kept on campaign. In the third year (1864), he helped storm the Faci Yuan camp, routed the enemy again at Moujiaba, pressed to the walls of Hanzhong, drove off the Nian leader Chen Deicai, retook the city, and had his rank restored. He defeated Chen Deicai again at Shangyuan Pass, retook Chenggu, and moved to besiege Jiezhou. In the fourth year (1865), he died on campaign; posthumous honors were granted, and he was given the posthumous title Weike.

Xiao Qinggao came from Xiangxiang in Hunan. He served in the Chu Army and rose through merit to deputy commander. When Xiao Qijiang marched into Sichuan, he was assigned to the campaign; for the victory at Jingyan he received the title Guoyong Batulu. He defeated Li Yonghe at Zizhou and was placed on the register for major general. He took part in the campaign against Lan Chaozhu and raised the siege of Mianzhou. In the second year of Tongzhi (1863), he marched with He Shengbi to relieve Hanzhong. At Youfang Street, Shengbi was beaten first and Qinggao could not reach him in time; both men lost their ranks but remained on campaign. In the third year (1864), Hanzhong was retaken and both had their ranks restored. Pursuing the enemy to Chenggu, they went over the walls on ladders and sent the rebels fleeing. In the fourth year (1865), besieging Yang County, he slipped daredevils inside as agents and took the city. The rebel chief Cao Canzhang fell back to Bali Ping; Qinggao crushed him in a pincer attack, took Canzhang alive, and was appointed major general of the Hanzhong garrison. In the fifth year (1866), he died and was given the posthumous title Wuyi.

In the eleventh year (1861), he fought at Niufudu in Fushun, helped relieve Dayi, and was promoted to garrison commander. He defeated Lan Chaozhu at Mianzhou and was promoted to battalion commander. He beat Chaozhu again at Chongqing, destroyed Shiyangchang, burned the rebel stronghold, and was promoted to brigade commander. In the first year of Tongzhi (1862), he retook Danling and was promoted to regimental commander. He took Qing Shen, stormed the Tieshan rebel camp, and helped capture Li Yonghe. He was promoted to deputy commander. In the fifth year (1866), Governor-General Luo Bingzhang reported Fudong's long record against Yunnan and Guizhou rebels and had him placed on the register for major general. In the seventh year (1868), he was appointed major general of the Chuanbei garrison in Sichuan. In the second year of the Guangxu reign (1876), he was transferred to the Kaifa garrison in Yunnan. Zhou Dawu, whose courtesy name was Mengxiong, came from Ningxiang in Hunan. In the fourth year of Xianfeng (1854), he enlisted in Li Xubin's command, helped take Yuezhou and Wuchang, and rose through merit to garrison commander. After fighting at Hukou, he was promoted to battalion commander. In every fight Dawu led the charge, brandishing his standard to break the enemy line; Xubin singled him out to command a hundred-man unit called the Xinzi Battalion, and he often served as the army's spearhead. In the eighth year (1858), he helped take Huang'an, was promoted to brigade commander, and received the peacock feather. In the assault on Shucheng with Xubin, Dawu led the scaling party; a musket ball tore his left ear; after the city fell he was left to hold it. Soon afterward Xubin's force was wiped out at Sanhe, the Shucheng garrison collapsed as well, and Dawu went home to Hunan to recover from his wounds. In the ninth year (1859), when Shi Dakai besieged Baqing, Governor Luo Bingzhang had Dawu raise five hundred men as the Zhangwu Army; marching with Prefect Liu Yuezhao to the relief, he held the east gate and repeatedly beat back rebel assaults. After the siege was raised, he was promoted to regimental commander. In the tenth year (1860), he campaigned in Guangxi, took the rebel camps at Pinggucheng and Liantang in Fuchuan, retook Hexian, was promoted to deputy commander, and received major-general rank. When Shi Dakai detached forces against Yongming and Zhepai, Dawu defeated them in a series of engagements and was promoted to major general. In the eleventh year (1861), he helped take Huitong; the rebels fled into Hubei and seized Laifeng. In the spring of the first Tongzhi year (1862), he followed Liu Yuezhao in retaking Laifeng and was granted a second-rank hereditary privilege.

When Luo Bingzhang took command in Sichuan, Dawu was ordered into the pacification campaign. At Fuzhou he intercepted the rebel chief Zhou Shaoyong as he fled from Daning toward Shaanxi, blocked him at Zhaizikou between two mountain ranges, and sent Li Huiwu over the heights to strike; the rebels scattered; he ran them down to Anji Field in Dazhu, captured Shaoyong and Wu Chongli, sent them in a cage to Chengdu for execution, and received the title Zhiyong Batulu. Shaoyong and Daodao had been the worst rebels in Sichuan; with both gone he was appointed major general of the Jianchang garrison and given provincial commander-in-chief rank. In the ninth year (1870), an imperial order sent him to take up the Guizhou command; he marched with six thousand men of his division and cleared Miao stockades all along the route. Until then Guizhou had depended on outside armies for pacification; beyond a hundred li from the capital those troops could no longer be kept in hand. Dawu and the governor agreed to raise strength to thirty thousand, split forces between field and garrison duty, advanced from Longli toward Kaidu, severed the rebel positions above and below the line, retook Duyun, and detached columns to break the enemy at Yongning and Weining. In the eleventh year (1872) he and Xi's troops routed the followers of the Miao chief Zhang Choumi at Chaniu Slope, took a great toll in heads, and accepted the surrender of tens of thousands. The pursuit reached Lengshuigou, where the rebel chief was taken alive; Li Gaojiao and Li Wencai fled to Jingpengkan, and a three-pronged pursuit wiped them out. He next broke the Miao hosts at Xianglu Hill near Qingping, and Baotian captured Zhang Choumi. With the Miao frontier pacified, he received a hereditary rank as Cavalry Commandant.

His younger brother Kang Lu campaigned with Dawu in Guangxi and Hunan and rose through repeated recommendations to magistrate. In the first Tongzhi year (1862), he followed Dawu into Sichuan, helped defeat Zhou Shaoyong, and was promoted to prefect. In the fourth year (1865), he took part in the capture of Jiezhou and was raised to prefectural magistrate. He followed Dawu to Guizhou and took charge of camp administration. In the eleventh year (1872), with the lower river country pacified, he was promoted to circuit intendant. He stationed troops at Pu'an New City and brought refugees back under government protection. In the twelfth year (1873), secret-society rebels stirred up trouble; Kang Lu led a hundred of his household troops against them, was overwhelmed, and fell in battle. The court posthumously made him an Inner Court Academician, granted a hereditary Cavalry Commandant rank, and gave him the posthumous name Zhuangjie (Stalwart Integrity).
31
李輝武，湖南衡山人。 周達武部將。 咸豐中，從剿粵匪，洊擢游擊。 十一年，從入四川，剿涪川鶴遊坪踞賊，擒賊酋周紹勇、郭刀刀。 輝武功為多，擢副將，賜號武勇巴圖魯。 同治三年，從援階州，輝武由伍家坪進軍，扼州城外北山條竹埡。 四年，攻破橋頭里賊壘，又破賊於孟家莊，殲城外賊殆盡。 穴地破城，輝武先登，擒賊目蔡四。 巡部，以總兵記名。 從討松潘叛番，拔其巢。 尋攻黑河番，焚芝麻第五寨，餘寨皆降。 乘勝連破大松樹及竹自三寨，以提督記名。
Li Huiwu came from Hengshan in Hunan. He served as a brigade commander under Zhou Dawu. In the Xianfeng era he campaigned against the Taiping forces in Guangdong and rose by repeated merit to battalion commander. In the eleventh year (1861), he entered Sichuan, cleared the rebels entrenched at Heyouping in Fuchuan, and captured the chiefs Zhou Shaoyong and Guo Daodao. Huiwu's deeds ranked foremost; he was made deputy commander and given the title Wuyong Batulu. In the third Tongzhi year (1864), marching to relieve Jiezhou, Huiwu advanced from Wujiaping and seized Tiaozhuya on the north hill outside the city walls. In the fourth year (1865), he stormed the camp at Qiaotouli, routed the enemy at Mengjiazhuang, and all but wiped out the rebels outside the walls. When mining brought down the wall, Huiwu was first over the breach and seized the rebel officer Cai Si. After inspecting the ranks, he was placed on the register for major general. He joined the campaign against the Tibetan rebels in Songpan and destroyed their stronghold. He next struck the Heihe tribes, burned the fifth stockade at Zhima, and the rest submitted. Pressing the advantage, he took Dasongshu and the three Zhuzi stockades in turn and was placed on the register for provincial commander-in-chief.
32
六年，捻匪竄陝西，輝武率步隊五營赴援，剿破汧陽、隴州、寶雞諸賊，西路肅清。 八年，剿董志原竄匪，斃賊目王明章，晉號福凌阿巴圖魯，授漢中鎮總兵。 九年，偕提督劉端冕分擊北山回匪，破翟三、禹得彥於縣頭鎮、陳村。 十一年，擢甘肅提督，仍留防漢中。 光緒四年，卒，賜卹。
In the sixth year (1867), when Nian rebels crossed into Shaanxi, Huiwu marched five infantry battalions to their relief, broke the enemy at Qianyang, Longzhou, Baoji, and neighboring districts, and cleared the western front. In the eighth year (1869), he hunted Nian bands at Dongzhiyuan, killed the rebel officer Wang Mingzhang, received an advancement of his title to Fuling'a Batulu, and was appointed major general of the Hanzhong garrison. In the ninth year (1870), he and Provincial Commander Liu Duanmian split their forces against the Muslim rebels of the northern hills and routed Zhai San and Yu Deyan at Xiantou Town and Chencun. In the eleventh year (1872), he was promoted to provincial commander-in-chief of Gansu but stayed on to guard Hanzhong. In the fourth year of Guangxu (1878), he died, and the court granted mourning favors.
33
輝武在漢中久，軍民相安。 疏濬府城東河道達漢川，旁引溝渠以資灌溉，民食其利； 又修復褎斜棧道，商旅便焉。 沒後，士民籥請建祠，從之。
Huiwu's long tenure at Hanzhong kept the army and the people at ease with one another. He dredged the eastern channel below the prefectural seat so it reached the Han River, cut feeder canals for irrigation, and the people reaped the benefit; he also restored the Baoxie plank road, which eased travel for merchants and couriers alike. After his death the local gentry and populace petitioned for a shrine, and the court assented.
34
唐友耕，雲南大關廳人。 咸豐中，滇匪起，陷賊，至四川敘州，自拔來歸。 從戰有功，授千總，署通江營守備。 賊擾​​鹽井，屢從戰擊走之，擢守備。 十年，戰峨眉索橋，受傷，破賊雙福場，進平天全茅山賊壘，擢都司，賜號額勒莫克依巴圖魯。 十一年，援潼川，破賊解圍，擢副將。 駱秉章督師至蜀，檄友耕會諸軍援綿州，令自石橋鋪進攻，友耕觀望不前，被劾，褫職留營。 既而會援眉州，友耕軍先至，戰比有功，圍解，復原官。 戰青神，陣斬賊目張興，身被二傷，裹創力戰，賊大敗。
Tang Yougeng came from Daguan in Yunnan. In the Xianfeng era, when Yunnan rebels rose, he was captured by them; reaching Xuzhou in Sichuan, he broke free and came over to the Qing side. Merit in subsequent fighting won him appointment as company commander and acting commandant of the Tongjiang garrison. When rebels raided the Salt Wells, he fought them off again and again and was promoted to garrison commandant. In the tenth year (1860), he fought at Suoqiao near Emei, was wounded, routed the enemy at Shuangfu Field, advanced to clear the Maoshan camp in Tianquan, was made battalion commander, and received the title Elemokei Batulu. In the eleventh year (1861), he marched to relieve Tongchuan, broke the siege, and was promoted to deputy commander. When Luo Bingzhang took command in Sichuan, he summoned Yougeng to join the relief of Mianzhou and attack from Shiqiaopu; Yougeng hung back, was impeached, stripped of rank but kept with the army. He then took part in relieving Meizhou; Yougeng's troops arrived first, fought well, lifted the siege, and his former rank was restored. At Qingshen he killed the rebel officer Zhang Xing in the field, took two wounds, bound them and kept fighting, and routed the enemy.
35
同治元年，破石達開黨賴裕新於工卩州。 三月，達開圍涪州，友耕馳援，解其圍，授四川重慶鎮總兵。 會諸軍复長寧，賊引去。 是年冬，達開屯敘州雙龍場，分黨屯橫江，友耕攻破江岸賊壘。 二年春，賊由橫江竄新灘溪，與屏山隔一水，友耕慮賊乘間偷渡，乃濟江設伏，誘賊深入，敗之。 六月，達開謀渡金沙江，官軍扼之不得進，改趨天全土司地，友耕擊沉賊筏； 達開奔老鴉漩，復為土兵所遏，遂就擒。 友耕擢雲南提督，留屯川南。 四年，丁母憂，詔改署提督，友耕請終制，許之。 七年服闋，署四川總督崇實奏緩陛見，令募勇防川北。 八年，調赴雲南，招降回寇李本忠等，賜黃馬褂。 光緒六年，署四川提督，八年，卒。
In the first Tongzhi year (1862), he defeated Lai Yuxin, a lieutenant of Shi Dakai, at Gong County. In the third month, when Dakai besieged Fuzhou, Yougeng raced to the relief, broke the siege, and was appointed major general of the Chongqing garrison. He then joined the allied forces in retaking Changning, and the rebels withdrew. That winter Dakai held Shuanglong Field in Xuzhou and detached a column at Hengjiang; Yougeng stormed the riverbank camps. In the spring of the second year (1863), the rebels slipped from Hengjiang to Xintan Creek, a river away from Pingshan; fearing a night crossing, Yougeng forded the river, laid an ambush, drew them in, and routed them. In the sixth month, when Dakai tried to cross the Jinsha, government troops blocked him; he turned toward the Tianquan tusi lands, and Yougeng sank his rafts; Dakai fled to Laoyaxuan, was stopped by local militia, and was taken. Yougeng was promoted to provincial commander-in-chief of Yunnan but remained on garrison duty in southern Sichuan. In the fourth year (1865), upon his mother's death, the court named him acting provincial commander-in-chief; Yougeng asked to observe full mourning, and the request was granted. When mourning ended in the seventh year (1868), Acting Sichuan Governor Chong Shi asked the court to defer his capital audience and set him to raising troops for the northern frontier. In the eighth year (1869), he was transferred to Yunnan, accepted the surrender of the Muslim rebel Li Benzhong and others, and received the yellow riding jacket. In the sixth year of Guangxu (1880), he served as acting provincial commander-in-chief of Sichuan; in the eighth year (1882), he died.
36
論曰：雷正綰、陶茂林、曹克忠皆多隆阿部下戰將。 多隆阿歿後，甘肅軍事實倚三人，以餉匱兵變，遂難成功。 克忠較有謀略，其軍獨全，終以病引退，後猶稱為宿將。 胡中和、周達武等皆以楚軍平蜀寇。 唐友耕以蜀軍頡頏其間，並躋專閫。 達武晚任貴州軍事，與席寶田同定苗疆，建樹較閎達焉。
Commentary: Lei Zhengwan, Tao Maolin, and Cao Kezhong were all field commanders who had served under Duolong'a. After Duolong'a died, Gansu strategy rested on the three of them, yet empty coffers and mutiny made victory elusive. Kezhong was the more resourceful; his command alone came through intact; he eventually retired on account of illness, yet men still spoke of him as a veteran. Hu Zhonghe, Zhou Dawu, and their fellows pacified Sichuan with Hunan armies. Tang Yougeng, leading Sichuan troops, held his own in that company and likewise rose to independent provincial command. Dawu's later career in Guizhou, where he and Xi Baotian together settled the Miao frontier, stands as the broadest achievement among them.
