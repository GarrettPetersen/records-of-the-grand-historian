1
自西人輪船之制興，有兵輪，有商輪。 其始僅往來東西洋各國口岸而已。 中國自開埠通商而後，與英吉利訂江寧條約，而外輪得行駛海上矣。 續與訂天津條約，而外輪得行駛長江矣。 商旅樂其利便，趨之若鶩。 於時內江外海之利盡為所佔。
Once the Western steamship system took hold, there came to be both naval vessels and merchant vessels. At first they sailed only between the treaty ports of Eastern and Western nations. After China opened its ports to trade, the Treaty of Nanjing with Britain allowed foreign steamers to operate on the coast. The Treaty of Tianjin then extended the same privilege to the Yangtze River. Merchants and travelers welcomed the convenience and flocked to them in droves. Inland waterways and coastal trade were soon dominated entirely by foreign lines.
2
同治十一年，直隸總督李鴻章建議設輪船招商局，論者謂妨河船生計。 鴻章謂當咸豐間河船三千餘艘，今僅存四百艘。 及今不圖，將利權盡失。 請破群議力行之。 十三年，鴻章又疏言：「同治間曾國籓、丁日昌在江蘇督、撫任，迭據道員許道身、同知容閎創議華商造船章程，分運漕米，兼攬客貨。 曾經寄請總理衙門核准，飭由江海關道曉諭各口商人試辦。 日久因循，未有成局。 同治七年，僅借用夾板船運米一次，旋又中止。 本年夏間，臣於驗收海運之暇，遵照總理衙門函示，商令浙局總辦海運委員知府硃其昂酌擬輪船章程。 嗣以現在官造輪船內並無商船可領，各省在滬殷商，或自置輪船行駛各埠，或挾資本依附西商之籍。 若中國自立招商局，則各商所有輪船股本必漸歸官局，似足順商情而強國體。 擬請先行試辦招商，為官商浹洽地步。 俟商船造成，即可隨時添補，推廣通行。 又海運米石，本屆江浙沙寧船不敷，應請以商船分運，以補沙寧之不足。 將來米數愈增，可無缺船之患。 請照戶部核准練餉制錢借給蘇、浙典章，准商等借領二十萬緡，以作設局商本，仍預繳息錢助賑。 所有盈虧，全歸商認，與官無涉。 當令硃其昂回滬設局招商。 商人爭先入股，現已購集堅捷輪船三艘。 經臣咨商浙江督撫臣飭撥明年漕米二十萬石，由招商輪船運津，其水腳耗米等項，悉照沙寧船定章。 至攬載貨物，報關納稅，仍照新關章程，以免藉口。 若從此輪船暢行，庶使我內江外海之利不致為洋人佔盡，其關於國計民生者實非淺鮮。 」疏入，報可。
In 1872 Li Hongzhang, governor-general of Zhili, proposed founding the China Merchants' Steam Navigation Company, but opponents warned it would ruin the native junk trade. Hongzhang replied that the Yangtze junk fleet had already shrunk from over three thousand vessels in the Xianfeng era to barely four hundred. Unless action was taken at once, China's shipping interests would be lost for good. He urged overriding the critics and pressing ahead. In 1874 he memorialized again: "During the Tongzhi period, while Zeng Guofan and Ding Richang held the Jiangsu posts, Circuit Intendant Xu Daoshen and Subprefect Rong Hong had repeatedly urged a scheme whereby Chinese merchants would build steamers to carry tribute grain while also taking commercial freight. The proposal had been sent to the Zongli Yamen for approval and the Jiangsu customs intendant had been instructed to invite merchants at each port to try it. Years of delay produced no concrete result. In 1868 a single schooner was hired for one grain shipment, then the effort lapsed. That summer, while inspecting the sea transport receipts, I followed the Zongli Yamen's instructions and asked Zhu Qiang, the Zhejiang sea-transport commissioner in Shanghai, to draft regulations for a steam navigation company. Government shipyards were building warships only, not merchant hulls merchants could charter; wealthy Shanghai traders therefore either bought their own steamers or registered capital under foreign firms. A state-sponsored China Merchants line, I argued, could gradually absorb those private investments, aligning commercial sentiment with national interest. I proposed a pilot merchants' bureau as a bridge between government and trade. As merchant hulls came online they could be added and routes expanded. This year's coastal grain lift also fell short of Jiangsu-Zhejiang junk capacity; merchant steamers should share the haul. Rising tribute volumes would no longer face a shortage of tonnage. I asked that merchants be allowed to borrow two hundred thousand strings of copper cash—the sum the Board had authorized for training levies through Jiangsu-Zhejiang pawnshops—as startup capital, with interest prepaid for famine relief. Profits and losses would remain wholly with the shareholders, not the state. Zhu Qiang was to return to Shanghai and open recruitment. Subscriptions were enthusiastic; three modern steamers were already purchased. I secured two hundred thousand piculs of the next Zhejiang tribute consignment for the new line, with freight and grain-loss terms matching the junk trade. Commercial cargo would clear customs under existing treaty rules to forestall disputes. If the venture succeeded, China might recover part of its river and coastal trade—a matter of real revenue and livelihood. " The throne approved.
3
先是閩廠專為製造兵輪而設。 學士宋晉言糜款過鉅，議請罷之。 事下，鴻章力持不可。 略言：「歐洲諸國闖入中國邊界腹地，無不款關而求互市。 海外之險，有兵船巡防，而我與彼可共分之。 長江及各海口之利，有輪船轉運，而我與彼亦共分之。 或不至讓洋人獨擅其利與險，而浸至反客為主也。 」又言：「沿江沿海各省，不準另行購雇西洋輪船。 若有所需，令其自向閩、滬兩廠商撥訂製。 至載貨輪船，與兵船規制迥異。 閩廠現造之船，商船皆不合用。 曾國籓前飭滬廠造兵船外，另造商船四五艘。 閩廠似亦可間造商船，以資華商雇領。 現與曾國籓籌議，中國殷商每不原與官交涉，且各口岸生意已被洋商佔盡。 華商領官船，另樹一幟，洋人勢必挾重貲以傾奪，則須華商自立公司，自建行棧，自籌保險，本鉅用繁，初辦恐亦無利可圖。 若行之既久，添造與租領稍多，乃有利益。 聞華商原領者，必准其兼運漕糧，方有專門生意，不至為洋商排擠。 將來各廠商船造有成數，再請敕下總理衙門，商飭各省籌辦。 」疏上，下所司議行。
The Fuzhou Arsenal had been founded solely to build warships. Academician Song Jin denounced the waste and urged abolition. Hongzhang strongly opposed the move when the matter was referred to him. He wrote in part: "Every European power that penetrates China's borders comes to the treaty ports seeking trade. Naval patrols on the high seas are a burden we can share with them. The Yangtze and coastal shipping routes are profits we can likewise share. Only thus might we prevent foreigners from monopolizing both profit and risk until the guest becomes master. " He added that coastal provinces must not buy Western steamers on their own. Needs were to be met through the Fuzhou and Shanghai yards. Merchant hulls, he noted, differed utterly from men-of-war. Fuzhou's current output was useless for trade. Zeng Guofan had already ordered several merchant hulls from Shanghai. Fuzhou might occasionally do the same for Chinese charterers. Wealthy merchants, he and Zeng agreed, shunned official dealings while foreign firms already dominated every port. Merchants operating under official colors would face foreign rivals armed with heavy capital; independent companies, warehouses, and insurance would cost dearly and yield little at first. Longer operation and a larger fleet would eventually pay. Charterers must be allowed tribute-grain contracts, giving them a niche foreign lines could not squeeze out. Once yards produced enough hulls, an edict through the Zongli Yamen could organize the provinces. " The memorial was sent down for action.
4
是年冬，招商局成立，以知府硃其昂主其事，道員盛宣懷佐之。 其昂以道員胡光墉、李振玉等招徠商股，入貲者極為踴躍，宣懷亦援粵人唐廷樞、徐潤董局事。 購船、設械、立埠，次第經營，悉屬商本，規模觕具。 光緒元年，鴻章奏獎其昂等有差。 三年，增購旗昌船艦，始假用直隸、江蘇、江西、湖北、東海關官款百九十萬兩有奇。 初擬購旗昌輪船，宣懷持之最力，需銀二百數十萬兩。 商本無幾，不足以應。 宣懷以國防大計、江海利源之說，力陳於江督沈葆楨。 葆楨為所動，撥銀百萬以濟，論者咸謂是舉為失計，至以「旗昌棄垂敝之裘，得值另制新衣，期於適體」為喻。 事後募集商股，應者寥寥，僅得銀四萬者以此也。 御史董俊翰言：「招商局每月虧至五六萬兩。 致虧之由，因置船過多，輪車行駛，經費過鉅，必須一船得一船之用，方可無虞折耗。 聞商局各船攬載之資，不敷經費，船多貨少。 刻下既未能遽赴外洋各國，以廣收貿易之利，祗宜量為變置，使所出之數不至浮於所入也。 」六年，祭酒王先謙請整頓招商局務，語涉宣懷。 疏下江督劉坤一，言宣懷於購旗昌輪船時，聲言有商款百餘萬，實無所有，有意欺謾，冀獲酬金，請奪宣懷職。 復請以官款概作官股，以其贏餘作海防經費。 疏入，均不報。
The China Merchants' bureau opened that winter under Zhu Qiang, assisted by Sheng Xuanhuai. Zhu Qiang and Hu Guangyong rallied subscriptions with exceptional zeal; Xuanhuai enlisted Tang Jingshu and Xu Run from Guangdong to manage operations. Ships, equipment, and agencies followed in order—all merchant-funded—and the enterprise took rough shape. In 1875 Li Hongzhang memorialized rewards for Zhu Qiang and his colleagues. In 1877, to buy the Russell & Company (Qichang) fleet, the bureau first borrowed more than 1.9 million taels of official funds from Zhili, Jiangsu, Jiangxi, Hubei, and the East Sea Customs. Xuanhuai had pushed hardest for the Russell & Company purchase, which required over two million taels. Merchant capital was far too small to cover the price. He pressed Shen Baozhen with arguments about national defense and control of river and sea profits. Shen allocated one million taels; critics likened the deal to paying full price for another man's cast-off coat and tailoring new garments to fit. Subsequent share offerings found few takers—barely forty thousand taels subscribed. Censor Dong Junhan reported that the bureau was losing fifty to sixty thousand taels a month. Too many hulls and running costs, he argued, meant each vessel must pay its way. Freight income failed to cover expenses—too many ships, too little cargo. Until routes reached foreign waters, he urged shedding tonnage so outlays would not exceed receipts. " In 1880 Libationer Wang Xianqian memorialized for a shake-up, implicating Xuanhuai. Liu Kunyi reported that Xuanhuai had claimed over a million in merchant funds that did not exist, seeking a reward for deception, and asked that he be dismissed. Liu also proposed converting official loans into state equity and dedicating profits to coastal defense. Both memorials were ignored.
5
招商局所假官帑，至光緒六年，應分期拔還。 乃償已逾半，復假洋債。 鴻章言兼籌並顧，招商局力有未逮，請先償洋債，後及官帑，格於部議。 嗣以遞年清還，而商股尚達四百萬兩焉。 當招商開辦之初，僅輪船三艘。 嗣承領閩、滬兩廠，購之英國，增至十二艘。 迨購入旗昌輪船十八艘，遂與英商太古、怡和並稱三公司。 貲本過鉅，收入轉微。
Official loans to the bureau were due for installment repayment by 1880. After more than half was repaid, the bureau borrowed abroad again. Li Hongzhang argued the bureau could not manage both obligations and asked to prioritize foreign creditors; the Board refused. Yearly repayments continued until merchant equity reached four million taels. The company began with three steamers. Taking over Fujian and Shanghai-built hulls and English purchases brought the fleet to twelve. Buying Russell & Company's eighteen vessels made a "big three" with Butterfield & Swire and Jardine Matheson. Capital ballooned while returns thinned.
6
是年，以言官劾奏招商局辦理毫無實濟，請飭認真整頓，諭李鴻章及江督吳元炳澈查。 鴻章等奏言：「輪船招商局之設，乃各商集股，自行經理，已於創辦之初奏明，盈虧全歸商認，與官無涉。 輪船商務牽涉洋務，更不便由官任之，與他項設立官局開支公款者，迥不相同。 惟此舉為收回中國利權，事體重大，故須官為扶持，並酌借官帑，以助商力之不足。 光緒三年冬曾將商局事宜籌畫整頓復奏，並飭江海、津海兩關道，於每年結帳，就近分赴滬洋各局清查帳目，如有隱冒，據實奏請參賠。 數年以來，雖有英商太古、怡和洋行極力傾擠，而局事尚足相持，官帑漸可拔還。 復先承運京倉漕米、各省賑糧，不下數百萬石，徵兵調餉、解送官物軍械者，源源不絕，豈得謂於國事毫無實濟？ 其攬載客貨，以及出入款目，責成素習商業之道員唐廷樞、徐潤總理其事，每年結帳後，分晰開列清冊，悉聽入本各商閱看稽查。 若局中稍有弊端，則眾商不待官查，必已相率追控。 而自開辦至今，並無入股商人控告者。 現值漕運攬載吃緊之時，若紛紛調簿清查，不特市面徒滋搖惑，生意難以招徠； 且洋商嫉忌方深，更必乘機傾擠，冀遂其把持專利之謀，殊於中國商務大局有礙。 總之，商局關係國課最重，而各關各納稅課，絲毫無虧，所借官款由商局運漕水腳分年扣還，公款已歸有着，其各商股本盈虧，應如前奏，全歸商認，與官無涉。 應俟每年結帳時，照案由滬、津兩關道就近清查，以符定章。 」疏入，報聞。
Censors charged mismanagement that year; Li Hongzhang and Wu Yuanbing were ordered to investigate. Li Hongzhang replied that the bureau was a merchant joint-stock concern from the outset, with profits and losses strictly private. Steam shipping touched foreign affairs and could not be run like an ordinary government office on public money. Yet the state had to subsidize it because recovering shipping rights was a matter of national weight. In late 1877 he had ordered annual audits by Shanghai and Tianjin customs officials, with penalties for fraud. Despite fierce pressure from Swire and Jardine, the bureau had held its own and official loans were being repaid. It had moved millions of piculs of capital tribute and relief grain and endless military stores—hardly a null contribution. Tang Jingshu and Xu Run, expert merchants, handled freight accounts opened annually to shareholders. Any fraud, he noted, would provoke shareholder suits before officials intervened. No investor had yet filed a complaint. A disruptive audit during the busy tribute season would unsettle markets and scare off business; Foreign rivals would exploit the turmoil to tighten their grip on the trade. Customs revenue remained intact, official loans were repaid from freight, and merchant equity stayed private as originally pledged. Annual audits by Shanghai and Tianjin customs should suffice. " The throne took note.
7
十一月，學士梅啟照言：「招商局自歸併旗昌輪船，各國輪船之利漸減，然祗在香港、福州、寧波、上海、天津、牛庄、長江等處碼頭，不如推廣，竟令其赴東西洋各國。 請飭南北洋大臣，督令局員，酌派豐順、保大等船，先赴東洋試行。 行之有效，漸及於西洋，則貿遷有無之利，中外分之。 」明年，祭酒王先謙亦以為言。 均下所司核議。 先是招商局船駛往新嘉坡、小呂宋、日本等處，不足與外輪競利，尋即停罷。 嗣遣和眾船往夏威仁國之檀香山、美之舊金山兩埠，華人麕集，航業頗振。 因復遣美富船往。 而各國商業，英為巨擘。 七年，粵人梁雲漢等設肇興公司於倫敦，船政大臣黎兆棠實倡斯議。 鴻章疏言：「西洋富強之策，商務與船政互相表裡。 以兵船之力衛商船，必先以商船之稅養兵船，則整頓尤為急務。 邇者各國商船爭赴中國，計每年進出口貨價約銀二萬萬兩以外。 洋商所逐什一之利，已不下數千萬兩，以十年計之，則數萬萬兩。 此皆中國之利，有往無來者也。 故當商務未興之前，各國原可閉關自治。 逮風氣大開，既不能拒之使不來，惟有自擴利源，勸令華商出洋貿易，庶土貨可暢銷，洋商可少至，而中國利權亦可逐漸收回。 前此招商局輪船嘗駛往新嘉坡、小呂宋、越南等埠攬載。 近年和眾、美富等船分駛夏威仁國之檀香山、美國之舊金山，載運客貨，究止小試其端，尚未厚集其力。 英國倫敦為通商第一都會，並無華商前往。 黎兆棠志在匡時，久有創立公司之議，盡心提倡，力為其難。 現既粗定規模，自當因勢利導，期於必成。 」報聞。
In November Academician Mei Qizhao argued that merging the Russell fleet had cut foreign rivals' profits but the line still hugged domestic ports; it should sail to Europe and America. He asked the coastal governors to send the Fengshun and Baoda to Japan first as a trial. Success overseas would split long-haul trade profits with foreign carriers. " Wang Xianqian made the same argument the following year. Both proposals were sent down for study. Earlier runs to Singapore, the Philippines, and Japan had failed against foreign competition and were dropped. The Hezhong then served Honolulu and San Francisco, where large Chinese communities made routes viable. The Meifu was sent next. Britain nonetheless dominated world trade. In 1881 Cantonese led by Liang Yunhan founded the Zhaoxing Company in London on the initiative of naval minister Li Zhaotang. Li Hongzhang wrote that in the West, commerce and naval power were two sides of the same policy of national strength. Protecting merchantmen with warships required merchant shipping revenues to sustain the navy—making reform all the more urgent. Foreign merchant fleets now crowded China's ports, with annual trade valued at well over 200 million taels. Their ten-percent margins alone ran to tens of millions of taels yearly—hundreds of millions over a decade. All of it was Chinese wealth flowing out and not returning. Before the age of global commerce, nations could still close their doors and govern themselves. Once the world opened, China could not keep foreigners out; it had to expand its own trade by sending Chinese merchants overseas so native goods would sell, foreign competition would thin, and commercial rights might gradually return. The bureau's steamers had earlier tried freight runs to Singapore, the Philippines, and Vietnam. Recently the Hezhong and Meifu had tried Honolulu and San Francisco, but these were only tentative ventures, not yet a concentrated effort. London, the greatest trading city in the world, still had no Chinese merchant presence. Li Zhaotang, eager to serve the times, had long championed founding a company and threw himself into the hardest part of the work. With a rough plan in place, the state should guide the venture to success. " The throne took note.
8
十年，法人來擾，海疆不靖，股商洶懼，局船慮為劫奪，以銀五百二十五萬兩暫售之旗昌行主。 事平收回，復增置江新、新昌、新康、新銘各艦。 而沈沒朽敝者，不一而足，其後共達二十九艘雲。 十二年，鄂督張之洞遣總兵王榮和至南洋，籌辦捐船護商事項。 宣統三年，設商船學校於吳淞。 凡此皆為擴充航業之張本，而局船行駛外洋之利，終不能與各國爭衡也。
In 1884, with French hostilities unsettling the coast, frightened shareholders sold the fleet temporarily to Russell & Company for 5.25 million taels lest it be seized. After peace returned the ships were bought back and new vessels—the Jiangxin, Xinchang, Xinkang, and Xinming—were added. Sinkings and decay took their toll; the fleet eventually numbered twenty-nine hulls. In 1886 Zhang Zhidong, governor of Huguang, sent Brigadier Wang Ronghe to Southeast Asia to raise donated ships for merchant protection. In 1911 a merchant marine school was founded at Wusong. These measures laid groundwork for expansion, yet the bureau's ocean routes never matched foreign competition.
9
招商局之設，本為挽回江海已失航利。 開辦之始，即知為洋商所嫉，而彌補之策，首在分運蘇、浙漕米，嗣更推之鄂、贛、江、安。 而滇之銅斤，蜀之燈木，江、浙之採辦官物，直、晉之賑糧，胥由局船經營其事。 光緒十一年，道員葉廷眷復條上扶持商局運鄂茶、鄂鹽，增加運漕水腳諸策。 事下直督李鴻章。 先是局船運漕，石銀五錢有奇。 嗣英、美人攬運，故廉其值，商局運費因之減少，勢益不支。 鴻章請稍增益之，格部議，不果行。 蓋招商局自開辦以來，局中之侵蝕與局外之傾擠，所有資力頗虞虧耗。 商股不足，貸及官款，繼以洋債。 當事者日言維持補救之策，裨益實鮮，而以用款浮濫，復屢為言官所劾。 至是部臣疏言：「三代之治法，貴本而抑末，重農而賤商，從古商務未嘗議於朝廷。 海上互市以來，論者乃競言商政。 竊謂商者逐什一之利，以厚居積、權子母為事者也。 厚居積，必月計之有餘； 權子母，必求倍入之息。 若計存本則日虧，問子母則無著，甚且稱貸乞假以補不足，猶號於眾曰『此吾致富之術也』，有是理乎？ 嘗見富商大賈，必擇忠信之人以主會計。 其入有經，其出有節。 守餘一餘三之法，核實厚積，乃能久遠。 若主計不得其人，生之者寡，食之者眾，取之無度，用之無節，不旋踵而終窶。 用人理財之道，與政通矣。 前者李鴻章、沈葆楨創立此局，謀深慮遠，實為經國宏謀，固為收江海之利，與洋商爭衡，轉貧為富、轉弱為強之機，盡在此舉。 乃招商局十餘年來，不特本息不增，而官款、洋債，欠負累累，豈謀之不臧哉？ 稽之案牘，證之人言，知所謂利權，上不在國，下不在商，盡歸於中飽之員紳。 如唐廷樞、硃其昂之被參於前，徐潤、張鴻祿之敗露於後，皆其明證。 主計之不得其人，出入之經，不能講求撙節，又安得以局本虧折，諉之於海上用兵耶？ 商局既撥有官款，又津貼以漕運水腳，減免於貨稅，其歲入歲出之款，即應官為稽察。 請飭下南北洋大臣，將局中現存江海輪若干只，碼頭幾處，委員商董銜名，及運腳支銷，分別造報。 此後總辦如非其人，原保大臣應即議處。 」報可。 然管理招商局之權，始終屬之直隸總督，部臣無從過問。 迨三十三年，商局與英商怡和、太古訂利益均享之約，始免互相傾擠，而其利漸著。 此招商局辦理之大略情形也。
The China Merchants line had been founded to recover shipping profits already lost on rivers and seas. From the outset foreign rivals resented it; the remedy was first sharing Jiangsu-Zhejiang tribute grain, then extending to Hubei, Jiangxi, the Yangtze, and Anhui. Yunnan copper, Sichuan timber, Jiangsu-Zhejiang official cargo, and Zhili-Shanxi relief grain all moved on bureau hulls. In 1885 Circuit Intendant Ye Tingjuan urged policies to support the bureau with Hubei tea and salt cargoes and higher tribute freight rates. The matter was referred to Li Hongzhang as governor of Zhili. Bureau tribute freight had initially been set at just over five qian of silver per picul. British and American carriers undercut the rates, forcing the bureau to cut freight and worsening its finances. Li Hongzhang asked for a modest increase, but the Board refused. Since its founding, internal graft and outside competition had steadily drained its capital. Insufficient merchant equity led to official loans and then foreign debt. Managers talked constantly of remedies that helped little, while profligate spending brought repeated censor impeachments. Board ministers then wrote: "The sages of the Three Dynasties honored the fundamental and restrained the secondary, prized farming and slighted trade; never before had commerce been a matter for the throne. Only since maritime trade opened had officials begun debating commercial policy. Merchants, I submit, chase ten-percent returns through stockpiling goods and compounding capital. Heavy stocks require monthly surpluses; and compounding capital must yield doubled returns. If capital shrinks daily and principal finds no return, if one must borrow to cover shortfalls yet still call it 'the way to grow rich'—where is the sense in that? Great merchants always place accounts in loyal, trustworthy hands. Income follows rules; spending stays disciplined. Keeping the classic reserve ratios and building verified reserves is how firms endure. Wrong accountants mean few earners, many spenders, reckless taking and spending—and swift ruin. Managing people and money follows the same principles as governing a state. When Li Hongzhang and Shen Baozhen founded the bureau, their far-sighted plan was a national strategy to recover river and sea trade, rival foreign lines, and turn weakness into strength. Yet after more than a decade the bureau had not grown its capital but piled up official and foreign debts—was the original design at fault? Records and rumor alike showed that profits went neither to the state nor to merchants but to officials and gentry who lined their pockets. Tang Jingshu and Zhu Qiang had been impeached earlier; Xu Run and Zhang Honglu were later exposed—clear evidence. With the wrong accountants and no discipline in spending, how could losses be blamed on the war at sea? The bureau received official funds, tribute freight subsidies, and tax breaks; its annual accounts deserved state audit. The ministers asked the coastal governors to report fleet size, wharves, director names, and freight accounts. Future mismanagement should bring punishment on the officials who had recommended the managers. " Approved. Management nonetheless remained with the Zhili governor-general; the Board could not intervene. In 1907 the bureau signed profit-sharing agreements with Jardine Matheson and Butterfield & Swire, easing cutthroat rivalry and improving returns. Such was the broad outline of how the China Merchants line was run.
10
招商輪船航行各埠，悉自滬始。 駛行長江者曰江輪，駛行海洋者曰海輪。 停泊口岸，大小不一，惟商務殷闐之所，設貨棧焉。 以故上海設總棧，而蘇之鎮江、南京，皖之蕪湖，贛之九江，鄂之漢口，浙之寧波、溫州，閩之福州、汕頭，粵之廣州、香港，魯之煙台，奉之營口，直之塘沽、天津，皆設行棧，而通州以漕運所關，亦設棧焉。 江輪、海輪，時統名之為大輪。 其與大輪並行於內江外海，或駛行大輪所不能達之處，則有小輪。 光緒初，商置小輪之行駛，僅限於通商口岸。 十年，明申禁令，小輪不得擅入內河。 官商僱用，須江海關給照乃可。 然祗限於蘇杭之間。 其輸運客貨、駛入江北內河者，皆在所禁。
China Merchants routes to every port radiated from Shanghai. Yangtze vessels were called river steamers; ocean-going hulls were sea steamers. Wharves varied in size; cargo warehouses were built only at busy commercial ports. Shanghai housed the head office; branch warehouses stood at Zhenjiang, Nanjing, Wuhu, Jiujiang, Hankou, Ningbo, Wenzhou, Fuzhou, Shantou, Guangzhou, Hong Kong, Yantai, Yingkou, Tanggu, Tianjin, and Tongzhou for the tribute trade. River and sea steamers were together called the large fleet. Smaller steamers served alongside the large fleet on rivers and coasts and reached places the big ships could not. In the early Guangxu era, privately owned small steamers could sail only among treaty ports. In 1884 the ban was reiterated: small steamers could not enter inland rivers without permission. Official or merchant use required a license from the Jiangsu Customs. Even then the allowance applied only between Suzhou and Hangzhou. Carrying passengers or cargo into the Yangtze's northern tributaries remained forbidden.
11
十六年，詹事志銳疏請各省試行小輪。 總署王大臣議以為不可。 護湘撫沈晉祥言：「湘民沿河居住，操舟為業者，實繁有徒。 自上海通商以後，僅有淮鹽一項，尚可往來裝運，其餘貨物，多由輪船載送，湘省民船祗能行抵江、漢而止，舵工水手失業者多。 今再加以小輪行駛內河，誠如總理衙門原奏所云，必至奪民船之利，有礙小民生計。 」江督劉坤一亦言小輪行駛內河，流弊滋多，礙民生，妨國課，病地方，請嚴禁之。 俱如所請。
In 1890 Junior Mentor Zhi Rui asked that provinces trial small steamers. The Zongli Yamen grand ministers ruled against it. Acting Hunan governor Shen Jinxiang wrote: "Countless Hunanese along the rivers live by boat-handling. Since Shanghai opened, only Huai salt still moved on native craft; steamers took most other cargo, leaving Hunan boats confined to the Yangtze-Han reach and many boatmen unemployed. Adding small steamers on inland rivers, as the Zongli Yamen had warned, would seize native boatmen's trade and harm livelihoods. " Liu Kunyi added that inland small steamers would breed abuses, harm livelihoods, revenue, and local order, and urged a strict ban. Both requests were granted.
12
初，外輪行駛長江，由滬至漢口而止。 二十一年，馬關約成，許日輪一自漢口達宜昌，更溯江上至重慶，一自上海入運河以抵蘇、杭，於時朝旨始許華商小輪於蘇杭間行駛。 而江督張之洞更推廣其航行之路於鎮江、江寧、清江浦及贛之鄱陽。 二十四年，長江通商約成，而通州蘆涇港、泰興天星橋、湖北荊河口悉定為洋輪上下搭客處，而桂之西江、直之白河、沈之遼河、松花江，亦先後許外輪行駛。 迨中英馬凱約成，更及於粵之北江、東江。 與英、日訂內港行輪章程，凡內地水道，外輪悉攫得行駛之權，於是向之華商小輪不得行駛各地，始一律弛禁焉。 江、浙、閩、粵輪船公司次第設立，轉輸客貨，人稱便捷。 特以洋商創始於前，華商瞠乎其後，而跌價傾擠，時有所聞，欲求贏利，蓋綦難矣。
Initially foreign Yangtze steamers ran only from Shanghai to Hankou. The 1895 Treaty of Shimonoseki opened the Yangtze to Japanese steamers as far as Chongqing and the canal to Suzhou and Hangzhou; only then did the court allow Chinese small steamers between Suzhou and Hangzhou. Zhang Zhidong as river governor extended routes to Zhenjiang, Nanjing, Qingjiangpu, and Poyang Lake. The 1898 Yangtze treaty named Lujing, Tianxing Bridge, and Jinghe mouth as foreign landing stages; the West River, White River, Liao River, and Songhua were opened to foreign steamers in turn. The 1902 Mackay Treaty extended the same to Guangdong's North and East Rivers. Inland steamer regulations with Britain and Japan gave foreigners navigation rights on every waterway; Chinese small steamers were then allowed the same access they had been denied. Steam companies sprang up along the coast, moving passengers and cargo with praised convenience. Foreign firms had the head start; Chinese lines lagged behind amid cutthroat price wars, making profit elusive.
13
三十年，商部參議王清穆言：「植商業之基礎，莫如內河航政一事。 凡鐵路之尚未通者，可藉航路控接之，凡軌路所不能達者，可由航路轉輸之。 江、鄂諸省，若漢湘，若九南，若鎮揚、鎮浦、蘇杭、蘇滬、常鎮各航路，四通八達，往往為外人所經營，其公司多不過數萬金，視軌路之動需千百萬者，難易迥殊，華商之力尚能興辦，洵為今日切要之舉。 請飭各省有航路處所，於華商輪船公司亟予保護。 未設者，提倡籌辦。 」報可。 自是小輪公司漸推漸廣，閩、粵濱海之區，輪檣如織，隨處可通。 直則有往來安東、天津、大連、營口、牛庄、煙台、龍口、義馬島、威海衛、海參崴之小輪，蘇則有往來鎮江、清江浦、通州、海門、上海、蘇、杭、江寧、揚州、六合之小輪，皖則有往來蕪湖、廬州、安慶、寧國、巢縣之小輪，贛則有往來南昌、九江、吳城、湖口、豐城、樟樹鎮、吉安、饒州之小輪，湘、鄂則有往來漢口、黃州、沙市、宜昌、武昌、嘉魚、長沙、株州、常德、咸寧、岳州、湘潭、益陽、仙桃鎮、老河口之小輪，桂則有往來梧州、南寧、貴縣、柳州之小輪，浙則有往來寧波、溫州、穿山、定海、象山、寧海、台州、海門、沈家門、普陀山、餘姚、西塢、瑞安、平望、震澤、南潯之小輪，川則有往來宜昌、重慶、嘉定、敘府之小輪，各公司盈虧不一，而航路四達，商旅便之，實與江海大輪有相輔而行之利。 此外則有各省官用小輪暨專用小輪，是又於商輪之外特設者也。
In 1904 Ministry of Commerce councillor Wang Qingmu wrote: "Nothing builds commerce like inland navigation. Where railways had not yet reached, waterways could connect them; where rails could not go, ships could carry the freight. Routes such as the Han-Xiang, Jiunan, Zhenyang, Zhenpu, Suzhou-Hangzhou, Suzhou-Shanghai, and Chang-Zhen lines crisscrossed Jiangsu and Hubei but were often foreign-run; companies needed only tens of thousands of taels compared with railways' millions—work Chinese merchants could still afford and urgently needed to undertake. He asked provinces with waterways to protect Chinese steam companies. Where none existed, promote their founding. " Approved. Small steamer firms then spread along the Fujian and Guangdong coasts until masts crowded every harbor. Zhili lines linked Andong, Tianjin, Dalian, Yingkou, Niuzhuang, Yantai, Longkou, Yima Island, Weihaiwei, and Vladivostok; Jiangsu lines served Zhenjiang through Shanghai, Suzhou, Hangzhou, Nanjing, Yangzhou, and Liuhe; similar networks covered Anhui, Jiangxi, Hunan-Hubei, Guangxi, Zhejiang's coastal towns, and Sichuan's upper Yangtze. Profits varied by company, but routes now reached everywhere, aiding travelers and merchants in ways that complemented the large river and sea fleet. Provincial official and dedicated small steamers were also established apart from merchant lines.
14
三十一年，修撰張謇醵銀五十萬，設大達輪步公司於上海。 宣統三年，吉林巡撫陳昭常創辦吉林圖長航業公司，自滬越日本長崎達圖們江，以滬商硃江募貲為之。 此皆於招商局外別樹一幟者也。
In 1905 Compiler Zhang Jian raised five hundred thousand taels to found the Dada Steam and Lighterage Company in Shanghai. In 1911 Jilin governor Chen Zhaochang launched a line from Shanghai via Nagasaki to the Tumen River, capital raised by Shanghai merchant Zhu Jiang. These ventures all stood as separate enterprises apart from the China Merchants line.
