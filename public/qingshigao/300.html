沈起元何師儉唐繼祖馬維翰餘甸王葉滋劉而位
Shen Qiyuan, He Shijian, Tang Jizu, Ma Weihan, Yu Dian, Wang Yezi, and Liu Erwei
3
沈起元，字子大，江南太倉人。 康熙六十年進士，選庶吉士，改吏部主事。 擢員外郎，以知府發福建用。 總督高其倬令權福州，調興化。 時世宗聞福建倉穀虧空，遣廣東巡撫楊文乾等往按，被劾者半，受代者爭為煩苛，起元獨持平。 莆田民因訟互鬥，其倬恐釀亂，令捕治。 起元責兩人而釋其餘，報曰：「罪在主者，餘不足問也。 」尋攝海關，裁陋規萬餘金。 巡撫常安有奴在關，以索費困商舶。 起元聞，立督收稅如額，令商舶行，白常安斥奴。 自是人皆奉法。 其倬奏開南洋，報可。 已，復令商出洋者，必戚里具狀，限期返，逾者連坐。 起元曰：「人之生死，貨之利鈍，皆無常，戚里豈能預料？ 且始不聽出洋則已，今聽之，商造船集貨費不貲，奈何忽撓以結狀？ 若令商自具狀，過三年不歸，勿聽回籍，不猶愈乎？ 」其倬從之。
Shen Qiyuan, styled Zida, came from Taicang in Jiangnan. He received his jinshi degree in the sixtieth year of Kangxi, entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor, and was then appointed a principal secretary in the Ministry of Personnel. Promoted to vice director, he was sent to Fujian to take up a prefectural post. Governor-General Gao Qizhuo had him serve as acting prefect of Fuzhou before transferring him to Xinghua. When the Yongzheng Emperor learned that Fujian's granaries were short, he sent Guangdong Governor Yang Wenqian and others to investigate. Half of those implicated were punished, and their successors competed to be oppressively severe—only Qiyuan held a steady, even hand. Litigants in Putian had come to blows; fearing unrest, Qizhuo ordered mass arrests. Qiyuan punished only two ringleaders and released the rest, reporting: "The crime rests with the leaders; the others are not worth pursuing." He soon took charge of the customs house as well and cut abusive levies worth more than ten thousand taels of silver. Governor Chang An kept a servant at the customs gate who shook down merchant ships for fees. On hearing of it, Qiyuan at once enforced the proper tax quota, cleared the ships to sail, and reported to Chang An to dismiss the servant. After that, everyone observed the regulations. Qizhuo memorialized to reopen trade with Southeast Asia, and the court approved. Soon afterward merchants sailing abroad were again required to obtain bonds from kinsmen, with a fixed date for return; failure would bring collective punishment on the family. Qiyuan said: "Life and death, profit and loss on the voyage—all are unpredictable. How can kinsmen possibly guarantee outcomes in advance?" Besides, if we had never allowed them to sail, that would be one matter; but now that permission is granted, merchants have sunk fortunes into building ships and gathering cargo—how can we suddenly disrupt them with bond formalities? If merchants post their own bonds, and those who fail to return within three years forfeit their right to re-register at home—would that not be better? Qizhuo adopted his proposal.
4
調台灣。 台灣田一甲準十一畝有奇，賦三則：上則一甲穀八石，中則六石，下則四石，視內地數倍。 然多隱佔，民不甚困。 時方清丈，佔者不得匿。 其倬欲使台灣賦悉視內地下則，恐不及額致部詰。 起元令著籍者仍舊額，丈出者視內地下則。 俟隱佔既清，更減舊額重者均於新額，賦不虧而民無累。 起元在福州，以辨冤獄忤按察使潘體豐，體豐中以他事，鐫四級，遂告歸。
He was transferred to Taiwan. In Taiwan one jia of land equals a little over eleven mu; tax falls in three grades—eight dan of grain per jia at the upper rate, six at the middle, four at the lower—several times the levies on the mainland. Yet much land was concealed from the registers, so the people were not greatly burdened in practice. A comprehensive land survey was then under way, and concealed holdings could no longer be hidden. Qizhuo wished to assess all Taiwan land at the mainland's lowest rate, fearing shortfalls would draw rebuke from the Board of Revenue. Qiyuan ruled that land already on the registers should keep its old quota, while newly surveyed fields should be taxed at the mainland lower rate. Once concealed holdings were cleared, the heaviest old quotas could be reduced and spread across the new assessments—revenue would not fall short, and the people would not be crushed. In Fuzhou, Qiyuan offended Surveillance Commissioner Pan Tifeng by overturning unjust verdicts; Pan found a pretext to demote him four ranks, and he resigned and went home.
5
高宗即位，起江西驛鹽道副使。 乾隆二年，擢河南按察使。 會久雨，被災者四十餘縣，饑民四走，或議禁之。 起元謂：「民飢且死，奈何止其他徙？ 」令安置未被水諸縣，給以糧，遂無出河南境者。 巡撫雅爾圖檄府縣修書院，以起元總其事，乃教群士省身克己之學。 立章善坊，書孝子、悌弟、義夫、貞婦名，採訪事實，為章善錄版行，一時風動。
When Gaozong came to the throne, Qiyuan was appointed vice commissioner of the Jiangxi Relay and Salt Circuit. In the second year of Qianlong he was promoted to Henan surveillance commissioner. Prolonged rains had flooded more than forty counties; starving people scattered in every direction, and some officials proposed barring their movement. Qiyuan said: "The people are starving to death—how can we forbid them to seek food elsewhere?" He had them settled in counties untouched by flood and issued grain rations, so none left the province. Governor Yarto ordered prefectures and counties to repair their academies and put Qiyuan in charge; he then instructed the scholars in the practice of moral self-scrutiny. He set up a pavilion of exemplary virtue, inscribed the names of filial sons, dutiful younger brothers, righteous husbands, and chaste wives, verified each case, and published a Record of Exemplary Goodness that stirred public morals throughout the province.
6
七年，遷直隸布政使。 大旱議賑，總督高斌欲十一月始行，起元力請先普賑一月，俟戶口查竣，再分別加賑。 有倡言賑戶不賑口者，起元曰：「一戶數口，止賑一二，是且殺七八人矣！ 」檄各屬似此者罪之。 戶部尚書海望奏清理直隸旗地，有司違限，旨飭責。 斌將劾數州縣應命，起元不可，曰：「旗地非旦夕可清，州縣方賑災，何暇及此？ 獨劾起元可也！ 」乃止。 九年，內轉光祿寺卿。 十三年，移疾歸。
In the seventh year he was transferred to financial commissioner of Zhili. During a severe drought, relief was debated; Governor-General Gao Bin wanted to wait until the eleventh month, but Qiyuan pressed for a month of universal relief first, then targeted additional aid once household registers were complete. Some proposed relief by household rather than by individual; Qiyuan said: "A household may have many mouths—if you feed only one or two, you will let seven or eight starve!" He ordered all subordinates to punish officials who did likewise. Minister of Revenue Haiwang memorialized to clear banner lands in Zhili; local officials missed their deadlines and received an imperial reprimand. Bin was about to impeach several prefectures and counties to force compliance; Qiyuan refused, saying: "Banner lands cannot be cleared overnight, and the counties are busy with famine relief—how can they attend to this now?" Impeach me alone—that will suffice!" The plan was dropped. In the ninth year he was transferred within the capital to director of the Court of Imperial Entertainments. In the thirteenth year he cited illness and retired home.
7
起元自少敦厲廉恥，晚歲杜門誦先儒書。 臨沒，言：「平生學無真得。 年來靜中自檢，仰不愧，俯不怍，或庶幾焉！ 」
From youth Qiyuan had cultivated integrity and a keen sense of shame; in his later years he shut his doors and devoted himself to the classics of the sages. On his deathbed he said: "In life my learning never reached true understanding." In recent years, examining myself in stillness, I can look up without shame and down without guilt—perhaps that is something! ”
8
何師儉，字桐叔，浙江山陰人。 以納貲，於康熙六十年選授兵部員外郎。 奉職勤懇，常數月不出署。 雍正元年，遷廣西右江道僉事，部請留任一年，世宗命以升銜留任，賜人葠、貂皮。 師儉以執法卻重賄，忤要人，因誣以避瘴故留部。 侍郎李紱昌言曰：「今部曹不名一錢，才者尤勞瘁，苟得郡，爭趨之，況監司耶？ 」期滿，復請留，加按察司副使銜。 司疏奏皆出其手，他司事難治者亦時委之。
He Shijian, styled Tongshu, came from Shanyin in Zhejiang. Having purchased office, in the sixtieth year of Kangxi he was selected and appointed a vice director in the Ministry of War. He served with tireless diligence, often not leaving the yamen for months at a time. In the first year of Yongzheng he was transferred to the Guangxi Right River Circuit; the ministry asked that he be kept another year, and the Yongzheng Emperor ordered him retained with an elevated rank, bestowing ginseng and sable fur. Shijian had refused heavy bribes while enforcing the law, offending powerful figures, who then slandered him as staying in the capital to avoid the southern miasma. Vice Minister Li Fu spoke plainly: "Nowadays ministry officers won't touch a single coin; the ablest are worn to exhaustion—if they could secure a prefecture they would rush for it at once, let alone a circuit post?" When his term expired he again asked to stay, and was given the rank of vice surveillance commissioner. All ministry memorials passed through his hand, and difficult cases from other bureaus were often entrusted to him as well.
9
三年，出為江南驛鹽道副使，上召對，勉以操守，复賜人葠、貂皮，許上疏言事。 四年，調廣東糧驛道副使。 歲大祲，師儉以存留米五萬石給餉，飭所屬緩徵。 或疑專擅獲咎，師儉曰：「請而後行，民已苦箠楚矣！ 」總督孔毓珣與巡撫楊文乾不相能，以師儉署鹽法道，欲引以為助。 文乾疑為毓珣黨，令買銅，將以賠累困之。 明年，文乾入覲，上示以毓珣彈事，亦及師儉，乃知師儉非阿毓珣者。 令署按察使，毓珣又疑師儉暱文乾。 及文乾卒，劾師儉違禁開礦，侵蝕銅價。 逾年，署巡撫傅泰會鞫，事始白。 上知其無罪，命往陝西佐治軍需。
In the third year he was posted as Jiangnan Relay and Salt vice commissioner; the emperor received him in audience, urged him to maintain his integrity, again bestowed ginseng and sable fur, and allowed him to submit memorials on any matter. In the fourth year he was transferred to Guangdong Grain and Relay vice commissioner. That year brought severe famine; Shijian drew on fifty thousand shi of retained grain for rations and ordered his subordinates to defer tax collection. Some worried he would be punished for acting on his own authority; Shijian said: "If we wait for approval before acting, the people will already have felt the rod!" Governor-General Kong Yupei and Governor Yang Wenqian were at odds; Kong had Shijian act as salt commissioner, hoping to win his support. Wenqian suspected him of being Kong's man and ordered him to purchase copper, intending to ruin him through indemnity losses. The following year Wenqian came to court; the emperor showed him Kong's impeachment memorials, which also mentioned Shijian, and realized Shijian was no partisan of Kong's. He was ordered to act as surveillance commissioner, whereupon Kong suspected Shijian of being close to Wenqian. When Wenqian died, Kong impeached Shijian for illegally opening mines and embezzling copper revenues. More than a year later, Acting Governor Fu Tai jointly investigated the case and Shijian was exonerated. The emperor knew him innocent and ordered him to Shaanxi to assist with military logistics.
10
師儉在兵部，諳悉諸邊形勢扼塞、戰守機宜、芻餉緩急。 至涼州，每集議，指畫如素習，總督查郎阿深重之。 署涼莊道參政。 師過涼州，檄至肅州支餉。 兩路遙遠，師儉即以涼州所蓄給之，師行無乏。 一日羽書數過，師儉策必調取生兵，峙餦以待。 已而果然。 肅州師將行，飛檄令截取公私騾馬，官民皇皇。 師儉曰：「在道官商皆赴肅者，若官頓於途，貨棄於地，非軍前所宜。 進剿未有定期，何如聽其至肅，釋所載而後供役？ 軍前得人與貨，亦省芻茭解送之煩，是獲兩利也。 檄雖嚴，吾自當之。 」於是官商皆安，軍事亦無誤。
At the War Ministry Shijian had mastered the strategic terrain of every frontier, the tactics of attack and defense, and the urgency of fodder and supply. At Liangzhou, at every council he laid out plans as though he had long practiced the art; Governor-General Zha Lang'a held him in the highest regard. He served as acting administrative commissioner of the Liangzhuang Circuit. When the army passed through Liangzhou, orders arrived to provision them at Suzhou. Both routes were distant; Shijian supplied the troops from Liangzhou's stores, and the army marched without shortage. One day urgent dispatches arrived in quick succession; Shijian surmised that fresh troops would be called up and prepared rations in advance. Events proved him right. At Suzhou, as the army was about to march, urgent orders came to seize all private and official mules and horses, throwing officials and commoners into panic. Shijian said: "Officials and merchants on the road are all bound for Suzhou—if we halt them en route and goods are abandoned along the way, that will not serve the army." The campaign has no fixed date—why not let them reach Suzhou, unload their goods, and then provide transport? The front gains both manpower and goods while sparing the trouble of hauling fodder over long distances—both sides benefit. Though the order is strict, I will bear the responsibility myself. Officials and merchants were reassured, and military operations proceeded without disruption.
11
尋調補西安鹽驛道副使。 關中旱，詔以湖廣米十萬石自商州龍駒寨運陝西。 師儉董其役，未半，大雨谿漲，騾馬少，不足供轉輸。 商於山中無頓積所，水次隘，運艘不齊。 師儉以秋穀將登，請止運，民亦不飢。 軍中馬缺，檄取驛馬。 師儉謂：「置郵傳命，如人身血脈，不能一日廢。 」拒不可，事竟寢。
He was soon transferred to fill the post of Xi'an Salt and Relay vice commissioner. Guanzhong was stricken by drought; an edict ordered one hundred thousand shi of Huguang grain transported from Shangzhou's Longjuzhai to Shaanxi. Shijian directed the transport; before it was half done, heavy rains swelled the streams, mules were scarce, and delivery fell short. In the mountains of Shangyu there were no depots for stockpiling; river landings were cramped and transport boats did not arrive together. Shijian noted that the autumn harvest was near and requested that transport be halted—the people would not go hungry. The army was short of horses; orders came to requisition relay horses. Shijian said: "The postal relay system is like the blood vessels of the body—it cannot be suspended for a single day." He refused; the matter was dropped.
12
擢按察使，數平疑獄。 吏有故入人罪者，必按如法，雖貴勢賢親不徇縱。 十三年，以目疾乞休。 高宗即位，赦詔至，時目已失明，令吏誦案牘，諦聽，得邀赦典者，立出之而後上陳。 留兩月，畢其事始歸。 後卒於家，陝西祀名宦。
Promoted to surveillance commissioner, he repeatedly resolved doubtful cases. Officials who falsely implicated others were punished according to law; even the powerful and well-connected were not spared. In the thirteenth year he requested retirement on account of eye disease. When Gaozong ascended the throne and the amnesty edict arrived, he was already blind; he had clerks read out the case documents, listened intently, immediately released those eligible for amnesty, and only then reported upward. He remained two months until all matters were concluded before returning home. He later died at home and was enshrined among celebrated local officials in Shaanxi.
13
唐繼祖，字序皇，江南江都人。 康熙六十年進士，選庶吉士。 雍正元年，散館，授編修，轉禮部員外郎。 五年，考選浙江道御史。 七年，授工科給事中。 命察八旗虧帑，律侵挪皆不赦，犯者貧，羈獄二三十年不結。 繼祖為核減開除，奏請豁免，積牘一清。 命巡西城，回民聚居，頑獷不法，嚴治之，有犯必懲，悉斂戢。 建倉東便門外，多發塚墓，毀祠宇，繼祖陳其不便，改地營建，塚墓祠宇並修復。 南漕愆期，命赴淮安巡視。 繼祖馳至，不更張成法，惟選幹吏催督，懲其疲惰。 兩閱月，糧艘悉抵通州。 條上漕務利病，下部議行。
Tang Jizu, styled Xuhuang, came from Jiangdu in Jiangnan. He received his jinshi degree in the sixtieth year of Kangxi and entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor. In the first year of Yongzheng he completed his Hanlin term, was appointed a compiler, and transferred to a vice directorship in the Ministry of Rites. In the fifth year he was examined and selected as censor of the Zhejiang Circuit. In the seventh year he was appointed supervising secretary of the Ministry of Works. He was ordered to investigate deficits in the Eight Banners' treasuries; the law held that embezzlement was unpardonable, yet offenders were often destitute and languished in prison twenty or thirty years without resolution. Jizu audited the reductions and dismissals, memorialized for exemptions, and cleared the backlog of cases entirely. Ordered to patrol the western quarter, where Hui communities were concentrated and notoriously unruly, he enforced the law strictly, punishing every offense until all were brought to heel. Plans to build a granary outside the East Convenient Gate would have opened many graves and destroyed shrines; Jizu memorialized against it, the site was moved, and the graves and shrines were restored. When southern tribute grain fell behind schedule, he was ordered to Huai'an to oversee transport. Jizu hurried there without changing established procedures, but selected capable officers to press the work forward and punished the negligent. Within two months every grain transport had reached Tongzhou. He submitted a detailed memorial on the strengths and weaknesses of the grain transport system, which the ministry adopted.
14
七年，命往湖南讞獄，並巡察湖南、湖北兩省，裹糧出，有餽觴酒豆肉，皆卻之，令行禁止。 與巡撫趙申喬同按永順苗變獄，群情帖服，苗疆以安。 湖南捕役多通盜，奏請捕役為盜，加重治罪，報可，入新例。 八年，擢通政司參議。 九年，擢鴻臚寺卿。 尋命以本銜署河南按察使，旋授湖北按察使。 繼祖在兩湖久，熟知吏民情偽。 楚俗刁健，黠吏與姦豪通，伺官喜怒，訟益難治。 繼祖閉諸胥於一室，不令與外通，訟風衰減。 雪監利女子冤獄，按鍾祥民變，皆為時所稱。 世宗馭吏嚴，內外大僚凜凜，救過不暇。 繼祖一意展舒，所陳奏無不允。 上欲大用之，出巡察，賜以摺匣，許奏事，曰：「朕於督撫賢者始賜摺匣，汝宜好為之！ 」調江西，未之任，以疾乞歸。 病癒將出，遽卒。
In the seventh year he was sent to Hunan to adjudicate cases and inspect both Hunan and Hubei; he traveled on his own provisions, refused every gift of food or drink, and issued orders banning such practices. Together with Governor Zhao Shenqiao he investigated the Yongshun Miao uprising; the people were satisfied and the Miao frontier was pacified. Hunan constables often colluded with bandits; he memorialized that constables who turned bandit should face heavier penalties; the court approved, and it entered the new code. In the eighth year he was promoted to vice commissioner of the Office of Transmission. In the ninth year he was promoted to director of the Court of Imperial Entertainments. He was soon ordered to act as Henan surveillance commissioner at his current rank, then appointed surveillance commissioner of Hubei. Having long served in the two Hu provinces, Jizu knew the officials and people through and through. Hubei folk were litigious and sharp; cunning clerks colluded with local strongmen, watching officials' moods, and lawsuits grew ever harder to settle. Jizu confined all clerks in one room, cut off their outside contacts, and the culture of litigation subsided. He cleared a wrongful conviction of a woman in Jianli and investigated the popular disturbance at Zhongxiang—both won wide praise. The Yongzheng Emperor ruled officials with iron discipline; high officers inside and outside the capital lived in dread, scrambling merely to cover their mistakes. Jizu alone acted with confidence and ease; every memorial he submitted won approval. The emperor intended to promote him further; when he went out on inspection he was given a memorial folder and permission to report directly, saying: "I grant the memorial folder only to worthy governors—you must make good use of it!" He was transferred to Jiangxi but never took up the post, citing illness to return home. Just as he recovered and was preparing to leave, he died suddenly.
15
馬維翰，字墨麟，浙江海鹽人。 康熙六十年進士。 雍正元年，授吏部主事。 甫視事，杖姦胥，銓政清肅。 轉員外郎，考選陝西道御史，遷工科給事中，監督倉場，所至有聲。 六年，命赴四川清丈田畝，時同奉使者四。 維翰分赴建昌道屬，具有條理，糧浮於田者必請減，逾年事竣。 御史吳濤在川東丈田不實，以維翰助之。 至則發其弊，遂以維翰代任。 巡撫憲德薦可大用。 八年，留補建昌道副使，疏陳二事：四川俗好訟，州縣斷獄苟簡，案牘不具，奸民輒翻控，淆亂是非，請設幕職以襄治理； 又民鮮土著，多結草屋，輕於遷徙，焚劫輒致災，請發官款造磚甓，勸民多建瓦屋。 上斥其非政要，以其疏示憲德，謂：「汝薦可大用者乃若此！ 」然維翰勇於任事，相度要害，改黎州千戶所設清溪縣。 烏蒙苗亂，出師會剿，維翰治軍需，供糗糧芻茭，鑿雪通道，與廝卒同甘苦。 論剿撫悉中機宜，事乃定。 涼山地震數百里，勘災散賑，民感之。 礦廠擾蠻，起為亂，方進剿。 維翰力陳營兵不戢及各廠病蠻狀，請罷廠撤兵，撫各番，止誅其魁。
Ma Weihan, styled Molin, came from Haiyan in Zhejiang. He received his jinshi degree in the sixtieth year of Kangxi. In the first year of Yongzheng he was appointed a principal secretary in the Ministry of Personnel. As soon as he took office he had corrupt clerks beaten, and personnel administration became scrupulously clean. Promoted to vice director, then examined and selected as Shaanxi Circuit censor, then supervising secretary of the Ministry of Works supervising the granaries—wherever he served he won renown. In the sixth year he was ordered to Sichuan to conduct a land survey; four commissioners were sent on the mission together. Weihan took charge of the Jianchang Circuit jurisdiction, worked methodically, always sought reductions where tax exceeded registered land, and finished within a year. Censor Wu Tao had falsified the land survey in eastern Sichuan and tried to implicate Weihan as his accomplice. When Weihan arrived he exposed the fraud, and was appointed to take over Wu's duties. Governor Xiande recommended him for higher appointment. In the eighth year he was retained as Jianchang Circuit vice commissioner and memorialized on two matters: Sichuan was litigious; prefectures and counties judged cases carelessly with incomplete records, allowing scoundrels to appeal repeatedly and confuse right and wrong—he proposed establishing staff posts to assist governance; Moreover few people were long-settled natives; most lived in thatched huts and moved readily—fires and raids brought frequent disaster—he proposed official funds to manufacture bricks and tiles and encourage the people to build tile-roofed houses. The emperor dismissed these as peripheral to governance, showed the memorial to Xiande, and said: "This is the man you recommended for great things!" Yet Weihan was bold in action; surveying strategic points, he converted the Lizhou guard post into Qingxi County. When the Wumeng Miao rebelled and troops were dispatched, Weihan managed military logistics, supplied rations and fodder, cut paths through snow, and shared hardship with the rank and file. His counsel on suppression and pacification proved sound, and the affair was settled. An earthquake in Liangshan devastated hundreds of li; he surveyed the damage and distributed relief, winning the people's gratitude. Mining operations provoked the tribes into rebellion, and a punitive campaign was under way. Weihan strongly memorialized on undisciplined soldiers and the tribes' suffering at each mine, urging closure of the mines, withdrawal of troops, pacification of each tribe, and punishment of only the ringleaders.
16
在川七年，不阿上官。 旋被構，維翰揭部請解職赴質。 時親王總部事，特威重，捽使免冠。 維翰以手按冠抗聲曰：「奉旨不免冠！ 」譙問故，則又抗聲曰：「旨解職，非革職也！ 」部乃疏請奪官。 事旋白。 乾隆二年，起授江南常鎮道參議。 丁父憂，歸，卒於家。
During seven years in Sichuan he never curried favor with his superiors. He was soon framed; Weihan petitioned the ministry to resign his post and come to the capital to answer the charges. A prince then oversaw ministry affairs with exceptional authority and had him seized and stripped of his official cap. Weihan held his cap in place and protested loudly: "By imperial decree I am not required to remove my cap!" When rebuked and asked why, he protested again: "The decree was to resign my post, not to strip me of rank!" The ministry then memorialized to strip him of his rank. The matter was soon cleared. In the second year of Qianlong he was recalled and appointed administrative commissioner of the Jiangnan Changzhen Circuit. After his father's death he returned home and died there.
17
餘甸，字田生，福建福清人。 康熙四十五年進士。 居鄉勵名節，巡撫張伯行重之，延主鼇峰書院。 授四川江津知縣，民投牒者，片言立決遣，訟為之簡。 日與諸生誦說文藝，疏解性理。 所徵賦即儲庫，不入私室。 時青海用兵，巡撫年羹堯督餉，多額外急徵，檄再三至，甸不應。 乃使僕持檄告諭，自朝至晡，甸不出，使者譁。 甸坐堂皇，命反接，將杖之，丞簿力為請，久之乃釋其縛。 越日，使者索檄，甸曰：「汝還報，我閉門待劾，檄已達京師矣。 」羹堯亦置之。 行取吏部主事，時尚書張鵬翮、侍郎湯右曾皆以幹濟名，甸遇當爭辯者，侃侃無所撓。 主選三年，權要富人請託多格不行。 將告歸，條文書已駁議未奏者十餘事，曰：「此皆作姦巧法易為所蒙，必上聞，吾乃去。 」父憂免喪，猶廬墓。
Yu Dian, styled Tiansheng, came from Fuqing in Fujian. He received his jinshi degree in the forty-fifth year of Kangxi. At home he cultivated reputation and integrity; Governor Zhang Boxing esteemed him and invited him to head the Aofeng Academy. Appointed magistrate of Jiangjin in Sichuan, he settled petitions in a few words and dismissed them at once, greatly reducing litigation. Each day he lectured students on literature and expounded moral philosophy. Taxes collected went straight to the public treasury, never into his private quarters. Qinghai was then at war; Governor Nian Gengyao supervised supplies and imposed many extra urgent levies—orders arrived repeatedly, but Dian refused to comply. Nian then sent a servant with orders; from morning until evening Dian did not emerge, and the messenger raised an uproar. Dian sat in the main hall, ordered the messenger bound and was about to have him beaten; his deputies pleaded earnestly, and only after a long delay were the bonds removed. The next day the messenger demanded the order back; Dian said: "Go report that I have shut my doors awaiting impeachment—the order has already reached the capital." Gengyao let the matter drop as well. Selected for the Ministry of Personnel as principal secretary; Minister Zhang Penghe and Vice Minister Tang Youzeng were both famed for capability—when debate was needed, Dian spoke forthrightly and would not yield. He oversaw appointments for three years, rejecting most requests from the powerful and wealthy. About to retire, he listed more than ten documents already rejected in deliberation but not yet submitted, saying: "These devise crafty loopholes easily exploited—I must report them before I leave." After his mourning period ended, he still kept vigil at his father's tomb.
18
以河道總督陳鵬年疏薦，擢山東兗寧道。 釐工剔弊，一袪積習，甚得士民心。 鵬年卒，齊蘇勒為河督，以工事劾甸，行河至濟寧，士民群聚乞還甸。 齊蘇勒疏陳，召入見。 雍正二年四月，授山東按察使。 攜二僕，買驢之官，務崇禮教，輕刑罰，政化大行。 十一月，召詣京師。 三年，擢順天府丞。
On Grand Canal Governor Chen Pengnian's recommendation he was promoted to the Shandong Yanning Circuit. He regulated public works and eliminated abuses, sweeping away entrenched customs and winning the hearts of gentry and commoners alike. When Pengnian died, Qisuole became canal governor and impeached Dian over engineering works; as he traveled the canal to Jining, scholars and commoners gathered begging for Dian's return. Qisuole memorialized on the matter, and Dian was summoned to court. In the fourth month of the second year of Yongzheng he was appointed Shandong surveillance commissioner. He traveled with two servants on a donkey he had bought, promoted ritual education, lightened punishments, and his civilizing influence spread widely. In the eleventh month he was summoned to the capital. In the third year he was promoted to vice director of the Shuntian Prefecture.
19
甸歷官盡革陋規，為按察使，愍囚不能自衣食，取鹽商歲饋三之一以資給之。 兼完囹圄，修學宮、書院，委有司出入註籍。 既去官，上命內閣學士繆沅清察山東鹽政諸弊，舉是劾甸，奪官，歸。 甸用唐人詩語為人書楹帖，其人有怨家，訐於有司，以為怨望。 有司以甸所書也，並下甸於獄。 事白，遽卒。
In every post Dian abolished abusive customs; as surveillance commissioner, pitying prisoners who could not feed themselves, he took one-third of the salt merchants' annual gifts to support them. He also repaired the prisons, restored the Confucian temple and academy, and had officials register all entries and exits. After he left office the emperor ordered Grand Secretariat academician Miu Yuan to investigate Shandong salt administration; citing this matter, Dian was impeached, stripped of rank, and sent home. Dian had written couplets for someone using lines from Tang poetry; the man's enemy reported to officials that the verses expressed disloyal resentment. Because Dian had written the couplets, officials imprisoned him as well. When the matter was cleared, he died suddenly.
20
王葉滋，字槐青，江南華亭人。 弱冠，補諸生。 浙江巡撫硃軾闢佐幕，器其才。 雍正元年，重開明史館，軾薦之，引見稱旨，命入館纂修。 舉順天鄉試。 福敏督湖廣，世宗命葉滋往贊其幕。 五年，應禮部試，甫畢，上召見，問湖廣吏治、民生利弊，奏對甚悉，趣馳傳還湖廣。 榜發中式，未與殿試，賜二甲進士，即授常德知府。 常德例，知府至，行戶更新照，規費四千金，葉滋革其例。 境數被水災，請帑增築花貓新陂堤堰，豁被水荒田額糧，民德之。 辰州關木稅為利藪，時議移關常德，葉滋恐累民，拒之，請仍舊制。 行法不避豪貴，興學造士，薦舉優行諸生陳悌為武平知縣，貴金馬為上蔡知縣，劉樵為清平知縣，並為良吏。
Wang Yezi, styled Huaiqing, came from Huating in Jiangnan. In his early twenties he became a licentiate. Zhejiang Governor Zhu Shi invited him into his staff and valued his talent. In the first year of Yongzheng the Ming History Office reopened; Zhu recommended him; the emperor received him favorably and ordered him to join the compilation staff. He passed the Shuntian provincial examination. Fu Min was appointed governor-general of Huguang; the Yongzheng Emperor ordered Yezi to assist his staff. In the fifth year he took the Ministry of Rites examination; as soon as it ended the emperor summoned him, questioned him on Huguang administration and the people's welfare, and he answered in thorough detail; the emperor urged him to return swiftly to Huguang. When the results were published he had passed; without taking the palace examination he was granted second-rank jinshi and immediately appointed prefect of Changde. By Changde custom, when a new prefect arrived the merchant guilds paid four thousand taels to renew their licenses; Yezi abolished the practice. The district had suffered repeated floods; he requested funds to enlarge the Huamao and Xinpi dikes and remitted grain tax on flooded wasteland—the people were grateful. The Chenzhou Pass timber tax was a lucrative source; some proposed moving the pass to Changde; Yezi feared burdening the people, refused, and urged keeping the old arrangement. He enforced the law without fear of the powerful, promoted learning, and recommended exemplary students Chen Ti for Wuping, Gui Jinma for Shangcai, and Liu Qiao for Qingping—all proved capable magistrates.
21
署岳州、辰州二府，攝岳常道副使。 久之，授辰沅靖道副使。 時苗疆初闢，清林箐，增汛堠，規模肅然。 所屬綏寧、城步與黔疆犬牙錯。 嘗率數騎，持酒肉鹽菸，循行苗砦。 群苗迎拜，謂「上官親我」。 召諸頭人集校場，賜花紅銀牌，宣上德意，勸以禮義。 因偕總兵閱兵耀軍容，群苗帖服。 署按察使，調糧儲道，舊有漕費，悉歸公用。 值貴州苗亂，師進剿，葉滋駐辰州治軍需，剋期辦。 綏寧苗蠢動，為貴州苗應。 葉滋條上剿撫事，悉中窾要。 大吏令駐綏寧指揮，積勞疾作，卒於山中。
He served as acting prefect of Yuezhou and Chenzhou and held the Yuechang Circuit vice commissioner post. After long service he was appointed Chenyuanjing Circuit vice commissioner. The Miao frontier had only recently been opened; he cleared forested ravines, increased guard posts, and established an orderly system. Suining and Chengbu under his jurisdiction bordered Guizhou in a tangled checkerboard of territories. He once led a few horsemen with wine, meat, salt, and tobacco on a tour of Miao stockades. The Miao came out to welcome him, saying, "Our superior treats us like family." He summoned the headmen to the drill ground, bestowed brocade and silver plaques, proclaimed the emperor's benevolence, and exhorted them in ritual and righteousness. Then with the regional commander he reviewed the troops to display military might, and the Miao submitted. He served as acting surveillance commissioner and was transferred to the grain storage circuit; former transport fees were all directed to public use. When the Guizhou Miao rebelled and troops marched to suppress them, Yezi remained at Chenzhou managing military logistics and met every deadline. The Suining Miao stirred in response to the Guizhou uprising. Yezi submitted a detailed memorial on suppression and pacification that hit every essential point. Senior officials ordered him to take command at Suining; worn out by accumulated labor, he fell ill and died in the mountains.
22
葉滋初以文學受知，及官於外，所至有聲績。 卒時年僅五十五，世咸惜之。
Yezi first won notice through his literary accomplishments; in every post he held abroad he earned renown for solid achievement. He died at only fifty-five, and all who knew of him mourned the loss.
23
劉而位，字爾爵，山西汾陽人。 康熙五十二年舉人，授河南安陽知縣。 有兄弟爭產構訟十餘年者，為據理剖解，至淚下，皆叩頭求罷，案牘遂稀。 雍正中，遷福建泉州知府，再遷興泉道參議。 鹽政窳敝，商居奇索高直，民苦淡食，不獲已，增價以市。 既而鹽不足，民惡其壟斷，聚而毆之。 海舶私梟動逾千百，往捕則持械拒，大獄迭興，羅織牽連，數歲不息。 而位創議裁引革商，歲額課稅歸灶完納，如農完賦，任人轉運，聽其所之，則諸弊可革而國賦不乏。 巡撫趙國麟心韙之，格於例不行。 未幾，引疾歸。 乾隆三年，起官四川鹽茶道副使。 蜀鹽產於井，課由井納，民便之。 雍正中有請設引招商增課者，四川鹽政自此壞。 商無餘貲，運不足額，民持錢不得鹽，而井鹽滯積不售，因以致訌。 而位欲事釐剔，​​大吏畏難不可，力爭，愈嫉之。 改松茂道，調永寧道參議。 居常鬱鬱，不得行其志，惟與諸生講學。 尋卒於官。
Liu Erwei, styled Erjue, came from Fenyang in Shanxi. A provincial graduate in the fifty-second year of Kangxi, he was appointed magistrate of Anyang in Henan. Two brothers had sued over an inheritance for more than ten years; he judged the case on its merits until both wept, kowtowed, and begged to withdraw—and litigation in his yamen grew scarce. Under Yongzheng he was transferred to prefect of Quanzhou in Fujian, then to administrative commissioner of the Xingquan Circuit. Salt administration had grown corrupt; merchants hoarded salt and demanded exorbitant prices, and the people, unable to obtain enough, were forced to pay more. When salt then ran short, the people, resenting the monopoly, gathered to beat the merchants. Private smugglers on seagoing vessels often numbered more than a thousand; when pursued they resisted with arms; major cases arose one after another with sprawling indictments, and the turmoil continued for years. Erwei proposed abolishing transport licenses and licensed merchants, having annual tax quotas paid directly at the salterns as farmers paid land tax, with free transport wherever one chose—thus eliminating abuses while state revenue would not suffer. Governor Zhao Guolin approved in principle, but existing regulations prevented implementation. Before long he cited illness and retired home. In the third year of Qianlong he was recalled as Sichuan Salt and Tea Circuit vice commissioner. Sichuan salt came from brine wells, with tax paid at the wells—a system convenient for the people. Under Yongzheng some proposed establishing transport licenses and recruiting merchant contractors to increase revenue—and Sichuan salt administration was ruined from that point on. Merchants lacked capital, deliveries fell short of quotas, people with money could not obtain salt, well salt piled up unsold, and disturbances broke out. Erwei wished to undertake thorough reform, but senior officials, fearing the difficulty, refused; he argued fiercely, and they resented him all the more. He was transferred to the Songmao Circuit, then to administrative commissioner of the Yongning Circuit. He lived in chronic frustration, unable to carry out his aims, and devoted himself to lecturing students. He soon died in office.
24
而位生平服膺王守仁，曰：「尊所聞，行所知，須不流於弊。 尊陽明而不知其流弊，非善學陽明； 尊硃子而不知其流弊，亦非善學硃子。 」蓋謂王氏高明，弊在躐等； 硃子格物，弊恐拘而不化。 著省克引、劉氏家訓，為學者所稱。
Erwei devoted himself to Wang Shouren, saying: "Honor what you have learned and practice what you know—you must not sink into its abuses." To honor Yangming without understanding its abuses is not to learn Yangming well; to honor Master Zhu without understanding its abuses is likewise not to learn Zhu well. He meant that Wang's teaching was sublime, but its abuse lay in skipping steps; Zhu's investigation of things—he feared its abuse was rigidity without true transformation. He wrote Reflections on Self-Examination and the Liu Family Instructions, both praised by scholars.
25
論曰：起元深於經術，當朝政尚嚴，能持以平恕。 師儉以勤敏，繼祖以明肅，並見重於時。 維翰有幹局，甸尤能澤以儒效。 葉滋撫循苗疆，未竟其用。 而位議變鹽法，亦不得申其志，而但以學術名。 國家重視監司，所以擴循良之績，儲封疆之選，若諸人者，可謂無忝矣。
The commentary says: Qiyuan was steeped in the classics; when court policy favored severity, he could still hold to fairness and leniency. Shijian for diligence and alertness, Jizu for clarity and severity—both were highly regarded in their day. Weihan had a talent for large undertakings; Dian above all could enrich governance with Confucian example. Yezi won over the Miao frontier but died before his full potential was realized. Erwei proposed reforming the salt law but likewise could not carry out his aims, and was known chiefly for his scholarship. The state values circuit intendants to extend the achievements of worthy officials and prepare selections for frontier posts; men such as these may truly be said not to have disgraced their offices.
