1
孫家鼐，字燮臣，安徽壽州人。 咸豐九年一甲一名進士，授修撰。 歷侍讀，入直上書房。 光緒四年，命在毓慶宮行走，與尚書翁同龢授上讀。 累遷內閣學士，擢工部侍郎。 江西學政陳寶琛疏請以先儒黃宗羲、顧炎武從祀文廟，議者多以爲未可，家鼐與潘祖蔭、翁同龢、孫詒經等再請，始議准。 十六年，授都察院左都御史、工部尚書，兼順天府尹。
Sun Jianai, whose style was Xiecheng, came from Shouzhou in Anhui. In Xianfeng 9 he topped the first class of jinshi graduates and was made a Compiler. He rose through the post of Reader-in-Waiting and joined the Southern Study. In Guangxu 4 he was assigned to the Palace of Cherished Growth, where he and Minister Weng Tonghe tutored the heir apparent. He was promoted step by step to Grand Secretariat academician and then Vice Minister of Works. When Jiangxi education commissioner Chen Baochen asked that Huang Zongxi and Gu Yanwu be enshrined in the Confucian temple, most officials objected; Jianai joined Pan Zuyin, Weng Tonghe, Sun Yijing, and others in pressing the request again until it was approved. In the sixteenth year he became Left Censor-in-Chief and Minister of Works, while also serving as Shuntian metropolitan magistrate.
2
二十年，中日事起，朝議主戰，家鼐力言釁不可啓。 二十四年，以吏部尚書協辦大學士。 命爲管學大臣。 時方議變法，廢科舉，興學校，設報編書，皆特交核覆，家鼐一裁以正。 嘗疏謂：「國家廣集卿士以資議政，聽言固不厭求詳，然執兩用中，精擇審處，尤賴聖知。 」其所建議，類能持大體。 及議廢立，家鼐獨持不可。 旋以病乞罷。
When the Sino-Japanese crisis broke out in the twentieth year, the court leaned toward war, but Jianai insisted that hostilities must not be opened. In the twenty-fourth year he became assisting Grand Secretary while retaining the Ministry of Personnel. He was placed in charge of education as Superintendent of Education. As the court debated abolishing the examinations, founding schools, launching newspapers, and commissioning new books, each proposal was sent to him for review, and he judged every one on its merits. In one memorial he wrote: "The state gathers eminent ministers to deliberate on policy, and it is right to hear every argument in full; yet to hold competing views in balance, choose wisely, and decide carefully depends above all on the emperor's discernment." His recommendations, as a rule, kept sight of the larger interest of the state. When the court debated deposing and replacing the emperor, he alone opposed the move. He soon sought retirement, pleading illness.
3
二十六年，乘輿西狩，召赴行在，起禮部尚書。 還京，拜體仁閣大學士。 歷轉東閣、文淵閣，晉武英殿。 充學務大臣，裁度規章，折衷中外，嚴定宗旨，一以敦行實學爲主，學風爲之一靖。 議改官制，命與慶親王奕劻、軍機大臣瞿鴻禨總司核定。 御史趙啓霖劾奕劻及其子貝子載振受賄納優，命醇親王載灃與家鼐往按，啓霖坐汚衊親貴褫職，而載振尋亦乞罷兼官。 資政院立，命貝子溥倫及家鼐爲總裁，一持正議不阿。 時詔諸臣輪班進講，家鼐撰尚書四子書講義以進。 三十四年二月，以鄉舉重逢，賞太子太傅。 歷蒙賜「壽」，頒賞御書及諸珍品，賜紫韁，紫禁城內坐二人暖輪，恩遇優渥。 宣統元年，再疏乞病，溫詔慰留。 尋卒，年八十有二，贈太傅，諡文正。
In the twenty-sixth year, after the court fled west, he was called to the traveling palace and restored as Minister of Rites. After the return to Beijing he was made Grand Secretary of the Hall of Embodied Benevolence. He moved from the Eastern and Literary Deep pavilions up to the Martial Eminence Hall. As Minister of Education he drafted regulations that balanced Chinese and Western learning, set a firm course centered on practical study, and brought the schools back under control. During the debate over bureaucratic reform he was ordered, together with Prince Qing Yikuang and Grand Councillor Qu Hongji, to oversee the final review. When Censor Zhao Qilin accused Yikuang and his son Beile Zai Zhen of bribery and keeping actors, Prince Chun Zaifeng and Jianai were sent to investigate; Qilin was stripped of office for defaming the imperial clan, and Zai Zhen soon resigned his extra posts as well. When the Political Consultative Council was founded, he and Beile Pulun were named its presidents, and he refused to trim his views to please anyone. When the throne ordered ministers to lecture in turn, he prepared commentaries on the Documents and the Four Books and presented them. In the second month of the thirty-fourth year, when the provincial examinations came round again, he was granted the title Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent. He was repeatedly honored with the character for longevity, imperial calligraphy, rare gifts, the purple bridle, and a heated two-seat carriage inside the Forbidden City—favors seldom bestowed so lavishly. In Xuantong 1 he again asked to retire on grounds of illness, but a gracious edict urged him to stay. He died soon after, at eighty-two, and was posthumously honored as Grand Tutor with the posthumous title Wenzheng.
4
家鼐簡約斂退，生平無疾言遽色。 雖貴，與諸生鈞禮。 閉門齋居，雜賓遠跡，推避權勢若怯。 嘗督湖北學政，典山西試，再典順天試，總裁會試，屢充閱卷大臣，獨無所私。 嘗拔一卷廁二甲，同列意不可，即屏退之，其讓不喜競類此。 器量尤廣，庚子，外人請懲禍首戮大臣，編修劉廷琛謂失國體，責宰輔不能爭，家鼐揖而引過。 其後詔舉御史。 家鼐獨保廷琛，謂曩以大義見責，知忠鯁必不負國，世皆稱之。
Jianai lived plainly and quietly, and never spoke sharply or showed sudden anger. Even at the height of his rank he treated students as equals. He kept to his study, turned casual visitors away, and shunned powerful patrons as though he were afraid of them. He had supervised education in Hubei, run the Shanxi and twice the Shuntian provincial exams, presided over the metropolitan examination, and served many times as chief reader—never once showing favoritism. Once he ranked a candidate in the second tier of the second class; when colleagues objected he withdrew the placement at once—he was always ready to yield and disliked contention. He was remarkably magnanimous. In 1900, when the powers demanded that ringleaders and senior ministers be punished, Compiler Liu Tingchen protested that this would humiliate the state and blamed the chief ministers for not resisting; Jianai bowed and accepted the reproach as his own fault. Later the throne ordered nominations for censorial posts. Jianai alone recommended Liu Tingchen, saying that a man who had once been rebuked for upholding the national interest would surely prove loyal; contemporaries praised the gesture widely.
5
張百熙，字埜秋，長沙人。 同治十三年進士，授編修。 督山東學政，典試四川。 命直南書房，再遷侍讀。
Zhang Baixi, whose style was Ye Qiu, came from Changsha. He passed the jinshi examination in Tongzhi 13 and was appointed Compiler. He supervised education in Shandong and ran the Sichuan provincial examination. He entered the Southern Study and was soon promoted again to Reader-in-Waiting.
6
光緒二十年，朝鮮釁起，朝議多主戰。 百熙疏劾李鴻章陽作戰備，陰實主和，左寶貴、聶士成皆勇敢善戰之將，以餉械不繼，遂致敗績，咎在鴻章； 又劾禮親王世鐸筦樞務，招權納賄，戰事起，一倚鴻章，貽誤兵機：皆不報。 時值太后萬壽，承辦典禮者猶競尚華飾，百熙奏罷之。 復偕侍講學士陸寶忠等合彈樞臣朋比誤國十大罪。 未幾，孫毓汶引疾歸，恭親王奕訢復入軍機，而百熙亦出督廣東學政。 累遷內閣學士。 二十四年，坐濫舉康有爲，革職留任。 二十六年，授禮部侍郎，擢左都御史，充頭等專使大臣。 拳匪亂定，下詔求言，百熙抗疏陳大計，請改官制，理財政，變科舉，建學堂，設報館。 明年，遷工部尚書，調刑部，充管學大臣。
In Guangxu 20, when conflict broke out in Korea, most at court favored war. Baixi impeached Li Hongzhang for feigning war preparations while secretly favoring peace; he argued that generals Zuo Baogui and Nie Shicheng were brave and capable but had been defeated for lack of supplies, and that the fault lay with Li Hongzhang; he also accused Prince Li Shiduo of monopolizing Grand Council power, taking bribes, and leaving military affairs entirely to Li Hongzhang—all to no response from the throne. When officials were still competing to lavish ornament on the Empress Dowager's birthday celebrations, Baixi persuaded the court to cancel them. He then joined Expositor Lu Baozhong and others in charging the Grand Council with ten crimes of factional collusion that had harmed the state. Soon Sun Yuwen retired for illness, Prince Gong Yixin returned to the Grand Council, and Baixi was sent out to supervise education in Guangdong. He was promoted step by step to Grand Secretariat academician. In the twenty-fourth year he was stripped of rank but kept at his post for having indiscriminately recommended Kang Youwei. In the twenty-sixth year he became Vice Minister of Rites, then Left Censor-in-Chief, and served as a first-rank special envoy. After the Boxer uprising was suppressed, the throne called for memorials; Baixi laid out a sweeping program to reform the bureaucracy, overhaul finances, abolish the examinations, build schools, and found newspapers. The next year he became Minister of Works, was transferred to Punishments, and was named Superintendent of Education.
7
京師之有大學堂也，始於中日戰後。 侍郎李端棻奏請立學，中旨報可，而樞府厭言新政，請緩行。 迄戊戌，乃奉嚴旨，促擬學章，命孫家鼐爲管學大臣。 及政變，惟大學以萌芽早得不廢。 許景澄繼管學，坐論義和團被誅。 兩宮西幸，百熙詣行在，以人望被斯任，於是海內欣然望興學矣。 百熙奏加冀州知州吳汝綸五品卿銜，總教大學。 汝綸辭不應，百熙具衣冠拜之，汝綸請赴日本察視學務。 大學教職員皆自聘，又薪金優厚，忌嫉者衆，蜚語浸聞。 汝綸返國，未至京，卒； 而百熙所倚以辦學者，門人沈兆祉亦受讒搆。 大學既負時謗，言官奏稱本朝定制，部官大率滿、漢相維，請更設滿大臣主教事，乃增命榮慶爲管學大臣。 旋別設學務處，以張亨嘉爲大學總監督，百熙權益分。 始議分建七科大學，又選派諸生游學東西洋。 榮慶意不謂可，而百熙持之堅，親至站送諸生登車。 各省之派官費生自此始。 值張之洞入覲，命改定學章，及還鎮，復命家鼐爲管學大臣。 凡三管學，百熙位第三矣。 百熙擬建分科大學，以絀於貲而止，惟創醫學及譯學館、實業館，遽謝學務。 賞黃馬褂、紫禁城騎馬。 後歷禮部、戶部、郵傳部尚書，政務、學務、編纂官制諸大臣。 卒，贈太子少保，諡文達。
Beijing's Imperial University dated from the aftermath of the Sino-Japanese War. Vice Minister Li Duanfen proposed founding the school and received imperial approval, but the Grand Council, hostile to reform, asked that it be postponed. Not until 1898 did a stern edict order school regulations drafted at once and Sun Jianai named Superintendent of Education. After the coup of 1898 only the university survived, because it had been founded early enough to take root. Xu Jingcheng succeeded him as superintendent but was executed for his views on the Boxers. When the court fled west, Baixi went to the traveling palace; his reputation won him the post, and the country looked to him to revive education with fresh hope. Baixi asked that Jizhou prefect Wu Rulun be given fifth-rank honorary rank and placed in overall charge of instruction at the university. Wu Rulun declined; Baixi dressed in full court robes and bowed to him in person; Rulun then asked leave to inspect schools in Japan. Because he hired the faculty himself and paid generous salaries, envy spread and rumors began to reach the throne. Wu Rulun died on his way back to Beijing before he could arrive; and Shen Zhaozhi, the disciple on whom Baixi had relied to run the schools, also fell victim to slander. With the university under attack, censors argued that ministry posts should alternate between Manchu and Han officials and asked for a Manchu minister to share control; Rongqing was therefore added as co-superintendent. Soon a separate Office of Educational Affairs was created, Zhang Hengjia was named overall university supervisor, and Baixi's authority was further reduced. He first proposed seven specialized universities and began selecting students to study abroad in Europe and Japan. Rongqing disapproved, but Baixi held firm and went in person to the station to see the students off. This marked the beginning of provincial government-funded study abroad. When Zhang Zhidong came to court he was told to revise the regulations; after he returned to his post Sun Jianai was again named superintendent. There had been three superintendents in all, and Baixi was the third. Baixi planned specialized universities but stopped for lack of funds; he founded only medical, translation, and industrial schools, then soon left educational affairs. He was granted the yellow jacket and the privilege of riding inside the Forbidden City. He later headed the Ministries of Rites, Revenue, and Posts and Communications, and served on the boards for government affairs, education, and bureaucratic reform. He died and was posthumously honored as Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent with the posthumous title Wenda.
8
唐景崇，字春卿，廣西灌陽人。 父懋功，舉人，有學行。 景崇，同治十年進士，授編修。 由侍讀四遷至內閣學士。 光緒二十年，典試廣東。 明年，主會試。 歷兵部、禮部侍郎，權左都御史，出督浙江學政，母憂歸。 拳禍起，命督辦廣西團練。 二十九年，以工部侍郎典試浙江，督江蘇學政，三十一年，詔罷科歲試，學政專司考校學務。 景崇條上十事。 明年，罷學政，還京供職。 疏陳立憲大要四事。
Tang Jingchong, whose style was Chunqing, came from Guanyang in Guangxi. His father Maogong was a provincial graduate known for scholarship and integrity. Jingchong passed the jinshi examination in Tongzhi 10 and was appointed Compiler. He rose from Reader-in-Waiting through four promotions to Grand Secretariat academician. In Guangxu 20 he ran the Guangdong provincial examination. The next year he presided over the metropolitan examination. He served as vice minister of War and Rites, acting Left Censor-in-Chief, then supervised education in Zhejiang until he returned home for his mother's mourning. When the Boxer crisis broke out he was ordered to organize militia in Guangxi. In the twenty-ninth year, as Vice Minister of Works, he ran the Zhejiang examination and supervised Jiangsu schools; in the thirty-first year the throne abolished annual provincial examinations and made education commissioners responsible only for school oversight. Jingchong submitted a ten-point program. The next year he left the education commission and returned to Beijing. He memorialized on four essentials of constitutional government.
9
時兩廣疆臣建議廣西省會移治南寧，京朝官皆持異議。 景崇奏陳：「遷省之議，以越南逼近龍州，法人時蓄狡謀，桂林距離遠，聲氣難通，不若改建南寧之便。 臣謂不然，今我兵力尚不能經營邕州，扼北海水陸衝要，徒虛張聲勢，招外人疑忌何爲？ 且遷徙締造之費，桂林善後之費，練兵設防之費，皆非巨款不辦。 方今俄居西陲，英窺南徼，蒙、藏、川、滇勢均岌岌，非獨一法人之可畏。 以大局論，決不能竭全力事廣西之一隅； 以廣西論，亦不能竭全力事南寧之一隅：明矣。 故爲今之計，誠能簡重臣駐龍州，於對汛邊地二千里，相度土宜，興辦樹藝、屯墾、畜牧、開礦諸端，俟地利漸興，人齒漸繁，再以兵法部勒。 此上策也。 至目前應變之方，莫如迅設龍州電綫，移提督駐南寧，增募十營，暫停廣西應解賠款，飭各省欠解廣西協餉，分年攤解，用抵賠款。 一轉移間，餉足則兵强，可紓朝廷南顧之憂。 若遷省之舉，勞民費財，無益於治。 」事得寢。
When frontier officials in Guangdong and Guangxi proposed moving the Guangxi capital from Guilin to Nanning, most officials in Beijing objected. Jingchong replied: "Advocates of the move argue that with Vietnam pressing on Longzhou and French designs never far away, Guilin is too remote to command the frontier, and Nanning would be more convenient." "I disagree. We cannot yet even hold Yongzhou and the Beihai land and sea approaches in force; what good is it to make an empty show of power and invite foreign suspicion?" "Moreover the costs of building a new capital, maintaining Guilin, and raising troops for defense cannot be met without enormous sums." "Russia presses the northwest, Britain watches the south, and Mongolia, Tibet, Sichuan, and Yunnan are all precarious; France is not our only concern." "From the standpoint of the empire, the court cannot pour all its strength into one corner of Guangxi;" "and Guangxi itself cannot pour all its resources into Nanning alone. That much is clear." "The better course is to station a senior official at Longzhou, develop the two-thousand-li border through forestry, reclamation, livestock, and mining, and only after the land grows prosperous and the population denser apply military organization." "That is the best long-term policy." "For immediate needs, the court should run a telegraph line to Longzhou, move the provincial commander to Nanning, raise ten new battalions, suspend Guangxi's indemnity payments for the time being, and have other provinces pay their overdue cooperative subsidies to Guangxi in yearly installments to offset indemnity obligations." "Once funds and troops are in place, the court's anxieties about the south can be eased." "Moving the capital would burden the people, waste money, and do nothing for good government." The proposal was dropped.
10
調吏部侍郎，充經筵講官。 景崇以績學端品受主知，屢司文柄。 迨科舉罷，廷試游學畢業生，皆倚景崇校閱。 宣統元年，戴鴻慈卒，遺疏薦景崇堪大用。 二年，擢學部尚書。 明年，詔設內閣，改學務大臣。 是時學說紛歧，景崇力謀溝通新舊，慎擇教科書。 兼任弼德院顧問大臣。 武昌變起，袁世凱總理內閣，仍命掌學務。 引疾去。 越三年，卒，諡文簡。
He was transferred to Vice Minister of Personnel and appointed lecturer at the Classics Colloquium. Known for solid scholarship and upright character, he won the emperor's trust and repeatedly controlled the civil examinations. After the examinations were abolished, the palace tests for students returning from study abroad were entrusted to his review. In Xuantong 1 Dai Hongci died and in his final memorial recommended Jingchong as a man of outstanding ability. In the second year he was promoted to Minister of Education. The next year, when the cabinet was created, he was renamed Minister of Education. At a time of fierce doctrinal dispute, he worked to reconcile old and new learning and chose textbooks with care. He also served as an adviser to the Hall for Nurturing Virtue. After the Wuchang uprising Yuan Shikai formed a cabinet and still left him in charge of education. He resigned, pleading illness. Three years later he died and received the posthumous title Wenjian.
11
景崇博覽羣書，通天文算術，尤喜治史。 自爲編修時，取新唐書爲作注，大例有三：曰糾繆，曰補闕，曰疏解，甄采書逾數百種。 家故貧，得秘籍精本，輒典質購之。 殫精畢世，唯缺地理志內羈縻州及藝文志，餘均脫稿。
Jingchong read widely, mastered astronomy and mathematics, and above all loved historical study. As a Compiler he began annotating the New Book of Tang under three headings—Correcting Errors, Supplementing Omissions, and Explication—drawing on more than four hundred works. His family was poor, and whenever he found a rare edition he would pawn belongings to buy it. He devoted his life to the project; only the sections on dependent prefectures in the geography monograph and the bibliography remained unfinished, and the rest was complete in draft.
12
于式枚，字晦若，賀縣人。 博聞强記，善屬文。 光緒六年進士，以庶吉士，散館用兵部主事。 李鴻章疏調北洋差遣，歷十餘年，奏牘多出其手。 性不樂爲外吏，又格於例不得保升京秩，久之不遷。 二十二年，鴻章賀俄皇加冕，因歷聘德、法、英、美諸國，式枚充隨員。 俄選授禮部主事，由員外郎授御史，遷給事中。 贊辛丑和約，賞五品京堂。 充政務處幫提調、大學堂總辦、譯學館監督。 三十一年，以鴻臚寺少卿督廣東學政，改提學使，疏辭，命總理廣西鐵路。 三十三年，擢郵傳部侍郎。
Yu Shimei, whose style was Huiruo, came from Hexian. He had a prodigious memory and wrote with ease. He passed the jinshi examination in Guangxu 6, served as a Hanlin bachelor, and on leaving the academy was assigned to the Ministry of War as a principal clerk. Li Hongzhang had him transferred to Beiyang service, and for more than ten years most of Li's memorials were drafted by him. He disliked service outside the capital, and precedent barred him from promotion to Beijing rank, so for years he received no advancement. In the twenty-second year Li Hongzhang attended the Russian coronation and toured Germany, France, Britain, and the United States, with Shimei on his staff. He was soon appointed to the Ministry of Rites, rose from vice director to censor, and was promoted to supervising secretary. For his role in the 1901 peace settlement he was granted fifth-rank capital bureau rank. He served as assistant coordinator of the Government Affairs Office, overall manager of the Imperial University, and supervisor of the School of Translation. In the thirty-first year he was sent to supervise Guangdong education as vice director of the Court of Imperial Entertainments, declined the post of education commissioner when it was offered, and was ordered to oversee the Guangxi railway. In the thirty-third year he was promoted to Vice Minister of Posts and Communications.
13
當是時，政潮激烈，有詔預備立憲，舉朝競言西法，無敢持異議者。 於是式枚奉命出使德國，充考察憲政大臣。 瀕行，疏言：「憲政必以本國爲根據，采取他國以輔益之，在求其實，不徒震其名。 我朝道監百王，科條詳備，行政皆守部章，風聞亦許言事，刑賞予奪，曾不自私。 有大政事、大興革，內則集廷臣之議，外或待疆吏之章。 勤求民隱，博采公論，與立憲之制無不符合。 上有教誡無約誓，下有遵守無要求。 至日久官吏失職，或有奉行之不善，海國開通，又有事例之所無，自可因時損益，並非變法更張。 惟人心趣向各異，告以堯、舜、周、孔之道，則以爲不足法； 告以英、德、法、美之制度，而日本所模仿者，則心悅誠服，以爲當行。 考日本維新之初，即宣言立憲之意。 後十四年，始發布開設國會之敕諭，二十年乃頒行憲法。 蓋預備詳密遲慎如此。 今橫議者自謂國民，聚衆者輒云團體，數年之中，內治外交，用人行政，皆有干預之想。 動以立憲爲詞，紛馳電函，上厪宸慮。 蓋以立憲爲新奇可喜，不知吾國所自有。 其關於學術者，固貽譏荒陋，以立憲爲即可施行，不審東洋之近事。 關於政術者，尤有害治安。 惟在朝廷本一定之指歸，齊萬衆之心志，循序漸進。 先設京師議院以定從違，舉辦地方自治以植根本，尤要在廣興教育，儲備人才。 凡與憲政相輔而行者，均當先事綢繆者也。 臣前隨李鴻章至柏林，略觀大概。 今承特簡，謹當參合中、西同異，歸極於皇朝典章，庶言皆有本而事屬可行。 是臣區區之至願。 」
Political passions ran high; the throne had announced constitutional preparation, and the whole court rushed to praise Western models with scarcely a dissenting voice. Shimei was then ordered to Germany as minister charged with studying constitutional government. Before leaving he wrote: "Constitutional government must be rooted in our own country and draw on foreign models only to supplement it; we must seek substance, not be dazzled by names." Our dynasty's institutions already encompass the wisdom of ages: regulations are detailed, ministries govern by rule, rumor may be reported upward, and rewards and punishments have never been arbitrary. Major policies and reforms are debated within the court and often await memorials from provincial officials. Diligent inquiry into popular grievances and broad consultation match constitutional practice in every respect. The throne instructs without binding oaths, and subjects obey without making demands. When officials fail in their duties, policies are poorly executed, or new maritime contacts raise unprecedented cases, adjustments may be made in due course without overturning the fundamental system. Minds differ widely: told of Yao, Shun, the Zhou, and Confucius, many deem them inadequate models; but shown the institutions of Britain, Germany, France, America, and Japan's adaptations of them, they embrace them wholeheartedly as what must be done. Japan proclaimed constitutional intentions at the very start of the Meiji Restoration. Fourteen years passed before it ordered a parliament convened, and twenty before the constitution was promulgated. Its preparation was as deliberate and cautious as that. Today rash critics call themselves "the people," agitators form "organizations," and within a few years they seek to intervene in domestic policy, foreign affairs, appointments, and administration alike. They invoke constitutionalism at every turn, bombard the throne with telegrams, and trouble the emperor's peace of mind. They treat constitutionalism as a novelty, unaware that China already possesses its essentials. In scholarship they invite ridicule for crudeness; in politics they assume a constitution can be imposed overnight without studying Japan's recent experience. Their political demands are especially dangerous to public order. The court must hold a clear course, align public opinion, and advance step by step. It should first establish a capital assembly, promote local self-government, and above all expand education and train talent. Every measure that supports constitutional government must be prepared in advance. I once accompanied Li Hongzhang to Berlin and gained some general impressions. Now specially commissioned, I shall compare Chinese and Western institutions and ground my report in the dynasty's own statutes, so that every proposal has a basis and can be carried out. That is my humble earnest wish. "
14
明年，調禮部侍郎。 時新黨要求實行立憲，召集國會日亟。 式枚上言：「臣徧考東西歷史，參校同異，大抵中法皆定自上而下奉行，西法則定自下而上遵守。 惟日本憲法，則纂自日臣伊藤博文，雖西國之名詞，仍東洋之性質。 其采取則普魯士爲多，其本原則德君臣所定，名爲欽定憲法。 夫國所以立曰政，政所以行曰權，權所歸即利所在。 定於一則無非分之想，散於衆則有競進之心。 行之而善，則爲日本之維新； 行之不善，則爲法國之革命。 法國當屢世苛虐之後，民困已深，欲以立憲救亡，而適促其亂。 日本當尊王傾幕之時，本由民力，故以立憲爲報，而猶緩其期。 中國名義最重，政治最寬，國體尊嚴，人情安習，既無法國之怨毒，又非日本之改造。 皇上俯順輿情，迭降諭旨，分定年期，自宜互相奮勉，靜待推行。 豈容欲速等於取償，求治同於論價？ 至敢言監督朝廷，推倒政府，胥動浮言，幾同亂黨。 欲圖補救之策，惟在朝廷舉錯一秉至公，不稍予以指摘之端，自無從爲煽惑之計。 至東南各省疆吏，當慎擇有風力、知大體者鎮懾之。 當十年預備之期，爲大局安危所繫。 日皇所謂『組織權限，爲朕親裁』，德相所謂『法定於君，非民可解』。 故必正名定分，然後措正施行。 臣濫膺考察，斷不敢附會時趨，貽誤國家，得罪名教。 」章下所司。 尋調吏部侍郎。
The next year he was transferred to Vice Minister of Rites. Reformers were pressing for immediate constitutional government and demanding that a parliament be convened without delay. Shimei wrote: "Having surveyed Eastern and Western history, I find that in China institutions are ordained from above and obeyed downward, whereas in the West they arise from below and are upheld upward." Japan's constitution was drafted by Ito Hirobumi: though couched in Western terms, it remains essentially East Asian in character. It drew chiefly on Prussia and was promulgated by the German emperor and his ministers as an imperially granted constitution. A state rests on government, government rests on power, and power is where interest lies. When power is unified, improper ambitions fade; when it is dispersed, rivalry and ambition flourish. Well managed, it produces Japan's restoration; poorly managed, France's revolution. France, after generations of oppression, was so deeply exhausted that constitutionalism meant to save the state only hastened disorder. Japan's restoration rested on popular support, so it rewarded the people with constitutional promises yet still moved slowly. China values ritual propriety above all, governs leniently, and enjoys settled customs; it lacks France's accumulated bitterness and is not undergoing Japan's wholesale transformation. The Emperor has already heeded public opinion, issued repeated edicts, and set a timetable; all should work diligently and wait calmly for gradual implementation. How can reform be rushed like collecting a debt, or good government bargained for like goods in a market? Those who dare speak of supervising the throne and overthrowing the government trade in empty slogans and are scarcely distinguishable from rebels. The remedy lies in the court's conducting itself with perfect fairness and giving agitators no handle for complaint. In the southeast, frontier officials of force and judgment should be chosen to keep order. The ten-year preparatory period is vital to the nation's safety. As the Japanese emperor said, "organizing authority is for Us personally to decide," and the German chancellor said, "law is fixed by the ruler and not subject to popular interpretation." Names and roles must be clarified before institutions can be put into proper practice. Though unworthy of this commission, I dare not trim my views to fashion, harm the state, or betray the moral order. The memorial was referred to the appropriate offices. He was soon transferred to Vice Minister of Personnel.
15
上海政聞社法部主事陳景仁等電請定三年內開國會，罷式枚謝天下，嚴旨申飭，褫景仁職。 式枚復奏言：「德皇接受國書，答言憲政紛繁，慮未必合中國用，選舉法尤未易行。 又昔英儒斯賓塞爾亦甚言憲法流弊，謂美國憲法本人民平等，行之久而治權握於政黨，平民不勝其苦。 蓋歐人言憲法，其難其慎如此。 今橫議遍於國中，上則詆政府固權，下則罵國民失職，專以爭競相勸導。 此正斯賓塞爾所云政黨者流，與平民固無與也。 伊藤博文論君臣相與，先道德而後科條。 君民何獨不然？ 果能誠信相接，則普與日本以欽定憲法行之至今； 如其不然，則法蘭西固民約憲法，何以革命者再三，改法者數十而猶未定？ 臣愚以爲中國立憲，應以日本仿照普魯士之例爲權衡，以畢士麥由君主用人民意見制定，及伊藤博文先道德後科條之言爲標準，則憲法大綱立矣。 」章下所司。 又以各省諮議局章程與普國地方議會制度不符，大恉謂：「改革未定之時，中央政權唯恐少統一堅强之力，而國民識政體知法意者極少。 驟以此龐大政權之地方議會，橫亙政府與國民之間，縱使被選者不皆營私武斷，而一國政權落於最少數人之手，劫持中外大臣，後患何可勝言？ 」因證以普制，逐條駁議。 先後譯奏普魯士憲法全文、官制位號等級，暨兩議院新舊選舉法。 式枚以三十三年冬行，宣統元年六月返國，以疾乞假。 張之洞遺疏薦式枚堪大用。 轉吏部侍郎，改學部侍郎，總理禮學館事、修訂法律大臣、國史館副總裁。 國變後，僑居青島。 未幾，卒，年六十三，諡文和。
Chen Jingren of Shanghai's Political News Society telegraphed demanding a parliament within three years and Shimei's dismissal; the throne rebuked them sharply and stripped Chen of office. Shimei reported that the German emperor, receiving China's credentials, said constitutional government was complex and might not suit China, and that election laws would be especially hard to apply. The English thinker Spencer likewise warned of constitutional abuses, noting that though the American constitution began in popular equality, power soon passed to parties and the common people suffered under them. Europeans themselves treat constitutional government with such difficulty and caution. Today reckless talk fills the land: critics above denounce the government for clinging to power, critics below blame the people for failing their duties, and all preach contention. These are precisely the party factions Spencer described, with nothing to do with the common people. Ito Hirobumi held that relations between ruler and minister require morality before statutes. Why should relations between ruler and people be any different? If sincerity and trust prevail, Prussia and Japan have governed successfully under imperially granted constitutions to this day; if not, why has France, for all its popular constitution, suffered repeated revolutions and dozens of constitutional revisions without ever settling? I believe China should follow Japan's Prussian model, Bismarck's principle that the monarch shapes law with popular input, and Ito's insistence on morality before statutes; then the constitutional framework would be sound. The memorial was referred to the appropriate offices. He also argued that provincial deliberative assembly regulations diverged from Prussian local parliaments and wrote: "While reform remains unsettled, central authority can scarcely afford to weaken unified control, and few citizens understand government or law." To thrust such vast authority into local assemblies between throne and people—even if the elected were not all corrupt—would concentrate national power in a tiny minority able to coerce ministers at home and abroad, with consequences beyond reckoning. He cited Prussian institutions and rebutted the regulations clause by clause. He translated and submitted the full Prussian constitution, the official rank system, and the old and new election laws of both chambers. Shimei left in the winter of the thirty-third year and returned in the sixth month of Xuantong 1, then asked sick leave. Zhang Zhidong's final memorial recommended Shimei as a man of outstanding ability. He became Vice Minister of Personnel, then Education, oversaw the Hall of Ritual Learning, served as minister for legal revision, and was vice president of the National History Office. After the fall of the dynasty he lived in exile in Qingdao. He died soon after, at sixty-three, with the posthumous title Wenhe.
16
式枚生而隱宮，精力絕人，夜倚枕坐如枯僧。 內介而外和易。 論事謇諤，頗有聲公卿間云。
Born with a hidden physical defect, he had extraordinary stamina and would sit through the night leaning on a pillow like an ascetic monk. Inwardly firm, outwardly genial. He spoke bluntly on public affairs and enjoyed a strong reputation among senior officials.
17
沈家本，字子惇，浙江歸安人。 少讀書，好深湛之思，於周官多創獲。 初援例以郎中分刑部，博稽掌故，多所纂述。 光緒九年，成進士，仍留部。 補官後，充主稿，兼秋審處。 自此遂專心法律之學，爲尚書潘祖蔭所稱賞。 十九年，出知天津府，治尚寬大，姦民易之，聚衆鬨於市，即擒斬四人，無敢復犯者。 調劇保定，甘軍毀法國教堂，當路懾於外勢，償五萬金，以道署舊址建新堂，侵及府署東偏。 家本據府志力爭得直。 拳匪亂作，家本已擢通永道、山西按察使，未及行，兩宮西幸。 聯軍入保定，教士銜前隙，誣以助拳匪，卒無左驗而解。 因馳赴行在，授光祿寺卿，擢刑部侍郎。
Shen Jiaben, whose style was Zidun, came from Gui'an in Zhejiang. From youth he loved deep study and made many original insights into the Offices of Zhou. He entered the Ministry of Punishments by precedent as a director, researched precedents exhaustively, and compiled many works. In Guangxu 9 he passed the jinshi examination and remained at the ministry. After regular appointment he served as chief drafter and also in the Autumn Review Office. Thereafter he devoted himself to legal studies and won the admiration of Minister Pan Zuyin. In the nineteenth year he became prefect of Tianjin. His rule was lenient, so ruffians took him lightly and rioted in the market; he seized and executed four ringleaders at once, and no one dared repeat the offense. Transferred to Baoding, he found that after Gansu troops had destroyed a French church the authorities, fearing foreign pressure, paid fifty thousand taels and planned a new church on the circuit yamen site, encroaching on the prefectural yamen. Jiaben cited the prefectural gazetteer and fought the case successfully. When the Boxer uprising broke out he had just been promoted to Tongyong circuit intendant and Shanxi surveillance commissioner but had not yet left office when the court fled west. When the allied armies entered Baoding, missionaries who bore old grudges accused him of aiding the Boxers, but with no evidence against him he was cleared. He hurried to the traveling court, was made Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, and was promoted to Vice Minister of Punishments.
18
自各國互市以來，內地許傳教，而中外用律輕重懸殊，民、教日齟齬。 官畏事則務抑民，民不能堪，則激而一逞，往往焚戮成巨禍。 家本以謂治今日之民，當令官吏普通法律。 然中律不變而欲收回領事審判權，終不可得。 會變法議起，袁世凱奏設修訂法律館，命家本偕伍廷芳總其事； 別設法律學堂，畢業者近千人，一時稱盛。 補大理寺卿，旋改法部侍郎，充修訂法律大臣。 宣統元年，兼資政院副總裁，仍日與館員商訂諸法草案，先後告成，未嘗以事繁自解。 其所著書，有讀律校勘記、秋讞須知、刑案匯覽、刺字集、律例偶箋、歷代刑官考、歷代刑法考、漢律摭遺、明大誥竣令考、明律目箋，他所著非刑律者又二十餘種，都二百餘卷。 卒，年七十四。
Since the treaty ports opened, missionaries had been allowed inland, but Chinese and foreign law differed sharply in severity, and friction between commoners and missionaries grew daily. Officials, fearing trouble, suppressed the people; when the people could bear it no longer they erupted in violence, and great massacres often followed. Jiaben held that governing the people of today required making law common knowledge among officials. Yet consular jurisdiction could never be recovered without reforming Chinese law itself. When reform was debated, Yuan Shikai proposed founding the Bureau for Revising the Laws and placed Jiaben and Wu Tingfang in joint charge; A law school was also founded, producing nearly a thousand graduates in what was for a time a celebrated institution. He became president of the Court of Judicial Review, then Vice Minister of Law, and served as minister for legal revision. In Xuantong 1 he also served as vice president of the Political Consultative Council, yet still met daily with bureau staff to draft laws until the codes were completed, never pleading the press of business as an excuse. His writings included Collation Notes on Reading the Code, Essentials of Autumn Review Cases, Compendium of Criminal Cases, Collection on Tattooing Marks, Occasional Notes on Statutes and Regulations, Studies of Punishment Officials and Punitive Law Through the Ages, Remnants of Han Law, studies of Ming admonitions and code headings, and more than twenty other non-penal works—over two hundred juan in all. He died at seventy-four.
19
論曰：自變法議興，凡新政特設大臣領之。 百熙管學務，家本修法律，並邀時譽。 景崇之主教育，謀溝通新舊； 式枚之論憲政，務因時損益。 而大勢所趨，已莫能挽救。 家鼐儒厚廉謹，常以資望領新政，每參大計，獨持正不阿。 賢哉，不愧古大臣矣！
The historians remark: Once reform was debated, every new policy was placed under a specially appointed minister. Baixi directed education and Jiaben revised the laws, and both won contemporary acclaim. Jingchong sought to reconcile old and new learning in education; Shimei argued for constitutional government that would adjust institutions to the times. Yet the tide of events was already beyond their power to reverse. Jianai was scholarly, generous, and scrupulous; by senior standing he often headed new policies, and whenever he joined in great decisions he alone refused to trim his views. Truly worthy—a minister in the ancient mold!
