Penal Law, Part 2
3
明律淵源唐代，以笞、杖、徒、流、死為五刑。 自笞一十至五十，為笞刑五。 自杖六十至一百，為杖刑五。 徒自杖六十徒一年起，每等加杖十，刑期半年，至杖一百徒三年，為徒五等。 流以二千里、二千五百里、三千里為三等，而皆加杖一百。 死刑二：曰斬，曰絞。 此正刑也。 其律例內之雜犯、斬絞、遷徙、充軍、枷號、刺字、論贖、凌遲、梟首、戮屍等刑，或取諸前代，或明所自創，要皆非刑之正。
The Ming Code drew on Tang law and recognized five punishments: cangue, beating, penal servitude, exile, and death. Cangue punishments ranged from ten to fifty blows across five grades. Beating punishments ranged from sixty to one hundred blows across five grades. Penal servitude began at sixty blows and one year, increasing by ten blows and six months per grade up to one hundred blows and three years—five grades in all. Exile had three distances—two thousand, two thousand five hundred, and three thousand li—each accompanied by one hundred blows of the staff. Capital punishment took two forms: decapitation and strangulation. These were the statutory punishments proper. Miscellaneous offenses, decapitation and strangulation, relocation, military exile, cangue and collar, tattooing, ransom, slicing, display of the severed head, and corpse dismemberment—some drawn from earlier dynasties, some invented by the Ming—were none of them punishments in the proper sense.
4
清太祖、太宗之治遼東，刑制尚簡，重則斬，輕則鞭撲而已。 迨世祖入關，沿襲明制，初頒刑律，笞、杖以五折十，注入本刑各條。 康熙朝現行則例改為四折除零。 雍正三年之律，乃依例各於本律註明板數。 徒、流加杖，亦至配所照數折責。 蓋恐撲責過多，致傷生命，法外之仁也。 文武官犯笞、杖，則分別公私，代以罰俸、降級、降調，至革職而止。
Under the Qing founders in Liaodong, penal law remained simple: serious offenders were beheaded, minor ones merely whipped or beaten. Once the Shizu entered China proper, the dynasty adopted Ming practice and issued its first penal code, counting five strokes of cangue or beating as ten and folding them into each principal punishment. Under Kangxi, the Current Regulations changed this to a four-tenths reduction with fractions dropped. The Yongzheng Code of year three specified the number of blows in the commentary to each principal statute. Additional beating for penal servitude and exile was likewise reduced according to the schedule at the place of assignment. The aim was to keep excessive beating from endangering life—a mercy beyond strict law. Civil and military officials who incurred cangue or beating were spared the rod: public and private offenses were distinguished, and salary fines, demotion, transfer, or dismissal substituted instead.
5
徒者，奴也，蓋奴辱之。 明發鹽場鐵冶煎鹽炒鐵，清則發本省驛遞。 其無驛縣，分撥各衙門充水火夫各項雜役，限滿釋放。
The character for penal servitude means “servant”—the punishment is essentially servile humiliation. The Ming sent convicts to salt pans and ironworks; the Qing sent them to courier stations within their home province. Where no courier network existed, they were assigned as water carriers and other menials in various offices until their term ended.
6
流犯，初制由各縣解交巡撫衙門，按照里數，酌發各處荒蕪及瀕海州縣。 嗣以各省分撥失均，不免趨避揀擇。 乾隆八年，刑部始纂輯三流道里表，將某省某府屬流犯，應流二千里者發何省何府屬安置，應流二千五百里者發何省何府屬安置，應流三千里者發何省何府屬安置，按計程途，限定地址，逐省逐府，分別開載。 嗣於四十九年及嘉慶六年兩次修訂。 然第於州縣之增並，道里之參差，略有修改，而大體不易。 律稱：「犯流妻妾從之，父祖子孫欲隨者聽。 」乾隆二十四年，將僉妻之例停止。 其軍、流、遣犯情原隨帶家屬者，不得官為資送，律成虛設矣。
Exiles were first forwarded from county to provincial governor and, by distance, assigned to barren or coastal districts. Uneven provincial allocation soon led to evasion and cherry-picking of destinations. In Qianlong 8 the Ministry of Justice compiled a table of exile routes: for each province and prefecture it fixed where convicts sentenced to two thousand, two thousand five hundred, or three thousand li were to be settled, listing destinations province by province. The table was revised twice—in Qianlong 49 and Jiaqing 6. Only minor adjustments were made for county mergers and distance discrepancies; the overall scheme held. The Code reads: “When a convict is exiled, his wife and concubines may follow; fathers, grandfathers, sons, and grandsons who wish to accompany him are permitted to do so. ” In Qianlong 24 the practice of collective wife settlement was abolished. Military exiles, banished convicts, and transported convicts who wished to bring kin could no longer rely on official escort—the statute became a dead letter.
7
斬、絞，同是死刑。 然自漢以來，有秋後決囚之制。 唐律除犯惡逆以上及奴婢、部曲殺主者，從立春至秋分不得奏決死刑。 明弘治十年奏定真犯死罪決不待時者，凌遲十二條，斬三十七條，絞十二條； 真犯死罪秋後處決者，斬一百條，絞八十六條。 順治初定律，乃於各條內分晰註明，凡律不注監候者，皆立決也； 凡例不言立決者，皆監候也。 自此京、外死罪多決於秋，朝審遂為一代之大典。 雜犯斬、絞准徒五年與雜犯三流總徒四年，大都創自有明。 清律於官吏受贓，枉法不枉法，滿貫俱改為實絞，餘多仍之。 名實混淆，殊形轇轕。
Decapitation and strangulation are both capital punishments. Since Han times, however, there has been the rule of autumn execution. Tang law excepted flagrant rebellion and bondservants or retainers who killed their master: from Beginning of Spring to Autumn Equinox no capital sentence could be reported for decision. In Ming Hongzhi 10 true capital offenses requiring immediate execution were fixed at twelve articles of slicing, thirty-seven of decapitation, and twelve of strangulation; true capital offenses for autumn execution at one hundred articles of decapitation and eighty-six of strangulation. Early Shunzhi law annotated each article: wherever the Code did not note “await execution in custody,” the sentence was immediate; wherever sub-statutes did not say “immediate execution,” the sentence awaited custody. Thereafter most capital cases in capital and provinces were decided in autumn, and the autumn court review became a great institution of the dynasty. Ming practice largely created commutation of miscellaneous decapitation and strangulation to five years of penal servitude and of the three exiles to four years. The Qing Code changed official bribery—perverting the law or not, reaching the full quota or not—to actual strangulation, while retaining most other Ming provisions. Names and substance were confused, and the forms hopelessly tangled.
8
遷徙原於唐之殺人移鄉，而定罪則異。 律文沿用數條，然皆改為比流減半、徒二年，並不徙諸千里之外。 惟條例於土蠻、瑤、僮、苗人仇殺劫擄及改土為流之土司有犯，將家口實行遷徙。 然各有定地，亦不限千里也。
Relocation derived from the Tang practice of moving a killer to another district, but conviction differed. Several Code articles remained, but all were changed to half exile distance and two years of penal servitude, without banishment a thousand li away. Sub-statutes alone required actual relocation of households for Tujia, Yao, Zhuang, and Miao feud killers and abductors, and for native chieftains under regular administration who offended. Each case had a fixed destination and was not limited to a thousand li.
9
明之充軍，義主實邊，不盡與流刑相比附。 清初裁撤邊衛，而仍沿充軍之名。 後遂以附近、近邊、邊遠、極邊、煙瘴為五軍，且於滿流以上，為節級加等之用。 附近二千里，近邊二千五百里，邊遠三千里，極邊、煙瘴俱四千里。 在京兵部定地，在外巡撫定地。 雍正三年之律，第於十五布政司應發省分約略編定。 乾隆三十七年，兵部根據邦政紀略，輯為五軍道里表，凡發配者，視表所列。 然名為充軍，至配並不入營差操，第於每月朔望檢點，實與流犯無異。 而滿流加附近、近邊道里，反由遠而近，司讞者每苦其紛歧，而又有發遣名目。 初第發尚陽堡、寧古塔，或烏喇地方安插，後並發齊齊哈爾、黑龍江、三姓、喀爾喀、科布多，或各省駐防為奴。 乾隆年間，新疆開闢，例又有發往伊犁、烏魯木齊、巴里坤各回城分別為奴種地者。 咸、同之際，新疆道梗，又復改發內地充軍。 其制屢經變易，然軍遣止及其身。 苟情節稍輕，尚得更赦放還。 以視明之永遠軍戍，數世後猶句及本籍子孫者，大有間也。 若文武職官犯徒以上，輕則軍台效力，重則新疆當差。 成案相沿，遂為定例。 此又軍遣中之歧出者焉。
Ming military exile chiefly strengthened the frontier and did not fully correspond to banishment. Early Qing abolished frontier garrisons but kept the name of military exile. Later five grades were fixed—nearby, near frontier, remote frontier, extreme frontier, and miasma regions—and above full exile they moderated increases in severity. Nearby meant two thousand li, near frontier two thousand five hundred, remote frontier three thousand, extreme frontier and miasma regions four thousand each. In the capital the Ministry of War fixed destinations; in the provinces the governor did. The Yongzheng Code of year three only roughly listed provinces for the fifteen provincial administrations. In Qianlong 37 the Ministry of War, using the Record of Frontier Administration, compiled a five-grade route table; all assignments followed it. Though called military exile, convicts never entered camps for drill but were inspected on the first and fifteenth of each month—no different from ordinary exiles. Full exile plus nearby or near frontier ran distances from far to near, vexing judicial officers—and there was also transportation. At first destinations were Shangyang Fort, Ningguta, or Wula; later Qiqihar, Heilongjiang, Sanxing, Khalkha, Kobdo, or enslavement in provincial garrisons were added. As Xinjiang opened in the Qianlong era, sub-statutes sent convicts to Yili, Urumqi, Barkol, and Muslim cities as slaves working the land. When Xinjiang routes were blocked in the Xianfeng and Tongzhi eras, assignment reverted to military exile within China proper. The system changed often, yet military exile and transportation applied only to the offender himself. Where circumstances were lighter, amnesty and release remained possible. Compared with Ming perpetual military guard, which generations later still implicated descendants in the home register, the difference was vast. Civil and military officials above penal servitude served at courier stations for light offenses and in Xinjiang for heavy ones. Accumulated precedents made this a fixed rule. This was yet another exceptional branch of military exile and transportation.
10
枷杻，本以羈獄囚。 明代問刑條例，於本罪外或加以枷號，示戮辱也。 清律犯罪免發遣條：「凡旗人犯罪，笞、杖各照數鞭責，軍、流、徒免發遣，分別枷號。 徒一年者，枷號二十日，每等遞加五日。 流二千里者，枷號五十日，每等亦遞加五日。 充軍附近者，枷號七十日，近邊、沿海、邊外者八十日，極邊、煙瘴者九十日。 」原立法之意，亦以旗人生則入檔，壯則充兵，鞏衛本根，未便離遠，有犯徒、流等罪，直以枷號代刑，強榦之義則然。 然犯系寡廉鮮恥，則銷除旗檔，一律實發，不姑息也。 若竊盜再犯加枷，初犯再犯計次加枷，犯奸加枷，賭博加枷，逃軍逃流加枷，暨一切敗檢逾閒、不顧行止者酌量加枷，則初無旗、民之別。 康熙八年，部議囚禁人犯止用細鍊，不用長枷，而枷號遂專為行刑之用。 其數初不過一月、二月、三月，後竟有論年或永遠枷號者。 始制重者七十觔，輕者六十斤。 乾隆五年，改定應枷人犯俱重二十五斤，然例尚有用百斤重枷者。 嘉慶以降，重枷斷用三十五斤，而於四川、陝西、湖北、河南、山東、安徽、廣東等省匪徒，又有系帶鐵杆石礅之例，亦一時創刑也。
The cangue and collar originally restrained prisoners in custody. Ming Interrogation Statutes sometimes added cangue and collar beyond the principal offense to display shame. The Qing statute on exemption from transportation reads: “Bannermen receive the full number of blows for cangue and beating; military exile, banishment, and penal servitude are not assigned but replaced by cangue and collar. One year of penal servitude: twenty days in the cangue and collar, five days added per grade. Exile at two thousand li: fifty days, five days added per grade. Military exile nearby: seventy days; near frontier, coastal, or outer frontier: eighty; extreme frontier or miasma: ninety. ” The intent was that Bannermen registered at birth and soldiered in maturity guarded the dynasty’s root; for penal servitude and banishment, cangue and collar substituted directly—strengthening the trunk. Offenses of wanton dishonor cancelled Banner registration and brought full assignment without indulgence. Repeat theft, adultery, gambling, desertion, and all who breached discipline received discretionary cangue and collar without Banner-commoner distinction. In Kangxi 8 imprisoned convicts were limited to light chains, not long cangues; cangue and collar became purely penal. Terms first did not exceed one to three months, but later ran to a year or perpetuity. Originally the heavy cangue weighed seventy jin and the light sixty. In Qianlong 5 all cangues were fixed at twenty-five jin, though sub-statutes still allowed one-hundred-jin cangues. From Jiaqing heavy cangues were capped at thirty-five jin, yet bandits in several provinces were shackled with iron rods and stone blocks—a temporary innovation.
11
刺字，古肉刑之一，律第嚴於賊盜。 乃其後條例滋多，刺緣坐，刺兇犯，刺逃軍、逃流，刺外遣、改遣、改發。 有刺事由者，有刺地方者，並有分刺滿、漢文字者。 初刺右臂，次刺左臂，次刺右面、左面。 大抵律多刺臂，例多刺面。 若竊盜責充警跡，二三年無過，或緝獲強盜二名以上、竊盜三名以上，例又准其起除刺字，復為良民。 蓋惡惡雖嚴，而亦未嘗不予以自新之路焉。
Tattooing was an ancient corporal punishment; the Code was strictest on thieves and robbers. Later sub-statutes multiplied: guilt by association, violent offenders, deserters, outer transportation, changed transportation, changed assignment. Some tattooed the offense, some the place; some used Manchu, some Chinese. First the right arm, then the left, then right and left face. Generally the Code tattooed arms and sub-statutes mostly the face. Thieves assigned as constables who after two or three years without offense, or who captured two robbers or three thieves, might have tattoos removed and status restored. Punishment of evil was severe, yet a path to renewal was never wholly denied.
12
贖刑有三：一曰納贖，無力照律決配，有力照例納贖。 二曰收贖，老幼廢疾、天文生及婦人折杖，照律收贖。 三曰贖罪，官員正妻及例難的決，並婦人有力者，照例贖罪。 收贖名曰律贖，原本唐律收贖。 贖罪名為例贖，則明代所創行。 順治修律，五刑不列贖銀數目。 雍正三年，始將明律贖圖內應贖銀數斟酌修改，定為納贖諸例圖。 然自康熙現行例定有承問官濫准納贖交部議處之條，而前明納贖及贖罪諸舊例又節經刪改，故律贖俱照舊援用，而例贖則多成具文。
Commutation had three kinds: payment commutation—for those unable to undergo statutory sentencing, the wealthy paid according to statute. Second, collection commutation—for the aged, infirm, imperial astronomers, and women subject to reduced beating. Third, offense commutation—for officials’ principal wives, cases where beating could not be applied, and wealthy women. Collection commutation, called statutory commutation, derived from Tang law. Offense commutation, called sub-statutory commutation, was a Ming innovation. The Shunzhi revision did not list ransom silver for the five punishments. In Yongzheng 3 Ming commutation charts were revised into payment commutation charts. Yet Kangxi regulations punished improper approval of payment commutation; Ming payment and offense provisions were deleted; statutory commutation continued while sub-statutory commutation became dead letters.

其捐贖一項，順治十八年，有官員犯流徒籍沒認工贖罪例； 康熙二十九年，有死罪現監人犯輸米邊口贖罪例； 三十年，有軍流人犯捐贖例； 三十四年，有通倉運米捐贖例； 三十九年，有永定河工捐贖例； 六十年，有河工捐贖例。 然皆事竣停止，其歷朝沿用者，惟雍正十二年戶部會同刑部奏准預籌運糧事例，不論旗、民，罪應斬、絞，非常赦所不原者，三品以上官照西安駝捐例捐運糧銀一萬二千兩，四品官照營田例捐運糧銀五千兩，五、六品官照營田例捐銀四千兩，七品以下、進士、舉人二千五百兩，貢、監生二千兩，平人一千二百兩，軍、流各減十分之四，徒以下各減十分之六，俱准免罪。 西安駝捐，行自雍正元年，營田例則五年所定也。 乾隆十七年，西安布政使張若震奏請另定捐贖笞、杖銀數。 經部議，預籌運糧事例，杖、笞與徒罪不分輕重，一例捐贖，究未允協。 除犯枷號、杖責者照徒罪捐贖外，酌擬分杖為一等，笞為一等。 其數，杖視徒遞減，笞視杖遞減。 二十三年，諭將斬、絞緩決各犯納贖之例永行停止。 遇有恩赦減等時，其憚於遠行者，再准收贖。 而贖鍰則仍視原擬罪名，不得照減等之罪。 著為令。 嗣後官員贖罪者，俱照運糧事例核奪。 刑部別設贖罪處，專司其事。 此又律贖、例贖而外，別自為制者矣。
Donated commutation began in Shunzhi 18 with officials guilty of banishment or penal servitude commuting by confiscation and labor; in Kangxi 29 capital convicts in custody commuted by delivering grain to the frontier; in Kangxi 30 military exiles and banished convicts commuted by donation; in Kangxi 34 delivering grain through Tongcang; in Kangxi 39 Yongding River works; in Kangxi 60 general river works. All ceased when projects ended; only Yongzheng 12 advance grain transport continued: third rank and above twelve thousand taels, fourth five thousand, fifth and sixth four thousand, seventh rank and below, jinshi, and juren two thousand five hundred, students two thousand, commoners one thousand two hundred; military exile and banishment reduced four-tenths, penal servitude and below six-tenths—all exempted. The Xi’an camel donation began in Yongzheng 1; the military colony precedent in year five. In Qianlong 17 Shaanxi commissioner Zhang Ruozhen memorialized separate amounts for cangue and beating. The ministries ruled that under advance grain transport, cangue, beating, and penal servitude were commuted at one rate regardless of severity—ultimately unsatisfactory. Apart from cangue-collar and beating offenses commuted at penal-servitude rates, beating and cangue were each set as a single grade. Beating amounts stepped down from penal servitude; cangue amounts stepped down from beating. In year 23 the precedent for commuting delayed decapitation and strangulation was permanently abolished. When amnesty reduced sentences, those who feared distant travel could again commute by collection. Ransom silver still followed the original offense, not the reduced grade. This was promulgated as an order. Thereafter officials commuting offenses were assessed under the grain transport precedent. The Ministry of Justice established a separate commutation office. This formed a separate system beyond statutory and sub-statutory commutation.
14
凌遲，用之十惡中不道以上諸重罪，號為極刑。 梟首，則強盜居多。 戮屍，所以待惡逆及強盜應梟諸犯之監故者。 凡此諸刑，類皆承用明律，略有通變，行之二百餘年。 至過誤殺之賠人，竊盜之割腳筋，重辟減等之貫耳鼻，強盜、貪官及窩逃之籍家產，或沿自盛京定例，或順治朝偶行之峻令，不久革除，非所論也。
Slicing applied to impiety among the Ten Abominations and was termed the extreme punishment. Display of the severed head was chiefly used for robbery. Corpse dismemberment punished flagrant impiety and robbers who should have been displayed but were held in custody. These punishments largely continued Ming law with slight adaptation for over two hundred years. Compensation for accidental killing, severing thieves’ tendons, piercing ears and nose for capital reduction, confiscating property for robbery, corrupt officials, and harboring fugitives—some from Shengjing regulations, some Shunzhi harsh measures soon abolished—lie outside this discussion.

自光緒變法，二十八年，山西巡撫趙爾巽奏請各省通設罪犯習藝所。 經刑部議准，徒犯毋庸發配，按照年限，於本地收所習藝。 軍、流為常赦所不原者，照定例發配，到配一律收所習藝。 流二千里限工作六年，二千五百里八年，三千里者十年。 遣軍照滿流年限計算，限滿釋放，聽其自謀生計，並准在配所入籍為民。 若為常赦所得原者，無論軍、流，俱無庸發配，即在本省收所習藝。 工作年限，亦照前科算。 自此五徒並不發配，即軍、流之發配者，數亦銳減矣。 二十九年，刑部奏准刪除充軍名目，將附近、近邊，邊遠併入三流，極邊及煙瘴改為安置，仍與當差並行。 自此五軍第留其二，而刑名亦改變矣。 三十年，劉坤一、張之洞會奏變法第二摺內，有恤刑獄九條。 其省刑責條內，經法律館議准，笞、杖等罪，仿照外國罰金之法，改為罰銀。 凡律例內笞刑五，以五錢為一等，至笞五十罰銀二兩五錢，杖六十者改為罰五兩。 每一等加二兩五錢，以次遞加，至杖一百改為罰十五兩而止。 如無力完納，折為作工。 應罰一兩，折作工四日，以次遞加，至十五兩折作工六十日而止。 然竊盜未便罰金，議將犯竊應擬笞罪者，改科工作一月； 杖六十者，改科工作兩月； 杖七十至一百，每等遞加兩月。 又附片請將軍、流、徒加杖概予寬免，無庸決責。 自此而笞、杖二刑廢棄矣。
During Guangxu legal reform, in year 28 Shanxi governor Zhao Erxun memorialized universal houses of correction. The ministries approved: penal servitude need not be assigned; convicts learned trades locally for the statutory term. Military exile and banishment outside regular amnesty were assigned as before and received in houses of correction upon arrival. Two thousand li: six years’ labor; two thousand five hundred: eight; three thousand: ten. Transportation and military exile followed full exile terms; upon expiry convicts were released, permitted to register locally. Under regular amnesty, military exile and banishment were not assigned but received in provincial houses of correction. Labor terms followed the preceding scale. Thereafter the five penal servitudes were not assigned, and even assigned military exile and banishment sharply declined. In year 29 the Ministry deleted military exile, merging nearby and near frontier into three exiles and converting extreme frontier and miasma to resettlement. Only two of the five military grades remained, and penal nomenclature changed again. In year 30 Liu Kunyi and Zhang Zhidong’s second reform memorial included nine articles on lenient detention. The Law Codification Office approved replacing cangue and beating with silver fines modeled on foreign practice. Five cangue grades at five cash each up to fifty blows (two taels five mace); sixty blows became five taels. Each grade added two taels five mace up to fifteen taels for one hundred blows. Those unable to pay converted fines to labor. One tael equaled four days’ labor, up to sixty days for fifteen taels. Theft could not be fined; thieves warranting cangue received one month’s labor instead; sixty blows: two months’ labor; seventy to one hundred blows: two months added per grade. A supplementary memorial exempted additional beating for military exile, banishment, and penal servitude. Thereafter cangue and beating were abandoned.
16
三十一年，修訂法律大臣沈家本等奏請刪除重法數端，略稱：「見行律例款目極繁，而最重之法，亟應先議刪除者，約有三事：一曰凌遲、梟首、戮屍。 凌遲之刑，唐以前無此名目。 遼史刑法志始列入正刑之內。 宋自熙寧以後，漸亦沿用。 元、明至今，相仍未改。 梟首在秦、漢時惟用諸夷族之誅，六朝梁、陳、齊、周諸律，始於斬之外別立梟名。 自隋迄元，復棄而不用。 今之斬梟，仍明制也。 戮屍一事，惟秦時成蟜軍反，其軍吏皆斬戮屍，見於始皇本紀。 此外歷代刑制，俱無此法。 明自萬曆十六年，定有戮屍條例，專指謀殺祖父母、父母而言。 國朝因之，後更推及於強盜。 凡此酷重之刑，固所以懲戒兇惡。 第刑至於斬，身首分離，已為至慘。 若命在頃忽，菹醢必令備嘗，氣久消亡，刀鋸猶難倖免，揆諸仁人之心，當必慘然不樂。 謂將以懲本犯，而被刑者魂魄何知； 謂將以警戒眾人，而習見習聞，轉感召其殘忍之性，實非聖世所宜遵。 請將凌遲、梟首、戮屍三項，一概刪除，死罪至斬決而止。 凡律例內凌遲、斬梟各條，俱改斬決。 斬決而下，依次遞減。 一曰緣坐。 緣坐之制，起於秦之參夷及收司連坐法。 漢高後除三族令，文帝除收孥相坐律，當時以為盛德。 惜夷族之誅，猶間用之。 晉以下仍有家屬從坐之法，唐律惟反叛、惡逆、不道，律有緣坐，他無有也。 今律則奸黨、交結近侍諸項俱緣坐矣，反獄、邪教諸項亦緣坐矣。 一案株連，動輒數十人。 夫以一人之故而波及全家，以無罪之人而科以重罪，漢文帝以為不正之法反害於民，北魏崔挺嘗曰『一人有罪，延及闔門，則司馬牛受桓魋之罰，柳下惠膺盜跖之誅，不亦哀哉』，其言皆篤論也。 今世各國，皆主持刑罰止及一身之義，與『罪人不孥』之古訓實相符合。 請將律內緣坐各條，除知情者仍坐罪外，其不知情者悉予寬免。 餘條有科及家屬者准此。 一曰刺字。 刺字乃古墨刑，漢之黥也。 文帝廢肉刑而黥亦廢，魏、晉、六朝雖有逃奴劫盜之刺，旋行旋廢。 隋、唐皆無此法。 至石晉天福間，始創刺配之制，相沿至今。 其初不過竊盜逃人，其後日加煩密。 在立法之意，原欲使莠民知恥，庶幾悔過而遷善。 詎知習於為非者，適予以標識，助其兇橫。 而偶罹法網者，則黥刺一膺，終身僇辱。 夫肉刑久廢，而此法獨存，漢文所謂刻肌膚痛而不德者，未能收弼教之益，而徒留此不德之名，豈仁政所宜出此。 擬請將刺字款目，概行刪除。 凡竊盜皆令收所習藝，按罪名輕重，定以年限，俾一技能嫻，得以餬口，自少再犯、三犯之人。 一切遞解人犯，嚴令地方官僉差押解，果能實力奉行，逃亡者自少也。 」奏上，諭令凌遲、梟首、戮屍三項永遠刪除。 所有現行律例內凌遲、斬梟各條，俱改為斬決； 其斬決各條，俱改為絞決； 絞決各條，俱改為絞監候，入於秋審情實； 斬監候各條，俱改為絞監候，與絞候人犯仍入於秋審，分別實緩。 至緣坐各條，除知情者仍治罪外，餘悉寬免。 其刺字等項，亦概行革除。 旨下，中外稱頌焉。
In year 31 Shen Jiaben and others memorialized deleting severe punishments: “Statutes are numerous; three should be deleted first: slicing, display of the severed head, and corpse dismemberment. Slicing had no name before the Tang. The Liao penal code first listed it among regular punishments. From Song Xining onward it was gradually adopted. Yuan, Ming, and the present have continued it unchanged. Qin and Han displayed severed heads only for barbarian executions; Six Dynasties Liang, Chen, Qi, and Zhou first named display apart from decapitation. Sui through Yuan abandoned it. Present decapitation with display still follows Ming practice. Corpse dismemberment appears only when Prince Chengjiao rebelled in Qin and his officers were decapitated and dismembered, in the First Emperor’s annals. No other dynasty’s penal code had this law. Ming Wanli 16 fixed corpse dismemberment for plotting to kill grandparents or parents. The present dynasty followed and later extended it to robbery. These cruel punishments restrain the vicious. Decapitation already separates body and head—the utmost misery. If life ends instantly yet every torment must be tasted, and when breath is gone the knife and saw still cannot be escaped—a humane heart must be deeply pained. To punish the principal offender—what does the executed soul know? To warn the multitude—constant sight and hearing summons cruelty instead; a sage age should not follow this. Delete slicing, display, and corpse dismemberment; capital punishment shall stop at decapitation. All slicing and decapitation-with-display articles shall become decapitation. Below decapitation, reduce grade by grade. Second: guilt by association. Guilt by association began with Qin’s triple extermination and collective punishment. Han Empress Gao abolished the three-clan order; Emperor Wen abolished implicating wives and children—then deemed great virtue. Yet clan extermination was still occasionally used. Jin onward still punished families; Tang had guilt by association only for rebellion, flagrant impiety, and impiety. Present law associates treacherous factions, palace associations, prison revolt, and heterodox sects. One case implicates dozens. One offense engulfing the whole family and punishing the innocent—Emperor Wen called this improper; Cui Ting said, “When one man is guilty and the whole gate suffers, Sima Niu bears Huan Tui’s punishment and Liuxia Hui Dao Zhi’s execution—is this not lamentable?”—weighty judgments both. Every nation today holds punishment reaches only the person, matching “guilt does not extend to children.” Except those who knew the crime, all ignorant parties in guilt-by-association articles shall be exempted. Other articles punishing families follow this. Third: tattooing. Tattooing is ancient ink punishment—the Han qing. Emperor Wen abolished corporal punishment and qing; Wei, Jin, and Six Dynasties tattooed escaped slaves and robbers but soon abolished it. Sui and Tang had no such law. Later Jin Tianfu created tattooing with assignment, continued to the present. At first only theft and fugitives; later it grew elaborate. The intent was to make wicked men ashamed and repent. Those accustomed to wrongdoing received a mark aiding ferocity instead. Those who chance into the net bear one chest tattoo and lifelong disgrace. Corporal punishment was long abolished yet tattooing remains—what Emperor Wen called cutting flesh without virtue, aiding instruction not gathered yet the name of non-virtue left—is this humane government? Delete all tattooing provisions. All thieves shall learn trades in houses of correction by offense severity, earning a living and reducing repeat offenders. Strictly order local officials to escort convicts; if enforced, fugitives will be few.” ” The throne ordered permanent deletion of slicing, display, and corpse dismemberment. All slicing and decapitation-with-display articles became decapitation; all decapitation articles became strangulation; all strangulation articles became strangulation awaiting custody in autumn confirmed review; decapitation awaiting custody became strangulation awaiting custody, entering autumn review with actual and deferred distinction. Guilt by association: except those who knew, all exempted. Tattooing was entirely abolished. When the edict descended, all praised it.
17
三十二年，法律館奏准將戲殺、誤殺、擅殺虛擬死罪各案，分別減為徒、流。 自此而死刑亦多輕減矣。 又是年法律館以婦女收贖，銀數太微，不足以資警戒，議准婦女犯笞、杖，照新章罰金。 徒、流、軍、遣，除不孝及奸、盜、詐偽舊例應實發者，改留本地習藝所工作，以十年為限，餘俱准其贖罪。 徒一年折銀二十兩，每五兩為一等，五徒准此遞加。 由徒入流，每一等加十兩，三流准此遞加。 遣、軍照滿流科斷。 如無力完繳，將應罰之數，照新章按銀數折算時日，改習工藝。 其犯該枷號，不論日數多寡，俱酌加五兩，以示區別。 自此而收贖銀數亦稍變矣。
In year 32 the Law Office reduced playful, accidental, unauthorized, and fictitious capital cases to penal servitude and exile. Capital punishment was also largely mitigated. That year the Law Office, finding women’s collection commutation too small, approved fines for women’s cangue and beating. Penal servitude, exile, military exile, and transportation—except unfilial conduct, adultery, theft, and fraud requiring actual assignment—meant ten years’ local labor; the rest could commute. One year of penal servitude: twenty taels, five taels per grade through five grades. Into exile, ten taels added per grade through three exiles. Transportation and military exile followed full exile. If unable to pay, convert silver to days of trade-learning under the new regulations. Cangue and collar offenses added five taels regardless of days. Collection commutation amounts also changed.
18
故宣統二年頒布之現行刑律，第將近數年奏定之章程採獲修入，於是刑制遂大有變更。 其五刑之目，首罰刑十，以代舊律之笞、杖。 一等罰，罰銀五錢，至十等罰，為銀十五兩，據法律館議覆恤刑獄之奏也。 次徒刑五，年限仍舊律。 次流刑三，道里仍舊律，然均不加杖，以法律館業經附片奏刪也。 次遣刑二：曰極邊足四千里及煙瘴地方安置，曰新疆當差。 以閏刑加入正刑，承用者廣，不得不別自為制也。 次死刑二：曰絞，曰斬。 時雖有死刑唯一之議，以舊制顯分等差，且凌遲、梟首等項甫經議減，不敢徑行廢斬也。 徒、流雖仍舊律，然為制不同。 按照習藝章程，五徒依限收入本地習藝所習藝； 流、遣毋論發配與否，俱應工作。 故於徒五等註明按限工作，流二千里注工作六年，二千五百里注工作八年，三千里注工作十年，遣刑俱注工作十二年。 收贖則根據婦女贖罪新章酌減銀數，改為通例。 罰刑照應罰之數折半收贖，徒一年贖銀十兩，每等加銀二兩五錢，至徒三年收贖銀二十兩。 流刑每等加銀五兩，至三千里贖銀三十五兩。 遣刑與滿流同科。 絞、斬則收贖銀四十兩。 亦分注於各刑條下。 然非例應收贖者，不得濫及也。 捐贖，據光緒二十九年刑部奏准照運糧事例，減半銀數，另輯為例。 其笞、杖雖不入正刑，仍留竹板，以備刑訊之用。 外此各刑具，盡行廢除，枷號亦一概芟削，刑制較為徑省矣。
The Xuantong 2 Current Penal Code incorporated recent approvals, and penal institutions changed greatly. The five punishments first list ten fines replacing cangue and beating. The first-grade fine is five mace up to fifteen taels for the tenth grade, per the Law Office’s leniency memorial. Next came five grades of penal servitude, with terms unchanged from the old Code. Next came three grades of exile at old distances, all without additional beating—the Law Office had memorialized its deletion. Next, two transportation grades: resettlement at four thousand li and miasma regions, and duty in Xinjiang. Because exile joined the principal punishments and was widely applied, it required a separate institution. Next, two capital punishments: strangulation and decapitation. Though some urged a single capital punishment, the old graded system and recent debate over slicing and display prevented abolishing decapitation outright. Penal servitude and exile followed the old Code in name but differed in practice. Under trade-learning regulations, all five penal servitudes were served in local houses of correction; exile and transportation, assigned or not, required labor. The five penal servitudes noted labor by term; two thousand li: six years; two thousand five hundred: eight; three thousand: ten; transportation: twelve years. Collection commutation adopted reduced amounts from the women’s commutation regulations as a general rule. Fines commuted at half: one year of penal servitude ten taels, two taels five mace per grade up to twenty for three years. Exile added five taels per grade up to thirty-five for three thousand li. Transportation matched full exile. Strangulation and decapitation commuted at forty taels. Amounts were noted under each punishment article. Those not statutorily eligible could not commute indiscriminately. Donated commutation followed the Guangxu 29 Ministry memorial, halving grain-transport amounts in separate sub-statutes. Cangue and beating left the principal punishments, but bamboo boards remained for interrogation. All other punishment implements and cangue and collar were abolished; the system grew more streamlined.

惟就地正法一項，始自咸豐三年。 時各省軍興，地方大吏，遇土匪竊發，往往先行正法，然後奏聞。 嗣軍務敉平，疆吏樂其便己，相沿不改。 光緒七八年間，御史胡隆洵、陳啟泰等屢以為言。 刑部聲請飭下各省，體察情形，仍照舊例解勘，分別題奏。 嗣各督撫俱覆稱地方不靖，礙難規復舊制。 刑部不得已，乃酌量加以限制，如實系土匪、馬賊、游勇、會匪，方准先行正法，尋常強盜，不得濫引。 自此章程行，沿及國變，而就地正法之制，訖未之能革。
Only execution on the spot was exceptional, beginning in Xianfeng 3. With warfare in every province, local grandees often executed bandits first and memorialized afterward. After pacification, frontier officials found it convenient and continued unchanged. In Guangxu 7–8 censors Hu Longxun and Chen Qitai repeatedly protested. The Ministry requested provinces examine circumstances and restore forwarding for investigation and separate memorials. All governors replied their regions were unsettled and the old system could not be restored. The Ministry limited on-the-spot execution to genuine bandits, horse bandits, roving soldiers, and secret-society members—not ordinary robbers. The regulation continued through dynastic change, and on-the-spot execution was never abolished.
