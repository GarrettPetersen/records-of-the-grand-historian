刑法二
Penal Law, Part Two
3
明律淵源唐代，以笞、杖、徒、流、死為五刑。 自笞一十至五十，為笞刑五。 自杖六十至一百，為杖刑五。 徒自杖六十徒一年起，每等加杖十，刑期半年，至杖一百徒三年，為徒五等。 流以二千里、二千五百里、三千里為三等，而皆加杖一百。 死刑二：曰斬，曰絞。 此正刑也。 其律例內之雜犯、斬絞、遷徙、充軍、枷號、刺字、論贖、凌遲、梟首、戮屍等刑，或取諸前代，或明所自創，要皆非刑之正。
The Ming code descended from Tang law and recognized five standard punishments: disciplinary beating, heavier beating, penal servitude, exile, and death. Light-stick beating ran in five steps from ten to fifty strokes. Heavy-stick beating ran in five steps from sixty to one hundred strokes. Penal servitude started at sixty strokes of the cane plus one year's labor; each step up added ten strokes and six months to the term, culminating at one hundred strokes and three years—five grades in all. Exile came in three distances—two thousand, two thousand five hundred, and three thousand li—with one hundred heavy strokes added in every case. Capital punishment had two forms: beheading and strangulation. These constituted the standard punishments. Statutes and subsidiary rules also prescribed punishments such as miscellaneous offenses, special beheading and strangulation, relocation, military exile, cangue detention, tattooing, ransom conversion, lingchi, displaying the head, and corpse mutilation—some borrowed from earlier ages, some invented under the Ming—all of them outside the five regular punishments.
4
清太祖、太宗之治遼東，刑制尚簡，重則斬，輕則鞭撲而已。 迨世祖入關，沿襲明制，初頒刑律，笞、杖以五折十，注入本刑各條。 康熙朝現行則例改為四折除零。 雍正三年之律，乃依例各於本律註明板數。 徒、流加杖，亦至配所照數折責。 蓋恐撲責過多，致傷生命，法外之仁也。 文武官犯笞、杖，則分別公私，代以罰俸、降級、降調，至革職而止。
When the Qing founders ruled the Eastern Liao, penal practice remained austere—capital cases ended in decapitation, minor ones in lashes and beatings, and little else. After the Shunzhi emperor crossed into China proper, the dynasty adopted Ming practice; the first Qing code reduced light- and heavy-stick counts to half the Ming scale (the "five-tenths of ten" rule), embedding the figures in each statutory article. Under Kangxi, the living regulations further cut the rate to four-tenths, discarding fractional strokes. The Yongzheng code of 1725 fixed the actual stroke counts in the text of each statute, as precedents required. Supplementary strokes for servitude and exile were also discounted to the prescribed figure once the convict reached his place of banishment. The aim was to keep flogging from becoming so severe that it endangered the convict's life—a humane allowance beyond strict legal form. Civil and military officials sentenced to light- or heavy-stick beating were treated differently for public and private offenses, paying fines in lieu of salary, suffering demotion or reassignment, or losing their posts altogether—never the physical blows themselves.
5
徒者，奴也，蓋奴辱之。 明發鹽場鐵冶煎鹽炒鐵，清則發本省驛遞。 其無驛縣，分撥各衙門充水火夫各項雜役，限滿釋放。
The character for penal servitude means "slave," and the punishment was meant to reduce the offender to servile shame. Ming convicts labored at salt pans and ironworks; Qing convicts served on the provincial relay network. Counties without relay stations distributed convicts among government offices as water carriers, stokers, and other menial hands, releasing them when their terms ended.
6
流犯，初制由各縣解交巡撫衙門，按照里數，酌發各處荒蕪及瀕海州縣。 嗣以各省分撥失均，不免趨避揀擇。 乾隆八年，刑部始纂輯三流道里表，將某省某府屬流犯，應流二千里者發何省何府屬安置，應流二千五百里者發何省何府屬安置，應流三千里者發何省何府屬安置，按計程途，限定地址，逐省逐府，分別開載。 嗣於四十九年及嘉慶六年兩次修訂。 然第於州縣之增並，道里之參差，略有修改，而大體不易。 律稱：「犯流妻妾從之，父祖子孫欲隨者聽。 」乾隆二十四年，將僉妻之例停止。 其軍、流、遣犯情原隨帶家屬者，不得官為資送，律成虛設矣。
At first, counties forwarded exiles to the provincial governor, who sent them to wasteland or coastal districts according to the statutory distance. Uneven provincial quotas soon led officials and convicts alike to game assignments and avoid the harshest destinations. In 1743 the Ministry of Justice compiled route tables for all three exile distances, specifying for every originating province and prefecture exactly which receiving province and prefecture would take convicts sentenced to two thousand, two thousand five hundred, or three thousand li—mileage calculated, destinations fixed, and each line entered in the register. The tables were revised twice, in 1784 and 1801. Later edits adjusted only for merged districts and shifted mileages; the overall allocation scheme stayed the same. Statute provided that a convict's wives and concubines must accompany him into exile, and that parents, grandparents, children, and grandchildren might follow if they chose. In 1759 the compulsory accompaniment of wives was abolished. Military exiles, ordinary exiles, and banished convicts who wished to take dependents along received no official escort or support, and the statutory family provisions became meaningless.
7
斬、絞，同是死刑。 然自漢以來，有秋後決囚之制。 唐律除犯惡逆以上及奴婢、部曲殺主者，從立春至秋分不得奏決死刑。 明弘治十年奏定真犯死罪決不待時者，凌遲十二條，斬三十七條，絞十二條； 真犯死罪秋後處決者，斬一百條，絞八十六條。 順治初定律，乃於各條內分晰註明，凡律不注監候者，皆立決也； 凡例不言立決者，皆監候也。 自此京、外死罪多決於秋，朝審遂為一代之大典。 雜犯斬、絞准徒五年與雜犯三流總徒四年，大都創自有明。 清律於官吏受贓，枉法不枉法，滿貫俱改為實絞，餘多仍之。 名實混淆，殊形轇轕。
Beheading and strangulation were both forms of capital punishment. Since Han times, however, capital sentences were normally carried out only after the autumn harvest. Tang law barred execution between spring and autumn except for the gravest treason and for slaves or dependents who murdered their masters. In 1497 the Ming court listed capital offenses to be executed immediately: twelve calling for lingchi, thirty-seven for decapitation, and twelve for strangulation; and those held for autumn execution: one hundred decapitations and eighty-six strangulations. The early Shunzhi code marked each article explicitly: any statute that did not say "imprisonment awaiting review" meant immediate execution; and any subsidiary rule that did not specify immediate execution meant imprisonment awaiting autumn review. Thereafter most death sentences in the capital and provinces were settled in autumn, and the autumn judicial review became one of the dynasty's defining institutions. Commuting miscellaneous capital offenses to five years' servitude and the three exile grades to four years' servitude were largely Ming inventions. The Qing code made full-scale official bribery—whether or not the law was perverted—actual strangulation rather than imprisonment awaiting review, while leaving most other Ming provisions intact. Labels and punishments no longer matched, and the system grew hopelessly entangled.
8
遷徙原於唐之殺人移鄉，而定罪則異。 律文沿用數條，然皆改為比流減半、徒二年，並不徙諸千里之外。 惟條例於土蠻、瑤、僮、苗人仇殺劫擄及改土為流之土司有犯，將家口實行遷徙。 然各有定地，亦不限千里也。
Relocation traced back to the Tang custom of banishing killers to another district, though the Qing defined the offense differently. A few statutory articles survived, but all were recast as half the statutory exile distance plus two years' servitude—never a thousand-li banishment. Subsidiary rules alone required whole households to be moved when tribal leaders among the Tuman, Yao, Zhuang, or Miao committed feud murders, raids, or kidnappings, or when former native chiefs whose domains had been regularized broke the law. Each case had a designated destination, but again without the thousand-li limit.
9
明之充軍，義主實邊，不盡與流刑相比附。 清初裁撤邊衛，而仍沿充軍之名。 後遂以附近、近邊、邊遠、極邊、煙瘴為五軍，且於滿流以上，為節級加等之用。 附近二千里，近邊二千五百里，邊遠三千里，極邊、煙瘴俱四千里。 在京兵部定地，在外巡撫定地。 雍正三年之律，第於十五布政司應發省分約略編定。 乾隆三十七年，兵部根據邦政紀略，輯為五軍道里表，凡發配者，視表所列。 然名為充軍，至配並不入營差操，第於每月朔望檢點，實與流犯無異。 而滿流加附近、近邊道里，反由遠而近，司讞者每苦其紛歧，而又有發遣名目。 初第發尚陽堡、寧古塔，或烏喇地方安插，後並發齊齊哈爾、黑龍江、三姓、喀爾喀、科布多，或各省駐防為奴。 乾隆年間，新疆開闢，例又有發往伊犁、烏魯木齊、巴里坤各回城分別為奴種地者。 咸、同之際，新疆道梗，又復改發內地充軍。 其制屢經變易，然軍遣止及其身。 苟情節稍輕，尚得更赦放還。 以視明之永遠軍戍，數世後猶句及本籍子孫者，大有間也。 若文武職官犯徒以上，輕則軍台效力，重則新疆當差。 成案相沿，遂為定例。 此又軍遣中之歧出者焉。
Under the Ming, military banishment was meant to reinforce the borders and did not map neatly onto ordinary exile. The early Qing abolished frontier garrisons but kept the term "military banishment." Five grades emerged—nearby, near border, distant border, extreme border, and miasmic regions—used to step punishments up or down from full exile. Nearby meant two thousand li, near border two thousand five hundred, distant border three thousand, and both extreme border and miasmic regions four thousand. The Ministry of War assigned destinations for convicts from the capital; provincial governors did so elsewhere. The 1725 code only roughly listed which provinces should receive convicts from each of the fifteen provincial administrations. In 1772 the Ministry of War, drawing on border-administration records, compiled route tables for all five military grades, and every banishment followed the listed assignment. Despite the name, banished convicts never joined garrison rolls or drill; they were checked only on the first and fifteenth of each month—in practice indistinguishable from ordinary exiles. Adding nearby and near-border distances on top of full exile reversed the logic of distance, baffling judicial officers—and then there was the separate category of "banishment dispatch." At first convicts went only to Shangyang Fort, Ningguta, or the Ula region; later also to Qiqihar, Heilongjiang, Sanxing, Khalkha, Kobdo, or into bondage at provincial garrisons. After Xinjiang was opened in the Qianlong era, precedents also sent convicts to Yili, Urumqi, Barkol, and Muslim towns as bondsmen to clear and farm the land. When the routes to Xinjiang were cut during the Xianfeng and Tongzhi reigns, convicts were again sent to military exile inside China proper. The rules shifted often, but military banishment and dispatch punished only the convict himself. Lighter cases could still win amnesty and return home. This was a far cry from the Ming practice of perpetual frontier service, which could still entangle a convict's descendants in his home district generations later. Civil and military officials guilty of more than penal servitude served lightly at relay stations or, in serious cases, at labor in Xinjiang. Accumulated case law turned the practice into fixed precedent. This was yet another special variant within military banishment and dispatch.
10
枷杻，本以羈獄囚。 明代問刑條例，於本罪外或加以枷號，示戮辱也。 清律犯罪免發遣條：「凡旗人犯罪，笞、杖各照數鞭責，軍、流、徒免發遣，分別枷號。 徒一年者，枷號二十日，每等遞加五日。 流二千里者，枷號五十日，每等亦遞加五日。 充軍附近者，枷號七十日，近邊、沿海、邊外者八十日，極邊、煙瘴者九十日。 」原立法之意，亦以旗人生則入檔，壯則充兵，鞏衛本根，未便離遠，有犯徒、流等罪，直以枷號代刑，強榦之義則然。 然犯系寡廉鮮恥，則銷除旗檔，一律實發，不姑息也。 若竊盜再犯加枷，初犯再犯計次加枷，犯奸加枷，賭博加枷，逃軍逃流加枷，暨一切敗檢逾閒、不顧行止者酌量加枷，則初無旗、民之別。 康熙八年，部議囚禁人犯止用細鍊，不用長枷，而枷號遂專為行刑之用。 其數初不過一月、二月、三月，後竟有論年或永遠枷號者。 始制重者七十觔，輕者六十斤。 乾隆五年，改定應枷人犯俱重二十五斤，然例尚有用百斤重枷者。 嘉慶以降，重枷斷用三十五斤，而於四川、陝西、湖北、河南、山東、安徽、廣東等省匪徒，又有系帶鐵杆石礅之例，亦一時創刑也。
The cangue and shackles were originally tools for restraining jail prisoners. Ming interrogation regulations sometimes added cangue time beyond the principal sentence as public humiliation. The Qing statute on exempt offenses reads: "Bannermen who commit crimes receive the full light- or heavy-stick flogging; for military banishment, exile, or penal servitude they are not sent away but wear the cangue instead." One year of servitude meant twenty days in the cangue, with five days added for each higher grade. Two thousand li of exile meant fifty days in the cangue, again increasing by five days per grade. Nearby military banishment brought seventy days in the cangue; near border, coastal, and outer-border banishment eighty days; extreme border and miasmic regions ninety days. The original idea was that Bannermen were registered at birth and mustered as soldiers in adulthood to guard the dynasty's foundation; they could not conveniently be sent far away, so the cangue substituted for exile or servitude—strengthening the trunk, as it were. But offenses of brazen moral corruption led to removal from the Banner rolls and actual banishment without leniency. Repeat theft, counted repeat offenses, adultery, gambling, desertion from banishment or exile, and every kind of moral lapse could bring discretionary cangue time—with no distinction between Bannermen and civilians. In 1669 the ministries ruled that jail prisoners should wear only light chains, not the long cangue, so cangue detention became purely a punitive sentence. Terms began at one to three months but eventually stretched to years or even life in the cangue. Originally the heavy cangue weighed seventy jin and the light sixty. In 1740 all cangues were standardized at twenty-five jin, though precedents still allowed hundred-jin heavy cangues. After Jiaqing the heavy cangue was capped at thirty-five jin, but bandits in Sichuan, Shaanxi, Hubei, Henan, Shandong, Anhui, Guangdong, and elsewhere could be shackled with iron rods and stone weights—another ad hoc punishment of the age.
11
刺字，古肉刑之一，律第嚴於賊盜。 乃其後條例滋多，刺緣坐，刺兇犯，刺逃軍、逃流，刺外遣、改遣、改發。 有刺事由者，有刺地方者，並有分刺滿、漢文字者。 初刺右臂，次刺左臂，次刺右面、左面。 大抵律多刺臂，例多刺面。 若竊盜責充警跡，二三年無過，或緝獲強盜二名以上、竊盜三名以上，例又准其起除刺字，復為良民。 蓋惡惡雖嚴，而亦未嘗不予以自新之路焉。
Tattooing was an ancient corporal punishment; the code applied it most strictly to thieves and robbers. Later subsidiary rules multiplied: tattoos for collective guilt, violent criminals, deserters from military banishment or exile, and those externally banished, re-banished, or re-assigned. Some marks recorded the offense, some the place of sentencing, and some used both Manchu and Chinese characters. The sequence was right arm, then left arm, then right cheek and left cheek. Statutes tended to tattoo the arms; subsidiary rules tended to tattoo the face. Thieves assigned as constabulary trackers who went two or three years without offense, or who captured two robbers or three thieves, could have their tattoos removed and regain good standing. Punishment of wrongdoing was severe, yet a path to reform was never wholly closed.
12
贖刑有三：一曰納贖，無力照律決配，有力照例納贖。 二曰收贖，老幼廢疾、天文生及婦人折杖，照律收贖。 三曰贖罪，官員正妻及例難的決，並婦人有力者，照例贖罪。 收贖名曰律贖，原本唐律收贖。 贖罪名為例贖，則明代所創行。 順治修律，五刑不列贖銀數目。 雍正三年，始將明律贖圖內應贖銀數斟酌修改，定為納贖諸例圖。 然自康熙現行例定有承問官濫准納贖交部議處之條，而前明納贖及贖罪諸舊例又節經刪改，故律贖俱照舊援用，而例贖則多成具文。
Commutation took three forms: payment ransom, for those with means who could buy off the statutory sentence while the poor were punished as the law required; collection ransom, for the aged, young, disabled, astronomy students, and women whose beating was commuted; and offense ransom, for officials' principal wives, cases where beating was impracticable, and women of means. Collection ransom, called "statutory ransom," came straight from Tang law. Offense ransom, or "precedent ransom," was a Ming innovation. The Shunzhi code did not list ransom amounts for the five punishments. In 1725 the Yongzheng court revised the Ming ransom tables and issued new charts for payment ransom. Yet Kangxi regulations punished officials who improperly allowed payment ransom, and many Ming payment- and offense-ransom precedents had been pared away—so statutory ransom continued in use while precedent ransom largely became a dead letter.
13
其捐贖一項，順治十八年，有官員犯流徒籍沒認工贖罪例； 康熙二十九年，有死罪現監人犯輸米邊口贖罪例； 三十年，有軍流人犯捐贖例； 三十四年，有通倉運米捐贖例； 三十九年，有永定河工捐贖例； 六十年，有河工捐贖例。 然皆事竣停止，其歷朝沿用者，惟雍正十二年戶部會同刑部奏准預籌運糧事例，不論旗、民，罪應斬、絞，非常赦所不原者，三品以上官照西安駝捐例捐運糧銀一萬二千兩，四品官照營田例捐運糧銀五千兩，五、六品官照營田例捐銀四千兩，七品以下、進士、舉人二千五百兩，貢、監生二千兩，平人一千二百兩，軍、流各減十分之四，徒以下各減十分之六，俱准免罪。 西安駝捐，行自雍正元年，營田例則五年所定也。 乾隆十七年，西安布政使張若震奏請另定捐贖笞、杖銀數。 經部議，預籌運糧事例，杖、笞與徒罪不分輕重，一例捐贖，究未允協。 除犯枷號、杖責者照徒罪捐贖外，酌擬分杖為一等，笞為一等。 其數，杖視徒遞減，笞視杖遞減。 二十三年，諭將斬、絞緩決各犯納贖之例永行停止。 遇有恩赦減等時，其憚於遠行者，再准收贖。 而贖鍰則仍視原擬罪名，不得照減等之罪。 著為令。 嗣後官員贖罪者，俱照運糧事例核奪。 刑部別設贖罪處，專司其事。 此又律贖、例贖而外，別自為制者矣。
Donated ransom appeared in many temporary forms: in 1661, officials sentenced to exile or servitude could work off sentences after confiscation; in 1690, death-row prisoners could deliver grain to the frontier; in 1691, military exiles and exiles could donate ransom; in 1695, convicts could ransom themselves by hauling grain to the Tongcang granary; in 1700, by donating to Yongding River works; and in 1721, by donating to general river works. All of these ended when the projects did. Only the 1734 precedent for pre-paying transport grain endured: Bannermen and civilians alike, for capital offenses not excluded from amnesty, could buy remission—twelve thousand taels for officials of third rank and above (per the Xi'an camel-donation scale), five thousand for fourth rank, four thousand for fifth and sixth, two thousand five hundred for seventh rank and below plus jinshi and juren, two thousand for tribute and academy students, twelve hundred for commoners, with military banishment and exile reduced forty percent and lesser sentences sixty percent. The Xi'an camel-donation scale dated from 1723; the military-colony scale from 1727. In 1752 Xi'an financial commissioner Zhang Ruozhen asked to set separate donated-ransom rates for light- and heavy-stick offenses. The ministries replied that the transport-grain precedent treated light stick, heavy stick, and servitude alike without regard to severity, and that a uniform ransom rate was unsatisfactory. Apart from cangue offenders and those liable to staff beating, who ransomed themselves at servitude rates, the ministries proposed separate scales for heavy-stick and light-stick offenses. Staff rates stepped down from servitude rates, and light-stick rates from staff rates. In 1758 the throne ordered an end to ransom payments for prisoners awaiting autumn review on capital charges. When amnesties reduced sentences, those who dreaded long journeys could again pay collection ransom. The ransom sum, however, was still calculated on the original charge, not the reduced one. These rules were promulgated as statute. Thereafter all officials who bought remission were assessed under the transport-grain scale. The Ministry of Justice set up a dedicated office to handle offense ransom. This was yet another system distinct from statutory and precedent ransom.
14
凌遲，用之十惡中不道以上諸重罪，號為極刑。 梟首，則強盜居多。 戮屍，所以待惡逆及強盜應梟諸犯之監故者。 凡此諸刑，類皆承用明律，略有通變，行之二百餘年。 至過誤殺之賠人，竊盜之割腳筋，重辟減等之貫耳鼻，強盜、貪官及窩逃之籍家產，或沿自盛京定例，或順治朝偶行之峻令，不久革除，非所論也。
Lingchi applied to the gravest offenses among the Ten Abominations from "unnatural conduct" upward and was called the supreme penalty. Displaying the head was chiefly reserved for robbers. Corpse mutilation punished treason and robbers who should have been displayed but died in prison. These punishments largely followed Ming law with minor Qing adjustments and remained in force for more than two centuries. Compensation for accidental homicide, cutting heel tendons for theft, piercing ears and nose when commuting capital sentences, and confiscating the property of robbers, corrupt officials, and harborers of fugitives—some from Mukden regulations, some harsh Shunzhi experiments soon repealed—lie outside this survey.
15
自光緒變法，二十八年，山西巡撫趙爾巽奏請各省通設罪犯習藝所。 經刑部議准，徒犯毋庸發配，按照年限，於本地收所習藝。 軍、流為常赦所不原者，照定例發配，到配一律收所習藝。 流二千里限工作六年，二千五百里八年，三千里者十年。 遣軍照滿流年限計算，限滿釋放，聽其自謀生計，並准在配所入籍為民。 若為常赦所得原者，無論軍、流，俱無庸發配，即在本省收所習藝。 工作年限，亦照前科算。 自此五徒並不發配，即軍、流之發配者，數亦銳減矣。 二十九年，刑部奏准刪除充軍名目，將附近、近邊，邊遠併入三流，極邊及煙瘴改為安置，仍與當差並行。 自此五軍第留其二，而刑名亦改變矣。 三十年，劉坤一、張之洞會奏變法第二摺內，有恤刑獄九條。 其省刑責條內，經法律館議准，笞、杖等罪，仿照外國罰金之法，改為罰銀。 凡律例內笞刑五，以五錢為一等，至笞五十罰銀二兩五錢，杖六十者改為罰五兩。 每一等加二兩五錢，以次遞加，至杖一百改為罰十五兩而止。 如無力完納，折為作工。 應罰一兩，折作工四日，以次遞加，至十五兩折作工六十日而止。 然竊盜未便罰金，議將犯竊應擬笞罪者，改科工作一月； 杖六十者，改科工作兩月； 杖七十至一百，每等遞加兩月。 又附片請將軍、流、徒加杖概予寬免，無庸決責。 自此而笞、杖二刑廢棄矣。
During the Guangxu reforms, in 1902 Shanxi governor Zhao Erxun asked every province to establish vocational workhouses for convicts. The Ministry of Justice approved: servitude convicts would no longer be sent away but held locally in workhouses for the statutory term. Military banishment and exile excluded from regular amnesty were still assigned as before, but upon arrival all entered workhouses. Two thousand li of exile meant six years' labor, two thousand five hundred eight years, and three thousand ten years. Banishment dispatch and military banishment followed full-exile terms; when terms expired convicts were released, free to make a living and register as civilians at their place of assignment. Those covered by regular amnesty, whether military banishment or exile, were not sent away but held in provincial workhouses. Labor terms were calculated as above. Thereafter the five servitude grades were never sent away, and even assigned military banishments and exiles dropped sharply in number. In 1903 the Ministry of Justice abolished military banishment as a category, folding nearby, near border, and distant border into the three exile grades and recasting extreme border and miasmic regions as resettlement, while retaining labor duty. Only two of the five former military grades survived, and penal terminology shifted again. In 1904 Liu Kunyi and Zhang Zhidong's joint reform memorial included nine articles on leniency in criminal justice. Under the article on reducing corporal punishment, the Law Codification Bureau approved replacing light- and heavy-stick beating with silver fines modeled on Western practice. The five light-stick grades were set at five cash per grade, up to fifty strokes at two taels five mace; sixty heavy strokes became a five-tael fine. Each higher grade added two taels five mace, topping out at one hundred heavy strokes converted to fifteen taels. Those unable to pay could work off the fine. One tael equaled four days' labor, scaling up to fifteen taels for sixty days. Theft, however, was not suited to fines; thieves who would have received light-stick sentences were instead assigned one month's labor; sixty heavy strokes to two months; and from seventy to one hundred strokes, two months added per grade. A supplementary memorial also asked to abolish supplementary beating for military banishment, exile, and servitude. Light- and heavy-stick beating were thus abolished.

三十一年，修訂法律大臣沈家本等奏請刪除重法數端，略稱：「見行律例款目極繁，而最重之法，亟應先議刪除者，約有三事：一曰凌遲、梟首、戮屍。 凌遲之刑，唐以前無此名目。 遼史刑法志始列入正刑之內。 宋自熙寧以後，漸亦沿用。 元、明至今，相仍未改。 梟首在秦、漢時惟用諸夷族之誅，六朝梁、陳、齊、周諸律，始於斬之外別立梟名。 自隋迄元，復棄而不用。 今之斬梟，仍明制也。 戮屍一事，惟秦時成蟜軍反，其軍吏皆斬戮屍，見於始皇本紀。 此外歷代刑制，俱無此法。 明自萬曆十六年，定有戮屍條例，專指謀殺祖父母、父母而言。 國朝因之，後更推及於強盜。 凡此酷重之刑，固所以懲戒兇惡。 第刑至於斬，身首分離，已為至慘。 若命在頃忽，菹醢必令備嘗，氣久消亡，刀鋸猶難倖免，揆諸仁人之心，當必慘然不樂。 謂將以懲本犯，而被刑者魂魄何知； 謂將以警戒眾人，而習見習聞，轉感召其殘忍之性，實非聖世所宜遵。 請將凌遲、梟首、戮屍三項，一概刪除，死罪至斬決而止。 凡律例內凌遲、斬梟各條，俱改斬決。 斬決而下，依次遞減。 一曰緣坐。 緣坐之制，起於秦之參夷及收司連坐法。 漢高後除三族令，文帝除收孥相坐律，當時以為盛德。 惜夷族之誅，猶間用之。 晉以下仍有家屬從坐之法，唐律惟反叛、惡逆、不道，律有緣坐，他無有也。 今律則奸黨、交結近侍諸項俱緣坐矣，反獄、邪教諸項亦緣坐矣。 一案株連，動輒數十人。 夫以一人之故而波及全家，以無罪之人而科以重罪，漢文帝以為不正之法反害於民，北魏崔挺嘗曰『一人有罪，延及闔門，則司馬牛受桓魋之罰，柳下惠膺盜跖之誅，不亦哀哉』，其言皆篤論也。 今世各國，皆主持刑罰止及一身之義，與『罪人不孥』之古訓實相符合。 請將律內緣坐各條，除知情者仍坐罪外，其不知情者悉予寬免。 餘條有科及家屬者准此。 一曰刺字。 刺字乃古墨刑，漢之黥也。 文帝廢肉刑而黥亦廢，魏、晉、六朝雖有逃奴劫盜之刺，旋行旋廢。 隋、唐皆無此法。 至石晉天福間，始創刺配之制，相沿至今。 其初不過竊盜逃人，其後日加煩密。 在立法之意，原欲使莠民知恥，庶幾悔過而遷善。 詎知習於為非者，適予以標識，助其兇橫。 而偶罹法網者，則黥刺一膺，終身僇辱。 夫肉刑久廢，而此法獨存，漢文所謂刻肌膚痛而不德者，未能收弼教之益，而徒留此不德之名，豈仁政所宜出此。 擬請將刺字款目，概行刪除。 凡竊盜皆令收所習藝，按罪名輕重，定以年限，俾一技能嫻，得以餬口，自少再犯、三犯之人。 一切遞解人犯，嚴令地方官僉差押解，果能實力奉行，逃亡者自少也。 」奏上，諭令凌遲、梟首、戮屍三項永遠刪除。 所有現行律例內凌遲、斬梟各條，俱改為斬決； 其斬決各條，俱改為絞決； 絞決各條，俱改為絞監候，入於秋審情實； 斬監候各條，俱改為絞監候，與絞候人犯仍入於秋審，分別實緩。 至緣坐各條，除知情者仍治罪外，餘悉寬免。 其刺字等項，亦概行革除。 旨下，中外稱頌焉。
In 1905 revising law minister Shen Jiaben and colleagues memorialized to abolish several harsh penalties, observing: "Current statutes and precedents are vast, but three supreme punishments should be removed first: lingchi, displaying the head, and corpse mutilation." Lingchi did not exist before the Tang. The Liao penal code first listed it among regular punishments. The Song adopted it gradually after the Xining era. Yuan, Ming, and Qing kept it without change. In Qin and Han, displaying the head applied only to clan extermination; the Six Dynasties codes of Liang, Chen, Qi, and Zhou first named it as a punishment separate from decapitation. From Sui through Yuan it fell out of use. Today's beheading with display of the head still follows Ming practice. Corpse mutilation appears only once in antiquity: when Prince Cheng Jiao rebelled in Qin, his officers were beheaded and their corpses dismembered, as recorded in the First Emperor's annals. No other dynasty's penal code included this punishment. The Ming fixed a corpse-mutilation rule in 1588 specifically for plotting to murder grandparents or parents. The Qing followed suit and later extended it to robbers. These savage punishments were meant to deter the vicious. Yet beheading already separates body and head—the utmost cruelty. If death comes in an instant, yet the victim must still endure dismemberment; when breath is long gone, the knife and saw still cannot be spared—any humane mind must recoil in horror. If the aim is to punish the offender, what can a dead man's spirit know? If the aim is to warn the public, habitual exposure only breeds cruelty—hardly fitting for an enlightened age. We ask that lingchi, displaying the head, and corpse mutilation be abolished altogether, with capital punishment ending at beheading. All statutory articles calling for lingchi or beheading with display should be changed to simple beheading. Lesser penalties should be stepped down accordingly. Second, collective punishment. Collective punishment began with Qin clan extermination and office-linked joint liability. Empress Lü abolished the three-clan extermination; Emperor Wen ended the law imprisoning relatives for joint guilt—both hailed as great acts of mercy. Yet clan extermination was still used from time to time. Jin and later dynasties still punished families; Tang law allowed collective punishment only for rebellion, treason, and unnatural conduct. Today's code implicates associates in factionalism, collusion with palace attendants, prison revolt, and heterodox sects alike. A single case could drag in dozens of people. To punish an entire family for one person's crime, to impose heavy sentences on the innocent—Emperor Wen called such laws harmful; Cui Ting of Northern Wei said: "When one man sins and the whole household suffers, Sima Niu pays for Huan Tui's crime and Liuxia Hui dies for Robber Zhi—how lamentable!"—sound words both. Modern nations hold that punishment should fall on the offender alone—a principle that matches the ancient teaching that guilt does not extend to one's family. We ask that collective-punishment articles be revised so that only those who knew the facts are punished and the ignorant are spared. Other articles punishing family members should follow the same rule. Third, tattooing. Tattooing was the ancient ink punishment—the Han branding penalty. Emperor Wen abolished corporal punishment and branding with it; Wei, Jin, and the Six Dynasties occasionally tattooed fugitive slaves and robbers, but the practice was repeatedly adopted and dropped. Sui and Tang law had no such provision. Only in the Later Jin Tianfu era was tattooing combined with banishment introduced, and it has continued ever since. At first it applied only to thieves and fugitives; later it grew ever more elaborate. The original intent was to shame wrongdoers into repentance and reform. Yet habitual offenders treated the mark as a badge that encouraged their brutality. Those who stumbled into the law once bore a lifelong mark of disgrace. Corporal punishment was abolished long ago, yet tattooing alone survived. Emperor Wen called such punishments pain without virtue—they do not reform but only leave an indelible stain. How can humane government tolerate them? We propose abolishing all tattooing provisions. All thieves should enter workhouses for terms fixed by offense severity, learn a trade, and support themselves—thereby reducing repeat offenders. If local officials strictly assigned reliable escorts for all transported convicts, escapes would become rare. The memorial was approved, and an edict permanently abolished lingchi, displaying the head, and corpse mutilation. All articles calling for lingchi or beheading with display were changed to simple beheading; all immediate-beheading articles were changed to immediate strangulation; all immediate-strangulation articles were changed to strangulation awaiting autumn review; all beheading-awaiting-review articles became strangulation awaiting review; all such prisoners still entered autumn review, classified as confirmed or reprieved. Collective-punishment articles were revised: only those who knew were punished; the rest were spared. Tattooing and similar punishments were abolished as well. When the decree was issued, praise resounded throughout the empire.
17
三十二年，法律館奏准將戲殺、誤殺、擅殺虛擬死罪各案，分別減為徒、流。 自此而死刑亦多輕減矣。 又是年法律館以婦女收贖，銀數太微，不足以資警戒，議准婦女犯笞、杖，照新章罰金。 徒、流、軍、遣，除不孝及奸、盜、詐偽舊例應實發者，改留本地習藝所工作，以十年為限，餘俱准其贖罪。 徒一年折銀二十兩，每五兩為一等，五徒准此遞加。 由徒入流，每一等加十兩，三流准此遞加。 遣、軍照滿流科斷。 如無力完繳，將應罰之數，照新章按銀數折算時日，改習工藝。 其犯該枷號，不論日數多寡，俱酌加五兩，以示區別。 自此而收贖銀數亦稍變矣。
In 1906 the Law Codification Bureau approved reducing sport killing, accidental killing, and unauthorized killing cases from nominal capital offenses to servitude or exile. Capital punishment was thus further lightened. That same year the Bureau found women's collection-ransom amounts too small to deter offense and approved fines under the new regulations for women guilty of light- or heavy-stick offenses. Servitude, exile, military banishment, and banishment dispatch—except unfilial conduct, adultery, theft, and fraud requiring actual assignment—were commuted to ten years' labor in local workhouses; all others could pay offense ransom. One year of servitude equaled twenty taels of silver, increasing by five taels per grade through all five servitude grades. From servitude into exile, each grade added ten taels through all three exile grades. Banishment dispatch and military banishment were assessed at full-exile rates. Those unable to pay could work off the sum in vocational labor at the rates set by the new regulations. Cangue sentences, regardless of duration, carried an additional five-tael surcharge. Collection-ransom amounts shifted accordingly.

故宣統二年頒布之現行刑律，第將近數年奏定之章程採獲修入，於是刑制遂大有變更。 其五刑之目，首罰刑十，以代舊律之笞、杖。 一等罰，罰銀五錢，至十等罰，為銀十五兩，據法律館議覆恤刑獄之奏也。 次徒刑五，年限仍舊律。 次流刑三，道里仍舊律，然均不加杖，以法律館業經附片奏刪也。 次遣刑二：曰極邊足四千里及煙瘴地方安置，曰新疆當差。 以閏刑加入正刑，承用者廣，不得不別自為制也。 次死刑二：曰絞，曰斬。 時雖有死刑唯一之議，以舊制顯分等差，且凌遲、梟首等項甫經議減，不敢徑行廢斬也。 徒、流雖仍舊律，然為制不同。 按照習藝章程，五徒依限收入本地習藝所習藝； 流、遣毋論發配與否，俱應工作。 故於徒五等註明按限工作，流二千里注工作六年，二千五百里注工作八年，三千里注工作十年，遣刑俱注工作十二年。 收贖則根據婦女贖罪新章酌減銀數，改為通例。 罰刑照應罰之數折半收贖，徒一年贖銀十兩，每等加銀二兩五錢，至徒三年收贖銀二十兩。 流刑每等加銀五兩，至三千里贖銀三十五兩。 遣刑與滿流同科。 絞、斬則收贖銀四十兩。 亦分注於各刑條下。 然非例應收贖者，不得濫及也。 捐贖，據光緒二十九年刑部奏准照運糧事例，減半銀數，另輯為例。 其笞、杖雖不入正刑，仍留竹板，以備刑訊之用。 外此各刑具，盡行廢除，枷號亦一概芟削，刑制較為徑省矣。
The penal code promulgated in 1910 incorporated regulations approved in recent years, and the penal system was transformed. Among the five punishments, ten grades of fines replaced the old light- and heavy-stick beating. The first fine grade was five mace of silver; the tenth fifteen taels—per the Law Codification Bureau's reply to the memorial on leniency in criminal justice. Next came the five grades of penal servitude, with terms unchanged from the old code. Next came the three exile grades at the old distances, but without supplementary beating—the Law Codification Bureau had already secured its abolition. Next came two banishment-dispatch grades: resettlement at the extreme frontier or in miasmic regions, and labor duty in Xinjiang. Because these supplementary punishments had been folded into regular practice so widely, they required a separate statutory category. Finally came two capital punishments: strangulation and beheading. Some argued for a single form of capital punishment, but the old system clearly distinguished grades, and with lingchi and display of the head only just abolished, beheading could not be discarded outright. Servitude and exile kept the old statutory labels but worked differently in practice. Under the vocational-workhouse regulations, all five servitude grades were held locally for the statutory term; exiles and banishment-dispatch convicts, whether or not sent away, were all required to work. The code therefore specified labor terms for each servitude grade, six years for two thousand li of exile, eight for two thousand five hundred, ten for three thousand, and twelve for banishment dispatch. Collection-ransom amounts were reduced on the model of the new women's-ransom regulations and made a general rule. Fines could be commuted at half the assessed amount; one year of servitude cost ten taels, increasing by two taels five mace per grade to twenty taels for three years. Each exile grade added five taels, up to thirty-five taels for three thousand li. Banishment dispatch was assessed at the full-exile rate. Strangulation and beheading could be commuted for forty taels. These amounts were noted under each relevant article. Only those statutorily eligible could claim collection ransom. Donated ransom followed the 1903 Ministry of Justice approval of halved transport-grain rates, compiled as a separate precedent. Light- and heavy-stick beating was no longer a regular punishment, but bamboo boards were kept for interrogation under torture. All other penal implements were abolished, cangue detention eliminated altogether, and the system became considerably simpler.

惟就地正法一項，始自咸豐三年。 時各省軍興，地方大吏，遇土匪竊發，往往先行正法，然後奏聞。 嗣軍務敉平，疆吏樂其便己，相沿不改。 光緒七八年間，御史胡隆洵、陳啟泰等屢以為言。 刑部聲請飭下各省，體察情形，仍照舊例解勘，分別題奏。 嗣各督撫俱覆稱地方不靖，礙難規復舊制。 刑部不得已，乃酌量加以限制，如實系土匪、馬賊、游勇、會匪，方准先行正法，尋常強盜，不得濫引。 自此章程行，沿及國變，而就地正法之制，訖未之能革。
Only summary execution on the spot, introduced in 1853, remained. When rebellion spread across the provinces, local governors often executed bandits on the spot and reported afterward. After the wars ended, frontier officials found the practice convenient and kept it up. In 1881–1882 censors Hu Longxun, Chen Qitai, and others repeatedly protested. The Ministry of Justice asked the provinces to investigate and restore the old procedure of forwarding cases for review and memorializing separately. Every governor replied that local conditions were too unsettled to restore the old system. The Ministry of Justice reluctantly limited the practice: only genuine bandits, mounted brigands, roaming soldiers, or secret-society members could be summarily executed; ordinary robbers could not be included. Once this regulation took effect, it continued through the fall of the dynasty, and summary execution on the spot was never abolished.
