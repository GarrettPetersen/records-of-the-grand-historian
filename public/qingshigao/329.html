宋元俊薛琮張芝元董天弼柴大紀
Song Yuanjun, Xue Cong, Zhang Zhiyuan, Dong Tianbi, and Chai Daji
3
宋元俊，字甸芳，江南懷遠人。 以武進士授四川成都營守備，遷懷遠營都司。 乾隆二十年，孔撒、麻書兩土司構釁，金川、綽斯甲布兩土司乘隙為亂，元俊為撫定，集孔撒、麻書、金川、綽斯甲布、革布什咱、綽沃、白立、章谷、瞻對諸土司斷曲直，使頂經立誓。 累遷阜和營游擊。
Song Yuanjun, courtesy name Dianfang, came from Huaiyuan in Jiangnan. Having passed the military examinations, he was appointed garrison commandant of the Chengdu Camp in Sichuan and later promoted to brigade commander of the Huaiyuan Camp. In Qianlong 20 (1755), the Kongsa and Mashu chieftains stirred up trouble, and the Jinchuan and Chosichab chieftains seized the moment to rise in revolt. Yuanjun was appointed to pacify the region. He convened the chieftains of Kongsa, Mashu, Jinchuan, Chosichab, Geshizhai, Zhuowo, Baili, Zhanggu, Zhando, and others, adjudicated their disputes, and had them swear binding oaths on scripture. He rose through successive promotions to acting colonel of the Fuhe Camp.
4
二十九年，金川土司郎卡侵丹壩、綽斯甲布兩土司，諸土司請兵，署總督阿桂、提督岳鍾琪奏令元俊偕署副將長清諭各土司合兵進剿。 移漳臘營參將，坐事左遷。 三十五年，小金川土司澤旺之子僧格桑掠鄂克什，阿桂檄元俊宣諭僧格桑還侵地及所掠番民。 复補阜和營游擊。 三十六年，革布什咱頭人結郎卡子索諾木據革布什咱官寨，戕土司策楞多布丹，總督阿爾泰復令元俊往宣諭。 小金川圍鄂克什、達木巴宗，侵明正土司，據納頂寨，元俊與參將薛琮、都司李天佑率兵討之，收納頂寨，進攻索布大寨。 琮率兵自山梁潛度，元俊與天佑渡河夾擊，獲石卡十八，屢戰皆捷，明正土司碉寨七百餘盡复。
In Qianlong 29 (1764), the Jinchuan chieftain Langka attacked the Dandan and Chosichab chieftains, and the affected chieftains appealed for military aid. Acting Governor Agui and Provincial Military Commissioner Yue Zhongqi recommended that Yuanjun, together with Acting Deputy General Chang Qing, urge the chieftains to unite their forces for a joint punitive campaign. He was reassigned as battalion major of the Zhangla Camp but was later demoted after being implicated in a disciplinary matter. In Qianlong 35 (1770), Senggesang, son of the Little Jinchuan chieftain Zewang, raided Ekezhi. Agui directed Yuanjun to demand that Senggesang restore the seized lands and return the tribal subjects he had carried off. He was subsequently reinstated as acting colonel of the Fuhe Camp. In Qianlong 36 (1771), Sonam, son of the Geshizhai headman Jielangka, seized the Geshizhai official fortress and murdered Chieftain Celeng Duobudan. Governor Altai once again dispatched Yuanjun to deliver an imperial admonition. Little Jinchuan forces besieged Ekezhi and Damubazong, encroached on the Mingzheng chieftain's domain, and occupied Nadai Stockade. Yuanjun, Battalion Major Xue Cong, and Brigade Commander Li Tianyou led a punitive force that retook Nadai and pressed the attack on Suobu's main fortress. Xue Cong led his men in a stealthy crossing along the mountain ridge while Yuanjun and Li Tianyou forded the river for a converging assault. They captured eighteen stone forts and won victory after victory until more than seven hundred Mingzheng towers and stockades had been fully restored.
5
師入小金川境，取噶中拉、莫如納、扎功拉等地，進克納咱。 阿爾泰及侍郎桂林以聞，擢松潘鎮總兵。 師攻甲木，賊據喇嘛寺為固。 元俊及守備陳定國攻破之，盡收所屬城、卡、碉、寨，據墨爾多山梁。 師復進，天佑、定國攻西山梁，元俊同侍衛六十一、參將巴克坦布等自喇嘛寺繞攻郭松，參領普寧自西山麓沿河攻甲木，侍衛哈青阿及琮自東山麓攻卡丫。 師行以夜半，戰自卯至巳，卡丫、郭松、甲木皆克。 賜元俊孔雀翎。
The army crossed into Little Jinchuan territory, seized Gazhongla, Moruna, Zhagongla, and neighboring positions, and went on to capture Nazan. Altai and Vice Minister Guilin reported his achievements to the throne, and Yuanjun was promoted to brigade general commanding the Songpan garrison. When the army assaulted Jiamu, the rebels fortified themselves in the Lama Temple. Yuanjun and Garrison Commandant Chen Dingguo stormed the temple and broke the rebel line, recovering every affiliated town, pass, tower, and stockade before securing the Mo'erduo ridge. The army pressed forward again on several axes: Li Tianyou and Chen Dingguo struck the western ridge; Yuanjun, Bodyguard Liushiyi, Battalion Major Baketanbu, and others swung around the Lama Temple to assault Guosong; Company Commander Puning moved up the river from the western foothills against Jiamu; and Bodyguard Haqing'a and Xue Cong attacked Kaya from the east. The columns moved out at midnight and fought from dawn until mid-morning; Kaya, Guosong, and Jiamu all fell in succession. Yuanjun was awarded the peacock feather insignia of distinction.
6
三十七年，師攻革布​​什咱，元俊請於桂林，分兵為五道：一自郭宗濟野宗攻木巴拉博租； 一自章谷渡河夾攻，俾賊前後受敵，兩軍既合，先據默資溝，截金川來路，進取吉地官寨； 一自巴旺之高石、嘉舉諸山，分道攻薩瑪多監藏布覺，取吉地； 一自茂紐攻沙沖； 一自喀勒塔爾攻黨哩，會兵取丹東。 策定，元俊及游擊吳錦江等自章谷渡河據格藏橋，哈青阿、天佑出郭宗濟野宗，兩隊軍夾攻，賊驚潰，遂克木巴拉博租、薩瑪多監藏布覺諸地。 進克吉地官寨及默資溝。 參將常泰等克黨哩，都司李天貴等克沙沖，元俊复克丹東。 复革布什咱地三百餘裡，民戶二千餘。
In Qianlong 37 (1772), during the campaign against Geshizhai, Yuanjun proposed to Guilin a five-pronged advance: the first column would move from Guozong Jiyezong against Mubalabozu; a second would cross from Zhanggu to hit the enemy from both front and rear; once the two columns united, they would first secure Mozi Gorge, sever Jinchuan's line of reinforcement, and then advance on the Jidi official fortress; a third would strike from the Gaoshi and Jiaju heights of Bawang in separate detachments against Samaduo Jianzang Bujue and seize Jidi; a fourth would advance from Maoniu against Shachong; and a fifth would move from Kaleta'er against Dangli before converging to capture Dandong. The plan was adopted. Yuanjun and Acting Colonel Wu Jinjiang crossed from Zhanggu and seized Gezang Bridge, while Haqing'a and Li Tianyou advanced from Guozong Jiyezong. The converging columns routed the stunned rebels and took Mubalabozu, Samaduo Jianzang Bujue, and neighboring strongpoints. The army then pressed forward and captured the Jidi official fortress together with Mozi Gorge. Battalion Major Chang Tai took Dangli, Brigade Commander Li Tiangui seized Shachong, and Yuanjun recovered Dandong as well. More than three hundred li of Geshizhai territory was restored, along with more than two thousand subject households.
7
桂林遣陳定國調綽斯甲布兵駐軍界上，備調遣。 上責桂林不令元俊乘勝取金川。 元俊旋與散秩大臣阿爾泰劾桂林欺誑及諸罪狀，上為奪桂林職，令阿爾泰署四川總督，命額駙、尚書、公福隆安按治。 未至，詔元俊督兵赴綽斯甲布率土兵進攻金川。 元俊奏：「自戰失利，士氣消沮，現在兵力不足並按兩金川。 請敕調湖南、湖北、山西、甘肅兵二萬，分三道進軍，計兩月可竟事。 」上以元俊請益師，未免張皇，令福隆安會阿爾泰、阿桂與元俊詳悉覈計。 上諭軍機大臣，謂：「元俊能治事，熟番情； 但其人似狡猾好事，當留意駕馭。 」
Guilin ordered Chen Dingguo to muster Chosichab troops and hold them along the frontier for possible deployment. The emperor rebuked Guilin for failing to let Yuanjun exploit the victory and press on into Jinchuan. Yuanjun soon joined Minister without Portfolio Altai in impeaching Guilin for deception and related offenses. The emperor removed Guilin from office, appointed Altai acting Governor of Sichuan, and dispatched Imperial Son-in-law, Minister, and Duke Fu Long'an to conduct a formal investigation. Before Fu Long'an arrived, an edict directed Yuanjun to take command at Chosichab and lead native levies in an assault on Jinchuan. Yuanjun memorialized: "Since our recent defeats morale has collapsed, and the forces now on hand are not enough to tackle both Jinchuans simultaneously. I beg that Your Majesty order twenty thousand reinforcements from Hunan, Hubei, Shanxi, and Gansu to advance in three columns; the campaign could then be finished within two months." The emperor regarded Yuanjun's call for reinforcements as unduly alarmist and ordered Fu Long'an to confer with Altai, Agui, and Yuanjun for a detailed reassessment of the plan. The emperor instructed the Grand Council: "Yuanjun is capable and thoroughly understands frontier tribal affairs; but he also seems crafty and prone to stirring trouble, so he must be kept firmly in hand." "
8
尋，福隆安疏陳所劾桂林狀不實，上以方進兵，元俊熟番情，諸事不必窮究； 惟言：「桂林以白金畀金川贖被掠官兵罪最重，今汪承霈自承出其意。 承霈以曹司從軍，不當與其事。 當詰汪騰龍，成信讞。 」福隆安复疏言：「騰龍以金囑王萬邦待巴旺、布拉底克歸迷道官兵予金為賞，元俊誘萬邦令具札言桂林使贖被掠官兵。 事為元俊陷。 」上乃怒，責元俊奸狡負恩，命奪職逮問，籍其家。 參贊阿桂疏言：「元俊在川日久，熟番情，為近邊土司所信服。 諸將能馭番無出其右。 臣遇事多與詢商，冀收指臂之效。 乞恩仍留軍中，倘奮勉出力，使詐使貪，原所不廢； 如剛愎逞私，即據實嚴劾。 」上命留總兵，還所籍財產。 元俊同副都統永平、博靈阿等潛赴墨壟溝，進至郡崢。 乘月督軍登山薄賊卡，正大霧，我師騰躍入卡，克山樑三道、碉卡二十有四，進克格魯克石。 金川酋圖佔丹壩官寨，綽斯甲布土司發兵往助，阿桂奏令元俊增兵往剿，未行，卒於軍。
Before long Fu Long'an reported that the charges against Guilin could not be substantiated. With the campaign still under way and Yuanjun indispensable for tribal affairs, the emperor declared that the matter need not be pursued further; except to note: "Guilin's gravest offense was paying Jinchuan silver to ransom captured officers and men; Wang Chengpei has now confessed that the scheme originated with him. As a clerical officer attached to the campaign, Chengpei should never have been involved in such dealings. Wang Tenglong must be interrogated until the truth is established." " Fu Long'an submitted a further memorial: "Tenglong had entrusted gold to Wang Wanbang to reward officers and men from Bawang and Buladike who strayed from the route and returned safely. Yuanjun induced Wanbang to draft a document claiming that Guilin had paid ransom for captured troops. The whole affair was a trap laid by Yuanjun." " The emperor flew into a rage, denounced Yuanjun as treacherous and ungrateful, stripped him of rank, ordered his arrest, and confiscated his estate. Campaign Counselor Agui memorialized: "Yuanjun has served in Sichuan for many years, knows frontier tribal affairs intimately, and enjoys the confidence of neighboring chieftains. Among all the commanders, none handles the tribes better than he does. I consult him on nearly every matter in the hope of making our efforts work as one coordinated force. I beg Your Majesty to let him remain with the army. If he throws himself into the fight, his cunning and appetite for gain may yet be turned to good use; but if he grows obstinate and pursues private ends, I shall impeach him without mercy." " The emperor allowed him to retain his rank as brigade general and ordered his confiscated property restored. Yuanjun, Vice Commander-in-Chief Yongping, Boling'a, and others made a stealthy advance to Molong Gorge and pushed on to Junzheng. Under cover of moonlight he led his men up the slopes to assault the rebel passes. A thick fog had settled in, and the Qing troops vaulted into the passes, seizing three ridges and twenty-four tower-forts before advancing to take Geluke Stone. When the Jinchuan chief tried to seize the Dandan official fortress, the Chosichab chieftain sent reinforcements. Agui recommended that Yuanjun lead additional troops against them, but before he could march he died in camp.
9
元俊在邊久，善馭諸土司。 往時賚諸土司繒帛輒窳敝，元俊必以善者，諸土司皆喜。 元俊出行邊，諸土司率妻子出謁，畀以茶、菸、簪珥，視若家人。 稍不循法度，即訶譴，皆悚息聽命。 打箭爐徼外夾壩出沒，元俊至，無敢犯行李者。 諸番小有動靜，爭來告，以故元俊諸所措置皆中窾要。 其得罪，上亦知其枉。 既卒，其子猶戍邊。 四十一年，金川平。 元俊部將張芝元請於阿桂，謂元俊有功無罪，徒以忤專閫被羅織，語甚切。 阿桂為疏請，赦其子還。
Yuanjun had served on the frontier for many years and excelled at managing the native chieftains. Imperial gifts of silk to the chieftains had often been shoddy, but Yuanjun always presented fine cloth, to their great satisfaction. Whenever Yuanjun toured the frontier, chieftains brought out their wives and children to greet him. He gave them tea, tobacco, pins, and earrings and treated them like members of his own household. Yet if they stepped even slightly outside the rules, he scolded them sharply, and they trembled before obeying his orders. Bandits known as jiaba prowled beyond the Dajianlu frontier, but once Yuanjun appeared none dared touch his baggage. The tribes rushed to report the slightest stir, which is why Yuanjun's every measure struck at the heart of the matter. Even when he was punished, the emperor knew the charge was unjust. After his death his son remained on frontier garrison duty. In Qianlong 41 (1776), Jinchuan was fully pacified. Yuanjun's subordinate Zhang Zhiyuan appealed to Agui, arguing that Yuanjun was guilty of nothing and had been framed solely for defying the supreme commander; his plea was passionate and pointed. Agui submitted a memorial on his behalf, and the emperor pardoned Yuanjun's son and allowed him to return home.
10
薛琮，陝西咸寧人。 父翼鳳，河南南陽鎮總兵。 琮以廕生入巡捕營。 累遷四川漳臘營參將。 阿爾泰討金川，以琮從。 克納頂、邊谷諸碉寨。 溫福代阿爾泰視師，攻巴朗拉，琮戰最力。 又克卡丫，取通甲木。 攻阿仰東山，總督桂林與都統鐵保、提督汪騰龍將兵取墨壟溝，令琮將三千人自甲木、噶爾金後繞山道應大軍夾擊。 桂林中道引還卡丫，又檄鐵保、騰龍令退。 琮深入，糧盡，待桂林不至。 桂林令都司廣著赴援。 賊據高峰曰博六古通，險阻，廣著師不得度。 琮督兵直進，毀柵十餘，奪碉七十餘。 賊力拒，琮督兵仰攻，中槍，沒於陣，軍盡覆，同死者都司張清士、陳定國等二十五人。 阿桂破翁古爾壟，立祠戰地祀琮等。
Xue Cong came from Xianning in Shaanxi. His father Yifeng served as brigade general of the Nanyang garrison in Henan. Cong entered service in the Patrol Battalion through hereditary privilege. He rose through successive promotions to battalion major of the Zhangla Camp in Sichuan. When Altai campaigned against Jinchuan, Cong served under his command. He helped capture Nadai, Biangu, and other tower-forts. When Wenfu replaced Altai as commander and assaulted Balangla, Cong fought with exceptional vigor. He also captured Kaya and secured the route to Jiamu. During the assault on Ayang Eastern Mountain, Governor Guilin, Commander-in-Chief Tiebao, and Provincial Military Commissioner Wang Tenglong advanced on Molong Gorge while ordering Cong to lead three thousand men around the heights behind Jiamu and Ga'erjin to strike the enemy in concert with the main force. Guilin turned back halfway to Kaya and ordered Tiebao and Tenglong to withdraw as well. Cong had pushed deep into enemy territory, exhausted his supplies, and waited in vain for Guilin. Guilin finally ordered Brigade Commander Guangzhu to march to his relief. The rebels held the formidable height called Bolugutong, and Guangzhu's column could not get through. Cong pressed straight ahead, destroying more than ten palisades and capturing more than seventy towers. The rebels resisted fiercely. Cong led an uphill assault, was struck by musket fire, and fell in battle. His entire force was wiped out, and twenty-five officers died with him, including Brigade Commanders Zhang Qingshi and Chen Dingguo. After Agui captured Wenguerlong, he erected a battlefield shrine to honor Cong and his fallen comrades.
11
琮在諸將中號能戰，元俊與最厚。 嘗與期旦日會師，孰後至當斬。 琮至後二刻，元俊遣騎持刀呼取薛參將頭。 琮望見笑曰：「琮頭當與賊，不與公也！ 」奮前奪數碉反。 元俊猶為琮請罪，以功論贖乃已。 及桂林誤琮戰沒，元俊憤激論劾，卒以是得罪。
Among the commanders Cong was renowned for his fighting prowess, and Yuanjun was especially close to him. They once agreed to rendezvous at dawn the next day on pain of death for whichever arrived late. Cong arrived half an hour late, and Yuanjun sent a mounted messenger with drawn sword demanding the head of Battalion Major Xue. Cong spotted him and laughed: "My head is for the enemy, not for you! " He charged forward, captured several towers, and returned in triumph. Yuanjun still interceded on Cong's behalf, and the affair was dropped only after his battlefield merit offset the offense. When Guilin's blunder cost Cong his life, Yuanjun impeached him in a fury and was himself punished in the end for doing so.
12
張芝元，四川清溪人。 以千總從副將軍明亮徵金川有功，積官至越巂營參將。 金川酋以番僧诇軍事，芝元言於明亮曰：「軍事每為賊知，非去其諜，滅賊無日矣。 」會大風雪，明亮命芝元率數十人偽若以他事出者，宿番僧寺中。 芝元故通番語，與僧飲甚歡，僧醉眠，芝元出寺聚柴焚之，僧皆死。 賊諜斷，因招降其眾。 尋從成都將軍特成額駐兵江卡，捕夾壩，圍本肯賊寨，焚其碉，斃賊甚眾，擢懋功協副將。 台灣林爽文為亂，芝元率屯練降番佐軍。 參贊海蘭察等分攻大埔林、中林、大埔尾三莊，芝元為策應。 賊據小半天山，將軍福康安等自前山進，芝元與領隊大臣普爾普領兵別為一隊，夜半先發，繞大山夾攻賊後。 黎明，諸軍同抵山麓，攀援上，賊力拒，芝元先登，拔其柵，斬獲無算，並堵賊去路。 未幾，爽文就擒。 台灣平，擢建昌鎮總兵，圖形紫光閣，列前二十功臣。 尋調松潘鎮總兵。 廓爾喀掠西藏濟嚨、聶拉木，上命芝元率屯練降番往討之。 芝元至，值大雪，山谷皆滿。 芝元手大刀指揮，士卒皆感激用命，賊敗走。 廓爾喀再叛，芝元偕提督成德督兵攻聶拉木，守拍甲嶺隘口斷賊援，聶拉木遂下； 乘勝攻濟嚨，复克之，賊懼，乞降。 未幾，卒。 五十八年，論平定廓爾喀功，再圖形紫光閣，列後十五功臣。
Zhang Zhiyuan came from Qingxi in Sichuan. Serving as company commander under Deputy General Mingliang in the Jinchuan campaign, he distinguished himself and rose through successive promotions to battalion major of the Yuexi Camp. The Jinchuan chief used Tibetan monks to spy on Qing operations. Zhiyuan told Mingliang: "The enemy always knows our movements. Unless we eliminate their spies, we will never defeat them." " A heavy blizzard followed. Mingliang ordered Zhiyuan to take several dozen men out under the pretense of routine business and lodge overnight in the monks' temple. Zhiyuan spoke the tribal languages fluently and drank freely with the monks until they passed out. He then left the temple, piled firewood against it, and set it ablaze, killing every monk inside. With enemy intelligence broken, he was able to induce many rebels to surrender. He later served under Chengdu General Techeng'e at Jiangka, hunted jiaba bandits, besieged the Benken rebel stockade, burned its towers, and killed large numbers of the enemy. He was promoted to deputy commander of the Maogong Battalion. When Lin Shuangwen rose in rebellion on Taiwan, Zhiyuan led garrison militia and surrendered tribesmen in support of the campaign. Campaign Counselor Hailancha divided his forces to assault the villages of Dapulin, Zhonglin, and Dapuwei while Zhiyuan provided supporting coordination. The rebels held Xiaobantian Mountain while General Fukang'an advanced from the front. Zhiyuan and Leading Minister Pu'erpu led a separate column that set out at midnight, swung around the main peak, and struck the enemy from the rear. At dawn the columns met at the foot of the mountain and scaled the slopes together. The rebels resisted fiercely, but Zhiyuan was first to reach the top, tore down their palisades, killed or captured a great number of the enemy, and sealed off their line of retreat. Before long Lin Shuangwen was captured. After Taiwan was pacified he was promoted to brigade general of Jianchang, had his portrait enshrined in the Hall of Purple Splendor, and was numbered among the first twenty meritorious officials. He was soon transferred to brigade general of the Songpan garrison. When Gurkha forces raided Jilong and Nyalam in Tibet, the emperor ordered Zhiyuan to lead garrison militia and surrendered tribesmen against them. Zhiyuan arrived amid heavy snow that had filled every valley. Wielding a great blade himself, Zhiyuan led from the front so inspiringly that his men fought with desperate loyalty and routed the enemy. When the Gurkhas rebelled again, Zhiyuan joined Provincial Military Commissioner Cheng De in assaulting Nyalam, held the Paijialing pass to sever enemy reinforcements, and Nyalam fell; They pressed the advantage, retook Jilong, and the enemy, terrified, sued for peace. He died not long afterward. In Qianlong 58, when honors for pacifying the Gurkhas were awarded, his portrait was again placed in the Hall of Purple Splendor and he was numbered among the later fifteen meritorious officials.

Dong Tianbi, courtesy name Lincang, came from Daxing in the Shuntian metropolitan district. Having passed the military examinations, he was appointed garrison commandant of the Front Camp under the Sichuan provincial military command. In the early Qianlong years, during the Jinchuan campaigns, Tianbi distinguished himself in the field. He rose through successive promotions to deputy commander of the Weizhou Battalion. When the Jinchuan chieftain Langka attacked the Dandan chieftain, Tianbi and Acting Colonel Song Yuanjun compelled him to return his plunder and dismantle his fortifications. After the campaign ended Tianbi was transferred to brigade general of Songpan. He was soon promoted to Provincial Military Commissioner of Sichuan. When Golok tribesmen robbed Tibetan tribute lamas, the emperor ordered Tianbi to punish them, but he failed to capture the ringleader and was rebuked by edict for half-hearted effort. In Qianlong 35, Senggesang, son of the Little Jinchuan chieftain Zewang, rose in revolt, attacked Ekezhi Chieftain Sedakela, and besieged his fortress. Tianbi stationed troops at Damubazong and ordered Senggesang to withdraw from Sedakela's fortress. When Sedakela's supplies ran out, he begged to relocate to Damubazong. Tianbi and Governor Altai agreed to leave a garrison there.

In Qianlong 36, Senggesang again besieged Damubazong and overran Mu'erzong, Balangla, and neighboring districts. Tianbi marched from Dajianlu, mustered provincial troops and garrison forces from Songpan and Weizhou, and reached Mianlonggang only to find the rebels already holding Balangla, fortifying tower-pass strongpoints for a prolonged defense, and severing his line of advance. Tianbi planned a strike through Shanshen Gorge to relieve Damubazong. He led four hundred men along that route to De'ermi, captured seven towers, and drove the rebels off; then advanced on Biwangla. The rebels attacked through the fog, the native auxiliaries panicked and fled, and both De'ermi and Biwangla were lost. Tianbi submitted a confession. The emperor noted that Tianbi had commanded too few men and that Governor Altai had failed to plan reinforcements, pardoned him, and admonished: "You must fight all the harder. If you fail again, your punishment will be far worse." Tianbi led five hundred men from Muping up the Yaoqi slopes and along the ridge opposite Jiajinda, capturing two towers. Seeing that the Ekezhi cattle pasture lay on a vital route, Tianbi detached troops to destroy the rebels holding it and garrisoned the position; He followed up with converging strikes from above and below until, with the interlocking domains of the Muping, Ekezhi, and neighboring chieftains, every strategic pass fell under Qing control. Before long the rebels retook the cattle pasture. The emperor stripped Altai of rank because his army had achieved nothing after months of campaigning. The emperor rebuked him: "Tianbi has bungled from start to finish and shares Altai's guilt. Strip his rank and keep him in the ranks as a common soldier. If he retreats again, execute him under military law." " He was soon ordered imprisoned in Chengdu. Before the edict arrived, Tianbi found Jiajinda Mountain too steep to assault directly, searched for a detour, and discovered a gorge between two cliffs. A blizzard struck. Tianbi led his men in a stealthy crossing through the gorge, reached Damubazong, and struck Senggesang and Sedakela; broke the encirclement, also retook Mu'erzong, and linked up with Wenfu's main force. When word reached the throne, the emperor commuted the death sentence and kept him with the army. Agui put Tianbi in charge of gunpowder and ordnance. In Qianlong 37, after Zili was captured, Agui assigned Tianbi five hundred men to garrison the post. He soon received brevet rank as deputy general and was appointed brigade general of Chongqing. He was ordered to lead troops to Zengtou Gorge and on to Suomo, where the Suomo chieftain's wife offered a thousand warriors to join him. When this was reported to the throne, he was awarded the peacock feather insignia. Tianbi assaulted Kanzhuo Gorge, took a detour to Nayunda, and pushed more than fifty li into enemy territory, seizing three ridges and destroying more than thirty tower-forts and three wooden strongholds. He rendezvoused with Wenfu at Bulangguozong, captured Dabanzhao and Muyazhai, and took thirty-six towers and sixteen passes. The emperor noted that Wenfu had already taken Bulangguozong and advanced on Dimuda while Tianbi's gains were mostly empty stockades, found his memorial exaggerated, and sent a personal edict of warning. He was soon appointed Leading Minister.

In Qianlong 38 he was restored as Provincial Military Commissioner of Sichuan. Little Jinchuan had been pacified, and Wenfu led the advance against Great Jinchuan with Tianbi assigned five hundred men to hold Dimuda. Wenfu advanced to Muguo Wood and established the main camp there; Dimuda stood on the enemy's line of advance and was a critical pass. Wenfu requisitioned three hundred of his men for the main camp, stripping away his rear support. Wenfu, emboldened by repeated victories, took the enemy lightly. Jinchuan headmen including Qitugela'er Sijabu feigned surrender with more than a thousand followers. Wenfu quartered them among camp servants, then incited the supposed defectors to revolt, calculating that Dimuda was weakly held and unsupported. On the first day of the sixth month they struck from the rear with a mass assault. Tianbi led his remaining two hundred men in a desperate sword fight, but by midnight hundreds of enemy muskets had ringed his position and cut him down. Nine days later they overran the main camp, and Wenfu was killed as well. The emperor had first ordered Tianbi to hold Dandan, then to move to Bulangguozong, while camp rumor spread that the enemy was about to strike. Tianbi was then encamped at Meinuo, and the emperor ordered him stripped of rank and arrested. Governor Liu Bingtian reported: "Tianbi had galloped from Meinuo toward Dimuda, encountered rebels on the road, was shot in the right side, and died. " Nevertheless, for dereliction in the campaign his estate was confiscated and his son Lian, a provincial graduate, was banished to garrison duty in Yili.

Chai Daji came from Jiangshan in Zhejiang. Having passed the military examinations, he was appointed garrison commandant in Fujian. He rose through successive promotions to brigade general of Haitan and was then transferred to command the Taiwan garrison. In the eleventh month of Qianlong 51, Lin Shuangwen's rebellion erupted. Shuangwen was a native of Zhangzhou who had settled in Zhanghua at the village of Dalijiao. Wicked elements had formed the Heaven and Earth Society under the Zhangzhou native Zhuang Yan as leader, and Shuangwen joined them in plotting revolt. Taiwan Prefect Sun Jingsui rushed to Zhanghua and directed District Magistrate Yu Jun, Deputy General Hesheng'e, and Acting Colonel Geng Shiwen to arrest the conspirators, burning several villages to intimidate them. Exploiting popular resentment, Shuangwen rallied his followers in a night attack that killed Hesheng'e and his officers. The next day they stormed Zhanghua, and Jingsui died defending the city. They then overran Zhuluo and Fengshan as well. Daji, then brigade general, held the prefectural capital. When rebels attacked along several routes he took position at Yancheng Bridge, sank dozens of their boats, and killed more than a thousand of the enemy.

In the spring of Qianlong 52, Naval Commissioner Huang Shijian and Army Commissioner Ren Chengen arrived in succession to reinforce the island. Daji marched out, recaptured Zhuluo, and transferred his force there to hold the city. He was soon awarded the peacock feather for defending the prefectural capital. Because Huang Shijian and Ren Chengen had achieved nothing after months in the field, the emperor appointed Governor Changqing as commanding general and sent him to Taiwan to take charge. Shuangwen assaulted Zhuluo ten times between the second and fourth months. Daji led Acting Colonel Yang Qilin, Garrison Commandant Qiu Nengcheng, and others in sorties that killed several thousand rebels. Shuangwen's follower Zhang Shenhui feigned surrender, but Daji saw through the ruse and had him executed. Taiwan's prefectures and districts were walled with woven bamboo that could not withstand assault, yet Daji rallied soldiers and civilians by appeal to loyalty and righteousness and swore to hold the line. The emperor praised Daji's exertions, sent him a purse and milk cakes as gifts, and ordered the ministries to consider his reward. In the sixth month he was made Army Commissioner of Fujian while retaining command of the Taiwan garrison. Yanshui Harbor was Zhuluo's lifeline to the prefectural capital. When rebels attacked it, Daji defended it with all his strength. The emperor pressed Changqing to hurry to the relief while granting Daji the title Valiant Baturu and appointing him campaign counselor. In the eighth month, judging Changqing too old to defeat the rebels, the emperor appointed Fukang'an as commanding general while retaining Daji as counselor; but Changqing ordered Brigade General Wei Dabin to relieve Zhuluo. Rebels blocked every route, and Wei withdrew to Luzicao; then ordered Brigade General Cai Panlong to relieve Zhuluo. Daji fought his way out to meet him and brought him into the city for a joint defense. The emperor promoted Daji to Naval Commissioner and gave Cai Panlong the army commissionership. In the eleventh month Daji was made Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent. Because Zhuluo's people had endured a long siege while holding firm in loyalty, the emperor renamed the county Jiayi, "Upholding Righteousness." As the siege intensified, the emperor secretly told Daji: "You need not die with the city. If matters grow desperate, break out with your troops and seek another opening for advance. " Daji replied in a memorial: "Zhuluo stands at the center of the island. Its walls are built of earth and bamboo, ringed by a deep moat topped with a low parapet and cannon. The defenses are formidable. If we abandon it now, the rebels will grow stronger and we will lose Yanshui Harbor and our supply line as well. More than forty thousand people inside the walls and refugees from surrounding villages have shared provisions and joined the defense until now. I cannot abandon these tens of thousands of lives to rebel butchery! I can only hold on with all my strength until relief arrives." " The emperor wrote back in his own hand: "Your memorial shows a loyal and righteous heart. Reading it brought tears to my eyes! Besieged for so long, Daji has only grown more resolute. He rallies soldiers and civilians, endures hunger, and holds the line with nothing but the welfare of the state and the people in mind. What famous general of antiquity could do more? " He was ennobled as a first-rank Baron of Righteous Courage with perpetual hereditary succession, Zhejiang Governor Langgan was ordered to grant his family ten thousand taels of silver, and Fukang'an was pressed to hurry to the relief.

In the twelfth month Fukang'an arrived and lifted the siege of Jiayi. Daji went out to greet him, confident that his ennoblement and rewards made him the hero of the hour, yet still harried from the siege and unable to observe full military courtesy. Fukang'an took offense and impeached him for deceit, deep Green Standard corruption, and unreliability. The emperor replied: "Daji held Jiayi while the rebels assaulted it by every means. He led soldiers and civilians in a determined defense. I told him that if he could not hold, he might withdraw with his force intact. Daji held firm, fought on with all his strength, and refused to abandon tens of thousands of lives to the rebels. Reading his memorial moved me to tears. Can Fukang'an not share my own view of the matter? Daji had reported that the rebels used siege carts mounted with muskets and cannon. Fukang'an now reports capturing those very carts along with abandoned firearms, proving Daji's earlier account true. Daji also reported that the city had run out of food, with sweet potatoes and peanuts exhausted and even oil-pressing residue eaten for sustenance. Loyal civilians were still contributing provisions then, and conditions may not yet have been quite so desperate. But Daji, desperate for relief, exaggerated by speaking of eating oil residue. The columns of Pujibao and Hengrui were still holding back. If it were reported that grain remained, those two relief forces would move even more slowly. With the city's fate still uncertain, how can one blame Daji for pressing his case so urgently? Daji had been repeatedly honored by the throne, but his manners toward Fukang'an may have been lacking, provoking resentment and a direct airing of his faults. Fukang'an should share my view, overlook faults in favor of merit, and thus practice true loyalty to the state." " Vice Minister Decheng, returning from a mission in Zhejiang at Fukang'an's instigation, denounced Daji. The emperor ordered Fukang'an, Li Shiyao, Xu Siceng, and Langgan to investigate. On the eve of the inquiry Fukang'an wrote the Grand Council: "Daji's troops provoked the people to revolt, and his defense of Jiayi owed everything to loyal civilians. When Daji heard the order he tried to withdraw his troops, but the loyal civilians would not let him leave the city, and the attempt was abandoned." " When this was reported, the emperor said: "I will not treat the defense of Zhuluo as Daji's crime, but his other misconduct and provoking revolt must be investigated. " He ordered Daji stripped of rank and arrested. Fukang'an soon recommended execution on charges of lax discipline, corruption, and dereliction, and sent Daji to the capital. The emperor ordered the Grand Council to review the case. Daji protested his innocence, alleging that Decheng had rigged the proceedings and coerced Jiayi civilians into testifying against him. At the court interrogation Daji still argued vigorously. On the xinsi day of the seventh month of Qianlong 53, he was ordered executed in public as Fukang'an had recommended, and his son was banished to Yili as a bondsman.

論曰：元俊、天弼在邊久，熟情偽，習形勢，諸番仰其威惠。 元俊戹於桂林，激而欲自白，不得直； 微阿桂右之，罪且不測。 天弼又見嫉於溫福，驅至寡之兵以投方張之寇，既死猶尚以為罪。 若大紀有功無罪，為福康安所不容。 高宗手詔，可謂曲折而詳盡矣，乃終不能貸其死。 軍旅之際，捐肝腦，冒鋒刃，求尺寸之效，困於媢嫉，功不成而死於敵，若功成矣，而又死於法。 嗚呼，可哀也已！
Commentary: Yuanjun and Tianbi had served long on the frontier, knew tribal affairs inside out, understood the terrain, and the tribes respected both their authority and their goodwill. Yuanjun clashed with Guilin, was provoked into trying to clear his name, yet could not obtain justice; had Agui not intervened on his behalf, his punishment might have been far worse. Tianbi likewise earned Wenfu's jealousy, was sent with too few men against a rising enemy, and was still treated as guilty even after he died fighting. As for Daji, meritorious and innocent though he was, Fukang'an would not tolerate him. Emperor Gaozong's own edict in Daji's defense was subtle and thorough, yet even that could not save him from execution. On campaign men give their lives and face the blade for the smallest gain. Jealousy thwarts them: some die by the enemy without winning glory; others win glory only to die by the law. Alas, how lamentable it is!
