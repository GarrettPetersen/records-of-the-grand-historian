-{于}-敏中和珅弟和琳蘇凌阿
Yu Minzhong; He Shen; his younger brother He Lin; and Su Linga
2
和珅，字致齋，鈕祜祿氏，滿洲正紅旗人。 少貧無藉，為文生員。 乾隆三十四年，承襲三等輕車都尉。 尋授三等侍衛，挑補黏桿處。 四十年，直乾清門，擢御前侍衛，兼副都統。 次年，遂授戶部侍郎，命為軍機大臣，兼內務府大臣，駸駸鄉用。 又兼步軍統領，充崇文門稅務監督，總理行營事務。 四十五年，命偕侍郎喀凝阿往雲南按總督李侍堯貪私事。 侍堯號才臣，帝所倚任。 和珅至，鞫其僕，得侍堯婪索狀，論重闢，奏雲南吏治廢弛，府州縣多虧帑，亟宜清釐。 上欲用和珅為總督，嫌於事出所按劾，乃以福康安代之。 命回京，未至，擢戶部尚書、議政大臣。 及復命，面陳雲南鹽務、錢法、邊事，多稱上意，並允行。 授御前大臣兼都統。 賜婚其子丰紳殷德為和孝公主額駙，待年行婚禮。 又授領侍衛內大臣，充四庫全書館正總裁，兼理籓院尚書事，寵任冠朝列矣。
He Shen, whose style was Zhizhai, belonged to the Niuhuru clan and came from the Manchu Plain Red Banner. In his youth he was destitute and without patrons, and he qualified as a licentiate in the civil examinations. In Qianlong 34 (1769), he inherited the rank of third-class Commandant of Light Chariots. He was soon made a third-class bodyguard and posted to the Nian'gan Office of imperial attendants. In Qianlong 40 (1775) he was stationed at the Gate of Heavenly Purity, promoted to Imperial Bodyguard, and also appointed Vice Commander-in-Chief. The following year he was appointed Vice Minister of Revenue and named a Grand Councilor, while also serving as Minister of the Imperial Household Department; his rise toward the highest offices was swift. He also took charge of the Metropolitan Gendarmerie, served as supervisor of Chongwen Gate customs, and directed affairs of the imperial traveling camps. In Qianlong 45 (1780) he was sent with Vice Minister Ka Ning'a to Yunnan to investigate corruption charges against Governor-General Li Shiyao. Li Shiyao was known as a gifted minister and enjoyed the emperor's deep trust. On arrival He Shen examined Shiyao's servants and uncovered proof of extortion; he urged the death penalty and reported that Yunnan's government had grown slack, that many prefectures and counties showed treasury shortfalls, and that a thorough cleanup was urgently required. The emperor wanted to make He Shen governor-general, yet hesitated because the post would follow an investigation he himself had conducted, and so Fuk'anggan was appointed instead. Ordered back to Beijing, he was promoted to Minister of Revenue and Deliberative Minister before he even reached the capital. On reporting back he personally outlined proposals on Yunnan's salt trade, currency, and frontier affairs; most met with the emperor's approval and were ordered carried out. He was made a Grand Minister in Attendance and also Commander-in-Chief. His son Fengshenyinde was betrothed as consort to Princess Hexiao, with the marriage ceremony deferred until she reached the proper age. He was also named Chief Guard of the Inner Palace, appointed chief director of the Complete Library of the Four Treasuries, and put in charge of the Court of Colonial Affairs; no one at court enjoyed greater favor.
3
四十六年，甘肅撒拉爾番回蘇四十三等叛，逼蘭州，額駙拉旺多爾濟、領侍衛內大臣海蘭察、護軍額森特等率兵討之。 命和珅為欽差大臣，偕大學士阿桂往督師。 阿桂有疾，促和珅兼程先進。 至則海蘭察等已擊賊勝之，即督諸將分四路進兵，海蘭察逼賊山梁，殲其伏。 賊掘溝坎深數丈，並斷小道，不能度。 總兵圖欽保陣亡。 後數日，阿桂至，和珅委過諸將不聽調遣。 阿桂曰：「是宜誅！ 」明日，同部署戰事，阿桂所指揮，輒應如響。 乃曰：「諸將殊不見其慢，當誰誅？ 」和珅恚甚。 上微察之，詔斥和珅匿圖欽保死事不上聞，赴師遲延，而劾海蘭察、額森特先戰顛倒是非； 又謂自阿桂至軍，措置始有條理，一人足辦賊，和珅在軍事不歸一，海蘭察等久隨阿桂，易節制，命和珅速回京。 和珅用是銜阿桂，終身與之齟《齒吾》。 尋兼署兵部尚書，管理戶部三庫。
In Qianlong 46 (1781) the Salar Muslim rebels led by Su Forty-three rose in Gansu and threatened Lanzhou; Imperial Son-in-Law Lawangduo'erji, Chief Guard Hailancha, Guardsman Esente, and others marched against them. He Shen was named Imperial Commissioner and sent with Grand Secretary Agui to direct the campaign. Agui fell ill and pressed He Shen to hurry forward by forced marches. When he arrived Hailancha and the others had already beaten the rebels; he at once ordered the generals to advance in four columns, and Hailancha drove the enemy onto a mountain ridge and wiped out their ambush. The rebels had dug trenches several zhang deep and severed the narrow paths, making them impassable. Brigade-General Tu Qinbao was killed in action. A few days later Agui arrived, and He Shen blamed the generals for refusing to follow his orders. Agui said, "They deserve to be put to death! " The next day, as they planned the battle together, every order Agui gave was obeyed instantly. He then said, "The generals show no disobedience whatsoever—who, then, deserves execution? " He Shen was furious. The emperor had taken note and issued an edict condemning He Shen for concealing Tu Qinbao's death, for his tardy arrival at the front, and for accusing Hailancha and Esente of having fought prematurely and distorted the facts; it added that only after Agui reached the army had operations become orderly, that Agui alone could finish off the rebels, that He Shen's presence left command divided, and that Hailancha and the others, long accustomed to Agui, would obey him readily; He Shen was ordered back to Beijing at once. From this He Shen nursed a lifelong grudge against Agui and remained at odds with him to the end. He was soon made acting Minister of War as well and placed in charge of the Ministry of Revenue's three treasuries.
4
四十七年，御史錢灃劾山東巡撫國泰、布政使於易簡貪縱營私，命和珅偕都御史劉墉按鞫，灃從往。 和珅陰袒國泰，即至，盤庫，令抽視銀數十封無缺，即起還行館。 灃請封庫，明日盡發視庫銀，得借市銀充抵狀，國泰等罪皆鞫實。 會加恩中外大臣，加太子太保，充經筵講官。 四十八年，賜雙眼花翎，充國史館正總裁、文淵閣提舉閣事、清字經館總裁。 甘肅石峰堡回匪平，以承旨論功，再予輕車都尉世職，並前職授一等男爵。 調吏部尚書、協辦大學士，管理戶部如故。
In Qianlong 47 (1782) Censor Qian Feng accused Shandong Governor Guotai and Administration Commissioner Yu Yijian of corruption; He Shen was sent with Censor-in-Chief Liu Yong to investigate, with Feng accompanying the mission. He Shen secretly shielded Guotai; on arrival he inspected the treasury, had a few dozen silver packets checked and declared sound, then went straight back to his quarters. Feng demanded that the treasury be sealed; the next day every packet was opened and they found borrowed silver from the market used to cover shortfalls; Guotai and his associates were fully convicted. During a general bestowal of favors on high ministers he was made Junior Tutor to the Heir Apparent and named a lecturer at the imperial Classics Colloquium. In Qianlong 48 (1783) he received double-eyed peacock feathers and was appointed chief director of the State Historiography Office, superintendent of Wenyuan Pavilion, and director of the Manchu Classics Institute. After the Muslim rebels at Shifeng Fort in Gansu were suppressed, the emperor ordered a merit review; He Shen received another hereditary Commandant of Light Chariots and, combined with his existing honors, was raised to first-class baron. He was moved to Minister of Personnel and Associate Grand Secretary while retaining control of the Ministry of Revenue.
5
五十一年，御史曹錫寶劾和珅家奴劉全奢僭，造屋踰制，帝察其欲劾和珅，不敢明言，故以家人為由。 命王大臣會同都察院傳問錫寶，使直陳和珅私弊，卒不能指實。 和珅亦預使劉全毀屋更造，察勘不得直，錫寶因獲譴。 踰月，授和珅文華殿大學士。 詔以其管崇文門監督已閱八年，大學士不宜兼榷務，且錫寶劾其家人，未必不因此，遂罷其監督。 部員湛露擢廣信知府，上見其年幼，不勝方面，斥和珅濫保。 又兩廣總督富勒渾縱容家人婪索，和珅請調回富勒渾，不興大獄。 京師米貴，和珅請禁囤積，逾五十石者交廠減糶，商民以為不便。 廷臣遷就原議，上並切責之。 五十三年，以台灣逆匪林爽文平，晉封三等忠襄伯，賜紫韁。 五十五年，賜黃帶、四開褉袍。 上八旬萬壽，命和珅偕尚書金簡專司慶典事。 內閣學士尹壯圖疏論各省庫藏空虛，上為動色，和珅請即命壯圖往勘各省庫，以侍郎慶成監之。 慶成每至一省輒掣肘，待挪移既足，然後啟榷，迄無虧絀，壯圖以妄言坐黜。
In Qianlong 51 (1786) Censor Cao Xibao accused He Shen's servant Liu Quan of living in unlawful luxury and erecting an overlarge residence; the emperor saw that Cao meant to attack He Shen himself but dared not say so openly, and so Cao framed the charge around a household slave. Princes and ministers were ordered, together with the Censorate, to summon Cao Xibao and urge him to state He Shen's abuses directly; he still could not prove a single charge. He Shen had already had Liu Quan tear down the house and rebuild it; the inspectors found nothing amiss, and Cao Xibao was punished instead. A month later He Shen was made Grand Secretary of the Wenhua Hall. An edict noted that he had held the Chongwen Gate supervisorship for eight years, that a Grand Secretary should not also run a tax office, and that Cao Xibao's attack on his servant may have played a part; his supervisorship was removed. When the ministry clerk Zhan Lu was promoted to prefect of Guangxin, the emperor found him too young for a regional post and rebuked He Shen for reckless patronage. When Governor-General Fulehun of the Two Guangs allowed his household to extort bribes, He Shen asked that Fulehun be recalled rather than face a major prosecution. As grain prices rose in Beijing, He Shen proposed banning hoarding and requiring anyone holding more than fifty shi to turn the surplus over to government granaries for sale at reduced prices; merchants and townspeople found the policy burdensome. Court ministers reverted to the earlier plan, and the emperor rebuked both sides sharply. In Qianlong 53 (1788), after the Taiwan rebel Lin Shuangwen was suppressed, he was raised to third-class Loyal and Assisting Earl and given the purple reins. In Qianlong 55 (1790) he received the yellow belt and a four-panel ceremonial robe. For the emperor's eightieth-birthday celebrations, He Shen and Minister Jin Jian were put solely in charge of the festivities. Cabinet Academician Yin Zhuangtu memorialized that provincial treasuries were empty; the emperor was visibly shaken; He Shen proposed sending Yin at once to inspect every provincial treasury under the supervision of Vice Minister Qing Cheng. At each province Qing Cheng hamstrung the inspection, waited until funds had been shuffled to cover gaps, and only then opened the treasuries; no shortfalls ever appeared, and Yin Zhuangtu was dismissed for spreading falsehoods.
6
五十六年，刻石經於辟雍，命為正總裁。 時總裁八人，尚書彭元瑞獨任校勘，敕編石經考文提要，事竣，元瑞被優賚。 和珅嫉之，毀元瑞所編不善，且言非天子不考文。 上曰：「書為御定，何得目為私書耶？ 」和珅乃使人撰考文提要舉正以攻之，冒為己作進上，訾提要不便士子，請銷毀，上不許。 館臣疏請頒行，為和珅所阻，中止，复私使人磨碑字，凡從古者盡改之。
In Qianlong 56 (1791) the stone classics were carved at the Imperial Academy, and he was named chief director. Eight directors served on the project, but Minister Peng Yuanrui alone handled collation; the emperor ordered him to compile the Stone Classics Textual Summary, and when the work was done Peng received lavish rewards. He Shen envied him, attacked Peng's edition as inferior, and claimed that textual collation was the emperor's prerogative alone. The emperor said, "The text was fixed by imperial authority—how can you call it a private work? " He Shen then had aides draft corrections to the Textual Summary to attack Peng's work, passed them off as his own memorial, complained that the Summary was unfair to scholars, and asked that it be destroyed; the emperor refused. Institute officials petitioned for publication, but He Shen blocked it; he also secretly had workers grind down the carved characters and alter every reading that followed ancient usage.
7
五十七年，廓爾喀平，予議敘，兼翰林院掌院學士。 六十年，充殿試讀卷官，教習庶吉士。 時朝審停勾，情重者請旨裁定。 和珅管理籓院，於蒙古重獄置未奏，鐫級留任。 又廷試武舉發策，上命檢實錄。 故事，實錄不載武試策問，和珅率對不以實，詔斥護過飾非，革職留任。 先是京察屢邀議敘，是年特停罷之。 嘉慶二年，調管刑部。 尋以軍需報銷，仍兼管戶部。 三年，教匪王三槐就擒，以襄贊功晉公爵。
In Qianlong 57 (1792), after the Gurkha campaign ended, he received a merit review and was also appointed Chancellor of the Hanlin Academy. In Qianlong 60 (1795) he served as reader of palace examination papers and tutor to Hanlin bachelors. At that time the annual court review suspended executions; only the gravest cases were referred to the throne for decision. While managing the Court of Colonial Affairs he failed to report a serious Mongol case and was demoted one rank, though kept in post. When he set the policy questions for the military graduates' palace examination, the emperor ordered the Veritable Records consulted. By precedent the Veritable Records do not record military examination questions; He Shen's answers were evasive; an edict condemned him for covering his mistakes, and he was stripped of rank but kept at work. He had repeatedly been slated for favorable review at the capital inspection, but that year the process was halted specifically for him. In Jiaqing 2 (1797) he was put in charge of the Ministry of Justice. Soon afterward, with military supply accounts pending, he resumed control of the Ministry of Revenue as well. In Jiaqing 3 (1798), after the rebel Wang Sanhuai was captured, he was promoted to duke for his supporting role in the campaign.
8
和珅柄政久，善伺高宗意，因以弄竊作威福，不附己者，伺隙激上怒陷之； 納賄者則為周旋，或故緩其事，以俟上怒之霽。 大僚恃為奧援，剝削其下以供所欲。 鹽政、河工素利藪，以徵求無厭日益敝。 川、楚匪亂，因激變而起，將帥多倚和珅，糜餉奢侈，久無功。 阿桂以勳臣為首輔，素不相能，被其梗軋。 入直治事，不與同止直廬。 阿桂卒，益無顧忌，於軍機寄諭獨署己銜。 同列嵇璜年老，以讒數被斥責。 王杰持正，恆與忤，亦不能制。 硃珪舊為仁宗傅，在兩廣總督任，高宗欲召為大學士，和珅忌其進用，密取仁宗賀詩白高宗，指為市恩。 高宗大怒，賴董誥諫免； 尋以他事降珪安徽巡撫，屏不得內召。 言官惟錢灃劾其黨國泰得直，後論和珅與阿桂入直不同止直廬，奉命監察，以勞瘁死。 曹錫寶、尹壯圖皆獲譴，無敢昌言其罪者。 高宗雖遇事裁抑，和珅巧彌縫，不悛益恣。 仁宗自在潛邸知其奸，及即位，以高宗春秋高，不欲遽發，仍優容之。
He Shen had held power for years, reading the Qianlong Emperor's moods with uncanny skill and turning that insight into personal authority; anyone who would not align with him he baited into provoking the emperor's wrath and ruin; those who paid bribes he interceded for, sometimes deliberately slowing a case until the emperor's anger had cooled. Senior officials treated him as their patron and squeezed subordinates to feed his demands. Salt administration and river conservancy had always been lucrative posts; under his insatiable levies both grew steadily more corrupt and depleted. The Sichuan-Hubei rebellions erupted from popular outrage; many generals depended on He Shen, squandered funds in luxury, and for years achieved nothing. Agui, the leading meritorious minister at the head of government, had never gotten along with him and was constantly blocked. When on duty in the Grand Council he would not share the same lodging with Agui. After Agui's death he grew still bolder and began signing Grand Council edicts with his name alone. His colleague Ji Huang, though elderly, was repeatedly censured on He Shen's slanders. Wang Jie stood on principle and clashed with him constantly, yet could not check him. Zhu Gui had once been tutor to the future Jiaqing Emperor; while governor-general of the Two Guangs the Qianlong Emperor meant to summon him as Grand Secretary; He Shen, fearing his rise, secretly showed Qianlong a congratulatory poem from the heir apparent and accused Zhu of courting favor. Qianlong flew into a rage; only Dong Gao's remonstrance saved Zhu; soon afterward Zhu was demoted on another pretext to governor of Anhui and kept from returning to the capital. Of the censors only Qian Feng's attack on He Shen's ally Guotai succeeded; later Feng criticized He Shen for refusing to share quarters with Agui on Grand Council duty, was ordered to investigate, and died from exhaustion. Cao Xibao and Yin Zhuangtu were both punished, and no one dared speak openly of He Shen's crimes. Though Qianlong sometimes reined him in, He Shen patched over every scrape and, unrepentant, grew still bolder. The Jiaqing Emperor had known his corruption since his years as heir apparent; on accession, with Qianlong in advanced age, he did not wish to move too quickly and continued to indulge him.
9
四年正月，高宗崩，給事中王念孫首劾其不法狀，仁宗即以宣遺詔日傳旨逮治，命王大臣會鞫，俱得實。 詔宣布和珅罪狀，略曰：「朕於乾隆六十年九月初三日，蒙皇考冊封皇太子，尚未宣布，和珅於初二日在朕前先遞如意，以擁戴自居，大罪一。 騎馬直進圓明園左門，過正大光明殿，至壽山口，大罪二。 乘椅橋入大內，肩輿直入神武門，大罪三。 取出宮女子為次妻，大罪四。 於各路軍報任意壓擱，有心欺蔽，大罪五。 皇考聖躬不豫，和珅毫無憂慼，談笑如常，大罪六。 皇考力疾批答章奏，字跡間有未真，和珅輒謂不如撕去另擬，大罪七。 兼管戶部報銷，竟將戶部事務一人把持，變更成例，不許部臣參議，大罪八。 上年奎舒奏循化、貴德二廳賊番肆劫青海，和珅駁回原摺，隱匿不辦，大罪九。 皇考升遐後，朕諭蒙古王公未出痘者不必來京，和珅擅令已、未出痘者俱不必來，大罪十。 大學士蘇凌阿重聽衰邁，因與其弟和琳姻親，隱匿不奏； 侍郎吳省蘭、李潢，太僕寺卿李光雲在其家教讀，保列卿階，兼任學政，大罪十一。 軍機處記名人員任意撤去，大罪十二。 所鈔家產，楠木房屋僭侈踰制，仿照寧壽宮制度，園寓點綴與圓明園蓬島、瑤台無異，大罪十三。 薊州墳塋設享殿，置隧道，居民稱和陵，大罪十四。 所藏珍珠手串二百馀，多於大內數倍，大珠大於御用冠頂，大罪十五。 寶石頂非所應用，乃有數十，整塊大寶石不計其數，勝於大內，大罪十六。 藏銀、衣服數逾千萬，大罪十七。 夾牆藏金二萬六千馀兩，私庫藏金六千馀兩，地窖埋銀三百馀萬兩，大罪十八。 通州、薊州當舖、錢店貲本十馀萬，與民爭利，大罪十九。 家奴劉全家產至二十馀萬，並有大珍珠手串，大罪二十。 」內外諸臣疏言和珅罪當以大逆論，上猶以和珅嘗任首輔，不忍令肆市，賜自盡。
In the first month of Jiaqing 4 (1799) Qianlong died; Supervising Secretary Wang Niansun was the first to impeach He Shen's crimes; on the day the succession edict was proclaimed Jiaqing ordered his arrest; princes and ministers jointly tried him, and every charge was proved. An edict listed He Shen's crimes in summary: "On the third day of the ninth month of Qianlong 60 my father invested me as Crown Prince, though the news was not yet public; on the second day He Shen presented the ruyi scepter to me ahead of others, posing as my patron in the succession—first great crime. Riding straight through the left gate of the Old Summer Palace, past the Hall of Supreme Harmony in Brightness, and on to Shoushan Kou—second great crime. Being carried in a sedan chair across the bridge into the inner palace and through the Gate of Divine Prowess—third great crime. Taking palace women out to make them his secondary wives—fourth great crime. Arbitrarily suppressing military dispatches from every front with intent to deceive—fifth great crime. When my father's health failed, He Shen showed no grief and jested as usual—sixth great crime. When my father, laboring through illness to annotate memorials, left characters imperfect, He Shen would say they should be torn up and redrafted—seventh great crime. While overseeing Ministry of Revenue accounts he seized the entire ministry for himself, changed established rules, and barred officials from deliberation—eighth great crime. Last year Kui Shu reported Tibetan bandits raiding Qinghai from Xunhua and Guide; He Shen returned the memorial and buried it without action—ninth great crime. After my father's death I ordered that Mongol princes who had not had smallpox need not come to Beijing; He Shen on his own authority extended the exemption to all, whether or not they had had smallpox—tenth great crime. Grand Secretary Su Linga was deaf and senile; because he was related by marriage to He Shen's brother He Lin, He Shen concealed this and did not report it; Vice Ministers Wu Shenglan and Li Huang and Court of Imperial Stud Director Li Guangyun, who had tutored in his household, were promoted to ministerial rank and given provincial education posts—eleventh great crime. Grand Council clerks were dismissed at his whim—twelfth great crime. Among the seized property were camphor-wood mansions built in unlawful luxury on the model of the Palace of Tranquil Longevity, with gardens decorated like the fairy isles of the Old Summer Palace—thirteenth great crime. His tomb at Jizhou had an offering hall and a tunnel; locals called it the He Mausoleum—fourteenth great crime. He hoarded more than two hundred pearl rosaries, several times the palace stores; some pearls exceeded those on the imperial crown—fifteenth great crime. He possessed dozens of gem-studded hat finials he had no right to wear, and countless large uncut gems surpassing the palace collection—sixteenth great crime. His hoarded silver and clothing were worth more than ten million taels—seventeenth great crime. He hid more than twenty-six thousand taels of gold in hollow walls, six thousand in a private vault, and over three million taels of silver in cellars—eighteenth great crime. His pawnshops and money shops at Tongzhou and Jizhou held capital exceeding one hundred thousand taels and competed with common merchants for profit—nineteenth great crime. His servant Liu Quan alone held property worth more than two hundred thousand taels, including large pearl rosaries—twentieth great crime. " Officials at court urged charging him with treason; the emperor, remembering that He Shen had once been chief minister, would not have him dismembered in public and granted him the cord.
10
諸劾和珅者比於操、莽。 直隸布政使吳熊光舊直軍機，上因其入覲，問曰：「人言和珅有異志，有諸？ 」熊光曰：「凡懷不軌者，必收人心，和珅則滿、漢幾無歸附者，即使中懷不軌，誰肯從之？ 」上曰：「然則治之得無太急？ 」熊光曰：「不速治其罪，無識之徒觀望夤緣，別滋事端。 發之速，是義之盡； 收之速，是仁之至。 」上既誅和珅，宣諭廷臣：「凡為和珅薦舉及奔走其門者，悉不深究，勉其悛改，咸與自新。 」有言和珅家產尚有隱匿者，亦斥不問。 和珅在位時，令奏事者俱副本送軍機處； 呈進方物，必先關白，擅自準駁，遇不全納者悉入私家。 步軍統領巡捕營在和珅私宅供役者千馀人，又令各部以年老平庸之員保送御史。 至是，悉革其弊。 吏、戶兩部成例為和珅所變更者，諸臣奏請次第修正。 初，乾隆中命和珅改入正黃旗，及得罪，仍隸正紅旗。
His accusers compared him to Cao Cao and Wang Mang. Wu Xiongguang of Zhili, a former Grand Council clerk, was received in audience; the emperor asked, "People say He Shen harbored treasonous ambitions—is that true? " Xiongguang replied, "A man plotting treason must win hearts; He Shen had almost no loyal followers among Manchus or Han—even if he secretly harbored treason, who would follow him? " The emperor said, "Then was our punishment perhaps too swift? " Xiongguang said, "Had we not punished him quickly, the foolish would have waited and schemed, and fresh troubles would have erupted. Moving swiftly was righteousness fulfilled; ending his life swiftly was benevolence at its height. " After He Shen's execution the emperor told the court, "All who were promoted through He Shen or who courted his favor shall not be prosecuted further; let them reform and begin anew. " Reports that more of his wealth remained hidden were likewise dismissed. While in power he required every memorialist to send a duplicate copy to the Grand Council; tribute had to pass through him first; he approved or rejected at will, and anything not fully accepted went into his private stores. More than a thousand gendarmes from the patrol battalion were stationed at his private residence; he also ordered ministries to nominate aged, mediocre officials as censors. All these abuses were now abolished. Ministers petitioned to restore step by step the rules of the Ministries of Personnel and Revenue that He Shen had changed. During the Qianlong reign he had been transferred to the Plain Yellow Banner; after his fall he remained enrolled in the Plain Red Banner.
11
子丰紳殷德，尚固倫和孝公主，累擢都統兼護軍統領、內務府大臣。 和珅伏法，廷臣議奪爵職。 詔以公主故，留襲伯爵。 尋以籍沒家產，正珠朝珠非臣下所應有，鞫家人，言和珅時於燈下懸掛，臨鏡自語。 仁宗怒，褫丰紳殷德伯爵，仍襲舊職三等輕車都尉。 嘉慶七年，川、楚、陝教匪平，推恩給民公品級，授散秩大臣。 未幾，公主府長史奎福訐丰紳殷德演習武藝，謀為不軌，欲害公主。 廷臣會鞫，得誣告狀。 詔以丰紳殷德與公主素和睦，所作青蠅賦，憂讒畏譏，無怨望違悖； 惟坐國服內侍妾生女罪，褫公銜，罷職在家圈禁。 十一年，授頭等侍衛，擢副都統，賜伯爵銜。 十五年，病，乞解任，賜公爵銜。 尋卒。 無子，以和琳子丰紳伊綿襲輕車都尉。
His son Fengshenyinde, consort to Princess Hexiao, rose through repeated promotions to Commander-in-Chief, Commander of the Guards, and Minister of the Imperial Household Department. After He Shen's execution the court debated stripping his son of rank and office. An edict allowed him to keep the earldom for the princess's sake. Soon after the confiscation, eastern-pearl court beads—impermissible for a subject—were found; interrogated servants said He Shen would hang them by lamplight and mutter to his reflection. Jiaqing was furious, stripped Fengshenyinde of the earldom, and left him only the inherited rank of third-class Commandant of Light Chariots. In Jiaqing 7 (1802), after the Sichuan-Hubei-Shaanxi rebels were suppressed, he received the courtesy rank of commoner duke and was named Minister Without Portfolio. Soon afterward the princess's intendant Kuifu accused Fengshenyinde of drilling in martial arts, plotting rebellion, and intending to harm the princess. A joint court inquiry found the charge false. An edict noted that he and the princess had always been on good terms, that his "Rhapsody on the Blue Fly" voiced fear of slander rather than rebellion, and that he harbored no disloyal resentment; he was punished only for taking a concubine and fathering a child during national mourning; his ducal title was stripped, his post ended, and he was confined at home. In Jiaqing 11 (1806) he was made a first-class bodyguard, promoted to Vice Commander-in-Chief, and given the title of earl. In Jiaqing 15 (1810) he fell ill, asked to resign, and was granted the title of duke. He died soon afterward. Without a son, He Lin's son Fengshenyimian inherited the Commandant of Light Chariots.
12
和珅伏法後越十五年，國史館以列傳上。 仁宗以事蹟疏略，高宗數加譴責，闕而未載，無以信今傳後，褫編修席煜職，特詔申戒焉。
More than fifteen years after He Shen's execution, the State Historiography Office submitted his biographical draft. Jiaqing objected that the account was too thin and omitted Qianlong's repeated censures, leaving nothing credible for posterity; Compiler Xi Yu was dismissed and a special edmonitory edict was issued.
13
弟和琳，自筆帖式累遷湖廣道御史。 劾湖北按察使李天培私交糧艘帶運木植，鞫得兩廣總督福康安寄書索購狀，帝嘉和琳伉直，下部議敘，由是遂見擢用。 自吏部給事中超擢內閣學士，兼禮部侍郎銜。 尋授兵部侍郎、正藍旗漢軍副都統。 廓爾喀擾後藏，將軍福康安往剿，帝命和琳督辦前藏以東台站烏拉等事。 尋命與鄂輝更番照料糧餉，擢工部尚書。 疏陳賊酋拉特納巴都爾悔罪狀，詔令福康安受降，偕和琳妥籌善後。 未幾，授鑲白旗漢軍都統。 命偕孫士毅、惠齡覈辦察木多以西銷算事，仍理藏務。 乾隆五十八年，予雲騎尉世職。 五十九年，授四川總督。 六十年，貴州苗石柳鄧叛，擾正大、嗅腦、松桃，湖南苗吳半生、石三保應之，圍永綏，帝命雲貴總督福康安往剿。 和琳時方入京，至卬州，松桃匪已闌入秀山境。 和琳聞警馳往，督參將張志林、都司馬瑜擊走之； 後復敗賊晏農，進攻砲木山黃陂，通道松桃：賞雙眼花翎。 時福康安已解正大、嗅腦、松桃圍，攻石柳鄧於大塘汛，和琳率兵會之，遂命參贊軍事； 克蝦覅碉、烏龍巖，降七十馀寨，封一等宣勇伯。 復攻下岩碧山，賞上服貂褂。 又以降吳半生功，賞黃帶。 龍角碉、鴨保、天星諸寨大捷，加太子太保，賞玄狐端罩。 嘉慶元年，克結石岡、廖家衝、連峰蜺諸隘，賞用紫韁。 福康安卒，命和琳督辦軍務。 時石三保已就獲，石柳鄧尚據平隴。 奪尖雲山砲台，复乾州，賞三眼花翎。 八月，進圍平隴，卒於軍。 晉贈一等公，諡忠壯，賜祭葬，命配饗太廟，祀昭忠、賢良等祠，准其家建專祠。 四年，和珅誅，廷臣論和琳藉勢邀功，上亦追咎其會剿苗匪，牽掣福康安，師無功，命撤出太廟，毀專祠，奪其子丰紳伊綿公爵，改襲三等輕車都尉。
His younger brother He Lin rose from clerk to censor of the Huguang circuit. He impeached Hubei's surveillance commissioner Li Tianpei for using grain boats to ship private timber; the inquiry exposed a purchase request from Fuk'anggan; the emperor praised He Lin's integrity, ordered a merit review, and thereafter promoted him. He was jumped from supervising secretary in the Ministry of Personnel to Cabinet Academician with the rank of Vice Minister of Rites. He was soon made Vice Minister of War and Vice Commander-in-Chief of the Han Plain Blue Banner. When Gurkha raided rear Tibet, Fuk'anggan marched to suppress them; the emperor put He Lin in charge of relay stations and transport east of front Tibet. He was soon ordered to alternate with E Hui in managing supplies and was promoted to Minister of Works. He reported the rebel chief Ratna Badur's submission; the emperor ordered Fuk'anggan to accept the surrender and plan the aftermath with He Lin. He was soon appointed Commander-in-Chief of the Han Bordered White Banner. He was ordered with Sun Shiyi and Huiling to audit accounts west of Chamdo while continuing to manage Tibetan affairs. In Qianlong 58 (1793) he received the hereditary rank of Cloud Cavalry Captain. In Qianlong 59 (1794) he was appointed governor-general of Sichuan. In Qianlong 60 (1795) the Guizhou Miao leader Shi Liudeng rebelled, raiding Zhengda, Xiunao, and Songtao; Hunan Miao Wu Bansheng and Shi Sanbao joined in and besieged Yongshui; the emperor sent Fuk'anggan from Yunnan-Guizhou to suppress them. He Lin was on his way to the capital when, at Yazhou, he learned that Songtao rebels had already crossed into Xiushan. Hearing the alarm he rushed back, directed Brigade-General Zhang Zhilin and Company Commander Ma Yu to drive them off; he later defeated them at Yannong, stormed Huangpi on Paomu Mountain, and reopened the road to Songtao, for which he received double-eyed peacock feathers. Fuk'anggan had already lifted the sieges of Zhengda, Xiunao, and Songtao and was attacking Shi Liudeng at Datang Station; He Lin joined him with troops and was named deputy commander; He took Xiafei Fort and Wulong Cliff, received the surrender of more than seventy stockades, and was made first-class Baron of Manifest Courage. He next captured Yanbi Mountain and was rewarded with an imperial sable jacket. For securing Wu Bansheng's surrender he was also given the yellow belt. After major victories at Longjiao Fort, Yabao, Tianxing, and other stockades he was made Junior Tutor to the Heir Apparent and given a black-fox formal robe. In Jiaqing 1 (1796) he took Jieshi Ridge, Liaojiachong, Lianfengni, and other passes and was granted the purple reins. After Fuk'anggan's death He Lin was put in overall charge of the campaign. Shi Sanbao had already been captured, but Shi Liudeng still held Pinglong. He took Jianyun Mountain battery, recovered Qianzhou, and received triple-eyed peacock feathers. In the eighth month he advanced to besiege Pinglong and died in camp. He was posthumously made first-class duke with the temple name Loyal and Stalwart, granted state burial, enshrined in the Imperial Ancestral Temple and the shrines of loyal and worthy officials, and his family was allowed to build a private shrine. In Jiaqing 4 (1799), after He Shen's execution, ministers argued that He Lin had traded on his brother's power for credit; the emperor also blamed him for hampering Fuk'anggan during the Miao campaign; he was removed from the Imperial Ancestral Temple, his private shrine demolished, and his son Fengshenyimian reduced from duke to third-class Commandant of Light Chariots.

蘇凌阿，滿洲正白旗人。 乾隆六年繙譯舉人。 自內閣中書累遷江西廣饒九南道。 左遷。 五十年，自吏部員外郎超擢，歷兵、工、戶三部侍郎。 遷戶部尚書。 出為兩江總督。 嘉慶二年，授東閣大學士，兼署刑部尚書。 和珅誅，休致，守護裕陵。 卒。
Su Linga was a Manchu of the Plain White Banner. In Qianlong 6 (1741) he passed the Manchu translation licentiate examination. He rose from Secretariat drafter to intendant of the Guangrao-Jiunan circuit in Jiangxi. He was demoted. In Qianlong 50 (1785) he was jumped from vice director in the Ministry of Personnel to vice minister in War, Works, and Revenue in succession. He was made Minister of Revenue. He was appointed governor-general of the Two Jiangs. In Jiaqing 2 (1797) he was made Grand Secretary of the Eastern Pavilion and acting Minister of Justice. After He Shen's execution he retired to guard the Yuling Mausoleum. He died.
15
論曰：高宗英毅，大臣有過失，不稍假借。 世傳敏中以高雲從事失上意，有疾，令休沐，遽賜陀羅尼經被，遂以不起聞。 觀罷祠之詔，至引嚴嵩為類，傳聞有無未可知矣。 和珅繼用事，值高宗倦勤，怙寵貪恣，卒以是敗。 仁宗嘗論唐代宗殺李輔國，謂：「代宗為太子，不為輔國所讒者幾希。 及即帝位，正其罪而誅之，一獄吏已辦。 」蓋即為和珅發也。
The commentators observe: The Qianlong Emperor was forceful and exacting; he showed ministers no indulgence when they erred. Tradition holds that Yu Minzhong lost the emperor's favor over his association with Gao Yun; when he fell ill he was ordered to rest, was suddenly given a dharani quilt, and word spread that he would not recover. The edict stripping his sacrifices even compared him to Yan Song; whether the rumors are true remains uncertain. He Shen then took power just as Qianlong wearied of rule; trusting in favor he grew greedy and unrestrained, and was ruined in the end. Jiaqing once remarked on how Tang Daizong killed Li Fuguo, saying, "As crown prince, Daizong nearly fell to Fuguo's slanders. Once on the throne he judged his crimes and had him killed—a single jailer sufficed. " This was plainly aimed at He Shen.
