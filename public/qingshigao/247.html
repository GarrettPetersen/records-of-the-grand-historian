=彭而述=彭而述，字子籛，河南鄧州人。 明崇禎進士，官陽曲知縣，母憂歸。 順治初，英親王徇湖廣，薦為提學僉事，遷永州道參議。 孔有德定湖南，薦而述授貴州巡撫，予兵三千以行。 次靖州，降將陳友龍叛，圍州城，而述夜開西門出，營山下，選勁騎乘霧衝陣，賊潰且走，副將賀進才戰死。 城兵大噪，欲與友龍合，而述拔眾退守寶慶，告有德益師，與賊相持紫陽河上。 永州陷，劾免官。
Peng Ershu, styled Zijie, came from Dengzhou in Henan Province. He earned his jinshi degree under the Chongzhen Emperor, served as magistrate of Yangqu, and went into mourning after his mother died. Early in the Shunzhi reign, as the Prince of Ying prosecuted the campaign in Huguang, he recommended Peng for the post of educational administration commissioner; Peng was later promoted to junior vice prefect of the Yongzhou circuit. After Kong Youde secured Hunan, he recommended Ershu for governor of Guizhou and gave him three thousand troops for the expedition. At Jingzhou the surrendered general Chen Youlong rebelled and besieged the city. Ershu slipped out through the west gate by night and camped below the hills, then sent picked horsemen charging through the mist; the rebels broke and fled, though Vice Commander He Jincai was killed in the fighting. The garrison troops grew unruly and nearly joined Chen Youlong, but Ershu withdrew his men to hold Shaocheng, asked Youde for reinforcements, and locked in a stalemate with the rebels along the Ziyang River. After Yongzhou fell he was impeached and removed from office.
2
吳三桂徵水西土司安坤，而述謀曰：「烏蒙、烏撒、鎮雄、東川四府與水西為脣齒，土司隴安籓又與安氏婚媾。 今四府雖名內附，狼子野心，勢必顧惜其種類。 以水西之強，而安籓與四府附之，安坤未易制也。 莫如先定四府，馘安籓，然後西南可無患。 」三桂用其策，誅安坤。 遷廣西右布政使。 三桂薦為雲南左布政使，而述乞歸，三桂留之，會有詔召，遂行，出會城三十里，一夕無疾卒。
When Wu Sangui marched against the Shuixi chieftain An Kun, Ershu urged him: "The four prefectures of Wumeng, Wusa, Zhenxiong, and Dongchuan stand shoulder to shoulder with Shuixi, and the chieftain Long Anfan has marriage ties with the An family. Although those four prefectures are nominally under imperial control, their rulers remain fiercely tribal-minded and will surely shield their kin. With Shuixi so strong and Long Anfan and the four prefectures rallying to it, An Kun will not be easily subdued. It would be better to secure the four prefectures first and eliminate Long Anfan; only then can the southwest be kept quiet. Wu Sangui took his advice and put An Kun to death. He was promoted to Right Administration Commissioner of Guangxi. Wu Sangui recommended him for Left Administration Commissioner of Yunnan. Ershu asked to go home, but Sangui kept him there until an imperial summons arrived and he set out. Thirty li beyond the provincial capital he died suddenly in the night, without any known illness.
3
=陸振芬=陸振芬，字令遠，江南華亭人。 順治六年進士。 時兩粵未平，廷議破格用人，即新進士中遴才除道府。 振芬授廣東惠潮道副使，從師南征。 是冬，克南雄。 七年春，度大庾嶺，次韶州。 韶州以南望風降，進規會城，既下，振芬與總兵郭虎率師赴惠州，剿撫歸善、海豐諸寨。 將至，諸寨窺兵寡，出拒。 振芬選精銳數百人繞出其旁擊之，獲一隊，諸寨皆懼。 於是諭以禍福，降者踵至。 至海豐，守者抗不下。 振芬與虎駐五坡驛，他將自羊氾嶺會師合攻之，遂克其城。 碣石衛亦降。
Lu Zhenfen, styled Lingyuan, was from Huating in Jiangnan. He passed the jinshi examination in the sixth year of Shunzhi (1649). With Guangdong and Guangxi still unsettled, the court decided to appoint men outside the usual channels and chose the most capable new jinshi for circuit and prefectural offices. Zhenfen was made Vice Commissioner of the Huichao Circuit in Guangdong and marched south with the army. That winter the forces took Nanxiong. In the spring of the seventh year they crossed the Dayu Pass and made camp at Shaozhou. South of Shaozhou cities submitted as the army advanced; after Guangzhou fell, Zhenfen and Commander Guo Hu marched on Huizhou to pacify the fortified villages of Guishan, Haifeng, and the surrounding districts. As they approached, the stockades saw how few troops they had and came out to fight. Zhenfen picked several hundred crack troops to flank them, captured one band, and every stockade grew fearful. He then explained what submission or resistance would bring, and surrendering parties arrived one after another. At Haifeng the defenders held out and the city would not yield. Zhenfen and Guo Hu camped at Wupo Post while another column came down from Yangfan Ridge; the combined attack finally took the city. Jieshi Guard surrendered as well.
4
八年，抵潮州，上官，聯結諸鎮，檢制土官，招集流亡，簡省徭役，民始有更生之樂。 亂甫定，用法嚴，郡縣輒濫禁無辜。 振芬與屬吏約，期五十日清庶獄，囹圄為空。 九年，會師復平遠，總兵郝尚久故降將，陰持兩端，聞將改授水師副總兵，結山海諸寇僭立帥府。 振芬牒大吏策弭變，不應。 十年春，尚久自署新泰侯，舉兵圍道署。 振芬諭以大義，不從，使告變。 秋，固山兵至，振芬約為內應，引外兵入，誅尚久。 事平，引疾歸里。 家居四十年乃卒。
In the eighth year he reached Chaozhou, took up his post, coordinated the garrison towns, kept native officials in check, gathered refugees, and cut back levies so that people at last felt they could live again. With the fighting barely over, strict enforcement swept the region, and local officials often jailed innocent people on slight suspicion. Zhenfen pledged to his staff that every ordinary case would be cleared within fifty days, and soon the jails stood empty. In the ninth year the combined army retook Pingyuan. Commander Hao Shangjiu, once a surrendered Ming general, had long played both sides; when he heard he would be reassigned to the navy, he joined coastal and mountain bandits and set up a rebel headquarters. Zhenfen sent urgent dispatches to his superiors outlining how to head off the revolt, but no action was taken. In the spring of the tenth year Hao styled himself Marquis of Xintai and marched on Zhenfen's headquarters. Zhenfen appealed to him on grounds of loyalty, but Hao refused to listen, so Zhenfen reported the rebellion. That autumn banner troops arrived; Zhenfen arranged an inside rendezvous, brought the relief force in, and Hao Shangjiu was killed. After order was restored he pleaded illness and retired to his home. He lived in retirement for forty years before he died.
5
=姚延著=姚延著，字象懸，浙江烏程人。 順治六年進士，除廣西慶遠知府。 從師南征，調柳州，有守禦功，又調平樂。 遷廣東嶺南道副使，撫僮寨，擢江南按察使。
Yao Yanzhu, styled Xiangxuan, was from Wucheng in Zhejiang. He became a jinshi in the sixth year of Shunzhi and was appointed prefect of Qingyuan in Guangxi. He campaigned in the south, was transferred to Liuzhou where he distinguished himself in defense, and later to Pingle. He was promoted to Vice Commissioner of the Lingnan Circuit in Guangdong, pacified Zhuang stockades, and was later made Surveillance Commissioner of Jiangnan.
6
十六年，鄭成功內犯，陷鎮江，入攻江寧。 延著佐總督郎廷佐繕守備，安輯危城，閭閻不擾。 民間時有羊尾黨，事發，株連數百人。 延著謂廷佐曰：「寇在門，不可興大獄、搖人心。 」獄乃解。 當事急，人多疑貳，民間有宿怨，輒誣以通敵。 延著嚴治反坐，多所全活。 城民有升高而望者，邏者執之，總管喀喀木以為敵諜，延著力爭，得不死。 喀喀木部兵擾城市，延著捕得械斃之。 吏卒私掠被難婦女，延著親駐江乾，召其家，遣還者一千七百人，以此忤喀喀木。 事定敘功，擢河南左布政使。 旋以憂歸，而金壇獄起。
In the sixteenth year Zheng Chenggong struck inland, took Zhenjiang, and pressed on toward Nanjing. Yanzhu helped Governor Lang Tingzuo ready the city's defenses, kept order in the threatened capital, and left ordinary neighborhoods largely undisturbed. A secret group known as the Yangwei Party had operated among the people; when the case broke, hundreds were swept up by association. Yanzhu told Tingzuo, "The enemy is at our gates—we must not launch a sweeping prosecution and unsettle the populace." The mass trial was called off. In the crisis many grew suspicious of their neighbors, and old feuds led to accusations of collusion with the enemy. Yanzhu cracked down hard on false accusers and thereby saved many lives. When a townsman climbed a height to look out, patrolmen seized him and Superintendent Kekemu declared him a spy; Yanzhu argued strenuously and saved his life. Soldiers under Kekemu were plundering the city; Yanzhu seized the culprits and had them executed. Officials and soldiers were seizing women made refugees by the fighting; Yanzhu went in person to the riverbank, called their families forward, and restored seventeen hundred women—an act that deeply offended Kekemu. After the crisis he was rewarded for his service and promoted to Left Administration Commissioner of Henan. He soon went home to mourn his parents, and then the notorious Jintan case erupted.
7
鎮江之陷也，屬縣戒嚴。 金壇知縣任體坤集縣中士大夫王重、袁大受等謀遣諸生十輩詣鎮江乞緩兵。 丹徒亂民王再興兵起，復令書吏、耆民數十人送款，盡竊庫帑以遁。 喀喀木等擊敗成功，體坤乃復至縣，賂重、大受謁大吏，謂士民送款，冀掩棄城罪。 重、大受居鄉多不法，為諸生所撓。 至是欲以叛坐諸生，泄私怨，列姓名以上。 巡按馬勝聲疏聞，下廷佐令延著鞫其獄。 延著縶縣吏李鍾秀，訊得實，欲但坐體坤，餘皆減罪。 大受騰書京師為蜚語，欲並陷延著，御史馮班發其狀。 時侍郎尼滿奉詔勘提督馬逢知獄，命即訊，乃坐重、大受及諸士大夫集議者。 諸生及書吏、耆民送款者皆斬，體坤以被逼迫減為絞。 巡按何可化又疏劾延著讞從叛罪人史記青、管得勝傅輕比，又有王天福、韓王錫並縱不擬罪，與金壇獄並論，亦坐絞。 時喀喀木主軍事，新破敵，尤威重，素不慊於延著。 民間謂延著之死，喀喀木實主之。 就刑日，江寧為罷市，士民哭踴。 喪歸，數百里祭奠不絕，建祠雞鳴山下私祀焉。
When Zhenjiang fell, the surrounding counties were placed under martial law. Jintan's magistrate Ren Tikun joined local notables Wang Chong and Yuan Dashou in a plan to send ten parties of students to Zhenjiang to plead for a ceasefire. When the Dantu rebel Wang Zai rose in arms, Ren again sent dozens of clerks and elders to offer surrender—and emptied the county treasury before fleeing. After Kekemu and others routed Zheng Chenggong, Tikun returned to his post and bribed Wang Chong and Yuan Dashou to tell superiors that the local elite had negotiated surrender, hoping to hide his own desertion of the county. Wang Chong and Yuan Dashou had long bullied the countryside until the local students stood up to them. Now they sought revenge by charging the students with treason and submitted lists of names to the authorities. Regional Inspector Ma Shengsheng reported the matter, and Lang Tingzuo was ordered to have Yanzhu investigate. Yanzhu arrested county clerk Li Zhongxiu, uncovered the truth, and sought to punish only Ren Tikun while mitigating charges against the others. Yuan Dashou sent inflammatory reports to the capital hoping to implicate Yanzhu as well, but Censor Feng Ban exposed the conspiracy. Vice Minister Niman had been sent to investigate another case and was ordered to take this one immediately; Wang Chong, Yuan Dashou, and the gentry who had joined the deliberations were all convicted. The students and the clerks and elders who had negotiated surrender were all executed; Ren Tikun, deemed to have acted under duress, received the reduced sentence of strangulation. Regional Inspector He Kehua further accused Yanzhu of leniency toward alleged rebels Shi Jiqing and Guan Desheng, and of releasing Wang Tianfu and Han Wangxi without charge; grouping these with the Jintan case, Yanzhu too was sentenced to death by strangulation. Kekemu then commanded the armies, fresh from victory and at the height of his influence, and had long borne a grudge against Yanzhu. Popular rumor held that Kekemu was really behind Yanzhu's execution. On the day he was executed Nanjing shut down its markets; scholars and commoners wept and stamped their feet in grief. As his coffin traveled home, mourners lined the route for hundreds of li, and a private shrine was erected for him below Jiming Hill.
8
子淳燾，康熙六年進士，授內閣中書舍人。 伏闕上書為延著訟冤。 累擢湖廣提學道僉事，坐事罷，未行，值叛卒夏逢龍之亂，誓死不為屈。 事聞，复官，授岳常澧道副使。 卒。
His son Chunyi passed the jinshi examination in the sixth year of Kangxi and was appointed a drafting officer in the Grand Secretariat. He knelt at the palace gate and submitted a memorial pleading his father's innocence. He rose to Educational Administration Commissioner of Huguang, but was dismissed before taking up the post; when the mutineer Xia Fenglong rebelled, he swore he would die rather than yield. When the court learned of his conduct he was reinstated and appointed Vice Prefect of the Yuechangli Circuit. He died in office.
9
=畢振姬=畢振姬，字亮四，山西高平人。 順治三年進士，授平陽教授。 入為國子監助教，累遷刑部員外郎。 曹事暇，獨坐陋室，布被瓦盆，讀書不稍倦。
Bi Zhenji, styled Liangsi, was from Gaoping in Shanxi. He became a jinshi in the third year of Shunzhi and was appointed instructor at Pingyang. He entered the capital as an assistant instructor at the Imperial Academy and eventually rose to Vice Director in the Ministry of Punishments. In his spare hours he would sit alone in a bare room furnished with nothing but a cotton quilt and a clay bowl, reading without tiring.
10
十年，出為山東濟南道參議。 歲旱，流民踞山谷為盜，振姬晝夜馳三百里往諭之，悉就撫，全活者七​​千餘人。 泰山香稅，歲羨餘七千金，例充公使錢，振姬悉以佐餉。 調廣東驛傳道僉事。 時三籓使命往來絡繹，胥吏乘以私派折價，民苦之，振姬一繩以法，閱數月，減船數百，減​​費七萬有奇。 調浙江金衢嚴道參政，擢廣西按察使。 所至以廉能聞。 遷湖廣布政使，乞病歸。
In the tenth year he was posted as Junior Vice Prefect of the Jinan Circuit in Shandong. During a famine year refugees had turned bandit in the hills; Zhenji rode three hundred li without rest to reason with them, brought them all back to loyalty, and saved more than seven thousand lives. The Taishan incense tax produced an annual surplus of seven thousand taels, normally reserved for official missions; Zhenji diverted all of it to army provisions. He was transferred to Commissioner of the Courier Circuit in Guangdong. With the Three Feudatories constantly sending envoys, clerks extorted kickbacks through corrupt pricing and the people groaned under the burden; Zhenji enforced the law strictly, and within months cut hundreds of boats from the fleet and saved more than seventy thousand taels. He was made Administrator of the Jinquyan Circuit in Zhejiang and later Surveillance Commissioner of Guangxi. Everywhere he served he won a reputation for integrity and ability. He was promoted to Administration Commissioner of Huguang but soon asked to retire on grounds of illness.
11
康熙中，詔舉博學鴻儒，左都御史魏裔介、副都御史劉楗疏薦之。 十八年，命廷臣舉清廉吏，裔介复疏言：「振姬清操絕世，才略過人。 請告十餘年，躬耕百畝，讀書不輟。 」楗亦言：「振姬居官不染一塵。 歸日一僕一馬，了無長物，真學行兼優之人。 」下部議，以振姬老，置勿用。 尋卒。
During the Kangxi reign the throne called for eminent scholars; Left Censor-in-Chief Wei Yijie and Vice Censor-in-Chief Liu Jian both recommended him. In the eighteenth year the court was instructed to nominate honest officials; Wei Yijie wrote again: "Zhenji's integrity is unrivaled, and his talent extraordinary. He has been retired more than ten years, farming a hundred mu with his own hands and never ceasing his studies." Liu Jian added: "In office Zhenji never stained his hands with the slightest corruption. When he left office he had but one servant and one horse and no possessions to speak of—a man of true learning and character." The ministries deliberated and, finding Zhenji too old, set the nomination aside. He died soon afterward.
12
=方國棟=方國棟，字幹霄，順天宛平人。 順治三年舉人，授蠡縣教諭。 入為國子監助教，累擢至刑部郎中。
Fang Guodong, styled Ganxiao, was from Wanping in the Shuntian metropolitan district. He passed the provincial examination in the third year of Shunzhi and was appointed county instructor at Lixian. He came to the capital as an Imperial Academy assistant instructor and eventually rose to Director in the Ministry of Punishments.
13
十六年，出為廣東海北道僉事。 海寇鄧耀居島中，時出剽掠。 國棟以三千人分五道進剿，檄鄰道出兵扼要隘，擒耀，解散餘黨。 事平，雷、廉兩部諸富人為賊所誣，械系者眾，國棟察其冤，為辨雪。 諸富人裒千金為報，國棟曰：「吾憫若無辜，柰何污我？ 」卻之。
In the sixteenth year he was posted as Commissioner of the Haibei Circuit in Guangdong. The pirate Deng Yao lived on an offshore island and raided the coast whenever he could. Guodong led three thousand men in five columns, ordered neighboring districts to block the passes, captured Deng Yao, and dispersed his followers. After the campaign many wealthy men in Leizhou and Lianzhou had been framed by the bandits and imprisoned in large numbers; Guodong saw they were innocent and cleared them. The wealthy men pooled a thousand taels as a gift; Guodong said, "I pitied your innocence—how could you try to bribe me?" He declined the gift.
14
遷山西寧武道參議。 康熙六年裁缺，改江南蘇松常道參議。 太湖堤岸傾圮，率吏民修葺，修沿海墩台及吳淞、劉河兩徬，工費不擾民。 師下閩、粵，徵調旁午，國棟一意與民休息，每遇急徵，從容部署。 芻茭糧糗，預儲以待，軍興無乏，閭左晏然。 戒屬吏無朘民，郡縣稍稍知斂戢，不敢事剝削。
He was posted as Junior Vice Prefect of the Ningwu Circuit in Shanxi. When his post was abolished in the sixth year of Kangxi, he was reassigned as Junior Vice Prefect of the Susongchang Circuit in Jiangnan. When the Taihu dikes gave way he led officials and people to rebuild them, restored coastal watchtowers and the Wusong and Liuhe embankments, and did all of it without burdening the populace. As imperial armies marched on Fujian and Guangdong, requisitions piled up one after another; Guodong focused on giving the people respite, and whenever urgent levies came he organized supplies calmly. He stockpiled fodder and grain in advance, so that armies never went wanting and neighborhoods stayed at peace. He forbade his staff to squeeze the people, and county officials gradually learned restraint and stopped their extortions.
15
連歲用兵，度支不給，詔各省籌裕餉之策。 國棟言：「古今生財之說，開與節二者而已。 議開於今日，已無可加，當議節，自朝廷始。 舊制，江南歲市布五萬匹供宮府賚予，宜可罷，歲省帑金三萬。 」議上，報可，滿洲兵駐防蘇州，議築營舍於王府基，當城中。 國棟以兵民雜居難久安，持不可，乃改營南城隙地，民便之。 宜興善權山中寺僧與豪族爭地，聚眾焚寺殺僧，知縣告亂，大吏將發兵。 國棟單騎馳往，得首禍寘法，餘無所問。 吳俗健訟，喜投缿告密，國棟輒不問，即有所案，亦從寬。 馭吏嚴，而拊循士民具有恩意。 十六年，卒。 吳民思之，建祠虎丘山麓以祀。
Years of warfare had drained the treasury, and the throne ordered every province to propose ways to secure adequate military revenue. Guodong argued, "Throughout history the ways to generate revenue boil down to two: expanding income and cutting costs." "To expand revenue today there is nothing left to add; we must discuss thrift, and it must begin at court." "Under the old system Jiangnan bought fifty thousand bolts of cloth annually for palace gifts—that custom should be abolished, saving thirty thousand taels a year." The proposal was adopted. When Manchu troops were stationed at Suzhou, officials proposed building barracks on a former princely residence in the heart of the city. Guodong argued that mixing troops and civilians could not last and objected firmly; the barracks were moved to open ground in the southern suburbs instead, to the people's relief. In the Shanchuan hills of Yixing, temple monks and a powerful clan fought over land; the mob burned the temple and killed monks. The magistrate reported a rebellion, and superiors prepared to send troops. Guodong rode there alone, arrested the ringleaders and punished them, and took no action against anyone else. The people of Wu were famously litigious and fond of anonymous denunciations; Guodong routinely ignored them, and even when he took up a case he leaned toward leniency. He was strict with his staff yet considerate and generous toward scholars and commoners alike. He died in the sixteenth year (1659). The people of Wu mourned him and built a shrine at the foot of Tiger Hill in his honor.
16
=於朋舉=於朋舉，字襄子，江南金壇人。 順治六年進士，改庶吉士，散館授檢討。 十二年，出為河南睢陳道副使，政不擾民。 郾城盜殺縣官而逸，士民洶洶，謂城將受屠。 朋舉馳至，撫諭毋恐。 營將以兵至，拒不使入城。 大吏召朋舉詰責，對曰：「郾城令，朋舉婦翁也。 豈不欲甘心是盜？ 獨柰何苦良民！ 」大吏悟，止兵，亦得盜正其罪。
Yu Pengju, styled Xiangzi, was from Jintan in Jiangnan. He became a jinshi in the sixth year of Shunzhi, entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor, and upon graduating was appointed a revising compiler. In the twelfth year he was posted as Vice Commissioner of the Suichen Circuit in Henan and governed without harassing the people. When bandits in Yancheng killed the magistrate and fled, the populace panicked, fearing the city would be put to the sword. Pengju hurried there and reassured them not to be afraid. When a garrison commander marched troops toward the city, he barred them from entering. His superiors summoned him to account for himself; he answered, "The magistrate of Yancheng was my father-in-law." "Do you think I did not want those bandits brought to justice?" "But why visit suffering on innocent people?" His superiors understood, halted the troops, and the bandits were duly punished by law.
17
遷福建福寧道參政。 興化瀕海，鎮將所部皆群盜受撫者。 有材官辱張氏僕，張氏以告。 鎮將撻材官，部卒大譁，毀張氏之室，欲劫鎮將為亂。 鎮將避去，則縊被撻者寘張氏，謂其僕殺之。 朋舉甫到官，廉得首惡，猝縛至，集文武吏會鞫，健兒帶刀環立瞋視。 朋舉從容曰：「若曹幹軍法，罪重。 念若曹約束無素，但用殺人律，罪有專屬。 」眾乃泥首，言殺人者為張氏僕。 朋舉曰：「若曹氣焰何等，彼能於千百健兒中奪一人縊之耶？ 」召訊證者，俱吐實，誅三人而事定。 泉州提督剿海盜，盜逸入興化界，鎮將獲數百人。 朋舉視其嘗薙發者，曰：「此良民被陷，當宥。 」有年少者，曰：「童穉何知，又當宥。 」全活甚眾。
He was made Administrator of the Funing Circuit in Fujian. Xinghua lay on the coast, and the commander's troops were mostly formerly pacified pirate bands. An officer insulted a servant of the Zhang family, who lodged a complaint. The commander had the officer flogged, whereupon the troops rioted, wrecked the Zhang home, and nearly seized their commander in mutiny. The commander fled; the mutineers hanged the flogged officer at the Zhang residence and accused their servant of murder. Pengju had only just taken office; he identified the ringleaders, seized them at once, and convened civil and military officials for trial while armed soldiers glared from every side. Pengju said calmly, "You have violated military law—a grave offense." "But given your undisciplined conduct, apply only the homicide statute—guilt rests with whoever actually killed him." The troops kowtowed and claimed the Zhang servant was the killer. Pengju asked, "With your numbers and ferocity, could a household servant seize one of you from your midst and hang him?" He called witnesses, all confessed, three men were executed, and the affair was settled. When the Quanzhou naval commander drove sea pirates inland, the regional commander seized several hundred men in Xinghua. Seeing those who had once shaved their heads in the Ming style, he said, "These are innocent people framed—they should be spared." Of the younger captives he said, "Children know nothing of such matters—spare them too." He thereby saved a great many lives.
18
鄭成功屯廈門，與漳州隔海相望。 固山額真駐會城，遣兵戍漳州，番代歲四易，民苦供役。 朋舉請駐防無屢更，不許； 固請展其期，歲再易，民稍蘇息。 擢四川按察使、山東右布政使。 父憂歸。
Zheng Chenggong held Xiamen, facing Zhangzhou across the strait. A banner general at the provincial capital garrisoned Zhangzhou with troops rotated four times a year, and the people groaned under the burden of supply. Pengju asked that the garrison not rotate so often; his request was denied; he pressed to extend the rotation interval to twice a year, and the people found some relief. He was promoted to Surveillance Commissioner of Sichuan and Right Administration Commissioner of Shandong. He returned home to mourn his father.
19
起授湖南布政使。 上官，見胥吏至數百，曰：「兵初罷，民方重困。 此曹鮮衣美食，縱橫市井間，何所​​取諸？ 」汰其十九，擇謹願者，取足供文書而已。 數為大吏言地方利病，有司賢不肖積與之忤，被劾鐫級，未行，而大吏以貪敗。 士民惜之。 尋卒。
When recalled from mourning he was appointed Administration Commissioner of Hunan. On taking office he saw hundreds of clerks and said, "The war has just ended and the people are still desperately poor." "Yet these men dress well, eat well, and swagger through the markets—where does it all come from?" He dismissed nineteen out of twenty, kept only the most conscientious, and retained just enough staff for paperwork. He repeatedly reported local problems to his superiors, clashed with good and bad officials alike, was impeached and demoted—and before he could leave, his accuser was himself ruined for corruption. Scholars and commoners mourned his departure. He died soon afterward.
20
=王天鑑=王天鑑，字近微，直隸萬全人。 順治三年進士，授山東恩縣知縣。 縣接直隸界，自明季為盜藪，嘗一歲七被寇。 天鑑上官，諭父老曰：「往歲寇至，縣輒不守，由人無固志。 自今勿復逃，視知縣所向。 」俄而寇大至，天鑑坐城上，從容指揮，寇疑有伏，逡巡去。 於是葺樓櫓，治城隍，嚴候望，時巡徼，守具大備。 按行鄉鄙，舉團練，立砦十有九，枹鼓相聞，久之得步卒萬八千、騎士三百。 巡按御史疏聞，令天鑑自治兵。 廉得境內賊渠數輩，夜突至其鄉呼之出，賊錯愕不能遁，皆誅之。 寇據曹縣，巡撫檄天鑑與諸道兵會剿，率所部為前鋒，冒矢石深入，諸軍踵之，復其城。 嘗以輕騎逐賊，日暮被圍，短兵相接，手格殺數賊，潰圍出，不失一騎。 在恩四年，屢與寇戰，俘馘無算，降者安撫之。 寇遠遁，招徠屯種，流亡復歸，墾荒千八百頃。 建書院，弦誦不輟。 政聲為山東最，上考，內遷禮部主事。 十一年，始行耤田親耕禮，天鑑參酌古今，悉合禮宜。 累遷郎中。 主山東鄉試。 十二年，出為陝西河西道參議。 與屬吏約，毋獵民枉法。
Wang Tianjian, styled Jinwei, was from Wanquan in Zhili. He became a jinshi in the third year of Shunzhi and was appointed magistrate of En County in Shandong. The county bordered Zhili and had been a bandit haven since the late Ming; one year it was raided seven times. On taking office he told the elders, "In past years when bandits came the county was abandoned because people lost heart." "From now on do not flee—follow where your magistrate leads." When a large bandit force arrived, Tianjian sat on the city wall and directed the defense calmly; the raiders suspected an ambush and withdrew. He repaired towers and walls, restored the moat, tightened watches, patrolled regularly, and made the defenses complete. Touring the countryside he organized militia, built nineteen stockades with linked drum signals, and eventually mustered eighteen thousand foot soldiers and three hundred horsemen. The touring censor reported his work, and the court authorized Tianjian to command local forces himself. He identified several local bandit leaders, raided their villages by night, caught them before they could flee, and executed them all. When bandits seized Caoxian, the governor ordered a joint campaign; Tianjian led his men as vanguard, charged through arrow fire, and the combined force retook the city. Once, pursuing bandits with light cavalry, he was surrounded at dusk; fighting hand to hand he killed several raiders, broke through, and did not lose a single trooper. Over four years at En he fought bandits repeatedly, took countless prisoners and heads, and pacified those who surrendered. When the bandits fled he encouraged settlement and farming; refugees returned and nearly two thousand qing of wasteland was brought under cultivation. He founded an academy where study never ceased. His reputation was the finest in Shandong; rated top in evaluation he was transferred to the Ministry of Rites. In the eleventh year, when the emperor first performed the sacred-field plowing ceremony in person, Tianjian coordinated the rites from historical precedent and all passed muster. He rose steadily to Director in the ministry. He served as chief examiner for the Shandong provincial examinations. In the twelfth year he was posted as Junior Vice Prefect of the Hexi Circuit in Shaanxi. He pledged to his staff not to prey on the people or pervert the law.
21
天鑑固長治兵，按籍討軍實，誡將弁毋以軍糈肥私橐。 性剛介負氣，數忤上官。 歲餘，謝病歸。 絕跡公府，門下士或有餽遺，不受，曰：「飭簠簋，惜名節，足以報舉主矣！ 」康熙初，大臣薦，不出。 尋卒。
Tianjian had long excelled at military affairs; he audited troop rolls and warned officers not to fill private purses from army rations. Stern and proud by nature, he often clashed with his superiors. After little more than a year he pleaded illness and went home. He avoided official circles entirely; when students offered gifts he refused them, saying, "Guarding one's integrity is repayment enough to those who recommended you!" Early in the Kangxi reign ministers recommended him, but he would not serve again. He died soon afterward.
22
=趙廷標=趙廷標，浙江錢塘人。 順治三年，以拔貢生授福建永定知縣。 廣東大埔逸寇江龍以萬餘人犯縣城，廷標城守。 寇穴地入，瀦池水以待，地砲不得發； 樹雲梯乘城，於城上懸柵墮之。 持三月，食垂盡。 值立春，廷標張鼓樂，開城門，迎春東郊。 寇疑有伏，引去。 密遣兵間道往伏兩山間，出不意夾擊，敗之。 進至龍磜寨，捕斬略盡。
Zhao Tingbiao was from Qiantang in Zhejiang. In the third year of Shunzhi, as a selected tribute student he was appointed magistrate of Yongding in Fujian. Jiang Long, a rebel leader from Dapu in Guangdong, attacked the county seat with more than ten thousand men; Tingbiao held the walls. The bandits tunneled under the walls; he flooded the moats so underground powder charges could not be fired; they raised scaling ladders; he hung timber barriers from the battlements to drop on them. The siege lasted three months until food was nearly gone. At the Beginning of Spring, Tingbiao staged drums and music, opened the gates, and went out to the eastern suburbs for the spring ritual. The bandits suspected an ambush and withdrew. He secretly sent troops along hidden paths to ambush them between the hills and routed them in a surprise attack. Pursuing to Longji stockade, he captured and executed most of the survivors.
23
擢湖廣衡州同知，署府事。 蠲賦墾荒，流亡復業。 歲大饑，賑恤有實惠。 經略大學士洪承疇薦廷標，十七年，擢雲南迤東道副使。 安普諸番為土官所誘，競作不靖。 廷標設方略、行間，解散之，遂復維摩舊地。 移檄諭寧州彌勒、巴盤、八甸，罷捕逐之令，令諸持田器者皆為良民，持兵者乃為賊。 巡行安撫，諸路悉平。 治迤東十八年。 康熙中，調廣東廣肇南韶道副使。 安普民、蠻聞其去，塹道塞城留之。 慰諭再三，乃得行。
He was promoted to Sub-Prefect of Hengzhou in Huguang and served as acting prefect. He remitted taxes, encouraged reclaiming wasteland, and brought refugees back to their farms. In a year of severe famine his relief reached people with real effect. Grand Secretary Hong Chengchou recommended him; in the seventeenth year he was made Vice Prefect of the Yidong Circuit in Yunnan. Various tribal peoples of the Anpu region, incited by native officials, rose in succession. Tingbiao devised strategy and used agents to break them up, and thereby recovered the old Weimo territory. He issued proclamations to Ningzhou, Mile, Bapan, and Badian, ending the policy of hunting tribesmen down: whoever held farming tools was a loyal subject; only those bearing arms were treated as rebels. He toured the region to pacify the people, and every district was brought to order. He governed the Yidong region for eighteen years. During the Kangxi reign he was transferred to Vice Commissioner of the Guangzhaonan Shao Circuit in Guangdong. When the people and tribes of Anpu heard he was leaving, they dug ditches across the roads and blocked the city gates to keep him from going. Only after repeated reassurances was he able to leave.
24
兩粵八排諸山寇聞廷標來，望風解散。 連州亂，至，立就撫。 逾年以憂去。 起湖南驛鹽道副使。 捕治劇寇，誅其渠，餘悉縱歸農。 湖南方用兵，芻茭械仗，儲峙供給，不誤晷刻，民不困役。 兼攝糧道。 會湘東民變，巡撫韓世琦令廷標往撫之。 單騎馳諭，皆悔泣聽命，散遣之。 事稍定，修岳麓書院，置田禀諸生。 嘗行部至衡州，父老羅拜車下，號以「慈母」。 俄遷陝西糧儲道參議。 已病，值武昌兵變，軍書至，猶強起視事。 病篤乞歸，至家卒。
When the mountain bandits of the Eight Ranks in Guangdong and Guangxi heard Tingbiao was coming, they dispersed at the news. When Lianzhou rebelled, he arrived and the rebels submitted at once. After a year he went home to mourn. When recalled he was appointed Vice Commissioner of the Courier and Salt Circuit in Hunan. He captured major bandits, executed their leaders, and released the rest to return to farming. With Hunan at war, he stockpiled fodder, weapons, and equipment and met every deadline without burdening the people with excessive levies. He also served concurrently as grain transport commissioner. When eastern Hunan erupted in unrest, Governor Han Shiqi sent Tingbiao to pacify the region. He rode out alone to reason with them; they wept in repentance, obeyed his orders, and were sent home. Once order was restored he restored the Yuelu Academy and endowed fields to support its students. Once on an inspection tour to Hengzhou, elders lined the road to bow before his carriage and hailed him as their "kind mother." He was soon transferred to Junior Vice Prefect of the Grain Storage Circuit in Shaanxi. Already ill, when the Wuchang mutiny broke out and urgent dispatches arrived, he still dragged himself to his desk. His illness worsened and he begged to go home; he died soon after arriving.

=【論】=論曰：自置督撫，而兩司權輕，況於各道； 然以賢者處之，奉職循理，視民之所急，弭亂解嬈，亦足以為治。 而述、振芬、振姬、天鑑皆有才略，根本尤在廉勤。 延著、國棟、廷標當治亂用重之日，濟之以寬仁，雖以是罷貶，甚或中危法，而一不自卹，是皆能舉其職者。 澤及於斯民，亦已多矣。
The commentators say: Once governors-general and governors were installed, the provincial commissions lost weight—and circuit offices even more so; yet when capable men hold such posts, perform their duties conscientiously, address what the people need most, and quell unrest, that is government enough. Ershu, Zhenfen, Zhenji, and Tianjian all had talent and drive; above all they were rooted in integrity and diligence. Yanzhu, Guodong, and Tingbiao served in times that demanded force; they tempered it with mercy. Some were demoted or even faced capital charges, yet none spared themselves—all fulfilled their duty. The good they did for the people was already beyond measure.
