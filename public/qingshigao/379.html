趙慎畛盧坤曾勝陶澍
Zhao Shen-dian, Lu Kun, Ceng Sheng, and Tao Shu
3
趙慎畛，字笛樓，湖南武陵人。 為諸生時，學政錢灃器之，曰：「人英也！ 」嘉慶元年，成進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 遷御史、給事中。 條上川、楚善後屯田保甲事宜。 巡通州漕，革陋規，廉得楊村通判科索剝船，奏褫其職。 湖南學政徐松矜愎失士心，欲附慎畛自固，常列其弟子優等，慎畛列款糾劾罷之。 兩廣總督蔣攸銛薦其才可大用。
Zhao Shen-dian, whose courtesy name was Dilou, came from Wuling in Hunan. While still a licentiate, the provincial education intendant Qian Feng took a strong liking to him and declared, "Here is a man of rare ability! " In the first year of the Jiaqing reign he became a jinshi, entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor, and received appointment as a compiler. He was later promoted to censor and then supervising secretary. He memorialized with detailed proposals on garrison colonization and baojia organization for the post-rebellion recovery of Sichuan and Huguang. On an inspection tour of the Tongzhou grain transport, he swept away abusive customs, uncovered extortion by the Yangcun subprefect who had been stripping transport boats, and had him dismissed by memorial. The Hunan education intendant Xu Song was haughty and obstinate and had alienated the scholarly community; hoping to win Shen-dian's support, he habitually gave top marks to his own students. Shen-dian drew up a detailed impeachment and had him dismissed. Jiang Youxian, governor-general of the Two Guangs, recommended him as a man whose abilities merited high appointment.
4
十七年，出為廣東惠潮嘉道。 嚴治械鬥，捕南澳、澄海、潮陽盜甚眾； 沿海民寮居為逋逃藪，悉編入保甲。 逾年，擢廣西按察使。 天地會匪結黨構亂，脅有貲者入其中，慎畛惟嚴罪匪首，被脅者不坐。 廣東洋匪投誠後，漸入廣西為盜。 設水路巡船以護商旅，督守令以捕盜多少為殿最。 遠郡招解重囚煩費，吏因諱盜，省文法，嚴舉劾，緝捕始力。 二十年，遷廣東布政使。 州縣多積虧，展轉相承，悉心鉤稽，除其糾轕，庫儲頓增。 南海、高要瀕河堤防多圮，民苦水患，籌款生息資歲修，屯田五千餘頃。 賦重為累，請減糧額，攤抵於沙坦輕則之地。 粵俗奢靡，刊發陳宏謀行政訓俗遺規，躬行節儉以示勸。
In the seventeenth year of Jiaqing he was posted as intendant of the Huizhou-Chaozhou-Jiazhou circuit in Guangdong. He cracked down hard on clan feuds and captured large numbers of bandits in Nan'ao, Chenghai, and Chaoyang; along the coast, where scattered hamlets had become refuges for fugitives, he registered every household under the baojia system. Within a year he was promoted to provincial judge of Guangxi. Tiandihui rebels had formed secret societies and stirred up trouble, forcing wealthy men to join. Shen-dian punished only the ringleaders with severity; those who had been coerced went unpunished. After coastal pirates in Guangdong surrendered, they gradually drifted into Guangxi and turned to robbery. He set up river patrol craft to protect merchants and travelers, and ranked prefects and magistrates by the number of bandits they captured. In remote prefectures the cost of escorting major prisoners discouraged reporting, so officials concealed crime. He streamlined legal procedure, tightened impeachment, and only then did pursuit become effective. In the twentieth year he was made provincial treasurer of Guangdong. Prefectures and counties had long carried deficits forward from one administration to the next; he audited accounts with care, untangled the backlog, and treasury reserves rose sharply. River dikes in Nanhai and Gaoyao had largely collapsed and the people suffered chronic flooding; he set aside capital to earn interest for annual repairs and reclaimed more than five thousand qing of garrison land. Heavy tax quotas had become an unbearable burden; he petitioned to reduce the grain assessment and spread the shortfall onto sandy tidal flats taxed at lower rates. Cantonese society was given to extravagance; he published Chen Hongmou's surviving regulations on government and moral reform, and himself lived frugally as an example.
5
二十三年，擢廣西巡撫。 習知粵西地勢如建瓴，旬日不雨即旱竭，勸民修隄塘，造龍骨車，開廕井，設井筒架，皆頒式俾仿行。 地連黔、楚，群盜出沒，宜山會匪廖五桂、藍耀青分踞新、舊兩墟，糾眾分黨，偽立名目，勒索殷戶，爭利相擾，親往捕誅之。 飭屬行保甲，置望樓，練民壯互相守望，縣建卡房數十座，府各督屬會營巡緝。 柳州至省千餘裡，設水汛四十三所，終任凡獲盜千七百餘人。 盜多出於流匪，編客民籍，驅其單身遊蕩者，礦廠窯榨傭丁皆立冊，有保者留，否則逐。 故事，梧、潯二關，巡撫例得動用盈餘。 慎畛曰：「吾家衣食粗足，身為大臣，取盈將安用之？ 當為國家布仁澤耳。 」乃於桂林設預備倉，增設書院，柳州、慶遠、思恩三府皆創設之； 繕城濬河，廣置棲流所，並取給焉。
In the twenty-third year he was appointed governor of Guangxi. Knowing that western Guangdong's terrain fell steeply like water tipped from a jar and that a fortnight without rain brought drought, he urged the people to repair dikes and ponds, build chain-pump waterwheels, dig sheltered wells, and erect well-hoist frames, issuing standard designs for each. The province bordered Guizhou and Huguang, and bandits roamed freely. At Yishan, Tiandihui chiefs Liao Wugui and Lan Yaoqing held the old and new market towns, raised factions under bogus titles, and extorted wealthy households until rivalries turned violent. Shen-dian went in person and had them captured and executed. He ordered his subordinates to enforce the baojia system, erected watch towers, drilled local militia for mutual defense, built dozens of checkpoint posts in each county, and had each prefecture coordinate joint patrols with the military. Along the thousand-odd li from Liuzhou to the capital he established forty-three river guard posts; by the end of his term he had captured more than seventeen hundred bandits. Most bandits were rootless drifters; he registered migrant populations, expelled unattached vagrants, and required registers for all hired hands at mines, kilns, and presses—those with guarantors could remain, the rest were driven out. By established practice, the governor was entitled to use surplus revenue from the Wuzhou and Xunzhou customs posts. Shen-dian said, "My family's needs are modestly met; as a senior minister, what would I do with surplus funds? They should be used to spread the state's benevolence." He therefore established a reserve granary in Guilin, expanded the provincial academy, and founded new academies in Liuzhou, Qingyuan, and Si'en; repaired city walls, dredged rivers, and built shelters for the destitute, drawing on the surplus for all of it.
6
道光二年，入覲，宣宗嘉其誠實不欺，溫諭褒勉，擢閩浙總督。 嚴申軍律，課諸鎮營汛勤訓練。 浙江提督沈添華玩縱，劾罷之。 責水師緝海盜，盜多就擒。 上游四府多山，客民租山立廠，遊匪群聚，遣兵搜山，捕誅其魁。 閩安所轄有瑯琦島，居民多為奸利，擒治之，移駐水師，建砲台，遂為省城門戶。 台灣自來多亂，動煩大兵，慎畛尤以為慮，盡選賢能以治。 鳳山莠民楊良斌煽眾起事，檄巡道孔昭虔、知府孔傳穟剿治，未一月而定，不煩一兵渡海。 驩瑪蘭初設治，部議賦則較重，奏減之。 民入山伐木，歲供道廠船料，匠首苛斂激變，捕誅首亂，更定採木章程，乃相安。 戍兵萬四千，更代時皆赴廈門，由提督點驗，遠者千里，改由各提鎮分驗，兵困以蘇。 台灣產米，漳、泉數郡仰給商運，江、浙、天津民無蓋藏，米貴輒生亂，於海口稽米船出數，酌豐歉為限制，常留有餘。 疏請漳浦明儒黃道周從祀文廟，下廷臣議行。 侯官謝金鑾、德化鄭兼才皆以學行著，素所敬禮，歿而舉祀鄉賢。 又旌表義烈，以振風俗。
In the second year of Daoguang he had an audience with the throne; the Xuanzong Emperor praised his scrupulous honesty, spoke words of warm encouragement, and appointed him governor-general of Fujian and Zhejiang. He enforced military discipline strictly and required every garrison and outpost to drill regularly. The Zhejiang provincial commander Shen Tianhua had been lax and indulgent; Shen-dian impeached him and had him dismissed. He pressed the navy to hunt pirates, and many were taken. The four upstream prefectures were mountainous; migrants rented hills for workshops while vagrants gathered in bands. He sent troops to sweep the hills and executed the leaders. Langqi Island fell under Min'an jurisdiction; its inhabitants had long profited from smuggling. He arrested the ringleaders, stationed the navy there, built batteries, and turned the island into the capital's seaward gate. Taiwan had long been turbulent and often required major expeditions; Shen-dian was especially anxious about it and appointed only capable officials to govern there. Yang Liangbin, a troublemaker of Fengshan, stirred up a popular rising. Shen-dian ordered Circuit Intendant Kong Zhaoqian and Prefect Kong Chuanyi to suppress it; within a month order was restored without a single soldier crossing the strait. When Hualien was first brought under civil administration, the Board of Revenue had proposed a heavy tax schedule; he memorialized successfully for its reduction. Locals felled timber in the hills each year for the transport bureau's shipbuilding needs; head craftsmen extorted so harshly that a riot broke out. He executed the ringleaders, revised the timber-cutting regulations, and calm returned. Fourteen thousand garrison troops had to travel to Xiamen for inspection by the provincial commander at every rotation—some a thousand li away. He had each regional commander inspect his own troops instead, greatly easing their burden. Taiwan's rice fed several Zhangzhou and Quanzhou prefectures by merchant shipping, while in Jiangsu, Zhejiang, and Tianjin the poor had no reserves and dearth often sparked unrest. He tallied rice exports at the ports, set quotas according to harvests, and always kept a margin in reserve. He petitioned to have the Ming scholar Huang Daozhou of Zhangpu admitted to the Confucian temple; the court deliberated and approved. Xie Jinluan of Houguan and Zheng Jiancai of Dehua were scholars of learning and integrity whom he had long revered; after their deaths he had them enshrined in the local worthies' temple. He also awarded honors to the righteous and the martyred in order to strengthen public morals.
7
五年，調雲貴總督。 銅礦、鹽務積疲，疏陳變通整頓之法。 以邊防莫便於屯田，方考訪形勢利便，未及議行而疾作。 病中拜疏劾貪黷不職者數十人。 未幾，遂卒。 代者急遞追回原疏，滇人惜之。 遺疏上，優詔賜卹，贈太子少保，諡文恪，祀名宦、鄉賢祠。
In the fifth year he was transferred to governor-general of Yunnan and Guizhou. The copper mines and salt monopoly had long been in decline; he memorialized with proposals for comprehensive reform. Convinced that garrison farming was the best means of border defense, he was surveying suitable terrain when illness struck before his plans could be carried out. From his sickbed he submitted a memorial impeaching several dozen corrupt and negligent officials. Shortly afterward he died. His successor had the memorial recalled by express courier; the people of Yunnan deeply regretted the loss. When his final memorial reached the throne, the court granted generous mourning honors, posthumously appointed him Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent, gave him the posthumous name Wenge, and enshrined him in the temples of eminent officials and local worthies.
8
慎畛服膺儒先，凡有益身心可致用者，皆身體力行。 好善嫉惡，體恤屬僚，訓懇切，如師之於弟子。 所至於文武官吏，常能識別其才否，人亦樂為之用。 所著奏議、從征錄、載年錄、讀書日記、惜日筆記等書及詩文集凡數十卷。
Shen-dian devoted himself to the teachings of the Confucian sages and personally practiced whatever could nourish character and serve practical ends. He loved virtue and hated vice, treated his subordinates with sympathy, and instructed them earnestly, like a teacher with his pupils. Wherever he served he could usually tell which civil and military officials had talent, and men were glad to work for him. His writings included memorials, campaign records, chronological memoirs, reading diaries, notes on making good use of time, and collected poetry and prose—several dozen juan in all.
9
盧坤，字厚山，順天涿州人。 嘉慶四年進士，選庶吉士，散館授兵部主事，洊遷郎中。 扈隨木蘭，校射，賜花翎。 十八年，出為湖南糧儲道，丁本生父母憂，服闋，歷廣東惠潮嘉道、山東兗沂曹濟道、湖北按察使、甘肅布政使。 道光元年，護理陝西巡撫。 二年，擢廣東巡撫，未之任，調陝西。 議者謂南山老林易藪姦，不宜開墾。 坤歷陳漢、蜀、唐、宋史事，及漢李翕郙閣頌，以徵墾治之利； 專任嚴如熤，假以便宜，墾務大興。 勘修南山各屬城工，漢江堤岸，築壩濬淤，審度形勢，移駐文武，增改官制。 又修復咸寧、長安、涇陽、盩厔、岐山、寶雞、華州、榆林河渠水利，籌補榆林、綏德兩屬常平倉穀，勸民捐建社倉。 疏陳：「察吏之要，不獨親民，官貪廉為民身家所繫，其勤​​惰、明昧、寬嚴，皆關民生休戚。 」宣宗深韙之。 五年，以母憂去官。
Lu Kun, whose courtesy name was Houshan, came from Zhuozhou in Shuntian. He passed the jinshi examination in the fourth year of Jiaqing, entered the Hanlin Academy, and on leaving was appointed a secretary in the Ministry of War, rising eventually to director. He accompanied the emperor on the Mulan hunt, distinguished himself in the archery contest, and was awarded the peacock feather. In the eighteenth year he was posted as Hunan grain intendant; after mourning his birth parents he served successively as intendant of the Huizhou-Chaozhou-Jiazhou circuit in Guangdong, the Yanzhou-Yizhou-Caozhou-Jining circuit in Shandong, provincial judge of Hubei, and provincial treasurer of Gansu. In the first year of Daoguang he served as acting governor of Shaanxi. In the second year he was promoted to governor of Guangdong but never took up the post, being transferred instead to Shaanxi. Critics argued that the old forests of the southern mountains were too good a refuge for bandits and should not be opened to cultivation. Kun cited precedents from the histories of Han, Shu, Tang, and Song, and Li Xi's Han inscription on the Postal Pavilion, to show the benefits of land reclamation; he put Yan Ruyi wholly in charge with discretionary powers, and reclamation advanced on a large scale. He surveyed and repaired city defenses throughout the southern mountains, strengthened Han River dikes, built dams and dredged silt, redeployed civil and military officials according to terrain, and revised the administrative structure. He also restored irrigation in Xianning, Chang'an, Jingyang, Zhouzhi, Qishan, Baoji, Huazhou, and Yulin, replenished the ever-normal granaries of Yulin and Suide, and encouraged the people to build community granaries by donation. He wrote: "The key to evaluating officials is not merely whether they are close to the people. Whether an official is honest or corrupt touches the people's livelihood and safety; his diligence or sloth, clarity or obtuseness, leniency or severity—all bear on the people's welfare." The Xuanzong Emperor strongly approved his view. In the fifth year he left office to mourn his mother.
10
六年，回疆用兵，特起駐肅州，偕總督鄂山治轉餉。 以托古遜為運糧首站，自烏魯木齊至阿克蘇，置三十二站，大兵五萬餘，日需糧五百石，每站備駝五百有奇，由山西、陝西採購； 又蒙古阿拉善部進駝千，烏里雅蘇台調撥官駝四千。 疏請軍需從寬籌備； 兵丁量增口糧； 給皮衣皮帽，以御寒； 出口駝馬芻秣； 時給買補缺額營馬，預備續調； 監造軍械務期堅實； 撥運陝省制錢，平市價； 添設台站夫馬； 僱用車輛，定例價； 招募護台民丁； 後路糧台亦添兵守護：凡十一事，並如議行。 回疆平，加太子少保。 及張格爾就擒，賜頭品頂戴。 服闋，授山東巡撫，調山西。 八年，裁撤肅州軍局。 始抵任，尋調廣東巡撫。
In the sixth year, when the campaign in the Muslim frontier began, he was specially recalled to garrison at Suzhou and, together with Governor-General Eshan, managed supply transport. He made Turgeshun the first supply depot; from Urumqi to Aksu he established thirty-two relay stations. The main army numbered more than fifty thousand men and consumed five hundred shi of grain a day; each station kept more than five hundred camels, procured from Shanxi and Shaanxi; the Alashan Mongols contributed another thousand camels, and four thousand government camels were allocated from Uliastai. He memorialized asking that military supplies be generously provided; soldiers' rations be increased as needed; fur coats and hats be issued against the cold; fodder for camels and horses on the march; replacement cavalry horses be purchased in good time for further deployments; arms manufacture be supervised to ensure durability; Shaanxi copper cash be shipped in to stabilize prices; additional relay station personnel and horses; hired carts at fixed standard rates; civilian guards for the relay stations; and troops to guard rear supply depots—all eleven proposals were approved and carried out. When the Muslim frontier was pacified he was promoted to Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent. When Jahangir Khoja was captured he was awarded the first-rank hat button. After his mourning period he was appointed governor of Shandong, then transferred to Shanxi. In the eighth year the Suzhou military supply bureau was abolished. He had barely assumed office when he was transferred to governor of Guangdong.
11
十年，又調江蘇，未至，擢湖廣總督。 兩湖鹺務，狃於封輪之例，道光初議散輪，七年復因加價，仍改封輪，引滯商疲。 坤至，疏請實行散輪，建鹽倉於漢岸，俾商船源源攬運。 尋量減售價，以銷楚岸積鹽。 設塘角總卡，按船編號，以杜內私外私之弊。 复湖南永興粵鹽定額，以保淮綱。 湖北水災，請免米稅，借帑十萬兩，購川米平糶。 疏調前兩淮鹽運使王鳳生綜理水利，擇要疏濬河道，修築堤堰，皆以次舉行。
In the tenth year he was transferred to Jiangsu but before he could take up the post was promoted to governor-general of Huguang. Huguang's salt trade was locked into the sealed-rotation system; early in Daoguang open rotation had been debated, but in the seventh year price increases brought sealed rotation back, stalling trade and exhausting merchants. When Kun arrived he petitioned for open rotation, built salt warehouses on the Han River bank, and allowed merchant boats to carry salt in a steady stream. He then reduced sale prices to clear the backlog of salt along the Chu River. He set up a main checkpoint at Tangjiao, numbered every boat, and closed off both official and unofficial smuggling. He restored the fixed quota for Guangdong salt at Yongxing in Hunan to protect the Huai monopoly zone. When floods struck Hubei he petitioned to exempt the rice tax, borrowed one hundred thousand taels from the treasury, bought Sichuan rice, and sold it at fair prices to stabilize the market. He petitioned to appoint Wang Fengsheng, former intendant of Liang-Huai salt transport, to oversee water works, dredging key waterways and building dikes and dams in turn.
12
十二年，湖南江華瑤趙金龍作亂，粵瑤應之，湖南提督海凌阿及副將、游擊等皆戰歿，坤親往督師，密陳湖北提督羅思舉能辦賊。 時桂陽、常德諸瑤蜂起應賊，常德水師、荊州駐防兵皆不習山戰，坤至，悉罷之，改調鎮筸苗疆兵，分屯要隘，堅壁清野，與賊相持。 俟兩湖兵大集，貴州提督餘步雲、雲南副將曾勝亦率軍至，乘雷雨襲擊洋泉街。 羅思舉督諸將晝夜環攻，斃賊數千，破其巢，擒金龍子女及頭目數百人。 金龍乘間逸，為亂軍所殲，獲其屍及劍印木偶諸物。 捷聞，賜雙眼花翎，世襲一等輕車都尉。 尚書禧恩、將軍瑚松額方奉命視師，未至，賊已平。 粵瑤趙青仔糾眾數千入楚界，聲言為金龍復仇，連敗之於濠江、銀江，擒青仔磔於市。 廣東連山黃瓜寨瑤猶猖獗，兩廣總督李鴻賓剿治不力，以罪逮，調坤代之。 偕禧恩等先後往督諸將進剿，瑤疆悉平。 合疏陳兩省善後事宜，改移文武官制駐所，並允行。
In the twelfth year Zhao Jinlong of the Jianghua Yao in Hunan rebelled, and Yao from Guangdong rose in support. Hunan provincial commander Hai Ling'a, along with brigade generals, regiment commanders, and others, all fell in battle. Kun went in person to take command and secretly recommended Hubei provincial commander Luo Siju as the man to suppress the rebels. Yao in Guiyang, Changde, and elsewhere rose in swarms to join the rebels. The Changde naval force and the Jingzhou garrison were ill suited to mountain warfare; when Kun arrived he dismissed them all, brought up Miao frontier troops from Zhenqian, posted them at key passes, fortified camps and cleared the countryside, and settled into a stalemate with the rebels. Once troops from both Hu provinces had massed, Guizhou provincial commander Yu Buxun and Yunnan brigade general Ceng Sheng arrived with their armies and, exploiting a thunderstorm, struck Yangquan Street by surprise. Luo Siju directed the generals in round-the-clock encirclement assaults, killing several thousand rebels, destroying their stronghold, and capturing Jinlong's children along with several hundred chieftains. Jinlong slipped away in the confusion but was cut down by the pursuing troops; his body was recovered together with his sword, seal, wooden idols, and other paraphernalia. When word of the victory arrived he was awarded the double-eyed peacock feather and a hereditary rank as first-class Commandant of Light Chariots. Minister Xien and General Husonge had just been dispatched to take command of the campaign, but before they arrived the rebels had already been suppressed. Zhao Qingzai of the Guangdong Yao rallied several thousand followers into Hunan, proclaiming vengeance for Jinlong; they were repeatedly defeated at Haojiang and Yinjiang, and Qingzai was captured and publicly dismembered. The Yao at Huangguazhai in Lianshan, Guangdong, remained unchecked; Governor-General Li Hongbin of the Two Guangs was arrested for failing to suppress them, and Kun was transferred to take his place. Together with Xien and others he went in turn to direct the generals in the advance, and the Yao regions were fully pacified. They jointly memorialized on post-pacification measures for the two provinces, including the relocation of civil and military posts and garrisons, and all proposals were approved.
13
十三年，越南盜陳加海結邊地遊民嘯聚狗頭山，潛入內洋，遣水師擊沉八船，擒加海誅之。 尋越南內訌，慎固邊防，拒其請兵，詔嘉得大體。
In the thirteenth year the Vietnamese pirate Chen Jiahai rallied frontier vagrants at Goutou Mountain, slipped into coastal waters, and was pursued by the navy, which sank eight of his ships, captured him, and executed him. When Vietnam soon fell into internal strife, he carefully reinforced the border and refused its request for military aid; the throne praised his grasp of the larger interest.
14
英吉利兵船擅入海口，要乞推廣通商，坤依故事停其貿易。 領事律勞卑挾二船入虎門，砲擊不退，且以砲拒，進泊黃埔。 坤設方略扼其歸路，斷其接濟，集水陸師臨以兵威，律勞卑窮蹙，引罪求去。 澳門洋商代請命，坤持之良久，乃驅之出口。 疏聞，詔嘉獎，先奪宮銜、花翎並復之。 於是嚴海防，勤訓練，自南山至大虎分三段，與沙角、大角相聯絡。 省河中流沙地增建砲台，以資保障，夷情斂懾。 坤久任封圻，所皆有名績，宣宗深倚之。 十五年，卒，贈太子太師、兵部尚書，從優恤，諡文肅。 子端黼，襲世職。
British warships entered the estuary without permission and demanded broader trade rights; Kun, following established practice, suspended their commerce. Consul Napier forced his way into Bocca Tigris with two ships; when fired upon he refused to withdraw, returned fire, and pressed on to anchor at Whampoa. Kun devised a strategy to block Napier's retreat, cut off his supplies, and mass naval and land forces to overawe him; cornered, Napier admitted fault and asked to withdraw. Macao foreign merchants pleaded on Napier's behalf, but Kun held his ground for a long while before driving him out of the port. When the memorial reached the throne, an edict commended and rewarded him; the court rank and peacock feather that had first been stripped were then restored. He then tightened coastal defenses and intensified training, dividing the coast from Nanshan to Dahu into three sections linked with Shajiao and Dajiao. He added fortifications on sandbanks in the provincial river for additional protection, and foreign behavior became noticeably subdued. Kun had long served in frontier governorships and everywhere left a record of distinction; the Xuanzong Emperor relied on him heavily. In the fifteenth year he died. He was posthumously made Grand Preceptor of the Heir Apparent and Minister of War, granted exceptional mourning honors, and given the posthumous title Wensu. His son Duanchu inherited the hereditary rank.
15
曾勝，廣西馬平人。 以行伍從剿湖南苗匪、川、楚教匪，積功至都司。 累遷雲南參將，以計擒梟渠徐黑二及宣威小梁山匪首，為時稱。 遷維西協副將。 瑤匪趙金龍之亂，率師會剿，擢湖南永州鎮總兵，殲金龍，及擒粵瑤趙青仔，戰皆力。 尋赴廣東剿連山瑤，迭戰大拱橋、分水嶺、砲台山、火燒坪、軍僚裡、大厓衝、上坻園。 瑤平，論功最，加提督銜，賜號瑚爾察圖巴圖魯，予雲騎尉世職。 調南韶連鎮，擢廣東陸路提督。 當英吉利兵船入內河，水師提督李增堦不能阻，勝獻策，以巨船載石沉塞海口老洲岡隘道，聚草船數百橫內河，備火攻，勝率兵臨之，英領事律勞卑悚懼聽令，事乃定。 十七年，卒於官，諡勤勇。
Ceng Sheng came from Maping in Guangxi. Starting as a common soldier, he took part in suppressing Miao bandits in Hunan and sectarian rebels in Sichuan and Huguang, rising on merit to regimental commander. He rose through successive promotions to battalion commander in Yunnan, where by stratagem he captured the ringleader Xu Heier and the bandit chief of Xiaoliangshan in Xuanwei, winning wide acclaim. He was transferred to brigade general of the Weixi garrison. During the Yao rebellion led by Zhao Jinlong he commanded troops in the joint campaign, was promoted to commander of Yongzhou garrison in Hunan, destroyed Jinlong, and captured Zhao Qingzai of the Guangdong Yao, distinguishing himself in every engagement. He then went to Guangdong to suppress the Lianshan Yao, fighting successive battles at Dagongqiao, Fenshuiling, Paotaishan, Huoshaoping, Junliaoli, Dayachong, and Shangdiyuan. When the Yao were pacified his merit was judged the greatest; he received the brevet rank of provincial commander, was granted the title Hu'erchatu Batueru, and was awarded a hereditary Cloud Cavalry Captaincy. He was transferred to the Nanshaolian garrison and promoted to land provincial commander of Guangdong. When British warships entered the inner river, naval commander Li Zengjie could not stop them. Sheng proposed loading large ships with stone and sinking them to block the narrow passage at Laozhougang, assembling several hundred straw boats across the inner river for a possible fire attack, and leading troops to confront the intruders. Consul Napier, alarmed, submitted to orders, and the crisis was resolved. In the seventeenth year he died in office and was posthumously titled Qinyong.
16
陶澍，字云汀，湖南安化人。 嘉慶七年進士，選庶吉士，授編修，遷御史、給事中。 疏劾吏部重簽，河工冒濫，及外省吏治積弊。 巡中城，決滯獄八百有奇。 巡南漕，革陋規，請濬京口運河。 二十四年，出為川東道，日坐堂皇，剖決獄訟如流。 請減鹽價，私絕課增。 總督蔣攸銛薦其治行為四川第一。 歷山西按察使、安徽布政使。
Tao Shu, whose courtesy name was Yunting, came from Anhua in Hunan. He became a jinshi in the seventh year of Jiaqing, entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor, was appointed a compiler, and later served as censor and supervising secretary. He memorialized against duplicate endorsements in the Ministry of Personnel, fraud in river works, and long-standing abuses in provincial administration. On an inspection tour of the central city he cleared more than eight hundred backlogged cases. While inspecting southern grain transport he swept away abusive customs and petitioned to dredge the Jingkou transport canal. In the twenty-fourth year he was posted as intendant of the eastern Sichuan circuit, holding court daily and dispatching lawsuits with remarkable speed. He petitioned to lower salt prices, suppress smuggling, and increase tax receipts. Governor-General Jiang Youtie recommended him as the foremost administrator in Sichuan. He served successively as provincial judge of Shanxi and provincial treasurer of Anhui.
17
道光三年，就擢巡撫。 安徽庫款，五次清查，未得要領。 澍自為籓司時，鉤覈檔案，分別應劾、應償、應豁，於是三十餘年之糾轕，豁然一清。 嚴交代，禁流攤，裁捐款，至是奏定章程，俾有司釋累，得專力治民。 瀕江水災，購米十萬石，勸捐數十萬金，賑務覈實，災民賴之無失所。 治壽州城西湖、鳳臺蕉岡湖、鳳陽花源湖； 又懷遠新漲沙洲阻水，並開引河，導之入淮。 淮水所經，勸民修隄束水，保障農田。 各縣設豐備倉於鄉村，令民秋收後量力分捐，不經吏役，不減糶，不出易，不假貸，歲歉備賑，樂歲再捐，略如社倉法而去其弊。 創輯安徽通志，旌表忠孝節烈以勵風俗。
In the third year of Daoguang he was promoted directly to governor. Anhui's treasury accounts had been audited five times without anyone getting to the bottom of them. While still provincial treasurer, Shu combed through the archives, sorting cases into those requiring impeachment, repayment, or remission, and in one stroke cleared more than thirty years of tangled accounts. He enforced strict account handovers, banned shifting of assessments, and cut extralegal levies; he then memorialized to establish fixed regulations so local officials could be freed of burdens and devote themselves to governing the people. When floods struck along the river he purchased one hundred thousand shi of rice, raised several hundred thousand taels in donations, verified relief work rigorously, and the victims were spared displacement. He undertook water-control work at West Lake in Shouzhou, Jiaogang Lake in Fengtai, and Huayuan Lake in Fengyang; and at Huaiyuan, where newly risen sandbars were blocking the flow, he opened diversion channels to lead the water into the Huai. Along the Huai he encouraged the people to repair dikes, confine the water, and protect farmland. Each county set up abundance reserve granaries in the countryside, requiring villagers after the autumn harvest to contribute according to their means without going through clerks and runners, without reducing grain sales, without exchange or loans; in lean years the stores supplied relief, in good years contributions were renewed—much like the community granary system, but with its abuses stripped away. He initiated compilation of the Anhui gazetteer and honored the loyal, filial, chaste, and martyred to strengthen public morals.
18
五年，調江蘇。 先是洪澤湖決，漕運梗阻，協辦大學士英和陳海運策，而中外紛議撓之。 澍毅然以身任，奏請蘇、松、常、鎮、太五府州漕糧百六十餘萬石歸海運，親赴上海，籌僱商船，體恤商艱，群情踴躍。 六年春，開兌，至夏全抵天津，無一漂損者，驗米色率瑩潔，過河運數倍。 商船回空，載豆而南，兩次得值船餘耗米十餘萬石，發部帑收買，由漕項協濟天津、通倉之用，及調劑旗丁，尚節省銀米各十餘萬。 事竣，優詔褒美，賜花翎。 明年，遂偕總督蔣攸銛合疏陳海運章程八條，冀垂令甲，永紓漕累，格於部議，未果行。 又以紳衿包完漕米，橫索陋規，為漕務之害，奏請懲辦。 學政辛從益意不合，爭之。 澍复疏言：「陋規日增，勢必取償小民。 若預計有司不減浮收，置陋規於不問，非釜底抽薪之計。 」仍執前議，治包抗從嚴焉。
In the fifth year he was transferred to Jiangsu. Earlier the Hongze Lake had breached its banks and blocked grain transport; Grand Secretary Yinghe proposed shifting to sea transport, but conflicting opinion at court and beyond disrupted the plan. Shu resolutely took personal responsibility, petitioning that more than 1.6 million shi of tribute grain from Suzhou, Songjiang, Changzhou, Zhenjiang, and Taicang be shifted to sea transport; he went in person to Shanghai, arranged the hiring of merchant vessels, showed genuine concern for merchants' hardships, and won an enthusiastic response. Transport began in the spring of the sixth year; by summer the entire shipment had reached Tianjin without a single loss at sea, and inspection showed the grain uniformly bright and clean—several times better than river transport. On the return voyage merchant ships carried beans south; twice over they yielded more than one hundred thousand shi of surplus transport grain, which the ministry treasury purchased; the grain transport fund then helped supply Tianjin and Tongzhou granaries and adjust banner transport labor, while still saving more than one hundred thousand taels of silver and more than one hundred thousand shi of grain. When the project was completed, an edict of special commendation was issued and he was awarded the peacock feather. The following year he and Governor-General Jiang Youtie jointly memorialized eight articles of sea transport regulations, hoping to establish permanent rules that would forever ease the burden of grain transport; the Board blocked the proposal and it was never carried out. He also identified a major abuse of grain transport: gentry and licentiates who contracted to deliver tribute grain extorted illegal fees, and he petitioned for their punishment. Education intendant Xin Congyi disagreed and contested the proposal. Shu memorialized again: "These corrupt fees grow daily, and the cost will inevitably be passed on to the common people. If we assume in advance that local officials will not reduce excess levies and leave these corrupt fees untouched, that is no way to strike at the root of the problem." He held to his earlier proposal and dealt strictly with contractors who resisted.
19
江蘇頻遭水患，由太湖水洩不暢。 疏言：「太湖尾閭在吳淞江及劉河、白茆河，而以吳淞江為最要。 治吳淞以通海口為最要。 」於是以海運節省銀二十餘萬興工，擇賢任事，至八年工竣。 又以江以南運道，徒陽運河最易淤阻，而練湖為其上游，孟瀆為其旁支。 澍自巡漕時，條奏利害，至是先濬徒陽河，將以次舉劉河、白茆、練湖、孟瀆諸工。 後在總督任，與巡撫林則徐合力悉加疏濬，吳中稱為數十年之利，語詳則徐傳。
Jiangsu suffered frequent floods because Lake Tai could not drain properly. He wrote: "Lake Tai drains through the Wusong River and the Liu and Baichao rivers, but the Wusong River is the most critical. And the essential task in managing the Wusong is to open its passage to the sea." He then allocated more than two hundred thousand taels saved from sea transport to the project, put able men in charge, and by the eighth year the work was finished. He also observed that among transport routes south of the Yangzi, the Tuyang Canal was most prone to silting, with Lian Lake as its upstream source and Mengdu as a lateral branch. Shu had memorialized on the matter in detail since his grain transport inspection tour; he now began by dredging the Tuyang River and planned to proceed in turn with the Liu and Baichao rivers, Lian Lake, and Mengdu. Later, as governor-general, he joined forces with Governor Lin Zexu to dredge them all; the Wu region hailed the result as a benefit lasting decades; details are given in Lin's biography.
20
十年，以捕獲戶部私造假照要犯，加太子少保銜，署兩江總督，尋實授。 時淮鹽敗壞，商困課絀，岌岌不可終日。 澍疏陳積弊，請大刪浮費，以為補救。 議者多主改法課歸場灶，命尚書王鼎、侍郎寶興赴江南查議。 澍謂除弊即以興利，無事輕改舊制，偕鼎等合疏臚陳利害，條上十五事。 鼎等復請裁鹽政歸總督管理，報可。 澍受事，繳還鹽政養廉五千兩，裁減衙門陋規十六萬兩有奇，凡淮南之窩價，淮北之壩槓，兩淮之岸費，分別減除，歲計數百萬兩，分設內外二庫，正款貯內庫，雜項貯外庫，杜絕挪墊。 革總商以除把持，散輪規以免淹滯，禁糧船回空帶蘆鹽，及商船借官行私，令行禁止，弊肅風清。 淮北尤疲累，先借款官督商運，繼仿山東、浙江票引兼行之法，於海州所屬中正、板浦、臨興三場擇要隘設局給票，註明斤數運地，無票越境以私論。 仍留暢銷之岸，江運八州縣、湖運十一州縣，歸商運。 十二年，奏准開辦，越半歲，溢銷逾額，复推廣於江運、湖運各岸，減價裁費，商販爭趨，而窩商蠹吏、壩夫岸胥一旦盡失其中飽需索之利，群議沸騰。 言官摭浮言，屢事彈劾，賴宣宗鑑其忠誠，倚畀愈專。 屢請復鹽政專職，皆不許，澍益感奮，力排眾議，毅然持之，卒獲成效。 道光元年至十年，淮南行六綱，淮北僅行三綱。 澍承極弊之後，自十一年至十七年，淮南已完六綱有餘，淮北率一歲行兩綱之鹽，盡完從前滯欠，且割淮南懸引，兩淮共完正雜銀二千六百四十餘萬兩，庫貯實存三百餘萬兩。 兩屆京察，並被褒獎優敘。 晚年將推淮北之法於淮南，已病風痺，未竟其施，然天下皆知票鹽減價敵私，為正本清源之計。 後咸豐中乃卒行之。 十九年，卒。 遺疏上，優詔軫惜，稱其「實心任事，不避嫌怨」，晉贈太子太保，依尚書例賜卹，賜其子桄主事，諡文毅。 祝名宦祠，於海州建專祠。
In the tenth year, for capturing a major forger of false Ministry of Revenue credentials, he was made Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent, served as acting governor-general of the Two Jiangs, and soon received full appointment. At the time the Huai salt administration was in ruins, merchants were exhausted, tax receipts fell short, and the system seemed on the verge of collapse. Shu memorialized on the accumulated abuses and petitioned for deep cuts in superfluous expenses as a remedy. Most advocates favored changing the law so that tax collection would rest with the salt fields and furnaces; Minister Wang Ding and Vice Minister Bao Xing were dispatched to Jiangnan to investigate. Shu argued that eliminating abuses was itself a way to create profit and that the old system need not be changed lightly; together with Wang Ding and the others he submitted a detailed joint memorial setting forth fifteen points on advantages and harms. Wang Ding and the others further proposed abolishing the salt intendant and placing management under the governor-general; the proposal was approved. When Shu took charge he returned the salt intendant's five-thousand-tael integrity stipend, cut more than one hundred sixty thousand taels in yamen corrupt fees, and separately reduced nest prices in Huainan, barrier exactions in Huaibei, and shore fees in both Huai regions—saving several million taels a year; he established separate inner and outer treasuries, keeping regular funds in the inner treasury and miscellaneous items in the outer, thereby blocking diversion and padding. He abolished chief merchants to break monopolistic control, opened the rotation system to end stagnation, and forbade grain boats returning empty from carrying smuggled reed salt and merchant boats from using official cover for private trade; his orders were enforced, abuses were swept away, and the administration was restored to order. Huaibei was especially strained; he first borrowed funds for government-supervised merchant transport, then adopted the combined ticket system used in Shandong and Zhejiang, establishing bureaus at strategic passes in the three salt fields of Zhongzheng, Banpu, and Linxing under Haizhou to issue tickets noting weight and destination; anyone crossing borders without a ticket was treated as a smuggler. He retained outlets where sales were brisk, while eight prefectures and counties on river transport and eleven on lake transport were returned to merchant carriage. In the twelfth year the plan was approved and launched; within half a year sales exceeded quota, and the system was extended to all river and lake transport outlets with price cuts and fee reductions. Merchants flocked to participate, while nest merchants, corrupt officials, barrier laborers, and shore clerks suddenly lost their illicit profits and extortion rackets, and opposition erupted everywhere. Censor-officials seized on rumor and repeatedly impeached him, but the Xuanzong Emperor recognized his loyalty and relied on him all the more exclusively. Repeated requests to restore the dedicated salt intendant were all denied; Shu, all the more energized, forcefully rejected opposition, held resolutely to his course, and ultimately achieved results. From the first to the tenth year of Daoguang, Huainan completed six transport cycles while Huaibei completed only three. Taking charge after the system had reached its lowest point, Shu from the eleventh to the seventeenth year completed more than six cycles in Huainan; Huaibei averaged two cycles' worth of salt in a single year and cleared all previous arrears; applying Huainan's suspended quotas, both Huai regions together remitted more than 26.4 million taels in regular and miscellaneous revenue, with more than three million taels actually remaining in the treasury. In two rounds of capital evaluation he received both commendation and preferential promotion. In his later years he planned to extend the Huaibei method to Huainan, but already stricken with paralysis he did not live to finish; yet everyone recognized that ticket salt sold at reduced prices to undercut smuggling was a plan to attack the problem at its root. It was finally fully implemented later, in the Xianfeng reign. In the nineteenth year he died. When his final memorial reached the throne, an edict of special mourning praised him as "sincere in office and unafraid of enmity"; he was posthumously made Senior Guardian of the Heir Apparent, granted mourning honors according to ministerial precedent, his son Guang was given a secretary post, and he received the posthumous title Wenyi. He was enshrined in the temple of distinguished officials, and a dedicated shrine was built for him at Haizhou.
21
澍見義勇為，胸無城府。 用人能盡其長，所拔取多至方面節鉞有名。 在江南治河、治漕、治鹽，並賴王鳳生、俞德源、姚瑩、黃冕諸人之力。 左宗棠、胡林翼皆識之未遇，結為婚姻，後俱為名臣。 所著奏議、詩文集、蜀輶日記、陶桓公年譜、​​陶淵明詩輯註並行世。
Shu acted boldly whenever he saw justice to be done and harbored no hidden designs. In appointing men he drew fully on their strengths, and many of those he promoted rose to distinguished frontier governorships and military commands. In Jiangnan his work on rivers, grain transport, and salt all depended on Wang Fengsheng, Yu Deyuan, Yao Ying, Huang Mian, and others. Zuo Zongtang and Hu Linyi both knew him before he had risen to prominence and formed marriage ties with his family; both later became celebrated statesmen. His writings—memorials, collected poetry and prose, Diary of a Sichuan Journey, Chronological Biography of Lord Tao, and Collected Annotations on Tao Yuanming's Poetry—all remain in circulation.
22
論曰：趙慎畛學有本源，察吏治民，嚴而能恕，所至政無不舉。 盧坤治回疆軍需，平湖南瑤，馭廣東夷商，皆有殊績。 陶澍治水利、漕運、鹽政，垂百年之利，為屏為翰，庶無媿焉。 道光中年後，海內多事，諸臣並已徂謝，遂無以紓朝廷南顧之憂。 人之雲亡，邦國殄瘁，其信然哉！
The historian writes: Zhao Shen-dian's learning was grounded in solid foundations; in inspecting officials and governing the people he was strict yet fair; everywhere he served, his policies were fully implemented. Lu Kun won outstanding achievements in managing military supplies on the Muslim frontier, pacifying the Hunan Yao, and handling foreign merchants in Guangdong. Tao Shu's reforms in water control, grain transport, and salt administration brought benefits that would last a century; as a bulwark and pillar of the state, he had little to be ashamed of. After the mid-Daoguang years troubles multiplied across the empire; these ministers had all died, and no one remained to ease the court's anxieties over the south. When men such as these are gone, the state itself withers—how painfully true that is!
