徐-{乾}-學，字原一，江南崑山人。 幼慧，八歳能文。 康熙九年，一甲三名進士，授編修。 十一年，副蔡啓僔主順天鄕試，拔韓菼-{於}-遺巻中，明年魁天下，文體一變。 坐副榜未取漢軍巻，與啓僔-{並}-鐫秩調用。 尋-{復}-故官，遷左贊善，充日講起居注官。 丁母憂歸，-{乾}-學父先卒，哀毀三年，喪葬一以禮； 及母卒，如之。 爲讀禮通考百二十巻，博採衆説，剖析其義。 服闋，起故官。 充明史總裁官，累遷侍講學士。
Xu Qianxue, whose style name was Yuanyi, came from Kunshan in Jiangnan. He was precocious as a boy and could write essays by the age of eight. In 1670 he took third place among the top three jinshi and was made a Hanlin compiler. In 1672 he assisted Cai Qipu in supervising the Shuntian provincial exams and picked Han Tan from the discarded papers; Han came in first nationwide the following year, and examination prose was transformed from that point on. He and Qipu were both stripped of rank and reassigned for having failed to pass any Han Military Banner candidates on the supplementary list. He soon regained his former office, was made Left Assistant to the Heir Apparent, and served as Daily Lecturer and Recorder of the Emperor's Actions. He went home to observe mourning for his mother; his father had already died, and for three years he mourned with extreme grief and conducted the funeral wholly by the rites; when his mother died he did likewise. He wrote the Comprehensive Studies on the Reading of Ritual in 120 juan, drawing widely on scholarly opinions and parsing their significance. After the mourning period he returned to his former post. He was appointed chief compiler of the History of the Ming and rose step by step to Reader-in-Waiting.
3
二十三年，-{乾}-學弟元文以左都-{御}-史降調，其子樹聲與-{乾}-學子樹屛-{並}-舉順天鄕試。 上以是科取中南皿巻皆江、浙人，而湖廣、江西、福建無一與者，下九卿科道磨勘。 樹屛等坐斥舉人。 是年冬，-{乾}-學進詹事。 二十四年，召試翰詹諸臣，擢-{乾}-學第一，與侍讀韓菼、編修孫-{岳}-頒、侍講歸允肅、編修喬萊等四人-{並}-降敕褒獎賞賚。 尋直南書房，擢内閣學士，充大淸會典、一統-{志}-副總裁，教習庶吉士。 時戸部郎中色楞額往福建稽察鼓鑄，請禁用明代舊錢，尚書科爾坤、-{餘}-國柱等議如所請。 -{乾}-學言：「自古皆新舊兼行，以從民便。 若設厲禁，恐滋紛擾。 」因考自漢至明故事，爲議以獻。 上然之，事遂寢。
In 1684 his younger brother Xu Yuanwen was demoted from Left Censor-in-Chief, while Yuanwen's son Shusheng and Qianxue's son Shuping both passed the Shuntian provincial exams. The emperor found that every candidate admitted from the southern rolls that year came from Jiangsu or Zhejiang, with none from Hunan-Guangdong, Jiangxi, or Fujian, and ordered a full review by the Nine Ministers and the censorate. Shuping and the others were stripped of their status as licentiates. That winter he was made Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent. In 1685 the emperor tested the Hanlin and Grand Secretariat corps and ranked Qianxue first; he and Han Tan, Sun Yuebin, Gui Yunsu, Qiao Lai, and three others were all rewarded by imperial edict. He was soon assigned to the Southern Study, made a Grand Secretariat academician, named associate chief compiler of the Great Qing Statutes and the Unified Gazetteer, and put in charge of instructing junior compilers. At that time Selengge of the Ministry of Revenue was inspecting minting in Fujian and asked to ban Ming-era coin; Ministers Korhün and Yu Guozhu endorsed his proposal. Qianxue argued: "Throughout history old and new currency have circulated side by side for public convenience. Harsh prohibition would only breed unrest." He researched precedents from the Han through the Ming and submitted a memorial setting out his view. The emperor agreed, and the proposal was shelved.
4
詔採購遺書，-{乾}-學以宋、元經解、李燾續通-{鑑}-長編及唐開元禮，或繕寫，或仍古本，綜其體要，條列奏進，上稱善。 時-{乾}-學與學士張英日侍左右，凡著作之任，皆以屬之。 學士例推巡撫，​​上以二人學問淹通，宜侍從，特諭吏部，遇巡撫缺勿預推。 未-{幾}-，遷禮部侍郎，直講經筵。 朝鮮使臣鄭載-{嵩}-訴其國王受枉，語悖妄。 -{乾}-學謂恐長外籓跋扈，劾其使臣失辭不敬，宜責以大義。 上見疎，獎，謂有關國體。 已而王上疎謝罪。 二十六年，遷左都-{御}-史，擢刑部尚書。 二十七年，典會試。
When the court ordered the purchase of rare books, Qianxue submitted Song and Yuan classical commentaries, Li Tao's Continuation of the Comprehensive Mirror, and the Tang Kaiyuan Rites, some freshly copied and some in old editions, with a summary of each work's main points; the emperor commended the selection. Qianxue and Academician Zhang Ying then attended the emperor daily, and every literary commission was given to them. Academicians were usually put forward as provincial governors, but the emperor, judging both men's scholarship too valuable to lose from court, told the Ministry of Personnel not to nominate them for such posts. Soon afterward he was made Vice Minister of Rites and lectured at the Classics Colloquium. The Korean envoy Zheng Zaisong petitioned that his king had been treated unjustly, speaking in insolent and extravagant terms. Qianxue warned that such language would encourage tributary states to grow arrogant, impeached the envoy for disrespectful speech, and urged that he be lectured on proper relations with the throne. The emperor read the memorial, praised it, and said it touched the honor of the empire. The Korean king soon submitted a memorial of apology. In 1687 he became Left Censor-in-Chief and was promoted to Minister of Justice. In 1688 he served as chief examiner for the metropolitan civil service examination.
5
初，明珠當國，勢張甚，其黨布中外，-{乾}-學不能立異同。 至是，明珠漸失帝眷，而-{乾}-學驟拜左都-{御}-史，即劾罷江西巡撫安世鼎，諷諸-{御}-史風聞言事，-{臺}-諫多所彈劾，不避權貴。 明珠竟罷相，衆皆謂-{乾}-學主之。 時有南、北黨之目，互相抨撃。 尚書科爾坤、佛倫，明珠黨也，-{乾}-學遇會議會推，輒與齟。 總河靳輔奏下河屯田，下九卿會議，-{乾}-學偕尚書張玉書言屯田所佔民地應歸舊業，科爾坤、佛倫勿從。 -{御}-史陸祖修因劾科爾坤等偏袒河臣，不顧公議，-{御}-史郭琇亦劾輔興屯累民，詔罷輔任。 湖廣巡撫張汧亦明珠私人，先是命色楞額往讞上荊南道祖澤深婪贓各款，-{並}-察汧有無穢跡，色楞額悉爲庇隱。 -{御}-史陳紫芝劾汧貪黷，命副都-{御}-史開音布會巡撫-{於}-成龍、馬齊覆訊，汧、澤深事倶實，-{復}-得澤深交結大學士-{餘}-國柱爲囑色楞額徇庇及汧遣人赴京行賄狀，下法司嚴議。 時國柱已爲琇劾罷，法司請檄追質訊，-{並}-詰汧行賄何人，汧指-{乾}-學。 上聞，命免國柱質訊，戒勿株連。 -{於}-是但論汧、澤深、色楞額如律，事遂寢。 -{乾}-學尋乞罷，疎言：「臣蒙特達之知，感激矢報，苞苴餽遺，一切禁絶。 前任湖北巡撫張汧橫肆汙衊，縁臣爲憲長，拒其幣問，是以銜憾誣攀。 非聖明在上，是非-{幾}-至混淆。 臣備位卿僚，乃爲貪吏誣構，皇上覆載之仁，不加譴責，臣-{復}-何顏出入禁廷，有玷淸班？ 伏冀聖慈放歸田裡。 」詔許以原官解任，仍領修書總裁事。
Earlier, while Songgotu dominated the government, his faction was everywhere and Qianxue could not afford to oppose him openly. By then Songgotu was falling from favor, and Qianxue, suddenly made Left Censor-in-Chief, at once impeached Jiangxi Governor An Shiding, urged the censors to speak on rumor and report, and the surveillance offices brought down many charges without sparing the powerful. Songgotu was finally removed from office, and the court widely credited Qianxue. The court then spoke of Southern and Northern factions and their mutual attacks. Ministers Korhün and Folun belonged to Songgotu's party, and Qianxue invariably clashed with them in joint conferences and nomination meetings. Jin Fu, Director-General of Rivers, proposed garrison farming on the lower Yellow River; at the Nine Ministers' conference Qianxue and Zhang Yushu argued that civilian land taken for the farms should be restored, but Korhün and Folun dissented. Censor Lu Zuxiu charged Korhün and his allies with favoring the river chief against public opinion; Censor Guo Xiu charged Jin Fu with garrison farming that burdened the people, and the emperor dismissed Jin Fu. Huguang Governor Zhang Qian was another of Songgotu's clients; Selengge had earlier been sent to try Circuit Intendant Zu Zeshen on charges of corruption and to investigate Zhang Qian as well, but Selengge covered everything up. Censor Chen Zizhi impeached Zhang Qian for corruption; Vice Censor-in-Chief Kaiyinbu joined Governors Yu Chenglong and Ma Qi in a reinvestigation that confirmed the charges against Zhang and Zu, and further showed that Zu had enlisted Grand Secretary Yu Guozhu to protect him through Selengge and that Zhang had sent agents to bribe officials in the capital; the judiciary was ordered to deliberate severely. Yu Guozhu had already been impeached out of office by Guo Xiu; the judiciary asked to summon him for questioning and pressed Zhang Qian on whom he had bribed; Zhang named Qianxue. The emperor heard of it, exempted Yu Guozhu from interrogation, and warned against widening the net. Only Zhang Qian, Zu Zeshen, and Selengge were then punished under the law, and the affair ended there. Qianxue soon asked to resign, writing: "Your servant has received extraordinary favor and is resolved to repay it; I have refused every gift and bribe offered to me. Former Huguang Governor Zhang Qian has slandered me outrageously because, as chief censor, I refused his gifts and visits, and he now bears a grudge and falsely implicates me. Were it not for Your Majesty's clarity, truth and falsehood would nearly have been confused. I hold ministerial rank yet am framed by a corrupt official; Your Majesty's mercy has spared me punishment, yet how can I still walk the inner court without disgracing the pure service? I beg Your Majesty's compassion to let me return home." The emperor allowed him to step down while keeping his rank and still directing the book-compilation projects.
6
二十八年，元文拜大學士，-{乾}-學子樹-{穀}-考選-{御}-史。 副都-{御}-史許三禮劾-{乾}-學：「律身不嚴，爲張汧所引。 皇上寬仁，不加譴責，即宜引咎自退，乞命歸-{里}-。 又-{復}-優柔-{系}-戀，潛住長安。 乘修史爲名，出入禁廷，與髙士奇相爲表裡。 物議沸騰，招搖納賄。 其子樹-{穀}-不遵成例，朦朧考選-{御}-史，明有所恃。 獨其弟秉義文行兼優，原任禮部尚書熊賜履理學醇儒，乞立即召用，以佐盛治。 -{乾}-學當逐出史館，樹-{穀}-應調部屬，以遵成例。 」詔-{乾}-學-{復}-奏，-{乾}-學疎辨，乞罷斥歸田，-{並}-免樹-{穀}-職。 疎皆下部議，坐三禮所劾無實，應鐫秩調用。 三禮益恚，复列款訐-{乾}-學贓罪，帝嚴斥之，免降調，仍留任。
In 1689 Yuanwen became Grand Secretary, and Qianxue's son Shugu was selected as a censor. Vice Censor-in-Chief Xu Sanli impeached Qianxue: "He has not kept himself in strict order and was named by Zhang Qian. The emperor has been merciful and has not punished him; he ought to accept blame and withdraw, begging leave to go home. Instead he lingers in indecision and secretly remains in the capital. Under cover of historical compilation he comes and goes within the palace and works hand in glove with Gao Shiqi. Public outrage is widespread, and he is accused of soliciting bribes. His son Shugu was irregularly passed in the censor selection, plainly counting on his father's protection. By contrast his younger brother Bingyi is outstanding in learning and conduct, and former Minister of Rites Xiong Cilü is a true scholar of Neo-Confucian principle; I beg that both be recalled at once to assist the flourishing reign. Qianxue should be expelled from the Historiography Office and Shugu transferred to a regular ministry post in accordance with precedent." The emperor ordered Qianxue to respond; Qianxue defended himself in a memorial, asked to be dismissed and sent home, and requested that Shugu be removed from office. Both memorials went to the ministries for review, which found Sanli's charges unsubstantiated and recommended demotion and transfer. Sanli, still angrier, renewed his charges of corruption against Qianxue; the emperor sharply rebuked him, set aside the recommended demotion, and kept Qianxue in office.
7
是年冬，-{乾}-學复上疎言：「臣年六十，精神衰耗，祗以受恩深重，依戀徘徊。 三禮私怨逞忿，幸聖主洞燭幽隱。 臣方寸靡-{寧}-，不能复事鉛槧。 且恐因循居此，更有無端彈射。 乞恩終始矜全，俾得保其衰病之身，歸省先臣丘隴，庶身心閒暇。 原比古人書局自隨之義，屛跡編摩，少報萬一。 」乃許給假回籍，降旨褒嘉，命攜書籍即家編輯。 二十九年春，陛辭，賜-{御}-書「光焰萬丈」榜額。 未-{幾}-，兩江總督傅-{臘}-塔疎劾-{乾}-學囑-{託}-蘇州府貢監等請建生祠，复縱其子侄交結巡撫洪之傑，倚勢競利，請敕部嚴議。 語具元文傳。 上置弗問，而予元文休致。
That winter Qianxue memorialized again: "Your servant is sixty, my strength is failing, and I have lingered only because Your Majesty's favor has been so great. Xu Sanli pursued a private vendetta, but Your Majesty saw through his malice. My mind is no longer at peace, and I cannot again devote myself to scholarship. I fear that if I linger here, further groundless attacks will follow. I beg Your Majesty's mercy from first to last, that I may preserve my failing health, return to tend my parents' graves, and find some peace of body and mind. I ask to follow the ancient precedent of carrying one's editorial work home, retire from public life to compile books, and repay Your Majesty in the smallest measure." The emperor granted him leave to return home, issued a commendatory edict, and ordered him to take his books and continue editing at home. In the spring of 1690 he took leave at court and received an imperial plaque inscribed "Radiance of Ten Thousand Zhang." Soon Liangjiang Governor-General Fu Latu impeached Qianxue for arranging a living shrine through Suzhou tribute students and for letting his sons and nephews collude with Governor Hong Zhijie to profit from his influence, asking the ministries to deliberate severely. The full account appears in Xu Yuanwen's biography. The emperor ignored the charges against Qianxue but allowed Yuanwen to retire.
8
三十年，山東巡撫佛倫劾濰縣知縣硃敦厚加收火耗論死，-{並}-及-{乾}-學嘗致書前任巡撫錢鎯庇敦厚。 -{乾}-學與鎯倶坐是奪職。 自是齮齕者不已。 嘉定知縣聞在上爲縣民訐告私派，逮獄，閲二年未定讞。 按察使髙承爵窮詰，在上自承嘗餽-{乾}-學子樹敏金，至事-{發}--{後}-追還，因坐樹敏罪論絞。 會詔戒内外各官私怨報-{復}-，樹敏得贖罪。 三十三年，諭大學士舉長-{於}-文章學問超卓者，王熙、張玉書等-{薦}--{乾}-學與王鴻緒、髙士奇，命來京修書。 -{乾}-學已前卒，遺疎以所纂一統-{志}-進，詔下所司，-{復}-故官。
In 1691 Shandong Governor Folun charged Weixian Magistrate Zhu Dunhou with illegal surcharges and sought his execution, also citing Qianxue's letter to former Governor Qian Yi shielding Zhu. Qianxue and Qian Yi were both stripped of office on this charge. From then on his enemies never let up. Jiading Magistrate Wen Zaishang was denounced by local people for illegal levies, thrown into prison, and after two years still had no final verdict. Provincial surveillance commissioner Gao Chengjue pressed the case hard; Wen confessed he had once given gold to Qianxue's son Shumin and returned it only after the affair was exposed; Shumin was therefore sentenced to strangulation. An edict then warned all officials against pursuing private vendettas, and Shumin was allowed to redeem his sentence. In 1694 the emperor asked the Grand Secretaries to recommend scholars outstanding in letters and learning; Wang Xi and Zhang Yushu nominated Qianxue, Wang Hongxu, and Gao Shiqi, and they were summoned to Beijing to compile books. Qianxue had already died; his posthumous memorial presented the Unified Gazetteer he had compiled, and the court ordered his former rank restored.
9
翁叔元，字寶林，江南常熟人。 康熙十五年，一甲三名進士，授編修，館試第一。 累遷國子監祭酒，洊擢吏部侍郎，遷工部尚書。 部例，毎有工作，先計其直上之，名曰「料估」。 工完多冒破，所司不敢以聞，有十年不銷算者，大工至四十三案。 叔元蒞部甫半載，積牘一淸。 調邢部，移疾歸，卒。 叔元愛-{才}-而褊隘，何焯在門下，初甚賞之； 叔元疎劾湯斌，焯請削門生籍，叔元擯之，竟不得成名。 以是爲世所誚-{雲}-。
Weng Shuyuan, whose style name was Baolin, came from Changshu in Jiangnan. In 1676 he took third place among the top three jinshi, became a compiler, and ranked first in the Hanlin academy examination. He rose to Chancellor of the Directorate of Education, was soon made Vice Minister of Personnel, and then Minister of Works. Ministry rules required every project to have its costs estimated and reported in advance under the name "material estimate." When projects ended, officials often padded expenses beyond the estimate, and the responsible offices dared not report it; some accounts remained unsettled for ten years, with as many as forty-three major projects outstanding. Within half a year of taking office Shuyuan had cleared the entire backlog. He was transferred to the Ministry of Punishments, resigned on grounds of illness, returned home, and died. Shuyuan loved talent but was narrow-minded; He Chao studied under him and was at first highly favored; when Shuyuan impeached Tang Bin, He Chao asked to be struck from the register of disciples; Shuyuan cast him out, and he never achieved renown. For this he was ridiculed by contemporaries.
10
王鴻緒，初名度心，字季友，江南婁縣人。 康熙十二年一甲二名進士，授編修。 十四年，主順天鄕試。 充日講起居注官。 累遷翰林院侍講。 十九年，聖祖諭獎講官勤勞，加鴻緒侍讀學士銜。 時湖廣有硃方旦者，自號二眉山人。 造中説補，聚徒橫議，常至數千人。 自詡前知，與人決休咎。 巡撫董國興劾其左道惑衆，逮至京，得旨寬釋。 及呉三桂反，順承郡王勒爾錦駐師荊州，方旦以佔驗出入軍營，巡撫張朝珍亦稱爲異人。 上密戒勒爾錦勿爲所惑。 方旦乃避走江、浙，會鴻緒得其所刊中質秘書，遂以奏進，列其誣罔君上、悖逆聖道、搖惑人心三大罪。 方旦坐誅。
Wang Hongxu, originally named Duxin and styled Jiyou, came from Lou County in Jiangnan. In 1673 he took second place among the top three jinshi and was made a Hanlin compiler. In 1675 he supervised the Shuntian provincial examinations. He served as Daily Lecturer and Recorder of the Emperor's Actions. He rose step by step to Lecturer in the Hanlin Academy. In 1680 the Kangxi Emperor commended the lecturers' diligence and granted Hongxu the rank of Reader-in-Waiting. At that time Huguang had a man named Zhu Fangdan who called himself the Hermit of the Two Eyebrows. He wrote a Supplement to the Doctrine of the Mean, gathered disciples, and held freewheeling public debates that often drew thousands. He claimed prophetic foresight and told people's fortunes for them. Governor Dong Guoxing charged him with heterodox teachings that misled the people; he was brought to Beijing but released by imperial grace. When Wu Sangui rebelled, Prince Le'erjin camped at Jingzhou; Fangdan moved freely through the camps offering divinations, and Governor Zhang Chaozhen also hailed him as a prodigy. The emperor secretly warned Le'erjin not to be taken in by him. Fangdan fled to the lower Yangtze; Hongxu obtained his published Secret Book on the Mean and presented it to the throne, charging him with deceiving the sovereign, violating the sage Way, and corrupting public morals. Fangdan was put to death.
11
二十一年，轉侍讀，充明史總裁。 累擢内閣學士、戸部侍郎。 二十四年，典會試。 二十五年，疎請回籍治本生母喪，遣官賜祭。 二十六年，擢左都-{御}-史。 疎劾廣東巡撫李士楨貪劣，潮州知府林杭學嘗從呉三桂反，乃舉其淸廉。 士楨坐罷，杭學奪職。 會靈-{臺}-郎董漢臣疎陳時事，以諭教元良、愼簡宰執爲言。 -{御}-史陶式玉劾漢臣摭拾浮言，欺世盜名，請逮治。 鴻緒疎言：「欽天監靈-{臺}-郎、博士等官，不擇流品，星-{卜}-屠沽之徒，粗識數字，便得濫竽。 請敕下考試，分別去留。 」下部議行。 漢臣及博士賈文然等十五人-{並}-以詞理舛誤黜。 初，以式玉疎下九卿集議，尚書湯斌謂大臣不言，慚對漢臣。 漢臣既黜，鴻緒偕左都-{御}-史璙丹、副都-{御}-史徐元珙合疎劾斌務名鮮實，-{並}-追論江-{寧}-巡撫去任時，巧飾文告，以博虚譽。 上素重斌淸廉，置弗問。
In 1682 he became a Reader and chief compiler of the History of the Ming. He rose to Grand Secretariat academician and Vice Minister of Revenue. In 1685 he served as chief examiner for the metropolitan civil service examination. In 1686 he asked leave to return home to mourn his birth mother, and the court sent officials to perform the sacrificial rites. In 1687 he was made Left Censor-in-Chief. In 1694 he was summoned to Beijing to compile books. When Shiqi arrived, he again served in the Southern Studio. In 1697 he asked leave to return home to care for his mother; the emperor granted it and specially appointed him Grand Preceptor in the Household of the Heir Apparent. He was soon promoted to Vice Minister of Rites but did not take up the appointment because his mother was elderly. In 1703, on the southern tour, Shiqi met the emperor at Huai'an and accompanied the procession to Hangzhou. On the return journey he followed the emperor to the capital, was received in audience many times, and received lavish rewards. The emperor said to his attendants: "When I first studied, eunuchs taught me the Four Books and examination essay writing; only when I met Shiqi did I learn the true path of scholarship. At first I marveled that Shiqi could tell at a glance the period of any classical poem or essay; before long I could do the same. Shiqi won no military distinction, yet I have treated him generously because he has greatly advanced my learning." Soon afterward he was sent home, and that year he died there. The emperor deeply mourned his loss, ordered a full state funeral subsidy, and appointed his son Yu, then a Hanlin probationer, as Compiler. He was soon given the posthumous title Wenke.

論曰：儒臣直内廷，謂之「書房」，存未入關前舊名也。 上書房授諸皇子讀，尊爲師傅； 南書房以詩文書畫供-{禦}-，地分淸切，參與密勿。 -{乾}-學、士奇先-{後}-入直，鴻緒亦以文學進。 乃憑藉權勢，互結黨援，納賄營私，致屢遭彈劾，聖祖-{曲}-予保全。 -{乾}-學、鴻緒猶得以書局自隨，竟編纂之業，士奇亦以恩禮終，不其幸歟！
The historians comment: Scholar-officials on direct duty in the inner court were called the 'Study,' preserving a pre-conquest name. The Eastern Study taught the imperial princes and was held in honor as their teachers; the Southern Study provided poetry, calligraphy, and painting for the emperor; its station was exalted and intimate, and it shared in secret deliberations. Xu Qianxue and Shiqi entered direct service in turn, and Hongxu also rose through literary talent. Yet they relied on power, formed factions, took bribes, and pursued private gain, drawing repeated impeachment—and still the Kangxi Emperor mercifully spared them. Xu Qianxue and Wang Hongxu still kept their compilation duties and finished their editorial work; Shiqi too died with imperial honor—were they not fortunate indeed!
