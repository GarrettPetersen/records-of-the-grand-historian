1
剛林，瓜爾佳氏，字公茂，滿洲正黃旗人，世居蘇完。 初來歸，隸正藍旗，屬郡王阿達禮。 授筆帖式，掌繙譯漢文。 天聰八年，以漢文應試，中式舉人，命直文館。 崇德元年，授國史院大學士，與範文程、希福等參與政事。 疏請重定部院承政以下官各五等，又疏請定試士之法，皆報可。 太宗四征不庭，疆宇日闢。 剛林屢奉使軍前，宣布威德，咸稱上旨。 積功，授世職牛錄章京。 八年，阿達禮有罪，改隸正黃旗。
Gang Lin, of the Guwalgiya clan, styled Gongmao, was a Manchu of the Bordered Yellow Banner from a family that had long lived in Suwan. When he first submitted, he was assigned to the Bordered Blue Banner and served under Prince Adali. He was made a bithesi clerk and placed in charge of translating Chinese documents. In the eighth year of Tiancong (1634), he sat for the Chinese examination, passed as a provincial graduate, and was assigned to the Literary Bureau. In the first year of Chongde (1636), he was made Grand Secretary of the State History Academy and joined Fan Wencheng, Hifu, and others in managing government affairs. He submitted memorials to reclassify officials from department directors downward into five grades and to set rules for civil examinations; the court approved both. Hong Taiji led four campaigns against recalcitrant states, and the empire steadily expanded. Gang Lin was repeatedly dispatched to the front to proclaim the emperor's authority and benevolence, and all acknowledged that he spoke with the throne's intent. For his accumulated service he received the hereditary rank of niru janggin. In the eighth year, after Adali was condemned, Gang Lin was reassigned to the Bordered Yellow Banner.
2
世祖定鼎，進世職二等甲喇章京。 三年、四年，迭主會試。 考滿，進世職一等阿達哈哈番。 五年，復進三等阿思哈尼哈番，賜號「巴克什」。 六年，充太宗實錄總裁，復主會試。 疏請令六科錄諸臣章奏並批答，月送史館，備纂修國史，報可。 八年，以編撰明史闕天啟四年至七年實錄，請敕懸賞購求； 崇禎一朝事責無考，其有野史、外傳，並令訪送。 章下所司。
After the Shunzhi Emperor fixed the capital at Beijing, Gang Lin was promoted to second-rank hereditary jalan janggin. In the third and fourth years he served in turn as chief examiner of the metropolitan examinations. After a satisfactory performance review, he was promoted to first-rank hereditary adaha hafan. In the fifth year he was further raised to third-rank ashan i hafan and given the honorific title Baksi. In the sixth year he was made chief compiler of Hong Taiji's Veritable Records and again chaired the metropolitan examinations. He proposed that the Six Offices of Scrutiny copy every minister's memorial and the emperor's response each month to the History Academy for use in compiling the national history; the court agreed. In the eighth year, noting that work on the History of Ming lacked the Veritable Records for Tianqi years 4–7, he asked that an edict offer a bounty for their recovery; and because Chongzhen-era affairs had no reliable record, any unofficial histories or outside accounts were likewise to be sought out and delivered. The memorial was referred to the appropriate agencies.
3
睿親王多爾袞薨，得罪。 剛林阿附睿親王，參與移永平密謀，又與大學士祁充格擅改太祖實錄，為睿親王削匿罪愆、增載功績，坐斬，籍沒。
After Prince Regent Dorgon died, he was posthumously condemned. Gang Lin had curried favor with Dorgon, joined the secret Yongping conspiracy, and with Grand Secretary Qi Chongge had tampered with Taizu's Veritable Records to conceal Dorgon's crimes and exaggerate his achievements; he was executed and his estate was confiscated.
4
祁充格，烏蘇氏，滿洲鑲白旗人，世居瓦爾喀。 國初從其族吉思哈等來歸。 太宗時號「四貝勒」，以祁充格嫺習文史，令掌書記。 天聰五年，初設六部，授禮部啟心郎。 八年，考績，授牛錄額真。 崇德元年，睿親王多爾袞伐明，攻錦州，命鞏阿岱往濟師，祁充格從師有功，還報捷。 三年，睿親王復伐明，太宗親餞於郊。 祁充格以不啟豫親王多鐸從上出送，又於是日私往屯莊，坐死，命寬之，奪官，貫耳鞭責，以隸睿親王。 順治二年，授弘文院大學士，充明史總裁官、冊封朝鮮世子正使。 四年，考滿，加授牛錄額真。 六年，充太宗實錄總裁官，與剛林等同主會試。 八年，與剛林同誅。
Qi Chongge, of the Usu clan, was a Manchu of the Bordered White Banner from a family long settled in Warka. In the dynasty's early years he submitted with his kinsman Jisiha and others. Under Hong Taiji, known as the "Four Beile" period, Qi Chongge was put in charge of secretarial work because he was adept at literature and history. In the fifth year of Tiancong (1631), when the Six Ministries were first set up, he was made a Qixinlang in the Ministry of Rites. In the eighth year, after his performance review, he was appointed niru ejen. In the first year of Chongde (1636), Dorgon attacked Ming forces at Jinzhou; when Gongadai was sent to reinforce the army, Qi Chongge served with distinction and returned bearing news of victory. In the third year Dorgon again marched against Ming, and Hong Taiji personally saw him off outside the capital. Qi Chongge was condemned to death for failing to notify Prince Dodo to join the send-off and for visiting his private estate that day; the sentence was commuted to loss of office, ear-piercing, flogging, and reassignment to Dorgon's service. In Shunzhi year 2 (1645) he became Grand Secretary of the Hongwen Academy, chief compiler of the History of Ming, and chief envoy to invest Korea's crown prince. In the fourth year, after a satisfactory review, he was additionally granted the rank of niru ejen. In the sixth year he was chief compiler of Hong Taiji's Veritable Records and, with Gang Lin and others, chaired the metropolitan examinations. In the eighth year he was executed alongside Gang Lin.
5
馮銓，字振鷺，順天涿州人。 明萬曆進士，授檢討。 諂事魏忠賢，累遷文淵閣大學士兼戶部尚書，加少保兼太子太保，以微忤罷去。 莊烈帝既誅忠賢，得銓罷官後壽忠賢百韻詩，論杖徒，贖為民。
Feng Quan, styled Zhenlu, came from Zhuozhou in Shuntian Prefecture. He passed the jinshi examination in the Wanli era and was appointed a Hanlin reviser. By currying favor with Wei Zhongxian he rose to Grand Secretary of the Wenyuan Pavilion and Minister of Revenue, with the added titles Junior Guardian and Junior Mentor of the Heir Apparent, until a minor clash of wills cost him his post. After Chongzhen executed Wei Zhongxian, officials found a hundred-rhyme birthday poem Feng had written for Zhongxian after his own dismissal; he was sentenced to beating and exile, then ransomed back to commoner status.
6
順治元年，睿親王既定京師，以書徵銓，銓聞命即至，賚冠服、鞍馬、銀幣。 令以大學士原銜入內院佐理機務，與大學士洪承疇疏請復明票擬舊制，又與大學士謝升等議定郊社、宗廟樂章。 十月朔，世祖禦皇極門受賀，給事中孫承澤疏糾朝班雜亂，語侵內院。 銓與升、承疇乞罷，諭令益殫忠猷，以襄新治。
In Shunzhi year 1 (1644), after Dorgon secured Beijing, he summoned Feng Quan by letter; Feng came at once and was rewarded with court dress, horses, and silver. He was told to join the Inner Court at his former grand secretary rank to help manage affairs; with Hong Chengchou he petitioned to restore Ming-style draft rescripts, and with Xie Sheng and others he fixed the music for suburban and ancestral rites. On the first day of the tenth month the Shunzhi Emperor received congratulations at the Huangji Gate; Supervising Secretary Sun Chengze complained of disorder in the court ranks in language that criticized the Inner Court. Feng Quan, Xie Sheng, and Hong Chengchou asked to resign, but were told to redouble their loyal service in support of the new regime.
7
二年，授弘文院大學士兼禮部尚書。 御史吳達劾銓向降將薑瓖索銀三萬，許以封拜，未稱其意； 內院政本所關，乃令其子源淮擅入，張宴歡飲。 給事中許作梅、莊憲祖、杜立德，御史王守履、羅國士、鄧孕槐、桑芸等亦交章劾銓得招撫侍郎江禹緒金； 為源淮賄招撫侍郎孫之獬充標下中軍； 禮部侍郎李若琳為銓黨羽，庸懦無行。 御史李森先疏繼入，語尤峻，略謂：「明二百餘年國祚，壞於忠賢，而忠賢當日殺戮賢良，通賄謀逆，皆成於銓。 此通國共知者。 請立彰大法，戮之於市。 」疏並下刑部鞫問，刑部以所劾不實，啟睿親王。 王集廷臣覆讞，以銓降後與之獬、若琳皆先薙發，之獬家男婦並改滿裝，諸臣遂謀陷害。 王謂三人者皆恪遵本朝法度，詰責科道諸臣。 給事中龔鼎孳言銓附忠賢作惡，銓亦反詰鼎孳嘗降李自成。 王問鼎孳：「銓語實否？ 」鼎孳曰：「豈惟鼎孳，魏徵亦嘗降唐太宗。 」王因斥鼎孳，遂寢其事。 以森先言過甚，奪官，互見森先傳。
In the second year he was made Grand Secretary of the Hongwen Academy and Minister of Rites. Censor Wu Da accused Feng Quan of demanding thirty thousand taels from the surrendered general Jiang Xiang in exchange for a title, which failed to satisfy him; yet because the Inner Court was the heart of government, he still had his son Yuanhuai enter without leave and hold a lavish banquet. Supervising Secretaries Xu Zuomei, Zhuang Xianzu, and Du Lide, and Censors Wang Shoulü, Luo Guoshi, Deng Yunhuai, and Sang Yun also filed memorials accusing Feng of taking gold from Pacification Vice Minister Jiang Yuxu; and of bribing Pacification Vice Minister Sun Zhixi to make Yuanhuai a company commander in his command; Vice Minister of Rites Li Ruolin was Feng's ally—mediocre, timid, and corrupt. Censor Li Senxian followed with an even harsher memorial, arguing in essence: "More than two centuries of Ming rule were ruined by Wei Zhongxian, and Zhongxian's slaughter of the worthy, bribery, and treason were all engineered by Feng Quan. This is known to the whole realm. I ask that the law be upheld and that he be executed in public." The memorials were referred to the Ministry of Justice, which found the charges unsubstantiated and reported to Prince Regent Dorgon. Dorgon convened the ministers to rehear the case and argued that Feng Quan, Sun Zhixi, and Li Ruolin had all shaved their foreheads after surrender, and that Sun's household had adopted Manchu dress—the accusers were trying to frame loyal collaborators. Dorgon declared that all three had faithfully followed Qing law and rebuked the censorate officials. Supervising Secretary Gong Dingzi said Feng had aided Zhongxian's crimes; Feng retorted that Dingzi had once surrendered to Li Zicheng. Dorgon asked Dingzi: "Is what Feng Quan says true? " Dingzi replied: "Not only I—even Wei Zheng once submitted to Tang Taizong." Dorgon rebuked Dingzi and the case was dropped. Li Senxian was stripped of office for going too far; see his biography for the full account.
8
三年正月，銓疏言：「臣蒙特召入內院，列同官舊臣之前，臣固辭不敢。 攝政王面諭：'國家尊賢敬客，卿其勿讓！ '今海宇漸平，制度略定。 金台駿骨，暫示招徠。 久假不歸，實逾涯分。 況叨承寵命，賜婚滿洲，理當附籍滿洲編氓之末。 回繹尊賢敬客之諭，輾轉悚懼，特懇改列範文程、剛林後。 如以新舊為次，並當列祁充格、寧完我後。 」得旨：「天下一統，滿、漢無分別。 內院職掌等級，原有成規，不必再定。 」是年命典會試，列範文程、剛林後，寧完我前。 四年，复典會試。 六年，加少傅兼太子太傅。
In the first month of the third year Feng Quan wrote: "Your servant was specially summoned to the Inner Court and placed ahead of long-serving colleagues; I firmly declined, not daring to accept. The Prince Regent told me in person: 'The state honors talent and welcomes guests—you must not refuse!' 'The realm is gradually pacified and institutions are largely in place. The throne seeks able men—this is only a temporary gesture of recruitment. To remain in office so long without stepping back truly exceeds what is fitting. Moreover I have received extraordinary favor, including marriage into a Manchu clan, and ought by rights to rank at the end of the Manchu registers. Reflecting on the charge to honor talent, I am deeply uneasy and beg to be ranked after Fan Wencheng and Gang Lin. If precedence follows seniority, I should also stand after Qi Chongge and Ning Wanwo." The response read: "Under unified rule, Manchu and Han are not to be distinguished. The Inner Court already has established ranks; there is no need to redefine them." That year he was ordered to chair the metropolitan examinations, placed after Fan Wencheng and Gang Lin but before Ning Wanwo. In the fourth year he again chaired the metropolitan examinations. In the sixth year he was made Junior Tutor and Junior Mentor of the Heir Apparent.
9
八年，上親覈諸大臣功績，諭：「銓先經吳達奏劾得叛將薑瓖賄，便當引去； 乃隱忍居官，七年以來，無所建白：令致仕。 李若琳憸險專擅，與銓朋比為奸，奪官，永不敘用。 」銓既罷，代以陳名夏，坐事奪官； 代以陳之遴，亦不久罷。 上復召銓還，諭曰：「國家用人，使功不如使過。 銓素有才學，博洽諳練，朕特召用，以觀自新。 」銓至，召見，又與承疇、文程等同夕對論翰林官賢否，上曰：「朕將親試之！ 」銓奏曰：「南人優於文而行不符，北人短於文而行或善。 今取文行兼優者用之可也。 」上頷之，仍授弘文院大學士。 以議總兵任珍罪坐欺飾論絞，上命寬之。 銓入謝，奏對失旨，諭誡之。
In the eighth year the emperor personally reviewed the grand ministers' records and said: "When Wu Da accused Feng Quan of taking bribes from the rebel Jiang Xiang, Feng should have resigned; yet he stayed on; for seven years he has offered nothing of value—let him retire." Li Ruolin is treacherous and domineering and has conspired with Feng Quan—strip him of office and never employ him again." After Feng Quan left office, Chen Mingxia replaced him but was soon dismissed for misconduct; Chen Zhilin replaced him and was dismissed not long after. The emperor recalled Feng Quan and said: "In appointing men, it is better to use those who have erred than those who merely claim merit. Feng Quan has long been learned and experienced; I summon him specially to see whether he will reform." When Feng Quan arrived he was received in audience; that evening he joined Hong Chengchou and Fan Wencheng in discussing which Hanlin officials were fit for office, and the emperor said: "I shall test them myself!" Feng Quan replied: "Southerners excel at writing but their conduct often falls short; northerners are weaker in letters but their conduct may be sound. We should appoint those who excel in both learning and conduct." The emperor nodded and reappointed him Grand Secretary of the Hongwen Academy. For his role in the case of Regional Commander Ren Zhen he was sentenced to strangulation for concealing the facts; the emperor commuted the sentence. Feng Quan came to give thanks, but his replies displeased the emperor and he was admonished.
10
龔鼎孳為左都御史，复劾銓，上命指實。 鼎孳言銓罪過頗多，惟以密勿票擬，非如諸曹有實可指，上切責鼎孳。 十二年，居母喪，命入直如故。 尋加少師兼太子太師。 十三年，上以銓衰老，加太保致仕，仍令在左右備顧問，銓疏請回籍，許之。 十六年，改設內閣，命以原銜兼中和殿大學士。 康熙十一年，卒，諡文敏。 旋命削諡。
When Gong Dingzi became Left Censor-in-Chief he again impeached Feng Quan; the emperor demanded concrete evidence. Dingzi said Feng had many faults, but because his work was confidential drafting there was nothing concrete to cite, unlike other offices; the emperor sharply rebuked him. In the twelfth year, while mourning his mother, he was ordered to continue regular attendance at court. He was soon made Junior Preceptor and Junior Tutor of the Heir Apparent. In the thirteenth year the emperor, finding Feng Quan elderly, made him Grand Guardian and let him retire while keeping him nearby for advice; Feng asked to return home and was allowed. In the sixteenth year, when the Grand Secretariat became the Cabinet, he was appointed Grand Secretary of the Zhonghe Hall at his former rank. He died in the eleventh year of Kangxi (1672) and was posthumously titled Wenmin. The posthumous title was soon revoked.
11
孫之獬，山東淄川人。 明天啟進士，授檢討，遷侍讀。 以爭毀三朝要典入逆案，削籍。 順治元年，侍郎王鰲永招撫山東。 土寇攻淄川，之獬斥家財守城。 山東巡撫方大猷上其事，召詣京師，授禮部侍郎。 二年，師克九江，之獬奏請往任招撫，從之，加兵部尚書銜以行。 三年，召還。 總兵金聲桓劾之獬擅加副將高進庫、劉一鵬總兵銜，市恩構釁； 之獬議撫諸將懷觀望，不力攻贛州。 之獬疏辨，下兵部議，奪之獬官。 四年，土寇復攻淄川，之獬佐城守，城破，死之，諸孫從死者七人，下吏部議卹。 侍郎陳名夏、金之俊議復之獬官，予卹； 馬光輝及啟心郎寧古里議之獬已削籍，不當予卹。 兩議上，命用光輝議。
Sun Zhixi came from Zichuan in Shandong. A Tianqi-era jinshi, he was made a reviser and later promoted to reader-in-waiting. For opposing the destruction of the Essentials of Three Reigns he was listed among traitors and struck from the rolls. In Shunzhi year 1, Vice Minister Wang Aoyong was sent to pacify Shandong. When bandits attacked Zichuan, Zhixi spent his family fortune to hold the city. Shandong Governor Fang Dayou reported his service; Sun was summoned to Beijing and made Vice Minister of Rites. In the second year, after the army captured Jiujiang, Zhixi asked to go as pacification commissioner; the court agreed and gave him the acting rank of Minister of War. In the third year he was recalled to the capital. Regional Commander Jin Shenghuan impeached Sun Zhixi for having arbitrarily bestowed regional-commander titles on Deputy Generals Gao Jinku and Liu Yipeng, trading favors and deliberately provoking conflict. Sun Zhixi maintained that the conciliation policy had bred hesitation among the commanders and kept them from pressing the assault on Ganzhou. Sun Zhixi filed a memorial in rebuttal; the case went to the Board of War, which recommended that his office be taken from him. In the fourth year, local rebels again besieged Zichuan. Sun Zhixi helped hold the city, and when it fell he was killed, along with seven of his grandsons. The case was sent to the Board of Personnel to determine mourning honors. Vice Ministers Chen Mingxia and Jin Zhijun argued that Sun Zhixi's rank should be restored and that he should receive posthumous compensation. Ma Guanghui and the Keshinlang Ningguli held that because Sun Zhixi had already been removed from the rolls, he was not entitled to mourning honors. When both opinions reached the throne, the emperor ruled in favor of Ma Guanghui's view.
12
李若琳，山東新城人。 明天啟進士，授檢討。 順治元年，起原官，累遷少詹事，兼國子監祭酒。 詹事府裁，改翰林院侍讀學士，兼祭酒如故。 二年，請更定孔子神牌，復元制曰大成至聖文宣王，下禮部議，定稱大成至聖先師。 再遷禮部侍郎。 五年，進尚書。 六年，加太子太保。 既罷歸，未幾卒。
Li Ruolin came from Xincheng, Shandong. Under the Ming he earned his jinshi degree in the Tianqi reign and was appointed a reviser in the Hanlin Academy. In the first year of Shunzhi he was recalled to his former post and rose in stages to Vice Grand Secretary of the Hanlin Academy, while also serving as Chancellor of the Imperial Academy. After the Heir Apparent's Secretariat was abolished, he was made a Reader-in-Waiting of the Hanlin Academy and continued to hold the chancellorship as before. In the second year he petitioned to revise Confucius's spirit tablet, proposing a return to the Yuan title Perfect Sage and Ultimate Exemplar, Literary Propagation King. The Board of Rites deliberated and settled on the form Perfect Sage and Ultimate Exemplar, Supreme Teacher. He was promoted again, this time to Vice Minister of Rites. In the fifth year he was made Minister of Rites. In the sixth year he received the additional rank of Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent. Once dismissed and sent home, he died before long.
13
陳名夏，字百史，江南溧陽人。 明崇禎進士，官修撰，兼戶、兵二科都給事中。 降李自成。 福王時，入從賊案。 順治二年，詣大名降。 以保定巡撫王文奎薦，復原官。 入謁睿親王，請正大位。 王曰：「本朝自有家法，非爾所知也。 」旋超擢吏部侍郎，兼翰林院侍讀學士。 師定江南，九卿科道議南京設官。 名夏言：「國家定鼎神京，居北制南。 不當如前朝稱都會，設官如諸行省。 」疏入稱旨。 三年，居父喪，命奪情任事，請終制，賜白金五百，暫假歸葬，仍給俸贍其孥在京者。 五年，初設六部漢尚書，授名夏吏部尚書，加太子太保。 八年，授弘文院大學士，進少保，兼太子太保。
Chen Mingxia, styled Baishi, came from Liyang in Jiangnan. Under the Ming he took his jinshi in the Chongzhen reign, served as a compiler, and at the same time held the posts of Chief Supervising Secretary in the Revenue and War sections. He submitted to Li Zicheng. Under the Prince of Fu he was caught up in the prosecution of those who had followed the rebel regime. In the second year of Shunzhi he went to Daming and surrendered to the Qing. Recommended by Baoding Governor-General Wang Wenkui, he was restored to his former rank. On being received by Prince Regent Dorgon, he urged the prince to take the throne himself. The prince replied, This dynasty has its own house law. That is not a matter for you to speak of. He was then swiftly elevated to Vice Minister of Personnel and concurrently made Reader-in-Waiting of the Hanlin Academy. After the armies had pacified Jiangnan, the Nine Ministers and the censorial officials debated how offices should be set up in Nanjing. Chen Mingxia argued, The dynasty has fixed its seat in the sacred capital and rules the south from the north. Nanjing should not be treated, as under the previous dynasty, as a full secondary capital with a provincial-style bureaucracy. The memorial pleased the emperor when it reached him. In the third year, while mourning his father, he was ordered to remain in office despite the mourning period. He asked to observe full mourning, was granted five hundred taels of silver and temporary leave to bury his father, and continued to receive salary to support the family he had left in the capital. In the fifth year, when Han ministers were first placed at the head of the Six Ministries, Chen Mingxia was made Minister of Personnel and given the additional rank of Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent. In the eighth year he became Grand Secretary of the Hongwen Academy, was promoted to Junior Guardian, and retained his concurrent rank as Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent.

While Chen Mingxia held the Ministry of Personnel, the Manchu minister Tan Tai curried favor with Prince Regent Dorgon, seized power for himself, and Chen Mingxia attached himself to Tan Tai in corrupting the government. After Prince Regent Dorgon died that summer, Censor Zhang Huang impeached Chen Mingxia for factionalism, private dealing, and biased appointments. The case was sent to a joint tribunal of princes and senior ministers. Tan Tai protected Chen Mingxia, Zhang Huang was found guilty of false accusation, and he was sentenced to death. The full account appears in Zhang Huang's biography. Around the same time, Censor Sheng Fuxuan was punished with dismissal for having impeached Chen Mingxia. That autumn Tan Tai was executed for his crimes. In the spring of the ninth year the emperor ordered princes and senior ministers to reopen the charges Zhang Huang had brought against Chen Mingxia, and Chen Mingxia defended himself with great force. Under repeated interrogation he ran out of answers, wept, and pleaded that his early submission had been a service to the dynasty, hoping thereby to escape death. The emperor said, This is a shifty, deceitful little man, and his guilt is real and hard to excuse! But I have already decreed that everyone connected with Tan Tai's case is to be pardoned and left unprosecuted. If I now convict Chen Mingxia again, I would be breaking my word. He was therefore spared, though stripped of office; he continued to draw salary, was assigned to the Plain Yellow Banner to attend court as an idle official, and was told to mend his ways.

In the tenth year he was again made Grand Secretary of the Secretariat Academy. When the Ministry of Personnel fell vacant, Vice Minister Sun Chengyze proposed that Chen Mingxia serve concurrently as acting minister. The emperor rebuked Chengyze for having a vice minister recommend a grand secretary for the post, saying that this was improper. The following day Chen Mingxia was ordered to serve as acting Minister of Personnel. Whenever the emperor visited the Inner Court he repeatedly warned his ministers, Manchus and Han are one body; do not band together in rival factions. Chen Mingxia would sometimes answer back sharply, and the emperor cautioned him, Do not cling to your errors and bring ruin on yourself. In deliberating the case of Regional Commander Ren Zhen, the senior ministers agreed that Ren had killed without authorization, that his family nursed grievances, and that the penalty should be increased accordingly. Chen Mingxia, Chen Zhilin, Jin Zhijun, and others dissented. In the eleventh year Grand Secretary Ning Wanwo impeached him, writing in part, Though repeatedly spared, Chen Mingxia still harbors treacherous designs. He once told me, If we kept our hair and restored Ming dress, the realm would be at peace at once. His true intentions are impossible to read. Chen Mingxia's son Yechen terrorized the countryside, and both gentry and common people hated him for it. After moving to Jiangning he seized government gardens and mansions, used his connections to take bribes, and Chen Mingxia knowingly let him do it. While serving as acting Minister of Personnel, Chen Mingxia broke precedent to promote his crony Zhao Yanxian. Supervising Secretary Guo Yie memorialized against it. Chen Mingxia tried to punish Guo, but desisted when Liu Zhengzong protested. The Zhejiang circuit intendant Shi Rugang was Chen Mingxia's in-law. When Shi was dismissed and arrested for investigation, Chen Mingxia pressed hard to have him restored to office. Supervising Secretary Wei Xiangshu, another of Chen Mingxia's in-laws, was implicated in a joint-liability case and should have been demoted, yet received only a salary reduction on the roster. His protection of his clique and traffic in favors are plain from these examples. We are responsible for drafting memorials for imperial approval, and the weight of a single word can affect public and private interests alike. We keep a ledger recording each man's name so that responsibility cannot be evaded. Chen Mingxia privately erased or changed one hundred and fourteen characters. The emperor had just issued a stern warning against factionalism among censorial officials, and Chen Mingxia's unauthorized alterations were deception of the same kind. We ask that senior ministers be ordered to establish the facts and punish him according to law. After the memorial was sent for joint investigation, Chen Mingxia denied every charge as false except the remark about keeping hair and restoring Ming dress, which he admitted he had made. Ning Wanwo and Liu Zhengzong jointly confirmed that all the charges against Chen Mingxia were true. The verdict was finalized as decapitation, but the emperor ordered the sentence changed to strangulation. Yechen was arrested, beaten with the rod, and banished to frontier military service.

In the second year of Shunzhi he surrendered and was appointed Reader-in-Waiting of the Secretariat Academy. In the fifth year he was moved to Vice Minister of Rites. At the time the authorities were pursuing Li Yingshi, a notorious criminal of the capital. When princes and senior ministers jointly examined the case, Chen Zhilin said nothing. Pressed for an answer, he said, If Your Majesty puts Yingshi to death at once, that is enough. But if he is spared, he will surely turn on me. That is why I will not speak. The princes and senior ministers reported this to the throne. The emperor questioned Chen Zhilin, and he submitted a memorial confessing his fault. Because Chen Zhilin had shown repentance, the emperor pardoned him. He was transferred to the post of Minister of Revenue. 十三年，上幸南苑，召諸大臣入對，諭之遴曰：「朕不念爾前罪，屢申誥誡，嘗以朕言告人乎？ 抑自思所行亦曾少改乎？ 」之遴奏曰：「上教臣，臣安敢不改？ 特臣才疏學淺，不能仰報上恩。 」上曰：「朕非不知之遴等朋黨而用之，但欲資其才，故任以職。 且時時教飭之者，亦冀其改過效忠耳。 」因責左副都御史魏裔介等媕阿緘默，裔介退，具疏劾之遴植黨營私，當上詰問，但云「才疏學淺」，良心已昧； 並言之遴諷禮部尚書胡世安舉知府沈令式，旋為總督李輝祖所劾，是為結黨之據。 給事中王楨又劾之遴市權豪縱，昨蒙詰責，不思閉門省罪，即於次日遨遊靈佑宮，逍遙恣肆，罪不容誅。 之遴疏引罪，有云：「南北各親其親，各友其友。 」上益不懌，下吏部嚴議，命以原官發盛京居住。 是冬，復命回京入旗。 十五年，復坐賄結內監吳良輔，鞫實，論斬，命奪官，籍其家，流徙尚陽堡，死徙所。
In the thirteenth year the emperor went to the Southern Park and summoned the senior ministers. He said to Chen Zhilin, I have overlooked your past offenses and warned you again and again. Have you ever repeated my words to others? Or, looking at your own behavior, have you changed even a little? Chen Zhilin answered, Your Majesty has taught me—how could I dare not reform? Only my talent is limited and my learning shallow, and I cannot adequately repay Your Majesty's grace. The emperor said, It is not that I do not know Chen Zhilin and men like him form factions. I employ them because I want to use their abilities, and so I give them office. The reason I keep instructing and correcting them is that I still hope they will reform and serve loyally. He then rebuked Left Vice Censor-in-Chief Wei Yijie and others for their timid silence. Afterward Yijie submitted a detailed impeachment accusing Chen Zhilin of building a faction and pursuing private interests. When the emperor had questioned him, Chen Zhilin had answered only that he was talent-poor and learning-shallow—a sign that his conscience was already dead. The memorial also said that Chen Zhilin had prompted Minister of Rites Hu Shi'an to recommend Prefect Shen Lingshi, who was soon impeached by Governor-General Li Huizu, and that this showed collusion among allies. Supervising Secretary Wang Zhen also impeached Chen Zhilin for selling influence and behaving with arrogant license. Though rebuked only the day before, he did not stay home to reflect on his guilt but went the next day to stroll at Lingyou Palace in open dissipation—a crime, Wang argued, that deserved death. Chen Zhilin submitted a confession in which he wrote, North and south each favor their own kin and each stand by their own friends. The emperor was still angrier. The case went to the Board of Personnel for severe deliberation, and Chen Zhilin was ordered to keep his former rank while being sent to live in Shengjing. That winter he was ordered back to the capital and enrolled in a banner. In the fifteenth year he was again found guilty of bribing and colluding with the eunuch Wu Liangfu. The facts were proved, decapitation was recommended, and he was stripped of office, his property confiscated, and exiled to Shangyang Fort, where he died in banishment.

In the sixteenth year the emperor, finding Liu Zhengzong petty in spirit, consumed all day with poetry and belles-lettres, and stubbornly treating his own opinion as law in court debate, sent down a stern rebuke. He also commanded: When I place my trust in a minister, I expect us to see the work through together—to honor the reason I chose him in the first place. That is why I have not yet punished you, but only warn you again and again. You must thoroughly mend your old ways and prove yourself worthy of my forbearance. In the seventeenth year he memorialized asking to be removed from office; again the request was denied. Left Censor-in-Chief Wei Yijie impeached him, writing: In his own memorial Liu Zhengzong never mentioned the emperor's sharp rebuke—a failure of the most basic ministerial decorum. Li Changzuo was implicated in a treason case, yet Liu Zhengzong's draft memorial nevertheless recommended his advancement. He recommended Dong Guoxiang and Liang Yuming in turn; both have since been ruined by scandal, yet when impeached he did not confess his own part. What defense can there be for deceiving the throne? Liu Zhengzong counted Zhang Jinyan among his friends; Zhang Jinyan's preface to Liu Zhengzong's poems praises him as a talent for bringing clarity—a sly compliment that defies all understanding. Liu Zhengzong's younger brother Zhengxue had served as a general under Zheng Chenggong; Liu Zhengzong prevailed upon Governor Geng Tun to promote him out of turn to Central Army Commander. He has gnawed at the state and thrown governance into disorder—and the offenses do not stop at one. I beg the throne to act decisively and cut off this evil before it can sprout. Censor Ji Zhenyi piled on with further impeachments, extending to Dong Guoxiang and Liu Zhengxue, and to Liu Zhengzong's bribery and self-enrichment as well. Liu Zhengzong answered with a defensive memorial, arguing in brief: Li Changzuo was a rebel—Wei Yijie himself heads the judiciary; why did he not move to impeach sooner? By established rule, when a recommended official fails in his new post, the fault is traced back to whoever recommended him. Dong Guoxiang and Liang Yuming both fell only after they had already been promoted. They claim Zhang Jinyan's preface to my poems reads talent for bringing clarity—but my drafts still exist, and Zhang Jinyan's preface contains no such words. When the memorial reached the throne, the emperor stripped Liu Zhengzong of rank and ordered the princes and senior ministers to try him jointly. The emperor also rebuked Wei Yijie and Ji Zhenyi for failing to impeach sooner, and stripped them of office as well, pending investigation. Before long the joint inquiry reported back: of the charges Wei Yijie and Ji Zhenyi had brought against Liu Zhengzong, eleven were substantiated—enough to warrant death by strangulation. The emperor denounced him: Liu Zhengzong is by nature violent and overbearing, small-minded and shallow, partisan in argument, and perverse in judgment. He cares only for reputation and winning arguments, with no regard for the larger interest—and his offenses have only deepened. Out of clemency his life is spared; half his family's property is confiscated; he is enrolled in the Banners; and he is forbidden to return home. In the eighteenth year the Kangxi Emperor took the throne; given the Shunzhi Emperor's dying testament and the record of Liu Zhengzong's crimes, he merited the severest penalty—but the new emperor, taking pity on his great age, granted him pardon. Not long after, he died of illness.

論曰：剛林相太宗，與範文程、希福並命，祁充格掌記室，於創業宜皆有功。 銓故明相，諳故事，與名夏皆善佔對。 名夏勸進雖不用，以此邀峻擢。 之遴、正宗各有所援引，知當時亦頗用事。 際初運，都高位，而不足以堪之。 誅夷削奪，曾莫之惜。 正宗傾名夏，亦不免於罪，尤可鑑矣。
The historian writes: Gang Lin served Hong Taiji as chancellor, appointed alongside Fan Wencheng and Hifu, while Qi Chongge kept the secretariat—each ought to have earned merit in the founding of the dynasty. Feng Quan, a former Ming chancellor, knew the old forms by heart; he and Chen Mingxia were both masters of quick, polished reply. Chen Mingxia's advice on how the dynasty should move forward went unused, yet he parlayed even that into rapid advancement. Chen Zhilin and Liu Zhengzong each commanded their own networks of patronage; at the time they plainly wielded considerable power. They rose to the highest offices at the dynasty's beginning, yet none proved equal to the burden. When they were executed, ruined, or stripped of rank, no one mourned the loss. Liu Zhengzong brought down Chen Mingxia, yet could not keep himself from falling—a warning if ever there was one.
