1
杜受田，字芝農，山東濱州人。 父堮，嘉慶六年進士，由翰林院編修累官禮部侍郎，重宴鹿鳴，加太子少保，卒贈太傅，諡文端。
Du Shoutian, whose courtesy name was Zhinong, came from Binzhou in Shandong. His father Fu had passed the metropolitan examination in the sixth year of the Jiaqing reign and advanced from compiler at the Hanlin Academy to vice minister of rites. When he was honored with a second Deer Cry banquet, he received the title of junior guardian of the heir apparent. After his death he was posthumously made grand tutor and given the posthumous name Wenduan.
2
受田，道光三年進士，會試第一，殿試二甲第一，選庶吉士，授編修。 大考擢中允，遷洗馬，督山西學政。 十五年，特召還京，直上書房，授文宗讀。 四遷內閣學士，命專心授讀，毋庸到閣批本。 十八年，擢工部侍郎，調戶部。 二十四年，連擢左都御史、工部尚書，尋充上書房總師傅。 文宗自六歲入學，受田朝夕納誨，必以正道，歷十餘年。 至宣宗晚年，以文宗長且賢，欲付大業，猶未決。 會校獵南苑，諸皇子皆從，恭親王奕訢獲禽最多，文宗未發一矢，問之，對曰：「時方春，鳥獸孳育，不忍傷生以乾天和。 」宣宗大悅，曰：「此真帝者之言！ 」立儲遂密定，受田輔導之力也。
Shoutian earned his jinshi degree in the third year of the Daoguang reign, placing first in the metropolitan examination and first in the second class of the palace examination. He was chosen as a Hanlin bachelor and appointed a compiler. In the triennial palace examination he was promoted to middle attendant, then transferred to groom of the heir apparent, and was appointed educational commissioner of Shanxi. In the fifteenth year of the reign he was specially recalled to the capital, assigned to the Upper Study, and charged with tutoring the future Emperor Wenzong. After four further promotions he became a grand secretariat academician and was instructed to devote himself entirely to teaching, exempt from attending the secretariat to endorse memorials. In the eighteenth year he was promoted to vice minister of works and transferred to the Ministry of Revenue. In the twenty-fourth year he was promoted in quick succession to censor-in-chief of the left and minister of works, and soon afterward was made chief tutor of the Upper Study. From the time Wenzong began his studies at the age of six, Shoutian instructed him morning and evening along the path of righteousness for more than a decade. In the late years of Emperor Xuanzong's reign, the emperor wished to entrust the succession to Wenzong, who was both the eldest son and regarded as virtuous, but he had not yet made up his mind. Once, during a hunting exercise in the Southern Park, all the princes took part. Prince Gong Yixin brought down the most game, but Wenzong did not shoot a single arrow. When asked why, he answered: "It is spring, and birds and beasts are breeding; I cannot bear to take life and disturb the harmony of Heaven." " The emperor was greatly pleased and exclaimed: "These are truly the words of one destined to rule!" " The choice of heir was thus quietly settled—owing in large part to Shoutian's tutelage.
3
三十年，文宗即位，加太子太傅，兼署吏部尚書，調刑部尚書、協辦大學士。 受田雖未入樞廷，國家大政及進退大臣，上必諮而後行。 廣西軍事亟，受田數陳方略，薦林則徐、週天爵，先後起用。 提督向榮老於軍事，以同列不和被謗，力陳輿論，數保全之。 咸豐元年，調管禮部。 二年，因河決豐北久未塞，山東、江北被災重，命偕福州將軍怡良往治賑務。 疏言：「災廣民眾，賑恤不可緩，尤在得人。 」薦山東布政使劉源灝、江寧布政使祁宿藻，皆持正有為，責成專任； 請截留江、廣漕米六十萬石分給兩省； 詔並允行。
In the thirtieth year, when Wenzong ascended the throne, Shoutian was made grand tutor of the heir apparent, served concurrently as acting minister of personnel, and was then transferred to minister of justice and appointed associate grand secretary. Although Shoutian never sat on the Grand Council, the emperor always consulted him before acting on major state affairs or the appointment and dismissal of high officials. As the military crisis in Guangxi intensified, Shoutian repeatedly proposed strategies and recommended Lin Zexu and Zhou Tianjue, both of whom were eventually brought back into service. The provincial military commander Xiang Rong was a seasoned soldier but came under attack because of friction with his colleagues. Shoutian repeatedly invoked public opinion on his behalf and several times saved him from dismissal. In the first year of the Xianfeng reign he was transferred to head the Ministry of Rites. In the second year, because the Yellow River breach at Fengbei had long remained unrepaired and Shandong and the region north of the Yangtze had suffered severe flooding, he was ordered to accompany the Fuzhou general Yiliang to oversee relief work. In a memorial he wrote: "The disaster covers a wide area and affects vast numbers of people; relief cannot be delayed, and success depends above all on appointing the right men." " He recommended Liu Yuanhao, provincial administration commissioner of Shandong, and Qi Suzao, provincial administration commissioner of Jiangning—both upright and capable men—and urged that they be given sole charge of the work; he asked that six hundred thousand piculs of tribute grain from the Jiangsu and Guangdong routes be held back and divided between the two provinces; and an edict approved all of these proposals.
4
受田自侍文宗學，未嘗離左右，當陛辭，不覺感戀流涕。 在途觸暑染疫，力疾治事，與源灝、宿藻等覈定施賑章程，疏陳而不言病，至清江浦遽卒。 遺疏念賊氛未靖，河患未平，尤以敬天法祖、勤政愛民、崇節儉、慎好惡、平賞罰為言。 文宗震悼，贈太師、大學士，入祀賢良祠，賜金五千兩治喪，遣近臣慰視其父堮，擢其子檢討翰為庶子，孫三人並賜舉人。 复特詔曰：「杜受田品端學粹，正色立朝，皇考深加倚重，特簡為朕師傅。 憶在書齋，凡所陳說，悉本唐、虞、三代聖聖相傳之旨，實能發明蘊奧，體用兼賅。 朕即位後，週諮時政利弊，民生疾苦，盡心獻替，啟沃良多！ 援嘉慶朝大學士硃珪故事，特諡文正。 」謂其公忠正直，足當「正」字而無愧。 柩至京，上親奠，撫棺哭甚哀，晉其父堮禮部尚書銜。 明年，上臨雍講學，复詔褒受田曩日講貫之功，即家賜祭一壇。 及柩歸，命恭親王奠送，遣官到籍致祭，飾終之典，一時無與比。 子，由翰林院編修累官戶部侍郎，督辦山東團練。
Shoutian had served at Wenzong's side throughout his studies and had never been far from him. When he took formal leave at court, he wept uncontrollably with emotion. On the journey he fell ill with an epidemic fever brought on by the summer heat, yet he pressed on with his duties. Working with Yuanhao, Suzao, and others, he finalized the relief regulations and submitted memorials without mentioning his illness, then died suddenly at Qingjiangpu. In his final memorial he noted that the rebels had not yet been subdued and the river disaster remained unresolved, and he especially urged reverence for Heaven and fidelity to ancestral precedent, diligent governance and care for the people, frugality, circumspection in personal preferences, and even-handed rewards and punishments. Wenzong was stricken with grief. Shoutian was posthumously made grand preceptor and grand secretary, enshrined in the Shrine of Worthies, and granted five thousand taels of silver for funeral expenses. A close attendant was sent to console his father Fu. His son Han, a reviser at the Hanlin Academy, was promoted to junior mentor, and all three grandsons were granted the status of provincial graduates. A special edict further declared: "Du Shoutian was upright in character and pure in learning; he stood in court with unwavering integrity. My late father placed great trust in him and specially chose him as my tutor." "I recall our days in the study: everything he taught drew on the teachings handed down from the sage rulers of antiquity through the Three Dynasties; he truly could illuminate their deepest meaning and unite principle with practice." "After my accession I constantly sought his counsel on the strengths and failings of current policy and the hardships of the people; he gave his whole heart to advising me and enriched my understanding immeasurably!" "Following the precedent of Grand Secretary Zhu Gui under the Jiaqing emperor, he was specially granted the posthumous title Wenzheng." " The title was meant to signify that his public loyalty and upright integrity fully deserved the character zheng without reproach. When the coffin reached the capital, the emperor personally offered libations, stroked the coffin, and wept bitterly. He also advanced Shoutian's father Fu to the nominal rank of minister of rites. The following year, when the emperor lectured at the Imperial Academy, another edict praised Shoutian's years of instruction and granted a sacrificial altar to be offered at his home. When the coffin was returned to his native place, Prince Gong was ordered to escort it and offer libations along the way; officials were sent to his home district to perform sacrifices. The funeral honors accorded him were unequaled in his time. His son advanced from compiler at the Hanlin Academy to vice minister of revenue and supervised militia training in Shandong.
5
翰，字繼園。 道光二十四年進士，選庶吉士，授檢討。 咸豐三年，降。 服闋，補庶子。 文宗念受田舊勞，數月間迭擢工部侍郎，命在軍機大臣上行走，辦理京城巡防事宜。 翰勇於任事，甚被倚任。 十年，隨扈熱河，以勞賜花翎。 上崩於行在，穆宗即位。 御史董元醇疏請兩宮皇太后垂簾聽政，載垣、端華、肅順等持不可，翰附之，抗言甚力，遂黜元醇議。 肅順曰：「君誠不愧杜文正之子也！ 」既而載垣等以竊奪政柄被罪，翰連坐，議革職戍新疆，詔原之，褫職，免其發遣。 同治五年，卒。
Han, whose courtesy name was Jiyuan. He passed the metropolitan examination in the twenty-fourth year of the Daoguang reign, was chosen as a Hanlin bachelor, and was appointed a reviser. In the third year of the Xianfeng reign he was demoted. When his mourning period ended, he was restored to the post of junior mentor. Mindful of Shoutian's long service, Wenzong promoted Han within a few months to vice minister of works and ordered him to serve above the Grand Council ministers in charge of capital patrol and defense. Han was bold in taking on responsibility and came to be greatly relied upon. In the tenth year he accompanied the court to Rehe and was awarded the peacock feather for his exertions. The emperor died at the traveling palace, and the future Emperor Muzong ascended the throne. Censor Dong Yuanchun memorialized asking that the two empress dowagers rule from behind a curtain. Zaiyuan, Duhua, Sushun, and others opposed the idea; Han sided with them and argued vigorously, and Dong's proposal was set aside. Sushun said: "You are indeed a worthy son of Du Wenzheng!" " Before long Zaiyuan and the others were condemned for seizing power. Han was implicated as well; it was proposed to strip him of office and exile him to Xinjiang, but an edict pardoned him, removed him from office, and spared him deportation. He died in the fifth year of the Tongzhi reign.
6
祁俊藻，字春圃，山西壽陽人。 父韻士，官戶部郎中，以事繫獄。 俊藻方幼，隨侍讀書不輟，賦春草詩以見志。 嘉慶十九年，成進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 道光元年，直南書房。 督湖南學政，累遷庶子。 十年，以母病陳情歸養，宣宗不許，予假省親。 逾年回京，補原官，遷侍講學士。 尋复予假省母，不開缺。 歷通政司副使、光祿寺卿、內閣學士。 母憂歸，十六年，將屆服闋，預授兵部侍郎，督江蘇學政。 歷戶部、吏部侍郎，留學政任，未滿，十九年，命偕侍郎黃爵滋視福建海防及禁煙事，連擢左都御史、兵部尚書。 迭疏陳總宜駐泉州治防務，改海口砲台為墩，查禁煙販，捕治漢奸，並禁漳、泉兩府行使夷錢，夾帶私鑄者治罪，嚴懲械鬥，並得旨允行。 在閩半載，還經浙江，按台、溫兩府私種罌粟，劾罷台州知府潘盛； 又劾溫州知府劉煜試行票鹽不善，被議，自呈枉屈，戍新疆。 時鄧廷楨奏擊英吉利兵船於廈門走之，忌者謂其不實，命俊藻復往按，具陳戰胜狀。 回京，仍直南書房。 二十一年，調戶部，命為軍機大臣。
Qi Junzao, whose courtesy name was Chunpu, came from Shouyang in Shanxi. His father Yunshi held the post of director in the Ministry of Revenue and was imprisoned in connection with an official matter. Junzao was still a boy. He attended his father in prison without interrupting his studies and wrote a poem on spring grass to express his determination. In the nineteenth year of the Jiaqing reign he passed the metropolitan examination, was chosen as a Hanlin bachelor, and was appointed a compiler. In the first year of the Daoguang reign he was assigned to the Southern Study. He served as educational commissioner of Hunan and was promoted in stages to junior mentor. In the tenth year, citing his mother's illness, he petitioned to return home to care for her. Emperor Xuanzong refused permission but granted him leave to visit his family. More than a year later he returned to the capital, resumed his former post, and was promoted to expositor of the Hanlin Academy. Soon afterward he was again granted leave to visit his mother, without vacating his post. He served in succession as vice commissioner of the Office of Transmission, director of the Court of Imperial Entertainments, and grand secretariat academician. He returned home to observe mourning for his mother. In the sixteenth year, as his mourning period was nearing its end, he was appointed in advance as vice minister of war and educational commissioner of Jiangsu. He served successively as vice minister of revenue and vice minister of personnel while retaining his educational commission before its term had expired. In the nineteenth year he was ordered, together with Vice Minister Huang Juezi, to inspect Fujian coastal defense and the opium prohibition effort, and was promoted in quick succession to censor-in-chief of the left and minister of war. In a series of memorials he urged that the commander-in-chief station himself at Quanzhou to direct coastal defense, that harbor batteries be converted into blockhouses, that opium trafficking be investigated and suppressed, and that Han collaborators be arrested and punished. He also called for a ban on foreign coin in Zhangzhou and Quanzhou prefectures, with penalties for those mixing in privately cast currency, and for severe punishment of armed brawls. All of these proposals received imperial approval. After half a year in Fujian, he returned by way of Zhejiang and investigated private opium cultivation in Taizhou and Wenzhou prefectures, impeaching Taizhou prefect Pan Sheng, who was dismissed; he also impeached Wenzhou prefect Liu Yu for mismanaging a trial of the ticket-salt system. Junzao himself was censured, submitted a memorial claiming he had been wronged, and was exiled to Xinjiang. At that time Deng Tingzhen reported that he had driven off British warships at Xiamen. Envious officials claimed the report was false, and Junzao was ordered back to investigate. He submitted a full account confirming the victory. On his return to the capital he was again assigned to the Southern Study. In the twenty-first year he was transferred to the Ministry of Revenue and appointed a Grand Council minister.
7
二十六年，偕尚書文慶按長蘆鹽運使陳鑑挪撥鹽課，彌補加價，褫其職，歷任鹽政運司議譴有差。 二十九年，以戶部尚書協辦大學士，命赴甘肅偕琦善按前任總督布彥泰清查舛誤、縱容家丁，下嚴議。 回京，請便道省墓，途次聞宣宗崩，過里門不入。 文宗即位，拜體仁閣大學士，仍管戶部。 俊藻自道光中論洋務與穆彰阿不合，至是文宗銳意圖治，罷穆彰阿，俊藻遂領樞務，開言路，起用舊臣，俊藻左右之。
In the twenty-sixth year, together with Minister Wen Qing, he investigated Changlu salt transport commissioner Chen Jian for diverting salt revenue to cover price supplements. Chen was stripped of office, and successive salt administrators and transport commissioners were censured to varying degrees. In the twenty-ninth year, as minister of revenue and associate grand secretary, he was ordered to Gansu with Qishan to investigate former governor Buyantai for errors in auditing accounts and indulgence of household retainers. Severe censure was imposed. On his return to the capital he asked permission to visit his ancestral graves by a convenient route. Along the way he learned that Emperor Xuanzong had died and passed his home without entering. When Wenzong ascended the throne, Junzao was made grand secretary of the Hall of Embodying Benevolence while continuing to head the Ministry of Revenue. Junzao had been at odds with Mu Zhang'a over foreign affairs since the Daoguang reign. Now Wenzong was determined to set the government in order, dismissed Mu Zhang'a, and Junzao took charge of Grand Council affairs, reopened channels for memorialists, and brought back veteran officials—initiatives that Junzao helped steer.
8
咸豐元年，調管工部，兼管戶部三庫事務。 二年，復調戶部。 廣西匪日熾，出湖南，遂不可製，湖北、江南數省先後淪陷。 軍興財匱，議者試行鈔法，又鑄當百、當五百大錢，皆行之未久而滋弊。 尚書肅順同掌戶部事，尚苛刻。 又湘軍初起，肅順力言其可用，上鄉之，俊藻皆意與齟《齒吾》，屢稱病請罷，溫詔慰留。 四年冬，复堅以為請，乃允致仕。 十年，英法聯軍犯天津，車駕將幸熱河，俊藻密疏切諫。 又言關中形勝可建都，釐捐病民，北省尤宜急停，並報聞。
In the first year of the Xianfeng reign he was transferred to head the Ministry of Works and was also put in charge of the three treasuries of the Ministry of Revenue. In the second year he was transferred back to the Ministry of Revenue. The rebels in Guangxi grew stronger by the day, spilled into Hunan, and could no longer be contained. Hubei, Jiangnan, and several other provinces fell in succession. War had drained the treasury, and advocates experimented with paper currency and also cast large coins denominated at one hundred and five hundred cash. All were tried briefly before serious abuses appeared. Minister Sushun shared responsibility for revenue affairs and remained harsh in his methods. When the Xiang Army was first organized, Sushun strongly argued for its usefulness and won the emperor's favor. Junzao disagreed on nearly every point, repeatedly pleaded illness to resign, and received warm edicts urging him to stay. In the winter of the fourth year he again pressed his request firmly, and permission to retire was granted. In the tenth year the Anglo-French allied armies threatened Tianjin. As the court prepared to move to Rehe, Junzao submitted a secret memorial urgently remonstrating against the move. He also argued that the strategic advantages of the Guanzhong region made it suitable as a capital site, that the lijin transit levy harmed the people and should be halted quickly in the northern provinces in particular, and reported these views to the throne.
9
十一年，穆宗即位，特詔起用。 疏陳時政六事：曰保護聖躬以崇帝學； 曰綏輯民心以清盜源； 曰重守令以固民心； 曰開制科以收人才； 曰速剿山東、河南賊匪，嚴防山西、陝西要隘，以衛畿輔； 曰敦崇節儉以培元氣。 言甚切摯，並被嘉納，次第施行。 命以大學士銜授禮部尚書。 同治元年，穆宗入學，命直弘德殿，偕翁心存、倭仁、李鴻藻同授讀，摘錄經史二帙進呈。 上讀大學畢，俊藻具疏推陳為人君止於仁之義，略曰：「大學一書，皇上已成誦，凡制治保邦之道，用人行政之源，胥在於是。 為人君之道，止於仁而已。 治國平天下兩章，言仁者六，終之以未有上好仁而下不好義。 蓋仁者必以仁親為寶，故能愛人，能惡人。 不好仁，則好人之所惡，惡人之所好。 仁者必以貪為戒，故忠信以得之，不仁者則驕泰以失之矣。 仁者以義為利，不以利為利，故以財發身，不仁者則以身發財，菑害並至矣。 千古治亂之機，判於義利，而義利之判，則由於上之好仁不好仁也。 如近日所講帝鑑圖說，下車泣罪，解網施恩，澤及枯骨等事，斯即帝王仁心所見端也。 若納諫求賢，尊儒遠佞，則仁親為寶，能好能惡之說也。 露台罷工，裘馬卻獻，則以義為利，不以利為利之說也。 帝鑑圖說講畢，請進講輿地，以會典諸圖簡明，易於指畫。 又耕織圖及內府石刻宋馬遠豳風圖為農桑衣食之原，皇上讀書之暇，隨時講求，庶知稼穡之艱難，懍守成之不易也。 」
In the eleventh year, when Muzong ascended the throne, a special edict recalled him to office. In a memorial he set forth six urgent matters of policy: first, protect the emperor's person in order to honor imperial learning; second, pacify the people's hearts in order to cut off the roots of rebellion; third, place greater weight on prefects and magistrates in order to secure the people's loyalty; fourth, open special examinations to recruit talent; fifth, swiftly suppress the rebels in Shandong and Henan and strictly guard the key passes of Shanxi and Shaanxi in order to protect the capital region; sixth, earnestly promote frugality in order to restore the nation's vitality. His language was earnest and sincere. All six proposals were praised and accepted, and were carried out in turn. He was appointed minister of rites with the nominal rank of grand secretary. In the first year of the Tongzhi reign, when Muzong began his studies, Junzao was assigned to the Hall of Manifest Virtue to teach jointly with Weng Xincun, Woren, and Li Hongzao, and compiled two volumes of selections from the classics and histories for presentation to the throne. When the emperor had finished reading the Great Learning, Junzao submitted a detailed memorial expounding the doctrine that the ruler's ultimate goal is benevolence. In summary he wrote: "Your Majesty has already completed recitation of the Great Learning. Every principle of ordering government and preserving the state, and every source of employing men and administering affairs, lies within it." The way of the ruler ends in benevolence and nothing more. The two chapters on bringing order to the state and pacifying the realm mention benevolence six times and conclude with the statement that never has a ruler above loved benevolence while those below did not love righteousness. For the benevolent ruler must take benevolent kinship as his treasure, and thus can love the worthy and hate the unworthy. If one does not love benevolence, one loves what good men hate and hates what good men love. The benevolent ruler must take greed as a warning, and thus gains the realm through loyalty and trust; the non-benevolent lose it through arrogance and excess. The benevolent take righteousness as profit, not profit as profit; thus they use wealth to enrich life. The non-benevolent use life to amass wealth, and calamity and harm arrive together. The pivot of order and disorder through the ages turns on the choice between righteousness and profit, and that choice depends on whether those above love benevolence or do not. As in the recent lectures on the Illustrated Mirror of Emperors—descending from the carriage to weep over a prisoner's crime, releasing animals from the net to show mercy, extending grace even to withered bones—these are the first signs of an emperor's benevolent heart. As for accepting remonstrance, seeking the worthy, honoring Confucian learning, and keeping flatterers at a distance—these embody the teaching on taking benevolent kinship as treasure and being able to love the worthy and hate the unworthy. Halting work on the terrace stage and declining gifts of fur and horses—these embody the teaching of taking righteousness as profit rather than profit as profit. When the lectures on the Illustrated Mirror of Emperors were finished, he asked to proceed to geography, noting that the maps in the Collected Statutes were concise and easy to use for instruction. He also recommended the Pictures of Tilling and Weaving and the palace stone engravings of Song Ma Yuan's Bin Feng illustrations as the foundations of agriculture, sericulture, clothing, and food. He urged that in His Majesty's leisure from reading he study these regularly, so as to understand the hardship of farming and appreciate how difficult it is to preserve what has been achieved. "
10
二年，上服除，俊藻偕倭仁、李鴻藻上疏曰：「皇上沖齡踐阼，智慧漸開。 當此釋服之初，吉禮舉行，聖心之敬肆於此分，風會之轉移即於此始，則玩好之漸可慮也，遊觀之漸可慮也，興作之漸可慮也。 嗜好之端一開，不惟分誦讀之心，海內之窺意旨者，且將從風而靡。 安危治亂之機，其端甚微，所關甚鉅，可無慎乎？ 方今軍務未平，生民塗炭，正君臣交儆之時，非上下恬熙之日。 伏原皇上恪遵慈訓，時時以憂勤惕厲為心，以逸樂便安為戒。 凡內廷服禦一切用項，稍涉浮靡，概從裁減； 向例所有，不妨量為撙節。 如是，則外務之紛華不接於耳目，詩書之啟迪益斂夫心思，聖學日新，聖德日固，而去奢崇儉之風，自不令而行矣。 」疏上，優詔褒答焉。
In the second year, when the emperor's mourning period ended, Junzao joined Woren and Li Hongzao in a memorial that read: "Your Majesty ascended the throne at a tender age, and your wisdom is gradually unfolding." At this moment when mourning garments are laid aside and auspicious rites are performed, whether the imperial mind will remain reverent or grow lax is decided now, and the turning of custom begins here. The gradual growth of fondness for curios is to be feared, as is the gradual growth of sightseeing and of grand construction projects. Once the first indulgence appears, it will not only distract the mind from study; those throughout the realm who watch for imperial intent will follow suit and fall into excess. The pivot of safety and danger, order and disorder, begins in something very slight yet concerns matters of the greatest moment—can one not be cautious? At present military affairs are not yet settled and the people are in misery. This is precisely the time when ruler and ministers should warn one another, not a day for ease and complacency throughout the court. We humbly pray that Your Majesty faithfully follow the empress dowagers' instruction, always taking anxious diligence and vigilance to heart, and regarding ease, pleasure, and comfortable security as warnings. As for all items of clothing and daily use within the inner palace, whatever is even slightly extravagant should be cut back; and what has long been customary may suitably be restrained in measure. If so, the splendor of external affairs will not reach the eyes and ears, the enlightenment of the classics will increasingly focus the mind, imperial learning will daily advance, imperial virtue will daily strengthen, and the spirit of rejecting extravagance and honoring frugality will prevail of itself without needing to be commanded. " When the memorial was submitted, an edict warmly praised and answered it.
11
俊藻提倡樸學，延納寒素，士林歸之。 疏言：「通經之學，義理與訓詁不可偏重。 後學不察，以訓詁專屬漢儒，義理專屬宋儒，使畫分界限，學術日歧。 」因舉素所知寒士端木埰、鄭珍、莫友芝、閻汝弼、王軒、楊寶臣，經明行修，堪資器使。 又疏言：「軍興以來，不講吏治，請下中外大臣，保舉循吏及伏處潛修之士，以備任用。 」自舉原任同知劉大紳、按察使李文耕、大順廣道劉煦，請宣付史館入循吏傳。 又薦直隸知縣張光藻、陳崇砥、王蘭廣，山東知縣蔣慶第，山西知縣程豫、吳輝祖及江南優貢端木埰，山西舉人秦東來。 並嘉納允行。 屢以病乞休，三年，詔致仕，食全俸。 五年，卒，晉贈太保，祀賢良祠，命鍾郡王奠醊，諡文端。 擢其子編修世長以侍讀用。
Junzao promoted plain scholarship, welcomed scholars of humble means, and the scholarly world rallied to him. In a memorial he wrote: "In the study of the classics, moral principle and textual exegesis cannot be overweighted on either side." Later students fail to see this, assigning exegesis solely to Han scholars and moral principle solely to Song scholars, drawing dividing lines until scholarship grows daily more fragmented. " He then cited poor scholars he had long known—Duanmu Cai, Zheng Zhen, Mo Youzhi, Yan Rubi, Wang Xuan, and Yang Baochen—men learned in the classics and upright in conduct, fit for appointment. In another memorial he wrote: "Since the wars began, local governance has been neglected. I ask that ministers at court and in the provinces be ordered to recommend honest officials and scholars in quiet retirement who have cultivated themselves, to be held in readiness for appointment." " He himself cited former sub-prefect Liu Dashen, provincial surveillance commissioner Li Wengeng, and Da-Shun-Guang circuit intendant Liu Xu, asking that edicts be issued ordering the Historiographical Institute to enter them in the Biographies of Honest Officials. He also recommended Zhili magistrates Zhang Guangzao, Chen Chongdi, and Wang Languang; Shandong magistrate Jiang Qingdi; Shanxi magistrates Cheng Yu and Wu Huizu; the distinguished tribute student from Jiangnan Duanmu Cai; and the provincial graduate from Shanxi Qin Donglai. All were praised and approved for implementation. He repeatedly pleaded illness to retire. In the third year an edict granted him retirement on full salary. In the fifth year he died. He was posthumously made grand guardian, enshrined in the Shrine of Worthies, and Prince Zhong was ordered to offer libations. He was given the posthumous title Wenduan. His son Shizhang, a compiler at the Hanlin Academy, was promoted for appointment as palace reader.
12
世長，字子禾。 咸豐十年進士。 年十三，侍父江蘇學政任，幕客俞正燮、張穆、苗夔諸人，並樸學通儒，世長濡染有素，尤篤守宋儒義理之說。 同治九年，服闋，補侍讀。 累遷內閣學士。 光緒初，連督安徽、順天、浙江學政，清勤愛士，一守俊藻舊規。 歷禮部、吏部侍郎，擢​​左都御史。 十年，命偕尚書延煦勘山東河工，疏言：「非疏海口不能洩盛漲。 修防以民埝為第一層屏障，守民埝即以守大堤。 巡撫陳士傑築修民埝多在大堤既決之後，殊為失計。 請乘時興修。 」從之。 迭疏陳時務，多持正議。 十六年，遷工部尚書，兼管順天府尹。 兩典會試，皆得士。 世長清操自勵，累世官卿貳，家如寒素，時以稱焉。 十八年，卒，優詔賜卹，諡文恪。 賜其孫師曾員外郎，子友蒙主事。
Shizhang, whose courtesy name was Zihe. He passed the metropolitan examination in the tenth year of the Xianfeng reign. At the age of thirteen he accompanied his father during his term as educational commissioner of Jiangsu. Staff members Yu Zhengxie, Zhang Mu, Miao Kui, and others were all distinguished scholars of plain learning, and Shizhang was steeped in their influence from youth, especially devoted to the moral teachings of Song Neo-Confucians. In the ninth year of the Tongzhi reign, when his mourning period ended, he was restored to the post of palace reader. He was promoted in stages to grand secretariat academician. In the early Guangxu reign he served in succession as educational commissioner of Anhui, Shuntian, and Zhejiang. Diligent, frugal, and devoted to scholars, he adhered entirely to Junzao's old standards. He served as vice minister of rites and vice minister of personnel and was promoted to censor-in-chief of the left. In the tenth year he was ordered, together with Minister Yanchi, to inspect Shandong river works. In a memorial he wrote: "Without dredging the sea mouth the great flood cannot be discharged." In repair and defense the people's dikes should serve as the first line of defense; guarding the people's dikes is guarding the main embankment. Governor Chen Shijie built and repaired people's dikes mostly only after the main embankment had already breached—a serious error in planning. He asked that repairs be undertaken while conditions permitted. " His request was approved. In successive memorials on current affairs he mostly upheld upright views. In the sixteenth year he was transferred to minister of works and was also put in charge of the Shuntian prefect. He presided twice over the metropolitan examination and both times selected worthy candidates. Shizhang disciplined himself with pure integrity. Though his family for generations had held ministerial rank, their household lived like that of the poor, and he was praised for this in his time. In the eighteenth year he died. A gracious edict granted funeral compensation and the posthumous title Wenkai. His grandson Shizeng was granted the rank of secretary in the outer court, and his son Youmeng was granted the rank of director.
13
翁心存，字二銘，江蘇常熟人。 父咸封，官海州學正。 知州唐仲冕見心存有異才，奇之，授之學。 道光二年，成進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 大考擢中允，督廣東學政。 任滿，入直上書房，授惠郡王讀。 尋督江西學政，累遷大理寺少卿。 十七年，复直上書房，授六阿哥讀。 逾年，以母老乞養。 家居十年，終母喪。 會子同書督貴州學政，陛辭，宣宗命傳諭促之來。 二十九年，至京，仍入直，授八阿哥讀。 補祭酒。 歷內閣學士、工部侍郎，調戶部。 江蘇巡撫請蘇州、松江、太倉漕米改徵折色，心存謂：「三屬額徵米一百十四萬餘石，一旦改折，慮京倉不敷支放，州縣假折色抑勒倍徵，便民適以累民。 」主駁議，事乃寢。
Weng Xincun, whose courtesy name was Erming, came from Changshu in Jiangsu. His father Xianfeng served as director of studies in Haizhou. Prefect Tang Zhongmian saw that Xincun had unusual talent, took a keen interest in him, and personally instructed him. In the second year of the Daoguang reign he passed the metropolitan examination, was chosen as a Hanlin bachelor, and was appointed a compiler. In the triennial palace examination he was promoted to middle attendant and was appointed educational commissioner of Guangdong. When his term ended he was assigned to the Upper Study to instruct Prince Hui. Soon he was appointed educational commissioner of Jiangxi and was promoted in stages to vice president of the Court of Judicial Review. In the seventeenth year he was again assigned to the Upper Study to instruct the Sixth Prince. More than a year later he petitioned to retire and care for his aged mother. He remained at home for ten years until his mother's mourning was completed. It happened that his son Tongshu was serving as educational commissioner of Guizhou. When Tongshu took formal leave at court, Emperor Xuanzong ordered a message conveyed urging Xincun to return. In the twenty-ninth year he reached the capital, resumed regular service, and was assigned to instruct the Eighth Prince. He was restored to the post of libationer of the Imperial Academy. He served as grand secretariat academician and vice minister of works, then was transferred to the Ministry of Revenue. The Jiangsu governor requested that tribute grain from Suzhou, Songjiang, and Taicang be collected in commuted silver instead. Xincun argued: "The three jurisdictions have a quota of more than 1,140,000 piculs of grain. If conversion is made all at once, I fear the capital granaries will lack sufficient grain for disbursement, and prefectures and counties will use commuted silver as a pretext to extort double levies, turning a measure meant to help the people into a burden on them." " He led the opposition, and the matter was dropped.
14
咸豐元年，擢工部尚書。 三年，江寧陷，心存疏陳兵事，請乘賊勢未定，飭向榮渡江，陳金綬進屯浦口，以上海水師溯流沖其前，江忠源、鄧紹良之師掩其後，四路進攻； 增重兵守江、淮杜北竄； 急清兗、豫、鳳、潁捻匪，毋令與粵寇合勢； 並覈軍需，卹災黎； 籌京倉積貯，整飭紀綱，以維根本。 疏上，多被採用。 又薦湖北按察使江忠源，請畀統帥重任，尋即擢為巡撫。 調刑部，再調工部，兼管順天府尹。
In the first year of the Xianfeng reign he was promoted to minister of works. In the third year, when Jiangning fell, Xincun memorialized on military affairs. He urged that while rebel strength was not yet consolidated, Xiang Rong be ordered to cross the Yangtze, Chen Jinshou advance and encamp at Pukou, the Shanghai naval force strike upstream at their front, and the armies of Jiang Zhongyuan and Deng Shaoliang fall upon their rear in a four-pronged attack; heavy forces be added to guard the Yangtze and Huai and block a northern breakout; the Nian bandits in Yan, Yu, Feng, and Ying be quickly suppressed so they do not join forces with the Guangdong rebels; military supplies be audited and disaster victims relieved; and stores in the capital granaries be built up and discipline rectified in order to preserve the foundations of the state. When the memorial was submitted, many of his proposals were adopted. He also recommended Hubei provincial surveillance commissioner Jiang Zhongyuan and asked that he be given major command responsibility. Jiang was soon promoted to governor. He was transferred to the Ministry of Justice, then back to the Ministry of Works, and was also put in charge of the Shuntian prefect.
15
粵匪北犯，心存疏言賊氛逼近，請扼河而守，畿南直駐重兵，河南、山西、陝西各要隘並力堵截，速調駐熱河、綏遠之蒙古馬隊進口內衛京畿； 京師九門嚴緝姦宄，運通倉存糧入城； 並敕琦善、鄧紹良規复揚州、鎮江，為會剿江寧之計。 又疏陳順天防務，畫分汛地，舉行團練； 府屬各營舊隸總督管轄，請旨暫歸調遣。 未幾，賊犯天津，僧格林沁率師進剿，命順天府設糧台。 心存請發內帑三十二萬兩、京倉米二千六百石以給軍食，添制軍需火藥。 又偕團防大臣會議京城防守事宜，舉光祿寺卿宋晉、太僕寺卿王茂廕綜理其事，並詔允行。 時議行鈔幣，心存疏言：「軍營搭放票鈔，諸多窒礙。 鈔幣之法，施行當有次第，此時甫經頒發，並未試用，勢難驟用之軍營。 」詔斥為阻撓，即責籌次等施行之法，俾無阻滯。 會言官論通州捕役勾結土匪行劫，命刑部侍郎文瑞鞫得實，心存以徇庇革職。
When the Guangdong rebels invaded northward, Xincun memorialized that the rebel threat was drawing near. He urged holding the river line, stationing heavy forces directly south of the capital region, jointly blocking key passes in Henan, Shanxi, and Shaanxi, and quickly transferring Mongol cavalry stationed at Rehe and Suiyuan inside the passes to guard the capital region; the nine gates of the capital strictly hunt down traitors and spies, and grain stored at the Tongcang granary be transported into the city; and Qishan and Deng Shaoliang be ordered to plan the recovery of Yangzhou and Zhenjiang as a step toward a joint campaign against Jiangning. He also memorialized on Shuntian defense, delineating garrison zones and organizing militia training; the various camps under the prefecture having formerly been under the governor's jurisdiction, he requested an edict placing them temporarily under his command. Before long the rebels threatened Tianjin. Sengge Rinchen led troops in pursuit, and Shuntian was ordered to establish a grain supply office. Xincun requested disbursement of 320,000 taels from the inner treasury and 2,600 piculs of grain from the capital granary to supply the army, along with additional manufacture of military gunpowder. He also joined the militia defense ministers in conference on capital defense and recommended Court of Imperial Entertainments director Song Jin and Court of the Imperial Stud director Wang Maoyin to oversee the work. An edict approved the proposal. At the time there was discussion of issuing paper currency. Xincun memorialized: "Distributing paper notes in military camps involves many obstacles." The method of paper currency should be implemented in proper sequence. It has only just been issued and not yet tried, and it will be difficult to apply it suddenly in military camps. " An edict rebuked this as obstruction and immediately charged him to devise graded methods of implementation so there would be no delay. It happened that censors reported Tongzhou constables colluding with bandits to commit robbery. Vice Minister of Justice Wen Rui was ordered to investigate and found the charges true. Xincun was stripped of office for showing favoritism.
16
四年，起授吏部侍郎，調戶部，擢兵部尚書，調吏部。 六年，疏陳江南軍事，略曰：「蘇、松、常、太三府一州，及浙之杭、嘉、湖三府，久為賊所窺伺。 今寧國先陷，逼近宜興，向榮近守丹陽，溧水、句容相繼失守，宜責向榮嚴扼丹陽，令張國樑率精兵駐宜興扼東壩，別簡水師駐太湖，庶蘇、常兩郡可保無事。 又近有按畝捐輸，失政體，竭民財，請查明停止。 」是年冬，兼翰林院掌院學士，以吏部尚書協辦大學士，尋調戶部。
In the fourth year he was recalled and appointed vice minister of personnel, transferred to the Ministry of Revenue, promoted to minister of war, and transferred to the Ministry of Personnel. In the sixth year he memorialized on Jiangnan military affairs, writing in summary: "The three prefectures and one department of Su, Song, Chang, and Tai, and the three prefectures of Hang, Jia, and Hu in Zhejiang, have long been eyed by the rebels." Now Ningguo has fallen and the threat draws near Yixing. Xiang Rong is holding Danyang, but Lishui and Jurong have fallen in succession. Xiang Rong should be ordered strictly to hold Danyang, Zhang Guoliang should lead elite troops to encamp at Yixing and hold Dongba, and a separate naval force should be stationed on Lake Tai, so that the prefectures of Suzhou and Changzhou may be kept secure. Moreover, there has recently been per-mu donation levies, which violate proper governance, exhaust the people's wealth, and should be investigated and halted. " That winter he was also made chancellor of the Hanlin Academy and, as minister of personnel, was appointed associate grand secretary. Soon afterward he was transferred to the Ministry of Revenue.
17
八年，充上書房總師傅。 英法聯軍北犯，天津戒嚴。 心存疏請聖駕還宮，以定眾志，力言京師重地，不可駐外國領事； 長江形勢不可失； 綏芬邊地不可捐； 兵費不可再償； 傳教不可推廣； 和議難成，宜速進剿。 湖北巡撫胡林翼奏除漕務中飽之弊，請改徵折色。 心存力贊其議； 由部定章程五事，滿、漢兵糧折價支給，上下衙門一切陋規概行裁革焉。 拜體仁閣大學士，管理戶部。 與肅順同官不相能，屢乞病，不許。 九年，复固請，乃予告去職。
In the eighth year he was appointed chief tutor of the Upper Study. The Anglo-French allied armies invaded northward and Tianjin was placed under martial law. Xincun memorialized asking that the imperial procession return to the palace to steady public resolve. He forcefully argued that the capital was too vital a place to station foreign consuls; the strategic position of the Yangtze must not be lost; the Suifen borderlands must not be surrendered; war indemnities must not be paid again; missionary activity must not be expanded; peace talks will be hard to conclude, and a rapid advance in suppression is appropriate. Hubei governor Hu Linyi memorialized to eliminate graft in tribute transport affairs and requested conversion to commuted silver collection. Xincun strongly endorsed his proposal; the ministry fixed regulations in five articles, Manchu and Han military grain rations were paid at converted prices, and corrupt practices throughout the bureaucracy were in general abolished. He was made grand secretary of the Hall of Embodying Benevolence and put in charge of the Ministry of Revenue. Serving in the same office as Sushun, he could not get along with him and repeatedly pleaded illness to retire, but was not permitted. In the ninth year he again pressed his request firmly and was granted temporary retirement from office.
18
十年，戶部迭興大獄，肅順主之，多所羅織。 怡親王載垣等會鞫，謂司員忠麟、王熙震以短號鈔兌換長號，曾面啟心存，心存回奏部院事非一二人所能專政，斷無立談數語改舊章之理。 載垣等遂請褫頂帶歸案訊質，文宗鑑其誣，僅以失察議處，免傳訊，議降五級，改俟補官，革職留任。 復以五「宇」商號添支經費，心存駁令議減，未陳奏，司員即列入奏銷，下嚴議，革職留任。 是年秋，車駕將幸熱河，心存上疏切諫。
In the tenth year the Ministry of Revenue repeatedly launched major prosecutions directed by Sushun, and many people were ensnared. Prince Yi Zaiyuan and others jointly investigated and alleged that clerks Zhonglin and Wang Xizhen had exchanged short-number notes for long-number notes. They claimed they had personally reported this to Xincun, who replied that ministry affairs were not something one or two men could control, and that old regulations could certainly not be changed in a brief standing conversation. Zaiyuan and others then asked that Xincun be stripped of rank and returned for interrogation. Wenzong saw through the false charge and censured him only for lack of vigilance, exempted him from being summoned, proposed demotion by five ranks, then changed this to awaiting reassignment, and stripped him of office while retaining his duties. Again, because five merchant firms bearing the character Yu added expenditures, Xincun rejected the charges and ordered a discussion of reductions. Before he could memorialize, the clerks had already listed the sums in accounts for disbursement. Severe censure was imposed, and he was stripped of office while retaining his duties. That autumn, as the court prepared to move to Rehe, Xincun submitted a memorial urgently remonstrating against the move.
19
十一年，文宗崩於行在，梓宮還京，心存偕諸臣迎謁，特詔起用，以大學士銜管理工部。 疏舉人材，詔嘉其不失以人事君之義。 又疏言：「東南之民鄉義甚堅，各郡縣陷後，流亡渡江者，日夜思招練義勇，克復鄉里。 請敕曾國籓擇能辦賊者馳赴通州東台​​，收拾將散之人心，激勵方興之義旅，進搗蘇、常，退保下河。 上海一隅賦稅所出，宜取江海關無窮之利，以供曾國籓有用之兵。 」疏上，被嘉納。 同治元年，入直弘德殿，偕祁俊藻等授穆宗讀。 兩宮皇太后慎重師傅之選，倚畀彌篤。 是年冬，寢疾，子安徽巡撫同書方緣事繫獄，詔暫釋侍疾。 尋卒，優詔賜卹，稱其「品端學粹，守正不阿」，贈太保，入祀賢良祠，諡文端。 賜其孫曾源進士，曾榮舉人，曾純、曾桂並以原官即用，曾翰賜內閣中書。 逾年，文宗實錄告成，以心存曾充監修總裁，賜祭一壇。 子同書、同龢自有傳，同爵官湖北巡撫。
In the eleventh year Emperor Wenzong died at the traveling palace. When the coffin returned to the capital, Xincun joined other ministers in receiving and paying homage. A special edict recalled him to office with the nominal rank of grand secretary to head the Ministry of Works. In a memorial he recommended talent. An edict praised him for not failing in the duty of serving one's ruler through proper appointments. In another memorial he wrote: "The people of the southeast hold firmly to local loyalty. After each prefecture and county fell, those who fled across the Yangtze day and night thought of recruiting and training volunteers to recover their native places." He asked that Zeng Guofan be ordered to select men capable of dealing with the rebels and hurry to Tongzhou and Dongtai, rally hearts about to scatter, encourage volunteer forces just arising, advance against Suzhou and Changzhou, and fall back to defend the lower river route. The taxes produced from the Shanghai region should draw the inexhaustible profits of the Jianghaiguan customs to supply Zeng Guofan's useful troops. " When the memorial was submitted, it was praised and accepted. In the first year of the Tongzhi reign he was assigned to the Hall of Manifest Virtue and taught Muzong jointly with Qi Junzao and others. The two empress dowagers were careful in selecting tutors and relied on him ever more deeply. That winter he fell gravely ill. His son Tongshu, governor of Anhui, was imprisoned in connection with an official matter, and an edict temporarily released him to attend his father's illness. Soon he died. A gracious edict granted funeral compensation and praised him as "upright in character, pure in learning, steadfast and unyielding." He was posthumously made grand guardian, enshrined in the Shrine of Worthies, and given the posthumous title Wenduan. His grandson Zengyuan was granted jinshi status, Zengrong the status of provincial graduate, Zengchun and Zenggui immediate appointment at their former ranks, and Zenghan was granted the post of secretariat secretary. More than a year later, when Emperor Wenzong's Veritable Record was completed, a sacrificial altar was granted because Xincun had once served as supervising chief compiler. His sons Tongshu and Tonghe have their own biographies; Tongjue served as governor of Hubei.
20
彭蘊章，字詠莪，江蘇長洲人，尚書啟豐曾孫。 由舉人入貲為內閣中書，充軍機章京。 道光十五年，成進士，授工部主事，仍留直軍機處。 累遷郎中，歷鴻臚寺少卿、光祿寺少卿、順天府丞、通政司副使、宗人府丞。 督福建學政，遷左副都御史。 二十八年，疏言：「漕船衛官需索旗丁日益增多，沿途委員及漕運衙門、倉場花戶皆有費，慾減旗丁幫費，宜探本窮源。 又州縣辦漕，應令督撫察其潔己愛民者，每歲酌保一二員； 辦理不善者，劾一二員。 運漕官及坐糧廳如能潔己剔弊，準漕督、倉場保奏，不稱職者劾罷。 」下部議行。 擢工部侍郎，仍留學政任。 咸豐元年，命在軍機大臣上行走。 四年，調禮部，尋擢工部尚書。 五年，協辦大學士。 六年，拜文淵閣大學士，管理工部及戶部三庫事務，充上書房總師傅。
Peng Yunzhang, whose courtesy name was Yong'e, came from Changzhou in Jiangsu and was the great-grandson of Minister Qifeng. As a provincial graduate he purchased appointment as a secretariat secretary and served as a Grand Council clerk. In the fifteenth year of the Daoguang reign he passed the metropolitan examination, was appointed a director in the Ministry of Works, and remained on regular duty at the Grand Council. He was promoted in stages to bureau director and served as vice president of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, vice president of the Court of Imperial Entertainments, vice prefect of Shuntian, vice commissioner of the Office of Transmission, and vice director of the Imperial Clan Court. He served as educational commissioner of Fujian and was transferred to vice censor-in-chief of the left. In the twenty-eighth year he memorialized: "The demands of transport guard officers upon banner sailors grow daily. Commissioners along the route, the tribute transport offices, and granary brokers all take fees. To reduce the sailors' assistance fees, the root cause must be traced to its source." Moreover, in prefectures and counties handling tribute transport, governors and governors-general should observe those who are clean in themselves and care for the people, and each year recommend one or two officers as appropriate; and those who handle affairs poorly should have one or two impeached. Transport officials and grain-accounting offices who can remain clean and eliminate abuses may be recommended by the tribute transport supervisor and granary superintendent; those unfit for office should be impeached and dismissed. " The matter was referred to the ministries for discussion and implementation. He was promoted to vice minister of works while retaining his educational commission. In the first year of the Xianfeng reign he was ordered to serve above the Grand Council ministers. In the fourth year he was transferred to the Ministry of Rites and soon promoted to minister of works. In the fifth year he was appointed associate grand secretary. In the sixth year he was made grand secretary of the Hall of Literary Depth, put in charge of the Ministry of Works and the three treasuries of the Ministry of Revenue, and appointed chief tutor of the Upper Study.
21
八年，京師旱，糧價踴貴，旗民生計益艱，蘊章奏請撥款採米，允之。 复疏言：「自改用大錢，城中米貴，疊荷加恩賑濟，又加米折，然民生疾苦未見轉機。 臣聞兵丁所領止有實米二成，其餘折色定價，每石京錢四千至三千不等，大米一石市價京錢三十千。 持此折價買米，不過升斗。 民生之蹙，不獨在無銀，並在無米。 本年海運多於上年，可將兵米酌量加增。 又各營養育兵及鰥寡孤獨小口米不過四萬餘名，每名歲支一石六斗，擬請此項酌給米，毋庸折色。 自前年以來，有提存部庫採買銀，又存四川、山東、山西、河南、陝西解京米價銀，共有四十七萬餘兩，堪以採買米石，加放兵米。 又有河南停運節省運腳銀二萬兩，堪為轉運之用。 伏乞飭部採買，以資搭放，實於旗兵生計大有裨益。 」疏入，下部議行。
In the eighth year the capital suffered drought, grain prices soared, and banner people's livelihoods grew harder. Yunzhang memorialized requesting funds to purchase grain, and the request was approved. In another memorial he wrote: "Since large cash coins were adopted, rice in the city has grown expensive. Repeated gracious relief has been granted, and grain conversion added, yet the people's hardship shows no turn for the better." I have heard that soldiers receive only twenty percent in actual grain. The rest is fixed in commuted silver at from four thousand to three thousand capital cash per picul, while a picul of rice on the market costs thirty thousand capital cash. Using this converted price to buy rice yields only a sheng or a dou. The people's distress lies not only in lacking silver but also in lacking rice. This year's sea transport exceeds last year's, so military grain allotments may suitably be increased. Moreover, soldiers in nurturing camps and widows, orphans, and solitary dependents receiving grain number a little more than forty thousand persons, each receiving 1.6 piculs per year. I propose that this allotment be given in grain as appropriate without commuted silver. Since the year before last there has been silver retained in the ministry treasury for purchases, and also silver from Sichuan, Shandong, Shanxi, Henan, and Shaanxi submitted for capital grain prices, totaling more than 470,000 taels—sufficient to purchase grain and increase distribution of military grain. There is also 20,000 taels saved from suspended transport costs in Henan, sufficient for transfer expenses. I humbly ask that the ministry be ordered to purchase grain for distribution; this will truly greatly benefit the livelihood of banner soldiers. " When the memorial was submitted, it was referred to the ministries for discussion and implementation.
22
蘊章久直樞廷，廉謹小心，每與會議，必持詳慎。 鈔票、科場諸大獄，婉體調護，與肅順等意忤。 兩江總督何桂清素以才敏自負，蘊章誤信之，數於上前稱薦。 十年，江寧大營潰，蘊章猶言桂清可恃。 未幾，蘇、常相繼陷，桂清逮治。 文宗以蘊章無知人鑑，眷注浸衰。 適有足疾，扶掖入直，命毋庸在軍機大臣上行走，以示體恤。 尋奏乞罷職，出都就醫。 詔曰：「卿久任樞垣，備悉時事。 現在軍務如有見及，並採訪輿論民情，隨時具疏交地方官大吏代遞。 」蘊章密陳時務六則，報聞。 十一年，病痊，署兵部尚書，尋兼署左都御史。 同治元年，復以病乞休。 未幾，卒，依大學士例賜卹，諡文敬。 子祖賢，官至湖北巡撫。
Yunzhang had long served on the Grand Council. Frugal, cautious, and careful, he always maintained thorough prudence whenever he joined deliberations. In major cases involving paper notes and the examination fields he tactfully mediated and protected others, putting him at odds with Sushun and his allies. Liangjiang governor-general He Guiqing had always prided himself on quick talent. Yunzhang mistakenly trusted him and several times recommended him before the throne. In the tenth year the great camp at Jiangning collapsed, yet Yunzhang still said He Guiqing could be relied upon. Before long Suzhou and Changzhou fell in succession, and He Guiqing was arrested for trial. Wenzong, seeing that Yunzhang lacked discernment in judging men, gradually diminished his favor. It happened that he had a foot ailment and needed assistance to enter service. He was ordered no longer to serve above the Grand Council ministers as a mark of consideration. Soon he memorialized requesting to leave office and left the capital to seek medical treatment. An edict stated: "You have long served on the Grand Council and are thoroughly familiar with current affairs." If you have views on present military affairs, and gather public opinion and popular sentiment, memorialize at any time and submit through local officials and high provincial officers for forwarding. " Yunzhang secretly set forth six points on current affairs, and acknowledgment was returned. In the eleventh year, when his illness healed, he served as acting minister of war and soon concurrently as acting censor-in-chief of the left. In the first year of the Tongzhi reign he again pleaded illness to retire. Before long he died. Funeral compensation was granted according to the precedent for grand secretaries, and he was given the posthumous title Wenjing. His son Zuxian rose to the post of governor of Hubei.
23
論曰：文宗初政，杜受田以師傅最被信任，贊畫獨多。 祈俊藻、彭蘊章皆久領樞務，翁心存數論軍事，久筦度支。 三人者並與肅順不協，先後去位； 同治初元，聯翩復起。 俊藻、心存三朝耆碩，輔導衝主，一時清望所歸焉。
The commentary states: In the early years of Wenzong's reign, Du Shoutian as imperial tutor was most trusted and contributed the most counsel. Qi Junzao and Peng Yunzhang both long directed Grand Council affairs; Weng Xincun repeatedly discussed military affairs and long managed revenue and expenditure. All three were at odds with Sushun and left office in succession; at the beginning of the Tongzhi reign they rose again in succession. Junzao and Xincun were venerable elders of three reigns who guided a young sovereign; for a time the highest esteem of the age converged upon them.
