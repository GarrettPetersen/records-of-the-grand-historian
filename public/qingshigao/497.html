無錫二孝子啞孝子清興關外，俗純樸，愛親敬長，內愨而外嚴。 既定鼎，禮教益備。 定旌格，循明舊。 親存，奉侍竭其力； 親歿，善居喪，或廬於墓； 親遠行，萬里行求，或生還，或以喪歸。 友於兄弟，同居三五世以上，號義門，及諸義行，皆禮旌。 親病，刲股刳肝； 親喪，以身殉：皆以傷生有禁，有司以事聞，輒破格報可。 所以教民者，若是其周其密也。 國史承前例，撰次孝友傳，亦頗及諸義行。 合之方志甄錄、文家傳述，無慮千百人。 採其尤者，用沈約宋書例，為孝義傳。 事親存沒能盡禮； 或遘家庭之變，能不失其正； 或遇寇難、值水火，能全其親。 若殉親而死，或為親復仇，友於兄弟，同居三五世以上，及凡有義行者，各以類聚。 事同，以時次。 孝為二卷，友與義合一卷。 硃用純，字致一，江南崑山人。 父集璜，明季以諸生死難。 用純慕王裒攀柏之義，自號曰柏廬。 棄諸生，奉母。 其學確守程、硃，知行並進，而程於至敬。 來學者授以小學、近思錄。 仿白鹿洞規，設講約，從者皆興起。 居喪哀毀，嘗曰：「宰我欲短喪，吾黨皆以為怪，然可見古人喪禮之盡，必蔬水饘粥哭泣哀毀無苟弛。 若今人飲酒食肉不改其常，雖更三年，豈謂久哉？ 」晚作輟講語，又為治家格言，語平易而切至。 病將革，設先人位，拜於堂，告無罪，顧弟子曰：「學問在性命，事業在忠孝。 」乃卒。 用純與徐枋、楊無咎稱「吳中三高士」，皆明季死事之孤也。 吳蕃昌，字仲木，浙江海鹽人。 父麟徵，明季死難，蕃昌事所後母查孝，居喪，水漿不入口。 既殯，啜粥，不茹蔬果。 寢苫，不脫衰絰。 比葬，嘔血數升，逾小祥遂卒。
Wuxi had its pair of filial sons and its mute filial son; beyond the Pass, when the Qing dynasty arose, the people were plain and unadorned in custom, devoted to parents and respectful to elders, inwardly reserved and outwardly severe. After the founding of the dynasty was settled, ritual teaching became ever more fully provided. The criteria for imperial commendation were set, in accord with Ming practice. While parents were alive, one attended them with every ounce of one's strength; when they died, one observed mourning properly, sometimes building a mourning hut beside the grave; when parents had gone far away, one traveled ten thousand li to seek them, sometimes bringing them home alive, sometimes returning with their remains. Brotherly harmony, households living together for three to five generations or more and styled "gates of righteousness," and every other act of righteousness—all were honored by ritual commendation. When parents fell ill, cutting flesh from one's thigh or excising one's liver; when parents died, giving one's life in their stead—although self-injury was prohibited by law, whenever officials reported such cases, exceptional approval was invariably granted. In this way the instruction of the people was carried to such thoroughness and such fineness. The dynastic history, following earlier models, arranged biographies of filial sons and friendly brothers and also gave considerable attention to other righteous conduct. Together with what local gazetteers had selected and what literary families had recorded, there were certainly no fewer than a thousand such cases. From among them the most outstanding were chosen, and following Shen Yue's precedent in the Book of Song, biographies of filial and righteous conduct were written. Those who served their parents with complete ritual propriety whether they lived or had died; or those who met upheaval within the family yet did not lose their moral footing; or those who in the face of bandits, disaster, fire, or flood were able to save their parents whole. Those who died for their parents, or who avenged them, those who were devoted to brothers and lived together for three to five generations or more, and everyone who performed righteous acts were each gathered under its proper heading. When the deeds were of the same kind, they were arranged in chronological order. Filial conduct fills two scrolls; brotherly devotion and righteous conduct share one scroll together. Zhu Yongchun, whose style was Zhiyi, came from Kunshan in Jiangnan. His father Jihuang, a licentiate, perished in the dynastic collapse at the close of the Ming. Yongchun admired Wang Pou's devotion among the cypress trees and took for himself the sobriquet Cypress Hut. He gave up his licentiate standing and devoted himself to serving his mother. In scholarship he held fast to the Cheng brothers and Zhu Xi, advancing knowledge and practice together, and he took utmost reverence as his standard in all things. Students who came to him were instructed in the Elementary Learning and Reflections on Things at Hand. Following the White Deer Grotto regulations, he set up lecture covenants, and everyone who joined him was roused to earnest effort. While in mourning he was shattered by grief. He once said, "When Zai Wo wanted to shorten the mourning period, our school all thought it odd—but it shows how completely the ancients fulfilled mourning rites: only vegetables, water, and thin gruel, weeping and wasting away without the slightest careless slackening. If people today drink wine and eat meat just as they always do, even if three years go by, what kind of mourning is that?" In later life he wrote Words on Ceasing Lectures and also household maxims; his language was simple and penetrating. As his illness was nearing its end, he set out his ancestors' tablets, bowed in the hall, and declared himself free of guilt; then he turned to his disciples and said, "Scholarship rests in the cultivation of nature and life; true achievement rests in loyalty and filial piety. " With that he died. Yongchun, Xu Fang, and Yang Wujiu were known as "the three lofty scholars of Wu"; each was the son of a man who had died for the Ming cause. Wu Fanchang, whose style was Zhongmu, came from Haiyan in Zhejiang. His father Linzheng died in the Ming collapse; Fanchang served his stepmother, Lady Zha, with deep filial devotion, and during mourning he would not take even water or gruel. After the encoffining he took only thin gruel and would not eat vegetables or fruit. He slept on a rush mat and never removed his mourning weeds. By the time of the burial he had vomited several sheng of blood; not long after the lesser mourning anniversary he died.

His younger cousin Qian Mu, whose style was Baozhong. He devoted himself to the learning of the Cheng brothers and Zhu Xi. He served his mother, Lady Zhu, with filial devotion; in mourning he was so frail he could not rise even with a staff. When his illness lifted a little, he set about editing his father's posthumous writings by hand, only to fall ill again. As he prepared the burial, his grief moved everyone who passed along the road. Qian Mu had always been physically frail; he could bear no more and died. Both Fanchang and Qian Mu were friends of Zhang Lüxiang, who spoke highly of them.

時以孝著者，復有歸安沈磊，亦履祥友也。 磊事母嚴，母不御酒肉，磊力請，終不聽。 有疾，醫為言，乃御酒肉。 磊客授於外，弟子具時食，不忍食，以為母未嘗也。 弟子乃先以饋母，曰：「太君食矣。 」乃食，率以為常。 周靖，江南吳縣人。 父茂蘭，刺血上書明父順昌冤，事具明史。 靖少補諸生，事親能盡力。 茂蘭卒，擗踴哭泣，喪葬悉如禮。 三年不脫衰絰，不飲酒食肉。 小祥，有疾作，或謂在禮得飲酒食肉，靖不可。 靖素善作篆，或請題榜，亦以喪辭。 耿燿，河南太康人。 世農。 父應科，好施與，七世同居，顏其堂曰「效藝」。 兄光，明諸生，孝後母而教諸弟嚴，燿從之學，事必諮而後行。 明末，流寇屠太康，燿與弟炳舁母避河北，貿布以養。 母病，朝出暮歸，不解帶累月。 母卒，挽車歸母喪。 炳亦純謹，定興耿權與弟極以孝友聞，炳慕其為人，分田舍處之，孫奇逢為作三耿傳焉。 方寇至，光前卒，未葬，子於彝號泣守其柩不去，寇執之，推隕城下，傷腰膂，幾死。 寇退，歸掬土掩柩乃去。 縣飢，知縣餽以粟，散贍貧乏。 督僮蔬，任飢者刈以食。
Another man of the time noted for filial piety was Shen Lei of Gui'an, who was also a friend of Zhang Lüxiang. Lei was strict in serving his mother; she would not touch wine or meat, and though he pleaded earnestly, she never yielded. When she fell ill, the physician persuaded her, and only then would she take wine and meat. Lei taught students while living away from home; when his pupils set out his meal he could not bring himself to eat, for he thought his mother had not yet tasted such food. His pupils would first send food to his mother and then tell him, "Your mother has eaten." " Only then would he eat; this became his regular habit. Zhou Jing came from Wu County in Jiangnan. His father Maolan wrote a blood petition to vindicate his own father Zhou Shunchang; the full account appears in the History of the Ming. In youth Jing was appointed licentiate and served his parents with all his strength. When Maolan died, Jing beat his breast and wept aloud; the funeral and burial were conducted entirely according to ritual. For three years he never removed his mourning weeds and took neither wine nor meat. At the lesser mourning anniversary he fell ill; some said ritual now permitted wine and meat, but Jing would not agree. Jing was skilled in seal script; when people asked him to write inscriptions, he likewise refused on account of mourning. Geng Yao came from Taikang in Henan. His family had been farmers for generations. His father Yingke was generous in giving; seven generations lived under one roof, and he named their hall Following the Arts. His elder brother Guang, a Ming licentiate, was filial to their stepmother and stern in instructing the younger brothers; Yao studied under him and would consult Guang before undertaking anything. At the close of the Ming, bandits ravaged Taikang; Yao and his younger brother Bing shouldered their mother and fled north of the Yellow River, trading in cloth to support her. When his mother fell ill, he went out at dawn and returned at dusk, going for months without even loosening his belt. When his mother died, he himself drew the cart that bore her coffin home. Bing was likewise upright and careful. Geng Quan of Dingxing and his brother Ji were renowned for filial devotion and brotherly harmony; Bing admired them, divided fields and houses to lodge them, and Sun Qifeng wrote a joint biography of the three Geng brothers. When the bandits came, Guang had already died and lay unburied; his son Yu Yi wept and would not leave the coffin. The bandits seized him and hurled him from the wall, injuring his back and sides so severely that he nearly died. After the bandits withdrew, he came back, heaped earth over the coffin with his hands, and only then left. During a county famine the magistrate gave him grain, which he distributed to support the destitute. He had his servants plant vegetables and let the hungry harvest them for food.

同時有耿輔，虞城人。 奉母避寇開封，寇決河灌城，倚浮木負母以渡。 母卒，哀毀，緇衣粗食終其世。 李景濂，字亦週，浙江鄞縣人。 幼喪母，父再娶於何而卒。 何年少，媒氏欲奪之，景濂聞，伺於道，出椎擊之，歸告何。 何相與慟哭，誓相依終身。 何教景濂嚴，景濂事何甚謹。 何嗜酪，景濂日入市求之，端捧急趨，如鳥張翼。 市人怪而求其故，則皆歎其孝，為讓道。 何老病，景濂侍疾七年不怠。 何卒，景濂亦六十，廬墓三年，作孺子泣。 景濂明諸生，明亡，棄諸生去為醫。 汪灝，江南休寧人。 晨、日昂、日昇，其弟也。 父病咯血，灝年十六，割股和藥進，良愈。 後數年病足，晨割股煉為末，敷治亦愈。 又數年復咯血，晨复割臂以療。 更數年，疾大作，灝复割臂，勿瘳。 晨病，日昂泣曰：「吾兄割臂愈父，吾不能割以愈吾兄乎？ 」眾尼之。 懵且僕，匠治棺，日昇持匠斧斷指，血淋漓，調藥以飲晨。 有司表其門曰「一門四孝友」。
At the same time there was Geng Fu of Yucheng. He took his mother to Kaifeng to escape the bandits; when they breached the dikes and flooded the city, he bore her across on a floating log. When his mother died, he was shattered by grief; he wore plain black clothes and ate coarse food for the rest of his days. Li Jinglian, whose style was Yizhou, came from Yin County in Zhejiang. He lost his mother while still young; his father remarried a woman of the He clan and then died. Lady He was still young; a matchmaker tried to take her away. Jinglian heard of it, lay in wait on the road, struck the man with a mallet, and went home to tell Lady He. He and Lady He wept together and swore to depend on each other for the rest of their lives. Lady He raised him strictly, and he served her with scrupulous care. Lady He loved curds; every day Jinglian went to market for them, holding the bowl steady and hurrying along with arms spread like wings. People in the market wondered and asked why; when they learned the reason, they all marveled at his filial devotion and cleared a path for him. When Lady He grew old and fell ill, Jinglian nursed her for seven years without slackening. When Lady He died, Jinglian was himself sixty; he lived in a mourning hut by her grave for three years and wrote Lament of an Infant's Weeping. Jinglian had been a Ming licentiate; when the dynasty fell he gave up that standing and took up medicine. Wang Hao came from Xiuning in Jiangnan. Chen, Ri'ang, and Risheng were his younger brothers. When his father coughed blood, the sixteen-year-old Hao cut flesh from his thigh, mixed it into medicine, and gave it to him; his father recovered. Years later, when his father suffered from a foot ailment, Chen cut flesh from his thigh, refined it to powder, applied it as a poultice, and he recovered as well. Several years later the coughing of blood returned; Chen again cut flesh from his arm to heal him. Several years later the sickness worsened sharply; Hao cut flesh from his arm once more, yet his father did not recover. When Chen fell ill, Ri'ang wept and said, "My brother cut his arm to save our father—why shouldn't I cut mine to save my brother?" Everyone held him back. Chen lay dazed and collapsed while a carpenter worked on a coffin. Risheng seized the carpenter's axe and cut off his finger; blood streaming down, he prepared medicine and gave it to Chen to drink. The authorities posted an honorary tablet at their gate proclaiming "One Family, Four Filial Brothers."

錢塘吳瑗及弟琦、璠、琰相友愛，年皆逾九十。 江蘇華亭姜應龍，應龍子世璜，世璜子文樞，文樞子超萃，超萃子懷權，懷權子栻，六世皆以孝行旌，人尤以為難。 黃農，江南元和人。 父袞，諸生。 農年十餘，母吳病六年，農侍疾不懈。 母卒，慟屢絕，坐臥母柩側。 袞客授於外，攜農俱。 久之，察其枕漬淚若膏，貌癯然如初喪。 袞客授稍遠家，農歸，五日一往省，袞止之，則私伺門外問安否，衣服器用，時其寒暑具以往。 一夕，心悸，走省，袞得暴疾，舁以歸。 會除夕禱神，原減算益父，袞愈。 農三十餘而卒，妻金，亦賢孝。 曹亨，陝西鎮安人。 年十一喪母，不能具棺，號泣於路，乞自鬻為斂。 或與之金，葬母畢，即詣其家執役終身。 黃嘉章，湖南桂陽人。 吳三桂之亂，從父避兵連珠崖。 父歿，兄嘉林年十六，嘉章亦年十一，自鬻以葬父。 嘉林稍長，力為傭，得錢贖嘉章還，兄弟相友愛。 鄭明允，江南歙縣人。 康熙間，耿精忠兵至，明允侍母抱譜牒及先世遺筆入山。 賊大索山中，明允夜負母匿僻塢，還挈二子，未至，霧溢山，虎聲震林木，納二子石穴中，疾趨侍母。 賊退，二子亦無恙。 兄病，視湯藥不去側。 及亡，每慟輒絕。 與其戚同賈，失其貲，明允發橐金盡與之。 族子縊客舍，明允為坐守達曙，白於官，出私財以斂。 有友盪其貲，困甚，明允罄所有佽之，無難色。 明允世業醫，精而不試，曰：「十得九，猶有一誤。 」業賈終其身。 劉宗洙，字長源； 弟恩廣，字錫三：湖北襄城人。 父漢臣，明季從軍。 襄城破，被數創，幾殆。 恩廣兩耳斷，號泣負以歸。 宗洙方走避寇，聞父難，往赴，賊截其耳鼻。 居數年，父病，嘗糞，時稱襄城「嘗糞孝子」。 父歾，與季弟宗泗同居，俄與恩廣皆得官，以母老不出。 母歾，恩廣嘔血至篤疾。 或慰解，曰：「勿復言，五內裂矣！ 」遂卒。 宗洙積哀兼痛弟，亦嘔血卒。
Wu Yuan of Qiantang and his brothers Qi, Fan, and Yan were deeply devoted to one another; each lived past ninety. Jiang Yinglong of Huating, Jiangsu, was followed by a line of filial heirs—his son Shihuang, then Wenshu, Chaoqi, Huaiquan, and Shi—for six generations honored for filial conduct, a feat people considered exceptionally rare. Huang Nong came from Yuanhe in Jiangnan. His father Gun had been a licentiate. When Nong was in his early teens, his mother Lady Wu fell ill for six years; he nursed her without ever letting up. When she died, he wept until he lost consciousness again and again, refusing to leave her coffin day or night. Gun taught as a hired tutor away from home and brought Nong with him. Before long Gun noticed that the boy's pillow was soaked with tears and that he looked as thin and grief-stricken as if his mother had just died. When Gun took a post farther from home, Nong came back every five days to check on him. Gun forbade it, so the boy would linger outside the door instead, asking after his father's health and sending in clothing and supplies according to the season. One night a sudden dread seized him; he rushed to see his father and found Gun stricken with a sudden illness, so they carried him home. On New Year's Eve he prayed to the gods, offering to shorten his own life in exchange for his father's; Gun recovered. Nong died in his thirties; his wife, Lady Jin, was likewise virtuous and devoted. Cao Heng came from Zhen'an in Shaanxi. He lost his mother at eleven; too poor to afford a coffin, he wept in the streets, offering to sell himself into bondage to pay for her burial. A benefactor gave him the money; once his mother was buried, he went to that man's home and served him for the rest of his life. Huang Jiazhang came from Guiyang in Hunan. During Wu Sangui's rebellion, he fled with his father to Lianzhuyan to escape the fighting. When their father died, Jialin was sixteen and Jiazhang only eleven; Jiazhang sold himself into servitude to pay for the burial. As Jialin matured, he worked as a hired laborer, saved enough to buy his brother's freedom, and the two remained devoted to each other. Zheng Mingyun came from She County in Jiangnan. During the Kangxi reign, when Geng Jingzhong's soldiers arrived, Mingyun escorted his mother into the mountains, clutching the family genealogy and his ancestors' writings. When rebel troops scoured the mountains, Mingyun carried his mother under cover of night to a hidden ravine, then returned for his two sons. Before he reached them, fog rolled over the hills and tiger roars shook the forest; he hid the boys in a stone cave and raced back to his mother. When the rebels withdrew, both sons emerged unscathed. When his elder brother fell ill, he tended his medicine and never left the bedside. After his death, each burst of grief left him unconscious. A relative who had gone into business with him lost his stake; Mingyun emptied his purse and gave him the entire sum. When a clansman's son hanged himself at an inn, Mingyun kept vigil until dawn, notified the authorities, and paid for the burial out of his own pocket. 崔長生，江南邳州人。 生而瘖，手又攣。 為傭養父母，出入必面。 歲大祲，乞食於市，得糟糠，上父母，自食草根木實以活。 拾字紙，得遺金，待失者踰月不得。 乃易母彘飼之，茁壯蕃息，為父母治送死之具。 喪父母，舁葬於中野，遂去，不知所終。
Cui Changsheng came from Pizhou in Jiangnan. Born mute, his hands were also crippled. He worked as a hired laborer to support his parents; going out and coming in he always bowed to them. In a year of great famine he begged food in the market; what chaff and husks he got he gave to his parents, eating roots and tree fruit himself to survive. While gathering waste paper he found lost gold; he waited for the owner over a month without anyone coming. So he traded for a sow and raised pigs; they flourished and multiplied, and he provided funeral goods for his parents. After his parents died he carried them on a bier and buried them in the open countryside, then departed—no one knew his end.
73
榮孝子，河南遂平人。 幼痴聾，無名。 家本饒，後中落，貧甚。 父卒，無所居，奉母居棲流鋪。 出乞食，擇所得供母，自食其餘。 得少，則但供母，而自忍飢歸。 見母必叩頭，食必跪進。 母食則起而舞，食減則泣。 母或故減食以食子，則泣不受。 母七十餘卒，縣人為具斂，朝暮泣，終其身。 吏以孝子旌其楣，亦不知孝子為何名也。 卒亦七十餘。
The Filial Son Rong came from Suiping in Henan. From youth he was simple-minded and deaf, without a personal name. His family had once been wealthy but later declined into extreme poverty. When his father died, with nowhere to live, he brought his mother to dwell in a vagrants' shelter. Going out to beg, he chose the best of what he got to supply his mother and ate the remainder himself. When he got little he provided only for his mother and returned home hungry himself. Whenever he saw his mother he kowtowed; when offering food he always knelt to present it. When his mother ate he rose and danced; when she ate less he wept. When his mother deliberately reduced her food to give some to her son, he wept and would not accept. When his mother died past seventy the county people provided her funeral; morning and evening he wept—for the rest of his life. Officials erected a filial-son plaque on his door lintel, yet no one knew what the filial son's name was. He too died past seventy.
74
無錫二孝子，皆失其姓氏。 其一瞽，磨粉為業，事母至孝，竭力供甘旨。 年至四十餘復明，人皆異之。 其一啞，行乞得錢以養母，必具酒脯。 母卒，食必祭，祭必伏地號痛。 既葬，哭於墓，見者皆感。
There were two filial sons in Wuxi, both surnames lost. One was blind and made a living grinding flour; filial toward his mother to the utmost, he exerted himself to provide delicacies. Past forty his sight was restored—all marveled at it. One was mute; he begged for money to support his mother and always provided wine and dried meat. When his mother died, before every meal he made offerings, and at each offering he prostrated himself on the ground wailing in pain. After burial he wept at the grave—all who saw were moved.
75
啞孝子，無姓氏，或曰雲南昆明人。 家有母，老矣，行乞以養。 得食必奉母，母食然後食。 母或怒，嬉戲拜且舞，必母樂乃已。 得錢密投諸井，母卒，鄉人有欲醵錢以助斂者，與如井，數數指水中，鄉人為出錢，營殮且葬。 事畢，遠遊不知所終。
The Mute Filial Son, surname unknown, said by some to be from Kunming in Yunnan. He had a mother who was old; he begged alms to support her. Whenever he got food he always offered it to his mother; only after she had eaten would he eat himself. When his mother grew angry he would play games, bow, and dance—he would not stop until she was pleased. When he got money he secretly dropped it into a well; when his mother died and villagers wished to collect money to help with burial, he led them to the well and repeatedly pointed at the water—the villagers produced the money and arranged encoffining and burial. When the matter was complete he traveled far away—no one knew his end.
