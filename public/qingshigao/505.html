王來咸，字征南，浙江鄞縣人。 先世居奉化，自祖父居鄞，至來咸徙同，從同里單思南受內家拳法。 內家者，起於宋武當道士張三峰，其法以靜制動，應手即僕，與少林之主於搏人者異，故別少林為外家。 其後流傳於秦、晉間，至明中葉，王宗岳為最著，溫州、陳州同受之，遂流傳於溫州。 嘉靖間，張松溪最著，松溪之徒三四人，寧波葉繼美為魁，遂流傳於寧波。 得繼美之傳者，曰吳崑山、周雲泉、陳貞石、孫繼槎及思南，各有授受。 思南從征關白，歸老於家，以術教，頗惜其精微。 來咸從樓上穴板窺之，得其梗概。 以銀卮易美檟奉思南，始盡以不傳者傳之。
Wang Laixian, whose style was Zhengnan, came from Yin County in Zhejiang. The family had originally lived in Fenghua, but his grandfather settled in Yin, and Laixian later moved to Tongqiu and studied internal-school boxing under his fellow townsman Shan Sinan. The internal school traces back to the Wudang Daoist Zhang Sanfeng of the Song. Its art subdues motion through stillness, and attackers fall the moment one engages them. Because this differs from Shaolin's emphasis on overpowering an opponent by force, Shaolin is treated as the external school. The art later circulated through Qin and Jin. In the mid-Ming, Wang Zongyue became its most famous exponent; both Wenzhou and Chenzhou received the tradition, after which it flourished in Wenzhou. In the Jiajing era Zhang Songxi stood at the height of fame. He had three or four disciples, with Ye Jimei of Ningbo as their leader, and the tradition thereafter took root in Ningbo. Jimei's disciples included Wu Kunshan, Zhou Yunquan, Chen Zhenshi, Sun Jiqiao, and Sinan, each maintaining a separate teaching line. Sinan had served in the campaign against Konose, and in retirement he taught the art but was reluctant to pass on its most refined secrets. Laixian drilled holes in the floor above and watched the lessons, learning the art in outline. He gave Sinan a silver cup in exchange for a fine coffin as a gift, and only then did Sinan teach him everything he had previously withheld.
5
來咸為人機警，不露圭角，非遇甚困不發。 凡搏人皆以其穴，死穴、暈穴、啞穴，一切如銅人圖法。 有惡少侮之，為所擊，數日不溺，謝過，乃得如故。 牧童竊學其法，擊伴侶，立死。 視之，曰：「此暈穴。 」不久果甦。 任俠，嘗為人報仇，有致金以讎其弟者，絕之，曰：「此以禽獸待我也！ 」明末，嘗入伍為把總，從錢肅樂起兵浙東，事敗，隱居於家。 慕其藝者，多通殷勤，皆不顧。 鋤地擔糞，安於食貧。 未嘗讀書，與士大夫談論蘊藉，不知為粗人。 黃宗羲與之遊，同入天童，僧少焰有膂力，四五人不能掣其手，稍近來咸，蹶然負痛。 來咸嘗曰：「今人以內家無可炫耀，於是以外家羼之，此學行衰矣！ 」因為宗羲論述其學源流。 康熙八年，卒，年五十三。 宗羲子百家從之學，演其說為內家拳法一卷，百家後無所傳焉。
Laixian was sharp and alert by nature, kept his abilities hidden, and would not use his art unless he was pushed to the limit. In every fight he struck the vital points—death points, fainting points, and mute points—exactly as prescribed on the bronze acupoint manikin. When a bully harassed him, Laixian struck him in a vital point, and the man could not urinate for days until he apologized, after which he returned to normal. A shepherd boy who had secretly picked up the technique struck a playmate, who collapsed and died at once. Laixian examined him and said, "That was a fainting point. " Before long the boy did revive. A man of chivalry, he sometimes took revenge for others; once someone offered him gold to kill the man's younger brother, but he refused, saying, "You treat me like an animal! " In the late Ming he enlisted as a platoon commander and joined Qian Sule's uprising in eastern Zhejiang; when the effort collapsed he withdrew into private life. Those who admired his skill often sent him gifts and courtesies, but he ignored them all. He hoed the earth and carried manure, content with a poor man's fare. Though he had never studied the classics, his talk with scholar-officials was cultivated and restrained, and no one would have taken him for a coarse fellow. Huang Zongxi befriended him and once visited Tiantong Monastery with him. The monk Shaoyan was immensely strong—four or five men together could not break his grip—but when he came near Laixian he suddenly crumpled in pain. Laixian once remarked, "People today find the internal school too subtle to show off, so they blend in external-school techniques—that is why the tradition is in decline! " He then laid out for Zongxi the lineage and sources of the art. He died in the eighth year of the Kangxi reign, at the age of fifty-three. Zongxi's son Baijia studied under him and set out his teachings in a single fascicle entitled Internal-School Boxing, but Baijia later left no successor.
6
清中葉，河北有太極拳，云其法出於山西王宗岳，其法式論解，與百家之言相出入。 至清末，傳習者頗眾雲。
By the mid-Qing, Taiji boxing had appeared in Hebei, traditionally traced to Wang Zongyue of Shanxi; its forms and theoretical writings differed in places from what Baijia had recorded. By the late Qing a great many people were practicing and teaching it.
7
褚士寶，字復生，江南上海人。 家素封，膂力過人，好技擊，遊學四方。 與畢昆陽、武君卿為友，遂精槍法，名曰四平槍，旋轉如風，人莫能近。 同邑有張擎者，虎頸板肋，力舉百鈞，橫行為閭里患，眾請士寶除之。 同飲酒，擎自誇其勇，酒酣，攘臂作勢，士寶徐以箸點其胸，曰：「子盍坐而言乎？ 」擎遂默然，少頃辭去，越日，死於橋亭。 明季福王南渡，兵部員外郎何剛薦士寶為伏波營游擊。 未之官，南​​都陷，終老於家。 所傳弟子有王聖蕃、池天榮。 天榮又傳浙江提督喬照。 其槍譜二種及治傷藥酒方，世猶有藏之者。
Chu Shibao, whose style was Fusheng, came from Shanghai in the lower Yangtze region. Born into a wealthy household, he possessed extraordinary strength, loved the martial arts, and wandered far and wide to study them. Through his friendship with Bi Kunyang and Wu Junqing he mastered spear fighting, known as the Four-Level Spear—its whirl was like wind, and no opponent could close on him. A townsman named Zhang Qing had a bull neck and barrel chest and could lift enormous weights; his bullying made him a neighborhood menace, and the community begged Shibao to deal with him. They shared a drinking bout. Qing bragged about his prowess, and when he was drunk he rolled up his sleeves threateningly. Shibao gently tapped his chest with his chopsticks and said, "Why don't you sit down and talk this over? " Qing fell silent, left shortly afterward, and was found dead the next day at the bridge pavilion. In the late Ming, when the Prince of Fu fled south, He Gang of the Ministry of War recommended Shibao for the post of mobile-detachment commander in the Fubo Camp. He never assumed the post; Nanjing fell, and he lived out his life at home. His disciples included Wang Shengfan and Chi Tianrong. Tianrong passed the art on to Qiao Zhao, the Zhejiang provincial commander-in-chief. Two of his spear manuals and his recipes for medicinal wines to treat wounds are still kept in private collections.
8
馮行貞，字服之，江南常熟人。 父班，以文學者。 兄行賢，傳其學。 行貞少亦喜讀書，工小詞，性倜儻不羈。 善射，能以後矢落前矢，投石子於百步外無不中。 實雞卵殼以礦灰，遇劇盜，輒先發雞卵中其目。 山東響馬老瓜賊為行旅患，聞馮氏名，莫敢攖。 從休寧程打虎及張老受槍法，馳突無敵。 山行遇虎，以短槍斃之。 嘗為客報仇。 康熙中，從康親王杰書軍南征，有功，當得官，尋棄歸。 僑居吳中婁門外村落，以經書教授，詩畫自娛。 年七十餘，卒。 以槍法授同縣陶元淳，元淳後無傳者。
Feng Xingzhen, whose style was Fuzhi, came from Changshu in the lower Yangtze region. His father Ban was a scholar of letters. His elder brother Xingxian carried on their father's scholarship. As a youth Xingzhen also loved books, wrote polished short lyrics, and had a bold, unconstrained temperament. He was a superb archer who could split one arrow in flight with the next, and at a hundred paces his thrown stones never missed their mark. He packed eggshells with powdered lime and, when confronted by violent robbers, would hurl them first to blind his foes. The Shandong mounted bandits known as the Old Melon Thieves terrorized travelers, yet once they heard the Feng name they dared not molest them. Under Cheng Dahu of Xiuning and Old Zhang he mastered the spear and was unmatched in the charge. While crossing the mountains he once met a tiger and brought it down with a short spear. He once took revenge on behalf of a patron. In the Kangxi reign he served under Prince Kang Jieshu in the southern campaigns, earned distinction, and was offered an official post, but soon resigned and went home. He settled in a village beyond Lou Gate in Wu, taught the classics for a living, and passed his days in poetry and painting. He died in his seventies. He taught his spear art to Tao Yuanchun of his home county, but Yuanchun left no heir to the tradition.
9
甘鳳池，江南江寧人。 少以勇聞。 康熙中，客京師貴邸。 力士張大義者慕其名，自濟南來見。 酒酣，命與鳳池角，鳳池辭，固強之。 大義身長八尺餘，脛力強大，以鐵裹拇，騰躍若風雨之驟至。 鳳池卻立倚柱，俟其來，承以手，大義大呼僕，血滿鞾，解視，拇盡嵌鐵中。 即墨馬玉麟，長軀大腹，以帛約身，緣牆升木，捷於猱。 客揚州巨賈家，鳳池後至，居其上。 玉麟不平，與角技，終日無勝負。 鳳池曰：「此勁敵，非張大義比！ 」明日又角，數蹈其瑕，玉麟直前擒鳳池，以駢指卻之，玉麟僕地，慚遁。 鳳池嘗語人曰：「吾力不逾中人，所以能勝人者，善藉其力以製之耳。 」手能破堅，握鉛錫化為水。 又善導引術，同里譚氏子病瘵，醫不效，鳳池於靜室窒牖戶，夜與合背坐，四十九日而痊。
Gan Fengchi came from Jiangning in the lower Yangtze region. He was known for bravery from an early age. During the Kangxi reign he was a guest in the households of great families in the capital. A wrestler named Zhang Dayi, who admired his fame, traveled from Jinan to meet him. After much wine they insisted he wrestle Fengchi; Fengchi refused, but they would not let him decline. Dayi stood over eight feet tall, with tremendous leg strength; he bound iron around his thumbs and sprang about like a sudden squall. Fengchi drew back against a pillar, waited for the charge, and caught him with his hand. Dayi shouted and collapsed, blood soaking his boots; when they unwrapped his thumbs, both were driven into the iron bindings. Ma Yulin of Jimo was tall and broad-bellied; swathed in cloth strips, he could run up walls and trees faster than a monkey. He was staying with a wealthy Yangzhou merchant when Fengchi arrived afterward and was given the better quarters upstairs. Yulin resented the slight and challenged him to a match; they wrestled all day without a decision. Fengchi said, "Here is a real opponent—not in Zhang Dayi's class! " They wrestled again the next day. Fengchi repeatedly found openings; Yulin lunged to grab him, but Fengchi drove him back with a two-finger strike. Yulin fell and slunk away in humiliation. Fengchi once said, "My strength is no greater than an average man's. I win because I know how to borrow an opponent's force and turn it against him. " His grip could shatter hard objects, and when he closed his hand on lead or tin they melted as if they were water. He was also adept at daoyin breathing exercises. When a neighbor's son in the Tan clan fell ill with consumption and doctors could not cure him, Fengchi sealed a quiet room and sat back to back with the boy each night; after forty-nine days the youth recovered.
10
喜任俠，接人和易，見者不知為賁、育。 雍正中，浙江總督李衛捕治江寧顧云如邪術不軌獄，株連百數十人，鳳池亦被逮，讞擬大辟。 世宗於此獄從寬，未盡駢誅。 或云鳳池年八十餘，終於家。 江湖間流傳其佚事多荒誕，著其可信者。
He loved acts of chivalry and treated everyone with easy warmth; strangers meeting him would never have guessed he was a man of Herculean strength. In the Yongzheng reign, Zhejiang governor-general Li Wei prosecuted the Jiangning case of Gu Yunru's sorcery and sedition, implicating scores of people. Fengchi was arrested as well, and the sentence called for execution. The Yongzheng Emperor showed mercy in the affair, and not all those implicated were executed together. Some accounts say Fengchi lived past eighty and died at home. Countless fanciful stories about him circulate in popular lore; only those that seem credible are recorded here.
11
曹竹齋，以字行，佚其名，福建人。 老而貧，賣卜揚州市。 江、淮間健者，莫能當其一拳，故稱曹一拳。 少年以重幣請其術，不可。 或怪之，則曰：「此皆無賴子，豈當授藝以助虐哉？ 拳棒，古先舞蹈之遺也，君子習之，所以調血脈，養壽命，其粗乃以御侮。 必彼侮而我御之，若以之侮人，則反為人所禦而自敗矣。 無賴子以血氣事侵凌，其氣浮於上，而立腳虛，故因其奔赴之勢，略藉手而僕耳。 一身止兩拳，拳之大才數寸，焉足衛五尺之軀，且以接四面乎？ 惟養吾正氣，使周於吾身，彼之手足近吾身，而吾之拳，即在其所近之處。 以彼虛囂之氣，與吾靜定之氣接，則自無幸矣。 故至精是術者，其徵有二：一則精神貫注，而腹背皆乾滑如臘肉； 一則氣體健舉，而額顱皆肥澤如粉粢。 是皆血脈流行，應乎自然，內充實而外和平，犯而不校者也。 」嘉慶末，歿於揚州，年八十餘。
Cao Zhuzhai was known only by his style; his given name is unrecorded. He came from Fujian. He grew old in poverty and earned his living telling fortunes in Yangzhou. No brawler along the Yangtze and Huai could withstand a single blow from him, and he was known as Cao One-Fist. Young men offered him large sums to teach his art, but he refused. When people questioned him, he replied, "They are all good-for-nothings. Why should I teach them skills that would only help them bully others? Boxing and staff work descend from the ritual dances of antiquity. A gentleman practices them to harmonize the circulation and prolong life; only in their cruder application are they meant for self-defense. The art is for when others provoke you, not for provoking them; use it to bully others and they will turn it against you and you will bring about your own ruin. Ruffians attack in a surge of brute courage; their energy rides high and their stance is hollow. One need only borrow the momentum of their charge and with a light touch send them sprawling. A man has only two fists, each a few inches wide—how could they alone defend a five-foot body against threats from every direction? Cultivate your upright qi until it fills the whole body; then wherever an opponent's limbs come near you, your fist is already there to meet them. When their hollow, blustering force meets your calm, grounded presence, they cannot prevail. The consummate practitioner shows two signs: first, his spirit is wholly focused and his belly and back are dry and smooth as cured meat; second, his breath is vigorous and lifted, and his brow and crown are full and lustrous like fresh rice cakes. Both show the blood circulating in harmony with nature—inwardly full, outwardly serene, the man who is provoked yet does not strike back in anger. " He died in Yangzhou toward the end of the Jiaqing reign, aged over eighty.
12
潘佩言，亦以字行，安徽歙縣人。 以槍法著稱，稱潘五先生。 其言：「槍長九尺，而桿圓四五寸，然槍入手，則全身悉委於桿。 故必以小腹貼桿，使主運； 後手必盡錞，以虎口實擫之； 前手必直，令盡勢。 以其掌根與後手虎口反正擰絞，而虛指使主導。 兩足亦左虛右實，進退相任以趨勢。 使槍尖、前手尖、前足尖、鼻尖五尖相對，而五尺之身，自託廕於數寸之桿，遮閉周匝，敵仗無從入犯矣。 其用，有戳、有打； 其法，曰二、曰叉。 二以取人，叉以拒人。 此叉則彼二，此二則彼叉。 叉二循環，兩槍尖交如繞指，分寸間，出入百合，不得令相附。 桿一附，則有僕者，故曰'千金難買一聲響'。 手同則爭目，目同則爭氣。 氣之運也，久暫稍殊，而勝敗分焉。 故其術為至靜。 」「吾授徒百數，而莫能傳吾術。 吾之術，受於師者才十之三，其十之七，則授徒時被其非法相取之勢迫而得之於無意者也。 是故名師易求，佳徒難訪。 佳徒意在得師，以天下之大，求之無不如意者。 至名師求徒，雖遇高資妙質，足以授道，而非其志之所存，不能耐勞苦以要之永久，則百貢而百見卻矣。 」
Pan Peiyan, also known by his style name, came from She County in Anhui. He was renowned for his spear art and was known as Master Pan the Fifth. He said, "A spear is nine feet long and the shaft four or five inches thick, yet once you take hold of it your entire body commits itself to the shaft. The lower abdomen must press against the shaft and govern every motion; the rear hand must reach its full extension, the web of the thumb clamping the shaft; the leading hand must stay straight to release the full force. The base of the leading palm and the rear hand's grip twist against each other, while the open fingers guide the action. The feet too keep the left empty and the right weighted, advance and retreat working together to carry the momentum. Line up the spear point, the leading hand, the front foot, and the nose—the five points aligned—so that your five-foot frame shelters itself behind a shaft only inches thick, sealing every angle, and no enemy weapon can find a way in. In application it may thrust or strike; The method comprises two techniques, called the strike and the cross. The strike seizes the opponent; the cross blocks him off. When you cross, he strikes; when you strike, he crosses. Strike and cross alternate in endless cycle; the spear points weave together like linked fingers, thrusting and withdrawing within inches through a hundred passes, and must never be allowed to lock. Let the shafts stick once and someone falls—hence the proverb, 'No sum in gold can buy the sound of a clean strike.' When hands are evenly matched, the contest shifts to the eyes; when eyes are evenly matched, it shifts to breath and spirit. Breath and stamina differ only slightly over time, yet that small margin decides who wins and who loses. That is why the art demands the utmost calm. "I have taught more than a hundred disciples, yet none could truly pass on my art. Only three-tenths of my art came from my master; the other seven-tenths I learned unawares while teaching, when disciples tried illicit tricks and the pressure of their gambits forced the answers from me. That is why a famous master is easy to find, but a worthy disciple is hard to come by. A worthy disciple wants only to find a master; in all the world he can search until he finds one who satisfies him. When a famous master seeks a disciple, he may meet someone gifted enough to receive the full transmission, yet unless the disciple shares his purpose and can endure hardship for the long term, the master will turn away a hundred times for every hundred courtesies offered. "

佩言與竹齋同時處揚州，後歸歙，不知所終。
Peiyan and Zhuzhai were in Yangzhou at the same time; Peiyan later returned to She County, and his later fate is unknown.
14
江之桐，字蘭崖，安徽和州人。 年十餘歲，傭於江寧賣餅家，嗜讀書，其主人異之。 招至家，居之樓上數年，讀左傳、國語、戰國策、史記、漢書、三國志畢。 乃謝主人去，自設小肆於市。 更習武藝，手​​臂刀矛，皆務實用，變通成法。 且讀書，且習藝，讀稍倦，則趫舉翕張，以作其氣。 已而默坐，以凝其神，晝夜無間。 至百日乃睡，睡十餘日，復如之。 讀史善疑，質之儒生，往往無以答。 其藝通綿長、俞刀、程棓、瓘嵋十八棍，多取洪門，敵硬鬥強，以急疾為用。 复及陣圖、形勢、器械，皆有理解。
Jiang Zhitong, whose style name was Lanya, came from Hezhou in Anhui. While still in his early teens he worked for a cake shop in Jiangning; he loved books, and his employer took notice. The man took him in and kept him on the upper floor for years until he had read through the Zuo Commentary, Discourses of the States, Stratagems of the Warring States, Records of the Historian, History of the Han, and Records of the Three Kingdoms. He then thanked his host and left to open a small shop in the market. He also trained in martial arts, working hand, arm, blade, and spear for practical effect and adapting the standard forms. He read and trained at once; when study tired him he would leap and stretch, flexing his limbs to stir his energy. Then he would sit in silence to focus his mind, day and night without pause. He would stay awake for a hundred days, then sleep for ten or more, and begin again. He read history with a skeptical eye and would press scholars with his doubts, often leaving them without a reply. He mastered the Mianchang, Yudao, Chengbang, and Guanmei eighteen-staff styles, drawing chiefly on Hongmen traditions—meeting force with force and prizing speed above all. He also understood battle formations, terrain, and weapons.

Western science had not yet taken firm root in China, and experimental equipment was almost impossible to find. Shou and Hua Hengfang of Jintan hunted them down together and at first found only eleven; through painstaking study he often reasoned his way to the truth. Unable to buy a glass prism, he ground a crystal seal into a triangle and proved that light separates into seven colors. Knowing that bullets follow a parabolic trajectory, he wondered whether shooting uphill or downhill made a difference and set up targets at many ranges to find out; most of his scientific breakthroughs came at such cost. In time he came to grasp the fundamentals of Western science and proved especially gifted at mechanical invention. In 1861 he joined Grand Secretary Zeng Guofan's forces and helped establish arsenals at Anqing and later Nanjing.
31
壽與蘅芳及吳嘉廉、龔芸棠試造木質輪船，推求動理，測算汽機，蘅芳之力為多； 造器罝機，皆出壽手製，不假西人，數年而成。 長五十餘尺，每一時能行四十餘裡，名之曰黃鵠。 國籓激賞之，招入幕府，以奇才異能薦。 既而設製造局於上海，百事草創，壽於船砲槍彈，多所發明。 自製強水棉花藥、汞爆藥。
Shou, Hengfang, Wu Jialian, and Gong Yuntang experimented with a wooden steamer, working out the laws of motion and calculating the steam engine, with Hengfang contributing most of the theory; but Shou built the machinery and installed the engines with his own hands, without Western help, and the vessel was finished after several years. More than fifty feet long, it could cover over forty li an hour and was named the Yellow Swan. Zeng Guofan was delighted, brought him onto his staff, and recommended him as a man of extraordinary gifts. When the Jiangnan Arsenal was founded in Shanghai, Shou devised many innovations in ships, artillery, firearms, and ammunition. He produced nitric acid, guncotton, and mercury fulminate on his own.
32
創議繙譯西書，以求製造根本。 於是聘西士偉力亞利、傅蘭雅、林樂知、金楷理等，壽與同志華蘅芳、李鳳苞、王德均、趙元益孳孳研究，先後成書數百種。 壽所譯述者，曰西藝知新及續編，化學鑑原及續編、補編，化學考質，化學求數，物體遇熱改易說，汽機發軔，營陣揭要，測地繪圖，寶藏興焉。 法律、醫學，刊行者凡十三種，西藝知新、化學鑑原二書，尤稱善本。
He urged translating Western books to lay the foundations of modern industry. Western scholars including Alexander Wylie, John Fryer, Young J. Allen, and Carl T. Kreyer were hired; Shou worked tirelessly with Hua Hengfang, Li Fengbao, Wang Dejun, and Zhao Yuanyi, and together they produced several hundred books. Shou's translations included New Knowledge of Western Arts and its sequels, Elements of Chemistry and its supplements, Examination of Chemical Substances, Chemical Calculation, On the Alteration of Bodies by Heat, Introduction to the Steam Engine, Essentials of Camp Array, Surveying and Mapping, and On the Wealth of Nations. Thirteen works on law and medicine were published; New Knowledge of Western Arts and Elements of Chemistry were especially acclaimed as model translations.
33
同治末，與傅蘭雅設格致書院於上海，風氣漸開，成就甚眾，壽名益播。 山東、四川仿設​​機器局，爭延聘壽主其事，以譯書事尤急，皆謝不往，而使其子建寅、華封代行。 大冶煤鐵礦、開平煤礦、漠河金礦經始之際，壽皆為擘畫規制。 購器選匠，資其力焉。 無錫產桑宜蠶，西商購繭奪民利，壽考求烘繭法，倡設烘灶，及機器繅絲法，育蠶者利驟增。
Late in the Tongzhi reign he and John Fryer founded the Polytechnic Academy in Shanghai; as scientific education took hold and many students flourished, Shou's reputation grew still wider. Shandong and Sichuan set up their own arsenals and competed to hire Shou to run them; translation work kept him too busy to accept, and he sent his sons Jianyin and Huafeng instead. When the Daye iron and coal works, the Kaiping coal mines, and the Mohe gold fields were first launched, Shou drafted the plans and specifications for each. He helped purchase machinery and recruit skilled workers for all three ventures. Wuxi's mulberry groves were ideal for sericulture, but Western merchants buying raw cocoons were squeezing local growers; Shou developed cocoon-drying methods, promoted drying ovens and mechanical reeling, and silkworm farmers' profits rose sharply.
34
壽狷介，不求仕進，以布衣終。 光緒中，卒，年六十七。 子建寅、華封，皆世其學。
Shou was proud and independent, never sought official rank, and died a private citizen. He died during the Guangxu reign, at the age of sixty-seven. His sons Jianyin and Huafeng both carried on his work.
35
建寅，字仲虎。 從父於江寧、上海，助任製造。 尋充山東機器局總辦，福建船政提調，出使德國二等參贊，洊擢直隸候補道。 光緒末，張之洞調至湖北監造無菸火藥，已成，藥炸裂，殞焉，賜優恤。
Jianyin, whose style name was Zhonghu. He followed his father to Nanjing and Shanghai and helped run the arsenals. He later headed the Shandong arsenal, supervised the Fujian Naval Dockyard, served as second secretary on the mission to Germany, and rose repeatedly to expectant circuit intendant of Zhili. Late in the Guangxu reign Zhang Zhidong summoned him to Hubei to oversee smokeless powder production; when the batch was complete the charge detonated and killed him, and the court granted special posthumous honors.
36
華封，字祝三。 性敏，為父所愛，秘說精器多授之，以製造為治生。 建寅、華封並從父譯書行於世。
Huafeng, whose style name was Zhusan. Quick-witted and his father's favorite, he received most of the family's secret techniques and precision tools and earned his living through manufacturing. Jianyin and Huafeng both assisted their father in translations that were published widely.
