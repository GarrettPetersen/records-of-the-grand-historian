萬承風周系英錢樾秦瀛李宗瀚韓鼎晉朱方增
Wan Chengfeng, Zhou Xiying, Qian Yue, Qin Ying, Li Zonghan, Han Dingjin, and Zhu Fangzeng
3
萬承風，字和圃，江西義寧人。 乾隆四十六年進士，選庶吉士，授檢討。 直上書房，侍宣宗讀。 六十年，典試雲南。 時仁宗在潛邸，賜詩寵行。 累遷翰林院侍讀。 嘉慶三年，大考，降檢討。 四年，督廣東學政。 瓊州海寇猝發，承風以聞，命總督吉慶按治，總兵西密揚阿等以恇怯置吏議。 累遷侍講學士，任滿還京，直上書房，擢詹事。 督山東學政，整厲士習，扶持善類。 洊擢禮部侍郎，命還京。
Wan Chengfeng (style Hepu) came from Yining, Jiangxi. He took the jinshi degree in Qianlong 46, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and was appointed reviser. He joined the Upper Study and tutored the future Daoguang Emperor. In the sixtieth year of the reign he presided over the Yunnan provincial examination. The future Jiaqing Emperor, still heir in his private residence, honored him with a parting poem. He rose step by step to Reader in the Hanlin Academy. The Jiaqing year 3 triennial review demoted him back to reviser. In year 4 he was made Guangdong education commissioner. When pirates suddenly flared up at Qionzhou, Chengfeng memorialized the court; the throne ordered Governor-General Ji Qing to investigate and pressed disciplinary action against Regional Commander Ximiyang'a and others for cowardice. Promoted to Hanlin Expositor, he returned to Beijing at term's end, rejoined the Upper Study, and was raised to Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent. As Shandong education commissioner he tightened scholarly discipline and stood up for the worthy. Further promotions made him Vice Minister of Rites, then he was recalled to Beijing.
4
十二年，督學江蘇。 以清江浦、荷花塘河工取勢太直，屢築屢圮，奏請復舊，詔如議行。 調兵部。 十四年，上五旬萬壽，陳請解任還京祝嘏，詔嚴斥，左遷內閣學士。 調安徽學政。 定遠士子與鳳陽胥役有隙，至試期輒修怨，當事者庇胥役，士益憤，承風疏請下巡撫嚴治胥役，置諸法。 擢兵部侍郎，還京，仍直上書房，充經筵講官。 十七年，引疾歸，尋卒，入祀鄉賢祠。 宣宗即位，追念舊學，贈禮部尚書銜，諡文恪。 道光十二年，晉贈太傅，子方楙等加恩有差。
In year 12 he took charge of Jiangsu education. The Qingjiangpu and Lotus Pond works had been routed too straight, so dikes failed again and again; he memorialized to restore the original channel, and the throne ordered it done. He was moved to the Ministry of War. In year 14, when the court celebrated the Emperor's fiftieth birthday, he asked to leave office and return to the capital to offer congratulations; the throne rebuked him harshly and demoted him to Grand Council academician. He was reassigned as Anhui education commissioner. Scholars in Dingyuan had fallen out with Fengyang yamen runners; each examination season revived the feud, and local officials protected the runners until the scholars' anger boiled over. Chengfeng memorialized for the governor-general to punish the runners severely and bring them to justice. Promoted to Vice Minister of War, he returned to Beijing, reentered the Upper Study, and served as a Classics Lecture lecturer. In year 17 he pleaded illness and went home; he died soon after and was enshrined in the local worthies' temple. When Daoguang took the throne, he remembered his old tutor and posthumously granted Chengfeng Minister of Rites rank with the posthumous name Wenké (Cultured and Reverent). In Daoguang 12 he was further ennobled as Grand Tutor, and his son Fangmao and others received graded imperial favors.
5
周系英，字孟才，湖南湘潭人。 乾隆五十八年進士，選庶吉士，授編修，累遷侍講。 嘉慶十年，督四川學政。 十四年，入直南書房，擢太常寺卿。 尋改直上書房，授三阿哥讀。 上諭：「不但授讀講習詩文，當教阿哥為人居心以忠厚為本。 」系英請加授資治通鑑，以知古今治亂興衰之故，悉民間疾苦，上韙之。 轉光祿寺卿，督山西學政。 任滿回京，仍直上書房。 十九年，擢兵部右侍郎，母憂去，服闋，補吏部侍郎。
Zhou Xiying (style Mengcai) came from Xiangtan, Hunan. He took the jinshi in Qianlong 58, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, became a compiler, and rose to expositor. In Jiaqing 10 he was made Sichuan education commissioner. In year 14 he joined the Southern Study and was promoted Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. He was soon moved to the Upper Study to teach the third imperial son. The Emperor told him: "Do not only teach reading and drill him in poetry and prose—you must teach the prince that in how one carries the heart, sincerity and generosity are the foundation. Xiying asked to add the Comprehensive Mirror so the prince would learn why dynasties rise and fall and know the people's hardships; the Emperor agreed. He became Director of the Court of Imperial Entertainments and Shanxi education commissioner. At term's end he returned to Beijing and rejoined the Upper Study. In year 19 he was made Right Vice Minister of War, left for his mother's mourning, and after mourning filled the Vice Minister of Personnel post.
6
二十四年，湘潭民與江西客民閧，相殺傷，巡撫吳邦慶亦籍江西，陳奏偏袒。 系英詢齎奏人，得事始末，於召對時面陳，乃調邦慶福建，詔以獄事畀總督察治。 系英素以樸直被眷遇，邦慶初與善，約地方事有見聞必告，至是手書言其曲直； 系英子汝楨亦致書在籍給事中石承藻詢獄事：書並為邦慶得，先後以兩書上聞。 上怒系英庇鄉人，部議革職，猶命以編修用。 繼以汝楨致書事，褫職回籍。
In year 24 Xiangtan locals and Jiangxi migrants fought with casualties; Governor Wu Bangqing, himself from Jiangxi, filed a one-sided report. Xiying questioned the courier, learned the whole story, and laid it out at an imperial audience; Bangqing was shifted to Fujian, and the throne handed the case to the governor-general. Xiying had long been trusted for blunt honesty; Bangqing had once been friendly and promised to share anything he saw of local affairs—now he wrote by hand to spell out who was right and wrong; Xiying's son Ruzhen also wrote the retired censor Shi Chengzao about the case; Bangqing seized both letters and sent them up to the throne in turn. The Emperor fumed that Xiying had favored his countrymen; the ministry recommended dismissal but still let him serve as compiler. Then, over Ruzhen's letters, he was stripped of rank and sent home.
7
道光初，以四品京堂召用，歷翰林院侍讀學士、內閣學士。 二年，遷工部侍郎，督江西學政，尋調江蘇，許密摺言地方利病，人才臧否。 會瀕江大水，學政駐江陰，系英目擊災狀，貽書督撫，留官吏素得民者治賑務，假庫帑三萬兩購米平糶，民感之。 四年，調戶部左侍郎，卒於任。
Early in Daoguang he was recalled as a fourth-rank capital official and served as Hanlin Reader and Grand Council academician. In year 2 he became Vice Minister of Works and Jiangxi education commissioner, then was shifted to Jiangsu with leave to send secret memorials on local ills and on who was fit for office. When floods ravaged the river counties he was stationed at Jiangyin; seeing the ruin, he wrote the governor-general and governor to keep trusted officials on relief, borrowed thirty thousand taels to buy grain and sell it fairly, and the people were grateful. In year 4 he was made Left Vice Minister of Revenue and died in post.
8
錢樾，字黼棠，浙江嘉善人。 乾隆三十七年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 典陝西鄉試，督四川學政。 直上書房。 兩典江西鄉試，督廣西學政，累擢少詹事。 嘉慶四年，還京，仍入直。 驟遷內閣學士、禮部侍郎，督江蘇學政。 時吳縣令甄輔廷治諸生糾控罪過當，學政平恕曲徇所請，斥革生員二十五人。 上聞之，解平恕任，以樾代，至則先复諸生名，僅坐首事者三人，士民稱慶。 方其赴任，途中見行船有大書「內廷南府」者，因上疏劾姦吏詭託，上累聖明，詔飭關津禁絕，嚴罪所司。
Qian Yue (style Futang) came from Jiashan, Zhejiang. He took the jinshi in Qianlong 37, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and was appointed compiler. He presided over the Shaanxi provincial exam and served as Sichuan education commissioner. He joined the Upper Study. He twice ran the Jiangxi provincial exam, served as Guangxi education commissioner, and rose to Junior Mentor of the Heir Apparent. In Jiaqing 4 he returned to Beijing and resumed palace duty. He was swiftly made Grand Council academician, Vice Minister of Rites, and Jiangsu education commissioner. Wu County magistrate Zhen Futing had punished student accusers too harshly; Education Commissioner Pingshu, being lenient, indulged him and struck twenty-five students from the rolls. The throne heard of it, removed Pingshu, and sent Yue; Yue restored the students at once and punished only three ringleaders, to the joy of town and gown. On the way to his post he saw boats boldly lettered "Imperial Southern Bureau"; he memorialized against clerks trading on the name, praised the Emperor's discernment, and the court shut the passes and punished the guilty.
9
時南河邵壩決口，瓜、儀私梟充斥，為閭閻害，命樾密訪以聞。 疏陳：「黃河自豫東界至桃、宿以上，水緩沙停，致河高堤淺，所在防潰。 請於霜降後鳩工疏正河，並增築堤防，先務所急。 又以私梟為患，皆由官鹽價貴，民利食私，若稍平鹽價，則私梟自絕。 」疏入，俱報可。 尋調吏部，任滿回京，調戶部，兼管錢法堂事務。 奏請申禁改漕折色，以清弊端。 復調吏部，九年，坐失察書吏舞弊，以告病治中趙曰濂虛選運同，降內閣學士，樾上疏置辯，議革職，加恩賜編修。 十年，擢鴻臚寺少卿，督山東學政。 累遷大理寺少卿、內閣學士。 母憂歸，服闋，引疾不出。 二十年，卒。
The Shao dam on the South Canal had broken, and smugglers swarmed Guazhou and Yizheng, plaguing the towns; Yue was told to investigate quietly and report. He wrote: "From the Henan border eastward to Taoyuan and Suxian and upstream, slow water lets silt settle, raising the riverbed and shallowing the dikes—every stretch fears a breach. He asked that after Frost's Descent crews dredge the channel and raise the dikes, tackling the worst spots first. On smuggling he said the root was dear government salt; ease the price and the illicit trade would die on its own. The memorial was approved in full. He was soon moved to Personnel, recalled, then to Revenue, where he also ran the Bureau of Currency. He asked the court to forbid converting grain tribute into cash payments, to cut corruption. Back in Personnel, in year 9 he was demoted for missing a clerk's fraud—Zhao Yuelian had rigged a transport commissioner's appointment; Yue protested in a memorial, faced dismissal, but was spared as compiler. In year 10 he became Vice Director of the Court of Imperial Banquets and Shandong education commissioner. He rose to Vice Director of the Court of Judicial Review and Grand Council academician. He went home for his mother's mourning; when it ended he pleaded illness and stayed out of office. He died in year 20.
10
秦瀛，字凌滄，江蘇無錫人，諭德松齡玄孫也。 乾隆四十一年，以舉人召試山東行在，授內閣中書，充軍機章京，洊遷郎中。 五十八年，出為浙江溫處道，有惠政。 嘉慶五年，擢按察使。 寧、紹、台三府水災，有司匿不報，瀛力言於巡撫，乃得賑。 調湖南，衡州歲歉，有司匿不報，方議派濟陝西兵米，瀛复力言於巡撫，留米平糶。 七年，以病歸。 逾兩年，起授廣東按察使，督郡縣治盜，擒著盜梁修平、吳叚喜置諸法。 撫瓊州黎匪，嚴禁賭博白鴿票。
Qin Ying (style Lingcang) of Wuxi, Jiangsu, was the four-times-great-grandson of Tutor Qin Songling. In Qianlong 41, summoned as a provincial graduate to the imperial camp in Shandong, he became a Grand Secretariat secretary, then a Grand Council clerk, and rose to director. In year 58 he became Wenzhou-Taichu circuit intendant and governed with kindness. In Jiaqing 5 he was made provincial surveillance commissioner. When Ningbo, Shaoxing, and Taizhou flooded, officials hid the disaster until Ying pressed the governor and relief finally came. In Hunan, Hengzhou's poor harvest was hushed up while grain was earmarked for Shaanxi troops; Ying again forced the governor's hand and kept the rice for fair-price sale. In year 7 illness sent him home. Two years later he was recalled as Guangdong surveillance commissioner, drove counties to hunt bandits, and executed the notorious Liang Xiuping and Wu Duanxi. He pacified Qionzhou's Li rebels and banned gambling and the white-dove lottery.
11
十年，遷浙江布政使，入覲，乞內用，授光祿寺卿，轉太常寺卿。 疏陳廣東治盜事宜，略曰：「海盜始在高、廉，近則闌入廣州。 大股如鄭一、烏石二、總兵寶、朱濆等，聲勢甚張。 內地順德、香山、新會三縣，連有肆劫，以馬觀、李英芳為之魁，與海盜勾結，捕急則遁入海中。 統將出海，藉詞遷延，不能盡力。 黜提督孫全謀，而魏大斌即為之續。 臣愚以為剿捕之法：一曰討軍實。 水師廢弛，則帑餉虛糜。 洋商、鹽商捐輸寬裕，經手之員尚有侵漁，遣委之將仍復驕惰，非立法痛懲，徒資耗費。 一曰樹聲威。 盜善偵探，非先聲讋人，盜已輕我。 兵行之日，督撫宜舉觴歡飲； 有功而歸，開轅行賞，不用命者，殺無赦。 一曰戒虛飾。 擒盜豈能皆真，一念邀功，讞多失實，偶有平反，不復深咎。 嗣後總期弋獲真盜，毋縱毋枉。 至守御之法，尤宜急講。 砲台防守口岸，口岸多而汛兵少，盜船乘間直入； 巡船复少，不能禦盜，且為盜資。 保甲僅屬虛名，縱役訛索，反成厲政。 欲行保甲團練，先須百姓服從。 臣以為嚴防守必先澄清吏治，澄吏治必先固民心。 一曰清獄訟。 粵民好訟，大小案件，諭旨嚴飭，尚多沉擱。 殆由案之初起，遲延不辦，土棍訟師，從而把持，遂至供情屢易，莫可窮究。 惟有督飭州縣，有一案即清一案，務洗慵惰偏私之習。 一曰抑冗濫。 六計尚廉，近海州縣有緝捕解犯之責，尤宜撙節，庶不虧倉庫而累閭閻。 一令到任，幕友長隨，多人坐食，勢不能復為廉吏。 雜職武弁，惟利是圖，稍授以權，即挾製文吏。 雜職差委過多，亦滋擾累。 一日懲蠹役。 胥役熟習地方情形，串同官親家屬，肆為民害。 廣東胥役，每有暗通盜匪，收受陋規，此尤不可不嚴行懲創也。 三者既舉而吏治澄，吏治澄而民心固，於以舉行保甲團練，無不可使之民，即無不可行之法矣。 」疏上，詔下疆吏採行。 遷順天府尹。
In year 10 he became Zhejiang administration commissioner; at court he asked for a capital post and was made Director of Imperial Entertainments, then Director of Imperial Sacrifices. He memorialized on Guangdong banditry, writing in part: "Pirates first plagued Gaozhou and Lianzhou; lately they have pushed into Guangzhou itself. Major gangs—Zheng Yi, Wushi Er, Zongbing Bao, Zhu Fen, and others—had grown terrifyingly bold. Inland, Shunde, Xiangshan, and Xinhui suffered repeated raids led by Ma Guan and Li Yingfang, who colluded with pirates and slipped to sea when pressed. Commanders at sea stalled on excuses and never fought at full strength. Provincial Military Commander Sun Quanmou was cashiered—and Wei Dabin took his place at once. In his view, suppression required three things; first, audit the troops' real strength. A slack navy only burned public money. Merchants gave lavishly, yet handlers skimmed and dispatched generals lounged; without stern law, donations merely fed waste. Second, build awe. Pirates scout well; without a reputation that cowed them first, they already despised the government. When troops sailed, governor-general and governor should toast them off; victors should be welcomed at the yamen gate with rewards; disobedience meant death without mercy. Third, shun empty show. Not every "bandit" caught was real; zeal for credit bred false convictions, and occasional reversals went unpunished. Henceforth he urged real captures—neither letting pirates go nor framing the innocent. Defense, he said, needed equal urgency. Batteries guarded the mouths, but mouths outnumbered garrisons, so pirate craft slipped through the gaps; patrol craft were too few to fight pirates and sometimes armed them instead. Baojia existed in name only; yamen runners extorted under its cover until it became a scourge. To make baojia and militia work, the people had to trust the government first. Strict defense, he argued, required clean government; clean government required winning the people's hearts. First, clear the courts. Guangdong folk loved lawsuits; despite stern edicts, cases large and small still piled up untouched. Likely because slow handling at the start let thugs and pettifoggers seize each file, testimony shifted again and again, and truth became impossible to find. Only by pressing prefectures and counties to clear every case as it arose could the habits of sloth and private favor be scrubbed away. Second, curb bloated staffs and pointless posts. The Six Evaluations prize integrity; seaboard counties that must chase fugitives and escort prisoners above all needed thrift, lest granaries run dry and villages bear the cost. Once a magistrate took his post, a swarm of secretaries and hangers-on lived off his table; under that weight no man could stay honest. Petty military officers chased profit alone; grant them the slightest power and they bent civil officials to their will. Too many petty commissions only bred fresh harassment. Third, punish corrupt yamen runners. Runners knew every local angle; with magistrates' kin they colluded and preyed on the people. In Guangdong, runners often tipped off pirates and took illicit fees—there, he urged, punishment had to be merciless. Carry out all three, he argued, and government would clear; clear government would steady the people; then baojia and militia could bind any community and enforce any rule. " The memorial went up; the throne ordered frontier officials to put it into practice. He was made Intendant of Shuntian Prefecture.
12
十二年，擢刑部侍郎。 以宗室敏學獄會擬輕縱，議褫職，詔原之，左遷光祿寺卿。 歷左副都御史、倉場侍郎。 詔整頓倉場，慮瀛齒衰，以二品頂戴調左副都御史。 尋授兵部侍郎，復調刑部。 瀛治獄平慎，在浙辨定海難民十二人非盜。 及海盜誣攀族人，已入告，卒更正省釋。 在部治運丁盜米，訐者謂以藥置米中立溢，試之不驗，仁宗親試明其枉，尤為時稱。 十五年，以病解任。 道光元年，卒。
In year 12 he was promoted Vice Minister of Justice. When a joint verdict in the clansman Minxue case was judged too lenient, the court debated stripping his post; the emperor spared him but demoted him to Director of Imperial Entertainments. He served as Left Vice Censor-in-Chief and Grain Depot Vice Minister. Tasked with reforming the grain depots, the court feared Ying's years and moved him to Left Vice Censor-in-Chief with a second-rank finial. Soon he was Vice Minister of War, then returned to Justice. Ying tried cases with calm care; in Zhejiang he proved twelve Dinghai refugees were no pirates. When pirates framed his kinsmen and indictments were already filed, he overturned the case and won their release. At the ministry he tried transport coolies for stealing rice; accusers claimed drugs swelled the grain in the sack—tests failed, and Emperor Renzong's own trial exposed the frame-up, winning wide praise. In year 15 illness ended his tenure. He died in Daoguang 1.
13
瀛工文章，與姚鼐相推重，體亦相近雲。
Ying wrote well and held Yao Nai in mutual esteem; their prose was said to be much alike.
14
李宗瀚，字春湖，江西臨川人。 乾隆五十八年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 嘉慶三年，大考二等，擢左贊善。 累遷侍講學士，充日講起居注官。 五年，典福建鄉試，母憂歸，服闋，補原官，轉侍讀學士。 九年，督湖南學政，歷太僕寺卿、宗人府丞、左副都御史。 二十年，丁本生母憂，服闋，在籍奏請終生祖母養，允之。 道光三年，遭祖母喪。 先是禮臣建議，為父後者為生祖母終三年喪，宗瀚幸奉功令，既而部議仍改期服，宗瀚本生父秉禮已老，而有子四人，以出繼不得終養。 五年，入都，召見，詢家世官資甚悉。 宗瀚具陳終養始末，宣宗為之嗟嘆，遂補原官。 八年，擢工部侍郎，典浙江鄉試，留學政。 十一年，丁本生父憂，哀毀，扶病奔喪，卒於衢州，以衰服殮，年六十三。
Li Zonghan (style Chunhu) came from Linchuan, Jiangxi. He took the jinshi in Qianlong 58, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and was appointed compiler. In Jiaqing 3 he ranked second class in the palace examination and was made Left Mentor of the Heir Apparent. He rose to Erudite Lecturer and served as Daily Lecturer and Recorder of the Emperor's Movements. In year 5 he ran the Fujian provincial exam; his mother's death sent him home, and after mourning he resumed office as Reader-in-Waiting. In year 9 he became Hunan education commissioner, then Director of the Imperial Stud, Deputy Director of the Imperial Clan Court, and Left Vice Censor-in-Chief. In year 20 he mourned his birth mother; when mourning ended he asked from home to spend his life nursing his birth father's grandmother, and the throne agreed. In Daoguang 3 his grandmother died. Rites officials had ruled that an heir in his father's line should mourn a birth grandmother three years; Zonghan briefly rejoiced at the decree, but the ministry soon reverted to one-year mourning. His birth father Bingli was aged, with four sons, yet Zonghan—given in adoption—could no longer keep the lifelong care he had promised. In year 5 he came to the capital; the emperor received him and questioned his family and career in minute detail. Zonghan told the whole story of his years at his grandmother's side; the Xuanzong Emperor sighed in sympathy and restored his former post. In year 8 he became Vice Minister of Works, ran the Zhejiang provincial exam, and stayed on as education commissioner. In year 11 his birth father died; grief shattered him, and he dragged himself to the funeral only to die at Quzhou, where he was buried in full mourning dress at sixty-three.
15
宗瀚孝謹恬退，中歲以養親居林下十年，書法尤為世重。
Filial, careful, and self-effacing, he spent a decade in retirement mid-career to nurse his elders; his calligraphy the age prized above all.
16
韓鼎晉，字樹屏，四川長壽人。 乾隆六十年進士，選庶吉士，授檢討。 嘉慶九年，改御史。 疏言天主教流傳之害，請申禁以絕根株，從之。 以母老請終養，十六年，服闋，補原官。 疏陳四川積弊六事，曰：禁科派以安閭閻，除啯匪以防積漸，查卡房以全民命，禁拐騙以警貪頑，嚴攤捐以養廉潔，覈戎政以歸實效。 又言京師賭風大熾，多屬王公大臣輿夫設局，倚勢骫法，帝命指實，下詔嚴治。 逾日，獲賭案三，大學士、步軍統領祿康輿夫為之魁。 親貴近臣，莫不悚息​​。
Han Dingjin (style Shuping) came from Changshou, Sichuan. He took the jinshi in Qianlong 60, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and was appointed reviser. In Jiaqing 9 he became a censor. He warned of Catholicism's spread and asked a strict ban to uproot it; the court agreed. His mother aged, he asked to care for her at home; in year 16, mourning done, he took up his old post. He listed six Sichuan abuses: end illegal levies to ease villages; crush gufei gangs before they spread; shut abusive checkpoints; stop kidnapping rackets to warn the greedy; tighten stall taxes to keep officials honest; and audit military affairs for real effect. He also charged that Beijing gambling ran wild, often run by princes, ministers, and their sedan-chair bearers, who bent the law by rank; the emperor demanded names and issued a stern purge. Next day three gambling rings fell; the ringleaders were chair-bearers of Grand Secretary Lu Kang, who also commanded the Steppe Brigade. Imperial kin and court favorites alike trembled.
17
巡視山東漕務，轉工科給事中、光祿寺少卿，督陝甘學政。 疏言：「榆、綏諸州縣倉貯空虛，宜設法籌補，其地資蒙古糧食接濟。 今腹裡邊外俱荒，當分別安置撫卹。 」又言：「南山善後事宜，宜行堅壁清野之法。 山內流民雜處，最為奸藪，當嚴行保甲，使姦宄無所匿。 軍中擄脅難民子女，請嚴禁。 南山附近及豫東並經兵燹，宜慎選牧令，以蘇民氣。 川北荒歉，與陝、甘毗連，鹽梟啯匪多出其中，請先事豫防。 」並下疆吏如所請行。 歷鴻臚寺卿、通政司副使、太常寺卿、左副都御史。
He inspected Shandong grain transport, became a Works supervising secretary and Vice Director of Imperial Entertainments, then Shaanxi-Gansu education commissioner. He wrote: "Yulin, Suide, and neighboring counties have empty granaries and need replenishment; the region depends on Mongol grain for relief. Famine now grips both interior and frontier alike; each district needs its own relief and resettlement." He also urged for the Southern Mountains after the rebellion: "Fortify villages and clear the countryside. Refugees crowd the hills—the worst nest of rogues; enforce baojia strictly so criminals have nowhere to hide. He asked a strict ban on troops seizing refugee women and children. The Southern Mountains and eastern Henan, scarred by war, need magistrates chosen with care to revive the people's spirit. Famine in northern Sichuan, adjoining Shaanxi and Gansu, breeds salt smugglers and gufei gangs—he asked preventive action before they spread. " The throne sent all of it to frontier officials to execute. He served as Director of Imperial Banquets, Vice Director of Transmission, Director of Imperial Sacrifices, and Left Vice Censor-in-Chief.
18
二十四年，命察視近畿水災，督黃村賑務。 督福建學政，疏言：「閩中吏治久窳，請不限資格，用廉幹吏補汀、漳、泉三郡望緊要缺，久其任以專責成。 漳、泉營伍通盜，請責提鎮立予重典，勿稍袒庇。 」道光六年，遷倉場侍郎，以病罷。 起補工部侍郎，京察，原品休致。 卒於家，祀鄉賢祠。
In year 24 he was sent to survey floods near the capital and oversee relief at Huangcun. As Fujian education commissioner he wrote: "Fujian government has rotted for years; waive seniority and send able, honest men to the urgent posts in Tingzhou, Zhangzhou, and Quanzhou, and keep them long enough to answer for results. Zhangzhou and Quanzhou troops trade with pirates; hold the provincial and regional commanders to swift, heavy punishment—no favoritism." In Daoguang 6 he became Grain Depot Vice Minister, then quit for illness. Recalled as Vice Minister of Works, he passed the capital evaluation and retired at full rank. He died at home and entered the Local Worthies Temple.
19
朱方增，字虹舫，浙江海鹽人。 嘉慶六年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 典雲南鄉試，遷國子監司業。 十八年，教匪之變，方增劾直隸總督溫承惠貽誤地方，黜之。
Zhu Fangzeng (style Hongfang) came from Haiyan, Zhejiang. He took the jinshi in Jiaqing 6, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and was appointed compiler. He ran the Yunnan provincial exam and became Vice Director of the Directorate of Education. In year 18, when the Teaching Bandit rising broke out, Fangzeng impeached Zhili Governor-General Wen Chenghui for bungling the province and had him removed.
20
應詔陳言，論用人理財，略曰：「近今大臣中，罕有以進賢為務者。 蓋薦舉之事，易於徇私，黨援交結，不得不防，而大臣亦遂引嫌自避。 夫大臣避徇私之名，而忘以人事君之責，所謂因噎廢食，非公忠體國者所宜有也。 至於任用之方，則無過於考言詢事。 皇上博訪週諮，徐為印證。 於召對時，各就所長，諭使面陳，果能洞悉原委，又當試之以事，以觀其能踐與否。 如或敷奏並無條理，則其人固不足用，而大臣之識見優絀，心地公私，亦可見矣。 抑臣思臣工居職，苟非闒茸齷齪者流，孰不思自效？ 況蒙皇上訓飭至再至三，而猶故習相仍，驟難振拔者，良有數端：條例過繁，文案蒨屑，雖有強敏之吏，而精神疲於具文，其實關於政治民生，轉致不能詳覈。 一也。 差務絡繹，公私賠累，身家之卹不遑，民物之懷漸恝。 二也。 訐告之風，至今益甚。 嘗有以田土、鬥毆細故而叩閽京控者，有司畏其挾制，不得不姑息委蛇。 雖有急公自好者，其尋常蒨屑之事，豈皆一一可達聖聰？ 甚至匿名揭帖，無主名之可指。 蠹吏猾胥，奸民惡僕，求謀不遂，懲治過嚴，皆可造作飛語，訐及陰私。 足使任事之心，不寒而栗，委曲隱忍。 姦宄橫行，大都由此。 三也。 今皇上欲整飭因循積習，臣愚以為必先除此三者之弊，庶廓然無所疑畏，而得專精實政矣。 經國之方，理財尤要。 古者以三十年之通制國用，斟酌盈虛，量入為出，用能經常不匱。 今戶部歲入歲出，年一匯奏。 惟中外未合為一，條緒繁賾，極難釐剔。 且凡撥解即謂之出，並未實計所用。 新舊牽溷，凌雜益甚，而出納諸款，又因有無定之款，盈朒參差。 以故一歲之中，所出幾何，覈之所入，贏餘若干，不能得其實數。 請旨敕下戶部，歲入歲出，宜合中外為一。 核計贏餘總數，仍取前一二歲所贏餘，確實比較，然後審其輕重緩急，舉一切例內例外諸用款，有可裁省停緩者，酌加撙節。 庶合於古人通年制用之法，而度支充裕矣。 」
Answering an imperial call for views on appointments and finance, he wrote in part: "Few ministers lately make promoting talent their business. Recommendations invite favoritism and clique ties, so ministers must guard against them—yet many now dodge the duty altogether out of scruple. To shun the charge of cronyism while neglecting the duty to put the right men before the throne is to choke on fear of choking—no true servant of the realm should do it. For appointments, nothing beats testing words against deeds. Your Majesty already asks widely and listens everywhere; verify slowly. At audience, let each man speak to his strength; if he truly grasps a matter's roots, set him a task and see whether he delivers. If his answers lack all order, the man is useless—and the recommending minister's judgment and private motives stand revealed. Yet which official, unless incompetent or corrupt, does not wish to serve? And though Your Majesty has warned them again and again, old habits linger and reform comes hard—for several reasons: rules multiply and paperwork swallows clerks, so even sharp men exhaust themselves on forms while matters that touch people's lives go unchecked. That is the first. Commissioned errands never cease; public duty and private purse both bleed; officials cannot shelter their families, and care for the people fades. That is the second. The plague of informers grows worse by the year. Men sue over field spats and fistfights by rushing to Beijing; magistrates fear their leverage and temporize. Even earnest officials cannot bring every petty quarrel before the throne. Anonymous placards appear with no accuser to name. Corrupt clerks, sly runners, rogues, and vicious servants—foiled in a scheme or punished too hard—invent slander and denounce private lives. Enough to make any man who takes responsibility shiver and yield in silence. Most villainy abroad springs from this. That is the third. If Your Majesty means to break entrenched habit, I believe these three abuses must go first; then officials will act without fear and give their full strength to real government. To govern a realm, sound finance matters most. Antiquity planned the treasury on thirty years' totals, balancing surplus and deficit and matching outlay to income, so the state never ran dry. Today the Ministry of Revenue reports income and outlay once a year. Yet capital accounts and provinces are never unified; the lines of revenue are hopelessly tangled. Any transfer counts as spent before anyone tracks what was actually used. Old and new entries blur together; receipts and payments mix fixed and uncertain items until surplus and deficit cannot be told apart. Thus within a single year no one can state true outlays, true income, or true surplus. I ask Your Majesty to order the Ministry of Revenue to merge capital and provincial accounts into one ledger. Total the surplus, compare it honestly with the prior year or two, weigh each expense by urgency, and cut, defer, or trim every routine and exceptional charge that allows. Then spending would match the ancients' multi-year planning, and the treasury would stand secure. "
21
二十年，入直懋勤殿，編纂石渠寶笈、秘殿珠林。 尋督廣西學政，累遷翰林院侍讀學士。 道光四年，大考第一，擢內閣學士。 典山東鄉試。 七年，督江蘇學政。 十年，卒。
In year 20 he joined duty in the Hall of Imperial Quickening to compile the Stone Canal Precious Collection and the Secret Hall Pearl Forest. Soon he was Guangxi education commissioner and rose to Hanlin Reader-in-Waiting. In Daoguang 4 he topped the palace examination and was made Grand Council academician. He presided over the Shandong provincial examination. In year 7 he became Jiangsu education commissioner. He died in year 10.
22
方增熟諳朝章典故，輯國史名臣事蹟，為從政觀法錄，行於世。
Fangzeng knew court ritual and precedent inside out; he compiled notable ministers' careers from the dynastic histories into Records for Observing Models in Governing, a book that circulated widely.
23
論曰：萬承風、周系英、錢樾以侍從之臣，軺車所至，建白卓然。 秦瀛之治績，李宗瀚之孝行，非僅以文藻稱。 韓鼎晉、朱方增侃侃獻納，言有體要，皆風采著於朝列矣。
The historian concludes: as palace tutors, Wan Chengfeng, Zhou Xiying, and Qian Yue left a striking record of memorials wherever their tours took them. Qin Ying's administrative record and Li Zonghan's filial devotion were admired for more than fine prose. Han Dingjin and Zhu Fangzeng spoke up forthrightly with weight and point; each cut a memorable figure in the halls of court.
