萬承風周系英錢樾秦瀛李宗瀚韓鼎晉朱方增
Wan Chengfeng, Zhou Xiying, Qian Yue, Qin Ying, Li Zonghan, Han Dingjin, and Zhu Fangzeng
3
萬承風，字和圃，江西義寧人。 乾隆四十六年進士，選庶吉士，授檢討。 直上書房，侍宣宗讀。 六十年，典試雲南。 時仁宗在潛邸，賜詩寵行。 累遷翰林院侍讀。 嘉慶三年，大考，降檢討。 四年，督廣東學政。 瓊州海寇猝發，承風以聞，命總督吉慶按治，總兵西密揚阿等以恇怯置吏議。 累遷侍講學士，任滿還京，直上書房，擢詹事。 督山東學政，整厲士習，扶持善類。 洊擢禮部侍郎，命還京。
Wan Chengfeng, whose style was Hepu, came from Yining in Jiangxi. He earned his jinshi degree in the forty-sixth year of the Qianlong reign (1781), entered the Hanlin Academy as a probationary scholar, and was appointed reviser. He served in the Upper Study, tutoring the future Daoguang Emperor. In the sixtieth year of Qianlong (1795), he served as chief examiner for Yunnan's provincial civil service examination. The future Jiaqing Emperor, still residing in his princely quarters, honored Chengfeng's departure with an inscribed poem. He rose through successive promotions to Reader in the Hanlin Academy. At the triennial palace examination evaluation in the third year of Jiaqing (1798), he was demoted to the rank of reviser. The following year he was appointed Guangdong Commissioner of Education. When sea pirates suddenly appeared off Qiongzhou, Chengfeng reported the situation; the court ordered Governor-General Ji Qing to conduct an investigation, and Regional Commander Ximiyang'a and others faced disciplinary proceedings for cowardice. After further promotion to Lecturer in the Hanlin Academy, he completed his term and returned to Beijing, resumed duty in the Upper Study, and was elevated to Junior Tutor of the Heir Apparent. As Commissioner of Education for Shandong, he disciplined scholarly conduct and championed men of integrity. He was soon promoted to Vice Minister of the Ministry of Rites and recalled to the capital.
4
十二年，督學江蘇。 以清江浦、荷花塘河工取勢太直，屢築屢圮，奏請復舊，詔如議行。 調兵部。 十四年，上五旬萬壽，陳請解任還京祝嘏，詔嚴斥，左遷內閣學士。 調安徽學政。 定遠士子與鳳陽胥役有隙，至試期輒修怨，當事者庇胥役，士益憤，承風疏請下巡撫嚴治胥役，置諸法。 擢兵部侍郎，還京，仍直上書房，充經筵講官。 十七年，引疾歸，尋卒，入祀鄉賢祠。 宣宗即位，追念舊學，贈禮部尚書銜，諡文恪。 道光十二年，晉贈太傅，子方楙等加恩有差。
In the twelfth year of Jiaqing (1807), he was appointed to oversee education in Jiangsu. Observing that the Qingjiangpu and Hehuatang river projects had been laid out too straight and had collapsed again and again despite repeated repairs, he memorialized to restore the original alignment; the throne approved his recommendation. He was transferred to the Ministry of War. In the fourteenth year of Jiaqing (1809), when the emperor celebrated his fiftieth birthday, Chengfeng asked leave to resign his post and return to Beijing to offer felicitations; the court sharply censured him and demoted him to Grand Secretary of the Grand Secretariat. He was reassigned as Commissioner of Education for Anhui. Scholars from Dingyuan were at odds with clerks and runners from Fengyang, and at every examination season old grievances flared anew; local officials protected the runners, stirring greater indignation among the scholars; Chengfeng memorialized the governor to punish the runners severely according to law. Promoted to Vice Minister of War, he returned to Beijing, resumed service in the Upper Study, and was appointed lecturer at the imperial Classics Colloquium. In the seventeenth year of Jiaqing (1812) he retired citing ill health; he died shortly thereafter and was enshrined in the hall of local worthies. After the Daoguang Emperor's accession, remembering their long-shared tutelage, the throne posthumously granted Chengfeng the honorary rank of Minister of Rites with the posthumous epithet Wenkè (Cultured and Reverent). In the twelfth year of Daoguang (1832), the court further promoted his posthumous rank to Grand Tutor, and his son Fangmao and others received graded imperial favors.
5
周系英，字孟才，湖南湘潭人。 乾隆五十八年進士，選庶吉士，授編修，累遷侍講。 嘉慶十年，督四川學政。 十四年，入直南書房，擢太常寺卿。 尋改直上書房，授三阿哥讀。 上諭：「不但授讀講習詩文，當教阿哥為人居心以忠厚為本。 」系英請加授資治通鑑，以知古今治亂興衰之故，悉民間疾苦，上韙之。 轉光祿寺卿，督山西學政。 任滿回京，仍直上書房。 十九年，擢兵部右侍郎，母憂去，服闋，補吏部侍郎。
Zhou Xiying, whose style was Mengcai, came from Xiangtan in Hunan. He earned his jinshi degree in the fifty-eighth year of Qianlong (1793), entered the Hanlin Academy as a probationary scholar, was appointed compiler, and rose to Expositor in the Hanlin Academy. In the tenth year of Jiaqing (1805), he was appointed Commissioner of Education for Sichuan. In the fourteenth year of Jiaqing (1809), he took up duty in the Southern Study and was promoted to Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. He was soon transferred to the Upper Study to tutor the Third Imperial Son. The emperor instructed him: "Your duty is not merely to teach reading and expound poetry and prose; you must teach the prince that loyalty and sincerity are the foundation of a man's character." Xiying asked permission to add instruction from the Comprehensive Mirror for Aid in Government, so that the prince might learn how dynasties rise and fall and understand the people's hardships; the emperor approved. He was transferred to Director of the Court of Imperial Entertainments and appointed Commissioner of Education for Shanxi. When his term ended he returned to Beijing and resumed duty in the Upper Study. In the nineteenth year of Jiaqing (1814), he was promoted to Right Vice Minister of the Ministry of War; he left office upon his mother's death, and after the mourning period was appointed Vice Minister of Personnel.
6
二十四年，湘潭民與江西客民閧，相殺傷，巡撫吳邦慶亦籍江西，陳奏偏袒。 系英詢齎奏人，得事始末，於召對時面陳，乃調邦慶福建，詔以獄事畀總督察治。 系英素以樸直被眷遇，邦慶初與善，約地方事有見聞必告，至是手書言其曲直； 系英子汝楨亦致書在籍給事中石承藻詢獄事：書並為邦慶得，先後以兩書上聞。 上怒系英庇鄉人，部議革職，猶命以編修用。 繼以汝楨致書事，褫職回籍。
In the twenty-fourth year of Jiaqing (1819), natives of Xiangtan and migrant settlers from Jiangxi clashed with casualties on both sides; Governor Wu Bangqing, himself a native of Jiangxi, submitted memorials that favored his fellow provincials. Xiying questioned the courier who bore the memorial and learned the full story; at an imperial audience he presented the facts in person; Bangqing was transferred to Fujian, and the throne ordered the governor-general to take charge of the case. Xiying had long enjoyed imperial favor for his plainspoken integrity; Bangqing had once been on friendly terms with him and had agreed to keep him informed of local affairs; on this occasion Bangqing wrote him a personal letter setting forth who was right and who was wrong; Xiying's son Ruzhen also wrote to the retired supervising secretary Shi Chengzao to inquire about the case; Bangqing obtained both letters and submitted them to the throne in succession. The emperor was enraged that Xiying had shielded his fellow townspeople; the ministry recommended dismissal, yet the throne still ordered him retained as a Hanlin compiler. When Ruzhen's letter came to light as well, Xiying was stripped of office and sent home.
7
道光初，以四品京堂召用，歷翰林院侍讀學士、內閣學士。 二年，遷工部侍郎，督江西學政，尋調江蘇，許密摺言地方利病，人才臧否。 會瀕江大水，學政駐江陰，系英目擊災狀，貽書督撫，留官吏素得民者治賑務，假庫帑三萬兩購米平糶，民感之。 四年，調戶部左侍郎，卒於任。
Early in the Daoguang reign he was recalled to the capital as a fourth-rank official and served successively as Hanlin Reader and Grand Secretary of the Grand Secretariat. In the second year of Daoguang (1822), he was appointed Vice Minister of Works and Commissioner of Education for Jiangxi, then transferred to Jiangsu with permission to submit secret memorials on local conditions and the character of officials. When catastrophic floods struck the river districts, Xiying was stationed at Jiangyin as commissioner; witnessing the devastation firsthand, he wrote the governor and governor-general urging them to retain officials trusted by the people to manage relief, borrowed thirty thousand taels from the treasury to buy grain for sale at fair prices, and won the people's gratitude. In the fourth year of Daoguang (1824), he was transferred to Left Vice Minister of the Ministry of Revenue and died in office.
8
錢樾，字黼棠，浙江嘉善人。 乾隆三十七年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 典陝西鄉試，督四川學政。 直上書房。 兩典江西鄉試，督廣西學政，累擢少詹事。 嘉慶四年，還京，仍入直。 驟遷內閣學士、禮部侍郎，督江蘇學政。 時吳縣令甄輔廷治諸生糾控罪過當，學政平恕曲徇所請，斥革生員二十五人。 上聞之，解平恕任，以樾代，至則先复諸生名，僅坐首事者三人，士民稱慶。 方其赴任，途中見行船有大書「內廷南府」者，因上疏劾姦吏詭託，上累聖明，詔飭關津禁絕，嚴罪所司。
Qian Yue, whose style was Futang, came from Jiashan in Zhejiang. He earned his jinshi degree in the thirty-seventh year of Qianlong (1772), entered the Hanlin Academy as a probationary scholar, and was appointed compiler. He served as chief examiner for Shaanxi's provincial examination and as Commissioner of Education for Sichuan. He served in the Upper Study. He twice served as chief examiner for Jiangxi, was appointed Commissioner of Education for Guangxi, and rose to Junior Tutor of the Heir Apparent. In the fourth year of Jiaqing (1799), he returned to Beijing and resumed regular palace duty. He was rapidly promoted to Grand Secretary of the Grand Secretariat and Vice Minister of Rites and appointed Commissioner of Education for Jiangsu. At that time Wu County Magistrate Zhen Futing had punished students too harshly on complaint; Commissioner Ping Shu indulged his requests and stripped twenty-five licentiates of their status. When word reached the throne, Ping Shu was dismissed and Yue appointed in his place; on arrival Yue first restored the students' standing, punishing only three ringleaders, to the acclaim of scholars and commoners alike. On his way to take up the post, he saw boats boldly marked "Inner Court Southern Residence"; he memorialized that corrupt officials were fraudulently invoking the palace and thereby impugning the emperor's reputation; the throne ordered passes and fords strictly policed and those responsible severely punished.
9
時南河邵壩決口，瓜、儀私梟充斥，為閭閻害，命樾密訪以聞。 疏陳：「黃河自豫東界至桃、宿以上，水緩沙停，致河高堤淺，所在防潰。 請於霜降後鳩工疏正河，並增築堤防，先務所急。 又以私梟為患，皆由官鹽價貴，民利食私，若稍平鹽價，則私梟自絕。 」疏入，俱報可。 尋調吏部，任滿回京，調戶部，兼管錢法堂事務。 奏請申禁改漕折色，以清弊端。 復調吏部，九年，坐失察書吏舞弊，以告病治中趙曰濂虛選運同，降內閣學士，樾上疏置辯，議革職，加恩賜編修。 十年，擢鴻臚寺少卿，督山東學政。 累遷大理寺少卿、內閣學士。 母憂歸，服闋，引疾不出。 二十年，卒。
At that time the Shao dam on the Southern River had breached; salt smugglers infested Guazhou and Yizhen to the harm of local communities; the emperor ordered Yue to investigate in secret and report. He memorialized: "From the eastern border of Henan upstream to Taozhou and Suzhou, the Yellow River's current slackens and sand accumulates, raising the riverbed while the dikes grow shallow; breaches are feared everywhere." He asked that after Frost's Descent work crews be assembled to dredge and correct the channel and strengthen the dikes, tackling the most urgent points first. He also argued that salt smuggling thrived because government salt was overpriced and the people preferred illicit salt; if the official price were lowered somewhat, smuggling would die out on its own. The memorial was submitted and all his recommendations were approved. He was soon transferred to the Ministry of Personnel; when his term ended he returned to Beijing, was transferred to the Ministry of Revenue, and concurrently oversaw the Bureau of Currency. He memorialized for a renewed ban on converting grain-transport quotas to cash payments, to eliminate corrupt practices. He was transferred again to the Ministry of Personnel; in the ninth year of Jiaqing (1804), he was demoted to Grand Secretary for failing to detect clerical fraud in the case of Acting Director Zhao Yuelian, who had falsely appointed a transport intendant while claiming illness; Yue memorialized in his own defense; though dismissal was recommended, the throne showed mercy and retained him as a Hanlin compiler. In the tenth year of Jiaqing (1805), he was promoted to Vice Director of the Court of Imperial Banquets and appointed Commissioner of Education for Shandong. He rose successively to Vice Director of the Court of Judicial Review and Grand Secretary of the Grand Secretariat. He returned home upon his mother's death; after the mourning period he cited ill health and declined further office. He died in the twentieth year of Jiaqing (1815).
10
秦瀛，字凌滄，江蘇無錫人，諭德松齡玄孫也。 乾隆四十一年，以舉人召試山東行在，授內閣中書，充軍機章京，洊遷郎中。 五十八年，出為浙江溫處道，有惠政。 嘉慶五年，擢按察使。 寧、紹、台三府水災，有司匿不報，瀛力言於巡撫，乃得賑。 調湖南，衡州歲歉，有司匿不報，方議派濟陝西兵米，瀛复力言於巡撫，留米平糶。 七年，以病歸。 逾兩年，起授廣東按察使，督郡縣治盜，擒著盜梁修平、吳叚喜置諸法。 撫瓊州黎匪，嚴禁賭博白鴿票。
Qin Ying, whose style was Lingcang, came from Wuxi in Jiangsu and was the great-great-grandson of the tutor Songling. In the forty-first year of Qianlong (1776), summoned for examination at the imperial camp in Shandong as a provincial graduate, he was appointed Grand Secretariat secretary, served as a Grand Council clerk, and rose to director. In the fifty-eighth year of Qianlong (1793), he was appointed intendant of Wenzhou and Chuzhou in Zhejiang, where his benevolent administration won praise. In the fifth year of Jiaqing (1800), he was promoted to provincial judicial commissioner. When floods struck Ningbo, Shaoxing, and Taizhou, local officials concealed the disaster; Ying pressed the governor forcefully, and relief was finally granted. Transferred to Hunan, when Hengzhou suffered a poor harvest officials again concealed it; grain was about to be diverted to supply Shaanxi troops; Ying again pressed the governor, retained the grain, and sold it at fair prices to the people. In the seventh year of Jiaqing (1802), he retired citing ill health. After two years he was recalled and appointed Guangdong judicial commissioner; he oversaw county efforts against banditry, captured the notorious outlaws Liang Xiuping and Wu Duanxi, and punished them according to law. He pacified the Li rebels in Qiongzhou and strictly banned gambling and white-pigeon lottery tickets.
11
十年，遷浙江布政使，入覲，乞內用，授光祿寺卿，轉太常寺卿。 疏陳廣東治盜事宜，略曰：「海盜始在高、廉，近則闌入廣州。 大股如鄭一、烏石二、總兵寶、朱濆等，聲勢甚張。 內地順德、香山、新會三縣，連有肆劫，以馬觀、李英芳為之魁，與海盜勾結，捕急則遁入海中。 統將出海，藉詞遷延，不能盡力。 黜提督孫全謀，而魏大斌即為之續。 臣愚以為剿捕之法：一曰討軍實。 水師廢弛，則帑餉虛糜。 洋商、鹽商捐輸寬裕，經手之員尚有侵漁，遣委之將仍復驕惰，非立法痛懲，徒資耗費。 一曰樹聲威。 盜善偵探，非先聲讋人，盜已輕我。 兵行之日，督撫宜舉觴歡飲； 有功而歸，開轅行賞，不用命者，殺無赦。 一曰戒虛飾。 擒盜豈能皆真，一念邀功，讞多失實，偶有平反，不復深咎。 嗣後總期弋獲真盜，毋縱毋枉。 至守御之法，尤宜急講。 砲台防守口岸，口岸多而汛兵少，盜船乘間直入； 巡船复少，不能禦盜，且為盜資。 保甲僅屬虛名，縱役訛索，反成厲政。 欲行保甲團練，先須百姓服從。 臣以為嚴防守必先澄清吏治，澄吏治必先固民心。 一曰清獄訟。 粵民好訟，大小案件，諭旨嚴飭，尚多沉擱。 殆由案之初起，遲延不辦，土棍訟師，從而把持，遂至供情屢易，莫可窮究。 惟有督飭州縣，有一案即清一案，務洗慵惰偏私之習。 一曰抑冗濫。 六計尚廉，近海州縣有緝捕解犯之責，尤宜撙節，庶不虧倉庫而累閭閻。 一令到任，幕友長隨，多人坐食，勢不能復為廉吏。 雜職武弁，惟利是圖，稍授以權，即挾製文吏。 雜職差委過多，亦滋擾累。 一日懲蠹役。 胥役熟習地方情形，串同官親家屬，肆為民害。 廣東胥役，每有暗通盜匪，收受陋規，此尤不可不嚴行懲創也。 三者既舉而吏治澄，吏治澄而民心固，於以舉行保甲團練，無不可使之民，即無不可行之法矣。 」疏上，詔下疆吏採行。 遷順天府尹。
In the tenth year of Jiaqing (1805), he was transferred to Zhejiang provincial treasurer; at audience he petitioned for a capital post and was appointed Director of the Court of Imperial Entertainments, then Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. He memorialized on suppressing banditry in Guangdong, stating in summary: "Sea pirates first appeared off Gaozhou and Lianzhou; recently they have penetrated as far as Guangzhou." Major bands led by Zheng Yi, Wushi Er, Zongbingbao, Zhu Fen, and others had grown formidable. Inland, Shunde, Xiangshan, and Xinhui suffered repeated open robbery; Ma Guan and Li Yingfang led the gangs, colluding with sea pirates and fleeing to sea when pursuit intensified. Commanding generals sent to sea used one pretext after another to delay and failed to exert themselves fully. Regional Commander Sun Quanmou was dismissed, but Wei Dabin immediately took his place. Your servant humbly proposes three measures for suppression: first, investigate the true state of the forces. When the navy is neglected and demoralized, treasury funds are squandered to no purpose. Foreign and salt merchants contribute generously, yet officials in charge still skim funds; dispatched generals remain arrogant and slack; without stern laws and punishments, the money is simply wasted. Second, establish prestige. Bandits are skilled scouts; unless we first overawe them, they already hold us in contempt. On the day troops set out, the governor and governor-general should raise cups and feast them; when they return victorious, open the yamen gates and reward them; those who disobey orders shall be executed without mercy. Third, guard against empty display. Not every man seized as a bandit is a real culprit; eager for credit, officials often convict the wrong men; when occasional reversals occur, no further blame follows. Henceforth the aim must be to catch real bandits—neither letting the guilty go nor wronging the innocent. Methods of defense deserve even more urgent attention. Forts guard the river mouths, but there are too many mouths and too few garrison troops; pirate vessels slip through the gaps; patrol boats are few as well, unable to repel pirates and sometimes even supplying them. The baojia system exists only in name; yamen runners extort under false pretenses, turning it into an oppressive burden. To make baojia militia training work, the people must first be won to compliance. Your servant holds that strict defense must begin with clean government, and clean government must begin with winning the people's trust. First, clear the backlog of lawsuits. The people of Guangdong are litigious; despite repeated imperial orders to expedite cases, many large and small suits remain shelved. This is largely because cases are delayed from the outset; local bullies and litigation masters then take control, testimony shifts again and again, and nothing can be thoroughly investigated. Only by pressing prefectures and counties to clear each case as it arises can the habits of indolence and favoritism be rooted out. Second, curb redundant staffing and wasteful expenditure. Integrity ranks foremost among the six criteria for evaluating officials; coastal prefectures and counties charged with arresting and escorting criminals should practice especial economy, lest the treasury be depleted and the people burdened. Once a magistrate takes office, with secretaries and attendants living off his salary in numbers, he can scarcely remain an honest official. Petty military officers care only for profit; given the slightest authority, they bully civil officials. Excessive assignment of petty posts also breeds harassment and abuse. Third, punish corrupt yamen runners. Yamen runners know local conditions intimately, collude with officials' kin and household staff, and prey on the people without restraint. In Guangdong, runners often collude secretly with bandits and pirates and accept illicit fees; this above all must be sternly punished. Once these three measures are in place, government will be clean; once government is clean, the people will be loyal; then baojia militia training can succeed—no people beyond governance, no law beyond enforcement. When the memorial reached the throne, an edict ordered provincial officials to adopt and implement it. He was transferred to Intendant of Shuntian Prefecture.
12
十二年，擢刑部侍郎。 以宗室敏學獄會擬輕縱，議褫職，詔原之，左遷光祿寺卿。 歷左副都御史、倉場侍郎。 詔整頓倉場，慮瀛齒衰，以二品頂戴調左副都御史。 尋授兵部侍郎，復調刑部。 瀛治獄平慎，在浙辨定海難民十二人非盜。 及海盜誣攀族人，已入告，卒更正省釋。 在部治運丁盜米，訐者謂以藥置米中立溢，試之不驗，仁宗親試明其枉，尤為時稱。 十五年，以病解任。 道光元年，卒。
In the twelfth year of Jiaqing (1807), he was promoted to Vice Minister of Justice. Because the joint recommendation in the case of the imperial clansman Minxue was unduly lenient, dismissal was recommended; the emperor pardoned him but demoted him to Director of the Court of Imperial Entertainments. He served successively as Left Vice Censor-in-Chief and Vice Minister of the Granary. When an edict ordered the granary reorganized, the court, concerned about Ying's advanced age, transferred him to Left Vice Censor-in-Chief with second-rank insignia. He was soon appointed Vice Minister of War and transferred again to the Ministry of Justice. Ying handled cases with fairness and care; in Zhejiang he established that twelve refugees from Dinghai were not bandits. When sea pirates falsely implicated his clansmen and charges had already been filed, he ultimately corrected the record and secured their release. At the ministry he tried transport laborers accused of stealing grain; accusers claimed they had placed medicine in the grain to make it overflow; tests failed to confirm this; the Jiaqing Emperor personally tested the claim and proved their innocence; the case won especial praise. In the fifteenth year of Jiaqing (1810), he resigned citing ill health. He died in the first year of Daoguang (1821).
13
瀛工文章，與姚鼐相推重，體亦相近雲。
Ying was an accomplished writer and held Yao Nai in mutual esteem; their literary styles were said to be much alike.
14
李宗瀚，字春湖，江西臨川人。 乾隆五十八年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 嘉慶三年，大考二等，擢左贊善。 累遷侍講學士，充日講起居注官。 五年，典福建鄉試，母憂歸，服闋，補原官，轉侍讀學士。 九年，督湖南學政，歷太僕寺卿、宗人府丞、左副都御史。 二十年，丁本生母憂，服闋，在籍奏請終生祖母養，允之。 道光三年，遭祖母喪。 先是禮臣建議，為父後者為生祖母終三年喪，宗瀚幸奉功令，既而部議仍改期服，宗瀚本生父秉禮已老，而有子四人，以出繼不得終養。 五年，入都，召見，詢家世官資甚悉。 宗瀚具陳終養始末，宣宗為之嗟嘆，遂補原官。 八年，擢工部侍郎，典浙江鄉試，留學政。 十一年，丁本生父憂，哀毀，扶病奔喪，卒於衢州，以衰服殮，年六十三。
Li Zonghan, whose style was Chunhu, came from Linchuan in Jiangxi. He earned his jinshi degree in the fifty-eighth year of Qianlong (1793), entered the Hanlin Academy as a probationary scholar, and was appointed compiler. At the triennial palace examination evaluation in the third year of Jiaqing (1798), he ranked second class and was promoted to Left Assistant to the Heir Apparent. He rose to Lecturer in the Hanlin Academy and served as recorder of the emperor's daily lectures and activities. In the fifth year of Jiaqing (1800), he served as chief examiner for Fujian; he returned home upon his mother's death; after mourning he resumed his former post and was transferred to Reader in the Hanlin Academy. In the ninth year of Jiaqing (1804), he was appointed Commissioner of Education for Hunan and served successively as Director of the Court of the Imperial Stud, Vice Director of the Imperial Clan Court, and Left Vice Censor-in-Chief. In the twentieth year of Jiaqing (1815), he mourned his birth mother; after the mourning period, while at home he memorialized to complete the full three-year mourning for his birth grandmother, and permission was granted. In the third year of Daoguang (1823), his grandmother died. Earlier the Ministry of Rites had ruled that an adopted heir should observe three years' mourning for a birth grandmother; Zonghan had been fortunate to follow this ruling, but the ministry later reverted to the one-year mourning period; Zonghan's birth father Bingli was already elderly with four sons, yet because Zonghan had been given in adoption he could not complete the full period of filial care. In the fifth year of Daoguang (1825), he went to the capital, was summoned for audience, and questioned at length about his family history and official career. Zonghan recounted the full story of his mourning; the Daoguang Emperor sighed with admiration and restored him to his former post. In the eighth year of Daoguang (1828), he was promoted to Vice Minister of Works, served as chief examiner for Zhejiang, and remained as Commissioner of Education. In the eleventh year of Daoguang (1831), his birth father died; overcome with grief, he rushed home despite illness and died at Quzhou; he was buried in mourning garments at the age of sixty-three.
15
宗瀚孝謹恬退，中歲以養親居林下十年，書法尤為世重。
Zonghan was filial, cautious, and retiring; in midlife he spent ten years in retirement caring for his parents, and his calligraphy was especially prized.
16
韓鼎晉，字樹屏，四川長壽人。 乾隆六十年進士，選庶吉士，授檢討。 嘉慶九年，改御史。 疏言天主教流傳之害，請申禁以絕根株，從之。 以母老請終養，十六年，服闋，補原官。 疏陳四川積弊六事，曰：禁科派以安閭閻，除啯匪以防積漸，查卡房以全民命，禁拐騙以警貪頑，嚴攤捐以養廉潔，覈戎政以歸實效。 又言京師賭風大熾，多屬王公大臣輿夫設局，倚勢骫法，帝命指實，下詔嚴治。 逾日，獲賭案三，大學士、步軍統領祿康輿夫為之魁。 親貴近臣，莫不悚息​​。
Han Dingjin, whose style was Shuping, came from Changshou in Sichuan. He earned his jinshi degree in the sixtieth year of Qianlong (1795), entered the Hanlin Academy as a probationary scholar, and was appointed reviser. In the ninth year of Jiaqing (1804), he was transferred to the Censorate. He memorialized on the harm of Catholicism's spread and asked for a renewed ban to uproot it; the throne approved. Citing his mother's advanced age, he asked leave to retire and care for her; in the sixteenth year of Jiaqing (1811), after the mourning period, he resumed his former post. He memorialized on six longstanding abuses in Sichuan: ban irregular levies to protect the people; eliminate gunkou bandits before they take root; investigate checkpoint stations to safeguard lives; ban kidnapping and fraud to warn the corrupt; strictly limit surcharges to encourage honest administration; and audit military affairs to restore real effectiveness. He also reported that gambling in the capital had grown rampant, often run by chair-bearers of princes and high ministers who bent the law through their influence; the emperor ordered specific identification and issued an edict for stern punishment. Within a day three gambling cases were uncovered; the ringleaders proved to be chair-bearers of Grand Secretary and Metropolitan Garrison Commander Lukang. Imperial kin and court favorites were all struck with fear.
17
巡視山東漕務，轉工科給事中、光祿寺少卿，督陝甘學政。 疏言：「榆、綏諸州縣倉貯空虛，宜設法籌補，其地資蒙古糧食接濟。 今腹裡邊外俱荒，當分別安置撫卹。 」又言：「南山善後事宜，宜行堅壁清野之法。 山內流民雜處，最為奸藪，當嚴行保甲，使姦宄無所匿。 軍中擄脅難民子女，請嚴禁。 南山附近及豫東並經兵燹，宜慎選牧令，以蘇民氣。 川北荒歉，與陝、甘毗連，鹽梟啯匪多出其中，請先事豫防。 」並下疆吏如所請行。 歷鴻臚寺卿、通政司副使、太常寺卿、左副都御史。
He inspected grain transport in Shandong, was transferred to supervising secretary of the Ministry of Works and Vice Director of the Court of Imperial Entertainments, and was appointed Commissioner of Education for Shaanxi and Gansu. He memorialized: "The granaries of Yulin, Suide, and other prefectures and counties stand empty and must be replenished; these regions depend on Mongol grain for relief." Now both the interior districts and the frontier are devastated; refugees should be settled and relieved according to their circumstances. He also stated: "For postwar recovery in the Southern Mountains, the method of fortified villages and cleared countryside should be applied." Within the mountains migrant populations are intermingled, forming the greatest nest of villainy; baojia registration should be strictly enforced so evildoers have nowhere to hide. Soldiers abduct and coerce refugee women and children; this must be strictly forbidden. The Southern Mountains and eastern Henan have all suffered warfare; magistrates should be carefully chosen to revive the people's morale. Northern Sichuan suffers famine and borders Shaanxi and Gansu; salt smugglers and gunkou bandits mostly emerge from these regions; preventive measures should be taken in advance. All recommendations were transmitted to provincial officials for implementation. He served successively as Director of the Court of Imperial Banquets, Vice Commissioner of the Office of Transmission, Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, and Left Vice Censor-in-Chief.
18
二十四年，命察視近畿水災，督黃村賑務。 督福建學政，疏言：「閩中吏治久窳，請不限資格，用廉幹吏補汀、漳、泉三郡望緊要缺，久其任以專責成。 漳、泉營伍通盜，請責提鎮立予重典，勿稍袒庇。 」道光六年，遷倉場侍郎，以病罷。 起補工部侍郎，京察，原品休致。 卒於家，祀鄉賢祠。
In the twenty-fourth year of Jiaqing (1819), he was ordered to inspect flood damage near the capital and supervise relief at Huangcun. As Commissioner of Education for Fujian he memorialized: "Official governance in Fujian has long been corrupt; I ask that qualifications be waived and honest, capable men appointed to key posts in Tingzhou, Zhangzhou, and Quanzhou, with extended terms to fix responsibility." Garrisons in Zhangzhou and Quanzhou collude with bandits; I ask that regional commanders and brigade generals be held to immediate severe punishment without the slightest favoritism. In the sixth year of Daoguang (1826), he was transferred to Vice Minister of the Granary and dismissed citing ill health. He was recalled and appointed Vice Minister of Works; at the capital evaluation he retired at his original rank. He died at home and was enshrined in the hall of local worthies.
19
朱方增，字虹舫，浙江海鹽人。 嘉慶六年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 典雲南鄉試，遷國子監司業。 十八年，教匪之變，方增劾直隸總督溫承惠貽誤地方，黜之。
Zhu Fangzeng, whose style was Hongfang, came from Haiyan in Zhejiang. He earned his jinshi degree in the sixth year of Jiaqing (1801), entered the Hanlin Academy as a probationary scholar, and was appointed compiler. He served as chief examiner for Yunnan and was transferred to Vice Director of the Imperial Academy. In the eighteenth year of Jiaqing (1813), during the White Lotus rebellion, Fangzeng impeached Zhili Governor-General Wen Chenghui for mishandling the province, and Wen was dismissed.
20
應詔陳言，論用人理財，略曰：「近今大臣中，罕有以進賢為務者。 蓋薦舉之事，易於徇私，黨援交結，不得不防，而大臣亦遂引嫌自避。 夫大臣避徇私之名，而忘以人事君之責，所謂因噎廢食，非公忠體國者所宜有也。 至於任用之方，則無過於考言詢事。 皇上博訪週諮，徐為印證。 於召對時，各就所長，諭使面陳，果能洞悉原委，又當試之以事，以觀其能踐與否。 如或敷奏並無條理，則其人固不足用，而大臣之識見優絀，心地公私，亦可見矣。 抑臣思臣工居職，苟非闒茸齷齪者流，孰不思自效？ 況蒙皇上訓飭至再至三，而猶故習相仍，驟難振拔者，良有數端：條例過繁，文案蒨屑，雖有強敏之吏，而精神疲於具文，其實關於政治民生，轉致不能詳覈。 一也。 差務絡繹，公私賠累，身家之卹不遑，民物之懷漸恝。 二也。 訐告之風，至今益甚。 嘗有以田土、鬥毆細故而叩閽京控者，有司畏其挾制，不得不姑息委蛇。 雖有急公自好者，其尋常蒨屑之事，豈皆一一可達聖聰？ 甚至匿名揭帖，無主名之可指。 蠹吏猾胥，奸民惡僕，求謀不遂，懲治過嚴，皆可造作飛語，訐及陰私。 足使任事之心，不寒而栗，委曲隱忍。 姦宄橫行，大都由此。 三也。 今皇上欲整飭因循積習，臣愚以為必先除此三者之弊，庶廓然無所疑畏，而得專精實政矣。 經國之方，理財尤要。 古者以三十年之通制國用，斟酌盈虛，量入為出，用能經常不匱。 今戶部歲入歲出，年一匯奏。 惟中外未合為一，條緒繁賾，極難釐剔。 且凡撥解即謂之出，並未實計所用。 新舊牽溷，凌雜益甚，而出納諸款，又因有無定之款，盈朒參差。 以故一歲之中，所出幾何，覈之所入，贏餘若干，不能得其實數。 請旨敕下戶部，歲入歲出，宜合中外為一。 核計贏餘總數，仍取前一二歲所贏餘，確實比較，然後審其輕重緩急，舉一切例內例外諸用款，有可裁省停緩者，酌加撙節。 庶合於古人通年制用之法，而度支充裕矣。 」
In response to an imperial edict soliciting opinions, he discussed appointing officials and managing finances, stating in summary: "Among senior ministers today, few make the advancement of worthy men their chief concern." Recommendation easily lends itself to favoritism, and factional ties must be guarded against; thus senior ministers avoid involvement out of scruple. For senior ministers to shun the appearance of favoritism and neglect the duty of serving the ruler through worthy appointments—this is giving up eating for fear of choking; it is not what a loyal servant of the state should do. As for the method of appointment, nothing surpasses examining a man's words and testing him on affairs. Your Majesty inquires broadly and consults thoroughly, gradually verifying the facts. At imperial audiences, each man should be questioned according to his strengths; if he truly grasps the full circumstances, he should then be tested with real duties to see whether he can perform them. If a candidate's presentation lacks all coherence, the man is unfit for office, and the recommending minister's judgment and whether his motives are public or private become equally clear. Yet your servant reflects that officials in office, unless they are incompetent and base, who does not wish to serve effectively? Moreover, though admonished by Your Majesty again and again, old habits persist and sudden reform is difficult—for several reasons: regulations are excessively numerous and paperwork trivially minute; even capable officials find their energy exhausted by documents, and matters that truly concern governance and the people's livelihood cannot be thoroughly examined. This is the first. Dispatch duties come in endless succession, draining public and private resources; officials have no leisure to care for their households, and concern for the people's welfare gradually fades. This is the second. The culture of malicious accusation grows worse by the day. Men have appealed to the capital over trifling land disputes or brawls; local officials fear being coerced and cannot but temporize. Even public-spirited officials cannot bring every trivial matter before the throne. Some even post anonymous placards with no name to identify. Corrupt officials, cunning runners, wicked commoners, and vicious servants whose schemes fail or who are punished too harshly can all fabricate slander touching private matters. This is enough to make officials who take on real duties shiver with dread and submit in compromise. The rampant spread of villainy stems largely from this. This is the third. Now Your Majesty wishes to rectify longstanding habits of routine indulgence; your servant humbly holds that these three abuses must be removed first, so that officials may act without fear and devote themselves fully to real administration. In governing the state, financial management is especially important. In antiquity a thirty-year comprehensive budget governed state expenditure, balancing surplus and deficit and measuring outgo by income, thereby maintaining regular sufficiency. Today the Ministry of Revenue reports annual income and expenditure once a year in summary. Yet capital and provincial accounts are not combined; the records are numerous and tangled, making thorough auditing extremely difficult. Moreover, whatever is allocated is counted as expenditure without calculating what was actually spent. Old and new accounts are entangled and confusion grows worse; receipts and disbursements further vary because of uncertain items, with surpluses and deficits unevenly reported. Thus within a single year how much was spent, how much received, and how much surplus remained cannot be known in real figures. I request an imperial order to the Ministry of Revenue to combine capital and provincial income and expenditure into a single account. Calculate the total surplus, compare it reliably with surpluses from the previous one or two years, then weigh priorities and, for all routine and exceptional expenditures, reduce or defer what can be cut. Thus the state may follow the ancient method of comprehensive annual budgeting, and the treasury will be ample. "

In the twentieth year of Jiaqing (1815), he took up duty in the Hall of Exertion in Diligence and compiled the Stone Canal Precious Collection and the Secret Hall Pearl Forest. He was soon appointed Commissioner of Education for Guangxi and rose to Reader in the Hanlin Academy. 方增熟諳朝章典故，輯國史名臣事蹟，為從政觀法錄，行於世。
Fangzeng was thoroughly versed in court regulations and historical precedent; he compiled the deeds of eminent ministers from the dynastic histories into Records for Observing Models in Governance, which circulated widely.

The appraisal states: As palace attendants, Wan Chengfeng, Zhou Xiying, and Qian Yue offered outstanding proposals wherever their inspection tours took them.
