1
倭仁，字艮峰，烏齊格里氏，蒙古正紅旗人，河南駐防。 道光九年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 歷中允、侍講、侍讀、庶子、侍講學士、侍讀學士。 二十二年，擢詹事。 二十四年，遷大理寺卿。 文宗即位，應詔陳言，略曰：「行政莫先於用人，用人莫先於君子小人之辨。 夫君子小人藏於心術者難知，發於事蹟者易見。 大抵君子訥拙，小人佞巧； 君子澹定，小人躁競； 君子愛惜人才，小人排擠異類； 君子圖遠大，以國家元氣為先，小人計目前，以聚斂刻薄為務。 剛正不撓、無所阿鄉者，君子也； 依違兩可、工於趨避者，小人也。 諫諍匡弼、進憂危之議，動人主之警心者，君子也； 喜言氣數、不畏天變，長人君之逸志者，小人也。 公私邪正，相反如此。 皇上天亶聰明，孰賢孰否，必能洞知。 第恐一人之心思耳目，揣摩者眾，混淆者多，幾微莫辨，情偽滋紛，愛憎稍涉偏私，取捨必至失當。 知人則哲，豈有他術，在皇上好學勤求，使聖志益明，聖德日固而已。 宋程顥雲，'古者人君必有誦訓箴諫之臣'。 請命老成之儒，講論道義，又擇天下賢俊，陪侍法從。 我朝康熙間，熊賜履上疏，亦以'延訪真儒'為說。 二臣所言，皆修養身心之要，用人行政之源也。 天下治亂系宰相，君德成就責講筵。 惟君德成就而後輔弼得人，輔弼得人而後天下可治。 」疏入，上稱其切直，因諭大小臣工進言以倭仁為法。 未幾，禮部侍郎曾國籓奏用人三策，上复憶倭仁言，手詔同褒勉焉。
Wo Ren, style name Genfeng, belonged to the Uqigeri clan. A Mongol of the Plain Red Banner, he was posted on garrison duty in Henan. He took the jinshi degree in the ninth year of Daoguang (1829), entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and was appointed reviser. He rose through the posts of middle attendant, expositor, reader, household tutor of the heir apparent, and expositor and reader of the Hanlin Academy. In the twenty-second year of his reign (1842), he was promoted to grand tutor of the heir apparent. Two years later he was appointed president of the Court of Judicial Review. When Emperor Wenzong acceded, Wo Ren answered the edict inviting memorials. In substance he wrote: 'In governing, nothing precedes the choice of personnel; in choosing personnel, nothing precedes telling the gentleman from the petty man.' What lies hidden in the heart is hard to discern; what shows in conduct is easy to see. As a rule, the gentleman is plain-spoken and unadorned, the petty man glib and artful; the gentleman composed and steady, the petty man restless and grasping; the gentleman values talent, the petty man squeezes out the unlike-minded; the gentleman looks to the long term and puts the state's underlying strength first, while the petty man counts only the present and makes harsh levies his trade. Upright, unbending, and beholden to no faction—that is the gentleman; equivocal, skilled at sidestepping—that is the petty man. He who remonstrates and steadies the throne, who offers warnings of peril to rouse the sovereign—that is the gentleman; He who talks of fate, shrugs at heaven's warnings, and indulges the ruler's wish for ease—that is the petty man. In public duty and private motive, in rectitude and depravity, they stand thus opposed. Your Majesty's Heaven-given discernment surely penetrates who is worthy and who is not. Yet I fear that a single mind and pair of ears, courted by so many who read its wishes, can be clouded by so many voices that the subtle is lost, truth and pretense tangle, a trace of favor skews judgment, and appointments go awry. 'To know men is wisdom'—there is no other method than Your Majesty's own love of learning and earnest inquiry, by which the imperial mind grows clearer and imperial virtue firmer day by day. Cheng Hao of the Song said, 'In antiquity every ruler had ministers to recite lessons and offer remonstrance.' I ask that venerable scholars be charged to expound the Way and its moral teaching, and that the finest talent of the realm be chosen to attend Your Majesty in study. In the Kangxi reign of our dynasty, Xiong Cilü likewise memorialized urging the court to 'seek out true Confucians.' What both ministers urged goes to the root of self-cultivation and to the source of sound appointment and governance. Order and disorder in the realm hinge on the chief minister; the maturing of the ruler's virtue rests on his teachers at the lecture seat. Only when the ruler's virtue is formed can worthy assistants be found; only then can the realm be brought to order. When the memorial arrived, the Emperor praised its blunt honesty and told officials at every level to take Wo Ren as their model in speaking up. Soon after, Zeng Guofan of the Ministry of Rites submitted three policies on appointments. The Emperor recalled Wo Ren's memorial as well and issued a personal edict praising both men together.
2
尋予副都統銜，充葉爾羌幫辦大臣。 大理寺少卿田雨公疏言倭仁用違其才，上曰：「邊疆要任，非投閒置散也。 若以外任皆左遷，豈國家文武兼資、內外並重之意乎？ 」咸豐二年，倭仁复上敬陳治本一疏，上謂其意在責難陳善，尚無不合，惟僅泛語治道，因戒以留心邊務，勿託空言。 候補道何桂珍上封事，言倭仁秉性忠貞，見理明決，生平言行不負所學，請任以艱鉅，未許。 三年，倭仁劾葉爾羌回部郡王阿奇木伯克愛瑪特攤派路費及護衛索贓等罪，詔斥未經確訊，率行參奏，下部議，降三級調用。
He was soon given the brevet rank of vice commander-in-chief and sent as assistant commissioner at Yarkand. Tian Yugong, vice president of the Court of Judicial Review, argued that Wo Ren had been misassigned. The Emperor replied: 'A key frontier post is not banishment to idleness.' If every provincial posting were treated as a demotion, how would that match the state's aim to use civil and military talent alike and to weigh inner and outer service equally? ' In the second year of Xianfeng (1852), Wo Ren again submitted a memorial on fundamental remedies. The Emperor allowed that its intent—to admonish while offering sound counsel—was acceptable, but said it spoke only in generalities about governance, and warned him to mind frontier duties rather than offer empty rhetoric. He Guizhen, an expectant circuit intendant, submitted a sealed memorial praising Wo Ren's loyalty, clarity of judgment, and conduct true to his learning, and asking that he be given heavy responsibility. The request was denied. In the third year (1853) he impeached Emirat, the Muslim beg of Yarkand, for extorting travel fees and bribes from his guards, among other offenses. The court rebuked him for accusing without full inquiry; after ministry review he was demoted three ranks and transferred.
3
四年，侍郎王茂廕等請命會同籌辦京師團練，上以軍務非所長，寢其議。 尋命以侍講候補入直上書房，授惇郡王讀。 五年，擢侍講學士。 歷光祿寺卿、盛京禮部侍郎。 七年，調戶部，管奉天府尹事，劾罷盛京副都統增慶、兵部侍郎富呢雅杭阿。 及頒詔中外，命充朝鮮正使。 召回京，授都察院左都御史。 同治元年，擢工部尚書。 兩宮皇太后以倭仁老成端謹，學問優長，命授穆宗讀。 倭仁輯古帝王事蹟，及古今名臣奏議，附說進之，賜名啟心金鑑，置弘德殿資講肄。 倭仁素嚴正，穆宗尤敬憚焉。
In the fourth year, Wang Maoyin and others asked that Wo Ren help organize militia training around the capital. The Emperor, judging military affairs not his forte, set the proposal aside. He was soon appointed, while still an expectant expositor, to the Imperial Study as tutor to the Prince of Dun. In the fifth year he was promoted to expositor of the Hanlin Academy. He served in turn as president of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and as vice president of the Ministry of Rites at Mukden. In the seventh year he moved to the Ministry of Revenue, oversaw the Fengtian prefecture, and impeached Zengqing, Mukden's vice commander-in-chief, and Funiyihang'a, vice president of the Ministry of War, securing their removal. When a major edict was issued to the realm, he was appointed chief envoy to Korea. Recalled to Beijing, he was made left censor-in-chief of the Censorate. In the first year of Tongzhi (1862) he was promoted to minister of Works. The two empress dowagers, finding Wo Ren seasoned, upright, and learned, appointed him tutor to Emperor Muzong. Wo Ren compiled deeds of ancient rulers and selected memorials of eminent ministers, annotated them, and presented the collection. It was named Mirror of the Heart to Open the Mind and kept in the Hall of Manifest Virtue for the emperor's instruction. Wo Ren was known for his severity and rectitude, and the young emperor especially revered and stood in awe of him.
4
尋兼翰林院掌院學士，調工部尚書、協辦大學士。 疏言：「河南自咸豐三年以後，粵、捻焚掠，蓋藏已空，州縣誅求仍復無厭。 朝廷不能盡擇州縣，則必慎擇督撫。 督撫不取之屬員，則屬員自無可挾以為恣睢之地。 今日河南積習，祗曰民刁詐，不曰官貪庸； 祗狃於愚民之抗官，不思所以致抗之由。 惟在朝廷慎察大吏，力挽積習，寇亂之源，庶幾可弭。 」是年秋，拜文淵閣大學士，疏劾新授廣東巡撫黃贊湯貪詐，解其職。
He soon added the chancellorship of the Hanlin, retained the Ministry of Works, and was named associate grand secretary. In a memorial he wrote: 'Since the third year of Xianfeng, Henan has been ravaged by Taiping and Nian bandits until granaries are bare, yet county and prefectural officials still squeeze the people without surcease.' If the court cannot vet every local magistrate, it must at least choose governors-general and governors with care. When governors do not profit from their staffs, subordinates have no leverage for arbitrary rule. The entrenched habit in Henan today is to blame only the people's cunning, never official greed or incompetence; only to fixate on ignorant peasants defying magistrates, without asking what drives such defiance. Only if the court scrutinizes its senior appointees and forcefully reverses these habits can the roots of rebellion perhaps be cut. That autumn he entered the Grand Secretariat. He impeached the newly appointed governor of Guangdong, Huang Zantang, for corruption and fraud, and secured his dismissal.
5
六年，同文館議考選正途五品以下京外官入館肄習天文算學，聘西人為教習。 倭仁謂根本之圖，在人心不在技藝，尤以西人教習為不可； 且謂必習天文算學，應求中國能精其法者，上疏請罷議。 於是詔倭仁保薦，別設一館，即由倭仁督率講求。 復奏意中並無其人，不敢妄保。 尋命在總理各國事務衙門行走。 倭仁屢疏懇辭，不允； 因稱疾篤，乞休，命解兼職，仍在弘德殿行走。 八年，疏言大婚典禮宜崇節儉，及武英殿災，复偕徐桐、翁同龢疏請勤修聖德，停罷一切工程，以弭災變，並嘉納之。 十年，晉文華殿大學士，以疾再乞休。 尋卒，贈太保，入祀賢良祠，諡文端。 光緒八年，河南巡撫李鶴年奏建專祠於開封，允之。
In the sixth year (1867) the Tongwen Guan proposed enrolling regular-route officials of the fifth rank and below from Beijing and the provinces to study astronomy and mathematics, with Westerners as instructors. Wo Ren argued that the fundamental remedy lay in men's hearts, not in technical skill, and above all that Western teachers were unacceptable; and that if such studies were needed, Chinese masters of the methods should be found. He memorialized to halt the plan. The court then ordered Wo Ren to nominate instructors and open a separate academy under his supervision. He replied that he knew no one fit for the task and dared not recommend blindly. He was soon ordered to serve in the Zongli Yamen for foreign affairs. Wo Ren repeatedly begged to be excused, without success; he then pleaded grave illness and asked to retire. The court relieved him of his extra posts but kept him at the Hall of Manifest Virtue. In the eighth year he urged frugality for the imperial wedding. After fire destroyed the Hall of Military Glory, he joined Xu Tong and Weng Tonghe in asking the emperor to cultivate virtue and halt all construction to appease heaven's warnings. The court approved. In the tenth year he was promoted to grand secretary of the Hall of Literary Glory and again asked to retire on account of illness. He died soon after. Posthumously he was made Grand Guardian, enshrined in the Shrine of Worthy Officials, and given the posthumous name Wenduan. In the eighth year of Guangxu (1882), Governor Li Henian of Henan memorialized for a private shrine at Kaifeng, and the request was granted.
6
初，曾國籓官京師，與倭仁、李棠階、吳廷棟、何桂珍、竇垿講求宋儒之學。 其後國籓出平大難，為中興名臣冠； 倭仁作帝師，正色不阿； 棠階、廷棟亦卓然有以自見焉。 倭仁著有遺書十三卷。 子福咸，江蘇鹽法道，署安徽徽寧池太廣道，咸豐十年，殉難寧國，贈太僕寺卿，騎都尉世職； 福裕，奉天府府尹。 從子福潤，安徽巡撫。 光緒二十六年，外國兵入京師，闔家死焉。
Earlier, while Zeng Guofan served in Beijing, he studied Song Neo-Confucianism together with Wo Ren, Li Tangjie, Wu Tingdong, He Guizhen, and Dou Kan. Guofan later went forth to quell the great rebellions and became the foremost statesman of the restoration; Wo Ren served as imperial tutor, unbending in manner; Tangjie and Tingdong likewise made names for themselves in their own spheres. Wo Ren left collected writings in thirteen juan. His son Fuxian served as Jiangsu salt intendant and acting Anhui circuit intendant; in the tenth year of Xianfeng (1860) he died defending Ningguo and was posthumously made vice president of the Court of the Imperial Stud with a hereditary captaincy; Fuyu became prefect of Fengtian. His nephew Furun rose to governor of Anhui. In the twenty-sixth year of Guangxu (1900), when foreign troops entered Beijing, the entire family perished.
7
李棠階，字文園，河南河內人。 道光二年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 五遷至侍讀。 二十二年，督廣東學政，擢太常寺少卿。 會巡撫黃恩彤奏請予鄉試年老武生職銜，嚴旨責譴，棠階亦因違例送考，議降三級調用，遂引疾家居。 文宗即位，复日講，曾國籓薦棠階醇正堪備講官，召來京。 既而日講中輟，棠階以病未赴。
Li Tangjie, style name Wenyuan, was from Henei in Henan. He took the jinshi in the second year of Daoguang (1822), entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and was appointed reviser. After five promotions he became a Hanlin reader. In the twenty-second year (1842) he served as Guangdong education commissioner and was promoted to vice president of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. When Governor Huang Entong asked to grant honorary titles to elderly military licentiates at the provincial exams, the court rebuked him sharply. Tangjie was also faulted for irregularly submitting candidates; after review he was to be demoted three ranks, and he retired home on grounds of illness. When Emperor Wenzong acceded, daily lectures resumed. Zeng Guofan recommended Tangjie as upright and fit for the lecture seat, and he was summoned to Beijing. The lectures soon lapsed again, and Tangjie, pleading illness, did not take up the post.
8
咸豐三年，粵匪北犯，河北土寇蜂起，用尚書周祖培薦，命治河北團練。 棠階聯絡村鎮，名曰「友助社」。 賊踞溫縣東河灘柳林，四出焚掠，棠階督團練擊之，村民未習戰，且無火器，殺賊數十人，卒不敵。 會山東巡撫李僡率兵至，賊引去。 賊自渡黃河，始知民間有備，稍稍牽綴。 洎河北肅清，敘勞，加四品卿銜，賜花翎。
In the third year of Xianfeng (1853), as Taiping forces pushed north and Hebei bandits rose everywhere, on Zhou Zupei's recommendation he was ordered to organize militia in Hebei. Tangjie linked villages and towns into units he called the Society of Mutual Aid. Bandits held the willow groves on Wen County's east river beach and raided widely. Tangjie led the militia against them, but the peasants were untrained and unarmed; though they killed dozens of bandits, they could not hold. When Shandong Governor Li Xin arrived with troops, the bandits withdrew. Once the bandits crossed the Yellow River themselves, they found the countryside prepared and their advance slowed. When Hebei was cleared, his service was rewarded with a brevet fourth-rank grand secretary title and a peacock feather.
9
同治元年，詔起用舊臣，棠階應召至。 上疏言：「用人行政，惟在治心。 治心之要，莫先克己。 請於師保匡弼之馀，豫杜左右近習之漸。 暇時進講通鑑、大學衍義諸書，以收物格意誠之效。 」又言：「紀綱之飭，在於嚴明賞罰。 凡朝廷通諭諸事，務飭疆臣實力奉行，庶中外情志可通，而禍亂可弭。 」兩宮嘉納焉。 授大理寺卿。 先是兩江總督何桂清僨事逮治，部讞從重擬斬決，廷臣有右之者，言部臣有意畸重，仍從本律監候。 棠階疏謂桂清貽誤封疆罪大，不當輕比，非公論。 後桂清卒伏法。 連擢禮部侍郎、左都御史，署戶部尚書。 召對，言：「治天下惟在安民，安民必先察吏。 今日之盜賊，即昔日之良民，皆地方有司貪虐激之成變。 為今日平亂計，非輕徭薄賦不能治本。 然非擇大吏，則守令不得其人，亦終不能收令行禁止之效。 」因極言河南亂事，及諸行省利病甚悉。 命為軍機大臣，具疏力辭，弗許。 二年，授工部尚書。
In the first year of Tongzhi (1862) an edict recalled veteran officials; Tangjie answered and came to court. He memorialized: 'Appointments and governance depend entirely on governing the heart.' The key to governing the heart is self-restraint above all. Beyond the tutors who guide and correct him, I ask that the emperor guard early against the gradual influence of those nearest his person. In spare hours let him study the Comprehensive Mirror, the Extended Meaning of the Great Learning, and similar works, to achieve the discipline of investigating things and making the will sincere. He added: 'To restore discipline, rewards and punishments must be strict and clear.' Every order from the court must be enforced in earnest by provincial officials, so that court and provinces may align and rebellion be checked. The two empress dowagers approved his counsel. He was appointed president of the Court of Judicial Review. Earlier, He Guiqing, governor-general of Jiangnan, had been arrested for abandoning his post. The ministries recommended aggravated decapitation, but some courtiers defended him, arguing the ministries had skewed the penalty; the sentence was reduced to imprisonment awaiting execution. Tangjie memorialized that He Guiqing's crime of abandoning the frontier was grave and should not be lightly punished; to do so was not justice. He Guiqing was eventually executed. He rose in quick succession to vice president of Rites, left censor-in-chief, and acting minister of Revenue. Called to audience, he said: 'Governing the realm depends on securing the people; securing the people begins with scrutinizing officials.' The bandits of today were yesterday's law-abiding people, driven to revolt by greedy and abusive local officials. To pacify disorder today, lighter burdens and lower taxes are the only way to reach the root of the problem. But without choosing the right senior appointees, local magistrates will remain unfit, and orders will never truly be enforced. He went on at length about Henan's turmoil and the strengths and weaknesses of the provinces. He was named to the Grand Council but memorialized repeatedly to decline; the court refused. In the second year he was appointed minister of Works.
10
三年，江寧克復，論功，加太子少保。 大憝既平，上諭中外臣工以兢業交勉。 棠階語恭親王及同直諸大臣，謂當設誠致行，久而不懈，勿徒以空言相文飾，王深然之。 翼日召對，王反复陳君臣交儆之義，棠階與同僚繼言之，兩宮改容嘉納。 尋調禮部尚書。 太后命南書房、上書房諸臣纂輯前史事蹟，賜名治平寶鑑，命諸大臣進講。 棠階因講漢文帝卻千里馬事，反复推言人主不宜有所嗜好，以啟窺伺之端。 自是每進講必原本經義，極論史事，歸於責難陳善。 四年，恭親王被劾退出軍機，棠階謂王有定難功，時方多故，不當輕棄親賢，入對，力言王非有心之失。 會惇、醇兩王亦奏言奕訢不可遽罷，乃復命入直。 僧格林沁戰歿曹州，棠階以朝廷賞多罰少，疆臣每存藐玩，上疏極言其弊，於是有申飭直省督撫之諭。
In the third year, after Jiangning was recovered, he was rewarded with the brevet title of Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent. After the great rebels were crushed, the Emperor urged officials throughout the realm to rededicate themselves to diligent service. Tangjie told Prince Gong and his colleagues on the Grand Council that they must act with sincere purpose and persevere, not trade empty phrases; the prince strongly agreed. At the next audience the prince expounded mutual vigilance between ruler and ministers; Tangjie and others echoed him, and the empress dowagers visibly approved. He was soon transferred to minister of Rites. The empress dowager ordered the Southern and Imperial Studies to compile historical precedents, named the collection Precious Mirror of Ordered Governance, and directed the senior ministers to lecture from it. In lecturing on Emperor Wen of Han's refusal of tribute horses, he urged that the ruler must not indulge private tastes, lest others exploit them. Thereafter every lecture began in the classics, drew lessons from history, and ended in frank admonition. In the fourth year, when Prince Gong was impeached and left the Grand Council, Tangjie argued that his service in the crisis merited retention; at audience he insisted the prince's error was not intentional. The Princes of Dun and Chun also pleaded for Yixin, and he was restored to the Grand Council. After Sengge Rinchen died at Caozhou, Tangjie memorialized that excessive rewards and lax punishments made frontier officials contemptuous of orders; the court then rebuked provincial governors.
11
棠階自入直樞廷，軍書旁午，一事稍有未安，輒憂形於色。 積勞致疾，十一月，卒，年六十八。 上震悼，遣貝勒載治奠醊，賜金治喪，贈太子太保，諡文清。
Once on the Grand Council, with military papers flooding in, the slightest unsettled matter showed on his face. Exhaustion brought on illness; in the eleventh month he died at sixty-eight. The Emperor mourned deeply, sent Prince Zaiye to offer libations, granted funeral funds, posthumously made him Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent, and gave the posthumous name Wenqing.
12
棠階初入翰林，即潛心理學，嘗手鈔湯斌遺書以自勗。 會通程、硃、陸、王學說，無所偏主，要以克己復禮、身體實行為歸。 日記自省，畢生不懈。 家故貧，既貴，儉約無改。 嘗曰：「憂患者生之門。 吾終身不敢忘忍飢待米時也！ 」
Early in his Hanlin career he devoted himself to Neo-Confucianism and copied Tang Bin's writings by hand for self-discipline. He studied the Cheng brothers, Zhu Xi, Lu Xiangshan, and Wang Yangming without partisan bias, aiming at self-restraint, ritual, and practice in daily life. He kept a diary of self-examination and never slackened. His family had been poor; even in high office he lived as frugally as before. He once said, 'Adversity is the gate of life.' I shall never forget going hungry while waiting for rice to arrive!' (End of quotation.)
13
吳廷棟，字竹如，安徽霍山人。 道光五年拔貢，授刑部七品小京官，洊遷郎中。 廷棟少好宋儒之學，入官益植節厲行，蹇蹇自靖。 咸豐二年，京察一等。 時侍郎書元兼崇文門副監督，獲販私釀者三十六人，承審者以漏稅擬滿杖。 已而覆訊得書元家人詐贓狀，部臣據以入奏。 文宗疑書元孤立，降旨切責，會廷棟召對，上詢是獄。 廷棟從容敷奏，且詳陳治道之要，言利之害，君子小人之辨，上首肯，獄竟得解。 因詢廷棟讀何書，廷棟以程、硃對。 上曰：「學程、硃者每多迂拘。 」對曰：「此不善學之過。 程、硃以明德為體，新民為用，天下未有有體而無用者。 皇上讀書窮理，以裕知人之識； 清心寡欲，以養坐照之明。 寤寐求賢，內外得人，天下何憂不治？ 」上韙之。
Wu Tingdong, style name Zhuru, was from Huoshan in Anhui. In the fifth year of Daoguang (1825) he passed the provincial tribute selection and entered the Ministry of Justice as a junior secretary, rising to director. From youth he loved Song Neo-Confucianism; in office he cultivated integrity and stern self-discipline. In the second year of Xianfeng he received top marks in the capital personnel review. Vice President Shuyuan, also deputy supervisor of the Chongwen Gate, seized thirty-six smugglers of illicit liquor; the examiners proposed the maximum bamboo penalty for tax evasion. Re-examination then uncovered extortion by Shuyuan's family, and the ministries memorialized accordingly. Emperor Wenzong suspected Shuyuan was being singled out and rebuked the ministries sharply; when Tingdong was summoned, the Emperor asked about the case. Tingdong explained the case calmly, then expounded governance, the dangers of profit-seeking, and the gentleman–petty man distinction; the Emperor approved and the case was closed. The Emperor asked what he studied; Tingdong named the Cheng brothers and Zhu Xi. The Emperor said, 'Students of Cheng and Zhu are often rigid.' He replied, 'That comes from poor learning.' Cheng and Zhu take illustrious virtue as substance and renewing the people as function; nothing has substance without function. If Your Majesty reads to exhaust principle, you will enlarge your discernment of men; purify the heart and lessen desires to nurture clear judgment. Seek the worthy without ceasing; with the right men within and without, how can the realm fail to be governed? The Emperor approved.
14
尋出為直隸河間知府。 粵匪北犯畿輔，廷棟練民兵巡防，民倚以為固。 內閣學士勝保督師至河間，責供張甚急，知縣王灴迫於應付，自刎不殊。 廷棟詣大營陳其事，勝保矍然，飭部下聽命。 連擢永定河道、直隸按察使。 以河間京師門戶，廷棟善守禦，得民心，仍留知府任。 四年，軍事定，乃之按察使任。 六年，遷山東布政使。 時部臣奏請畿內賦稅兼收大錢鈔票各三成，上下交病，總督譚廷襄不敢言。 會廷棟入覲，面奏：「大錢鈔票實不流通。 立法必先便於民方可行，必先信於民方能久。 今條科太多，朝夕更改，國家先不能自信，何以取信於民？ 」上首肯者再。 既而廷襄入朝，遂奏罷前議。 山東吏治久窳，廷棟獎廉懲貪。 方議海口立局收貨捐，持不可。 八年，坐奏銷遲誤，降補直隸按察使。 十一年，復調山東。 同治二年，入為大理寺卿，尋擢刑部侍郎。
He was soon sent out as prefect of Hejian in Zhili. When Taiping forces pushed into the capital region, he trained militia for defense and the people relied on him. Grand Secretary Sengge Rinchen arrived to command troops and demanded supplies urgently; Prefect Wang Hong, unable to meet them, attempted suicide. Tingdong went to the camp and reported the case; Sengge Rinchen was startled and restrained his men. He was promoted to Yongding River commissioner and Zhili judicial commissioner. Because Hejian guarded the approach to the capital and Tingdong had won the people's trust, he was kept as prefect. In the fourth year, after the fighting ended, he took up the judicial commissionership. In the sixth year he became Shandong administration commissioner. Ministries proposed collecting capital-region taxes thirty percent each in large cash, notes, and standard coin, harming all parties; Governor Tan Tingxiang dared not object. At audience he said frankly, 'Large cash and notes do not circulate.' Laws must first benefit the people to work, and first win trust to endure. Too many tax items change too often; if the state lacks confidence, how can the people trust it? The Emperor nodded twice in agreement. When Tan Tingxiang came to court, he memorialized to repeal the proposal. Shandong government had long been corrupt; Tingdong rewarded the honest and punished the greedy. When officials proposed a commodity levy bureau at the estuary, he opposed it. In the eighth year he was demoted to Zhili judicial commissioner for delayed accounts. In the eleventh year he returned to Shandong. In the second year of Tongzhi he became president of the Court of Judicial Review and soon vice president of Punishments.
15
三年，江南平，廷棟上疏，略曰：「萬方之治亂在朝政，百工之敬肆視君心。 事不貴文，貴其實； 下不從令，從所好。 夫治亂決於敬肆，敬肆根於喜懼。 自古功成志遂，人主喜心一生而驕心已伏，宦寺有乘其喜而貢諂媚者矣，左右有乘其喜而肆蒙蔽者矣，容悅之臣有因此而工諛佞者矣，屏逐之姦有因此而巧夤緣者矣。 諂媚貢則柄暗竊，蒙蔽肆則權下移，諛佞工則主志惑，夤緣巧則宵小升。 於是受蠱惑，塞聰明，遠老成，惡忠鯁。 從前戒懼之念，一喜敗之； 此後侈縱之行，一喜開之。 方且矜予智，樂莫違，逞獨斷，快從欲，一人肆於上，群小扇於下，流毒蒼生，貽禍社稷，稽諸史冊，後先一轍。 推原其端，祗一念由喜入驕而已。 軍興以來，十數省億萬生靈慘遭鋒鏑，即倡亂之奸民，何一非朝廷赤子？ 大兵所加，盡被誅夷。 皇太后、皇上體上天好生之心，必有哀矜不忍喜者。 況旗兵乏食，根本空虛，新疆缺餉，邊陲搖動。 兼之強鄰偪處，邪教肆行，豈惟不可喜，而實屬可懼。 假使萬幾之馀，或有一念之肆，臣工效之，視彰癉為故事，輕告戒為具文，積習相沿，工為粉飾，將仍成為叢脞怠荒之局矣。 是非堅定刻苦，持之以恆，積數十年恭儉憂勤，有未易培國脈復元氣者。 夫上行必下效，內治則外安，而其道莫大於敬，其幾必始於懼。 懼天命無常，則不敢恃天； 懼民碞可畏，則不敢玩民。 懼者敬之始，敬者懼之終。 大智愈明，神武愈彰，紹祖宗富有之大業，開子孫無疆之丕基，是皆由皇心之懼始而敬成也。 易曰：'危者使平，易者使傾，懼以終始，其要無咎。 '詩曰：'敬之敬之，天維顯思！ '可弗以為永鑑歟？ 」疏上，優詔嘉納，命存其疏於弘​​德殿以備省覽。 皇太后召對時，諭曰：「皇帝衝齡踐阼，國家大事，汝宜直言無隱，以無負先帝知遇。 」廷棟感激出涕。 五年，以衰病乞休，許之，歸寓江寧。 十二年，卒，年八十有一。 遺疏入，詔褒其廉靜自持，賜卹如例。 直隸、山東皆祀名宦祠。
In the third year, after the south was pacified, he memorialized: 'Order and disorder depend on the court; official discipline reflects the ruler's heart.' What matters is substance, not rhetoric; subordinates follow what the ruler favors, not what he commands. Order and disorder turn on respect or laxity, which root in joy and fear. From antiquity, when success is won the ruler's joy breeds pride: eunuchs flatter, attendants deceive, sycophants multiply, and banished schemers claw their way back. Flattery steals power in secret; deception shifts authority downward; sycophancy confuses the ruler; schemers elevate petty men. Then the ruler is deluded, deafened, estranged from the seasoned, and hostile to the bluntly loyal. The earlier spirit of vigilance is undone by one burst of joy; and the later path of extravagance is opened by that same joy. The ruler trusts his wit, delights in having his way, indulges arbitrary rule and his desires; one man revels above while petty men fan the flames below, poisoning the people and endangering the state—as history shows again and again. The root is a single thought: joy slipping into pride. Since the wars began, tens of millions in a dozen provinces have suffered under arms; even the rebels were once the court's own subjects. Where imperial armies marched, all were cut down. The empress dowager and Emperor, embodying Heaven's love of life, must feel compassion, not joy. Banner troops lack grain, the dynasty's base is hollow, Xinjiang lacks funds, and the frontier is unsettled. Strong neighbors press close and sectarian movements spread—this is not a time for joy but for fear. If even in a moment of leisure the ruler indulges himself and officials follow, treating discipline as routine and warnings as empty forms, the court will sink again into petty laxity and neglect. Only firm, painstaking constancy over decades of reverence, frugality, and anxious diligence can rebuild the state's strength. The ruler's example shapes all below; inner order secures outer peace—and the greatest principle is reverence, which must begin in fear. Fearing Heaven's inconstant mandate, one dare not rely on Heaven; fearing the people's formidable wrath, one dare not trifle with the people. Fear is the beginning of respect; respect is its fulfillment. Through fear and respect, wisdom grows, martial glory shines, the ancestors' legacy continues, and the foundation for posterity is laid. The Changes says, 'Peril leads to peace, ease to ruin—begin and end in fear, and blame may be avoided.' The Odes say, 'Revere it, revere it—Heaven clearly sees!' Can this not serve as an everlasting mirror? When the memorial arrived, the court praised it and ordered it kept in the Hall of Manifest Virtue for reference. At audience the empress dowager told him: 'The Emperor is young; on great affairs speak frankly and honor the late emperor's trust in you.' Tingdong wept with gratitude. In the fifth year he retired on grounds of age and illness and settled in Jiangning. In the twelfth year he died at eighty-one. His final memorial was praised for integrity and quiet self-restraint; condolence followed precedent. Zhili and Shandong enshrined him in their halls of renowned officials.
16
廷棟學以不欺為本。 官臬司時，畿輔連有逆倫獄，總督慮一月頻入奏幹上怒，廷棟曰：「此吾儕不能教化之過，待罪不暇，敢欺飾耶？ 」及去官，僑居清貧，不受餽遺。 著有拙修集十卷。
Tingdong made honesty the root of his learning. As judicial commissioner, when the capital region saw repeated parricide cases, the governor feared frequent memorials would anger the throne. Tingdong said, 'We failed to teach them—how dare we cover it up?' After leaving office he lived in honest poverty and accepted no gifts. He left the Clumsy Cultivation Collection in ten juan.
17
論曰：倭仁晚為兩宮所敬禮，際會中興，輔導衝主，兢兢於君心敬肆之間，當時舉朝嚴憚，風氣賴以維持。 惟未達世變，於自強要政，鄙夷不屑言，後轉為異論者所藉口。 李棠階、吳廷棟正色立朝，不負所學，翕然笙磬同音，而棠階尤平實持大體，可謂體用兼備矣。
The appraisal: Wo Ren in his later years was honored by the two palaces; at the restoration he tutored the young emperor and guarded the boundary between reverence and laxity in the imperial heart; the whole court stood in awe of him, and public morals were sustained. His failing was that he did not grasp how the world had changed: he scorned the policies of self-strengthening and refused to discuss them, a stance later seized upon by critics of reform. Li Tangjie and Wu Tingdong served at court with unbending integrity true to their learning, moving in concert like matched instruments; Tangjie above all was plain, practical, and steady in grasping the larger pattern—truly a man in whom principle and practice were one.
