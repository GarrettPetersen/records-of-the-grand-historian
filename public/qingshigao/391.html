1
倭仁，字艮峰，烏齊格里氏，蒙古正紅旗人，河南駐防。 道光九年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 歷中允、侍講、侍讀、庶子、侍講學士、侍讀學士。 二十二年，擢詹事。 二十四年，遷大理寺卿。 文宗即位，應詔陳言，略曰：「行政莫先於用人，用人莫先於君子小人之辨。 夫君子小人藏於心術者難知，發於事蹟者易見。 大抵君子訥拙，小人佞巧； 君子澹定，小人躁競； 君子愛惜人才，小人排擠異類； 君子圖遠大，以國家元氣為先，小人計目前，以聚斂刻薄為務。 剛正不撓、無所阿鄉者，君子也； 依違兩可、工於趨避者，小人也。 諫諍匡弼、進憂危之議，動人主之警心者，君子也； 喜言氣數、不畏天變，長人君之逸志者，小人也。 公私邪正，相反如此。 皇上天亶聰明，孰賢孰否，必能洞知。 第恐一人之心思耳目，揣摩者眾，混淆者多，幾微莫辨，情偽滋紛，愛憎稍涉偏私，取捨必至失當。 知人則哲，豈有他術，在皇上好學勤求，使聖志益明，聖德日固而已。 宋程顥雲，'古者人君必有誦訓箴諫之臣'。 請命老成之儒，講論道義，又擇天下賢俊，陪侍法從。 我朝康熙間，熊賜履上疏，亦以'延訪真儒'為說。 二臣所言，皆修養身心之要，用人行政之源也。 天下治亂系宰相，君德成就責講筵。 惟君德成就而後輔弼得人，輔弼得人而後天下可治。 」疏入，上稱其切直，因諭大小臣工進言以倭仁為法。 未幾，禮部侍郎曾國籓奏用人三策，上复憶倭仁言，手詔同褒勉焉。
Wo Ren, styled Genfeng, belonged to the Ujigri clan—a Mongol of the Plain Red Banner stationed in Henan. In Daoguang 9 (1829) he passed the jinshi examination, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and was appointed reviser. He rose through the posts of junior censor, reader-in-waiting, expositor-in-waiting, junior tutor, and both reader- and expositor-in-waiting of the Hanlin Academy. In the twenty-second year he was promoted to junior mentor of the heir apparent. In the twenty-fourth year he was transferred to president of the Court of Judicial Review. When the Xianfeng Emperor took the throne, Wo Ren answered the call for memorials and wrote, in essence: "Governing well begins with choosing the right people, and choosing people begins with telling gentlemen from petty men." What gentlemen and petty men keep hidden in their hearts is hard to discern, but what shows in their actions is easy to see. As a rule, gentlemen are plain-spoken and unadorned, petty men glib and cunning; gentlemen are calm and steady, petty men restless and grasping; gentlemen cherish talent, petty men squeeze out anyone unlike themselves; gentlemen look to the long term and put the nation's underlying strength first; petty men fix on the moment and make harsh levies and meanness their trade. Those who are upright, unbending, and beholden to no faction are gentlemen; those who sit on the fence and excel at dodging responsibility are petty men. Those who speak bluntly to correct the ruler, who offer warnings of danger and stir the sovereign to vigilance, are gentlemen; those who talk of fate and omens without dread of heaven's warnings, and who encourage the ruler's taste for ease, are petty men. Public duty and private interest, integrity and corruption—they stand opposed in just this way. Your Majesty is gifted by Heaven with keen insight; who is worthy and who is not, you can surely discern. Yet I fear that a single mind and pair of ears, however sharp, face a crowd of courtiers guessing your wishes and clouding your judgment—until the subtle is lost, truth and pretense tangle, a trace of favoritism skews love and hate, and appointments go awry. "To know men is wisdom"—there is no other art: Your Majesty need only study diligently, so that your purpose grows clearer and your virtue firmer day by day. Cheng Hao of the Song said, "In antiquity every ruler kept counselors who recited lessons and offered frank remonstrance." I ask that venerable scholars be charged to expound the Way, and that the finest minds in the empire be chosen to attend you as moral exemplars. In our own dynasty, during Kangxi's reign, Xiong Cilü submitted a memorial making the same plea—to seek out true scholars. What these two ministers said goes to the heart of self-cultivation—and to the root of appointing officials and governing well. Whether the realm is ordered or chaotic hangs on the chief minister; whether the ruler's virtue matures rests on his teachers at the lecture seat. Only when the ruler's virtue is formed can he secure worthy ministers; only with worthy ministers can the realm be well governed. When the memorial arrived, the emperor praised its blunt honesty and told officials at every level to speak up as Wo Ren had done. Soon after, Zeng Guofan of the Ministry of Rites submitted three proposals on appointments; the emperor recalled Wo Ren's memorial as well and by personal edict praised both men.
2
尋予副都統銜，充葉爾羌幫辦大臣。 大理寺少卿田雨公疏言倭仁用違其才，上曰：「邊疆要任，非投閒置散也。 若以外任皆左遷，豈國家文武兼資、內外並重之意乎？ 」咸豐二年，倭仁复上敬陳治本一疏，上謂其意在責難陳善，尚無不合，惟僅泛語治道，因戒以留心邊務，勿託空言。 候補道何桂珍上封事，言倭仁秉性忠貞，見理明決，生平言行不負所學，請任以艱鉅，未許。 三年，倭仁劾葉爾羌回部郡王阿奇木伯克愛瑪特攤派路費及護衛索贓等罪，詔斥未經確訊，率行參奏，下部議，降三級調用。
He was soon given the brevet rank of vice commander-in-chief and appointed assistant commissioner at Yarkand. Tian Yugong, vice president of the Court of Judicial Review, argued that Wo Ren was being misused; the emperor replied, "A key frontier post is no banishment to idleness." If every post beyond the capital were treated as exile, how would that square with a state that values civil and military talent alike and weighs inner and outer service equally?" In Xianfeng 2, Wo Ren submitted another memorial on root remedies; the emperor said his intent to admonish and offer sound counsel was acceptable, but that he spoke only in generalities about governance, and warned him to mind frontier duties rather than indulge in empty talk. He Guizhen, an expectant circuit intendant, submitted a sealed memorial praising Wo Ren's loyalty, clarity of judgment, and conduct worthy of his learning, and asking that he be given heavy responsibility—the request was denied. In the third year Wo Ren impeached Aimait, the Muslim beg at Yarkand, for levying travel fees and taking bribes through his guards; the court rebuked him for accusing without a full inquiry, and after ministry review he was demoted three ranks and transferred.
3
四年，侍郎王茂廕等請命會同籌辦京師團練，上以軍務非所長，寢其議。 尋命以侍講候補入直上書房，授惇郡王讀。 五年，擢侍講學士。 歷光祿寺卿、盛京禮部侍郎。 七年，調戶部，管奉天府尹事，劾罷盛京副都統增慶、兵部侍郎富呢雅杭阿。 及頒詔中外，命充朝鮮正使。 召回京，授都察院左都御史。 同治元年，擢工部尚書。 兩宮皇太后以倭仁老成端謹，學問優長，命授穆宗讀。 倭仁輯古帝王事蹟，及古今名臣奏議，附說進之，賜名啟心金鑑，置弘德殿資講肄。 倭仁素嚴正，穆宗尤敬憚焉。
In the fourth year Wang Maoyin and others asked that Wo Ren help organize capital militia training; the emperor, judging military affairs not his forte, set the idea aside. He was soon ordered into the Southern Study as an expectant reader and appointed tutor to the Prince of Dun. In the fifth year he was promoted to reader-in-waiting of the Hanlin Academy. He served successively as president of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and vice president of the Mukden Ministry of Rites. In the seventh year he moved to the Ministry of Revenue and oversaw the Fengtian prefecture; he impeached and brought down Mukden vice commander Zengqing and War Ministry vice president Funiyehang'a. When a major edict was issued at home and abroad, he was appointed chief envoy to Korea. Recalled to the capital, he was made left censor-in-chief of the Censorate. In Tongzhi 1 (1862) he was promoted to minister of works. The two empress dowagers, finding Wo Ren seasoned, upright, and learned, appointed him tutor to the Tongzhi Emperor. Wo Ren compiled deeds of ancient rulers and memorials of great ministers, with his own commentary, and presented the work; it was named Mirror to Open the Mind and kept in Hongde Hall for the emperor's instruction. Wo Ren was famously stern and upright, and the young emperor especially respected and feared him.
4
尋兼翰林院掌院學士，調工部尚書、協辦大學士。 疏言：「河南自咸豐三年以後，粵、捻焚掠，蓋藏已空，州縣誅求仍復無厭。 朝廷不能盡擇州縣，則必慎擇督撫。 督撫不取之屬員，則屬員自無可挾以為恣睢之地。 今日河南積習，祗曰民刁詐，不曰官貪庸； 祗狃於愚民之抗官，不思所以致抗之由。 惟在朝廷慎察大吏，力挽積習，寇亂之源，庶幾可弭。 」是年秋，拜文淵閣大學士，疏劾新授廣東巡撫黃贊湯貪詐，解其職。
He soon added the post of Hanlin chancellor, was transferred to minister of works, and made grand secretary without portfolio. He memorialized: "Since Xianfeng 3 in Henan, Taiping and Nian raids have emptied every granary, yet county and prefectural levies know no limit." If the court cannot pick every county magistrate, it must choose governors-general and governors with care. When governors refuse graft from subordinates, those subordinates lose the leverage to tyrannize the people. Henan's entrenched habit today is to blame crafty, deceitful commoners—not greedy, incompetent officials; officials fixate on peasants defying authority and never ask what drives that defiance. Only if the court scrutinizes its senior appointees and forcefully reverses these habits can the roots of rebellion perhaps be cut." " That autumn he became grand secretary of the Wenyuan Pavilion; he memorialized against the newly appointed Guangdong governor Huang Zantang for corruption and fraud, and Huang was dismissed.
5
六年，同文館議考選正途五品以下京外官入館肄習天文算學，聘西人為教習。 倭仁謂根本之圖，在人心不在技藝，尤以西人教習為不可； 且謂必習天文算學，應求中國能精其法者，上疏請罷議。 於是詔倭仁保薦，別設一館，即由倭仁督率講求。 復奏意中並無其人，不敢妄保。 尋命在總理各國事務衙門行走。 倭仁屢疏懇辭，不允； 因稱疾篤，乞休，命解兼職，仍在弘德殿行走。 八年，疏言大婚典禮宜崇節儉，及武英殿災，复偕徐桐、翁同龢疏請勤修聖德，停罷一切工程，以弭災變，並嘉納之。 十年，晉文華殿大學士，以疾再乞休。 尋卒，贈太保，入祀賢良祠，諡文端。 光緒八年，河南巡撫李鶴年奏建專祠於開封，允之。
In the sixth year the Tongwen Guan proposed enrolling regular-route officials below the fifth rank to study astronomy and mathematics, with Westerners as instructors. Wo Ren argued that the root remedy lay in men's hearts, not in technical skills, and above all that Western teachers were unacceptable; and if such studies were needed, Chinese masters of the methods should be found; he memorialized to stop the plan. The court then ordered Wo Ren to nominate teachers and set up a separate academy under his direction. He replied that he knew no one fit for the task and dared not recommend blindly. He was soon ordered to serve in the Zongli Yamen for foreign affairs. Wo Ren repeatedly begged off by memorial; the court refused; then pleaded serious illness and asked to retire; he was relieved of concurrent posts but kept on duty in Hongde Hall. In the eighth year he urged frugality for the imperial wedding; after fire destroyed the Hall of Martial Glory, he joined Xu Tong and Weng Tonghe in asking the emperor to cultivate virtue and halt all building projects to avert disaster—and the court approved. In the tenth year he was promoted to grand secretary of the Wenhua Pavilion and again asked to retire on account of illness. He died soon after; posthumously he was made grand guardian, enshrined in the Shrine of Worthy Officials, and given the posthumous name Wenduan. In Guangxu 8 (1882) Henan governor Li Henian asked to erect a private shrine to Wo Ren at Kaifeng; the court agreed.
6
初，曾國籓官京師，與倭仁、李棠階、吳廷棟、何桂珍、竇垿講求宋儒之學。 其後國籓出平大難，為中興名臣冠； 倭仁作帝師，正色不阿； 棠階、廷棟亦卓然有以自見焉。 倭仁著有遺書十三卷。 子福咸，江蘇鹽法道，署安徽徽寧池太廣道，咸豐十年，殉難寧國，贈太僕寺卿，騎都尉世職； 福裕，奉天府府尹。 從子福潤，安徽巡撫。 光緒二十六年，外國兵入京師，闔家死焉。
Early on, while Zeng Guofan served in the capital, he studied Song Neo-Confucianism with Wo Ren, Li Tangjie, Wu Tingdong, He Guizhen, and Dou Kan. Later Guofan took the field to end the rebellions and became the foremost statesman of the Restoration; Wo Ren served as imperial tutor, stern and unbending; Tangjie and Tingdong likewise made their own mark. Wo Ren left thirteen juan of posthumous writings. His son Fuxian served as Jiangsu salt commissioner and acting Anhui intendant; in Xianfeng 10 he was killed at Ningguo, posthumously made vice president of the Court of Imperial Stud, and granted the hereditary rank of commandant of cavalry; Fuyu was prefect of Fengtian. His nephew Furun became governor of Anhui. In Guangxu 26 (1900), when foreign troops entered Beijing, the whole family perished.
7
李棠階，字文園，河南河內人。 道光二年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 五遷至侍讀。 二十二年，督廣東學政，擢太常寺少卿。 會巡撫黃恩彤奏請予鄉試年老武生職銜，嚴旨責譴，棠階亦因違例送考，議降三級調用，遂引疾家居。 文宗即位，复日講，曾國籓薦棠階醇正堪備講官，召來京。 既而日講中輟，棠階以病未赴。
Li Tangjie, styled Wenyuan, was from Henei in Henan. In Daoguang 2 (1822) he passed the jinshi, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and was appointed reviser. After five promotions he reached expositor-in-waiting. In the twenty-second year he served as Guangdong education commissioner and was promoted to vice president of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. When Governor Huang Entong asked to grant honorary titles to elderly military candidates at the provincial exams, the court rebuked him sharply; Tangjie too was found to have broken rules in forwarding candidates, was slated for demotion three ranks, and retired home on grounds of illness. When the Xianfeng Emperor took the throne, daily lectures resumed; Zeng Guofan recommended Tangjie as upright and fit to lecture, and he was summoned to Beijing. The lectures soon lapsed, and Tangjie, citing illness, never took up the post.
8
咸豐三年，粵匪北犯，河北土寇蜂起，用尚書周祖培薦，命治河北團練。 棠階聯絡村鎮，名曰「友助社」。 賊踞溫縣東河灘柳林，四出焚掠，棠階督團練擊之，村民未習戰，且無火器，殺賊數十人，卒不敵。 會山東巡撫李僡率兵至，賊引去。 賊自渡黃河，始知民間有備，稍稍牽綴。 洎河北肅清，敘勞，加四品卿銜，賜花翎。
In Xianfeng 3, as Taiping forces pushed north and Hebei bandits multiplied, Zhou Zupei recommended Tangjie to organize militia training in Hebei. Tangjie linked villages and towns in an organization he called the Society of Mutual Aid. Bandits held the willow groves on Wen County's east river beach and raided widely; Tangjie led the militia against them, but the peasants were untrained and unarmed; they killed dozens of bandits but could not hold. When Shandong governor Li Xin arrived with troops, the bandits withdrew. After crossing the Yellow River, the rebels found the countryside armed and their advance slowed. When Hebei was cleared, his service was rewarded with a brevet fourth-rank grand secretary title and the peacock feather.
9
同治元年，詔起用舊臣，棠階應召至。 上疏言：「用人行政，惟在治心。 治心之要，莫先克己。 請於師保匡弼之馀，豫杜左右近習之漸。 暇時進講通鑑、大學衍義諸書，以收物格意誠之效。 」又言：「紀綱之飭，在於嚴明賞罰。 凡朝廷通諭諸事，務飭疆臣實力奉行，庶中外情志可通，而禍亂可弭。 」兩宮嘉納焉。 授大理寺卿。 先是兩江總督何桂清僨事逮治，部讞從重擬斬決，廷臣有右之者，言部臣有意畸重，仍從本律監候。 棠階疏謂桂清貽誤封疆罪大，不當輕比，非公論。 後桂清卒伏法。 連擢禮部侍郎、左都御史，署戶部尚書。 召對，言：「治天下惟在安民，安民必先察吏。 今日之盜賊，即昔日之良民，皆地方有司貪虐激之成變。 為今日平亂計，非輕徭薄賦不能治本。 然非擇大吏，則守令不得其人，亦終不能收令行禁止之效。 」因極言河南亂事，及諸行省利病甚悉。 命為軍機大臣，具疏力辭，弗許。 二年，授工部尚書。
In Tongzhi 1 an edict recalled retired officials; Tangjie answered the call and came to court. He memorialized: "Appointments and policy all hinge on governing the mind." The heart of that discipline is self-restraint above all. Beyond the tutors who guide you, I ask that you guard early against the gradual influence of court favorites. In spare hours lecture on the Comprehensive Mirror, the Extended Meaning of the Great Learning, and the like, to cultivate sincerity through understanding things. " He added: "Restoring discipline depends on clear rewards and punishments." Every imperial order must be enforced in earnest by frontier officials, so that court and provinces stay aligned and rebellion may be quelled." " The empress dowagers approved. He was appointed president of the Court of Judicial Review. Earlier, Two Jiangs governor-general He Guiqing had been arrested for abandoning his post; the ministry recommended aggravated decapitation; some courtiers defended him, arguing the ministry had overreached, and the sentence was reduced to imprisonment awaiting execution. Tangjie memorialized that Guiqing's crime of abandoning the frontier was grave and should not be softened—that would not be justice. Guiqing was eventually executed. He was promoted in succession to vice president of rites and left censor-in-chief, and served as acting minister of revenue. Called to audience, he said: "Governing the realm means settling the people, and that begins with scrutinizing officials." Today's rebels were yesterday's law-abiding subjects, every one driven to revolt by greedy, brutal local officials. To pacify the realm today, nothing remedies the root like light taxes and reduced levies. But without choosing the right senior appointees, magistrates will remain unfit, and orders will never truly be obeyed. He went on at length about unrest in Henan and the strengths and weaknesses of every province. He was appointed grand councilor; he memorialized earnestly to decline, but the court refused. In the second year he was appointed minister of works.
10
三年，江寧克復，論功，加太子少保。 大憝既平，上諭中外臣工以兢業交勉。 棠階語恭親王及同直諸大臣，謂當設誠致行，久而不懈，勿徒以空言相文飾，王深然之。 翼日召對，王反复陳君臣交儆之義，棠階與同僚繼言之，兩宮改容嘉納。 尋調禮部尚書。 太后命南書房、上書房諸臣纂輯前史事蹟，賜名治平寶鑑，命諸大臣進講。 棠階因講漢文帝卻千里馬事，反复推言人主不宜有所嗜好，以啟窺伺之端。 自是每進講必原本經義，極論史事，歸於責難陳善。 四年，恭親王被劾退出軍機，棠階謂王有定難功，時方多故，不當輕棄親賢，入對，力言王非有心之失。 會惇、醇兩王亦奏言奕訢不可遽罷，乃復命入直。 僧格林沁戰歿曹州，棠階以朝廷賞多罰少，疆臣每存藐玩，上疏極言其弊，於是有申飭直省督撫之諭。
In the third year, when Nanjing was recovered, his merit was rewarded with the title junior guardian of the heir apparent. With the great rebellion crushed, the emperor urged officials everywhere to encourage one another in diligence and vigilance. Tangjie told Prince Gong and his fellow councilors that the court must act in earnest and persevere—not trade in empty words; the prince strongly agreed. The next day at audience the prince repeated the theme of mutual vigilance between ruler and ministers; Tangjie and colleagues spoke further; the empress dowagers visibly approved. He was soon transferred to minister of rites. The empress dowager ordered scholars of the Southern and Upper Studies to compile historical precedents, named the work Mirror of Ordered Peace, and charged senior ministers to lecture on it. Lecturing on Emperor Wen of Han rejecting tribute horses, Tangjie argued that a ruler must avoid personal indulgences that invite courtiers to pry and scheme. Thereafter every lecture grounded itself in the classics, drew moral lessons from history, and ended in frank admonition. In the fourth year Prince Gong was impeached and removed from the Grand Council; Tangjie said the prince had saved the dynasty and that in troubled times close royal kin should not be cast aside; at audience he insisted the fault was not deliberate. When the princes of Dun and Chun also urged that Yixin not be dismissed hastily, the emperor ordered him back to the council. After Sengge Rinchen died at Caozhou, Tangjie memorialized that rewards outweighed punishments and frontier governors grew contemptuous; the court issued a stern edict admonishing provincial governors.
11
棠階自入直樞廷，軍書旁午，一事稍有未安，輒憂形於色。 積勞致疾，十一月，卒，年六十八。 上震悼，遣貝勒載治奠醊，賜金治喪，贈太子太保，諡文清。
Once in the Grand Council, with military dispatches pouring in, the slightest setback showed on his face. Overwork brought on illness; in the eleventh month he died, aged sixty-eight. The emperor was deeply grieved; he sent Prince Zaiye to offer libations, granted gold for the funeral, posthumously made him junior guardian of the heir apparent, and gave him the posthumous name Wenqing.
12
棠階初入翰林，即潛心理學，嘗手鈔湯斌遺書以自勗。 會通程、硃、陸、王學說，無所偏主，要以克己復禮、身體實行為歸。 日記自省，畢生不懈。 家故貧，既貴，儉約無改。 嘗曰：「憂患者生之門。 吾終身不敢忘忍飢待米時也！ 」
When Tangjie first entered the Hanlin, he devoted himself to Neo-Confucianism and copied Tang Bin's posthumous writings by hand to exhort himself. He studied the Cheng-Zhu and Lu-Wang schools without partiality, aiming at self-restraint, return to propriety, and earnest practice. He kept a diary of self-examination and never slackened throughout his life. His family had been poor; even after rising high, he remained as thrifty as before. He once said, "Affliction is the gateway to life." I shall never forget going hungry while waiting for rice!" "

吳廷棟，字竹如，安徽霍山人。 道光五年拔貢，授刑部七品小京官，洊遷郎中。 廷棟少好宋儒之學，入官益植節厲行，蹇蹇自靖。 咸豐二年，京察一等。 時侍郎書元兼崇文門副監督，獲販私釀者三十六人，承審者以漏稅擬滿杖。 已而覆訊得書元家人詐贓狀，部臣據以入奏。 文宗疑書元孤立，降旨切責，會廷棟召對，上詢是獄。 廷棟從容敷奏，且詳陳治道之要，言利之害，君子小人之辨，上首肯，獄竟得解。 因詢廷棟讀何書，廷棟以程、硃對。 上曰：「學程、硃者每多迂拘。 」對曰：「此不善學之過。 程、硃以明德為體，新民為用，天下未有有體而無用者。 皇上讀書窮理，以裕知人之識； 清心寡欲，以養坐照之明。 寤寐求賢，內外得人，天下何憂不治？ 」上韙之。
Wu Tingdong, styled Zhuru, was from Huoshan in Anhui. In Daoguang 5 (1825) he was selected as a tribute student, appointed a seventh-rank secretary in the Ministry of Punishments, and rose to director. From youth Tingdong loved Song Neo-Confucianism; in office he cultivated integrity and stern, cautious conduct. In Xianfeng 2 he received the top grade in the capital personnel review. When Vice President Shu Yuan, deputy supervisor of the Chongwen Gate, seized thirty-six smugglers of illicit liquor, the trial officers proposed the full statutory bamboo punishment for tax evasion. On re-examination it emerged that Shu's family had extorted bribes, and the ministry memorialized accordingly. The Xianfeng Emperor suspected Shu was being isolated and rebuked the ministry sharply; when Tingdong was summoned, the emperor asked about the case. Tingdong explained calmly, setting forth the essentials of governance, the harm of profit-seeking, and the distinction between gentlemen and petty men; the emperor nodded assent, and the case was resolved. He then asked what Tingdong read; Tingdong answered, the Cheng-Zhu school. The emperor said, "Students of Cheng and Zhu are often narrow and rigid." He replied, "That is the fault of poor study." Cheng and Zhu take illustrious virtue as substance and renewing the people as function; nothing in the world has substance without function. If Your Majesty reads to exhaust principle, you will enlarge your knowledge for discerning men; if you clear the mind and limit desires, you will nurture the clarity of insight. Seek worthies waking and sleeping, gain good men within and without—why should the realm not be governed? The emperor approved.
14
尋出為直隸河間知府。 粵匪北犯畿輔，廷棟練民兵巡防，民倚以為固。 內閣學士勝保督師至河間，責供張甚急，知縣王灴迫於應付，自刎不殊。 廷棟詣大營陳其事，勝保矍然，飭部下聽命。 連擢永定河道、直隸按察使。 以河間京師門戶，廷棟善守禦，得民心，仍留知府任。 四年，軍事定，乃之按察使任。 六年，遷山東布政使。 時部臣奏請畿內賦稅兼收大錢鈔票各三成，上下交病，總督譚廷襄不敢言。 會廷棟入覲，面奏：「大錢鈔票實不流通。 立法必先便於民方可行，必先信於民方能久。 今條科太多，朝夕更改，國家先不能自信，何以取信於民？ 」上首肯者再。 既而廷襄入朝，遂奏罷前議。 山東吏治久窳，廷棟獎廉懲貪。 方議海口立局收貨捐，持不可。 八年，坐奏銷遲誤，降補直隸按察使。 十一年，復調山東。 同治二年，入為大理寺卿，尋擢刑部侍郎。
He was soon sent out as prefect of Hejian in Zhili. When Taiping rebels pushed toward the capital region, Tingdong trained militia for patrol and defense, and the people relied on him as their bulwark. Grand Secretary Sheng Bao led troops to Hejian and demanded supplies urgently; Magistrate Wang Hong, unable to meet the demand, attempted suicide but survived. Tingdong went to headquarters and reported the matter; Sheng Bao was startled and ordered his men to obey. He was promoted in succession to commissioner of the Yongding River and surveillance commissioner of Zhili. Because Hejian guarded the capital approaches and Tingdong defended well and won the people's hearts, he was kept in the prefect's post. In the fourth year, when military affairs settled, he took up the surveillance commissioner's post. In the sixth year he was transferred to financial commissioner of Shandong. Ministry officials proposed that taxes in the capital region accept thirty percent each in large cash, bank notes, and copper cash; everyone suffered; Governor-General Tan Tingxiang dared not object. At audience Tingdong stated face to face, "Large cash and bank notes truly do not circulate." Laws must first benefit the people before they can work; they must first win the people's trust before they can endure. Today tax items multiply and change from day to day; if the state lacks confidence in its own laws, how can the people trust them? The emperor nodded assent twice. When Tan Tingxiang came to court, he memorialized to halt the proposal. Shandong administration had long been corrupt; Tingdong rewarded the honest and punished the greedy. When officials proposed a customs bureau at the estuary to levy transit dues, he opposed it. In the eighth year, for delayed account clearance, he was demoted to surveillance commissioner of Zhili. In the eleventh year he was transferred back to Shandong. In Tongzhi 2 he entered the capital as president of the Court of Judicial Review and was soon promoted to vice president of punishments.

三年，江南平，廷棟上疏，略曰：「萬方之治亂在朝政，百工之敬肆視君心。 事不貴文，貴其實； 下不從令，從所好。 夫治亂決於敬肆，敬肆根於喜懼。 自古功成志遂，人主喜心一生而驕心已伏，宦寺有乘其喜而貢諂媚者矣，左右有乘其喜而肆蒙蔽者矣，容悅之臣有因此而工諛佞者矣，屏逐之姦有因此而巧夤緣者矣。 諂媚貢則柄暗竊，蒙蔽肆則權下移，諛佞工則主志惑，夤緣巧則宵小升。 於是受蠱惑，塞聰明，遠老成，惡忠鯁。 從前戒懼之念，一喜敗之； 此後侈縱之行，一喜開之。 方且矜予智，樂莫違，逞獨斷，快從欲，一人肆於上，群小扇於下，流毒蒼生，貽禍社稷，稽諸史冊，後先一轍。 推原其端，祗一念由喜入驕而已。 軍興以來，十數省億萬生靈慘遭鋒鏑，即倡亂之奸民，何一非朝廷赤子？ 大兵所加，盡被誅夷。 皇太后、皇上體上天好生之心，必有哀矜不忍喜者。 況旗兵乏食，根本空虛，新疆缺餉，邊陲搖動。 兼之強鄰偪處，邪教肆行，豈惟不可喜，而實屬可懼。 假使萬幾之馀，或有一念之肆，臣工效之，視彰癉為故事，輕告戒為具文，積習相沿，工為粉飾，將仍成為叢脞怠荒之局矣。 是非堅定刻苦，持之以恆，積數十年恭儉憂勤，有未易培國脈復元氣者。 夫上行必下效，內治則外安，而其道莫大於敬，其幾必始於懼。 懼天命無常，則不敢恃天； 懼民碞可畏，則不敢玩民。 懼者敬之始，敬者懼之終。 大智愈明，神武愈彰，紹祖宗富有之大業，開子孫無疆之丕基，是皆由皇心之懼始而敬成也。 易曰：'危者使平，易者使傾，懼以終始，其要無咎。 '詩曰：'敬之敬之，天維顯思！ '可弗以為永鑑歟？ 」疏上，優詔嘉納，命存其疏於弘​​德殿以備省覽。 皇太后召對時，諭曰：「皇帝衝齡踐阼，國家大事，汝宜直言無隱，以無負先帝知遇。 」廷棟感激出涕。 五年，以衰病乞休，許之，歸寓江寧。 十二年，卒，年八十有一。 遺疏入，詔褒其廉靜自持，賜卹如例。 直隸、山東皆祀名宦祠。
In the third year, when Jiangnan was pacified, Tingdong memorialized in essence: "Whether the realm is ordered or chaotic depends on court governance; whether officials are diligent or lax depends on the ruler's mind." Affairs should be valued for substance, not ornament; subordinates do not follow orders—they follow what the ruler favors. Order and disorder turn on reverence or laxity; reverence and laxity root in joy or fear. Since antiquity, when success is won and ambitions fulfilled, joy enters the ruler's heart and pride lies hidden; eunuchs use joy to offer flattery; attendants use joy to deceive; sycophants use it to perfect their craft; disgraced schemers use it to claw back favor. Flattery secretly usurps power; deception shifts authority downward; sycophancy confuses the ruler's will; scheming elevates base men. Then the ruler accepts delusion, blocks his ears, distances the mature, and hates blunt loyalty. Former vigilance and fear are undone by a single burst of joy; thereafter extravagance and indulgence are opened by that same joy. The ruler prides himself on wisdom, delights when none disobey, indulges arbitrary decisions and every desire—one man unrestrained above, petty men fanning below, poisoning the people and endangering the state; history shows the same pattern again and again. Trace it to the root, and it is only a single thought of joy turning to pride. Since the wars began, hundreds of millions in a dozen provinces have suffered under arms; even the rebels who rose—were they not the court's own children? Where imperial armies struck, all were slaughtered. The empress dowager and emperor, embodying Heaven's love of life, must feel compassion, not joy. Banner troops lack food, the dynasty's foundation is hollow, Xinjiang lacks pay, and the frontier trembles. Powerful neighbors press close and heterodox sects run wild—not only is there no cause for joy, but there is real cause for fear. If amid myriad affairs a single indulgent thought spreads, if officials treat rewards and punishments as routine and admonitions as empty words, if habit piles on habit and whitewash prevails—the old pattern of confusion and neglect returns. Unless the ruler is firm, painstaking, and for decades perseveres in reverence, frugality, worry, and diligence, it is hard to restore the nation's vital strength. What superiors do, inferiors follow; inner order brings outer peace—and the greatest Way is reverence, whose subtle beginning is fear. Fear Heaven's mandate is inconstant, and one dare not rely on Heaven; fear the people's anger is formidable, and one dare not toy with the people. Fear is the beginning of reverence; reverence is the end of fear. Greater wisdom grows clearer, martial spirit more manifest, the ancestors' rich enterprise continued, boundless foundation opened for posterity—all begin in the sovereign's fear and are completed in reverence. The Book of Changes says, "In peril one seeks peace; in ease one invites ruin; with fear from beginning to end, the key is no blame." The Book of Odes says, "Reverent, reverent—Heaven clearly sees!" May this not serve as an eternal mirror? When the memorial was submitted, an excellent edict approved it and ordered it kept in Hongde Hall for the emperor's review. At audience the empress dowager said, "The emperor is young; on great affairs speak frankly and hold nothing back, lest you fail the late emperor's trust." Tingdong wept with emotion. In the fifth year he begged retirement on grounds of age and illness; permission was granted, and he settled in Jiangning. In the twelfth year he died, aged eighty-one. When his final memorial arrived, an edict praised his integrity and quiet conduct and granted condolence as prescribed. Both Zhili and Shandong enshrined him in their shrines of notable officials.

廷棟學以不欺為本。 官臬司時，畿輔連有逆倫獄，總督慮一月頻入奏幹上怒，廷棟曰：「此吾儕不能教化之過，待罪不暇，敢欺飾耶？ 」及去官，僑居清貧，不受餽遺。 著有拙修集十卷。
Tingdong took honesty as the root of learning. As surveillance commissioner, when the capital region saw successive cases of parricide and the governor-general feared frequent memorials would anger the throne, Tingdong said, "This is our failure to teach and transform—we await punishment; how dare we cover it up?" After leaving office he lived in honest poverty as a guest, accepting no gifts. He left the Collected Plain Cultivation in ten juan.

論曰：倭仁晚為兩宮所敬禮，際會中興，輔導衝主，兢兢於君心敬肆之間，當時舉朝嚴憚，風氣賴以維持。 惟未達世變，於自強要政，鄙夷不屑言，後轉為異論者所藉口。 李棠階、吳廷棟正色立朝，不負所學，翕然笙磬同音，而棠階尤平實持大體，可謂體用兼備矣。
The commentary says: Wo Ren in his later years was revered by the two empress dowagers; in the Restoration he tutored the young emperor, ever watchful over reverence and laxity in the ruler's heart; the whole court stood in awe of him, and public morals were sustained. Yet he failed to grasp the changing times; he scorned the policies of self-strengthening and would not speak of them, giving later critics their chief argument against him. Li Tangjie and Wu Tingdong served with stern integrity true to their learning, moving in concert like matched instruments—and Tangjie especially was plain, steady, and held to the larger pattern, embodying both substance and practice.
