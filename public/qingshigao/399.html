1
呂賢基，字鶴田，安徽旌德人。 道光十五年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 遷御史、給事中，持正敢言，數論時政得失，多所採用。 文宗即位，應詔上封事，請懋聖學，正人心，育人才，恤民隱，尤被嘉納。 遷鴻臚寺卿。 咸豐元年，超擢工部侍郎。 二年，以時事可危，疏請下詔求言，略曰：「粵西會匪滋事，二年以來，命將出師，尚無成效，甚至圍攻省城，大肆猖獗。 南河豐工未能合龍，重運阻滯，災民屯聚，在在堪虞。 河工費五百萬，軍需費一千馀萬，部臣束手無措，必致掊克朘削，邦本愈搖。 今日事勢，譬之於病，元氣血脈，枯竭已甚，外邪又熾，若再諱疾忌醫，愈難為救。 惟有開通喉舌，廣覓良方，庶可補救萬一。 請特旨令大小臣工悉去忌諱，一改洩沓之故習，各抒所見，以期集思廣益。 」疏入，諭部院大臣、九卿、科道有言責者，各據見聞，直言無隱。
Lv Xianji, whose style was Hetian, came from Jingde in Anhui. He earned his jinshi degree in the fifteenth year of Daoguang, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and received an appointment as compiler. Promoted to censor and then supervising secretary, he was upright and outspoken, repeatedly weighing the strengths and failings of current policy, and much of what he urged was put into practice. After Emperor Wenzong's accession, he answered the call for sealed memorials by urging stronger royal study, moral renewal among the people, investment in talent, and attention to popular hardship; the throne especially welcomed his advice. He was then made Minister of the Court of Imperial Entertainments. In the first year of Xianfeng he was abruptly elevated to Vice Minister of Works. In the second year, seeing how grave affairs had become, he memorialized for an edict inviting candid counsel. He wrote in part: 'The Guangxi secret-society rebels have been in revolt for two years. Armies have been dispatched again and again, yet there is still no decisive result; the insurgents have even besieged the provincial capital and grown brazen beyond measure.' The Feng River project on the South River still has not been closed, heavy transport on the Grand Canal is stalled, disaster victims are crowding together, and danger is visible on every side. River works alone will cost five million taels and military supplies more than ten million; the Board ministers are at a loss, which can only drive ever harsher exactions until the very foundations of the realm tremble. The present situation is like a patient whose vital force is already spent and whose outward malady is raging anew; if the court again hides the illness and refuses the doctor, recovery will only grow harder. Only by giving voice free rein and gathering remedies from every quarter can we hope to salvage even a fraction of what remains. He asked for a special edict requiring every official, high or low, to drop their taboos, break the old habit of foot-dragging, and speak plainly so that many minds might widen the benefit to the state. After the memorial arrived, the throne ordered ministers of departments and provinces, the Nine Chief Ministers, and every censor and supervising secretary with remonstrative duty to speak from what they had witnessed, frankly and without reserve.
2
三年正月，命賢基馳赴安徽會同巡撫蔣文慶及週天爵辦理防剿事宜，賢基疏言：「江寧以東西梁山為要隘，必先扼守。 廬州為江淮門戶，宜令重臣駐★。 巢湖出江當梁山上游，地方匪徒宜招撫，免為賊用，且可與梁山為犄角。 」上嘉納，不及施行，而安慶、江寧先後陷。 奏調給事中袁甲三、知府趙畇幫辦團練防剿，又調編修李鴻章等襄軍事。 偕週天爵疏言：「事當分任。 團練專令殲除土匪； 牧令守本境，統帥勦賊，不得遠駐百里之外，以免推諉。 」上韙之。
In the first month of the third year he was ordered posthaste to Anhui to work with Governor Jiang Wenqing and Zhou Tianjue on defense and suppression. Xianji memorialized that Jiangning's eastern and western Liang Mountains were the vital passes and had to be held first. Luzhou was the gateway between the Yangzi and Huai basins, and he urged that a senior minister be stationed there. Chaohu Lake enters the Yangzi above the Liang Mountains; local bandits there should be pacified so they would not serve the rebels, and they could then support the Liang positions in a pincer. The throne approved his plan, but before it could be implemented Anqing and Jiangning had already fallen in turn. He secured the transfer of Supervising Secretary Yuan Jiasan and Prefect Zhao Yun to help organize militia for defense and suppression, and also brought in Compiler Li Hongzhang and others to assist in military affairs. Together with Zhou Tianjue he submitted a memorial arguing that duties had to be divided clearly. Militia should be tasked solely with wiping out local bandits; magistrates should hold their own districts, and field commanders must not camp more than a hundred li away when suppressing rebels, lest each pass the blame to the other. The throne endorsed their proposal.
3
安徽境內無大枝勁旅，團練亦散漫無可恃。 七月，湖北敗賊竄陷英山，擾太湖，分犯洪家埠，賢基檄游擊賡音太、伍登庸擊走之。 八月，賊復自江西竄踞安慶，賢基赴舒城、桐城勸募團練，為官軍聲援。 賡音太、伍登庸戰歿於集賢關。 賊犯桐城，紳士馬三俊率練勇迎戰失利，遂失守。 已革按察使張熙宇退駐大關，賢基抗疏劾之。 時方駐舒城，或告以無守土責，未轄一兵，賊鋒甚銳，可退守以圖再舉。 賢基曰：「奉命治鄉兵殺賊，當以死報國。 敢避寇倖免乎？ 」十月賊至，登陴守禦，城陷，死之。
Anhui had no strong regular contingents on the ground, and the militia were scattered and unreliable. In the seventh month defeated rebels from Hubei swept into Yingshan, harried Taihu, and struck Hongjiabu in separate columns. Xianji ordered Brigade Commanders Geng Yintai and Wu Dengyong to beat them back. In the eighth month the rebels again poured in from Jiangxi and seized Anqing. Xianji went to Shucheng and Tongcheng to raise militia and lend the regular forces moral support. Geng Yintai and Wu Dengyong were killed fighting at Jixian Pass. When the rebels struck Tongcheng, the local gentleman Ma Sanjun led militia into battle and was beaten, and the city fell. The dismissed Provincial Judicial Commissioner Zhang Xiyu had pulled back to Daguan, and Xianji filed a blunt impeachment memorial against him. At the time he was at Shucheng, and some urged that he had no territorial defense duty, commanded no troops of his own, and faced a very sharp rebel thrust, so he ought to fall back and regroup. Xianji replied: 'I was ordered to organize local militia and kill rebels. I should repay the state with my life.' How could I flee the enemy and hope to survive by chance? In the tenth month the rebels arrived. He mounted the walls to defend the city, and when it fell he died with it.
4
文宗初聞舒城失守，即曰：「賢基素懷忠義，必能大節無虧。 」及奏上，深悼惜之，贈尚書銜，加恩於舒城建專祠，擢其子編修錦文以侍讀用，賜銀三千兩，命錦文即日回籍治喪。 予騎都尉世職，祀京師及本籍府城昭忠祠。 後安徽請祀鄉賢，特諭：「賢基品行端正，居官忠直，名副其實。 」即報可。
When Emperor Wenzong first heard that Shucheng had fallen, he said at once, 'Xianji has always been loyal and righteous; he is sure to die without dishonor.' When the report arrived, the emperor mourned him deeply, granted him posthumous ministerial rank, ordered a special shrine built at Shucheng, promoted his son Compiler Jinwen to Reader-in-Waiting, gave three thousand taels of silver, and commanded Jinwen to return home at once to conduct the funeral. His family received the hereditary office of Commandant of Cavalry, and he was enshrined in the capital and in his home prefecture's Shrine of Loyal Martyrs. Later Anhui asked that he be honored among local worthies, and a special edict declared, 'Xianji's character was upright and his service loyal and direct; the honor truly fits the man.' The request was promptly approved.
5
鄒鳴鶴，字鍾泉，江蘇無錫人。 道光二年進士，雲南即用知縣。 親老告近，改發河南，署新鄭，補羅山，有惠政。 母喪，去官。 巡撫程祖洛疏陳鳴鶴政績，羅山紳民籥請保留河南，特旨允俟服闋以南、汝、陳、光四府州所屬酌補選缺，異數也。
Zou Minghe, whose style was Zhongquan, came from Wuxi in Jiangsu. He took his jinshi degree in the second year of Daoguang and received an immediate magistracy in Yunnan. When his parents grew old he asked to serve closer to home, was reassigned to Henan, served as acting magistrate of Xinzheng and then as magistrate of Luoshan, and won a reputation for humane rule. He left office when his mother died. Governor Cheng Zuluo reported Minghe's record in office, and Luoshan's gentry and common people petitioned jointly to keep him in Henan. A special edict promised that after his mourning he might be placed in any suitable vacancy within the Nan, Ru, Chen, and Guang prefectures—an exceptional honor.
6
尋補光山，調祥符，擢蘭儀廳河工同知，護開歸陳許道。 以治河勞，晉秩知府。 歷衛輝、陳州、開封。 二十一年，河決祥符，水圍省城，鳴鶴露宿城上，盡力堵禦。 有議遷省城於洛陽者，鳴鶴上議有六不可。 欽差大臣王鼎等據以疏陳，乃決議堅守。 凡曆七十馀日，水退城安。 論功，晉秩道員。 二十三年，河決中牟，褫職留工，工竣，復原官，仍在工效力。 丁生母憂，服闋，署彰衛懷道，尋授江西督糧道。 文宗即位，詔舉賢才，戶部侍郎侯桐、兩江總督陸建瀛交章以鳴鶴薦，擢順天府尹。
He was soon confirmed at Guangshan, transferred to Xiangfu, promoted to subprefect of river works in the Lanyi Office, and placed in charge of the Kaigui-Chenxu Circuit. His exertions on the rivers earned him promotion to prefect. He served in turn as prefect of Weihui, Chenzhou, and Kaifeng. In the twenty-first year the Yellow River broke at Xiangfu and surrounded the provincial capital. Minghe camped on the walls and threw all his strength into holding the flood back. Some proposed relocating the provincial capital to Luoyang, but Minghe memorialized six reasons why that could not be done. Imperial Commissioner Wang Ding and others took up his argument in their memorial, and the court resolved to hold the city. After more than seventy days the flood withdrew and the city was safe. For his service he was promoted to intendant. In the twenty-third year the river broke at Zhongmou. He was dismissed from office but kept on the works; when the project was finished his rank was restored and he continued to serve on the river. After mourning for his birth mother he acted as intendant of the Zhangwei-Huai Circuit and was soon made Jiangxi's grain transport commissioner. After Emperor Wenzong's accession the throne called for worthy men. Vice Minister Hou Tong and Governor-General Lu Jianying both recommended Minghe, and he was made intendant of Shuntian Prefecture.
7
咸豐元年，擢廣西巡撫。 匪亂方熾，大學士賽尚阿督軍事，鳴鶴課吏治，治團練，撫卹被兵災民。 二年，賊由永安突犯桂林，城中兵僅千人，倉猝防禦，提督向榮馳援，民心始定。 總兵秦定三等續至，鳴鶴以諸軍無所統屬，自請督戰。 分遣諸將擊賊，相持月馀，賊百計攻城，屢卻。 賊遂分竄，賽尚阿促向榮追擊，鳴鶴堅留防賊回竄，互疏爭。 賊尋陷興安、全州，入湖南，詔褫鳴鶴職，以守城功免治罪。
In the first year of Xianfeng he was made governor of Guangxi. Rebel turmoil was raging, and Grand Secretary Sai Shanga directed military affairs while Minghe tightened administration, organized militia, and relieved those harmed by the fighting. In the second year the rebels burst out of Yong'an and struck Guilin. The city had barely a thousand troops, yet they threw up a hurried defense until Regional Commander Xiang Rong raced to the rescue and the people regained their nerve. Brigade Commander Qin Dingsan and others arrived in turn. Seeing that the armies lacked unified command, Minghe volunteered to direct the fighting himself. He sent his generals out against the rebels. The struggle lasted more than a month; the rebels tried every tactic to take the city and were driven back again and again. The rebels then broke up and fled in columns. Sai Shanga pressed Xiang Rong to pursue, but Minghe insisted on staying to guard against their return, and the two clashed in opposing memorials. The rebels soon took Xing'an and Quanzhou and pushed into Hunan. Minghe was dismissed from office, but his defense of Guilin spared him further punishment.
8
洎回籍，賊已陷武昌。 三年正月，陸建瀛赴九江督師，疏請起鳴鶴籌辦沿江防務。 已病，或沮其行。 曰：「此吾補過報國之日也！ 」建瀛旋退江寧，獲罪，命鳴鶴與將軍祥厚等籌商守禦。 建瀛見其病甚，欲為奏請還家養疴，鳴鶴不可。 及江寧陷，書絕命詞曰：「臣力難圖報稱，臣心仰答九重。 三次守城盡節，庶幾全始全終。 」遣人持付其子，自率隊出，至三山街，賊見識之，曰：「此守桂林之鄒巡撫也！ 」呼其名詬之。 鳴鶴亦罵不絕口，被支解而死。 事聞，贈道銜，賜卹。
By the time he returned home, the rebels had already taken Wuchang. In the first month of the third year Lu Jianying went to Jiujiang to take command and memorialized to recall Minghe to organize defenses along the Yangzi. He was already ill, and some urged him not to go. He replied, 'This is my day to atone and repay the state!' Lu Jianying soon fell back to Jiangning and was punished. Minghe was ordered to work with General Xianghou and others on the city's defense. Seeing how ill he was, Lu Jianying wanted to memorialize for permission to send him home to recover, but Minghe refused. When Jiangning fell he wrote his last testament: 'My strength can no longer repay Your Majesty, yet my heart still turns upward to the throne.' Three times I have held a city to the last; may I now finish as I began.' He sent the note to his son by messenger, then led a detachment out himself. At Sanshan Street the rebels recognized him and cried, 'This is Governor Zou who held Guilin!' They shouted his name and cursed him. Minghe cursed them without pause until they cut him to pieces. When word reached the throne, he was granted posthumous intendant rank and mourning compensation.
9
同治初，江南既平，曾國籓疏陳鳴鶴生平政績及殉節狀，請加恩優恤。 御史硃震言鳴鶴匿民居遇害，非臨陣捐軀者比，請罷之。 編修硃福基等復以鳴鶴被難聞見各殊，呈請下兩江總督馬新貽確查。 新貽覆奏紳耆咸稱鳴鶴協同防守，誓以身殉，罵賊被戕，無避匿民居之事。 詔依巡撫例議卹，予騎都尉兼雲騎尉世職，諡壯節。 後祀河南名宦祠。
Early in the Tongzhi reign, after Jiangnan had been pacified, Zeng Guofan memorialized on Minghe's career and martyrdom and asked for added honors and generous condolence. Censor Zhu Zhen argued that Minghe had been killed while hiding in a private house and was not comparable to a man who died in battle, and asked that the honors be withdrawn. Compiler Zhu Fuji and others noted that reports of Minghe's death conflicted and asked that Governor-General Ma Xinyi of the Two Jiangs investigate. Ma Xinyi reported that local gentry and elders agreed Minghe had helped defend the city, vowed to die with it, cursed the rebels until he was killed, and had not hidden in any private house. The throne ordered condolence on the scale used for governors, granted hereditary offices of Commandant of Cavalry and Commandant of Light Cavalry, and gave him the posthumous title Zhuangjie, 'Steadfast in Integrity.' He was later enshrined in Henan's Shrine of Eminent Officials.
10
戴熙，字醇士，浙江錢塘人。 道光十二年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 大考二等，擢贊善，遷中允。 十八年，入直南書房。 督廣東學政，任滿，請終養。 二十五年，服闋，未補官，复督廣東學政，累遷內閣學士。 二十八年，授兵部侍郎，仍直南書房。
Dai Xi, whose style was Chunshi, came from Qiantang in Zhejiang. He earned his jinshi degree in the twelfth year of Daoguang, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and received an appointment as compiler. In the palace examination he placed in the second class, was promoted to preceptor, and then made sub-reader. In the eighteenth year he entered the Southern Study. He served as Guangdong education commissioner, and when his term ended he asked leave to care for his parents in their old age. In the twenty-fifth year, after mourning he had not yet taken a new post when he was again made Guangdong education commissioner and rose step by step to grand secretary of the Grand Secretariat. In the twenty-eighth year he was made Vice Minister of War while continuing in the Southern Study.
11
先是，廣東因士民阻英人入城，相持者數年。 至二十九年，英人懾於民怒，暫罷議。 宣宗嘉悅，以為奇功，錫封總督徐廣縉子爵，巡撫葉名琛男爵。 會熙召對，論及之。 熙言廣東民風素所諳悉，督撫所奏，恐涉鋪張，非可終恃，上不懌。 尋命書扇，有帖體字，傳旨申飭。 越日，命南書房書扁額，內監傳諭指派同直張錫庚，戒勿交寫誤字之戴熙。 未幾，罷其入直。 熙知眷衰，稱病請開缺，上益怒，降三品京堂休致。
Earlier, Guangdong's scholars and common people had blocked the British from entering the city, and the standoff had dragged on for years. By the twenty-ninth year the British, intimidated by popular anger, suspended the talks for the time being. Emperor Xuanzong was delighted and treated it as a great victory, ennobling Governor Xu Guangjin as a baron and Governor Ye Mingchen as a baronet. When Xi was summoned to audience, the matter came up. Xi said he knew Guangdong's popular temper well and feared the governor and provincial governor were exaggerating—nothing to rely on forever. The emperor was displeased. Soon he was told to write on a fan and used clerical-style characters. The throne sent down a rebuke. The next day the Southern Study was ordered to write plaque inscriptions. The eunuchs directed that his colleague Zhang Xigeng do the work and warned them not to entrust it to Dai Xi, who had written erroneous characters. Before long he was removed from the Southern Study. Seeing imperial favor fade, Xi pleaded illness and asked to leave office. The emperor grew angrier still and retired him as a third-rank Beijing official.
12
咸豐初，詔舉人才，尚書孫瑞珍以熙薦，召來京候簡用，因病未至。 粵匪踞江寧，浙江戒嚴。 熙偕官紳勸諭捐輸，舉行團練。 八年，粵匪由江西擾浙東，熙助巡撫晏端書籌調兵食，乞援鄰境。 援師至，賊未得逞，漸退。 以治團練勞，加二品頂戴。 杭州初有民兵八百人，又選鋒數百，事緩，以資絀，減少半。 十年，粵匪由安徽廣德入浙，連陷數縣，犯湖州、武康。 熙以所部練勇付按察使段光清，會旗兵防獨松、千秋等關。 賊至，斂兵入城守。 熙謂用兵無獨守孤城之理，宜分營城外相犄角，又議乘賊初至迎擊，皆未行。 熙與弟燾助守西北隅，砲斃黃衣賊一人，賊遽退匿山後。 眾謂賊且遁，熙料其詐，偵之，果轉赴西南。 晝夜環攻，久雨，兵疲。 賊於宋鎮湖門故址穴地轟城，遂陷，熙赴水死之。 弟煦、媳金、及甥王朝榮，同殉。 事聞，贈尚書銜，建專祠，予騎都尉兼雲騎尉世職，諡文節。 弟煦，精算學，自有傳。
Early in Xianfeng the throne called for talent. Minister Sun Ruizhen recommended Xi, and he was summoned to the capital to await appointment, but illness kept him away. The rebels held Jiangning, and Zhejiang went on alert. Xi worked with officials and gentry to urge donations and organize militia. In the eighth year rebels from Jiangxi harried eastern Zhejiang. Xi helped Governor Yan Duanshu arrange troops and supplies and seek aid from neighboring provinces. Relief forces arrived, the rebels failed to break through, and gradually withdrew. For his work organizing militia he was granted second-rank insignia. Hangzhou first raised eight hundred militia and several hundred picked shock troops; when the crisis eased they were kept in reserve and the force was cut in half. In the tenth year rebels from Guangde in Anhui poured into Zhejiang, took several counties in succession, and threatened Huzhou and Wukang. Xi handed his militia to Provincial Judicial Commissioner Duan Guangqing and joined banner troops in defending Dusong, Qianqiu, and other passes. When the rebels arrived, the troops were pulled back inside the walls. Xi argued that armies should not defend a lone city in isolation, that camps ought to be posted outside the walls in mutual support, and that the rebels should be met as they arrived; none of it was done. Xi and his younger brother Tao helped hold the northwest corner. Cannon fire killed a rebel in yellow, and the enemy abruptly fell back behind the hills. Others thought the rebels were retreating, but Xi suspected a ruse, sent scouts, and found them turning toward the southwest. They besieged the city day and night. Endless rain exhausted the defenders. The rebels tunneled under the old Humen Gate site and blasted the wall. The city fell, and Xi drowned himself. His brother Xu, his daughter-in-law Jin, and his nephew Wang Chaorong died with him. When word reached the throne, he was granted posthumous ministerial rank, a special shrine was built, his family received hereditary offices of Commandant of Cavalry and Commandant of Light Cavalry, and he was given the posthumous title Wenjie, 'Refined Integrity.' His brother Xu, a master of mathematics, has a biography of his own.
13
熙雅尚絕俗，尤善畫。 當視學廣東，陛辭，宣宗諭曰：「古人之作畫，須行萬里路。 此行遍歷山川，畫當益進。 」其見重如此。 後以直言黜。 及殉節，遂益為世重。 同時湯貽汾畫負盛名，與熙相匹。 亦殉江寧之難，同以忠義顯，世稱戴、湯雲。
Xi cultivated an aloof refinement and was especially gifted at painting. When he took leave at court before going to Guangdong as education commissioner, Emperor Xuanzong told him, 'The ancients said a painter must travel ten thousand li.' When Bingyuan arrived he worked with Governor Lao Chongguang on strategy, and Chongguang held him in high regard.
26
七年春，按試南寧畢，警報日至，居民洶洶驚避。 炳垣倡言城險可保，條列守禦法，捐俸濟餉，守三晝夜。 賊知有備，引去。 潯州陷，江路梗塞，間道至梧州。 艇匪陳開等眾數万突來犯，炳垣率知府陳瑞枝等嬰城固守，凡三閱月，糧盡援絕。 官吏以炳垣無守土責，遣兵衛之出，炳垣不可。 八月，城陷，仰藥未死，賊擁去，厚遇之。 炳垣罵賊，求死不得。 久之，乘間為書致巡撫劉長佑，請出兵襲城，密約城內民夾擊。 事泄，賊恨甚，磔而焚之。 有老卒睹炳垣慘死狀，走省城首於官。 贈內閣學士，諡文節，建專祠桂林。
In the spring of the seventh year, after examinations at Nanning, alarms arrived daily and the people fled in panic. Bingyuan argued that the city's terrain made it defensible, laid out a defense plan, gave his salary for provisions, and held the walls for three days and nights. Seeing the city prepared, the rebels withdrew. Xunzhou fell and the river routes were cut. He reached Wuzhou by a back road. Boat bandits led by Chen Kai attacked with tens of thousands of men. Bingyuan joined Prefect Chen Ruizhi in a desperate defense. After three months food and relief were gone. Officials said he had no territorial defense duty and sent troops to escort him out, but he refused. In the eighth month the city fell. He took poison but did not die. The rebels seized him and treated him well. Bingyuan cursed the rebels and could not die. After a long while he smuggled a letter to Governor Liu Changyou asking for a strike on the city and secretly arranged for residents inside to attack from within. The plot was discovered. The rebels hated him fiercely, dismembered him, and burned his body. An old soldier who saw his cruel death ran to the provincial capital and reported it. He was granted posthumous rank as grand secretary, given the posthumous title Wenjie, and a special shrine was built at Guilin.
27
張錫庚，字星白，江蘇丹徒人，大學士玉書裔孫。 道光十六年二甲一名進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 遷御史，擢順天府丞，丁父憂，服闋，補原官。 疏論綠營冒濫頂名及緝捕諸弊，詔下其疏於各直省，實力整頓。 又疏言殿試貢士不限字數，聽其發抒，刪去頌辭，下部議行。 歷太僕寺卿、左副都御史。
Zhang Xigeng, whose style was Xingbai, came from Dantu in Jiangsu and was a descendant of Grand Secretary Yushu. In the sixteenth year of Daoguang he placed first in the second class of jinshi graduates, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and became a compiler. He became a censor, was promoted to vice intendant of Shuntian Prefecture, mourned his father, and after mourning resumed his post. He memorialized on Green Standard abuses such as padded rolls and lax policing. An edict sent his memorial to every province for thorough reform. He also urged that palace examination candidates not be limited in length, that they be allowed free expression, and that laudatory formulas be dropped. The Board was ordered to act. He served as Minister of the Court of Imperial Studs and then as left vice censor-in-chief.
28
咸豐八年，督浙江學政，擢刑部侍郎，仍留學政任。 十年，杭州陷而旋复，錫庚助城守，其子恩然率家屬自焚，錫庚以聞，予旌卹。 團練大臣王履謙劾巡撫王有齡籌餉按缺派捐，命錫庚訪按。 錫庚以有齡一月內更易州縣二十馀員，非政體，請予處分，從之。 十一年，任滿，代者未至，杭州復被圍，錫庚同守城。 城陷，或勸之去，錫庚曰：「吾大臣也，不可辱國！ 」遂自縊，賊稱其忠，為具棺斂。 贈尚書銜，予騎都尉兼雲騎尉世職，祀浙江昭忠祠，諡文貞。
In the eighth year of Xianfeng he became Zhejiang education commissioner, was promoted to vice minister of justice, and kept the education post. In the tenth year Hangzhou fell and was soon retaken. Xigeng helped defend the city. His son Enran led the family in self-immolation. Xigeng reported it, and they received honors and condolence. Militia Minister Wang Lvqian impeached Governor Wang Youling for assigning contributions by official vacancy. Xigeng was ordered to investigate. Xigeng found that Youling had replaced more than twenty prefectural and county officials within a month, which was improper, and asked that he be punished. The request was granted. In the eleventh year his term ended before his replacement arrived. Hangzhou was besieged again, and Xigeng helped defend it. When the city fell some urged him to flee. Xigeng said, 'I am a senior minister. I cannot disgrace the state!' He hanged himself. The rebels praised his loyalty and gave him a coffin. He was granted posthumous ministerial rank, hereditary offices of Commandant of Cavalry and Commandant of Light Cavalry, enshrined in Zhejiang's Shrine of Loyal Martyrs, and given the posthumous title Wenzhen, 'Refined Constancy.'
29
論曰：呂賢基以忠鯁受主知，其治兵安徽也，志欲大有所為，當殘破之馀，驟無藉手，倉猝殞身，文宗惜之。 鄒鳴鶴久著循聲，戴熙亦負清望，張芾守江西、防皖南，雖無偉績，備歷艱難。 三人以在籍搢紳治團籌防，雖久暫不同，皆事權不屬，或以城亡與亡，或以犯難遇害。 黃琮初因措置失宜獲咎，繼亦原之，而終不免於難。 馮培元、孫銘恩、沈炳垣、張錫庚，文學之臣，職非守土，死皆慘烈，朝廷報忠之典悉從優渥，固不以成敗論已。
The commentators say: Lv Xianji won the throne's trust through blunt loyalty. When he took charge of militia in Anhui he meant to accomplish much, but amid the ruins he had nothing to work with and died suddenly. Emperor Wenzong mourned him. Zou Minghe had long been known for humane rule, Dai Xi for moral stature, and Zhang Fei for defending Jiangxi and southern Anhui. None won great victories, but all endured hardship. All three were local gentry organizing militia and defense. Their terms differed in length, but none held full command. Some died when their cities fell; others were killed facing danger. Huang Cong was first punished for mishandling affairs, later forgiven, but in the end could not escape death. Feng Peiyuan, Sun Mingen, Shen Bingyuan, and Zhang Xigeng were literary officials without territorial defense duties. All died cruel deaths. The court honored them generously, for loyalty is not judged by success or failure alone.
