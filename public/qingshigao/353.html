達椿子薩彬圖鐵保弟玉保和瑛覺羅桂芳
Da Chun (and his son Sa Bintu), Tie Bao (and his younger brother Yu Bao), He Ying, and Jueluo Guifang
3
達椿，字香圃，烏蘇氏，滿洲鑲白旗人。 乾隆二十五年進士，選庶吉士，散館授戶部主事，遷員外郎。 歷翰林院侍講、侍讀、國子監祭酒、詹事府詹事、大理寺卿。 二十九年，入直上書房，充四庫全書總閱，累擢禮部侍郎，兼副都統。 四十五年，坐會同四譯館屋壞，斃朝鮮使臣，革職留任。 五十四年，左遷內閣學士。 達椿直內廷，不附和珅，數媒孽其短，以曠直褫職，仍留上書房效力行走。 尋授翰林院侍講學士，复迭以大考降黜授檢討。 仁宗知其屈抑，至嘉慶四年，詔：「達椿因曠班被譴，其過輕，當時劉墉亦緣此降官； 今劉墉已為大學士，達椿尚未遷擢，加恩補授內閣學士兼副都統。 」子薩彬圖，時亦同官，命達椿班次列薩彬圖之前。 歷禮部、吏部侍郎，兼翰林院掌院學士，擢左都御史兼都統，遷禮部尚書。 六年，典會試。 七年，卒。
Da Chun, courtesy name Xiangpu, of the Wusu clan, was a Manchu bannerman of the Bordered White Banner. He took his jinshi degree in Qianlong 25, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and after leaving the Academy was appointed a principal clerk in the Ministry of Revenue, then promoted to vice director. He rose through the posts of Hanlin lecturer and reader, chancellor of the Imperial Academy, grand tutor in the Heir Apparent's household, and president of the Court of Judicial Review. In Qianlong 29 he joined the Imperial Study and served as chief reviewer for the Siku Quanshu, eventually rising to vice minister of Rites while also holding a vice commandership of a banner. In Qianlong 45 a collapse at the Joint Four Translation Offices killed a Korean envoy; he was dismissed from his post but kept at his duties. In Qianlong 54 he was demoted to Grand Secretariat academician. Da Chun served in the inner court and refused to align with Heshen, who repeatedly traduced him; he was stripped of rank for absenteeism and bluntness but was kept on in the Imperial Study. He was soon made Hanlin associate lecturer, yet repeated poor showings in the triennial examination reduced him again and again to reviser. The Jiaqing Emperor knew he had been unfairly held down; in Jiaqing 4 an edict declared: 'Da Chun was punished for absenteeism, a light offense; Liu Yong had been demoted for the same reason at the time; Liu Yong is now a grand secretary while Da Chun has still not been advanced; let favor be shown and let him be appointed Grand Secretariat academician, concurrently vice commander of a banner.' ' His son Sa Bintu held the same post; the emperor ordered Da Chun to rank ahead of his son in court precedence. He served as vice minister of Rites and of Personnel, concurrently headed the Hanlin Academy, was promoted to left censor-in-chief with a banner command, and became minister of Rites. In Jiaqing 6 he presided over the metropolitan civil service examination. He died in Jiaqing 7.
4
薩彬圖，乾隆四十五年進士，授戶部主事，遷員外郎。 典貴州鄉試，改歷翰詹，累遷內閣學士兼副都統。 和珅既伏法，仁宗不欲株連興獄，而薩彬圖屢疏言和珅財產多寄頓隱匿，有嘗管金銀使女四名，請獨至慎刑司訊鞫。 詔嚴斥之，命從王大臣訊，不得實，議革職，予七品筆帖式，效力萬年吉地。 尋以其父年老，召還京，授戶部主事，累擢倉場侍郎。 十二年，出為漕運總督。 逾三歲，京倉虧缺事覺，降光祿寺卿。 遷盛京戶部侍郎，十六年，坐奉天災民流徙出邊，褫職。 尋卒。
Sa Bintu, who took his jinshi in Qianlong 45, was appointed a principal clerk in the Ministry of Revenue and later promoted to vice director. After supervising the Guizhou provincial examination he moved through Hanlin and Household offices, eventually becoming Grand Secretariat academician with a concurrent banner vice commandership. Once Heshen had been executed, the emperor wished to avoid widening the purge, but Sa Bintu repeatedly reported that much of Heshen's wealth had been hidden away and that four maidservants who had managed his bullion should be examined alone at the Imperial Prison. The emperor sharply rebuked him and had him join the princely commissioners' inquiry; when nothing was substantiated, he was stripped of office and given a seventh-rank clerkship at the imperial mausoleum works. Soon afterward, as his father was elderly, he was recalled to Beijing, reappointed a Revenue clerk, and rose to vice minister overseeing the capital granaries. In Jiaqing 12 he was appointed director-general of the Grand Canal grain transport. After three years, shortfalls in the capital granaries were exposed and he was demoted to director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. He was moved to Mukden as vice minister of Revenue; in Jiaqing 16 he was dismissed after famine in Fengtian drove refugees across the border. He died soon after.
5
鐵保，字冶亭，棟鄂氏，滿洲正黃旗人。 先世姓覺羅，稱為趙宋之裔，後改今氏。 父誠泰，泰宁鎮總兵，世為將家。 鐵保折節讀書，年二十一，成乾隆三十七年進士，授吏部主事，襲恩騎尉世職。 於曹司中介然孤立，意有不可，爭辯勿撓。 大學士阿桂屢薦之，遷郎中，擢少詹事，因事罷。 尋補戶部員外郎，調吏部。 擢翰林院侍講學士，仍兼吏部行走，歷侍讀學士、內閣學士。 五十四年，遷禮部侍郎，兼副都統。 校射中的，賜花翎。 調吏部。
Tie Bao, courtesy name Yeting, of the Dong'e clan, was a Manchu of the Plain Yellow Banner. His forebears had borne the surname Jueluo and were claimed as descendants of the Song house of Zhao before adopting the present clan name. His father Cheng Tai was regional commander at Taipingzhen; the family had been soldiers for generations. Tie Bao bent himself to scholarship; at twenty-one he took his jinshi in Qianlong 37, entered the Ministry of Personnel as a principal clerk, and inherited the hereditary Enqiwei rank. In the ministries he stood apart; whenever he thought a matter wrong, he argued and would not back down. Grand Secretary Agui repeatedly recommended him; he rose to director and then junior tutor in the Heir Apparent's household before being dismissed over a dispute. He was soon reappointed a Revenue vice director and transferred to Personnel. He was made Hanlin associate lecturer while still serving in Personnel, then reader and Grand Secretariat academician. In Qianlong 54 he became vice minister of Rites with a concurrent banner vice commandership. He hit the mark in the archery review and was awarded the peacock feather. He was transferred to the Ministry of Personnel.
6
嘉慶四年，奏劾司員，帝責其過當，左遷內閣學士，轉盛京兵部、刑部侍郎，兼奉天府尹。 尋復召為吏部侍郎，出為漕運總督。 五年，值車駕將幸盛京，疏請御道因舊址，勿闢新道； 裁革餽送扈從官員土儀； 禁從官妄拿車馬：上嘉納之。 七年，遷廣東巡撫，調山東。 河決衡家樓，詔預籌運道。 九年三月，漕運迅速，加太子少保。 尋以水淺船遲，革職留任。 十年，擢兩江總督，命覆鞫安徽壽州武舉張大有妒姦毒斃族侄獄，蘇州知府週鍔受賄輕縱，及初彭齡為安徽巡撫，勘實置法。 鐵保坐失察，褫宮銜，降二品頂戴，​​尋復之。
In Jiaqing 4 he impeached a subordinate; the emperor called his action excessive and demoted him to Grand Secretariat academician, then sent him to Mukden as vice minister of War and Punishments while also making him Fengtian prefect. He was soon recalled as vice minister of Personnel and appointed director-general of grain transport. In Jiaqing 5, as the court prepared to visit Mukden, he urged that the imperial route follow the old road rather than cut a new one; to abolish local gifts to officials in the entourage; and to forbid attendants from requisitioning carts and horses at will—the emperor approved these proposals warmly. In Jiaqing 7 he was made governor of Guangdong, then transferred to Shandong. When the Yellow River burst at Hengjialou, he was ordered to plan alternate transport routes in advance. In the third month of Jiaqing 9, swift grain transport earned him the additional title Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent. Soon shallow water slowed the fleet; he was dismissed but kept at his post. In Jiaqing 10 he was made governor-general of the Two Jiangs and told to reopen the case of Zhang Dayou, a Shouzhou military licentiate who had poisoned a kinsman's son in a jealous affair, and of Suzhou prefect Zhou E, who had taken bribes and let the crime go lightly; once Chu Pengling became Anhui governor, the facts were proved and justice done. Tie Bao was faulted for lax oversight, stripped of his palace rank and reduced to second-rank insignia, which was soon restored.
7
十二年，疏請八旗兵米酌給二成折色，詔斥妄改舊章，革職留任。 先後疏論治河，請改建王營減壩，培築高堰、山盱堤后土坡及河岸大堤，修復雲梯關外海口，遣大臣勘議，並採其說施行。 十四年，運河屢壞堤，荷花塘決口合而復潰，鐫級留任。 山陽知縣王伸漢冒賑，酖殺委員李毓昌，至是事覺，詔斥鐵保偏聽固執，河工日壞，吏治日弛，釀成重獄，褫職，遣戍烏魯木齊。 逾年，給三等侍衛，充葉爾羌辦事大臣。 尋授翰林院侍講學士，調喀什噶爾參贊大臣。 授浙江巡撫，未之任，改吏部侍郎。 擢禮部尚書，調吏部。 請芟吏、兵兩部苛例，條陳時政，多見施行。 林清之變，召對，極言內監通賊有據，因窮治逆黨，內監多銜恨，遍騰謗言。 會伊犁將軍松筠劾鐵保前在喀什噶爾治叛裔玉素普之獄，誤聽人言，枉殺回民毛拉素皮等四人，上怒，追念江南李毓昌之獄，斥其屢蹈重咎，褫職，發往吉林效力。 二十三年，召為司經局洗馬。 道光初，以疾乞休，賜三品卿銜。 四年，卒。
In Jiaqing 12 he proposed commuting twenty percent of banner grain rations to cash payment; the emperor denounced this as a rash change to established rules and dismissed him while keeping him at his post. He sent a series of memorials on river works, urging reconstruction of the Wangying relief dam, strengthening the Gaoyan and Shanxu rear slopes and main embankments, and restoring the estuary beyond Yuntiguan; imperial commissioners surveyed the plans and largely put them into effect. In Jiaqing 14 the canal dikes failed repeatedly and the Hehuatang breach, once sealed, opened again; his rank was reduced but he stayed in office. When it emerged that Shanyang magistrate Wang Shenhan had embezzled relief grain and poisoned Commissioner Li Yuchang, the emperor blamed Tie Bao for partiality and stubbornness, declaring that river works and local government had decayed until a capital crime resulted; Tie Bao was dismissed and exiled to Urumqi. A year later he was made a third-class bodyguard and appointed commissioner at Yarkand. He was soon made Hanlin associate lecturer and transferred to assistant commissioner at Kashgar. He was named governor of Zhejiang but never took office and was made vice minister of Personnel instead. He rose to minister of Rites and was transferred to head Personnel. He sought to prune harsh rules in Personnel and War and laid out policy proposals, many of which were adopted. During Lin Qing's uprising he was called in audience and insisted there was proof that eunuchs had aided the rebels; as the conspiracy was pursued to the end, many eunuchs hated him and spread calumny against him. When Ili General Songyun charged that at Kashgar Tie Bao had, in trying the rebel descendant Yusupu, trusted false reports and wrongly executed four Muslims including Mulla Supi, the emperor, still mindful of the Li Yuchang affair in the south, rebuked him for repeated grave failures, dismissed him, and sent him to Jilin. In Jiaqing 23 he was recalled as director of studies in the Directorate of Education. Early in the Daoguang reign he retired on grounds of illness and was granted honorary third-rank ministerial status. He died in Daoguang 4.
8
鐵保慷慨論事，高宗謂其有大臣風。 及居外任，自欲有所表見，倨傲，意為愛憎，屢以措施失當被黜。 然優於文學，詞翰並美。 兩典禮闈及山東、順天鄉試，皆得人。 留心文獻，為八旗通志總裁。 多得開國以來滿洲、蒙古、漢軍遺集，先成白山詩介五十卷，復增輯改編，得一百三十四卷，進禦，仁宗制序，賜名熙朝雅頌集。 自著曰懷清齋集。
Tie Bao argued his views with bold candor, and the Qianlong Emperor said he had the bearing of a statesman. In provincial office he sought to make his mark, grew arrogant, let personal likes govern his actions, and was repeatedly dismissed for misjudged policies. Yet he excelled in belles-lettres, and both his prose and verse were accomplished. He twice ran the metropolitan examination and the Shandong and Shuntian provincial tests, each time choosing able candidates. He devoted himself to documentary work as chief compiler of the Comprehensive Gazetteer of the Eight Banners. He gathered many literary remains of Manchu, Mongol, and Han Banner writers since the founding, first compiling fifty juan of Baishan Shijie, then expanding them to 134 juan for presentation at court; the Jiaqing Emperor wrote a preface and named the collection Xichao Yasong Ji. His own collected works were entitled Huaiqingzhai Ji.
9
弟玉保，字閬峰。 乾隆四十六年進士，入翰林，有才名。 高宗親試八旗翰詹，與兄鐵保並被擢，時比以郊、祁，軾、轍。 官至兵部侍郎，究心兵家言。 川、楚教匪起，嘗原自效行間。 會上欲用為巡撫，為和珅所阻，鬱鬱卒，年甫四十。
His younger brother Yu Bao, courtesy name Langfeng. A jinshi of Qianlong 46, he entered the Hanlin and was known for literary talent. When the Qianlong Emperor personally examined Banner Hanlin scholars, he and his brother Tie Bao were promoted together; contemporaries likened them to the Jiao brothers and to Su Shi and Su Zhe. He rose to vice minister of War and steeped himself in military theory. When the White Lotus rebellion broke out in Sichuan and Hubei, he once offered to serve at the front. The court was about to appoint him governor when Heshen blocked it; he died dispirited at only forty.
10
和瑛，原名和寧，避宣宗諱改，字太葊，額勒德特氏，蒙古鑲黃旗人。 乾隆三十六年進士，授戶部主事，歷員外郎。 出為安徽太平知府，調潁州。 五十二年，擢廬鳳道，歷四川按察使，安徽、四川、陝西布政使。 五十八年，予副都統銜，充西藏辦事大臣。 尋授內閣學士，仍留藏辦事。 和瑛在藏八年，著西藏賦，博採地形、民俗、物產，自為之注。
He Ying, born Hening and renamed to avoid the Xuanzong taboo, courtesy name Taian, of the Erde'ete clan, was a Mongol bannerman of the Bordered Yellow Banner. He took his jinshi in Qianlong 36, entered the Ministry of Revenue as a principal clerk, and rose to vice director. He served as prefect of Taiping in Anhui, then was transferred to Yingzhou. In Qianlong 52 he became Luzhou-Fengyang circuit intendant, then served as judicial commissioner in Sichuan and as provincial treasurer in Anhui, Sichuan, and Shaanxi. In Qianlong 58 he was granted a banner vice commander's rank and appointed Tibet commissioner. He was soon made Grand Secretariat academician while continuing to administer Tibet. After eight years in Tibet he wrote Xizang Fu, drawing widely on geography, customs, and local products, which he annotated himself.
11
嘉慶五年，召為理籓院侍郎，歷工部、戶部，出為山東巡撫。 七年，金鄉皁役之孫張敬禮冒考被控，知縣汪廷楷置不問，學政劉鳳誥以聞，下和瑛提鞫，誤聽濟南知府德生言誣斷，為給事中汪鏞所糾。 上以和瑛日事文墨，廢弛政務，即解職，命鏞從侍郎祖之望往按，得實，褫和瑛職，又以匿蝗災事覺，譴戍烏魯木齊。 尋予藍翎侍衛，充葉爾羌幫辦大臣，調喀什噶爾參贊大臣。
In Jiaqing 5 he was recalled as vice minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs, served in Works and Revenue, and was appointed governor of Shandong. In Jiaqing 7, Zhang Jingli, grandson of a Jinxian clerk, was accused of examination fraud; Magistrate Wang Tingkai ignored the case until Education Commissioner Liu Fenggao reported it; He Ying was ordered to try it, trusted Jinan prefect Desheng's false account, and was impeached by supervising secretary Wang Yong. The emperor declared that He Ying had neglected government for literary pursuits and dismissed him; Wang Yong joined Vice Minister Zu Zhiwang in investigating, confirmed the charges, and stripped He Ying of office; when a concealed locust disaster also surfaced, he was exiled to Urumqi. He was soon made a blue-lance bodyguard, served as assistant commissioner at Yarkand, and was transferred to Kashgar.
12
九年，授理籓院侍郎，仍留邊任。 疏言：「喀什噶爾、英吉沙爾倉儲足供軍食，請減運伊犁布疋，改徵雜糧四千石，減價出糶，且請嗣後折收制錢，以免運費。 」允之。 劾喀喇沙爾歷任辦事大臣私以庫款貸與軍民，及土爾扈特、回子取息錢入己，降革治罪有差。 十一年，召還京為吏部侍郎，調倉場。 未幾，復出為烏魯木齊都統。 十三年，塔爾巴哈台參贊大臣愛星阿欲調瑪納斯戍兵四百人番上屯田，和瑛謂瑪納斯處極邊，戍兵專事操防，不諳耕作，諮駁以聞，上韙之。
In Jiaqing 9 he was made vice minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs while keeping his frontier assignment. He memorialized that Kashgar and Yengisar granaries could supply the army, proposing to cut cloth shipments to Ili, levy four thousand shi of grain instead, sell it at reduced rates, and thereafter collect payment in cash to save transport costs. The proposal was approved. He impeached successive Karashahr commissioners for lending treasury funds privately to troops and civilians and for Torghuts and Muslims who pocketed interest, with varying demotions and punishments. In Jiaqing 11 he was recalled to Beijing as vice minister of Personnel, then put in charge of the capital granaries. Soon afterward he was again sent out as commander at Urumqi. In Jiaqing 13, Tarbagatai assistant commissioner Aixing'a proposed rotating four hundred Manas garrison troops into farming; He Ying argued that Manas was an extreme frontier post whose troops were trained for defense, not agriculture; his objection was upheld.
13
十四年，授陝甘總督。 坐前在倉場失察盜米，降大理寺少卿。 十六年，遷盛京刑部侍郎。 復州、寧海、岫岩飢，將軍觀明以匿災罷免，授和瑛為將軍，廉得邊門章京塔清阿等承觀明意，諱災不報，降革有差。 尋以誤捕屯民張建謨為盜，鍛鍊成獄，刑部覆訊雪其冤，議革和瑛職，詔寬之，留任。 調熱河都統，未上，召為禮部尚書，調兵部。 坐失察盛京宗室裕瑞強娶有夫民婦為妾，降盛京副都統，遷熱河都統。 二十一年，授工部尚書。 命赴甘肅按倉庫虧缺，得總督先福徇庇及貪縱狀，治如律。 二十二年，調兵部，加太子少保，歷禮部、兵部。 二十三年，授軍機大臣、領侍衛內大臣，充上書房總諳達、文穎館總裁。 逾一歲，調刑部，罷內直。 道光元年，卒，贈太子太保，諡簡勤。
In Jiaqing 14 he was appointed governor-general of Shaanxi and Gansu. He was demoted to vice president of the Court of Judicial Review for failing to detect grain theft while overseeing the granaries. In Jiaqing 16 he was transferred to Mukden as vice minister of Punishments. When famine hit Fuzhou, Ninghai, and Xiuyan, General Guan Ming was dismissed for concealing it and He Ying was appointed in his place; he found that frontier secretary Ta Qing'a and others had followed Guan Ming in suppressing reports of the disaster, and punished them with varying demotions. He was soon faulted for wrongly arresting colonist Zhang Jianmo as a bandit and framing him; when the Ministry of Punishments retried the case and cleared Zhang, dismissal was proposed but the emperor showed leniency and kept him in office. He was transferred to Rehe commander but before taking office was recalled as minister of Rites and then moved to head the Ministry of War. He was demoted to Mukden vice commander for failing to stop bannerman Yurui from forcibly taking a married commoner woman as concubine, then was made Rehe commander. In Jiaqing 21 he was appointed minister of Works. Ordered to Gansu to investigate granary shortfalls, he exposed Governor Xianfu's favoritism and corruption and punished the offenders according to law. In Jiaqing 22 he moved to the Ministry of War, was made Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent, and served in both Rites and War. In Jiaqing 23 he joined the Grand Council, became chief commandant of the Imperial Bodyguard, headed instruction in the Imperial Study, and served as chief compiler of the Wenyin Hall. A year later he was transferred to Punishments and relieved of inner-court duties. He died in Daoguang 1 and was posthumously made Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent with the posthumous name Jianqin.
14
和瑛嫺習掌故，優於文學，著書多不傳。 久任邊職，有惠政。 後其子璧昌治回疆，回部猶歸心焉。 璧昌自有傳。
He Ying knew court precedent thoroughly and excelled in literature, though many of his writings have not survived. His long service on the frontier won him a reputation for benevolent rule. Later his son Bichang governed the Muslim frontier, and the local peoples still looked to the family with loyalty. Bichang has a separate biography.
15
覺羅桂芳，字香東，隸鑲藍旗，總督圖思德孫。 嘉慶四年進士，選庶吉士，授檢討。 嘗召對，仁宗曰：「奇才也！ 」不數年，累擢內閣學士。 十一年，入直上書房，遷禮部侍郎，歷吏部、戶部侍郎，兼副都統、總管內務府大臣、翰林院掌院學士。 迭典順天、江南鄉試，兼直南書房。 桂芳家素貧，有門生餽納，曰：「執贄禮甚古。 某忝佐司農，俸入粗給，無藉乎此。 」封還之。 大學士祿康輿夫聚博，命偕侍郎英和按治，無所徇。 上嘉其不避嫌怨。
Jueluo Guifang, courtesy name Xiangdong, was a Bordered Blue Bannerman and grandson of Governor Tusi De. A jinshi of Jiaqing 4, he entered the Hanlin as a bachelor and was appointed reviser. Once summoned to audience, the Jiaqing Emperor exclaimed, 'A rare talent! ' Within a few years he rose to Grand Secretariat academician. In Jiaqing 11 he joined the Imperial Study, became vice minister of Rites, then served in Personnel and Revenue while also commanding a banner, heading the Imperial Household Department, and chancellor of the Hanlin Academy. He repeatedly ran the Shuntian and Jiangnan provincial examinations and also served in the Southern Study. Guifang's family had always been poor; when a student offered a gift he said, 'The ritual of presenting tribute is ancient indeed. I am unworthy to serve in Revenue; my salary is adequate; I have no need of this.' He sealed the gift and returned it. When Grand Secretary Lu Kang's wife was found running gambling gatherings, he was ordered to investigate with Vice Minister Ying He and showed favor to no one. The emperor praised him for fearing neither enmity nor resentment.
16
十八年，教匪林清逆黨闌入禁城，桂芳方直內廷，偕諸王大臣率兵殲捕，敘勞，加二級。 上遇變修省，訓誡臣工，頒御製文七篇，示內廷諸臣，命各抒所見，書以進禦。 桂芳書罪己詔后曰：「皇上臨御以來，承列聖深仁厚澤，日以愛民為政，四海之內，莫不聞睹。 今茲事變，豈不怪異？ 而臣竊以為此未足為聖德之累。 昔孔子論仁至於濟眾，論敬至於安百姓，皆曰：'堯、舜其猶病諸。 '豈真以堯、舜之聖為未至哉？ 夫天下之大，萬民之眾，而決其無一夫之梗者，蓋自古其難之。 然而揆之人事，則實有未盡者。 夫林清先以習教被系，既釋歸，轉益煽亂。 數年之間，往來糾結於曹、衛、齊、魯之間，其黨至數千人。 閹寺職官，竟有與其謀者，而未事之先，曾無一人抉發，是吏無政也。 藏利刃，懷白幟，度越門關，飲於都市，無诇而知者，是邏者、門者無禁也。 禁兵千計，賊不及百，闔門而擊之，俄頃可盡，乃兩日一夜始悉擒戮，是軍無律也。 夫吏惰卒驕，文武並弛，而法制禁令為虛器，則事之可憂，豈獨在賊？ 我皇上觀微知著，洞悉天下之故，詔曰'方今大弊在因循怠玩'，至哉言乎！ 臣敬繹之，蓋因循怠玩，亦有所由。 無才與識，則有因循而已； 無志與氣，則有怠玩而已。 是故得人而任之，則因循怠玩之習不患不除。 儻非其人，微獨不能除其習而已； 就令除之，不因循而且為煩苛，不怠玩而且為躁競，其無裨於治則均耳。 是在皇上詢事考言，循名責實，器使之以奏其能，專任之以收其效，因小失而崇丕業，在陛下一旋轉間耳。 」
In Jiaqing 18, Lin Qing's rebels broke into the Forbidden City while Guifang was on inner-court duty; he joined the princes and ministers in crushing them and was rewarded with two additional ranks. After the crisis the emperor turned inward in reflection, admonished his officials, issued seven imperial essays, showed them to the inner court, and ordered each minister to submit written views. Following the emperor's self-reproach edict, Guifang wrote: 'Since Your Majesty took the throne, inheriting the deep benevolence of the sage emperors before you, you have governed daily with love for the people, and all within the four seas have witnessed it. Yet this sudden crisis—is it not strange? Yet I privately believe this is not enough to diminish your sagely virtue. Confucius, speaking of benevolence as aiding the multitude and of respect as securing the people, said in each case that even Yao and Shun would still find it difficult. Did he truly mean that Yao and Shun had not reached sagehood? The realm is vast and the people countless; to guarantee that not a single man is disaffected has always been difficult. Yet measured against human affairs, there truly were things left undone. Lin Qing was first arrested for sect activity; once released, he stirred rebellion all the more. Within a few years he organized across Cao, Wei, Qi, and Lu until his followers numbered thousands. Eunuch officials even plotted with him, yet before the attack not one person exposed the conspiracy—this shows that civil administration had failed. They hid blades, carried white banners, passed the gates, and drank in the markets without anyone detecting them—this shows that patrolmen and gatekeepers enforced nothing. The palace guard numbered over a thousand against fewer than a hundred rebels; with the gates shut they could have been destroyed in moments, yet it took two days and a night to capture and kill them all—this shows the army had no discipline. When officials grow slack and soldiers arrogant, when civil and military discipline alike relax and laws become empty forms, the danger lies not only in the rebels themselves? Our emperor, seeing the subtle and grasping the whole, understood the realm's ills perfectly when he declared in an edict that 'the great evil today is routine and slack indulgence'—how penetrating the words! I respectfully interpret this: routine and slack indulgence also have their causes. without talent and judgment, there is only routine; without resolve and spirit, there is only slack indulgence. Therefore if the right men are found and entrusted, the habits of routine and slack indulgence can surely be uprooted. if it is not the right man, he cannot even remove the habit; even if he tried, he would trade routine for vexatious harshness and slackness for rash contention—equally useless to good government. This depends on Your Majesty inquiring into affairs, testing words against deeds, matching names to realities, employing each man according to his capacity, entrusting him fully to secure results, and turning a small setback into a greater enterprise—all within a single turn of Your Majesty's will. '
17
書行實政論后曰：「實心者何？ 忠是也。 忠者一於為國，而不亟亟於求上之知。 其所以急於公者如急於己，一政而便於民，其行之而恐不及也； 一政而不便於民，其去之恐不速也。 不以避疑謗而易其是非之公，不以處疏逖而違其夙夜之志。 故其於政也，籌之至審，而不為旦夕之謀； 行之務當，而不揣詔旨之合； 惟力是視，不必其事之諒於人； 惟善之從，不必其謀之出於己。 若是者謂之實政。 夫為臣之道，疇不當忠，然而忠之實蓋如此。 非然者，初無寸勞，而已為見功之地； 未必加譴，而已存巧避之心。 取容於唯諾，而不以國事為憂； 快意於愛憎，而不以人才為惜。 如斯人者，雖我皇上日討而訓之，尚望其能行實政乎？ 夫政者，上所以治天下之具。 然而行之以實，乃能有功，不則文具而已。 官無實政，民乃不治，非細故也。 皇上震動恪恭，求賢納諫，敕中外諸臣，改慮易志。 稍有人心者，疇敢不勉； 而臣所欲言者，則又在陛下之心矣。 臣昨歲恭錄乾隆朝臣孫嘉淦三習一弊疏於御製養心殿記冊末，伏原萬幾之暇，時賜觀覽。 用其說以考諸臣之政，因以識諸臣之心，則賢才不患其不思奮，庶績不患其不咸熙。 較臣管蠡之見，似更有助於高深焉。 」
In his essay On Practicing Real Government he asked: 'What is a sincere heart? Loyalty. Loyalty means single-minded devotion to the state, not urgent pursuit of the sovereign's notice. He is as urgent for the public good as for himself: if a policy benefits the people, he implements it fearing he may be too late; if a policy harms the people, he removes it fearing he may not be swift enough. He does not alter right and wrong to avoid suspicion, nor abandon his day-and-night resolve because he stands at a distance from power. In government he plans with utmost care and does not scheme for short-term gain; he carries policy out as it ought to be done and does not guess whether it matches the edict's intent; he looks only to doing his utmost and does not require that others understand his actions; he follows only what is good and does not require that the plan be his own. Such a man practices real government. Who serving as minister ought not to be loyal? Yet true loyalty is truly thus. Those who are not so, though they have done no real work, already prepare ground to display achievement; though they may not yet be punished, already harbor a heart of crafty evasion. they win favor through compliant assent and do not worry over state affairs; they indulge personal likes and dislikes and do not cherish talent. Men such as these—even if Your Majesty admonishes them daily—can one still hope they will practice real government? Government is the instrument by which the sovereign governs the realm. Yet only when carried out in earnest can it achieve results; otherwise it is mere paperwork. When officials practice no real government, the people cannot be well governed—this is no small matter. Your Majesty has been stirred to reverent care, sought the worthy and accepted remonstrance, and ordered officials throughout the empire to change their thoughts and alter their resolve. those who retain any conscience—who would dare not strive? Yet what I wish to say lies again in Your Majesty's own heart. Last year I respectfully copied at the end of the Yangxindian record book Sun Jiagan's Qianlong memorial on Three Habits and One Evil; I humbly pray that amid your myriad duties you may read it from time to time. Use its doctrine to test officials' conduct and thereby know their hearts; then worthy talent will strive without prompting and the myriad tasks will flourish together. Compared with my narrow view, it would seem more helpful to your lofty purposes. '
18
又論致變之源，由於民窮，民窮由於幣輕，幣輕則國與民交病。 論刑用重典而不得其平，則不能格姦定亂。 論民惑邪教，由士大夫好言因果利益有以導之。 因事納規，所言多切中時弊。 於是復條陳時事，或見之，謂其未必盡合上意。 桂芳慨然曰：「此何時，尚以迎合為言耶？ 」及上，嘉納之，命暫在軍機處學習行走。 未幾，授軍機大臣。
He also argued that the crisis stemmed from popular poverty, that poverty stemmed from debased currency, and that debased currency afflicted both state and people alike. He argued that harsh punishments applied without fairness cannot reform crime or settle disorder. He argued that popular delusion by heterodox sects was led by scholar-officials who delighted in preaching karma, retribution, and profit. Seizing the occasion to remonstrate, his words largely struck at the evils of the age. He then laid out further proposals on current affairs; some observers said they might not fully accord with the emperor's intent. Guifang said indignantly, 'At a time like this, and you still speak of currying favor? ' When it was submitted, the emperor praised and accepted it and ordered him to study temporarily in the Grand Council. Soon afterward he was appointed Grand Councilor.
19
十九年，軍事竣，以贊畫功賜桂芳子炳奎七品小京官。 尋命往廣西按事，授漕運總督。 未至廣西，於武昌途次病疫，卒。 上以桂芳明慎直爽，方鄉用，至是優詔褒卹，嘆為「良才難得」，贈太子少保，加尚書銜； 復以曾授三阿哥讀書，喪至京師，命三阿哥往奠，御製詩悼之，諡文敏。 著有經進、敬儀堂詩存，才華豐贍，為時所稱。
In Jiaqing 19, when the campaigns ended, Guifang's son Bingkui was granted a seventh-rank capital post for his father's planning merit. He was soon ordered to Guangxi to investigate affairs and was appointed director-general of grain transport. Before reaching Guangxi he contracted plague on the road at Wuchang and died. The emperor, who had valued Guifang's clarity, caution, and frankness and was about to employ him in the provinces, issued a generous edict of praise and condolence, sighing that "worthy talent is hard to find," and posthumously made him Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent with ministerial rank; because he had once taught the Third Imperial Son, when the coffin reached Beijing the prince was ordered to offer libation; the emperor composed an elegy and gave the posthumous name Wenmin. His works include Jingjin and Jingyitang Shicun; his literary talent was abundant and widely praised.
20
論曰：承平既久，八旗人士起甲科、列侍從者，亦多以文字被恩眷。 達椿忤權相，晚乃見用，其守正有足稱。 鐵保、和瑛並器識淵雅，述作斐然。 桂芳通達政體，建言諤諤，最為一時俊才，年命不永，未竟其用，惜哉！
The commentator observes: After long peace, many Bannermen who rose through the examinations and entered court attendance also won imperial favor through literary accomplishment. Da Chun offended the powerful minister and was employed only late in life; his steadfast integrity deserves praise. Tie Bao and He Ying alike possessed deep discernment and elegant learning, and their writings were brilliant. Guifang understood government thoroughly and remonstrated with blunt loyalty; he was the foremost talent of his age, yet his life was short and his promise unfulfilled—alas!
