2
李長庚子廷鈺胡振聲王得祿邱良功陳步雲
Li Changgeng, his adopted heir Ting Yu, Hu Zhensheng, Wang Delu, Qiu Lianggong, and Chen Buyun
3
許松年黃標林國良許廷桂
Xu Songnian, Huang Biao, Lin Guoliang, and Xu Tinggui
4
李長庚，字西岩，福建同安人。 乾隆三十六年武進士，授藍翎侍衛。 出為浙江衢州營都司，累遷樂清協副將。 五十二年，署福建海壇鎮總兵。 鄰海有盜，誤指所轄界，坐褫職。 罄家財募鄉勇，捕獲巨盜，起用，補海壇游擊，遷銅山參將。 自乾隆季年，安南內亂，招瀕海亡命劫內洋，以濟餉為患，粵東土盜鳳尾、水澳兩幫附之，遂益肆擾。 五十九年，夷艇始犯福建三澎，長庚擊走之。
Li Changgeng, whose courtesy name was Xiyan, came from Tong'an in Fujian. In 1771 he earned his military jinshi degree and was appointed an imperial bodyguard bearing the blue feather insignia. He was posted as battalion commander of the Quzhou Garrison in Zhejiang and rose through successive promotions to deputy commander of the Yueqing Garrison. In 1787 he served in an acting capacity as regional commander of the Haitan Garrison in Fujian. Pirates operating just outside his jurisdiction were wrongly alleged to have struck within his district, and he was dismissed from office on that account. He spent his entire family fortune recruiting local militia, captured a major pirate leader, was reinstated in service, filled a vacancy as battalion officer at Haitan, and was promoted to brigade commander at Tongshan. In the closing years of Qianlong's reign civil war in Annam drew desperate men from along the coast to raid China's inner seas for supplies, and the plague worsened when the Guangdong pirate bands Fengwei and Shuiao joined in, making the depredations steadily bolder. In 1794 foreign pirate junks raided Fujian's Three Pescadores for the first time, and Changgeng repelled them.
5
嘉慶二年，遷澎湖協副將，擢浙江定海鎮總兵。 三年，迭擊洋匪於衢港及普陀。 四年，鳳尾幫引夷艇入溫州洋，敗之，賜花翎。 五年夏，夷艇合水澳、鳳尾百餘艘萃於浙洋，逼台州。 巡撫阮元奏以長庚總統三鎮水師擊之，會師海門。 賊泊松門山下相持，颶風大作，覆溺幾盡，其泊岸及附敗舟者皆就俘，獲安南偽侯倫貴利等四總兵，磔之，以敕印擲還其國。 是年，擢福建水師提督，尋調浙江。 安南烏艚船百餘號，總兵十二人，分前中後三隊，所獲四總兵，其後隊也。
In 1797 he was made deputy commander of the Penghu Garrison and promoted to regional commander of the Dinghai Garrison in Zhejiang. In 1798 he fought foreign pirates repeatedly at Qu Port and Putuo. In 1799 the Fengwei gang brought foreign junks into Wenzhou waters; he defeated them and was awarded the peacock feather insignia. In the summer of 1800 more than a hundred foreign junks together with the Shuiao and Fengwei gangs massed in Zhejiang waters and threatened Taizhou. Grand Coordinator Ruan Yuan petitioned that Changgeng take overall command of the three garrisons' naval forces to strike them, and the fleets rendezvoused at Haimen. The pirates lay at anchor below Songmen Mountain in a stalemate until a violent typhoon struck and nearly all were capsized and drowned; those who reached shore or clung to wreckage were taken prisoner. Four Annamese pretender marquises including Lun Guili were captured, executed by dismemberment, and their imperial patent was cast back toward their country. That year he was promoted to naval commander-in-chief of Fujian and soon transferred to Zhejiang. The Annamese force numbered more than a hundred black-painted junks under twelve regional commanders organized in front, middle, and rear squadrons; the four commanders taken were from the rear squadron.
6
未幾，安南新阮內附，受封守約束，艇匪無所巢穴。 其在閩者，皆為漳盜蔡牽所並，有艇百餘，粵盜硃濆亦得數十艘。 牽，同安人，奸猾善用眾，既得夷艇，凡水澳、鳳尾諸黨悉歸之，遂猖獗。 阮元與長庚議夷艇高大，水師戰艦不能制，乃集捐十餘萬金付長庚，赴閩造大艦三十，名曰霆船，鑄大砲四百餘配之。 連敗牽等於海上，軍威大振。
Before long the new Nguyen regime in Annam submitted to the Qing and accepted investiture under restraint, leaving the junk pirates without a base. Those still operating in Fujian were absorbed by the Zhangzhou pirate Cai Qian, who commanded more than a hundred junks, while the Cantonese pirate Zhu Fen acquired several dozen vessels of his own. Qian came from Tong'an, was cunning and skilled at marshaling men, and once he seized foreign junks every faction from Shuiao and Fengwei onward fell in behind him, after which his raids grew brazen. Ruan Yuan and Changgeng agreed that the foreign junks towered too high for existing naval warships to overcome, so they raised more than a hundred thousand taels in donations and sent Changgeng to Fujian to build thirty large vessels called Thunder ships, fitting them with more than four hundred heavy cannon. He defeated Qian and his allies again and again at sea, and the navy's prestige soared.
7
八年，牽竄定海，進香普陀山，長庚掩至，牽僅以身免，窮追至閩洋，賊船糧盡帆壞，偽乞降於總督玉德，遣興泉永道慶徠赴三沙招撫，玉德遽檄浙師收港，牽得以其間修船揚帆去。 浙師追擊於三沙及溫州，毀其船六。 牽畏霆船，賄閩商造大艇，高於霆船，出洋以被劫報，牽得之，渡橫洋，劫台灣米以餉硃濆，遂與之合。
In 1803 Qian slipped into Dinghai and went to offer incense at Mount Putuo; Changgeng surprised him and Qian barely escaped alive. Changgeng pursued him into Fujian waters until the pirate fleet ran out of provisions and its sails were ruined. Qian then feigned surrender to Governor-General Yu De, who sent Circuit Intendant Qing Lai to Sansha to negotiate amnesty and hastily ordered the Zhejiang fleet back to port, giving Qian time to refit his ships and sail away. The Zhejiang fleet gave chase at Sansha and Wenzhou and destroyed six of his ships. Fearing the Thunder ships, Qian bribed Fujian merchants to build junks taller than them, sent the vessels to sea under false reports of robbery, and took possession of them. He then crossed the open ocean, raided Taiwan for rice to supply Zhu Fen, and joined forces with him.
8
九年夏，連宗八十餘入閩，戕總兵胡振聲，詔治閩將不援罪，長庚總統兩省水師。 秋，牽、濆共犯浙，長庚合諸鎮兵擊之於定海北洋，衝賊為二，自當牽，急擊，逐至盡山。 牽以大艇得遁，委敗硃濆，濆怒，於是復分。 十年夏，調福建提督。 牽聞長庚至，遂竄浙，追敗之青龍港，又敗之於台州斗米洋。 復調浙江提督。
In the summer of 1804 more than eighty vessels of the Lian clan entered Fujian and killed Regional Commander Hu Zhensheng. An edict punished Fujian officers for failing to reinforce, and Changgeng was placed in overall command of the two provinces' naval forces. That autumn Qian and Fen invaded Zhejiang together. Changgeng united the garrison fleets and struck them in the northern sea off Dinghai, splitting the enemy force in two. He took Qian on himself, pressed the attack hard, and drove him as far as Jingshan. Qian escaped in his large junk and left Zhu Fen to be defeated; Fen was furious, and the two allies parted ways once more. In the summer of 1805 he was transferred to naval commander-in-chief of Fujian. When Qian learned that Changgeng had arrived, he fled into Zhejiang, where Changgeng pursued and defeated him at Qinglong Port and again at Doumiyang off Taizhou. He was transferred back to naval commander-in-chief of Zhejiang.
9
十一年正月，牽合百餘艘犯台灣，結土匪萬餘攻府城，自號鎮海王，沉舟鹿耳門阻援兵。 長庚至，不得入，諜知南汕、北汕、大港門可通小舟，遣總兵許松年、副將王得祿繞道入，攻洲仔尾，連敗之。 二月，松年登洲仔尾，焚其藔，牽反救，長庚遣兵出南汕，與松年夾擊，大敗之。 牽無去路，困守北汕。 會風潮驟漲，沉舟漂起，乃奪鹿耳門逸去，詔奪花翎、頂戴。 四月，蔡牽、硃濆同犯福寧外洋，擊敗之，追至台州斗米洋，擒其黨李按等。
In the first month of 1806 Qian assembled more than a hundred vessels to invade Taiwan, allied with more than ten thousand local bandits to assault the prefectural seat, styled himself King Who Pacifies the Sea, and sank ships at Luer Gate to block relief forces. Changgeng arrived but could not force an entrance until scouts reported that South Shoal, North Shoal, and Dagang Gate could admit small boats. He sent Regional Commander Xu Songnian and Deputy Commander Wang Delu in by a roundabout route to attack Zhouzaiwei and defeated the enemy repeatedly. In the second month Songnian landed at Zhouzaiwei and burned the pirate stockades. When Qian turned back to relieve them, Changgeng sent troops out from South Shoal to strike him in concert with Songnian and won a crushing victory. With no escape route left, Qian was trapped at North Shoal. Then wind and tide surged, the sunken blockships floated free, and Qian broke through Luer Gate and escaped. An edict stripped Changgeng of his peacock feather and cap insignia. In the fourth month Cai Qian and Zhu Fen together raided the outer seas off Funing. Changgeng defeated them, pursued them to Doumiyang off Taizhou, and captured their follower Li An and others.
10
長庚疏言：「蔡逆未能殲擒者，實由兵船不得力，接濟未斷絕所致。 臣所乘之船，較各鎮為最大，及逼近牽船，尚低五六尺。 曾與三鎮總兵原預支養廉，捐造大船十五號，而督臣以造船需數月之久，借帑四五萬之多，不肯具奏。 且海賊無兩年不修之船，亦無一年不壞之槓料。 桅柁折則船為虛器，風篷爛則寸步難行。 乃逆賊在鹿耳門竄出，僅餘船三十，篷朽硝缺； 一回閩地，裝篷燂洗，煥然一新，糧藥充足，賊何日可滅？ 」詔逮治玉德，以阿林保代。 既至福建，諸文武吏以未協剿、未斷岸姦接濟、懼得罪​​，交譖長庚。 阿林保密劾其逗留，章三上，詔密詢浙江巡撫清安泰。 清安泰疏言：「長庚熟海島形勢、風雲沙線，每戰自持柁，老於操舟者不及。 兩年在軍，過門不入。 以捐造船械，傾其家貲。 所俘獲盡以賞功，士爭效死。 八月中戰漁山，圍攻蔡逆，火器瓦石雨下，身受多創，將士傷百四十人，鏖戰不退。 賊中語：'不畏千萬兵，只畏李長庚。 '實水師諸將之冠。 」且備陳海戰之難，非兩省合力不能成功狀。 時同戰諸鎮，亦交章言長庚實非逗留。 仁宗震怒，切責阿林保，謂：「朕若輕信其言，豈不自失良將？ 嗣後勦賊專倚長庚，儻阿林保從中掣肘，玉德即前車之鑑！ 」並飭造大同安梭船三十，未成以前，先僱商船備剿。 長庚聞之，益感奮。 是年秋，擊賊於漁山，受傷，事聞，復還翎頂。
Changgeng memorialized: "The rebel Cai has not yet been destroyed and captured chiefly because our warships are inadequate and shore supply lines to the pirates have not been severed." "The vessel I command is the largest in the fleet, yet when we close with Qian's ship we still stand five or six feet lower." "I once pledged my official stipend together with the three garrison commanders to fund fifteen large ships, but the governor refused to memorialize the project, citing the months required to build them and a treasury loan of forty or fifty thousand taels." "Moreover, sea pirates do not keep ships that go two years without repair, nor spars and timbers that last a year without replacement." "Break the mast or rudder and the ship becomes useless; let the sails rot and it can barely move." "Yet when the rebel slipped out of Luer Gate he had only thirty ships left, with rotten sails and depleted powder stores;" "once he was back in Fujian he rerigged sails, recaulked and refitted the hulls until they looked new, and restocked grain and munitions in full—on what day can such bandits ever be destroyed?" " An edict ordered Yu De arrested and replaced him with Alinbao. Once he reached Fujian, civil and military officials who feared punishment for poor coordination, failure to sever coastal supply lines to the pirates, and official censure joined in slandering Changgeng. Alinbao secretly impeached him for stalling, sent up three memorials, and the throne secretly asked Zhejiang Grand Coordinator Qing Antai for his view. Qing Antai replied: "Changgeng knows every island, wind pattern, and sandbar; in every battle he takes the helm himself, and veterans of the sea cannot match him." "For two years on campaign he passed his own door without stopping." "He spent his family fortune donating ships and weapons to the fleet." "He gave every capture entirely as reward for merit, and his men vied to fight to the death." "In the eighth month he fought at Yushan and besieged the rebel Cai while firearms and stones rained down. He took many wounds himself, one hundred forty officers and men were injured, yet he fought on without retreating." "Among the pirates they say: 'We do not fear millions of troops; we fear only Li Changgeng.'" "' He is truly the foremost of the navy's commanders." " He also explained in detail how hard naval warfare was and that the two provinces would have to combine their strength to succeed." The garrison commanders fighting alongside him also sent memorial after memorial stating that Changgeng was not stalling. Emperor Renzong was furious and sharply rebuked Alinbao: "Had I believed him lightly, would I not have lost a fine general of my own?" "From now on rely on Changgeng alone to suppress the pirates. If Alinbao obstructs him from within, Yu De is the warning you should heed!" " He also ordered thirty large Tong'an-style patrol sloops built and, until they were ready, merchant vessels hired for the campaign." When Changgeng heard this, his resolve burned all the brighter. That autumn he fought pirates at Yushan and was wounded; when the report reached court, his feather insignia and cap rank were restored.
11
十二年春，擊敗牽於粵洋大星嶼。 十一月，又擊敗於閩洋浮鷹山。 十二月，遂偕福建提督張見升追牽入澳，窮其所向，至黑水洋。 牽僅存三艇，皆百戰之寇，以死拒。 長庚自以火攻船掛其艇尾，欲躍登，忽砲中喉，移時而殞。 時戰艦數十倍於賊，見升庸懦，遙見總統船亂，遽退，牽乃遁入安南外洋。 上震悼，褒血卩，初擬俟寇平錫以伯爵，乃追封三等壯烈伯，諡忠毅，於原籍建專祠。
In the spring of 1807 he defeated Qian at Daxing Island in Guangdong waters. In the eleventh month he defeated him again off Fuying Mountain in Fujian waters. In the twelfth month he joined Fujian Naval Commander-in-Chief Zhang Jiansheng in pursuing Qian into the open ocean, tracking him all the way to the Black Water Sea. Qian had only three junks left, crewed by hardened veterans who fought to the death. Changgeng himself took a fire ship, hooked it to the stern of Qian's junk, and was about to board when a cannonball struck his throat; he died shortly afterward. The fleet outnumbered the pirates many times over, but Jiansheng was timid and incompetent. Seeing the commander's ship in disorder from a distance, he withdrew at once, and Qian escaped into the open sea beyond Annam. The emperor was deeply grieved and lavished posthumous honors on him. He had been slated for a marquisate once the pirates were destroyed, but was instead posthumously enfeoffed as Baron of Fierce Valor, third rank, with the posthumous title Loyal and Resolute, and a dedicated shrine was built in his home district.
12
長庚治軍嚴，信賞必罰，自偏裨下至隊長水手，耳目心志如一，人人皆可用。 與阮元同心整厲水師，數建功，為玉德所忌。 及阿林保之至閩也，置酒款長庚，謂曰：「大海捕魚，何時入網？ 海外事無左證，公但斬一酋，以牽首報，我飛章告捷，以餘賊歸善後辦理。 公受上賞，我亦邀次功，孰與窮年冒風濤僥倖萬一哉？ 」長庚謝曰：「吾何能為此？ 久視海船如廬舍，誓與賊同死，不與同生！ 」阿林保不懌。 既屢劾不得逞，則飛檄趣戰。 長庚緘所落齒寄其妻，志以身殉國。 既歿，詔部將王得祿、邱良功嗣任，勉以同心敵愾​​，為長庚雪仇。 二人遵其部勒，卒滅蔡牽，竟全功焉。
Changgeng ran a strict command with faithful rewards and certain punishments. From his deputies down to squad leaders and ordinary sailors, every man moved as one will, and each was fit for use. Working in concert with Ruan Yuan, he reorganized and strengthened the navy and won repeated victories, which drew Yu De's resentment. When Alinbao reached Fujian he entertained Changgeng with wine and said, "Fishing the open sea—when will the catch ever land in the net?" "Out at sea there are no witnesses. You need only cut off one chieftain's head and report it as Qian's. I will rush a victory memorial to court and leave the rest for later cleanup." "You take the highest reward and I claim a share of the credit. Is that not better than spending years in wind and waves for a one-in-ten-thousand chance?" " Changgeng declined. "How could I do such a thing?" "I have long treated my ship as home. I swear to die with the pirates rather than live while they do!" " Alinbao was displeased. When repeated impeachments failed, he sent urgent dispatches pressing for battle. Changgeng sealed the teeth he had lost and sent them to his wife, resolved to die for the state. After his death an edict appointed his subordinates Wang Delu and Qiu Lianggong to succeed him, urging them to unite against the enemy and avenge Changgeng. The two men followed his regulations, destroyed Cai Qian in the end, and brought the campaign to full completion.
13
長庚無子，養同姓子廷鈺為嗣，襲伯爵，授二等侍衛。 道光中，出為南昌副將，累擢浙江提督。 因病不能巡洋，奪職家居。 咸豐初，治本籍團練，迭克廈門、金島、仙遊，授福建提督。 尋以誤報軍情解任，仍會辦團練。 十一年，卒，孫經寶襲爵。
Changgeng had no son and adopted a clansman, Ting Yu, as his heir. Ting Yu inherited the barony and was appointed a second-rank imperial bodyguard. Under Daoguang he was posted as deputy commander at Nanchang and rose through successive promotions to naval commander-in-chief of Zhejiang. Illness kept him from patrolling the coast, and he was dismissed to live at home. Early in the Xianfeng reign he organized local militia in his home district, recaptured Xiamen, Jindao, and Xianyou in succession, and was appointed naval commander-in-chief of Fujian. He was soon removed for misreporting military intelligence but continued to assist with militia organization. In 1861 he died, and his grandson Jingbao inherited the title.
14
胡振聲，亦同安人，提督貴子。 起行伍，累擢至溫州鎮總兵。 從長庚大破夷艇於台州松門洋，自是屢從長庚擊賊海上。 嘉慶九年六月，率二十六艘運舟材赴福建，至浮鷹洋，遇賊，與總兵孫大剛夾攻，殲賊甚眾，而舟為砲焚，閩師不能救，遂被害。 優恤，諡武壯，予騎都尉兼雲騎尉世職。
Hu Zhensheng, also from Tong'an, was the son of Naval Commander-in-Chief Hu Gui. He rose from the ranks and was promoted in succession to regional commander of the Wenzhou Garrison. Serving under Changgeng, he helped win a major victory over foreign junks at Songmen off Taizhou and thereafter fought beside him at sea again and again. In the sixth month of 1804 he led twenty-six vessels carrying ship timber to Fujian. Off Fuying they met pirates and, with Regional Commander Sun Dagang, attacked from both sides and destroyed a large enemy force. His ship was burned by cannon fire, the Fujian fleet could not reach him in time, and he was killed. He received generous posthumous honors, was given the posthumous title Martial and Robust, and was granted the hereditary offices of Cavalry Commandant and Cloud Cavalry Commandant.
15
王得祿，字玉峰，福建嘉義人。 林爽文倡亂，陷縣城。 得祿家素豐，捐貲募鄉勇，助官軍復之，授把總。 明年，賊复圍城，從總兵柴大紀固守。 及圍解，率鄉勇搜捕大坪頂等處餘匪，焚瑯嶠賊巢，賊渠莊大田就擒。 台灣平，賜花翎、五品頂戴，遷千總。 嘉慶元年，巡洋至獺窟，遇賊，得祿先登，擒吳興信等。 歷年出洋捕海盜，號勇敢，累擢金門營游擊。 七年，從李長庚擊蔡牽於東滬洋，擒賊目徐業等百餘人，又擒呂送於崇武洋，被獎敘。 九年，從總兵羅仁太擊賊於虎頭山洋面，獲船械甚多。 十年，擊蔡牽於虎井洋，敗之，署澎湖協副將。 九月，遇牽於水澳，焚其舟，擒殲硃列等百餘人。 十一年春，牽入台灣，圍府城。 李長庚令得祿與許松年駕小舟自安平港入偵之，帆檣彌望，夜縱火焚賊舟，遂入屯柴頭港。 明日，賊自洲仔尾攻府城北門，得祿率兵躡其後，大呼以前，賊驚卻。 城內軍出夾攻，大敗之，乘勝至洲仔尾，破其營，賊乃遁。 五月，牽复竄鹿耳門，得祿首先沖擊，獲船十，沈船十一。 敘功，加總兵銜。 尋擢福寧鎮總兵。
Wang Delu, whose courtesy name was Yufeng, came from Jiayi in Fujian. When Lin Shuangwen raised his rebellion, the county seat fell. Delu came from a wealthy family. He donated funds to raise local militia, helped government troops recover the city, and was appointed a platoon leader. The next year the rebels besieged the city again, and he followed Regional Commander Chai Daji in holding the defenses. After the siege was lifted he led local militia to hunt down remaining rebels around Dapingding, burned the rebel stronghold at Langjiao, and captured the rebel leader Zhuang Datian. When Taiwan was pacified he was awarded the peacock feather and fifth-rank cap insignia and promoted to company commander. In 1796, while on coastal patrol, he reached Taxue, encountered pirates, boarded first, and captured Wu Xingxin and others. Year after year he patrolled the coast against pirates, earned a reputation for courage, and rose in succession to battalion officer of the Jinmen Garrison. In 1802 he fought under Li Changgeng against Cai Qian at Donghu, captured more than a hundred pirate leaders including Xu Ye, and also took Lü Song off Chongwu, earning official commendation. In 1804 he fought under Regional Commander Luo Rentai against pirates off Hutou Mountain and seized a large quantity of ships and arms. In 1805 he defeated Cai Qian at Hujing and served in an acting capacity as deputy commander of the Penghu Garrison. In the ninth month he met Qian at Shuiao, burned his vessels, and killed or captured more than a hundred men including Zhu Lie. In the spring of 1806 Qian invaded Taiwan and besieged the prefectural seat. Li Changgeng sent Delu and Xu Songnian in small boats from Anping Harbor to scout the enemy. Sails filled the horizon; that night they set fire to the pirate fleet, then landed and encamped at Chaitou Harbor. The next day the pirates attacked the north gate from Zhouzaiwei. Delu led troops up behind them, shouting as he charged, and the enemy fell back in alarm. Troops from inside the city sallied out to attack from both sides and won a crushing victory. Pressing the advantage, they reached Zhouzaiwei, destroyed the camp, and the pirates fled. In the fifth month Qian slipped back toward Luer Gate. Delu led the charge, captured ten ships, and sank eleven. His merits were recorded and he was given the brevet rank of regional commander. He was soon promoted to regional commander of the Funing Garrison.

In 1807 he was transferred to the Nan'ao Garrison. In the seventh month he defeated Zhu Fen off Jilong and captured fourteen ships. In the eleventh month he defeated their allies again off Gulei, shot dead the pirate chief Zhu Jin, captured Zhang Qi, and earned commendation. Before long Li Changgeng fell in battle, and Delu and Qiu Lianggong were ordered to take over command. In 1808 he was promoted to naval commander-in-chief of Zhejiang. He was soon transferred to Fujian, with Qiu Lianggong taking his place in Zhejiang. Ruan Yuan was again grand coordinator of Zhejiang and Zhang Shicheng grand coordinator of Fujian. With the two provinces cooperating, Delu and Lianggong worked as one to destroy the pirates. In the eighth month of 1809 they jointly attacked Cai Qian at Yushan off Dinghai and defeated him. Qian fled southeast to the Black Water Sea, where the allies fought him for days. Lianggong lined the Zhejiang ships along the enemy's east side while Delu placed the Fujian fleet east of them. In the heat of battle Lianggong's ship was damaged and fell back briefly; Delu pressed forward, grappled Qian's junk, and cut off the pirate followers from reinforcing. Qian ran out of lead shot and used foreign silver coins instead. Delu was wounded in the forehead and both wrists, hurled fire that burned the stern tower of Qian's junk, then charged again and broke off its rudder. Seeing no escape, Qian fired his own cannon into the hull and sank his ship. An edict noted that Qian had ravaged the coast for fourteen years and that destroying the ringleader was a signal achievement. Delu was enfeoffed as second-rank viscount and granted the double-eyed peacock feather. The remaining twelve hundred followers later surrendered, and coastal piracy finally subsided.

Delu spent ten years as Fujian naval commander-in-chief, repeatedly memorializing on coastal defense and revising naval ship regulations, all of which were adopted as he proposed. In 1820 he was transferred to naval commander-in-chief of Zhejiang. In 1821 he retired on grounds of illness. In 1832 Zhang Bing rebelled in Taiwan. Delu led his family to capture the rebel chief Zhang Hongtou and others and was made Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent. In 1838 the Taiwan bandit Shen He raided widely. Delu supplied grain to aid the defense and was promoted to Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent. In 1841, when Britain attacked Xiamen, he was ordered to garrison Penghu. He died the following year and was posthumously granted the rank of earl with the posthumous title Resolute and Bold. His second son Chaolun inherited the viscountcy and served as a secretary in the Ministry of Revenue.

邱良功，福建同安人。 起行伍，屢以獲盜功，洊擢閩安協副將。 嘉慶十年，偕許松年會剿蔡牽，追至小琉球，見台灣師船二為賊圍，赴援，松年舉旗招之，未至。 以違調遣被劾，褫職逮訊。 得白，復原官，署台灣副將。 十一年春，從李長庚擊蔡牽，破洲仔尾賊巢，牽乘間逸，奪頂戴。 五月，破牽於鹿耳門，賜花翎。 十二年，硃濆犯淡水，偕王得祿追至雞籠洋，連敗之，擒殲甚眾，被優敘。 十三年，擢浙江定海鎮總兵。 十四年，擢浙江提督。 偕王得祿合擊蔡牽於漁山外洋，乘上風逼之，夜半浪急，不得進。 明日，复要截環攻，牽且戰且走，傍午逾黑水洋，見綠水。 良功恐日暮賊遁，大呼突進，以己舟逼牽舟，兩篷相結。 賊以椗衝船，陷入死鬥。 良功腓被矛傷，毀賊椗，得脫出。 閩師繼之，牽遂裂舟自沉。 論功，錫封三等男爵，次於王得祿。 或為之不平，良功曰：「海疆肅清，已為快事，名位軒輊何足計？ 」二十二年，入覲，卒於途，賜卹，諡剛勇。 子聯恩襲男爵，官直隸河間協副將。
Qiu Lianggong came from Tong'an in Fujian. He rose from the ranks, won repeated merit for capturing pirates, and was promoted in succession to deputy commander of the Min'an Garrison. In 1805 he joined Xu Songnian in a joint campaign against Cai Qian and pursued as far as Little Ryukyu. Seeing two Taiwan government ships surrounded by pirates, he went to their aid, but Songnian's signals summoning him went unanswered. He was impeached for disobeying orders, stripped of office, and arrested for investigation. He was cleared, restored to his former rank, and served in an acting capacity as deputy commander of Taiwan. In the spring of 1806 he fought under Li Changgeng against Cai Qian and destroyed the nest at Zhouzaiwei, but Qian slipped away and Lianggong was stripped of his cap insignia. In the fifth month he defeated Qian at Luer Gate and was awarded the peacock feather. In 1807 Zhu Fen raided Tamsui. Lianggong and Wang Delu pursued him to Jilong, defeated him repeatedly, killed or captured a great many, and received superior commendation. In 1808 he was promoted to regional commander of the Dinghai Garrison in Zhejiang. In 1809 he was promoted to naval commander-in-chief of Zhejiang. With Wang Delu he attacked Cai Qian in the open sea off Yushan and used the wind to press him, but at midnight the seas were too rough to advance. The next day they tried again to intercept and encircle him. Qian fought as he fled, and by noon they had crossed the Black Water Sea into green water. Fearing the pirates would escape at dusk, Lianggong shouted and charged, driving his junk against Qian's until their sails locked together. The pirates grappled the ships with iron hooks, and both sides were locked in a fight to the death. Lianggong was wounded in the calf by a spear, smashed the enemy grappling hooks, and broke free. The Fujian fleet pressed the attack, and Qian blew his ship apart and went down with it. For his merit he was enfeoffed as third-rank baron, ranking below Wang Delu. Some protested on his behalf, but Lianggong said, "The coast is pacified—that is joy enough. Why fret over differences in rank?" " In 1817 he set out for an audience at court and died on the journey. Condolence gifts were granted and he was given the posthumous title Bold and Brave. His son Lian'en inherited the barony and served as deputy commander of the Hejian Garrison in Zhili.

Chen Buyun came from Ruian in Zhejiang. He enlisted in the navy, captured pirates repeatedly, earned a reputation for courage and strength, and was appointed a platoon leader in the Wenzhou Garrison. Pursuing Cai Qian under Lianggong, Buyun took forty men in a small boat and closed with Qian's flagship. When the two commanders-in-chief arrived, he hurled fire pots onto the pirate ship, hooked the rail with a long halberd, led several men aboard, fought hand to hand, and killed Qian's wife and her followers. The pirate ship was already crippled, but Qian still held a blade on the rudder tower and turned as if to strike Buyun down. Lianggong shouted from the other ship, "Your boat is level with the water—get out now!" He lowered a rope into the sea and hauled Buyun up just as Qian's ship went under. Buyun bore more than a dozen wounds, and both commanders-in-chief came in person to comfort him. When the report reached court he was awarded a martial silver medal and promoted to company commander. He rose in succession to deputy commander of Min'an. Governor-General Sun Erzhun proposed cutting back naval ships. Buyun argued that the vessels built under Commander Li were large, solid, and far superior to Tong'an double-planked ships and swift colts; scrapping them would leave the navy without the means to hunt pirates or defeat the enemy in a crisis. The proposal was dropped. Erzhun recommended him for independent command. At court audience Emperor Xuanzong asked, "Are you Chen Buyun, who fought under Commanders Qiu and Wang to sink Cai Qian?" " He questioned him closely about the battle. He was promoted to regional commander of the Dinghai Garrison and later held commands at Qiongzhou, Funing, Jinmen, and Haitan. In 1839 his old wounds reopened and he requested retirement. He died in 1850.

Xu Songnian, whose courtesy name was Rongjun, came from Ruian in Zhejiang. A military licentiate, he served in the navy under Li Changgeng and rose through accumulated merit to brigade commander on the provincial staff. In 1805 he served in an acting capacity as regional commander of the Jinmen Garrison. He fought Cai Qian at Little Ryukyu; attacked Zhu Fen and Wushi Er at Gongzai, and under Li Changgeng pursued and defeated them at Jiazi on the Fujian-Guangdong border. He fought Qian repeatedly at Qinglong Port and Doumiyang. In 1806 he and Wang Delu defeated Qian at Zhouzaiwei in Taiwan, waded ashore through the surf, and burned or drowned countless pirates. That summer Li Changgeng attacked Qian at Luer Gate while Songnian held Zhangkeng and Fancheng waters, captured one pirate ship, sank three, and at Shuiao took Cai Sanlai and others. Li Changgeng praised Songnian as capable of independent command, and Governor Alinbao reported this to court. In 1807 he fought under Changgeng at Daxing Island and Fuying; Songnian boarded a pirate ship and captured it, earning superior commendation. In 1808 Zhu Fen hid in the outer seas off Dongyong. Songnian was ordered to hunt him down and moved his forces into Guangdong. Pursuing to Changshanwei, he sighted more than forty pirate ships and identified the largest as Fen's. They surrounded it together; Fen was wounded by cannon fire and died shortly afterward. An edict praised Songnian's valor in destroying the ringleader, awarded him the peacock feather, and granted him the hereditary office of Cloud Cavalry Commandant. The Guangdong bandit Zhang Baozi raided Jinmen and Xiamen waters. Songnian used fishing boats as bait, surrounded him with the fleet, captured seven ships, sank six, and earned superior commendation. In 1810 his wounds reopened and he returned home; he soon entered mourning for his mother. In 1814 he was appointed regional commander of the Xining Garrison in Gansu and later held commands at Yansui, Zhangzhou, Tianjin, and Jieshi. In 1821 he was promoted to land commander-in-chief of Guangdong and transferred to naval commander-in-chief of Fujian. In 1826 armed clashes broke out in Taiwan while Songnian was inspecting troops; he suppressed and dispersed them. Governor-General Sun Erzhun was at odds with him, and Songnian was soon criticized for lax governance, stripped of office, and kept in Taiwan to serve. He retired on grounds of illness and died at home. His son Xilin inherited the hereditary office.

Huang Biao, whose courtesy name was Dianhao, came from Chaozhou in Guangdong. Selected from the ranks, he was made up as company commander and promoted to garrison commandant. In 1790 junk pirates raided freely. Governor-General Fuk'anggan proposed training the navy and recruiting men of exceptional ability to lead it. Biao's skill and courage were extraordinary. Raised on the coast, he knew every channel's hazards and could stay submerged for long periods, counting objects underwater with perfect clarity. He was singled out for promotion. For capturing pirates off Longmen and bandits at Goutou Mountain he was promoted to battalion commander and served in an acting capacity as battalion officer.

23
自安南夷艇散後，餘黨留粵者分五幫：曰林阿發、曰總兵保、曰郭學顯、曰烏石二、曰鄭乙。 提督錢夢虎、孫全謀皆庸材，不能辦賊。 標歿後，益無良將，惟林國良、許廷桂以死事聞。
After the Annamese pirate junks dispersed, the remnants in Guangdong split into five gangs led by Lin Afa, Zongbing Bao, Guo Xuexian, Wushi Er, and Zheng Yi. Commanders-in-Chief Qian Menghu and Sun Quanmou were both mediocre and unable to suppress the pirates. After Biao's death there were even fewer capable commanders; only Lin Guoliang and Xu Tinggui were remembered for dying in battle.
24
國良，福建海澄人。 世襲騎都尉，授廣東碣石鎮標游擊，累遷海澄副將，繼標為左翼鎮總兵。 十三年，追剿烏石二於丫洲洋，擊沉數艘，賊艦續至益多。 國良以傷殞，優恤，諡果壯。
Guoliang came from Haicheng in Fujian. He inherited the office of Cavalry Commandant, served as battalion officer of the Jieshi Garrison standard in Guangdong, rose to deputy commander at Haicheng, and succeeded Biao as regional commander of the Left Wing Garrison. In 1808 he pursued Wushi Er off Yazhou, sank several ships, and ever more pirate vessels kept arriving. Guoliang died of his wounds, received generous posthumous honors, and was given the posthumous title Resolute and Robust.
25
廷桂，廣東歸善人。 由行伍擢千總。 乾隆中，從征台灣，累遷海門營參將。 國良歿，護理左翼鎮總兵。 十四年，擊殲匪首總兵保於外洋，圍其餘黨。 張保仔率大隊來援。 眾寡不敵，廷桂死之。 賜卹，予雲騎尉世職。
Tinggui came from Guishan in Guangdong. He rose from the ranks to company commander. During the Qianlong reign he campaigned in Taiwan and rose in succession to brigade commander of the Haimen Garrison. When Guoliang died he served in an acting capacity as regional commander of the Left Wing Garrison. In 1809 he destroyed the bandit chief Zongbing Bao in the open sea and surrounded the rest of his faction. Zhang Baozi led a large relief force. Outnumbered, Tinggui was killed. Condolence gifts were granted and his family received the hereditary office of Cloud Cavalry Commandant.
26
洎蔡牽既滅，惟粵匪存，於是百齡為兩廣總督，乃斷接濟，整軍紀，越一年，剿撫以次定。 東南海氛始靖。
Once Cai Qian was destroyed, only Guangdong bandits remained. Bailin became governor-general of the two Guang provinces, severed their supply lines, tightened military discipline, and within a year settled them through suppression and amnesty in turn. The southeastern coast was finally pacified.
27
論曰：東南海寇之擾，始末十有餘年。 惟浙師李長庚一人能辦賊，以閩帥牽掣而阻成功，然長庚忠誠勇略聞於海內，上結主知，廟算既孚，乃專倚畀。 洎閩、浙合力，賊勢浸衰，不幸長庚中殞，而王得祿、邱良功等以部將承其遺志，卒殲渠魁。 粵將惟黃標可用，而未盡其才。 百齡乘閩、浙殄賊之後，剿撫兼施，遂如摧枯拉朽。 要之海戰惟恃船堅砲利，與斷接濟而已，循之則勝，違之則敗。 得失之林，故無幸哉！
The historian comments: The southeastern pirate disturbances lasted more than ten years from start to finish. Only Li Changgeng of the Zhejiang fleet could truly suppress the pirates, though Fujian commanders hampered him and blocked final success. His loyalty, courage, and strategic skill were known throughout the empire; he won the emperor's trust, and once court strategy aligned with his methods the throne relied on him alone. Once Fujian and Zhejiang combined forces the pirates weakened steadily. Changgeng fell in battle, but his subordinates Wang Delu and Qiu Lianggong carried on his purpose and finally destroyed the ringleader. Among Guangdong commanders only Huang Biao was truly capable, yet his talents were never fully used. After Fujian and Zhejiang had destroyed the main pirate forces, Bailin applied both suppression and amnesty, and the remaining Guangdong bandits collapsed like dry timber. In short, naval warfare depends on stout ships, powerful cannon, and severing supply lines. Follow that formula and you win; ignore it and you lose. Success and failure followed that pattern—there was no room for chance.
