=甘文焜=甘文焜，字炳如，漢軍正藍旗人，其先自豐城徙瀋陽。 父應魁，從入關，官至石匣副將。
Gan Wenkun, styled Bingru, belonged to the Han military Plain Blue Banner; his family had earlier moved from Fengcheng to Shenyang. His father Yingkui followed the armies south at the conquest and eventually became deputy commander at Shixia.
3
文焜善騎射，喜讀書，尤慕古忠孝事。 以官學生授兵部筆帖式，累遷禮部啟心郎，屢奉使稱旨。 康熙初，授大理寺少卿，遷順天府府尹。 崇文門榷稅不平，疏劾之。 廷議令兼攝，文焜曰：「言之而居之，是利之也。 」固辭。 六年，授直隸巡撫，奏复巡歷舊制。 單車按部，適保定、真定所屬諸縣患水災，疏請蠲歲賦。 總督白秉真以賑費浩繁，請聽官民輸銀米。 文焜斥廉俸以助。 議敘，加工部侍郎。
Wenkun was skilled in horsemanship and archery, devoted to books, and above all drawn to ancient tales of loyalty and filial devotion. Starting as an academy student, he became a clerk in the Ministry of War, rose to Qixin Lang in the Ministry of Rites, and on successive missions so pleased the throne that he won repeated commendation. Early in the Kangxi reign he was appointed vice-president of the Court of Judicial Review and then promoted to governor of Shuntian Prefecture. Finding the levies at Chongwen Gate unfair, he submitted a memorial of impeachment. When the court proposed that he take the post himself, Wenkun said, "To denounce an abuse and then hold the office is to profit from it. " He refused firmly. In the sixth year he became governor of Zhili and memorialized to revive the former practice of touring the province. Traveling alone with a single cart on his inspection, he found flood damage in counties under Baoding and Zhending and memorialized to waive the year's land tax. Governor-General Bai Bingzhen, citing the enormous cost of relief, proposed that officials and commoners be permitted to donate silver and grain. Wenkun drew on his own integrity stipend to help. When his service was reviewed, he received the additional rank of vice-president of the Ministry of Works.
4
七年，遷雲貴總督，駐貴陽。 時吳三桂鎮雲南，欲藉邊釁固兵權，詭報土番康東入寇，紿文焜移師，又陰嗾凱里諸苗乘其後。 文焜策康東無能為，凱里近肘腋，不制將滋蔓，先督兵搗其巢，斬苗酋阿戎。 既平，約雲南會剿康東。 三桂慮詐洩，謂康東已遠遁，繇是益憚之。 文焜巡歷雲、貴各府州皆遍。 十年，遭母憂，上命在任守制。 文焜又遣兵擊殺臻剖苗酋阿福。 疏乞歸葬，許給假治喪。 三桂請以雲南巡撫兼督篆，令督標兵悉詣雲南受節度，而以利啗之，冀為己用。
In the seventh year he was made governor-general of Yunnan and Guizhou, with his headquarters at Guiyang. Wu Sangui then held Yunnan and sought to use border alarms to keep his armies under his hand; he fabricated a report that the Kangdong tribesmen had raided, lured Wenkun into shifting his forces, and secretly set the Kaili Miao to attack his rear. Wenkun reckoned Kangdong no serious threat, but that Kaili lay too near his flank and would grow worse if left alone; he marched first against their stronghold and beheaded the Miao chief Arong. After the victory he arranged with Yunnan for a joint expedition against Kangdong. Sangui, afraid his fraud would be exposed, claimed Kangdong had already fled into the distance—and from that point feared Wenkun still more. Wenkun made inspection tours through every prefecture and department in Yunnan and Guizhou. In the tenth year his mother died; the emperor ordered him to observe mourning without leaving his post. Wenkun again sent troops against the Zhenpou Miao and killed their chief Afu. He memorialized to go home for the burial, and was granted leave to perform the rites. Sangui asked that Wenkun also hold the Yunnan governorship and commander's seal, that all troops of the governor-general's banner be sent to Yunnan under his orders, and tempted him with rewards, hoping to win him over.
5
十二年，文焜還本官，適撤籓議起。 三桂反，殺巡撫硃國治，遣其黨偪貴陽。 文焜聞變，使族弟文炯齎奏入告，牒貴州提督李本深率兵扼盤江。 本深已懷貳，先以書覘文焜意。 文焜手書報之，期效張巡、南霽雲誓死守，而本深不之顧。 本標兵已受三桂餌，紛潰弗聽調。 文焜度貴陽不可守，令妾盛率婦女七人自經死，獨攜第四子國城赴鎮遠，思召湖北兵扼險隘，使賊不北出。 十二月丙申朔，癸卯至鎮遠，守將江義已受偽命，拒弗納。 文焜渡河至吉祥寺，義遣兵圍之。 文焜望闕再拜，拔佩刀將自殺，國城大呼請先死，奪其刀以刎而還之，屍乃踣，血濺文焜衣。 文焜曰：「是兒勇過我！ 」遂自殺，年四十有二。 從者筆帖式和善雅圖殉。
In the twelfth year Wenkun resumed his former office just as the court began debating the abolition of the feudatories. Sangui rose in rebellion, killed the Yunnan governor Zhu Guozhi, and sent his followers to threaten Guiyang. When he heard of the uprising, Wenkun sent his kinsman Wen Jiong with a memorial to the capital and ordered Guizhou commander Li Benshen to take troops and block the Pan River crossing. Li Benshen was already of two minds and first wrote to sound Wenkun out. Wenkun answered in his own hand, vowing to follow Zhang Xun and Nan Jiyun in holding the place to the death—but Benshen ignored him. Li's own banner troops had already been bought by Sangui; they broke ranks and would not obey. Seeing Guiyang indefensible, Wenkun had his concubine Sheng lead seven women of the household to hang themselves, then set out alone with his fourth son Guocheng for Zhenyuan, hoping to rally Hubei troops at the mountain passes and keep the rebels from breaking north. On the first day of the twelfth month (day bingshen) he set out; on day guimao he reached Zhenyuan, where the garrison commander Jiang Yi, having already taken orders from the rebels, shut him out. Wenkun crossed the river to Jixiang Temple, and Jiang Yi sent troops to surround him. Wenkun bowed twice toward the imperial court, drew his sword to take his own life; Guocheng shouted that he should die first, wrested away the blade, cut his own throat, and handed the sword back—then collapsed, his blood spattering Wenkun's robes. Wenkun said, "This child is braver than I! " Then he took his own life. He was forty-two. His clerk Heshanyatu died at his side.
6
亂平，貴州巡撫楊雍建以文焜治績及死事狀上聞，予優恤。 遣其長子宣化同知國均迎喪還京師，使內大臣佟國維迎奠盧溝橋，贈兵部尚書，諡忠果。 建祠貴陽，上賜「勁節」二字顏其額。 子七，國璧尤知名。
After order was restored, Guizhou governor Yang Yongjian memorialized Wenkun's record in office and the circumstances of his death, and the court granted him exceptional posthumous honors. His eldest son Guojun, sub-prefect of Xuanhua, was sent to escort the coffin to Beijing; Inner Minister Tong Guowei received the bier and offered sacrifice at Lugou Bridge; Wenkun was posthumously made Minister of War and given the posthumous name Zhongguo, "Loyal and Resolute." A shrine was raised at Guiyang, and the emperor inscribed its plaque with the two characters "Steadfast Integrity." He left seven sons, of whom Guobi became especially renowned.
7
國璧，字東屏，以任子授陝州知州，改蘇州同知，擢山西平陽、浙江寧波知府，名循吏。 聖祖南巡，幸杭州，御書硃子詩及「永貞」額以賜。 諭曰：「汝父盡節，朕未嘗忘，此為汝母書也。 」累遷雲南巡撫。 坐事罷。 雍正間，起為正黃旗漢軍都統。 乾隆三年，授綏遠城右翼副都統。 复罷。 十二年，卒。
Guobi, styled Dongping, entered office as prefect of Shaanzhou through his father's rank, later served as sub-prefect of Suzhou, and rose to govern Pingyang in Shanxi and Ningbo in Zhejiang, winning a reputation as a model magistrate. When the Kangxi Emperor toured the south and visited Hangzhou, he wrote out a poem in the manner of Zhu Xi and bestowed a plaque bearing the words "Ever Chaste." He told him, "Your father died in loyal service, and I have not forgotten; I write this for your mother. " He was eventually promoted to governor of Yunnan. He was dismissed after an offense. Under Yongzheng he was recalled to command the Han military Plain Yellow Banner. In Qianlong year three he was appointed deputy lieutenant-general of the right wing at Suiyuan. He was dismissed again. In the twelfth year he died.
8
=范承謨=范承謨，字覲公，漢軍鑲黃旗人，文程次子。 順治九年進士，選庶吉士，授弘文院編修。 累遷秘書院學士。 康熙七年，授浙江巡撫。 時去開國未久，民流亡未復業，浙東寧波、金華等六府荒田尤多。 總督趙廷臣請除賦額，上命承謨履勘。 承謨遍歷諸府，請免荒田及水沖田地賦凡三十一萬五千五百馀畝。 杭州、嘉興、湖州、紹興四府被水，民飢，承謨出布政使庫銀八萬，糴米湖廣平糶，最貧者得附老弱例，肩鹽給朝夕，全活甚眾。 並疏請「漕米改折，石銀一兩。 明年麥熟，補徵白糧，以三年帶徵。 災重者如例蠲免」。 得旨允行。 十年，以疾請解職，召還。 總督劉兆麒、提督塞白理疏言浙民請留承謨一百五十馀牒，給事中姜希轍、柯聳，御史何元英等亦言：「承謨受事三載，愛民如子，不通請謁饋遺。 劾罷貪墨，廉治巨猾，剔除加耗、陋規、私派諸弊。 浙民愛戴，深於飢渴。 」上命承謨留任。 十一年，承謨复疏言湖、嘉兩府白糧加耗，多寡不一，請每石加四斗五升為限； 又奏蠲溫、台二衛康熙九年以前逋賦及石門、平陽未完輕齎月糧：皆下戶部議行。
Fan Chengmo, styled Jingong, belonged to the Han military Bordered Yellow Banner and was the second son of Fan Wencheng. He took his jinshi in Shunzhi year nine, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and was made a compiler in the Hongwen Academy. He rose through posts to academician of the Secretariat. In Kangxi year seven he became governor of Zhejiang. Not many years had passed since the founding; refugees had not yet returned to their farms, and in eastern Zhejiang the six prefectures including Ningbo and Jinhua held especially large tracts of abandoned land. Governor-General Zhao Tingchen proposed cutting the tax assessments, and the emperor ordered Chengmo to inspect the land in person. Chengmo traveled through every prefecture and memorialized to exempt tax on wasteland and flood-damaged fields—more than 315,500 mu in all. When flood struck Hangzhou, Jiaxing, Huzhou, and Shaoxing and the people went hungry, Chengmo took eighty thousand taels from the provincial treasury, purchased grain in Huguang, and sold it at a fair price; the poorest were allowed, under the rule for the aged and helpless, to earn their meals by peddling salt, and a great multitude were saved from starvation. He also memorialized: "Let grain tribute be commuted to silver at one tael per shi. When the wheat ripens next year, the white-grain levy may be made up, spread over three years. Where the damage is worst, grant remissions as usual." The emperor approved, and the plan was put into effect. In the tenth year he asked to resign on grounds of illness and was recalled to court. Governor-General Liu Zhaoqi and commander Saibaili reported that Zhejiang commoners had sent more than 150 petitions begging Chengmo to stay; supervising secretaries Jiang Xizhe and Ke Song and censor He Yuanying wrote: "In three years in office Chengmo has treated the people like his own children and refused every sort of solicitation and gift. He has impeached corrupt officials, punished powerful wrongdoers, and rooted out surcharges, petty exactions, and illegal levies. The people of Zhejiang cherish him as one longs for food and drink in want. " The emperor ordered Chengmo to remain in office. In the eleventh year Chengmo memorialized again that surcharges on white grain in Huzhou and Jiaxing varied wildly and asked that they be capped at four dou and five sheng per shi; he also asked remission of tax arrears before Kangxi year nine for the Wenzhou and Taizhou guards and of unpaid light-transport grain for Shimen and Pingyang—all referred to the Ministry of Revenue for approval.
9
十月，擢福建總督，疏辭未允，請入覲。 十二年七月，至京師，入對。 承謨疾未癒，命御醫診視，賜藥餌。 疾稍差，趣赴官，賜冠服、鞍馬。 福建總督初駐漳州，至是以將撤籓，命移駐福州。 吳三桂反，承謨察精忠有異志，時方議裁兵，承謨疏請緩行。 又報巡歷邊海，欲置身外郡，便徵調防禦。 事未行而精忠叛，陽言海寇至，約承謨計事。 巡撫劉秉政附精忠，趣同行。 承謨知有變，左右請擐甲從，承謨曰：「眾寡不敵，備無益也。 」遂往。 精忠之徒露刃相脅，承謨挺身前，罵不絕口。 精忠拘之土室，加以桎梏，絕粒十日，不得死。 精忠遣秉政說降，承謨奮足蹴之僕，叱左右掖之出，曰：「賊就僇當不遠，我先褫其魄！ 」為賊困逾二年，日冠賜冠，衣辭母時衣，遇朔望，奉時憲書一帙懸之，北鄉再拜。 所居室迫隘，號曰蒙谷。 為詩文，以桴炭畫壁上。
In the tenth month he was made governor-general of Fujian; when he tried to decline, the court refused, and he asked leave to appear before the emperor. In the seventh month of the twelfth year he reached Beijing and was received in audience. Chengmo was still ill; the emperor sent court physicians to treat him and gave him medicine. When he improved a little he was pressed to take up his post and was given court robes and a saddle horse. The Fujian governor-general had first been posted at Zhangzhou; with the feudatories about to be abolished, he was ordered to move his headquarters to Fuzhou. When Wu Sangui rebelled, Chengmo saw that Geng Jingzhong was disloyal at heart; the court was then debating troop reductions, and Chengmo memorialized to proceed slowly. He also reported that he would tour the coast, planning to station himself in an outlying prefecture where he could more easily call up troops for defense. Before he could act, Geng Jingzhong rose in rebellion, pretended that pirates had appeared, and summoned Chengmo to discuss the matter. Governor Liu Bingzheng had thrown in with Geng Jingzhong and pressed Chengmo to go with them. Chengmo knew something was wrong; his attendants urged him to go in armor, but he said, "We are outnumbered; armor would do no good. " He went anyway. Geng Jingzhong's men drew their swords to threaten him; Chengmo stepped forward and rained curses on them. Geng Jingzhong shut him in a cell of beaten earth, put him in irons, and starved him for ten days, yet he would not die. Geng Jingzhong sent Bingzheng to win him over; Chengmo kicked the envoy to the ground and ordered his men to drag him out, crying, "The rebels' day of execution is near—I will break their spirit first! " For more than two years in rebel hands he daily wore the court cap the emperor had given him and the robe he had worn when bidding his mother farewell; on the first and fifteenth of each month he hung up the official calendar and bowed twice toward the north. His quarters were cramped, and he called them the Dark Valley. He wrote poems and essays on the walls in charcoal from a pine torch.
10
時有部曲張福建者，手刃奪門入，連斬數賊，力竭死。 蒙古人嘛尼為偽散騎郎，精忠遣守承謨，感承謨忠義，謀令出走。 事泄，精忠將磔之，大言曰：「吾寧與忠臣同死，不原與逆賊同生！ 」
One of his soldiers, Zhang Fujian, fought his way through the gate with a drawn sword, killed several rebels in succession, and fell when his strength gave out. A Mongol named Mani held a false post as Gentleman Attendant in the Cavalry and was set to guard Chengmo; touched by his loyalty, Mani plotted to help him escape. When the plot was discovered, Geng Jingzhong was about to have him torn apart; Mani cried, "I would rather die with a loyal minister than live with rebel traitors! "
11
十五年，師克仙霞關，精忠將降，冀飾詞免死，懼承謨暴其罪。 九月己酉朔，甲子夜半，精忠遣黨偪承謨就​​縊。 幕客嵇永仁、王龍光、沈天成，從弟承譜，下至隸卒，同死者五十三人。 語互詳忠義傳。 舊役王道隆奉遣他出，還至延平，聞變，自刎死。 賊焚承謨屍，棄之野，泰宁騎兵許鼎夜負遺骸藏之。 十六年，喪還京師。 上遣內大臣侍衛迎奠，贈兵部尚書、太子少保，諡忠貞，御書碑文賜其家。 十九年，精忠伏誅。 赴市曹日，承謨子時崇臠其肉祭墓。 福建民請建祠祀之，御書「忠貞炳日」扁於楣。 承謨所為畫壁集，上親制序。
In the fifteenth year imperial forces took Xianxia Pass; Geng Jingzhong was ready to surrender and hoped to talk his way out of execution, but feared Chengmo would expose his crimes. On the first day of the ninth month (day jiyou), at midnight on day jiazi, Geng Jingzhong sent his men to force Chengmo to hang himself. His secretaries Ji Yongren, Wang Longguang, and Shen Tiancheng, his cousin Chengpu, and servants down to runners—fifty-three died with him. Their accounts are set out at length in the Biographies of Loyalty and Righteousness. His former servant Wang Daolong had been sent away on business; when he returned to Yanping and heard what had happened, he killed himself. The rebels burned Chengmo's body and threw it in the open country; a Taining cavalryman named Xu Ding carried off the remains by night and hid them. In the sixteenth year his coffin was brought back to Beijing. The emperor sent an inner minister and guards to receive the bier and offer sacrifice; Chengmo was posthumously made Minister of War and Junior Tutor to the Heir Apparent, given the posthumous name Zhongzhen, "Loyal and Constant," and an imperially written epitaph for his family. In the nineteenth year Geng Jingzhong was put to death. On the day Geng Jingzhong went to the execution ground, Chengmo's son Shichong cut flesh from his body to offer at his father's tomb. The people of Fujian petitioned to build a shrine in his honor, and the emperor himself wrote the plaque "Loyal and Constant, Shining Like the Sun" for its lintel. The emperor personally wrote a preface for Chengmo's collection of paintings on walls.
12
時崇，字自牧。 以難廕出知遼陽州，遷直隸順德知府，有惠政。 累遷福建按察使。 陛辭日，上顧謂群臣曰：「此開國名臣孫，殉難忠臣子也！ 」四十七年，擢廣東巡撫，兼鹽政。 越二年，擢福建浙江總督。 五十四年，入為左都御史。 明年，授兵部尚書。 命出塞築莫代察罕廋爾、鄂爾齋圖杲爾台站凡四十有七所。 又明年，還朝。 尋卒。 閩人思其德，附祀承謨祠。
Shichong, styled Zimu. Granted office because his father had died in service to the state, he became magistrate of Liaoyang Prefecture, later prefect of Shunde in Zhili, where he governed with kindness. He rose in succession to become Fujian provincial surveillance commissioner. When he took leave at court, the emperor turned to the ministers and said, "This is the grandson of a founding minister of the dynasty, the son of a loyal servant who gave his life in the national crisis! " In the forty-seventh year he was promoted to governor of Guangdong, with charge of salt revenues as well. Two years on, he was made governor-general of Fujian and Zhejiang. In the fifty-fourth year he was recalled to the capital as Left Censor-in-Chief. The next year he was appointed Minister of War. He was ordered beyond the frontier to build forty-seven relay stations at Modai Chahan Ao'er, E'erzhai Tu Gao'er, and other posts. The following year he returned to court. He died soon after. The people of Fujian remembered his kindness and added him to the worship at Chengmo's shrine.
13
=馬雄鎮=馬雄鎮，字錫蕃，漢軍鑲紅旗人，鳴佩子。 以廕補工部副理事官，歷遷左僉都御史、國史院學士。 康熙八年，授山西巡撫。 未上，改廣西。 時群盜蝟起，構瑤、僮掠梧州、平樂二府，不數月討平之。 累疏請平鹺價，建學宮，定有司邊俸，省軍糧運費，並罷諸採買累民者，皆得旨允行。
Ma Xiongzhen, styled Xifan, belonged to the Han military Bordered Red Banner and was a son of Mingpei. Through hereditary privilege he entered the Ministry of Works as an assistant supervising secretary and eventually rose to Left Assistant Censor-in-Chief and academician of the State Historiography Institute. In the eighth year of the Kangxi reign he was appointed governor of Shanxi. Before he could take up the post he was transferred to Guangxi. At that time bandits swarmed like hedgehogs; Yao and Zhuang raiders plundered Wuzhou and Pingle prefectures, but within a few months he had suppressed them. He repeatedly memorialized to stabilize salt prices, build Confucian schools, fix border stipends for local officials, cut military grain transport costs, and abolish purchase levies that burdened the people; the throne approved every proposal.
14
十二年，吳三桂反。 十三年，孫延齡以廣西叛應之，圍雄鎮廨，脅降。 時巡撫無標​​兵，雄鎮督家人拒守。 密令守備易友亮赴柳州趣提督馬雄來援，弗應。 雄鎮自經，為家人救免，以蠟丸馳疏請兵。 延齡诇知之，幽雄鎮，置家人別室。 三桂使招降，雄鎮不為屈。 會傅弘烈勸延齡反正，延齡躊躇未決，雄鎮得以間遣長子世濟齎疏詣京師，友亮導之出，客楊啟祥護行，至贛州，江西巡撫董衛國以聞。 上遣使護入京，至，授世濟四品京卿。 居數月，雄鎮又具疏陳粵西可復狀，付長孫國楨，俾與客硃昉鑿垣出。 既，又遣州人唐守道、唐正發潛負次子世永出，次第詣京師。 又為延齡知，係其孥於獄。 雄鎮憤自剄，復為賊所奪，幽之別室。
In the twelfth year Wu Sangui rebelled. In the thirteenth year Sun Yanling rebelled in Guangxi in support of Wu Sangui, besieged Xiongzhen's yamen, and tried to force his surrender. The governor had no properly marked troops at hand, so Xiongzhen led his household retainers in a defensive stand. He secretly ordered the garrison commander Yi Youliang to go to Liuzhou and urge Grand Coordinator Ma Xiong to send relief, but received no response. Xiongzhen tried to hang himself but was saved by his family, then sent an urgent petition for troops sealed in a wax ball. Yanling learned of this through spies, confined Xiongzhen, and kept his family in separate quarters. Wu Sangui sent envoys to win him over, but Xiongzhen would not yield. When Fu Honglie urged Yanling to return to loyalty and Yanling wavered, Xiongzhen seized the opportunity to send his eldest son Shiji to the capital with a memorial; Youliang guided him out, the guest Yang Qixiang escorted him, and when they reached Ganzhou, Jiangxi Governor Dong Weiguo reported the matter to the throne. The emperor sent an escort to bring him safely to the capital; on arrival, Shiji was made a fourth-rank capital official. After several months Xiongzhen prepared another memorial detailing how western Guangdong could be recovered, entrusted it to his eldest grandson Guozhen, and had him break through a wall with the guest Zhu Fang to escape. Then he secretly sent two local men, Tang Shoudao and Tang Zhengfa, to smuggle out his second son Shiyong, who also made his way to the capital in turn. Yanling discovered this as well and imprisoned his wife and children. In anger Xiongzhen cut his own throat, but the rebels seized him again and held him in a separate room.
15
十六年十月，三桂遣其從孫世琮殺延齡，擁雄鎮至賊壘，迫使降，雄鎮大呼曰：「吾義守封疆，不能寸斬汝以報國，死吾分也！ 」賊戕其幼子世洪、世泰怵之，罵益厲，賊殺之，時年四十有四。 從者馬雲皋、唐進寶、諸兆元等九人同時死，妻李，妾顧、劉，女二人，世濟妻董、妾苗，並殉。 語互詳列女傳。 雄鎮屍暴四十馀日，友亮收其骸骨，藁葬焉。
In the tenth month of the sixteenth year Wu Sangui sent his grand-nephew Shizong to kill Yanling, dragged Xiongzhen to the rebel camp, and tried to force his surrender; Xiongzhen shouted, "I have dutifully guarded the frontier and could not cut you down piece by piece to repay the state—death is my portion! " The rebels killed his younger sons Shihong and Shitai to frighten him; he cursed all the more fiercely, and the rebels killed him. He was forty-four. Nine followers including Ma Yun'gao, Tang Jinbao, and Zhu Zhaoyuan died with him; his wife Li, concubines Gu and Liu, two daughters, Shiji's wife Dong and concubine Miao—all perished as well. Their stories are given in detail in the Biographies of Exemplary Women. Xiongzhen's body lay exposed for more than forty days before Youliang gathered his bones and gave him a straw burial.
16
雄鎮被縶三年，日著書賦詩。 既死，客孫成、陳文煥乘間脫走，抵蒼梧，以所著擊笏樓遺稿及匯草辨疑歸世濟。 十七年，弘烈以雄鎮死狀入告，命議卹。 擢世濟大理寺少卿。 成以舉人授同知，文煥授知縣。 旋又授友亮、守道、正發、啟祥游擊、守備有差。 十八年，世濟如廣西迎雄鎮喪至京師，贈太子太傅、兵部尚書，諡文毅。 三桂既平，歲正，上宴群臣，特命世濟及陳啟泰子汝器至御座前賜酒。 世濟官至遭運總督，世永曆運使； 國楨官江南常鎮道，督餉入藏，卒於軍。
Held captive for three years, Xiongzhen wrote books and composed poetry every day. After his death the guests Sun Cheng and Chen Wenhuan seized an opportunity to escape, reached Cangwu, and delivered to Shiji his surviving writings from the Strike-the-Hu Hall together with Collected Drafts and Disputed Points. In the seventeenth year Honglie reported Xiongzhen's death to the throne, which ordered mourning honors to be deliberated. Shiji was promoted to vice-president of the Court of Judicial Review. Cheng was appointed sub-prefect on the strength of his juren degree, and Wenhuan was made a county magistrate. Soon afterward Youliang, Shoudao, Zhengfa, and Qixiang were appointed to various posts as brigade commanders and garrison commanders. In the eighteenth year Shiji went to Guangxi to bring Xiongzhen's coffin to the capital; Xiongzhen was posthumously made Grand Tutor to the Heir Apparent and Minister of War, with the posthumous name Wenyi. After Wu Sangui was suppressed, at New Year's the emperor feasted the ministers and specially summoned Shiji and Chen Qitai's son Ruqi before the throne to grant them wine. Shiji rose to Zhaoyun Governor; Shiyong became salt transport commissioner. Guozhen served as Chang-Zhen Circuit intendant in Jiangnan, supervised grain supplies into Tibet, and died on campaign.
17
=傅弘烈=傅弘烈，字仲謀，江西進賢人。 明末，流寓廣西。 順治時，以總督王國光薦，授韶州同知，遷甘肅慶陽知府。
Fu Honglie, styled Zhongmou, was a native of Jinxian in Jiangxi. At the end of the Ming dynasty he wandered and settled in Guangxi. During the Shunzhi reign, on the recommendation of Governor-General Wang Guoguang, he was appointed sub-prefect of Shaozhou and later transferred to prefect of Qingyang in Gansu.
18
吳三桂蓄逆謀久，康熙七年，弘烈密以告，逮治，坐誣，論斬。 九年，上特命減死戍梧州。 及三桂反，將軍孫延齡、提督馬雄以廣西叛應之。 弘烈欲集兵圖恢復，陽受三桂偽職，入思州、泗城、廣南、富川諸土司，歷交阯界，募義軍得五千人，遂移檄討賊，從尚可喜軍規肇慶。 三桂甚惎之，使馬雄如柳州害其家百口。 弘烈說延齡反正。 鎮南將軍覺羅舒恕軍贛州，弘烈密致書言延齡妻孔四貞，定南王有德女，未忘國恩，延齡可招撫。 又致書奉詔招撫督捕理事官麻勒吉，言王師速進南安，弘烈自韶州策應，則兩粵可定。 舒恕、麻勒吉先後以聞，上嘉其忠誠，授廣西巡撫、徵蠻滅寇將軍，俾增募義兵，便宜行事。
Wu Sangui had long harbored rebellious designs; in the seventh year of the Kangxi reign Honglie secretly reported it, was arrested, convicted of false accusation, and sentenced to death. In the ninth year the emperor specially ordered the sentence reduced from death to exile at Wuzhou. When Wu Sangui rebelled, General Sun Yanling and Grand Coordinator Ma Xiong rebelled in Guangxi in support. Honglie sought to gather troops for recovery; outwardly he accepted a false appointment from Wu Sangui, entered the chieftaincies of Sizhou, Sicheng, Guangnan, and Fuchuan, crossed into Annam, raised five thousand volunteers, issued a manifesto against the rebels, and marched with Shang Kexi's army toward Zhaoqing. Wu Sangui hated him bitterly and sent Ma Xiong to Liuzhou to slaughter his entire household of a hundred souls. Honglie urged Yanling to return to loyalty. The Zhennan General Aisin Gioro Shuxu was stationed at Ganzhou; Honglie secretly wrote that Yanling's wife Kong Sizhen, daughter of Prince Dingnan Youde, had not forgotten imperial grace and that Yanling could be won over. He also wrote to the imperial commissioner for pacification and bandit suppression Maledji, urging that if the imperial army advanced quickly on Nan'an while Honglie coordinated from Shaozhou, both Guangdong and Guangxi could be secured. Shuxu and Maledji reported this in turn; the emperor praised his loyalty and appointed him governor of Guangxi and General for Suppressing Barbarians and Exterminating Bandits, with authority to raise volunteers and act at discretion.
19
弘烈克梧州，下昭平、賀、鬱林、博白、北流、陸川、興業諸州縣，進复潯州，遣平樂知府劉曉齎疏上方略。 論功，加太子少保，並加曉參議道。 當是時，馬雄據柳州，三桂諸將分據平樂、南寧、橫州，勢洶洶。 弘烈雖屢捷，惟新軍缺砲馬，假於尚之信，弗應。 吳世琮既殺延齡，陷平樂，襲弘烈梧州，弘烈擊敗之。 十七年，與將軍莽依圖圍平樂，戰失利，弘烈與互訐。 詔謂弘烈兵未支俸餉，奮勇收復諸路。 莽依圖自平樂退賀縣，又言糧乏，再退梧州，使弘烈所复郡縣盡棄於賊，因飭莽依圖圖效。 弘烈督兵進，賊數万渡左江，弘烈戰敗。 賊陷藤縣，逼梧州。 十八年，之信軍至，弘烈分兵水陸，乘賊攻城時三面夾擊，賊潰​​走，遂下藤縣，克平樂，進复桂林。
Honglie captured Wuzhou, took Zhaoping, He, Yulin, Bobai, Beiliu, Luchuan, and Xingye, recovered Xunzhou, and sent Pingle Prefect Liu Xiao to deliver a strategic memorial. For his merit he was made Junior Guardian to the Heir Apparent, and Liu Xiao was also given the rank of vice commissioner. At that time Ma Xiong held Liuzhou while Wu Sangui's generals occupied Pingyue, Nanning, and Hengzhou; the rebel position looked formidable. Although Honglie won repeated victories, his new army lacked artillery and mounts; he asked Shang Zhixin for help, but received no response. After Wu Shizong killed Yanling, seized Pingyue, and attacked Honglie at Wuzhou, Honglie repulsed him. In the seventeenth year he and General Manggitu besieged Pingyue but were defeated, and Honglie and Manggitu exchanged accusations. An edict noted that Honglie's troops had received no pay or rations yet had fought bravely to recover many routes. Manggitu withdrew from Pingyue to Hexian, then citing lack of supplies withdrew again to Wuzhou, leaving all the prefectures and counties Honglie had recovered to the rebels; Manggitu was therefore ordered to prove himself. Honglie led his troops forward, but tens of thousands of rebels crossed the Zuo River and he was defeated. The rebels took Teng County and threatened Wuzhou. In the eighteenth year Zhixin's army arrived; Honglie split his forces by land and water and, as the rebels besieged the city, struck them from three sides; the rebels broke and fled; he retook Teng County, captured Pingyue, and recovered Guilin.
20
弘烈密疏言延齡舊部宜善為解散，又言之信怙惡反覆，當早為之所。 馬雄死，子承廕仍附三桂，受偽封懷寧公，詭言乞款附，弘烈許之，為疏聞。 詔授承廕昭義將軍統其眾。 弘烈規取雲、貴。 十九年二月，次柳州，承廕期弘烈會議，弘烈至，承廕以其眾叛，襲破其營，執送貴陽。 世璠誘以偽職，弘烈曰：「爾祖未反時，吾已劾奏，料汝家必為叛逆。 汝敢以此言污我邪？ 」世琮百計說之，罵益厲。 十月辛丑，遇害。 十一月，征南將軍穆佔复貴陽，收遺骸，以死狀聞，贈太子太師、兵部尚書，諡忠毅。 二十二年，允廣西巡撫郝浴請，建雙忠祠於桂林，祀弘烈及馬雄鎮。
Honglie secretly memorialized that Yanling's old troops should be properly disbanded and warned that Zhixin was vicious and unreliable and should be dealt with early. After Ma Xiong died, his son Chengyin still adhered to Wu Sangui, held the false title Duke of Huaining, feigned a desire to surrender, and Honglie agreed and reported this to the throne. An edict appointed Chengyin Zhaoyi General to command his followers. Honglie planned to take Yunnan and Guizhou. In the second month of the nineteenth year he halted at Liuzhou; Chengyin summoned Honglie to a council; when Honglie arrived, Chengyin rebelled with his followers, overran his camp, and sent him captive to Guiyang. Wu Shihuan tempted him with a false office; Honglie said, "Before your grandfather rebelled I had already memorialized against him—I knew your house would turn traitor. How dare you try to defile me with such words? " Wu Shizong tried every persuasion; Honglie cursed all the more fiercely. On day xinchou of the tenth month he was murdered. In the eleventh month the southern expedition general Muzhan recovered Guiyang, gathered his remains, reported his death, and posthumously made him Grand Preceptor to the Heir Apparent and Minister of War, with the posthumous name Zhongyi. In the twenty-second year, granting Guangxi Governor Hao Yu's request, the Double Loyalty Shrine was built at Guilin to honor Honglie and Ma Xiongzhen.
21
=【論】=論曰：方諸籓盛強，朝廷所置督撫，勢不足以相抗。 文焜雖與三桂分疆而治，所部貳於三桂久矣。 若承謨之於精忠，雄鎮之於延齡，皆同城逼處，惟以身殉，無他術也。 弘烈異軍特起，又與莽依圖相失，勢孤，遂困於承廕。 要其忠義激烈，作士氣，怵寇心，皆不為徒死者。 嗚呼，烈已！
Commentary: When the feudatories were at their height, the governors and governors-general appointed by the court lacked sufficient power to resist them. Although Wenkun and Wu Sangui governed separate territories, Wenkun's subordinates had long leaned toward Wu Sangui. As with Chengmo facing Geng Jingzhong and Xiongzhen facing Sun Yanling—all shared the same city with the rebels and had no recourse but martyrdom; there was no other way. Honglie rose as an independent force, then fell out with Manggitu; isolated, he was finally trapped by Chengyin. In essence their fierce loyalty rallied morale, deterred the enemy, and none died in vain. Alas, how fierce!
