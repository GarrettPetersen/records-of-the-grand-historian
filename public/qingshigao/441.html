[Biographies of] Pan Zuyin, Li Wentian, Sun Yijing, Xia Tongshan, and Zhang Jiaxiang

[Biographies of] Zhang Yinglin, Zhang Renfu, and Zhang Hengjia
4
潘祖廕，字伯寅，江蘇吳縣人，大學士世恩孫。 咸豐二年一甲三名進士，授編修。 遷侍讀，入直南書房，充日講起居注官。 累遷侍讀學士，除大理寺少卿。 左宗棠被劾，召對簿，罪不測，祖廕上疏營救，且密薦其能，獄解，乃起獨領一軍。 十一年，詔求直言，祖廕念車駕還都，首斥奸佞，綱紀一新，為上勤聖學、求人才、整軍務、裕倉儲四事。 並請免賦以蘇民困，汰釐以紓民力，嚴軍律以拯民生，廣中額以收民心。 纚纚數千言，稱旨。 遷光祿寺卿。 與修治平寶鑑，書成，被賞賚。 先後糾彈官吏不職狀，書凡數上，文若欽差勝保、直隸總督文煜、陝西巡撫英棨、布政使毛震壽、甘肅布政使恩麟、道員田在田諸人； 武若提督孔廣順、總兵閻丕敘、副將張維義諸人。 繇是直聲震朝端。
Pan Zuyin, styled Boyin, came from Wuxian in Jiangsu and was the grandson of Grand Secretary Pan Shi'en. In 1852 he placed third among the top three graduates of the palace examination and was appointed a compiler in the Hanlin Academy. He rose to Reader, joined the Southern Studio staff, and served as Daily Lecturer and Recorder of the Emperor's Activities. Through successive promotions he became a Reader of the Hanlin Academy and was then appointed Vice Minister of the Court of Revision. When Zuo Zongtang was impeached and summoned to answer charges with his fate uncertain, Zuyin memorialized in his defense and quietly praised his talents. After the case was closed, Zuo was restored and placed in sole command of an army. In 1861 an edict called for candid remonstrance. Noting that the court had returned to the capital and had already begun by driving out corrupt favorites and renewing discipline, Zuyin urged the emperor to pursue four priorities: devoted study, recruitment of capable men, military reform, and fuller granary reserves. He also asked for tax relief to ease popular hardship, abolition of surtax collection to lighten burdens, stern military discipline to protect civilian life, and increased provincial examination quotas to win public trust. His orderly memorial of several thousand words pleased the throne. He was promoted to Minister of the Court of Imperial Entertainments. He helped compile the Precious Mirror of Governance and Peace and was rewarded when the work was finished. Over time he repeatedly impeached negligent officials in several memorials, naming among civil officials the imperial commissioner Sheng Bao, Zhili governor-general Wen Yu, Shaanxi governor Ying Qi, provincial administration commissioners Mao Zhenshou and En Lin of Gansu, circuit intendant Tian Zaitian, and others; and among military officers provincial commander Kong Guangshun, regional commander Yan Pixu, brigade commander Zhang Weiyi, and others. From this his reputation for integrity resounded throughout the court.
5
同治三年，授左副都御史。 坐會議何桂清罪未列銜，絓吏議。 明年，恭親王奕訢獲譴，下群臣議。 祖廕念重臣進退，關係安危，疏請持平用中，酌予轉圜，袪世人惑。 補工部侍郎。 七年，調戶部，充經筵講官。 坐失部印，褫職留任。 典順天鄉試，再坐中式舉人徐景春文理荒謬，鐫二級。 十三年，特旨賞編修，仍入直。 錄輸餉功，釋處分。
In 1864 he was appointed Left Vice Censor-in-Chief. He was censured because, in the deliberation on He Guiqing's case, his name had been omitted from the memorial heading. The following year Prince Gong Yixin was reprimanded, and the matter was referred to the ministers for deliberation. Mindful that the rise and fall of great ministers bore on the dynasty's safety, Zuyin memorialized asking that the court hold to moderation, grant a measure of accommodation, and clear away public confusion. He was appointed Vice Minister of Works. In 1868 he was transferred to the Board of Revenue and appointed lecturer at the Classics Colloquium. He was punished for losing the ministry seal and was stripped of rank but kept in his post. While presiding over the Shuntian provincial examination, he was again censured because the successful candidate Xu Jingchun's essay was absurdly poor, and was demoted two ranks. In 1874, by special edict he was restored to the rank of compiler and again entered regular palace service. His merit in delivering supplies was recorded, and his punishment was lifted.
6
光緒改元，授大理寺卿，補禮部右侍郎。 數遷工部尚書，加太子少保。 五年，主事吳可讀以死請為穆宗立嗣，祖廕被命集議，與徐桐等請申不建儲，彝訓疏存毓慶宮。 明年，偕惇親王奕脤等辦中俄交涉。 約既成，籌善後，條列練兵、簡器、開礦、備餉四事進。 命入直軍機，父憂歸。 服闋，起權兵部尚書，調補工部，兼管順天府尹事。 大婚禮成，晉太子太保。 十六年，卒，贈太子太傅，諡文勤。 寶坻士紳感其救災勤勞，籥建專祠，報可。
At the beginning of the Guangxu reign he was appointed Minister of the Court of Revision and then Right Vice Minister of Rites. He rose through several posts to Minister of Works and was given the additional title Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent. In 1879 the department director Wu Kedu died petitioning that an heir be named for the late Emperor Muzong. Zuyin was ordered to join the deliberation; with Xu Tong and others he argued that precedent against formally establishing an heir should be invoked, and the relevant canonical instructions and memorials were kept in the Palace of Nurturing Joy. The following year, together with Prince Dun Yishun and others, he handled Sino-Russian negotiations. After the treaty was concluded, he planned for the aftermath and submitted four proposals: drill the troops, streamline armaments, open mines, and stock provisions. He was ordered into regular service on the Grand Council, then returned home to observe mourning for his father. When mourning ended he was restored as acting Minister of War, transferred to the Board of Works, and concurrently oversaw the Shuntian metropolitan magistrate. After the grand wedding ceremony was completed, he was advanced to Senior Guardian of the Heir Apparent. In 1890 he died; he was posthumously given the title Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent and the posthumous name Wenqin. The gentry and scholars of Baodi, moved by his diligent work in disaster relief, petitioned to build a shrine in his honor, and the request was approved.
7
祖廕嗜學，通經史，好收藏，儲金石甚富。 先後數掌文衡，典會試二、鄉試三，所得多真士。 時與翁同龢並稱翁潘雲。
Zuyin was devoted to learning, well versed in the classics and histories, an avid collector, and possessed a very rich store of bronzes and stone inscriptions. He presided over literary examinations several times, conducting two metropolitan and three provincial examinations, and selected many genuinely capable scholars. At the time he and Weng Tonghe were together spoken of as the twin luminaries of Weng and Pan.
8
李文田，字芍農，廣東順德人。 咸豐九年一甲三名進士，授編修。 入直南書房，充日講起居注官。 同治五年，大考，晉中允。 九年，督江西學政。 累遷侍讀學士。 秩滿，其母年已七十有七矣，將乞終養，會聞朝廷議修園籞，遂入都覆命。 既至，謁軍機大臣寶鋆，告以東南事可危，李光昭姦猥無行，責其不能匡救。 寶鋆曰：「居南齋亦可言，奚必責樞府？ 」文田日：「正為是來耳！ 」疏上，不報。 逾歲，上停止園工封事，略言：「巴夏禮等焚毀圓明園，其人尚存。 昔既焚之而不懼，安能禁其後之不復為？ 常人之家偶被盜劫，猶必固其門牆，慎其管鑰，未聞有揮金誇富於盜前者。 今彗星見，天象譴告，而猶忍而出此，此必內府諸臣及左右憸人導皇上以朘削窮民之舉。 使朘削而果無他患，則唐至元、明將至今存，大清何以有天下乎？ 皇上亦思圓明園之所以興乎？ 其時高宗西北拓地數千里，東西諸國讋憚天威，府庫充盈，物力豐盛，園工取之內帑而民不知，故皆樂園之成。 今皆反是，聖明在上，此不待思而決者矣。 」疏入，上為動容。 俄乞假歸。 光緒八年，遭母憂。 服竟，起故官，入直如故。 數遷至禮部侍郎，充經筵講官，領閣事。 二十年，疏請起用恭親王奕訢及前布政使遊智開，依行。 明年，卒，卹如製，諡文誠。
Li Wentian, styled Shaonong, came from Shunde in Guangdong. In 1859 he placed third among the top three graduates of the palace examination and was appointed a compiler in the Hanlin Academy. He joined the Southern Studio staff and served as Daily Lecturer and Recorder of the Emperor's Activities. In 1866, in the triennial examination of Hanlin academicians, he was promoted to Middle Sublector. In 1870 he was appointed educational commissioner of Jiangxi. He rose through successive promotions to Reader of the Hanlin Academy. When his term ended, his mother was already seventy-seven; he was about to request leave to care for her in retirement, but upon hearing that the court was deliberating repairs to the imperial gardens, he went to the capital to report on his duties. On arrival he called on Grand Councilor Bao Kun, warned him that affairs in the southeast were perilous, denounced Li Guangzhao as corrupt and depraved, and reproached him for failing to set matters right. Bao Kun said, "Serving in the Southern Studio you may speak up as well; why must you blame the Grand Council?" Wentian said, "That is precisely why I have come!" He submitted his memorial, but received no response. More than a year later he submitted a memorial to halt work on the gardens, stating in brief: "Parkes and others burned the Old Summer Palace, and those men are still alive. If they burned it once without fear, how can we be sure they will not do it again?" When an ordinary household is robbed, it still strengthens its gates and guards its keys; one never hears of flaunting wealth before the thief. Now a comet has appeared as a heavenly warning, yet the court still bears to proceed — this must be inner-palace officials and sycophants at the emperor's side leading him into measures that drain and impoverish the people. If draining the people truly brought no other calamity, dynasties from Tang through Yuan and Ming would still rule today — how then did the Great Qing come to possess the realm? Does Your Majesty also reflect on why the Old Summer Palace was built in the first place? At that time the Qianlong Emperor had extended the northwest frontier by thousands of li; states east and west were awed by imperial might; the treasuries were full and resources abundant; garden works were paid from the inner treasury without the people knowing, and all rejoiced in their completion. Now all is the reverse; with a sagely ruler above, the decision needs no deliberation. When the memorial was received, the emperor was visibly moved. Shortly afterward he requested leave to return home. In 1882 he entered mourning for his mother. When mourning ended he was restored to his former office and resumed regular palace service as before. He rose through several posts to Vice Minister of Rites, served as lecturer at the Classics Colloquium, and headed secretariat affairs. In 1894 he memorialized requesting the restoration of Prince Gong Yixin and the former provincial administration commissioner You Zhikai; both requests were approved. The following year he died; condolence followed the regulations, and he was given the posthumous name Wencheng.
9
文田學識淹通，述作有體，尤諳究西北輿地。 屢典試事，類能識拔績學，士皆稱之。
Wentian's learning was broad and penetrating, his writings were well formed, and he was especially expert in the geography of the northwest. He repeatedly presided over examinations and generally recognized and elevated solid scholars; the literati all praised him.
10
孫詒經，字子授，浙江錢塘人。 咸豐十年進士，選庶吉士。 聞杭州城陷，乞假歸，奉親闢居定海。 參寧紹台道張景渠軍，平浙東有功，還授檢討。 以倭仁薦，入直南書房。 同治四年，擢司業。 上言：「弭災在卹刑，治獄先平法。 本律盜案不分首從，聖祖、世宗加以區別。 自頃盜風充斥，概用重典，行十餘年，案不減少。 則知弭盜之術，不在用法之嚴。 請敕刑部改成例，复祖制。 」議行。 會上將侍太后幸惇親王府，既，與夏同善諫罷。 未幾，復將詣恭親王府祀神，詒經再上疏，言：「聖學方新，宸修宜懋。 經帷屢曠，則神誌難專； 法駕時勤，則見聞易惑。 一日行幸，一日已荒念典之功； 今日行禮，異日或啟遊觀之漸。 」士論歸之。 遭父憂去，服除，仍原官，入直如故。 十年，遷侍講。 五月朔，日食。 詒經以天道感應，本諸人事，於是有遇災修省之請。 十三年夏，彗星見，越數日，太白經天，人心惶駭。 詒經復有廣開言路及罷圓明園工程之請。 遷侍讀學士。 德宗纘業，大考一等，擢詹事。 召對，命直抒所見，連上澄吏治、慎海防機宜甚悉。
Sun Yijing, styled Zishou, came from Qiantang in Zhejiang. In 1860 he passed the palace examination and was selected as a Hanlin bachelor. When he heard that Hangzhou had fallen, he requested leave to return home, cared for his parents, and took refuge at Dinghai. He joined the forces of Zhang Jingqu, intendant of the Ning-Shao-Tai Circuit, and helped pacify eastern Zhejiang; afterward he was appointed Reviser. On Woren's recommendation he joined the Southern Studio staff. In 1865 he was promoted to Vice Director of Studies. He addressed the throne: "Quelling calamity lies in compassionate punishment; governing prisons must first even out the law. The original code for robbery cases did not distinguish principals from accomplices; the Kangxi and Yongzheng emperors added such distinctions. Recently robbery has been widespread and heavy penalties have been applied uniformly; this policy has been in force for more than ten years, yet cases have not diminished. This shows that the art of quelling robbery does not lie in severity of punishment. I request that the Ministry of Justice be ordered to revise the regulations into precedent and restore the ancestral system." The proposal was approved and carried out. When the emperor was about to accompany the Empress Dowager on a visit to Prince Dun's residence, he and Xia Tongshan remonstrated and the visit was canceled. Before long the emperor was again about to go to Prince Gong's residence to sacrifice to the gods; Yijing submitted another memorial, stating: "Sacred learning has just been renewed; the emperor's cultivation should be vigorous. If the Classics Colloquium is repeatedly neglected, the mind cannot remain focused; if the imperial carriage is frequently abroad, what the emperor sees and hears is easily confused. One day spent on an outing wastes a day's work of recalling the canon; today performing rites, tomorrow perhaps opening the way to sightseeing excursions. Scholarly opinion rallied to him. He left office to observe mourning for his father; when mourning ended he retained his former office and resumed regular palace service as before. In 1871 he was promoted to Lecturer. On the first day of the fifth month there was a solar eclipse. Yijing held that heavenly signs respond to human affairs, and on this basis submitted a request to meet calamity with self-examination and reform. In the summer of 1874 a comet appeared; several days later Venus crossed the heavens, and public alarm spread. Yijing again requested broader avenues of remonstrance and an end to work on the Old Summer Palace. He was promoted to Reader of the Hanlin Academy. When Emperor Guangxu succeeded to the throne, he took first rank in the triennial examination and was promoted to Junior Mentor. Summoned for audience, he was told to speak frankly; he submitted in succession detailed memorials on clarifying official governance and prudent coastal defense.
11
光緒六年，俄釁啟，東西海陸邊防亟。 詒經言：「能戰然後能和，兵力專顧海口，北塘覆轍可鑑。 」請調勁旅守東路，並津、永舉辦民團。 再遷刑部侍郎，明年，調戶部。 會左宗棠請修畿輔水利，乃疏薦張之洞、張佩綸資治理，並以山東河患，河員專治河堤，不講修導，建議購泰西機船及時修濬。 十一年，入直毓慶宮。 山東河工領部銀百萬，詒經廉得書吏史恩濤苛索狀，嚴責繳還，將懲治，章未上，而御史王賡榮等輒劾以輕縱。 上令明白回奏，覆奏入，卒陷吏議，並罷直。 有勸引退者，詒經曰：「吾被恩遇久，遑敢佚吾身邪？ 」於是專治部事，佐度支凡十年。 時議設銀行，造鐵路，慮利權外溢，龂龂持異議。
In 1880 the Russian crisis erupted, and coastal and land border defenses east and west grew urgent. Yijing stated: "Only after one can fight can one make peace; military strength should focus on guarding the estuary — the repeated disaster at Beitang stands as a warning. He requested that strong troops be transferred to guard the eastern route, and that militia be organized in Tianjin and Yongping as well. He was again promoted to Vice Minister of Justice; the following year he was transferred to the Board of Revenue. When Zuo Zongtang requested repair of waterworks in the metropolitan region, he memorialized recommending Zhang Zhidong and Zhang Peilun as qualified administrators, and citing the river calamity in Shandong — where river officials devoted themselves only to dikes and neglected dredging and diversion — proposed purchasing Western steam vessels for timely dredging. In 1885 he entered regular service in the Palace of Nurturing Joy. For Shandong river works he received a million taels of ministry silver; Yijing uncovered evidence that the clerk Shi Entao had been extorting funds, severely reproved him and ordered repayment, and was about to punish him — but before his memorial was submitted, Censor Wang Gengrong and others impeached him for undue leniency. The emperor ordered a clear reply; when his reply was received, he was ultimately censured and dismissed from regular palace service. When some urged him to withdraw, Yijing said, "I have long received imperial favor — how dare I indulge myself?" Thereupon he devoted himself solely to ministry affairs and assisted in revenue matters for ten years. When banks and railways were under discussion, fearing that economic advantage would flow abroad, he stubbornly opposed both.
12
詒經持躬清正，思以儒術救時敝。 不阿權要，為同列所忌，卒不得行其志。 先後數司文柄，深惡末學骫骳積習，擯之惟恐不遑，所得多知名士。 生平論學不分漢、宋，謂經學即理學。 又曰：「學所以厲行也，博學而薄行，學奚足尚？ 」一時為學者所宗。 十六年，卒，優詔賜卹，諡文愨。
Yijing conducted himself with integrity and sought to remedy the age's ills through Confucian learning. He would not curry favor with the powerful, was resented by his colleagues, and ultimately could not carry out his aims. He presided over literary examinations several times, deeply detested the obsequious habits of shallow scholarship, and excluded such candidates as fast as he could; those he selected were mostly renowned scholars. Throughout his life he did not distinguish Han and Song learning in his scholarship, holding that classical learning and Neo-Confucian learning were one. He also said, "Learning exists to strengthen conduct; broad learning with thin conduct — what is learning worth?" For a time he was revered by scholars. In 1890 he died; an exceptional edict granted condolence, and he was given the posthumous name Wenque.
13
夏同善，字子鬆，浙江仁和人。 咸豐六年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 累遷右庶子，充日講起居注官。 十年，粵寇陷江南，諸軍無所統，請屬之曾國籓； 又以北塘之役，僧格林沁軍退頓通州，桂良再就議款，同善建言敵情叵測，宜專任僧格林沁備戰守：敕並依行。 父憂歸，服闋，起故官。 同治六年，遷少詹事。 其時傳言車駕將幸惇親王府，召集梨園，同善聞之，與孫詒經合疏諫止。 略言：「皇上沖齡，敬天未至南郊，遊幸先臨府第，未安者一。 聖學端資養正，耳目玩好偶有所娛，恐疏而不密，未安者二。 近頃軍事未寧，遊觀之事傳播四方，曷以慰臣民望？ 未安者三。 英、俄人士雜處京畿，稍示以懈，何能帖伏？ 未安者四。 夫孝以禮為歸，禮以時為大，非時不舉，古有明箴。 乞罷止以彰聖德。 」出督江蘇學政，遭繼母喪去職。 起詹事。 十年，遷兵部右侍郎。 秋，患霪雨，奉其狀以上，乞申虔禱，實行敦節儉、廣賑濟、開言路、清庶獄諸政，語至剴切。 十三年，偕尚書廣壽詣四川按事，奏請撤永川等兵差局、綿竹等伕馬局。
Xia Tongshan, styled Zisong, came from Renhe in Zhejiang. In 1856 he passed the palace examination, was selected as a Hanlin bachelor, and was appointed a compiler in the Hanlin Academy. He rose to Right Sublector and served as Daily Lecturer and Recorder of the Emperor's Activities. In 1860 the Taiping rebels seized Jiangnan; the various armies had no unified command — he requested that they be placed under Zeng Guofan; After the Beitang campaign, when Sengge Rinchen's army withdrew to Tongzhou and Gui Liang again went to negotiate, Tongshan advised that the enemy's intentions were unpredictable and that Sengge Rinchen alone should be entrusted with war preparations and defense — both recommendations were approved. He returned home to observe mourning for his father; when mourning ended he was restored to his former office. In 1867 he was promoted to Junior Mentor. At that time rumor spread that the emperor would visit Prince Dun's residence and summon opera troupes; on hearing this, Tongshan joined Sun Yijing in a joint memorial to stop the visit. They stated in brief: "Your Majesty is still young; you have not yet performed the suburban sacrifice to Heaven, yet an excursion would first visit a princely residence — this is the first cause for unease. Sacred learning properly relies on nurturing rectitude; if the ears and eyes find occasional diversions, one fears they will grow lax and unfocused — this is the second cause for unease. Recently military affairs are unsettled; if sightseeing spreads in all directions, how can the hopes of officials and people be comforted? This is the third cause for unease. British and Russian nationals are present throughout the capital region; if we show the slightest slackness, how can they be kept in check? This is the fourth cause for unease. Filial piety takes ritual as its fulfillment, and ritual takes timeliness as paramount; what is not timely is not performed — antiquity has clear admonitions on this. We beg that the visit be canceled to display sagely virtue. He went out as educational commissioner of Jiangsu and left office on his stepmother's mourning. He was restored as Mentor. In 1871 he was promoted to Right Vice Minister of War. That autumn, amid prolonged rains, he presented the situation to the throne, begging for earnest prayer and urging policies of thrift, broad relief, open remonstrance, and clearing of common prisons — his words were most earnest. In 1874, together with Minister Guangshou, he went to Sichuan to investigate affairs and memorialized to abolish the Yongchuan and other military courier bureaus and the Mianzhu and other transport-horse bureaus.
14
光緒元年，命直毓慶宮授讀，固辭不獲，益屏家事勿問，退唯默坐觀書，思所以為獻納地。 先後累言盜案刑例宜復舊制，分首從； 畿輔旱，請鑿井灌田甦之； 晉、豫飢，請移海防關稅經費卹之。 四年，復命視學江蘇，陛辭日，力陳捐納有礙民生，無裨國用，稱旨。 明年，被命巡視山東黃河，條上治下游三事：曰濬海口，曰直河灣，曰通支河，請移機器局經費治之。 其秋，閱緣江砲台，又歷陳三不可恃，請合數省力助守江口，已築者毋廢，未築者毋增，上然其言。 嘗割俸濬江陰城河，植松五萬餘於君山，民德之。 六年，卒，德宗聞之遽泣，其忠誠荷主知如此。 遺疏入，賜卹如例，諡文敬。 子庚复，主事； 敦复，御史。
In 1875 he was ordered into regular service in the Palace of Nurturing Joy as tutor to the heir apparent; he firmly declined but could not refuse, set household affairs aside entirely, and in private hours sat silently reading, thinking how he might find occasion to remonstrate. He repeatedly urged that robbery cases should restore the old regulations distinguishing principals from accomplices; when the metropolitan region suffered drought, he requested digging wells to irrigate fields and revive agriculture; when Shanxi and Henan suffered famine, he requested shifting coastal defense customs funds for relief. In 1878 he was again ordered to inspect education in Jiangsu; at his farewell audience he forcefully argued that the sale of offices harmed the people's livelihood and did not benefit state revenue — this pleased the emperor. The following year he was ordered to inspect the Yellow River in Shandong and submitted three proposals for the lower reaches: dredge the sea mouth, straighten river bends, and open branch channels — requesting that Arsenal Bureau funds be used for the work. That autumn, inspecting coastal batteries along the Yangtze, he again argued that three defenses were unreliable and requested that several provinces combine forces to guard the river mouth — existing batteries should not be abandoned, but new ones should not be added — the emperor approved. He once donated his salary to dredge the Jiangyin city moat and planted more than fifty thousand pines on Jun Hill; the people were grateful. In 1880 he died; when Emperor Guangxu heard of it he wept — such was the loyalty that had won his sovereign's trust. His final memorial was received; condolence was granted according to precedent, and he was given the posthumous name Wenjing. His son Gengfu was a department director; Dunfu was a censor.
15
張家驤，字子騰，浙江鄞縣人。 同治元年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 督山東學政，調山西。 遭父憂解職，服除，起故官。 遷侍講，入直南書房。 光緒元年，轉侍讀，充日講起居注官。 五年，命直毓慶宮，遷侍講學士。 明年，劉銘傳奉召入都，疏請籌造清江浦鐵路，下李鴻章等議。 家驤念典學方新，講求上理，萬一言利之臣隨聲附和，一言僨事，關係匪輕，乃力陳三弊阻止之。 疏入，仍令鴻章覈覆，鴻章力主銘傳策。 然自是御史洪良品陳五害，侍講張楷陳九不利，並隨家驤而上諫書矣，事竟寢。 數遷內閣學士，充經筵講官。 九年，授工部右侍郎，調吏部。
Zhang Jiaxiang, styled Ziteng, came from Yin County in Zhejiang. In 1862 he passed the palace examination, was selected as a Hanlin bachelor, and was appointed a compiler in the Hanlin Academy. He served as educational commissioner of Shandong, then was transferred to Shanxi. He left office to observe mourning for his father; when mourning ended he was restored to his former office. He was promoted to Lecturer and joined the Southern Studio staff. In 1875 he was transferred to Reader and served as Daily Lecturer and Recorder of the Emperor's Activities. In 1879 he was ordered into regular service in the Palace of Nurturing Joy and was promoted to Lecturer of the Hanlin Academy. The following year Liu Mingchuan was summoned to the capital and memorialized to build the Qingjiangpu railway; the matter was referred to Li Hongzhang and others for deliberation. Jiaxiang, mindful that the emperor's classical education had just begun and that higher principles were being sought, feared that if profit-seeking ministers echoed in agreement, a single misstep could have grave consequences, and forcefully set forth three harms to stop the project. When the memorial was received, the court still ordered Hongzhang to verify and reply; Hongzhang strongly upheld Mingchuan's plan. Yet thereafter Censor Hong Liangpin set forth five harms, Lecturer Zhang Kai set forth nine disadvantages, and all followed Jiaxiang in submitting remonstrance memorials — the project was ultimately shelved. He rose to Grand Secretariat Academician and served as lecturer at the Classics Colloquium. In 1883 he was appointed Right Vice Minister of Works and transferred to the Board of Civil Office.
16
家驤純謹好學，一謝時趨。 蒞官端慎。 授帝讀，朝夕納誨，頗能盡心所職。 十年，卒，上悼惜，賜祭葬如製，諡文莊。
Jiaxiang was pure, cautious, and devoted to learning, and wholly rejected contemporary fashions. In office he was upright and cautious. As tutor to the emperor, offering admonition morning and evening, he devoted himself fully to his duties. In 1884 he died; the emperor mourned him, granted sacrificial burial according to regulations, and gave the posthumous name Wenzhuang.
17
張英麟，字振卿，山東歷城人。 同治四年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 十三年，命偕檢討王慶祺在弘德殿行走。 英麟甫入直，即乞假歸省。 未幾，穆宗崩，慶祺以有罪褫職。 眾皆稱其志節。 歷典福建、雲南鄉試，累遷祭酒，充經筵講官。 光緒十七年，以詹事授奉天府丞，兼學政。 奉省士民樸素，隨軺所至，力加獎勸，學風興起。 晉內閣學士，簡順天學政，擢吏部侍郎。 二十六年，通州試竣回京，兩宮西狩，官吏遷避，英麟獨守學政關防待交替。 明年，召赴行在，應詔上疏，請力崇節儉。 乘輿回鑾，議變法，英麟言祖宗法制，可整飭不可遽更張。 二十九年，充會試副總裁，借闈河南，改試策論、經義。 英麟嚴衡校，多取績學。 會改官制，英麟以侍郎遷副都統，漢員授旗官自此始。 旋晉都統。 三十四年，授都御史。 時議行憲政，許士民上書，英麟必詳審為代達。 御史江春霖直劾親貴，斥回原衙門，英麟率全台合疏留之。
Zhang Yinglin, styled Zhenqing, came from Licheng in Shandong. In 1865 he passed the palace examination, was selected as a Hanlin bachelor, and was appointed a compiler in the Hanlin Academy. In 1874 he was ordered, together with Reviser Wang Qingqi, to serve in the Hall of Illustrious Virtue. Yinglin had only just entered regular service when he requested leave to return home to visit his parents. Before long Emperor Muzong died; Qingqi was stripped of office for misconduct. All praised his moral resolve. He presided over the Fujian and Yunnan provincial examinations in succession, rose to Libationer, and served as lecturer at the Classics Colloquium. In 1891, as Junior Mentor, he was appointed Vice Magistrate of Shuntian Prefecture and concurrently educational commissioner. The gentry and people of Fengtian were plain and simple; wherever his inspection carriage went, he vigorously encouraged them, and scholarly culture flourished. He was advanced to Grand Secretariat Academician, appointed Shuntian educational commissioner, and promoted to Vice Minister of the Board of Civil Office. In 1900, when the Tongzhou examination ended and he returned to the capital, the two palaces fled west; officials fled their posts, but Yinglin alone guarded the educational commissioner's seal awaiting his successor. The following year he was summoned to the traveling court, responded to the edict with a memorial, and urged vigorous promotion of thrift. When the court returned to the capital, reform was discussed; Yinglin argued that ancestral institutions could be rectified but must not be rashly overturned. In 1903 he served as Associate Metropolitan Examination Director, used the Henan examination compound, and changed the examination format to policy essays and classical exposition. Yinglin graded strictly and mostly selected solid scholars. When the official system was reformed, Yinglin moved from vice minister to vice commandant — the first time a Han official received a banner appointment. Shortly afterward he was advanced to commandant. In 1908 he was appointed Censor-in-Chief. When constitutional government was discussed and commoners were permitted to submit memorials, Yinglin always examined them carefully and conveyed them to the throne. When Censor Jiang Chunlin directly impeached imperial relatives and was dismissed to his original post, Yinglin led the entire censorate in a joint memorial to retain him.
18
宣統改元，攝政監國，复舉輪講之典。 英麟撰資治通鑑講章以進，皆發明精義，比附近情，冀以誠意相感動，章上，但循故事留覽而已。 三年，武昌變起，內閣改制，飭都察院及凡有言責者皆停奏事，英麟嘆息以為奇變。 遜位詔下，遂乞罷歸。 德宗永遠奉安，猶奔赴崇陵謁送。 重宴瓊林，加太子太保。 乙丑冬，卒，年八十有八。
At the beginning of the Xuantong reign, with the regent supervising the state, the rotating lecture ceremony was revived. Yinglin composed lecture chapters on the Comprehensive Mirror for Aid in Government and submitted them; all elucidated refined meaning and drew parallels close to human affairs, hoping to move the regent through sincerity — when submitted, they were only kept for review according to precedent. In 1911 the Wuchang uprising erupted; the Grand Secretariat was reorganized, and the Censorate and all remonstrating officials were ordered to cease submitting memorials; Yinglin sighed, taking it as an extraordinary change. When the abdication edict was issued, he requested dismissal and return home. When Emperor Guangxu was permanently laid to rest, he still rushed to Chongling to pay his respects at the farewell. At the repeat Banquet of the Forest of Brilliants he was given the additional title Senior Guardian of the Heir Apparent. In the winter of 1925 he died, aged eighty-eight.
19
張仁黼，字劭予，河南固始人。 光緒二年進士，選庶吉士，授編修，入直上書房。 出督湖北學政，以硃子小學、近思錄訓士。 累遷洗馬，充日講起居注官，補侍講。 二十年，日本釁起，樞臣被劾。 乃與李文田等請起用恭親王奕訢，稱旨。 遷鴻臚寺卿，典試四川。 除奉天府府丞，父憂，未之官。
Zhang Renfu, styled Shaoyu, came from Gushi in Henan. In 1876 he passed the palace examination, was selected as a Hanlin bachelor, appointed a compiler, and joined the Upper Study staff. He went out as educational commissioner of Hubei, instructing scholars with Zhu Xi's Elementary Learning and Reflections on Things at Hand. He rose to Groom, served as Daily Lecturer and Recorder of the Emperor's Activities, and was appointed Lecturer. In 1894 the war with Japan erupted and Grand Councilors were impeached. He then joined Li Wentian and others in requesting the restoration of Prince Gong Yixin — this pleased the emperor. He was promoted to Minister of the Court of State Ceremonial and presided over the Sichuan examination. He was appointed Vice Magistrate of Shuntian Prefecture, but entered mourning for his father before taking up the post.
20
二十六年，拳亂作，奉命在籍治團練。 服闋，赴行在。 時財匱，議加丁口稅。 仁黼謂：「今日國勢極危，而人心未去者，良由世祖除明季三餉； 聖祖詔丁口以五十年為率，嗣後滋生永不加賦：深仁厚澤，民不能忘。 今議加丁稅，違祖制，拂民情，必不可。 」事遂寢。 還京，擢順天府府尹。 再遷兵部侍郎，典試江西，歷學部、法部。
In 1900 the Boxer uprising erupted; he was ordered to organize militia in his native place. When mourning ended he went to the traveling court. At the time finances were depleted, and there was discussion of increasing the poll tax. Renfu said, "Today the national situation is extremely perilous, yet the people's hearts have not departed — this is truly because the Shunzhi Emperor abolished the three levies of the late Ming; the Kangxi Emperor decreed that poll registers take fifty years as the standard, and that thereafter new growth would never be added to the levy — profound benevolence that the people cannot forget. To increase the poll tax now would violate ancestral institutions, go against the people's feelings, and absolutely cannot be done. The proposal was shelved. Returning to the capital, he was promoted to Metropolitan Magistrate of Shuntian. He was again promoted to Vice Minister of War, presided over the Jiangxi examination, and served in the Boards of Education and Justice.
21
三十三年，補大理院正卿，奏請敕部院大臣會訂法律，略言：「法律主要在乎組織立法機關，而所以成之者有三，曰：定法律宗旨，辨法律性質，編法律成典。 中國數千年來，禮陶樂淑，人人皆知尊君親上。 此乃國粹所在，必宜保存，用各國之法以補其不足。 尤須造就法律人才，治法治人，相因為用，然後可收實效。 」又言：「立法之要，規模不可不閎，推行必宜有漸。 否則未當於人心而貿然以試，誠恐外國屬人主義勢力日益擴張，而吾國屬地主義處理愈形棼糾。 有司奉行不善，反使外人得以藉口，為患甚大。 」疏入，多議行。 俄授吏部侍郎，充經筵講官。 三十四年，丁母憂。 未幾，卒。
In 1907 he was appointed Chief Justice of the Court of Revision and memorialized requesting that ministers of the boards and courts meet to revise the laws, stating in brief: "Law chiefly lies in organizing legislative bodies, and completing it requires three steps: defining the purpose of law, distinguishing the nature of law, and compiling law into a code. For several thousand years in China, ritual has molded and music has refined the people; everyone knows to honor the ruler and cherish superiors. This is the national essence and must be preserved, using foreign laws to supplement what is lacking. Especially one must train legal talent — governing law and governing people reinforcing each other — only then can real results be obtained. He also said, "The essentials of legislation — the scope must be broad, yet implementation must proceed gradually. Otherwise, if it does not accord with popular sentiment yet is rashly tried, I truly fear that foreign personal jurisdiction will daily expand, while our territorial jurisdiction will grow ever more tangled. If officials carry it out poorly, foreigners will gain a pretext — the harm would be very great. When the memorial was received, most of its proposals were approved. Shortly afterward he was appointed Vice Minister of the Board of Civil Office and served as lecturer at the Classics Colloquium. In 1908 he entered mourning for his mother. Before long he died.
22
仁黼內行修，不自標異。 嘗被命治河，卻例饋節省金，同官懼，謂將興大獄。 仁黼忽索取金，眾始安，然頗怪其失操。 已而河南巡撫上言紳士助學校金，不受獎敘，數與之同。 朝士益服其清不絕物雲。
Renfu's private conduct was cultivated, and he did not set himself apart. He was once ordered to manage river works and declined the customary gifts of saved funds; his colleagues were afraid, saying a major investigation would be launched. Renfu suddenly demanded the gold; his colleagues were reassured, yet many wondered at his lapse in integrity. Before long the Henan governor memorialized that gentry and scholars had contributed school funds, which Renfu did not accept for reward or citation — the amount was the same. Court scholars increasingly admired his integrity without rejecting worldly affairs.
23
張亨嘉，字燮鈞，福建侯官人。 光緒九年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 十四年，視學湖南，念儒官為士模範，不激濁揚清，曷以勵風教？ 疏薦文行交修者數人，士習為一變。 二十三年，入直南書房。 越二年，除司業，頻轉太常寺少卿。 一歲五遷，殊數也。
Zhang Hengjia, styled Xiejun, came from Houguan in Fujian. In 1883 he passed the palace examination, was selected as a Hanlin bachelor, and was appointed a compiler in the Hanlin Academy. In 1888 he inspected education in Hunan, reflecting that Confucian officials are models for scholars — if one does not stir the muddy and elevate the clear, how can one encourage custom and teaching? He memorialized recommending several men of cultivated literary conduct, and scholarly custom was transformed. In 1897 he joined the Southern Studio staff. Two years later he was appointed Vice Director of Studies and was quickly transferred to Vice Minister of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. Five promotions in one year — an extraordinary number.
24
二十六年夏，親貴大臣信拳民有神術能攘外，飾詞入告，上疑之，命亨嘉察視。 亨嘉知其不可恃，條上弭釁機宜甚悉，疏甫入而亂作。 西狩還，獨先賜用，徙大理寺卿。 明年，出督浙江學政，頗採西國政教命題試士，多得通材。 尚書張百熙、榮慶既為學務大臣，別置大學總監督，亨嘉遂被命任校事，仍不離內廷職。 大學中更寇亂，肄業生不盈百，乃闢學舍，廣集高材生。 類別學科，禮聘儒宿及東西邦學人專門教授。 書籍儀器，粲然具備。 兼攝進士館監督，進士習法政自此始。 歷光祿寺卿、左副都御史、兵部侍郎。 逾歲，疏辭校職，轉禮部侍郎，充經筵講官。
In the summer of 1900, imperial relatives and great ministers believed the Boxers had divine arts that could repel foreigners; they embellished their reports to the throne; the emperor was doubtful and ordered Hengjia to investigate. Hengjia knew they could not be relied upon and submitted a detailed list of measures to quell the crisis; his memorial had only just arrived when disorder broke out. After the court returned from its flight west, he alone was first restored to office and transferred to Minister of the Court of Revision. The following year he went out as educational commissioner of Zhejiang, largely drawing on Western politics and education for examination topics, and selected many broadly capable men. After Ministers Zhang Baixi and Rong Qing became educational affairs ministers, a separate post of university superintendent was established; Hengjia was ordered to manage the university while retaining his inner-court duties. The university had again suffered from turmoil; enrolled students numbered fewer than a hundred — he opened new school buildings and broadly gathered talented students. He classified subjects by discipline and ceremonially invited veteran Confucians and scholars from East and West for specialized instruction. Books and instruments were abundantly provided. He concurrently served as superintendent of the Advanced Scholar Hall; training advanced scholars in law and administration began from this time. He served in succession as Minister of the Court of Imperial Entertainments, Left Vice Censor-in-Chief, and Vice Minister of War. After more than a year he memorialized to resign his university post, was transferred to Vice Minister of Rites, and served as lecturer at the Classics Colloquium.
25
亨嘉為人敦實，嗜古精鑑賞。 事母孝，母黃氏，壽百歲，同列奏庥瑞。 中興後命婦享高耄者，與詹事袁葆恆祖母郭氏二人而已。 上聞之嘆異，加恩賜予。 三十四年，遭喪去，終服，仍入直。 宣統二年，卒，賜祭葬，諡文厚。
Hengjia was solid and sincere, fond of antiquities and skilled in connoisseurship. He was filial toward his mother; his mother, née Huang, lived to a hundred years; his colleagues memorialized this as a blessing and auspicious sign. After the restoration, among titled ladies who enjoyed great old age, only his mother and Junior Mentor Yuan Baoheng's grandmother, née Guo, were the two. When the emperor heard of it he sighed in wonder and granted additional favors and gifts. In 1908 he left on mourning; when mourning ended he again entered regular palace service. In 1910 he died; sacrificial burial was granted, and he was given the posthumous name Wenhou.
26
論曰：同、光典學內直諸臣，每兼授讀，體制較隆； 而文學侍從，亦多選績學，時備顧問，稱榮幸焉。 祖廕好賢勤事，文田學識淹雅，同以通博稱。 詒經重實學，同善崇聖德，家驤盡心誨納，英麟早勵風節，並無愧師儒。 仁黼、亨嘉尤惓惓於明法修學，後先相望，其風采皆隱然可見焉。
The commentary says: During the Tongzhi and Guangxu reigns, the ministers who presided over learning in inner regular service each also held tutoring posts — the institution was comparatively elevated; and among the literary attendants, many solid scholars were also selected to serve as advisers at court — all called it an honor. Zuyin loved worthies and was diligent in affairs; Wentian's learning was broad and elegant — both were famed for comprehensive erudition. Yijing valued practical learning; Tongshan revered sagely virtue; Jiaxiang devoted himself to instruction and remonstrance; Yinglin early cultivated moral resolve — all were worthy teacher-scholars. Renfu and Hengjia were especially earnest in clarifying law and cultivating learning; succeeding one another in time, their bearing can still be dimly discerned.
