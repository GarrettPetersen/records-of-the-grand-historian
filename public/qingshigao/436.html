1
沈桂芬，字經笙，順天宛平人，本籍江蘇吳江。 道光二十七年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 咸豐二年，大考一等，擢庶子。 累遷內閣學士。 先後典浙江、廣東鄉試，督陝甘學政，充會試副總裁。 八年，丁父憂。 服闋，補原官。 晉禮部左侍郎。 同治二年，出署山西巡撫，明年，實授。 連上移屯、練兵諸疏，並稱旨。 桂芬以山西民食不敷，自洋藥弛禁，栽種罌粟，糧價踴增。 於是刊發條約，飭屬嚴禁。 疏陳現辦情形，上韙之，頒行各省，著為令。 旋丁母憂。 六年，起禮部右侍郎，充經筵講官，命為軍機大臣。 歷戶部、吏部，擢都察院左都御史，兼總理各國事務大臣。 遷兵部尚書，加太子少保。 光緒元年，以本官協辦大學士。 京畿旱，編修何金壽援漢代天災策免三公為言，請責斥樞臣，諭交部議。 桂芬坐革職，特旨改為革職留任。 旋復原官，充翰林院掌院學士，晉太子太保。
Shen Guifen, whose style was Jingsheng, came from Wanping in Shuntian, though his family register was in Wujiang, Jiangsu. He earned his jinshi degree in 1847, entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor, and was made a reviser. In 1852 he placed in the top class of the triennial examination and was promoted to junior tutor of the Heir Apparent. He rose through successive appointments to Grand Secretariat Bachelor. He later supervised the provincial examinations in Zhejiang and Guangdong, served as educational commissioner for Shaanxi and Gansu, and acted as associate chief examiner of the metropolitan examination. In 1858 he withdrew to observe mourning for his father. After the mourning period he returned to his former office. He was promoted to Left Vice Minister of Rites. In 1863 he was named acting governor of Shanxi, and the next year received the substantive post. He sent up a series of memorials on land reclamation and troop training, each of which met with the emperor's approval. Guifen found that Shanxi's grain supply could not meet its needs: once the ban on foreign opium was eased, farmers turned to poppies, and grain prices shot up. He thereupon published regulations and ordered his subordinates to enforce a strict prohibition. In a memorial he described the measures then in force; the emperor approved, had it circulated to every province, and made it standing policy. Soon afterward he entered mourning for his mother. In 1867 he was recalled as Right Vice Minister of Rites, appointed lecturer at the Classic Lectures, and named a Grand Councilor. He served in the Ministries of Revenue and Personnel, was promoted to Left Censor-in-Chief, and concurrently headed the Office for the Management of Foreign Affairs. He was transferred to Minister of War and given the title Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent. In 1875 he was made Associate Grand Secretary while keeping his existing post. When drought afflicted the capital region, Compiler He Jinshou invoked the Han precedent of dismissing the Three Excellencies after natural calamities and called for the Grand Councilors to be held to account; the emperor ordered the matter referred to the ministries for deliberation. Guifen was sentenced to dismissal, but a special edict reduced the penalty to dismissal with retention in office. He was soon restored to his former rank, made Chancellor of the Hanlin Academy, and promoted to Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent.
2
桂芬遇事持重，自文祥逝後，以諳究外情稱。 日本之滅琉球也，廷論多主戰，桂芬獨言勞師海上，易損國威，力持不可。 及與俄人議還伊犁，崇厚擅訂約，朝議紛然； 桂芬委曲斡旋，易使往議，改約始定，而言者猶激論不已。 桂芬久臥病，六年，卒，年六十有四，贈太子太傅，諡文定。
Guifen handled affairs with caution and steadiness; after Wen Xiang died, he was known as the man who understood foreign conditions. When Japan annexed Ryukyu, most at court favored war, but Guifen alone argued that sending troops to sea would squander strength and harm national prestige, and he firmly opposed war. When talks with Russia over returning Ili began, Chonghou had signed a treaty on his own authority, and court debate grew heated; Guifen mediated delicately, sent a new envoy to negotiate, and the revised treaty was finally settled—yet critics still argued without end. Guifen had long been ill in bed; in 1880 he died at sixty-four, was posthumously made Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent, and given the posthumous title Wendian.
3
桂芬躬行謹飭，為軍機大臣十餘年，自奉若寒素，所處極湫隘，而未嘗以清節自矜，人以為難雲。
Guifen was scrupulous in personal conduct; for more than ten years as Grand Councilor he lived like a poor scholar in cramped quarters, yet never flaunted his integrity—something contemporaries found hard to equal.
4
李鴻藻，字蘭孫，直隸高陽人。 咸豐二年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 典山西鄉試，督河南學政。 十年，上擇儒臣為皇子師，大學士彭蘊章以鴻藻應。 召來京，明年，特詔授大阿哥讀。 穆宗登極，皇太后懿旨命直弘德殿。 同治元年，擢侍講。 累遷內閣學士。 署戶部左侍郎。 四年，命直軍機。 五年，授禮部右侍郎。 遭母憂，皇太后懿旨，援雍正、乾隆年大臣孫嘉淦等故事，命鴻藻開缺守孝，百日後仍授讀，兼參機務。 並諭：「移孝作忠，勿以守禮固辭。 」鴻藻懇終制，不允。 倭仁等亦代為陳請，仍命恭親王傳諭慰勉。 鴻藻連疏稱疾，遂得賜告，卒終制始出。
Li Hongzao, style Lansun, was from Gaoyang in Zhili. In 1852 he earned his jinshi degree, entered the Hanlin as a bachelor, and was appointed reviser. He supervised the Shanxi provincial examination and served as educational commissioner of Henan. In 1860 the emperor sought scholar-officials to instruct the princes; Grand Secretary Peng Yunzhang nominated Hongzao. He was called to Beijing, and the next year a special edict made him tutor to the heir apparent. When Emperor Tongzhi took the throne, the Empress Dowager's rescript assigned him to the Hall of Manifest Virtue. In 1862 he was promoted to expositor. He was successively promoted to Grand Secretariat Bachelor. He served as acting Left Vice Minister of Revenue. In 1865 he was ordered onto the Grand Council. In 1866 he was appointed Right Vice Minister of Rites. When his mother died, the Empress Dowager cited Yongzheng- and Qianlong-era precedents such as Sun Jiahan's and ordered Hongzao to leave office for mourning, but after a hundred days to resume as tutor while still serving on the Grand Council. She also said: "Turn mourning into loyal service; do not cling to ritual and refuse." " Hongzao pleaded to observe the full mourning term, but was refused. Woren and others petitioned for him as well, but the emperor still had Prince Gong convey words of comfort and urging. Hongzao sent repeated memorials pleading illness and was granted leave; he did not return until mourning was finished.
5
七年，捻擾畿疆，鴻藻方里居，以各路統兵大員事權不一，疏請特派親王為大將軍，坐鎮京師，以固北路； 左宗棠、李鴻章為參贊大臣，分扎保定、河間東西兩路，各率所部兵勇相機剿辦； 陳國瑞為幫辦軍務，專統一軍為游擊之師； 直隸總督官文專顧省城，籌備諸軍餉需，以資接濟； 丁寶楨駐紮直、東交界，防賊東竄； 李鶴年駐紮直、豫交界，防賊南竄； 直、晉交界，由左宗棠等分撥勁旅扼要駐紮； 並請敕下各該大臣和衷商辦，迅奏膚功。 奏入，上遂命各路統兵大臣均歸恭親王節制。 旋起禮部左侍郎，仍直弘德殿及軍機如故。
In 1868, as Nian bandits threatened the capital region, Hongzao was at home and, seeing that field commanders lacked unified authority, memorialized that a prince be appointed Grand General to hold Beijing and secure the northern front; Zuo Zongtang and Li Hongzhang were to serve as assisting ministers, one holding Baoding and one Hejian on the eastern and western routes, each leading his troops to pursue the rebels as opportunity allowed; Chen Guorui was to assist in military affairs and command a separate corps as a mobile striking force; Zhili Governor-General Guanwen was to hold the provincial capital and prepare supplies for all forces; Ding Baozhen was to guard the Zhili-Shandong border against an eastern breakout; Li Henian was to guard the Zhili-Henan border against a southern breakout; On the Zhili-Shanxi border, Zuo Zongtang and others were to station picked troops at critical points; He also asked that all these ministers cooperate harmoniously and report victory without delay. When the memorial arrived, the emperor ordered every field commander placed under Prince Gong's authority. He was soon recalled as Left Vice Minister of Rites and continued at the Hall of Manifest Virtue and on the Grand Council as before.
6
十年，擢都察院左都御史，加太子少保。 時有修葺圓明園之旨，朝臣同起力爭。 鴻藻亦言：「粵、捻初平，回氛方熾，宜培養元氣，以固根本。 不應虛糜帑糈，為此不急之務。 」乃止。 十三年，上有疾，命代批答章奏； 旋崩，自劾輔導無狀，罷弘德殿行走。
In 1871 he was promoted to Left Censor-in-Chief and given the title Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent. An edict then went out to restore the Old Summer Palace, and officials across the court protested in unison. Hongzao also said: "The Taiping and Nian campaigns have only just ended and Muslim revolts are still fierce; the court should conserve its strength and secure the foundations. It should not waste revenue on so inessential a project. " The work was stopped. In 1874 the emperor fell ill and ordered him to draft responses to memorials; The emperor soon died; Hongzao accused himself of failing as tutor and was dismissed from the Hall of Manifest Virtue.
7
光緒二年，命兼總理各國事務衙門。 尋丁本生母憂，服闋，起故官，以兵部尚書協辦大學士，調吏部。 時崇厚與俄擅定伊犁約，鴻藻堅持不可，爭於廷。 卒治崇厚罪，議改約。 及法越啟釁，言路愈奮發，劾罷樞臣。 鴻藻謫遷內閣學士。 後復累遷禮部尚書。
In 1876 he was ordered to serve concurrently in the Office for the Management of Foreign Affairs. He soon mourned his birth mother; after mourning he returned, served as Minister of War and Associate Grand Secretary, and was transferred to Personnel. When Chonghou had signed the Ili treaty with Russia on his own authority, Hongzao firmly opposed it and debated in court. Chonghou was finally punished and a new treaty negotiated. When war broke out between France and Vietnam, memorialists grew bolder and impeached the Grand Councilors out of office. Hongzao was demoted to Grand Secretariat Bachelor. He was later promoted again, eventually to Minister of Rites.
8
十三年，河決鄭州，上命鴻藻馳往督辦。 先是河道總督李鶴年、河南巡撫倪文蔚議於西壩興工，鴻藻至，仍之。 又續興東壩工。 疊遇奇險，皆力為固守。 會伏秋汛至，西壩失事，請暫停工。 上以鴻藻督率無方，革職留任； 並奪李鶴年河道總督，命鴻藻暫行署理。 尋回京，復以禮部具奏典禮漏繕簽改日期，再議革職，上特寬免。 大婚禮成，復原官。
In 1887 the Yellow River broke at Zhengzhou; the emperor ordered Hongzao to rush there and supervise the works. River Conservancy Director Li Henian and Henan Governor Ni Wenwei had already planned work on the western embankment; Hongzao upheld their plan on arrival. He also pressed ahead with the eastern embankment project. Despite repeated crises he did everything to hold the works firm. When the autumn floods came, the western embankment gave way, and he asked to suspend work temporarily. The emperor blamed Hongzao for poor supervision and punished him with dismissal while retaining him in office; Li Henian was removed as River Conservancy Director, and Hongzao was ordered to act in his place. He soon returned to Beijing; the Ministry of Rites then submitted a ceremonial document with a misdated endorsement, and dismissal was proposed again, but the emperor specially spared him. After the grand wedding he was restored to his former rank.
9
二十年，日韓事棘，命鴻藻商辦軍務，再授軍機大臣。 與翁同龢皆主戰，並爭和約，卒不能阻。 旋以禮部尚書協辦大學士，調吏部。 歷蒙頒賞書畫及諸上方珍物。 充鄉試、會試、殿試等閱卷大臣。 二十三年，以病乞假，疾篤，賞給藥餌，命御醫往視。 卒，年七十有八。 遺疏入，上震悼，予諡文正，贈太子太傅。 子焜瀛、煜瀛，均賞給郎中。
In 1894, as the Korean crisis sharpened, Hongzao was ordered to advise on military affairs and again made Grand Councilor. He and Weng Tonghe both favored war and fought the peace treaty together, but could not stop it in the end. He was soon Minister of Rites and Associate Grand Secretary, then transferred to Personnel. He received repeated imperial gifts of paintings, calligraphy, and other treasures from the palace. He served as chief examiner for the provincial, metropolitan, and palace examinations. In 1897 he asked for sick leave; when his illness worsened, the emperor sent medicines and ordered the imperial physician to attend him. He died at seventy-eight. When his death memorial arrived, the emperor was deeply grieved, granted the posthumous title Wenzheng, and posthumously made him Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent. His sons Kunying and Yuying were both granted the rank of director in a ministry.
10
鴻藻性至孝，為學守程硃，務實踐，持躬儉約。 傅穆宗十餘年，盡心啟沃。 一日，穆宗學書，故為戲筆。 鴻藻立前捧上手曰：「皇上心不靜，請少息。 」穆宗改容謝之。 其在樞府，獨守正持大體。 御史王鵬運諫止修頤和園，幾獲重譴，鴻藻力解之，得免。 德宗間日一往頤和園侍起居，時留駐蹕。 言官有言其不便者，太后大怒，欲黜之，鴻藻謂如此必失天下臣民之望，乃止。 所薦引多端士。 朝列有清望者，率倚以為重，然亦不免被劫持雲。
Hongzao was deeply filial by nature; in scholarship he followed the Cheng-Zhu school and valued practice over theory, and he lived frugally. He instructed Emperor Tongzhi for more than ten years and devoted himself wholeheartedly to the young emperor's development. One day, while the emperor was practicing calligraphy, he deliberately scrawled in play. Hongzao stepped forward, took the emperor's hand, and said: "Your Majesty's mind is unsettled; please pause a moment. " The emperor immediately composed himself and apologized. In the Grand Council he alone held to the larger principles with integrity. When Censor Wang Pengyun protested against restoring the Summer Palace and nearly suffered severe punishment, Hongzao intervened forcefully and saved him. Emperor Guangxu went to the Summer Palace every other day to attend the Empress Dowager, and sometimes stayed there for long stretches. When censors criticized the arrangement, the Empress Dowager was furious and wished to dismiss them; Hongzao said this would surely forfeit the trust of officials and people everywhere, and she relented. Most of those he recommended and advanced were men of talent. Men of standing at court generally leaned on him, yet he was not entirely free from pressure, it is said.
11
翁同龢，字叔平，江蘇常熟人，大學士心存子。 咸豐六年一甲一名進士，授修撰。 八年，典試陝甘，旋授陝西學政，乞病回京。 同治元年，擢贊善。 典山西試。 父憂歸，服闋，轉中允。 命在弘德殿行走，五日一進講，於簾前說治平寶鑑，兩宮皇太后嘉之。 累遷內閣學士。 母憂服闋，起故官。 同龢居講席，每以憂勤惕厲，啟沃聖心。 當八年武英殿之災也，恭錄康熙、嘉慶兩次遇災修省聖訓進禦，疏言：「變不虛生，遇災而懼。 宜停不急之工，惜無名之費。 開直臣忠諫之路，杜小人幸進之門。 」上覽奏動容。 又圓明園方興工，商人李光昭矇報木價，為李鴻章所劾論罪。 廷臣多執此入諫，恭親王等尤力諍，上不懌。 同龢面陳江南輿論，中外人心惶惑，請聖意先定，待時興修。 乃議定停園工，並有停工程、罷浮費、求直言之諭。
Weng Tonghe, style Shuping, was from Changshu in Jiangsu, son of Grand Secretary Weng Xincun. In 1856 he took first place in the jinshi examination and was appointed reviser. In 1858 he supervised the Shaanxi-Gansu examination, was appointed educational commissioner of Shaanxi, then asked for sick leave and returned to Beijing. In 1862 he was promoted to tutor. He supervised the Shanxi provincial examination. He went home for his father's mourning; when it ended he was made junior mentor. He was assigned to the Hall of Manifest Virtue, lecturing every five days and expounding the Mirror of Good Governance before the curtain; both Empress Dowagers commended him. He rose successively to Grand Secretariat Bachelor. After his mother's mourning he returned to his former office. At the lecture seat he constantly urged vigilance and diligence to cultivate the emperor's mind. When the Hall of Military Glory burned in 1882, he copied the Kangxi and Jiaqing emperors' instructions on self-examination after disasters and presented them, writing: "Calamities do not arise without cause; one must fear them when they strike. Unurgent projects should stop and pointless spending be cut. Open the way for upright ministers to speak frankly and shut the door on petty men who advance by flattery. " The emperor read the memorial and was visibly moved. Meanwhile the Old Summer Palace was under repair; merchant Li Guangzhao had falsified timber prices and was impeached and punished on Li Hongzhang's motion. Many officials used the case to remonstrate; Prince Gong and others argued especially hard, to the emperor's displeasure. Tonghe told the emperor face to face of Jiangnan opinion and widespread anxiety at home and abroad, asking him to settle his mind first and postpone repairs until the time was right. The garden works were halted, with edicts to stop projects, cut waste, and welcome frank speech.
12
光緒元年，署刑部右侍郎。 明年四月，上典學毓慶宮，命授讀，再辭，不允。 旋遷戶部，充經筵講官，晉都察院左都御史。 遷刑部尚書，調工部。 六年，廷臣爭俄約久不決，懿旨派惇親王、醇親王及同龢與潘祖廕每日在南書房看摺件電報，擬片進呈取進止，至俄約改定始止。 八年，命充軍機大臣。 十年，法越事起，同龢主一面進兵，一面與議，庶有所備。 又言劉永福不足恃，非增重兵出關不可。 旋與軍機王大臣同罷，仍直毓慶宮。 前後充會試總裁、順天鄉試考官，兩蒙賜「壽」，加太子太保，賜雙眼花翎、紫韁。 嘗請假修墓，傳旨海上風險，命馳驛回京，恩眷甚篤。
In 1875 he served as acting Right Vice Minister of Justice. The next April the emperor began study in the Palace of Nurturing Joy; Tonghe was ordered to tutor him, declined twice, and was refused. He was soon transferred to Revenue, made lecturer at the Classic Lectures, and promoted to Left Censor-in-Chief. He became Minister of Justice, then was transferred to Works. In 1880 the Russian treaty debate dragged on; the Empress Dowager assigned Prince Dun, Prince Chun, Tonghe, and Pan Zuyin to review memorials and telegrams daily in the Southern Study and draft summaries for her decision, until the treaty was revised. In 1882 he was ordered onto the Grand Council. In 1884, when war with France over Vietnam began, Tonghe favored advancing troops while also negotiating, so the court would not be caught unprepared. He also said Liu Yongfu was not dependable and that major reinforcements must cross the border. He was soon dismissed from the Grand Council with the other councilors but remained at the Palace of Nurturing Joy. He served as metropolitan chief examiner and Shuntian examiner, twice received the imperial gift of the character "longevity," was made Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent, and granted double-eyed peacock feathers and a purple bridle. Once when he asked leave to repair his family's tomb, an edict cited the dangers of sea travel and ordered him back to Beijing by courier; imperial favor ran very deep.
13
二十年，再授軍機大臣。 懿旨命撤講，上請如故。 同龢善伺上意，得遇事進言。 上親政久，英爽非復常度，剖決精當。 每事必問同龢，眷倚尤重。 時日韓起釁，同龢與李鴻藻主戰，孫毓汶、徐用儀主和。 會海陸軍皆敗，懿旨命赴天津傳諭李鴻章詰責之，同龢並言太后意決不即和。 歸薦唐仁廉忠赤可用，請設巡防處籌辦團防。 於是命恭親王督辦軍務，同龢、鴻藻等會商辦理。 上嘗問諸臣：「時事至此，和戰皆無可恃！ 」言及宗社，聲淚並發。 及和議起，同龢與鴻藻力爭改約稿，並陳：「寧增賠款，必不可割地。 」上曰：「台灣去，則人心皆去。 朕何以為天下主？ 」毓汶以前敵屢敗對，上責以賞罰不嚴，故至於此。 諸臣皆引咎。 上以和約事徘徊不能決，天顏憔悴。 同龢以俄、英、德三國謀阻割地，請展期換約，以待轉圜。 與毓汶等執爭，終不可挽，和約遂定。 明年，兼總理各國事務大臣。 二十三年，以戶部尚書協辦大學士。
In 1894 he was again made Grand Councilor. The Empress Dowager ordered lectures withdrawn; the emperor asked that they continue unchanged. Tonghe was adept at reading the emperor's mood and could speak frankly when occasions arose. The emperor had ruled in person for years; his mind was sharp and no longer what it had been, and his judgments were exact. He consulted Tonghe on every matter, and his trust in him was especially great. As the Korean crisis sharpened, Tonghe and Li Hongzao favored war while Sun Yuwen and Xu Yongyi favored peace. When army and navy both failed, a rescript sent him to Tianjin to rebuke Li Hongzhang; Tonghe also said the Empress Dowager would not accept peace at once. On his return he recommended Tang Renlian as loyal and capable and asked that a patrol office be set up to organize local defense. Prince Gong was then ordered to supervise military affairs, with Tonghe, Hongzao, and others to consult together. The emperor once asked his ministers: "Matters have come to this—neither war nor peace can be trusted! " Speaking of the dynasty's altars, he wept as he spoke. When peace talks began, Tonghe and Hongzao fought to revise the draft, saying together: "Better to increase indemnity than cede territory. " The emperor said: "If Taiwan is lost, the people's hearts will go with it. How can I remain sovereign of all under Heaven? " Sun Yuwen cited repeated defeats; the emperor blamed lax discipline for bringing matters to this point. Every minister accepted blame. The emperor could not decide on the treaty, and his face grew haggard. Tonghe noted that Russia, Britain, and Germany opposed ceding territory and asked to delay exchanging ratifications for a turn in events. He argued fiercely with Sun Yuwen and others but could not prevail, and the treaty was signed. The next year he was ordered to serve concurrently in the Office for the Management of Foreign Affairs. In 1897 he was Minister of Revenue and Associate Grand Secretary.
14
二十四年，上初召用主事康有為，議行新政。 四月，硃諭：「協辦大學士翁同龢近來辦事多不允協，以致眾論不服，屢經有人參奏。 且每於召對時諮詢事件，任意可否，喜怒見於詞色，漸露攬權狂悖情狀，斷難勝樞機之任。 本應查明究辦，予以重懲； 姑念其在毓慶宮行走有年，不忍遽加嚴譴。 翁同龢著即開缺回籍，以示保全。 」八月，政變作，太后復訓政。 十月，又奉硃諭：「翁同龢授讀以來，輔導無方，往往巧藉事端，刺探朕意。 至甲午年中東之役，信口侈陳，任意慫恿。 辦理諸務，種種乖謬，以致不可收拾。 今春力陳變法，濫保非人，罪無可逭。 事後追維，深堪痛恨！ 前令其開缺回籍，實不足以蔽辜，翁同龢著革職，永不敘用，交地方官嚴加管束。 」三十年，卒於家，年七十有五。 宣統元年，詔復原官。 後追諡文恭。
In 1898 the emperor first summoned Principal Clerk Kang Youwei and began deliberating new policies. In April a vermilion rescript read: "Associate Grand Secretary Weng Tonghe has lately handled affairs in ways that displease the court, drawing repeated impeachment and widespread dissatisfaction. At audiences he arbitrarily approves or rejects what he is asked, joy and anger plain in his speech, gradually revealing a reckless urge to monopolize power—clearly unfit for the Grand Council. He ought to be investigated and severely punished; yet considering his years in the Palace of Nurturing Joy, the court will not immediately impose harsh punishment. Weng Tonghe is ordered to leave office and return home, as an act of lenient preservation. " In August the coup occurred and the Empress Dowager resumed regency. In October another vermilion rescript read: "Since Weng Tonghe became tutor, his guidance has failed; he has often used incidents to probe the emperor's intentions. During the Sino-Japanese War of 1894–95 he spoke rashly and recklessly urged war. In handling affairs he erred repeatedly until matters spun out of control. This spring he strongly urged reform and recklessly recommended unworthy men—offenses that cannot be pardoned. Looking back, the harm is deeply to be regretted! Earlier he was sent home, which does not answer his guilt; Weng Tonghe is dismissed forever, never to be employed again, and placed under strict supervision by local officials. In 1908 he died at home at seventy-five. In 1909 an edict restored his former rank. He was later given the posthumous title Wengong.
15
同龢久侍講幃，參機務，遇事專斷。 與左右時有爭執，群責怙權。 晚遭讒沮，幾獲不測，遂斥逐以終。 著有瓶廬詩稿八卷、文稿二十卷。 其書法自成一家，尤為世所宗雲。
Tonghe long served at the lecture seat and on the council, often deciding matters on his own. He quarreled often with colleagues, and many blamed him for grasping power. Late in life he suffered slander and nearly came to harm, and was finally expelled to end his days. He left eight fascicles of Poetic Drafts from the Bottle Studio and twenty fascicles of prose. His calligraphy formed a school of its own and was especially revered.
16
孫毓汶，字萊山，山東濟寧州人，尚書瑞珍子。 咸豐六年，以一甲二名進士授編修。 八年，丁父憂。 十年，以在籍辦團抗捐被劾，革職遣戍。 恭親王以毓汶世受國恩，首抗捐餉，深惡之。 同治元年，以輸餉復原官。 五年，大考一等一名，擢侍講學士。 先後典四川鄉試，督福建學政。 光緒元年，丁母憂。 服闋，起故官。 尋遷詹事，視學安徽。 擢內閣學士，授工部左侍郎。 十年，命赴江南等省按事。 時法越事起，毓汶以習於醇親王，漸與聞機要。 適奉硃諭盡罷軍機王大臣，毓汶還，遂命入直軍機，兼總理各國事務大臣。 時當國益厭言路紛囂，出張佩綸等會辦南北洋、閩海軍務，餘亦因事先後去之，風氣為之一變。 十五年，擢刑部尚書，尋調兵部，加太子少保。 歷典會試、順天鄉試，賞黃馬褂、雙眼花翎、紫韁。 二十年，中日媾和，李鴻章遣人齎約至。 廷臣章奏凡百上，皆斥和非計。 翁同龢、李鴻藻主緩，俄、法、德三國亦請毋遽換約。 毓汶素與鴻章相結納，力言戰不可恃，亟請署，上為流涕書之，和約遂成。 明年，稱疾乞休。 二十五年，卒，予諡文恪。
Sun Yuwen, style Laishan, was from Jining Prefecture in Shandong, son of Minister Sun Ruizhen. In 1856 he placed second in the jinshi examination and was appointed reviser. In 1858 he entered mourning for his father. In 1860, while at home organizing militia to resist tax levies, he was impeached, dismissed, and banished. Prince Gong, noting that Yuwen's family had long enjoyed imperial favor yet he led resistance to levies, deeply detested him. In 1862 he was restored to office for contributing funds. In 1866 he placed first in the triennial examination and was promoted to expositor. He supervised the Sichuan provincial examination and served as educational commissioner of Fujian. In 1875 he entered mourning for his mother. After mourning he resumed his former post. He was soon made Junior Mentor and conducted an educational inspection of Anhui. He was promoted to Grand Secretariat Bachelor and Left Vice Minister of Works. In 1884 he was ordered to investigate affairs in Jiangnan and other provinces. When the Franco-Vietnamese war began, Yuwen, being close to Prince Chun, gradually gained access to confidential affairs. A vermilion rescript then dismissed all Grand Councilors; when Yuwen returned he was ordered onto the council and into the Office for the Management of Foreign Affairs. Those in power had grown weary of memorialists' clamor; Zhang Peilun and others were sent to handle naval affairs, and the rest were removed on various pretexts—the tone of court debate shifted at once. In 1889 he was promoted to Minister of Justice, soon transferred to War, and made Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent. He served as metropolitan chief examiner and Shuntian examiner, and received a yellow riding jacket, double-eyed peacock feathers, and a purple bridle. In 1894 China and Japan made peace and Li Hongzhang sent an envoy with the treaty. Hundreds of memorials from court officials condemned peace as no strategy. Weng Tonghe and Li Hongzao favored delay; Russia, France, and Germany also asked that ratification wait. Yuwen was long allied with Li Hongzhang; he argued that war could not be trusted and urgently pressed for signature; the emperor wept as he approved, and the treaty was concluded. The next year he asked for sick leave. In 1899 he died and received the posthumous title Wenge.
17
毓汶權奇饒智略，直軍機逾十年。 初，醇親王以尊親參機密，不常入直，疏牘日送邸閱，謂之「過府」。 諭旨陳奏，皆毓汶為傳達。 同列或不得預聞，故其權特重雲。
Yuwen was resourceful and full of stratagems; he served on the Grand Council more than ten years. At first Prince Chun, as the emperor's father, took part in confidential affairs but seldom attended council; memorials went daily to his residence for review, called "passing through the mansion." Imperial instructions and memorials were all conveyed through Yuwen. Colleagues sometimes learned of affairs only afterward, so his power was especially great.
18
論曰：光緒初元，复逢訓政，勵精圖治，宰輔多賢，頗有振興之象。 首輔文祥既逝，沈桂芬等承其遺風，以忠懇結主知，遇事能持之以正，雖無老成，尚有典型。 及甲申法越、甲午日韓，外患內憂，國家多故。 慈聖倦勤，經營園囿，稍事遊幸，而政紀亦漸弛矣。 鴻藻久參樞密，眷遇獨隆。 桂芬以持重見賞，同龢以專斷致嫌。 毓汶奔走其間，勤勞亦著，大體彌縫，賴以無事。 然以政見異同，門戶之爭，牽及朝局，至數十年而未已。 賢者之責，亦不能免焉。
The commentator says: At the start of Guangxu, with regency resumed, the court strove to reform; chief ministers were largely capable, and revival seemed possible. After Wen Xiang died, Shen Guifen and others carried on his style, winning trust through loyal earnestness and holding to principle; though not elder statesmen, they still set a standard. By the wars of 1884 and 1894–95, foreign troubles and domestic anxieties multiplied. The Empress Dowager grew weary of daily rule, tended gardens and travel, and political discipline gradually slackened. Hongzao had long served on the Grand Council and enjoyed favor beyond his peers. Guifen was prized for steadiness; Tonghe drew dislike for deciding matters on his own. Yuwen shuttled between them, his industry evident; on broad matters he patched things together so affairs passed without crisis. Yet policy disagreements fed factional strife that touched the court itself, and the struggle ran on for decades. Even men of talent could not escape bearing part of the blame.
