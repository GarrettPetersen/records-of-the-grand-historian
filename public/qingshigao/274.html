楊雍建姚締虞硃弘祚子綱王騭宋犖陳詵
Yang Yongjian, Yao Dingu, Zhu Hongzuo, Zigang, Wang Zhi, Song Luo, and Chen Shen
3
楊雍建，字自西，浙江海寧人。 順治十二年進士，授廣東高耍知縣。 時方用兵，總督駐高耍。 師行徵民夫，吏慮其逃，縶之官廨。 當除夕，雍建命徙廊廡，撤餚饌畀之。 師中索榕樹枝製繩以燃砲，軍吏檄徵，語不遜，雍建笞之。 總督王國光以是稱雍建方剛，特疏薦。 蒞官甫一年，擢兵科給事中。
Yang Yongjian, styled Zixi, came from Haining in Zhejiang. He earned his jinshi degree in the twelfth year of Shunzhi and was appointed magistrate of Gaoyao in Guangdong. The province was at war, and the governor-general had his headquarters at Gaoyao. Whenever the army marched it drafted laborers, and the clerks, fearing desertion, kept them shackled in the yamen. On New Year's Eve he had them moved to the verandas and sent out the holiday feast for them to eat. The army demanded banyan branches to braid fuses for the guns; when a military clerk requisitioned them with insolent words, Yongjian had him beaten. Governor-General Wang Guoguang cited this as proof of Yongjian's firm integrity and memorialized the throne to recommend him specially. After barely a year in office he was promoted to supervising secretary in the Bureau of Military Affairs.
4
十六年春，世祖幸南苑，雍建疏言：「昨因聖體違和，傳諭孟春饗太廟，遣官致祭。 至期皇躬康豫，仍親廟祀，此敬修祀典之盛心也。 乃回宮未幾，复幸南苑，寒威未釋，陟歷郊原，恐不足以慎起居。 且古者蒐苗狝狩，各有其時。 設使獸起於前，馬逸於後，驚屬車之清塵，豈能無萬一之慮？ 」疏入，上甚怒，宣雍建入，諭以閱兵習武之意。 雍建奏對不失常度，上意亦解。
In the spring of the sixteenth year the Shizu Emperor visited the Southern Park, and Yongjian memorialized: "Yesterday, because Your Majesty was unwell, an edict announced that the spring sacrifice at the Grand Temple would be performed by dispatched officials." When the day came Your Majesty had recovered and still went in person to the temple—proof of how earnestly you honor the ritual calendar. Yet hardly back at the palace you have returned to the Southern Park; the cold has not yet broken, and to roam the open countryside may not be prudent for Your Majesty's health. Besides, in antiquity the spring muster, summer hunt, autumn battue, and winter chase each had its proper season. If a beast should spring up ahead or a horse bolt behind and startle the imperial train, can there be no fear of the slightest accident? " When the memorial arrived the emperor was furious and had Yongjian brought in to explain that the visit was for troop review and martial training. Yongjian answered without losing his composure, and the emperor's anger subsided.
5
時平南王尚可喜、靖南王耿繼茂並鎮廣東，雍建疏陳廣東害民之政八：委吏太濫，雜派太繁，裡役無定例，用夫無定制，鹽埠日橫，私稅日盈，伐薪採木，大肆流毒，均宜亟為革除。 且兩籓並建，供億維繁。 今川、貴底定，請移一籓鎮撫其地，俾粵民甦息。 上尋命繼茂移鎮福建，雍建發之也。 十七年，疏言：「朋黨之患，釀於草野。 欲塞其源，宜嚴禁盟社，請飭學臣查禁。 」從之。 轉吏科給事中。 聖祖即位，輔臣秉政，奏事者入見，皆長跪，雍建獨立語。 比退，輔臣目之曰：「此南苑上書諫獵者也。 」自是奏事者見輔臣皆不跪。
At the time the Prince of Pingnan, Shang Kexi, and the Prince of Jingnan, Geng Jimao, both held Guangdong; Yongjian listed eight abuses there: too many delegated clerks, too many miscellaneous levies, village corvée without fixed rules, labor drafts without fixed quotas, salt monopolies growing more oppressive by the day, private taxes mounting daily, and timber-cutting spreading misery everywhere—all of which should be abolished at once. With both princely establishments in place, provisioning the court had become crushing. Now that Sichuan and Guizhou were pacified, he asked that one princedom be transferred to garrison there so the people of Guangdong could breathe again. The emperor soon ordered Geng Jimao to transfer his garrison to Fujian—Yongjian had set this in motion. In the seventeenth year he memorialized: "The scourge of faction begins in the countryside." To cut it off at the source, alliance societies should be strictly banned, and he asked that the educational commissioners be ordered to investigate and suppress them. " The court approved his request. He was transferred to supervising secretary in the Bureau of Personnel. When the Kangxi Emperor ascended the throne, the regents held power; everyone who came to report affairs knelt, but Yongjian alone stood and spoke. As he left, a regent eyed him and said, "That is the man who memorialized against the hunt at the Southern Park." " From then on officials reporting to the regents no longer knelt.
6
康熙三年，彗星見。 雍建奏言：「天心仁愛，垂象示警。 乞齋心修省，廣求直言，詳詢利病，並飭內外臣工，滌慮洗心，共修職業，」上優旨褒答。 四年，疏言：「治化未醇，由於臣職未盡。 比者部臣以推諉為卸責，明為本部應議之事，或請諮別部，或請飭督撫，致一案之處分，因一人之口供未到而更待另議； 一事之行止，因一時文卷小誤而重俟行查； 至地方利弊所關，憚於釐正，輒云已經題定，無庸再議。 如此，則一二胥吏執定例以駁之足矣，不知滿、漢堂司各官所司為何事也。 督撫以蒙蔽為苟安，民苦於差徭，而額外之私徵，未聞建長策以除積困； 吏橫於貪暴，而有司之掊克，不過摘薄罪以引輕條。 向日行考滿之法，則題報者皆稱職，曾無三等以下之劣員； 平時上彈劾之章，則特糾者僅末僚，不及道府以上之大吏。 凡此推諉蒙蔽之習，請嚴飭內外臣工各圖報稱，儻仍蹈故轍，立予罷斥，以儆官常。 」疏入，報聞。 尋自刑科都給事中累擢左副都御史。
In the third year of Kangxi a comet appeared. Yongjian memorialized: "Heaven is benevolent; it sends omens as a warning." He asked the emperor to purify his mind and examine himself, to seek blunt counsel widely, to inquire into what helps and harms the realm, and to order officials everywhere to clear their minds and devote themselves to duty." The emperor answered with a gracious edict of praise. In the fourth year he memorialized: "Good government has not yet matured because officials are not doing their jobs." Lately ministry officials treat buck-passing as discharge of duty: matters plainly within their own jurisdiction they refer to other ministries or to the governors, so that a case cannot be decided because one witness has not yet testified; whether a matter proceeds or stalls because of a minor clerical error, they order yet another review; and when local abuses are at stake they shrink from reform, saying the matter was already settled and need not be reopened. At this rate a couple of clerks citing precedent could run the ministries, and one wonders what the Manchu and Han directors are for. Governors treat concealment as peace; the people groan under corvée and levies while extra private exactions go unchecked, and no one proposes a lasting remedy for these chronic ills; subordinates run wild in greed and violence, yet when superiors are caught extorting they are charged only with minor offenses under the lightest statutes. Under the triennial evaluation every reported official was rated competent, and never once was anyone graded below the third rank; and when impeachments were filed they targeted only junior clerks, never circuit intendants, prefects, or higher officials. For all these habits of buck-passing and cover-up, he asked that officials everywhere be strictly ordered to earn their keep; anyone who persists in the old ways should be dismissed at once as a warning to the service. " The memorial was received and noted. Soon he rose from chief supervising secretary in the Bureau of Punishments to left vice censor-in-chief.
7
十八年，典會試，授貴州巡撫。 疏請立營製，減徭役，招集流亡，禁革私派。 土司謁巡撫，故事，必鳴鼓角，交戟於門，俾拜其下。 雍建悉屏去，引至座前問疾苦，予以酒食，土司咸輸服。 始，貴陽斗米值錢五千，雍建請轉餉以給。 既，令民翦荒茅，教以耕種。 比三年，稻田日闢，民食以裕。 二十三年，召授兵部侍郎。 尋以親老乞終養，許之。 四十三年，卒，賜祭葬。 子中訥，進士，官右中允。
In the eighteenth year he presided over the metropolitan examination and was appointed governor of Guizhou. He asked to establish garrison regulations, reduce corvée, recall refugees, and abolish unauthorized levies. When native chieftains came to call on the governor, custom required drums and horns and crossed halberds at the gate, forcing them to prostrate themselves outside. Yongjian abolished all of this, brought them before his desk to ask after their hardships, and gave them food and wine; the chieftains were won over completely. At first a dou of rice in Guiyang cost five thousand cash; Yongjian requested grain shipments from elsewhere to feed the people. Then he had the people clear the wild scrub and taught them how to farm. Within three years paddy fields spread steadily and the people had enough to eat. In the twenty-third year he was recalled to the capital and appointed vice minister of War. Soon afterward, citing aged parents, he asked to retire and care for them at home, and the request was granted. In the forty-third year he died, and the court granted him a state funeral. His son Zhongne, a jinshi, served as right attendant in the Hanlin Academy.
8
姚締虞，字歷升，湖廣黃陂人。 順治十五年進士，授四川成都府推官。 四川殘民多聚為盜，互告訐，釀大獄。 締虞平恕讞鞫，輒得其情，審釋叛案株連獄囚十七人。 總督苗澄、巡撫張德地薦廉能，舉卓異，會裁缺，改陝西安化知縣。 行取，康熙十五年，授禮科給事中。 疏請嚴選庶吉士，考覈翰林，報聞。 十七年，典試江西，還，奏：「江西被賊殘破州縣在丁缺田荒案內者，請敕督撫酌量輕重，限三年或五年勸墾，以漸昇科。 全省逋賦二百二十萬，歷年追比，僅報完三萬。 此二百十餘萬，雖敲骨吸髓，勢必不能復完。 請早予蠲免，俾小民得免死亡。 」
Yao Dingu, styled Lisheng, came from Huangpi in Huguang. He earned his jinshi degree in the fifteenth year of Shunzhi and was appointed investigating censor of Chengdu Prefecture in Sichuan. In Sichuan many survivors of the wars had turned bandit; mutual accusations bred major prosecutions. Dingu judged cases with calm fairness and always got to the truth; he reviewed a treason case and released seventeen prisoners caught in its dragnet. Governor-General Miao Cheng and Governor Zhang Dedi recommended him as incorrupt and capable and rated him outstanding; when posts were cut he was reassigned as magistrate of Anhua in Shaanxi. Selected for service in the capital, in the fifteenth year of Kangxi he was appointed supervising secretary in the Bureau of Rites. He asked for stricter selection of Hanlin bachelors and tighter review of the Academy; the memorial was noted. In the seventeenth year, after presiding over the Jiangxi provincial examination, he reported: "For counties in Jiangxi ruined by rebels and listed under missing registers and abandoned fields, order the governors to set a three- or five-year limit for bringing land back under cultivation before taxes resume." The province owed 2.2 million taels in back taxes; after years of relentless collection only 30,000 had been reported paid. That remaining 2.1 million could never be collected even if the people were squeezed dry. He asked that it be remitted soon so the common people would not die under the burden. "
9
十八年，地震，求言。 締虞上疏曰：「科道乃朝廷耳目之官，原期知無不言，有聞則告。 自故憲臣艾元徵請禁風聞條奏，自此言路氣靡，中外多所顧忌。 臣請皇上省覽世祖朝諸臣奏議，如何謇諤； 今者相率以條陳為事，輭熟成風。 蓋平時無以作其敢言之氣，一旦欲其慷慨直陳，難矣。 乞敕廷臣會議，嗣後有矢志忠誠、指斥奸佞者，即少差謬，亦賜矜全。 如或快意恩仇，受人指使，章奏鈔傳，眾目難掩，縱令彈劾得實，亦難免於徇私之罪。 如此，則言官有所顧忌，不敢妄言； 中外諸臣有所顧忌，不敢妄為。 」疏下九卿科道會議。 越日，召廷臣等集中左門，上問：「締虞疏如何定議？ 」吏部尚書郝惟訥等暨給事中李宗孔等俱言風聞之例，不宜復開。 上問：「締虞，爾意如何？ 」締虞對曰：「皇上明聖，從未譴罪言官。 但有處分條例在，言官皆生畏懼。 」上曰：「如汝言，條例便當廢耶？ 」締虞對曰：「科條雖設，當辨公私誠偽。 」上意稍解。 諭言：「官宜敷陳國家大事，如有大奸大貪，糾劾得實，法在必行，決不姑貸。 且魏象樞彈奏程汝璞，亦是風聞，已鞫問得實，原未嘗有風聞之禁也。 」上宣締虞前，指內閣所呈世祖時章奏示之曰：「汝以朕為未閱此乎？ 」締虞對曰：「惟久經聖覽，臣故不憚盡言。 」上命以所言宣付史館。 次日，復命締虞入起居注，授筆札記之。 尋轉工科掌印給事中。 上考察科道，黜孫緒極、傅廷俊、和鹽鼎三人，而嘉締虞與王曰溫、李迥稱職。 二十一年，疏論外吏積習，視事偷惰，公務沉閣，文移遲緩； 僚屬宴會，遊客酬酢，廢時糜費。 請敕部禁飭。 累擢左僉都御史。
In the eighteenth year an earthquake struck, and the court called for candid advice. Dingu memorialized: "The censorate and supervising secretaries are the court's eyes and ears; they are meant to speak of whatever they know and report whatever they hear." Ever since the late censor Ai Yuanzheng asked to ban memorials based on hearsay, the avenue of remonstrance has withered and officials everywhere have grown cautious. I ask Your Majesty to read the memorials of the Shunzhi reign and see how blunt officials once were; whereas now everyone files routine bulletins and a culture of smooth complacency has taken hold. For if nothing in ordinary times encourages them to speak boldly, you cannot expect frank counsel on demand. He asked the court to deliberate: hereafter officials who speak in loyal earnest against corruption, even if slightly mistaken, should be treated with forbearance. But if they settle private scores, take orders from others, and circulate their memorials so that everyone sees, then even a substantiated impeachment will still be punished as self-serving. That way remonstrating officials would have reason to be careful and not speak rashly; and officials everywhere would think twice before acting rashly. " The memorial was referred to the Nine Ministers and the censorate for joint deliberation. The next day the emperor summoned the ministers to the Left Gate and asked, "How do you rule on Dingu's memorial?" " Minister of Personnel Hao Weine, supervising secretary Li Zongkong, and others all said the ban on hearsay memorials should not be lifted. The emperor asked, "Dingu, what do you think?" " Dingu answered, "Your Majesty is enlightened and has never punished a remonstrating official." But the disciplinary regulations still exist, and that makes remonstrating officials afraid. " The emperor said, "So by your logic the regulations should be abolished?" " Dingu answered, "The statutes may remain, but each case should be judged on whether the speaker acted from public duty or private motive." " The emperor's anger eased somewhat. He instructed them: "Officials should speak frankly on affairs of state; when great traitors or great corruption are exposed and the charges are proved, the law will be enforced without mercy." When Wei Xiangshu impeached Cheng Rupu it was also on hearsay, yet investigation proved the charge— there never was a true ban on hearsay memorials. " He had Dingu brought forward, pointed to Shunzhi-era memorials from the Grand Secretariat, and said, "Do you think I have not read these?" " Dingu answered, "Because Your Majesty has long read them, I did not hesitate to speak plainly." " The emperor ordered his words recorded and sent to the Historiography Office. The next day Dingu was again summoned to the Diary of Activity and Repose and given brush and paper to write the record. Soon he was transferred to chief supervising secretary in the Bureau of Works. When the emperor reviewed the censorate he dismissed Sun Juji, Fu Tingjun, and He Yanding, but praised Dingu along with Wang Yuewen and Li Jiong as competent officials. In the twenty-first year he memorialized on the bad habits of provincial officials: neglecting their duties, letting business pile up, and delaying documents; holding banquets for staff and visitors, wasting time and money on entertainment. He asked the ministries to forbid these practices. He was promoted to left assistant censor-in-chief.
10
二十四年，授四川巡撫。 締虞先為推官有聲，百姓喜其來。 締虞至，榜上諭於廳事，嚴約束，禁私徵雜派，杜絕餽遺，屬吏憚之。 疏言：「四川迭經兵火，荒殘已極。 官戶鄉紳，多流寓外省，雖令子弟復業，迨入學鄉舉登仕版後，仍棄本籍他往。 百姓見其如此，亦裹足不歸。 若招回鄉宦一家，可抵百姓數戶。 紳宦既歸，百姓亦不招而自至。 今察明各屬流寓外省紳衿，請敕部移行，飭令復業。 」從之。 蜀人困於採木，締虞陛辭，首陳其害。 會鬆威道王騭入覲，亦舉是以奏，詔特免之。 復請免運白蠟，停解鐵稅，皆獲施行。 二十七年，卒官，賜祭葬。
In the twenty-fourth year he was appointed governor of Sichuan. Dingu had already won fame as investigating censor there, and the people rejoiced at his return. When Dingu arrived he posted strict rules in the yamen hall, banned unauthorized levies and gifts, and his subordinates were afraid of him. He memorialized: "Sichuan has been ravaged by war after war until the land lies in utter ruin. Official households and local gentry had mostly fled to other provinces; even when they sent sons back to reclaim the family estate, once those sons entered school, passed the provincial exams, and were posted to office they again abandoned their native register and left. Seeing this, commoners too held back and would not return home. Recalling one gentry family to the countryside was worth as much as summoning back several dozen commoner households. Once the gentry returned, commoners arrived on their own without needing to be called. Having identified the gentry-scholars from each district who were living abroad, he asked that orders be sent through the ministries commanding them to resume their occupations at home. His proposal was approved. The people of Shu were exhausted by timber-cutting labor; when Dingu took leave of the throne at court he was the first to describe the harm. About the same time Wang Zhi of Songwei Circuit came to audience at court and memorialized on the same grounds; the emperor commanded a special exemption. He also asked to end the transport of white wax and the delivery of iron taxes, and all these measures were carried out. In the twenty-seventh year he died in office and was granted funeral sacrifices.
11
硃弘祚，字徽廕，山東高唐人，昌祚弟。 弘祚自舉人授江南盱眙知縣，有惠政，舉卓異。 康熙十四年，行取御史，以昌祚子紱官大理寺卿迴避，改刑部主事。 再遷兵部督捕郎中，出為直隸天津道僉事，調直隸守道參議。
Zhu Hongzuo, styled Huiyin, came from Gaotang in Shandong and was the younger brother of Changzuo. After receiving his juren degree Hongzuo was appointed magistrate of Xuyi in Jiangnan; his humane governance won him recommendation as outstanding. In the fourteenth year of Kangxi he was tapped for promotion to investigating censor, but had to decline because Changzuo's son Fu was vice minister of the Dali Court; he was reassigned as a clerk in the Ministry of Punishments. He was promoted to supervising-arrests director in the Ministry of War, then sent out as assistant commissioner on the Tianjin Circuit in Zhili and later transferred to vice commissioner on the Defender Circuit of Zhili.
12
二十六年，超擢廣東巡撫。 入見，奏對稱旨，賜帑金千，及內廝鞍馬。 過庾嶺，察知夫役苦累，首禁革之。 复牒兵部，凡使者過境，有驛站供億，不得更有所役。 廣東軍興後，無藝之徵，浮於正供，悉罷免。 劾墨吏尤者數人，餘悉奉法。 鹽法為籓下奸民所亂，據引地莫敢譙訶。 弘祚疏陳整飭鹽政數事，如議行。
In the twenty-sixth year he was promoted ahead of schedule to governor of Guangdong. At his audience his answers pleased the throne; he received a thousand taels from the imperial treasury and saddled horses from the inner palace. Crossing the Dayu Pass he saw how corvée porters suffered and was the first to ban the practice. He also notified the Ministry of War that traveling envoys must rely on postal stations for provisions and must not levy additional labor. After the wars in Guangdong, unauthorized levies had piled above the regular taxes; he abolished them all. He impeached the worst corrupt clerks and the rest fell into line with the law. Rascals attached to the princely fief had wrecked the salt trade, monopolizing licensed districts so that no one dared complain. Hongzuo memorialized reforms to restore the salt administration, and they were carried out as he proposed.
13
高州屬縣吳川，瓊州屬縣臨高、澄邁，戶少田蕪，積逋十二萬兩有奇，疏請豁免。 衛所屯田歲輸糧三斗，額重多逃亡。 弘祚言：「民糧重，則每畝八升八合起科，今屯田浮三之二，非卹兵之道，當比例裁減。 」事皆允行。 逆亂方定，奸民告訐無已，疏請嚴妄首株連之例，略謂：「當定南分鎮，聞風投冒倚藉聲勢者，實繁有徒，迨經平定，籓下人應歸旗者，悉已簿錄解京； 籍內無名者，釋放為民。 嗣有旨：'籓下官兵、奴僕及貿易人等，除實係遼東舊人及價買人外，逐一清查，發出為民。 '臣尋繹詔意，原以諸人皆朝廷赤子，不忍株累。 且十餘年來，或補伍，或歸農，或死亡遷徙，無籍可稽。 乃姦宄之徒，蔓引株連，或在部呈首，或向有司告訐； 及事白省釋，而官民之被累已深。 請敕部嚴議。 」從之。
Wuchuan under Gaozhou and Lin'gao and Chengmai under Qiongzhou had few households and abandoned fields, with more than 120,000 taels in arrears; he memorialized for their remission. Military colony lands paid three dou of grain per year—a burden so heavy that many peasants fled. Hongzuo said: "Regular farmland is taxed at eight sheng eight he per mou; military colony lands are taxed two-thirds higher—not a humane policy for the soldiers and should be reduced in proportion. All were approved. With the rebellion freshly suppressed, informers kept filing endless accusations; he asked for strict penalties on false accusers and collective punishment, writing in part: "When the southern pacification commissioner was first established, countless people rushed to claim ties to the rebellion and borrow its prestige; after order was restored those under the princely banner who should return to the Eight Banners had all been registered and sent to Beijing; Anyone not listed in the registers was released as a commoner. Later an edict declared: "Troops, servants, and traders under the princely establishment—except true Liaodong natives or legally purchased bondservants—must be examined one by one and released to civilian status. I followed the intent of that edict: these people were all subjects of the Son of Heaven, and the court did not wish to punish them collectively. Over more than a decade many had joined garrison units, returned to farming, died, or moved away, leaving no records to verify. Yet scoundrels kept expanding accusations and implicating others, filing charges at the ministries or before local officials; By the time cases were cleared and prisoners released, officials and commoners had already suffered severe harm. He asked that the ministries be ordered to enact strict rules. The court approved.

三十一年，擢福建浙江總督。 值大計，弘祚疏言「福建地瘠民佻」，上責弘祚失言，謂：「賢才不擇地而生。 四川巡撫張德地署延綏巡撫，言'延綏邊地，無可舉博學鴻詞者'； 少詹事邵遠平奏'南方人輕浮不可用'。 朕心甚不愜，因皆罷斥。 今弘祚又以謬言陳奏，下部議降調。 」三十九年，命修高家堰河工，病卒。
In the thirty-first year he was made governor-general of Fujian and Zhejiang. During the great evaluation Hongzuo memorialized that "Fujian's soil is poor and its people frivolous." The emperor rebuked him for improper speech, saying: "Talent is not born only in fertile lands. Zhang Dedi of Sichuan, acting as governor of Yansui, had said that 'on the Yansui frontier there was no one qualified to recommend for the Special Literary Examination'; Junior Mentor Shao Yuanping had memorialized that 'southerners are frivolous and unfit for office.' I was deeply displeased and dismissed them all. Now Hongzuo has again memorialized with such reckless words; refer the matter to the ministries for demotion. In the thirty-ninth year he was assigned to repair the Gaojia Embankment hydraulic works and died of illness.

子絳，官至廣東布政使； 綱，初授兵部主事，累官湖南布政使，雍正間，擢雲南巡撫，疏劾署巡撫楊名時徇隱廢弛，籓庫借支未清款項至十九萬有奇，名時坐是得罪。 尋調撫福建，卒，諡勤恪。
His son Jiang rose to be provincial administration commissioner of Guangdong; Zigang began as a clerk in the Ministry of War and rose to provincial administration commissioner of Hunan; in the Yongzheng era he was made governor of Yunnan and memorialized against Acting Governor Yang Mingshi for covering up misconduct and neglect—unpaid advances from the provincial treasury totaled more than 190,000 taels—and Yang Mingshi was punished accordingly. 王騭，字辰岳，山東福山人。 順治十二年進士，授戶部主事。 康熙五年，典試廣東。 歷刑部郎中。 十九年，出為四川松威道。 時徵雲南，騭督運軍糧，覆舟墜馬，屢經險阻，師賴以濟。 二十四年，壘溪大定堡山後生番出掠，巡撫韓世琦檄兵追剿，令騭駐茂州，與總兵高鼎議剿撫。 騭赴堡開諭，番族據巴豬寨，陽就撫，負嵎如故。 騭招撫附近諸​​寨，遣兵自廟山進，圍寨，斬獲無算。 追至黑水江，賊渠挖子被焚死，山後番眾悉降。 調直隸口北道，未行。
Wang Zhi, styled Chenyue, came from Fushan in Shandong. He earned his jinshi in the twelfth year of Shunzhi and was appointed a clerk in the Ministry of Revenue. In the fifth year of Kangxi he served as chief examiner for Guangdong. He served as a bureau director in the Ministry of Punishments. In the nineteenth year he was sent out as intendant of the Songwei Circuit in Sichuan. During the Yunnan campaign he supervised military grain transport, surviving capsized boats and falls from horses through repeated perils, and the army depended on him to get supplies through. In the twenty-fourth year raw tribesmen beyond Dading Fortress at Leixi raided the border; Governor Han Shiqi sent troops in pursuit and stationed Zhi at Maozhou to plan pacification with Regional Commander Gao Ding. Zhi went to the fort to offer terms; the tribes held Bazhu Stockade, pretending to accept pacification while remaining defiant. He won over neighboring stockades, sent troops in from Miaoshan, besieged the stronghold, and killed or captured countless enemies. The pursuit reached the Blackwater River; the rebel leader Wazi was burned to death and all the tribes beyond the mountains surrendered. He was transferred to the Koubei Circuit in Zhili but had not yet left.
17
時以太和殿工，命採蜀中柟木。 騭入覲，疏言：「四川大半環山巉岩，惟成都稍平衍。 巨材所生，必於深林窮壑，人跡罕到，斧斤難施，所以久存。 民夫入山採木，足胝履穿，攀藤側立，施工既難； 而運路自山抵江，或百餘裡，或七八十里，深澗急灘，溪流紆折，經時歷月，始至其地。 木在溪間，必待暴水而出，故陸運必於春冬，水運必於夏秋，非可一徑而行，計日而至，其艱如此。 且四川禍變相踵，荒煙百里。 臣當年運糧行間，滿目瘡痍。 自蕩平以後，休養生息。 然計通省戶口，仍不過一萬八千餘丁，不及他省一縣之眾。 就中抽撥五千入山採木，衣糧器具，盈千累百，遣發民夫，遠至千里，近亦數百里，耕作全廢，國賦何徵？ 請敕下撫臣，親詣採柟處察勘，量材取用，其必不能採運者，奏請上裁。 」疏入，上諭曰：「四川屢經兵火，困苦已極，採木累民。 塞外松木，取充殿材，足支數百年，何必柟木？ 令免採運。 」未幾，吏部循例疏請司道內擢京堂，騭未與，特命內升。 尋授光祿寺少卿，累遷太常寺卿。
Work on the Hall of Supreme Harmony required nanmu timber from Sichuan. Zhi came to court and memorialized: "Most of Sichuan is ringed by steep mountains; only Chengdu lies on relatively level ground. The largest timber grows in deep forest and remote ravines where few people go and axes can scarcely reach—hence it survives. Laborers who enter the mountains return with callused feet and torn shoes, clinging to vines on sheer cliffs—the cutting alone is grueling; Yet the haul from mountain to river may run over a hundred li or seventy or eighty li through deep gorges and rapids along winding streams, taking months to reach open water. Logs stranded in streams must wait for floodwaters; land transport is possible only in spring and winter, water transport only in summer and autumn—not a single route that can be finished on a schedule. Such is the hardship. Moreover Sichuan has suffered one catastrophe after another until mile upon mile lies deserted. When I transported grain through the province years ago, every road showed the scars of war. Since pacification the people have begun to recover. Yet the whole province counts barely eighteen thousand registered males—fewer than a single county elsewhere. Draft five thousand of them for timber work, with thousands of sets of clothing, grain, and tools, and corvée sent as far as a thousand li or as near as several hundred li—all farming stops. How will land tax be collected? I ask that the provincial governor be ordered to inspect the cutting sites in person, take only what can be used, and memorialize for the emperor's decision on timber that cannot possibly be harvested and transported. When the memorial arrived the emperor said: "Sichuan has been repeatedly ravaged by war and its people are in extreme misery; timber-cutting only adds to their burden. Pine from beyond the passes can serve for the palace for centuries—why insist on nanmu? Exempt Sichuan from cutting and transport. Soon thereafter the Ministry of Personnel routinely proposed promoting circuit intendants to capital posts; Zhi was omitted, but the emperor specially ordered his promotion. He was soon appointed vice minister of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and eventually rose to minister of the Court of Imperial Music.

二十六年，授江西巡撫。 陛辭，上諭曰：「大吏以操守為耍，大法則小廉，百姓蒙福。 」騭對曰：「臣向在四川，不取民間粒米束草，日費取給於家。 」上曰：「身為大臣，日費必取給於家，勢有所不能。 但操守廉潔，念念愛民，便為良吏，且亦須安靜。 貪污屬吏，先當訓誡； 不悛，則糾劾。 」瀕行，賜帑金千。 二十七年，擢閩浙總督。 疏言：「江西自蕩平後，積年蠲免銀米二百萬有奇，民生漸裕。 然徵收之弊，尚為民累，錢糧明加火​​耗，暗加重戥，部院司道府皆有解費。 臣入境之初，火耗已減，解費尚存，即揭示剔除積弊，盡革官役上下大小雜費。 南昌、新建二縣漕糧尚仍民兌，俱行革除，漕運積年陋規，搜剔無遺。 但在民則省費，在官則失利。 恐臣去後，空言無用，乞天語嚴禁，不致前弊復生。 」下所司知之。
In the twenty-sixth year he was appointed governor of Jiangxi. At his farewell audience the emperor said: "Great officials must hold integrity paramount; uphold the greater law and petty graft will cease, and the people will prosper." Zhi replied: "When I was in Sichuan I took not a grain of rice or blade of grass from the people; my daily expenses came from my own household." The emperor said: "As a high minister you cannot entirely live off your own purse—that is scarcely possible. But if you keep your integrity, keep the people always in mind, and govern quietly, you will be a fine official. For corrupt subordinates, admonish them first; If they do not reform, then impeach them." As he was about to depart the emperor bestowed a thousand taels from the imperial treasury. In the twenty-seventh year he was made governor-general of Fujian and Zhejiang. He memorialized: "Since pacification Jiangxi has received tax remissions totaling more than two million taels in silver and grain, and livelihoods have gradually improved. Yet collection abuses still burden the people: open surcharges for melting fees and secret overweighting on the scales, with every level from ministry to prefecture charging 'delivery fees.' When I first arrived I had already cut melting surcharges, but delivery fees remained; I posted notices abolishing accumulated abuses and all miscellaneous fees at every level of officialdom. Nanchang and Xinjian still forced commoners to deliver grain transport in person—I abolished that practice and rooted out every longstanding abuse in the transport system. The people saved money, but officials lost their illicit gains. I fear that after I leave these reforms will vanish into empty talk; I beg Your Majesty to issue strict prohibitions so the old abuses do not return. The court forwarded the memorial to the responsible agencies for implementation.

時湖廣叛卒夏逢龍據武昌，陷黃州。 騭次邵武，聞警，恐蔓及江西，奏撥福建兵協剿。 自海禁既弛，奸民雜入商販，出洋劫掠。 騭既上官，即檄溫州總兵蔣懋勳、黃岩總兵林本植、定海總兵董大本以舟師出洋搜捕。 懋勳、本植得賊舟七、大本於白沙灣獲巨艦一，斬盜渠楊仕玉等十六輩，釋被擄難民百十一人。 二十八年，上幸浙江，賜騭御用冠服。 諭曰：「爾任總督，實心任事，浙、閩黎庶稱爾清廉，故特加優賚。 」未幾，召拜戶部尚書，以老病累疏乞休，詔輒慰留。
At that time the rebel soldier Xia Fenglong in Huguang held Wuchang and had taken Huangzhou. Zhi was passing through Shaowu when he heard the alarm; fearing the rebellion might spread into Jiangxi, he memorialized to dispatch Fujian troops for joint suppression. After the maritime ban was lifted, rogues mixed with merchants and went to sea as pirates. As soon as he took office Zhi ordered Wenzhou Regional Commander Jiang Maoxun, Huangyan Regional Commander Lin Benzhi, and Dinghai Regional Commander Dong Daben to send fleets to search the seas. Maoxun and Benzhi seized seven pirate vessels; Daben captured a large ship at Baisha Bay, beheaded the pirate chief Yang Shiyu and fifteen others, and freed 111 kidnapped civilians. In the twenty-eighth year the emperor toured Zhejiang and granted Zhi imperial court robes. He said: "As governor-general you have served conscientiously; the people of Zhejiang and Fujian praise your integrity, and for this I grant you special favor." Soon he was summoned as minister of the Ministry of Revenue; age and illness led him to memorialize repeatedly for retirement, but each time the emperor urged him to stay.

In the thirty-third year the emperor summoned Grand Secretaries, the Nine Ministers, and River Controller Yu Chenglong; he accused Chenglong of framing Jin Fu and implicated Zhi, Censor-in-Chief Dong Ne, and Academician Li Yingjian for siding with Chenglong. Zhi and the others submitted confessions; Ne and Yingjian were dismissed, while Zhi was allowed to retire at his original rank. In the thirty-fourth year he died at home and was granted funeral sacrifices.

宋犖，字牧仲，河南商丘人，權子。 順治四年，犖年十四，應詔以大臣子列侍衛。 逾歲，試授通判。 康熙三年，授湖廣黃州通判。 以母憂去。 十六年，授理籓院院判，遷刑部員外郎，榷贛關，還遷郎中。 二十二年，授直隸通永道。 二十六年，遷山東按察使。 再遷江蘇布政使，察司庫虧三十六萬有奇，犖揭報督撫，責前布政使劉鼎、章欽文分償。 戶部採銅鑄錢，定值斤六分五釐，犖以江蘇不產銅，採自他省，值昂過半，牒巡撫田雯，疏請停採。 下部議，改視各關例，斤一錢。
Song Luo, styled Muzhong, came from Shangqiu in Henan and was the son of Song Quan. In the fourth year of Shunzhi, at age fourteen, he answered an imperial summons and was enrolled among the guards as a minister's son. A year later he passed an examination and was appointed deputy prefect. In the third year of Kangxi he was made deputy prefect of Huangzhou in Huguang. He resigned to observe mourning for his mother. In the sixteenth year he became vice-director of the Court of Colonial Affairs, then transferred to the Ministry of Punishments as vice director, took charge of the Gan River customs, and on returning was promoted to director. In the twenty-second year he was made intendant of the Tongyong Circuit in Zhili. In the twenty-sixth year he was transferred to provincial surveillance commissioner of Shandong. He was next made provincial treasurer of Jiangsu and found the treasury short by more than 360,000 taels. Luo reported this to his superiors and held former treasurers Liu Ding and Zhang Qinwen liable to make restitution. The Board of Revenue was buying copper for coinage at six fen five li per jin. Luo pointed out that Jiangsu produced no copper and had to import it at more than half again the official price; he wrote Governor Tian Wen and memorialized the throne to halt procurement. The ministries deliberated and adopted the rate used at the customs posts—one qian per jin.

二十七年，擢江西巡撫。 湖廣叛卒夏逢龍為亂，徵江西兵赴剿，次九江，挾餉缺幾譁變。 犖行次彭澤，聞報，檄發湖口庫帑充行糧，兵乃進。 至南昌受事，舊裁督標兵李美玉、袁大相糾三千餘人，謀劫倉庫，應逢龍以叛。 犖诇知之，捕得美玉、大相，眾恟恟。 犖令即斬以徇，諭眾受煽惑者皆貸不問，眾乃定。
In the twenty-seventh year he was promoted to governor of Jiangxi. When the Huguang mutineer Xia Fenglong rebelled, Jiangxi troops were called up for the campaign. They halted at Jiujiang, and with pay running short they nearly erupted in mutiny. Luo was passing through Pengze when he heard the news; he ordered funds from the Hukou treasury released as marching rations, and the troops moved on. When he reached Nanchang to take office, dismissed governor's guards Li Meiyu and Yuan Daxiang rallied more than three thousand men to raid the warehouses and join Xia Fenglong's rebellion. Luo uncovered the plot through informants, arrested Meiyu and Daxiang, and the mob grew restive. Luo had them executed on the spot as an example, announced amnesty for anyone merely led astray, and the disturbance subsided.

江西採竹木，饒州供紫竹，南康、九江供檀、柟諸木，通省派供貓竹，名雖官捐，實為民累，犖疏請動支正帑採買。 上命歲終巡撫視察布政司庫，犖疏請糧驛道庫，布政使察覈； 府庫，道員察覈。 漢軍文武官吏受代，家屬例當還旗，經過州縣，點驗取結。 犖曰：「是以罪人待之也。 」疏請自贓私斥革並侵挪帑項解部比追外，止給到京定限咨文，俾示區別。 皆下部議行。
Jiangxi levied bamboo and timber: Raozhou furnished purple bamboo, Nankang and Jiujiang sandalwood and nanmu, and the whole province mao bamboo. Though called an official levy, the cost fell on the people; Luo memorialized that purchases be made from the regular treasury instead. The emperor ordered governors to inspect provincial treasuries at year's end; Luo proposed that grain-transport and courier circuit treasuries be audited by the provincial treasurer; and prefectural treasuries by circuit intendants. When Han Banner officials completed their terms, their families were required to return to the banners; at every prefecture and district along the way they were inspected and made to obtain clearance certificates. Luo said, "This treats them like criminals. " He memorialized that except for officials dismissed for corruption or whose embezzled funds were being recovered by the ministry, returning families should receive only a travel pass with a deadline for reaching the capital, to mark the distinction. All were referred to the ministries, approved, and put into effect.

三十一年，調江蘇巡撫。 蘇州濱海各縣遇颶，上元、六合諸縣發山水，淮、揚、徐屬縣河溢，疏請視被災輕重，蠲減如例。 發江寧、鳳陽倉儲米麥散賑。 別疏請除太湖傍坍地賦額，戶部以地逾千畝，令詳察。 犖再疏上陳，上特允之。
In the thirty-first year he was transferred to governor of Jiangsu. Typhoons struck the coastal counties of Suzhou; mountain floods hit Shangyuan, Liuhe, and neighboring counties; rivers overflowed throughout the Huai, Yang, and Xu circuits. He memorialized for tax remissions scaled to the severity of each disaster, as precedent allowed. He opened the Jiangning and Fengyang granaries and distributed rice and wheat as relief. In a separate memorial he asked to cancel tax assessments on land washed away beside Lake Tai. The Board of Revenue, noting the area exceeded a thousand mu, ordered a detailed investigation. Luo memorialized again, and the emperor granted his request as a special favor.

犖在江蘇，三遇上南巡，嘉犖居官安靜，迭蒙賞賚，以犖年逾七十，書「福」、「壽」字以賜。 四十四年，擢吏部尚書。 四十七年，以老乞罷，瀕行，賜以詩。 五十三年，詣京師祝聖壽，加太子少師，复賜以詩，還裡。 卒，年八十，賜祭葬。
During Luo's tenure in Jiangsu the emperor made three southern tours, praised his steady and unassuming conduct, and showered him with rewards. When Luo passed seventy, the emperor wrote the characters for "fortune" and "longevity" and presented them as gifts. In the forty-fourth year he was promoted to Minister of Personnel. In the forty-seventh year he asked to retire on account of age; as he was about to leave, the emperor presented him with a poem. In the fifty-third year he traveled to the capital for the emperor's birthday celebration, was named Junior Tutor of the Heir Apparent, received another imperial poem, and returned home. He died at eighty and was granted state funeral rites.
26
陳詵，字叔大，浙江海寧人。 康熙十一年舉人，授中書科中書舍人。 二十八年，考授吏科給事中，乞養歸。 三十六年，起補原官。 輔刑科掌印給事中。 疏言：「淮、黃自古不兩行。 邇者修歸仁堤，開胡家溝，出睢湖之水； 閉六壩，加築高家堰，出洪澤湖之水。 此借淮敵黃不易之理。 然淮水入運者多，則敵黃仍弱。 舊設天妃閘，自淮、黃交會處至清江浦，凡為五閘，重運到時，更迭啟閉，過即下板鎖斷，是以全淮注黃。 其引入運河者，不過暫資濟運。 自改建草壩，淮、黃盡趨運河，清江浦民居可危。 宜复天妃閘舊制，使淮易敵黃，有裨大工。 」疏下河督張鵬翮議行。 尋疏劾山東蒲台知縣俞宏聲以赦前細故，拘系監生王觀成，迫令自殺； 巡撫王國昌僅以杖責解役結案，玩視民命。 命侍郎吳涵偕詵往按，宏聲坐奪官，國昌等議處。 授鴻臚寺卿，再遷左副都御史。
Chen Shen, styled Shuda, came from Haining in Zhejiang. He passed the provincial examinations in the eleventh year of Kangxi and was appointed a secretarial clerk in the Secretariat Section. In the twenty-eighth year he passed an examination for the post of supervising secretary in the Personnel Section, then asked leave to return home and care for his parents. In the thirty-sixth year he was recalled to his former post. He served as acting seal-holding supervising secretary of the Punishments Section. He memorialized: "The Huai and Yellow Rivers have never run together—this is an ancient truth. Recently the Guiren embankment was repaired and the Hujia Ditch opened to drain Sui Lake; the six barrages were closed and the Gaojia Weir raised to channel Hongze Lake. This applies the old principle of pitting the Huai against the Yellow—a difficult and risky expedient. But when too much Huai water is diverted into the Grand Canal, the Huai's power to hold back the Yellow is weakened. The old Tianfei sluices ran from the Huai–Yellow confluence to Qingjiangpu—five gates in all. During the grain transport season they opened in rotation and closed again once the barges passed, directing the full force of the Huai into the Yellow. Water sent into the Grand Canal was only a temporary aid to shipping. Since the sluices were rebuilt as earthen barrages, both rivers pour into the Grand Canal, putting the homes of Qingjiangpu in grave danger. The Tianfei sluices should be restored to their former design so the Huai can once again hold back the Yellow—this would greatly aid the river-control project. The memorial was referred to River Controller Zhang Penghe, who approved and carried it out. He soon memorialized against Yu Hongsheng, magistrate of Putai in Shandong, who over a petty pre-amnesty dispute had imprisoned the student Wang Guancheng and driven him to suicide; Governor Wang Guochang had merely caned the bailiffs and closed the case, showing contempt for human life. Vice Minister Wu Han was sent with Shen to investigate. Hongsheng was dismissed from office; Guochang and others were referred for disciplinary action. He was appointed director of the Court of State Ceremonial, then promoted to Left Vice Censor-in-Chief.
27
四十三年，授貴州巡撫。 疏言：「貴州田地俱在層岡峻嶺間，土性寒涼，收成歉薄，人牛種蓺維艱。 前撫臣王蓺因合屬田地荒蕪十之四五，減輕舊則，招徠開墾成熟，六年後起科。 有續報者亦如之。 」疏下部，如所請。 四十七年，調湖北。 疏劾布政使王毓賢虧帑，命解任。 尋以盤驗已完，奏免其罪。 五十年，擢工部尚書。 五十二年，調禮部。 五十八年，乞休，命致仕。 六十一年，卒，賜祭葬，諡清恪。 子世倌，自有傳。
In the forty-third year he was appointed governor of Guizhou. He memorialized: "Guizhou's farmland lies on steep, terraced slopes where the soil is cold and poor and harvests are thin; both farmers and oxen struggle to bring land under cultivation. Former governor Wang Yi, finding four or five tenths of the land in his jurisdiction lying fallow, reduced the old tax rates, encouraged settlers to reclaim and cultivate the land, and deferred taxation for six years after fields were brought into production. Subsequent reports received the same treatment. The memorial was referred to the ministries and approved as requested. In the forty-seventh year he was transferred to Hubei. He memorialized against Provincial Treasurer Wang Yuxian for treasury shortfalls, and Wang was ordered to step down. When the audit was completed, he memorialized that Wang be cleared of wrongdoing. In the fiftieth year he was promoted to Minister of Works. In the fifty-second year he was transferred to the Ministry of Rites. In the fifty-eighth year he asked to retire and was granted honorable retirement. In the sixty-first year he died and was granted state funeral rites, with the posthumous epithet Qingke (Pure and Reverent). His son Chen Shichang has a separate biography.
28
論曰：當三籓亂時，雲、貴、閩、粵，其發難地也； 蹂躪所及，湖南北、江西、四川，受害最甚。 伊闢、王繼文撫雲南，從師而南，參與軍畫，其事已別見； 雍建於貴州，締虞於四川，弘祚於廣東，騭於江西，犖承騭，詵遙繼雍建，兵後撫綏甚勤。 大亂方定，起衰救弊，出水火，登袵席，偉哉諸人之功歟！
Commentary: During the Rebellion of the Three Feudatories, Yunnan, Guizhou, Fujian, and Guangdong were where the uprising began; but Hunan, Hubei, Jiangxi, and Sichuan, where the armies marched, suffered the worst devastation. Yang Yongjian and Wang Jiwen governed Yunnan, marched south with the armies, and took part in military planning—their careers are told elsewhere; Yang Yongjian in Guizhou, Yao Dingu in Sichuan, Zhu Hongzuo in Guangdong, and Wang Zhi in Jiangxi—Song Luo succeeded Wang Zhi, and Chen Shen in turn followed Yang Yongjian's path; all labored tirelessly to restore order after the war. When the great rebellion had barely ended, they revived devastated provinces, rescued the people from misery, and restored them to peace and comfort—how magnificent their service was!
