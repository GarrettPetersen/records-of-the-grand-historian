西藏：禹貢三危之地。 在四川、雲南徼外，至京師萬有四千餘里。 周為西戎，漢為西羌。 唐為吐蕃，其君長號贊普。 至宋朝貢不絕。 元憲宗始於河州置吐蕃宣慰司都元帥府，四川徼外置碉門、魚通、黎雅、長河、西寧等處宣撫司。 世祖時，復置烏斯藏，郡縣其地。 明為烏斯藏，賜封號，設指揮、宣慰等司，以示羈縻。 宣德、成化間，又累加封號。 其地有僧號達賴喇嘛，居拉薩之布達拉廟，號為前藏； 有班禪喇嘛，居日喀則城之札什倫布廟，號為後藏。 太宗崇德七年，有達賴喇嘛及班禪，重譯來貢。 未幾，為蒙古顧實汗所據。 四傳至曾孫拉藏汗，而準噶爾並之。 康熙五十九年，官兵西討，殲偽藏王，以西藏地賜達賴喇嘛，使蒙古舊臣頗羅鼐等五人分守。 乾隆四年，敕封頗羅鼐為郡王，領藏事。 至其子襲封，以罪誅，遂除西藏王爵。 所有輔國公三，一等台吉一，噶布倫四，戴琫五，碟巴三，堪布一。 設駐藏辦事、幫辦大臣，分駐前後藏以轄之。 其俗稱國曰圖伯特，又曰唐古忒。 近因藏民不遵光緒十六年與英所定條約，辱其邊務大臣，致英兵入拉薩，要挾西藏立約十條，主權盡失。 光緒三十一年，特派員至印度與英協商，其新改條約：一，西藏路礦電線，由中、英兩國妥議辦理，他國不得干預； 二，西藏用人權，概歸英員與駐藏大臣會議辦理； 三，西藏有亂，中政府須與英協商後派兵彈壓； 四，西藏增設商埠，由中、英兩國會同辦理； 五，西藏土地，非得中、英兩國承辦，不得租借轉賣。 據條約觀之，西藏蓋為兩屬之國矣。
Tibet: the territory of Sanwei recorded in the Yu Gong. It stands beyond the borders of Sichuan and Yunnan, over fourteen thousand li from the capital. Under the Zhou it was the Western Rong; under the Han, the Western Qiang. In Tang times it was Tibet, whose chieftains bore the title zanpu. Throughout the Song, tribute missions continued without interruption. Under the Yuan Emperor Xianzong, the Tubo Pacification Commission and Grand Marshalate were first established at Hezhou, while beyond the Sichuan frontier Pacification Offices were placed at Diaomen, Yutong, Liya, Changhe, Xining, and elsewhere. Under the Shizu Emperor, Usang was re-established and the region was organized into prefectures and counties. In Ming times it was Usang; the court granted titles and established commandant and pacification offices as a form of loose control. During the Xuande and Chenghua reigns, additional honors were conferred again and again. In the region a monk bears the title Dalai Lama, residing at the Potala in Lhasa and known as Front Tibet; and there is the Panchen Lama, who resides at Tashilhunpo in Shigatse and is known as Rear Tibet. In Chongde 7 under Emperor Taizong, the Dalai Lama and Panchen came once more through interpreters to present tribute. Soon afterward the region fell under the control of the Mongol Güshi Khan. Four generations later power passed to his great-grandson Lha-bzang Khan, after which the Dzungars absorbed the territory. In Kangxi 59 imperial forces marched west, destroyed the pretender king of Tibet, granted Tibetan territory to the Dalai Lama, and assigned five former Mongol ministers, including Pholhané, to divide its defense. In Qianlong 4 Pholhané was enfeoffed by imperial edict as a commandery prince and placed in charge of Tibetan affairs. When his son inherited the title, he was executed for crimes, and the Tibetan princely rank was abolished. The offices included three assistant state dukes, one first-rank taiji, four kalöns, five daibing, three dieba, and one khenpo. Resident and assistant commissioners for Tibet were appointed, stationed in Front and Rear Tibet respectively to administer the region. In local usage the country is called Tubet, also known as Tangut. Recently, because Tibetans failed to observe the treaty concluded with Britain in Guangxu 16 and insulted the frontier commissioner, British forces entered Lhasa and compelled Tibet to accept ten articles, by which sovereignty was largely surrendered. In Guangxu 31 a special envoy was dispatched to India to negotiate with Britain. The revised treaty provided: first, Tibet's roads, mines, and telegraph lines were to be arranged by agreement between China and Britain, with no intervention by other powers; second, Tibet's appointments were to be handled jointly by British officials and the resident commissioner; third, in the event of unrest in Tibet, the Chinese government had to consult Britain before sending troops to suppress it; fourth, new trading ports in Tibet were to be established jointly by China and Britain; fifth, Tibetan land could not be leased or sold without the approval of both China and Britain. Judging by these treaties, Tibet had effectively become a state under dual suzerainty.

境內分四部：曰衛，曰康，曰藏，曰阿里。 東界四川，東南界雲南，西界西域回部大沙海，北界青海及回部。 廣六千餘里，袤五千餘里。 北極高三十度三十五分，京師偏西二十四度十五分。
Within its borders the region is divided into four parts: Wei, Kang, Zang, and Ali. On the east it borders Sichuan; on the southeast Yunnan; on the west the great desert of the Muslim Western Regions; on the north Qinghai and the Muslim territories. It measures more than six thousand li across and over five thousand li in length. Its latitude is 30°35′ N; it lies 24°15′ west of the capital.

衛：一曰前藏，即古之危，亦稱中藏，即烏斯藏也。 乾隆十五年，設大臣鎮守。 其城曰布達拉城。 有坐床，為達賴喇嘛所駐，協理藏事。 東界喀木，西界後藏，南界不丹，北界青海及新疆。 轄城二十八。 喇薩城即布達拉，在打箭爐西北三千四百八十里。 札什城在喇薩南七里。 德慶城在喇薩東南三十八里。 柰布東城在喇薩東南二百二十里。 桑里城在喇薩東南二百五十一里。 垂佳普郎城在喇薩東南二百六十里。 野而古城在喇薩東南三百一十里。 達克匝城在喇薩東南三百三十七里。 則庫城在喇薩東南三百四十里。 滿撮納城在喇薩東南四百四十里。 拉巴隨城在喇薩東南四百四十里。 札木達城在喇薩東南五百四十里。 達喇馬宗城在喇薩東南五百六十里。 古魯納木吉牙城在喇薩東南六百二十里。 碩噶城在喇薩東南六百四十里。 硃木宗城在喇薩東南七百五十里。 東順城在喇薩東南七百七十里。 則布拉岡城在喇薩東南八百七十里。 納城在喇薩東南九百六十里。 吉尼城在喇薩東南九百八十里。 日噶牛城在喇薩西南三十里。 楚舒爾城在喇薩西南百十五里。 日喀爾城在喇薩西南百二十里。 公喀爾城在喇薩西南百四十里，為衛地最大之城。 岳吉牙來雜城在喇薩西南三百三十里。 多宗城在喇薩西南四百二十里。 僧格宗城在喇薩西南四百三十里。 董郭爾城在喇薩西二十五里。 第巴達克匝城在喇薩東北九十二里。 倫硃布宗城在喇薩東北一百二十里。 墨魯恭噶城在喇薩東北百五十里。 蓬多城在喇薩東北百七十里。 設大汛為護防。 藏地凡大汛四，一在前藏城，一在後藏。 又台站二，自打箭爐至此有站五。 魚通即打箭爐、里塘、巴塘，均屬四川有。 前藏者二。 曰察木多，曰拉里。 城西南：巴則山。 西：招拉筆洞山。 又布達拉山，高百餘丈。 又西：東噶爾山，高約四百餘丈，為西藏要隘。 南：牛魔山，高二百餘丈。 東北：郎路山、薩木多嶺。 北：布克沙克河，源出噶爾占古察嶺，南流，西合沙克河。 又東南流，西受庫蘭河，北受布克河。 又東南，入喀喇烏蘇。 雅魯藏布江，即大金沙江，古之跋布川也。 源出藏西界卓書特部西北達木楚克哈巴布山，三源，俱東北流而合，折東流，枯木岡前山水自西南來會。 又東北，江加蘇木拉河自西北沙苦牙拉麻拉山東南流來會。 又東，阿拉楚河北自沙拉木岡前山水會而南流，又東稍北，拉烏克藏布必拉自東北桑里池西南流，合數水來會。 又東南，郭永河自東南昂則嶺東北流，合數水來會。 又東，薩楚藏布河自東北合諸水來會。 又東，甕出河、式爾的河、滿楚藏布河、薩克藏布河，合諸水來會。 又東南，加木租池水北自章阿布林城合東一水南流來注之。 又東南，受西南來一水，又正北流，折向西北，受西北隆左池水。 又東北，莽噶拉河南自那拉古董察山來注之。 又東北，鍾里山水自東南來注之。 又東北，經章拉則城北，又東北，鄂宜楚藏布河自西北札木楚克池合諸水東南流來注之。 又北流，戒忒楚河、札克北朋楚河自北來注之。 又東南，會薩普楚河。 又東逕普冬廟前，烏雨克河自拉公山來注之。 又東過薩喇硃噶鐵索橋，逕林奔城北，龍前河自南合二水來注之。 又東北，捏木河自西北來注之。 折東南流，逕拜的城北岸山北，受西北來一小水，東北過鐵索橋，逕楚舒爾城南，東南至日喀爾公喀爾城北，噶爾招木倫江自東北合諸水，西南流逕衛地喇薩來會，疑即古吐蕃之臧河也。 雅魯藏布江既會噶爾招木倫江，東南流，至打格布衣那城北，共八百里。 年褚河自北合諸水來會。 又東經叉母哈廟北，受東北薩母龍拉嶺水，南流入羅喀布占國。 穆楚河合柰楚河，南流入哲孟雄。 滕格里池，在境西北，藏地日喀則城東北，隔山即潞江源之布喀諸池。 其北隔山即大流沙也。 池廣六百餘里，周一千餘里，東西甚長，南北稍狹，蒙古呼天為「滕格里」，言水色同天青。 其東有三水流入，皆名查哈蘇太河。 西有二水流入，北曰羅薩河，南曰打爾古藏布河，合西來數池水，東流入此池。 次曰牙母魯克於木卒池，中有三山，水成五色。 曰馬品木達賴池，郎噶池，即狼楚河也。 次曰布喀池，潞江源也。 東噶爾山上有關。
Wei: also known as Front Tibet, the ancient land of Sanwei, also called Central Tibet—that is, Usang. In Qianlong 15 a resident minister was appointed to guard the region. Its principal city is Potala. There is a throne seat where the Dalai Lama resides and assists in governing Tibetan affairs. On the east it borders Kham; on the west Rear Tibet; on the south Bhutan; on the north Qinghai and Xinjiang. It administers twenty-eight cities. Lhasa City is Potala, lying three thousand four hundred eighty li northwest of Dartsendo. Zhaxi lies seven li south of Lhasa. Deqing lies thirty-eight li southeast of Lhasa. Naibudong lies two hundred twenty li southeast of Lhasa. Sangli lies two hundred fifty-one li southeast of Lhasa. Chuijiapulang lies two hundred sixty li southeast of Lhasa. Ye'ergu lies three hundred ten li southeast of Lhasa. Dakeza lies three hundred thirty-seven li southeast of Lhasa. Zeku lies three hundred forty li southeast of Lhasa. Mancuona lies four hundred forty li southeast of Lhasa. Labasui lies four hundred forty li southeast of Lhasa. Zhamuda lies five hundred forty li southeast of Lhasa. Dalama Zong lies five hundred sixty li southeast of Lhasa. Gulunamujiya lies six hundred twenty li southeast of Lhasa. Shuoga lies six hundred forty li southeast of Lhasa. Zhumu Zong lies seven hundred fifty li southeast of Lhasa. Dongshun lies seven hundred seventy li southeast of Lhasa. Zebulagang lies eight hundred seventy li southeast of Lhasa. Na lies nine hundred sixty li southeast of Lhasa. Jini lies nine hundred eighty li southeast of Lhasa. Riganiu lies thirty li southwest of Lhasa. Chushu'er lies one hundred fifteen li southwest of Lhasa. Rikaze lies one hundred twenty li southwest of Lhasa. Gongka'er lies one hundred forty li southwest of Lhasa and is the largest city in the Wei region. Yuejiyalaiza lies three hundred thirty li southwest of Lhasa. Duozong lies four hundred twenty li southwest of Lhasa. Sengge Zong lies four hundred thirty li southwest of Lhasa. Dongguo'er lies twenty-five li west of Lhasa. Dibadakeza lies ninety-two li northeast of Lhasa. Lunzhu Buzong lies one hundred twenty li northeast of Lhasa. Molugongga lies one hundred fifty li northeast of Lhasa. Pengduo lies one hundred seventy li northeast of Lhasa. A major garrison was established for frontier defense. Throughout Tibetan territory there are four major garrisons, one in Front Tibet and one in Rear Tibet. There are also two relay stations; five stations lie between Dartsendo and this point. Yutong comprises Dartsendo, Litang, and Batang, all of which belong to Sichuan. In Front Tibet there are two. They are Qamdo and Lhari. To the southwest of the city: Baze Mountain. To the west: Zhaolabidong Mountain. Also Potala Mountain, rising more than a hundred zhang. Farther west: Dongga'er Mountain, roughly four hundred zhang high, a strategic pass of Tibet. To the south: Niumo Mountain, over two hundred zhang high. To the northeast: Langlu Mountain and Samdo Ridge. To the north: the Bukshak River, which rises on Garzhangucha Ridge, flows south, and joins the Shak River from the west. It then flows southeast, receiving the Kulan from the west and the Buke from the north. It continues southeast and enters the Karakusu. The Yarlung Tsangpo is the great Jinsha River, known in antiquity as the Babu River. It rises on Damuchukehababu Mountain northwest of the Zhuoshute Department on Tibet's western frontier; three headstreams flow northeast and merge, then turn east, where the waters below Kumugang join from the southwest. Flowing northeast, it receives the Jiangjiasumula, which runs southeast from Shakuyalamala Mountain in the northwest. It continues east as the northern Alachu, formed by streams below Shalamugang, flows south, then bends slightly north of east; the Lawukangbubila flows southwest from Sangli Pool in the northeast, gathering several tributaries before joining. It turns southeast as the Guoyong flows northeast from Angze Ridge in the southeast, gathering several streams. It flows east again as the Sachu Tsangpo joins from the northeast with its tributaries. Farther east it receives the Wengchu, Shierde, Manchu Tsangpo, and Sak Tsangpo, each bringing its tributaries. It turns southeast as waters from Jiamuzu Pool flow south from Zhang'abulin City, where an eastern stream joins and flows south into it. Continuing southeast, it takes a stream from the southwest, then flows due north, bends northwest, and receives the waters of Longzuo Pool from the northwest. It flows northeast as the Mangiala enters from the south, descending from Nalagudongcha Mountain. It continues northeast, receiving the waters of Zhongli Mountain from the southeast. It flows northeast past the north side of Zhanglaze, then northeast again as the Eyichu Tsangpo flows southeast from Zhamuchuke Pool in the northwest, gathering its tributaries before joining. It turns north, receiving the Jietechu and Zhakebeipengchu from the north. It continues southeast and joins the Sapuchu. It flows east past Pudong Temple, where the Wuyuke enters from Lagong Mountain. It continues east past the Salazhuga iron-chain bridge, runs north of Linben, and receives the Longqian, which joins two southern streams before entering. It turns northeast as the Niemu enters from the northwest. It bends southeast, runs north of the hills along the north bank above Baidi, takes a small stream from the northwest, crosses an iron-chain bridge northeast of Chushu'er, and reaches north of Rikaze Gongka'er; the Garzhaomulun gathers northeastern tributaries, flows southwest past Lhasa in the Wei region, and joins it—likely the ancient Tibetan Zang River. After the Yarlung Tsangpo joins the Garzhaomulun, it flows southeast to north of Dagebuyina, a stretch of eight hundred li in all. The Nianchu joins from the north with its tributaries. It flows east past Chamuha Temple, takes the waters of Samulongla Ridge from the northeast, and turns south into the country of Rokabuzhan. The Muchu joins the Naichu and flows south into Zhemengxiong. Tengri Pool lies in the northwest of the region, northeast of Shigatse; beyond the mountains lie the Buka pools that form the source of the Lujiang. To its north, beyond the mountains, lies the great desert. The lake measures over six hundred li across and more than a thousand li in circumference, elongated east to west and narrower north to south. Mongols call heaven tengri, saying its waters share the blue of the sky. Three streams enter from the east, all named the Zhahasutai. Two streams enter from the west: the Luosa to the north and the Da'ergu Tsangpo to the south; they gather waters from western lakes and flow east into the pool. Next is Yamuluke Yumuzu Pool, which contains three mountains and waters of five hues. There are Mapinmudalai Pool and Langga Pool—the latter being the Langchu River. Next is Buka Pool, the source of the Lujiang. Dongga'er Mountain has a frontier pass.

康：一曰喀木。 要寨曰察木多。 在前藏東千二百五十里，東界四川，南界珞瑜境及英屬阿薩密，西界衛地，北界青海。 喀木今曰昌都，亦稱前藏，本屬呼圖克圖。 康熙五十八年始納款。 設台站，置糧員一。 有土城。 西南有羅隆宗、舒班多、達隆宗，西北有類伍齊等部落，其南有乍丫。 康熙五十八年招撫。 又南有江卡，雍正元年招撫，設有官寨。 東：達蓋喇山、沖得喇山。 南：安靜大山，與川、滇分界。 西：嘉松古木山。 東南：奪布喇山、鼎各喇山。 西南：魚別喇山、里角大山，冬春積雪。 又巴貢山、蒙堡山、擦瓦山、雲山、雪山、白奪山、納奪山、黃雲山、隱山、喇貢山。 東有列木喇嶺。 羅隆宗東有得貢喇山，山勢陡峻。 西：得噶喇山。 舒班多東有章喇山。 西南：吾抵喇山、巴喇山。 西：朔馬喇山，即賽瓦合山。 達隆宗西有必達喇山，沙貢喇山、魯貢喇山，兩山相連。 類伍齊西南有瓦合大山，山大而峻，冬春積雪。 又有擦噶喇山、葉達喇山。 察木多左有昂楮河，源出中壩，因通雲南，亦名雲河。 右有雜楮河，源出九茹，因通四川，亦名川河。 二水合流，入雲南。 瀾滄江二源，一源發於匝坐里岡城西北格爾吉匝噶那山，名匝楚河，一源發於匝坐里岡城西北巴喇克拉丹蘇克山，名鄂穆楚河，俱東南流，至匝坐里岡城東北察木多廟前，二水合流，名拉楚河，南流至包敦入乍丫。 又南流至察瓦寺，甲倉河東北來會。 又東南，左受色爾恭河，折南流，至角占，受左貢河。 又東南流，逕茶利大雪山入雲南，始名瀾滄江。 潞江在瀾滄西，發源於衛地之布喀大澤，淵澄黝黑，又多伏流，蒙古呼黑為「喀喇」，水為「烏蘇」，故名喀喇烏蘇。 逕拉薩北，有池名布喀，橢圜形，廣六十里，袤一百五十里，從此池西北流出，入額爾吉根池，轉東北，入衣達池，又折東南流，入喀喇池。 三池俱縱廣五六十里。 中有三山，四池環抱。 復從喀喇池東南出，納布倫河，又東受北來二小水，折南轉東，至喀喇烏蘇，為西寧進藏大道，皮船為渡。 轉東北流，逕蒙古三十九族地，至伊庫山，沙克河西北來會。 又東北流，逕蘇圖克土司，索克河自北來會。 折南流，左右各受一小水，轉西南會衛楚河。 折而東，受雄楚河。 又東納沙隆錫河，轉東南流，類烏齊河自北來會。 又東南逕必蚌山，至嘉玉橋，為滇、蜀入藏之大道。 又東南流，江陽為巴克碩游牧，江陰為波密野番。 又東南流，逕桑昂曲宗入江卡。 江之外為怒夷，故名怒江。 又東南，入雲南維西廳，折而南下，逕雲龍州西徼，右納扞江，入保山乃名潞江。 南流逕潞江安撫司。 又南流少東，左納沙河，轉西南至遮放土司，從此出滇境入緬甸。 羅隆宗西有偶楮河，源出噶爾藏骨岔海子，海合瀾滄江南峽。 隆喜楮河源出噶喇山，東流，合偶楮河。 舒班多有納碩布楚河，源出中義溝，北流，逕舒班多城西，合三溪，東北流，入喀喇烏蘇。 又柱嗎郎錯河，源出噶喇山，胄楮河，源出吾抵山，均流歸偶楮河。 達隆宗北有撒楮河，源出朔馬喇山。 東南：邊楮河，流合胄楮河。 有俄楮河，源出沙貢喇山，流合葉楮河。 類烏齊東北有扎楮河，即昂楮河下流。 乍丫有勒楮河，源出昂喇山。 樂楮河，源出作喇山。 又有甲倉河，源出官角，西南流，逕草里工，又西南，至洛隆宗，合洛楚河，又西南至乍丫寺前。 與猛楚河合。 有色楮河，源出上納，奪流入察木多大河。
Kang: also known as Kham. Its principal stronghold is Qamdo. It lies one thousand two hundred fifty li east of Front Tibet, bordering Sichuan on the east, Luoyu and British Assam on the south, the Wei region on the west, and Qinghai on the north. Kham is now called Qamdo, also termed Front Tibet, and originally belonged to the Khutuktu. It first submitted allegiance in Kangxi 58. Relay stations were established and one grain officer appointed. It has an earthen walled town. To the southwest lie Luolong Zong, Shubanduo, and Dalong Zong; to the northwest are tribes such as Leiwuqi; to the south is Zhaya. They were brought under control by appeasement in Kangxi 58. Farther south is Jiangka, brought under control in Yongzheng 1 with an official stockade established. To the east: Dagaila Mountain and Chongdela Mountain. To the south: Anjing Great Mountain, which marks the border with Sichuan and Yunnan. To the west: Jiasonggumu Mountain. To the southeast: Duobula Mountain and Dinggela Mountain. To the southwest: Yubiela Mountain and Lijiao Great Mountain, snow-covered through winter and spring. Also Bagong, Mengbao, Cawa, Yun, Xue, Baiduo, Naduo, Huangyun, Yin, and Lagong mountains. To the east lies Liemula Ridge. East of Luolong Zong stands Degongla Mountain, steep and precipitous. To the west: Degala Mountain. East of Shubanduo lies Zhangla Mountain. To the southwest: Wudila Mountain and Bala Mountain. To the west: Shuomala Mountain, also called Saiwahe Mountain. West of Dalong Zong lie Bidala, Shagongla, and Lugongla mountains, the last two linked together. Southwest of Leiwuqi stands Wahe Great Mountain, large and rugged, snow-covered in winter and spring. There are also Cagala and Yedala mountains. To the left of Qamdo is the Angchu, which rises in Zhongba and, because it leads to Yunnan, is also called the Yun River. To the right is the Zazhu, which rises in Jiuru and, because it leads to Sichuan, is also called the Chuan River. The two streams merge and flow into Yunnan. The Lancang has two headwaters: one rises northwest of Zuozuoligang on Ge'erjizhagana Mountain as the Zachu; the other rises northwest of the same city on Balakeladansuke Mountain as the Emuchu. Both flow southeast to Qamdo Temple northeast of Zuozuoligang, where they merge as the Lachu, which flows south to Baodun and enters Zhaya. It continues south to Chawa Temple, where the Jiacang joins from the northeast. It turns southeast, takes the Se'ergong on the left, bends south, and at Jiaozhan receives the Zuogong. It flows southeast past Chali Great Snow Mountain into Yunnan, where it first takes the name Lancang. The Lujiang lies west of the Lancang, rising from the great Buka marsh in the Wei region—deep, dark, and clear, with many underground channels. Mongols call black kara and water usu, hence Karakusu. It passes north of Lhasa, where Buka Pool lies—oval, sixty li across and one hundred fifty li long. From Buka it flows northwest into E'erjigen Pool, turns northeast into Yida Pool, then bends southeast into Kara Pool. All three lakes measure fifty to sixty li across. Three mountains stand within, ringed by four lakes. It emerges southeast from Kara Pool, takes the Bulun, then two small northern streams from the east, bends south and east to Karakusu—the main route from Xining into Tibet, crossed by hide boats. It turns northeast through the lands of the Mongol thirty-nine tribes to Yiku Mountain, where the Shak joins from the northwest. It continues northeast past the Sutuke chieftaincy, where the Sok joins from the north. It bends south, taking a small stream on each side, then turns southwest to join the Weichu. It turns east and receives the Xiongchu. It flows east, takes the Shalongxi, turns southeast, and receives the Leiwuqi from the north. It continues southeast past Bibeng Mountain to Jiayu Bridge—the main route from Yunnan and Sichuan into Tibet. It flows southeast; the sunny bank is Bakshuo pastureland, the shaded bank the wild tribes of Bomi. It continues southeast past Sang'angqu Zong into Jiangka. Beyond the river live the Nu tribes, whence its name Nujiang. It flows southeast into Yunnan's Weixi Subprefecture, turns south along Yunlong's western frontier, takes the Han on the right, and upon reaching Baoshan is named the Lujiang. It flows south past the Lujiang Pacification Commission. It flows south by east, takes the Sha on the left, turns southwest to Zhemao, and from there leaves Yunnan for Burma. West of Luolong Zong is the Ouchu, which rises from Garzanggucha Lake and joins the southern gorge of the Lancang. The Longxichu rises on Gala Mountain, flows east, and joins the Ouchu. At Shubanduo the Nashuobuchu rises in Zhongyigou, flows north past the west of the town, gathers three streams, and flows northeast into Karakusu. Also the Zhuma Langcuo rises on Gala Mountain and the Zhouchu on Wudi Mountain; both flow into the Ouchu. North of Dalong Zong is the Sazhu, which rises on Shuomala Mountain. To the southeast: the Bianchu flows to join the Zhouchu. The Echu rises on Shagongla Mountain and flows to join the Yechu. Northeast of Leiwuqi is the Zhazhu, the lower reach of the Angchu. At Zhaya the Lezhu rises on Angla Mountain. The Lezhu rises on Zuola Mountain. Also the Jiacang rises at Guanjiao, flows southwest past Caoligong, then to Luolong Zong, joins the Luochu, and continues southwest to Zhaya Temple. There it joins the Mengchu. The Sezhu rises in Shangna and cuts across to enter the great Qamdo River.

拉里：一名喇里。 在前藏東五百九十里，察木多西六百六十里，達隆宗西北。 康熙五十五年，其地有黑喇嘛，附於準噶爾。 尋討平之，以地屬前藏。 設台站，置糧員一。 無城。 西南有工布江達。 江達稱沃壤。 亦平西藏時就撫。 又其南有達克，東北有西藏大臣所屬三十九土司。 亦有入甘肅西寧界者，皆喀喇烏蘇番眾也。 拉里有拉里大山，勢如龍，上下險峻，四時積雪。 西南有瓦子山，番人呼為卓拉大山，延亙數百里，多積雪。 江達西有鹿馬嶺，高約四十里，為西藏要隘。 拉里東有同妥楮河，源出魯貢喇山，流合得楮河。 有熱水塘，四時常溫，番人呼為擦楮卡。 江達有岡布藏布河，自衛地東納東北察拉嶺水，又東南，有危楚河自東北來會。 又東南，有牛楚河自西北來會。 東流過打克拉崩橋，又東，受東北二水，又南，逕的牙爾山西，入岡布部落。 至撇皮唐他拉東喀木境內，有薄藏布河自東北來會，土人曰喀克布必拉。 逕噶克布衣書里東城西，又南，逕塞母龍拉嶺東，朵格拉岡里山西，出岡布境。 逕公拉岡里山西，又南入羅喀布占國，下流入雅魯藏布江。 匝楚藏布江，即年渚必拉江，源出沙羽克岡拉山，即喀爾靼廟東南山也。 有水東流，曰馬木楚河，與南來巴拉嶺之巴隆楚河會。 又東北，與北來烏山之烏斯江會。 又東，逕鹿馬嶺，至順達，有水曰佳囊河，發源過拉松多，東南流，逕江達城東，折而南流，合東二小水來會。 又東南曲曲流，至工布什噶城南，有水東北自巴麻穆池南流，合東一水來會。 又折而西南，有西來齊布山之牛楚河，合而南流，至工布珠穆宗城東、底穆宗城西，又東南至布拉岡城東，合於雅魯藏布江。 又桑楚河，南流，有雅隆布河出舒班多南境來注之，是為薄藏布河，又南入羅喀布占國，注雅魯藏布江。
Lhari: also known as Lali. It lies five hundred ninety li east of Front Tibet, six hundred sixty li west of Qamdo, and northwest of Dalong Zong. In Kangxi 55 a Black Lama in the region aligned himself with the Dzungars. The rebellion was soon suppressed, and the territory was assigned to Front Tibet. Relay stations were established and one grain officer appointed. It has no walled town. To the southwest lies Gongbu Jiangda. Jiangda is known as fertile land. It was likewise brought under control when Tibet was pacified. Farther south is Dake; to the northeast lie the thirty-nine chieftaincies under the Tibetan commissioner. Some extend into the Xining frontier of Gansu; all are Karakusu Tibetan peoples. At Lhari stands Lhari Great Mountain, dragon-shaped and precipitous throughout, snow-covered year-round. To the southwest is Wazi Mountain, which Tibetans call Zhuola Great Mountain, stretching for hundreds of li and mantled in snow. West of Jiangda is Luma Ridge, roughly forty li high—a strategic pass of Tibet. East of Lhari the Tongtuochu rises on Lugongla Mountain and flows to join the Dezhu. There is a hot spring, warm year-round, which Tibetans call Cazhuka. At Jiangda the Gangbu Tsangpo, flowing from the Wei region, takes the waters of Chala Ridge from the northeast, then southeast receives the Weichu from the northeast. It continues southeast as the Niuchu joins from the northwest. It flows east past Dakelabeng Bridge, takes two northeastern streams, turns south along the west of Diya'er Mountain, and enters Gangbu territory. At Piepitangtala in eastern Kham, the Bocangbu joins from the northeast; locals call it Kakebubila. It runs east of Gakebuyi Shuli East City, turns south past Samulongla Ridge and west of Duogelagangli Mountain, and leaves Gangbu territory. It passes west of Gonglagangli Mountain, enters Rokabuzhan to the south, and flows downstream into the Yarlung Tsangpo. The Zachu Tsangpo, also called the Nianzhubila, rises on Shayukegangla Mountain—the southeastern peak near Kaltan Temple. A stream flows east as the Mamuchu and joins the Balongchu from Bala Ridge to the south. It turns northeast and joins the Wusi from Wushan Mountain in the north. It flows east past Luma Ridge to Shunda, where the Jianang rises beyond Lasongduo, runs southeast past east of Jiangda, turns south, and joins two small eastern streams. It winds southeast to south of Gongbu Shiga, where a stream flows south from Bamamu Pool in the northeast and joins an eastern tributary. It bends southwest as the Niuchu from Qibu Mountain joins and flows south between Gongbu Zhumu Zong and Dimu Zong, then southeast to east of Bulagang, where it joins the Yarlung Tsangpo. The Sangchu flows south as the Yalongbu enters from southern Shubanduo—forming the Bocangbu, which flows south into Rokabuzhan and joins the Yarlung Tsangpo.

藏：即後藏，一曰喀齊。 在前藏西南五百餘里地，曰札什倫布，即古之藏也。 南界尼泊爾，東界衛地，西界阿里，北界新疆。 乾隆十五年，設大臣鎮守。 其城曰札什倫布城，有坐床，為班禪額爾德尼所駐，協理藏事。 有汛三：在本城一； 外二，曰江孜，曰定日。 西境彭錯嶺。 北境那木嶺。 北有雅魯藏布江，出阿里西南界山，東流，有郭永河東北流注之。 又受那烏克藏布河、薩布楚河、薩爾格藏布河，又近城北會多克楚河，至城西會南來之當出河，又逕布克什里山南，江至此已行二千五百餘里，又東入前藏界。 北有打爾古藏布河，流入前藏，瀦為騰格里池，廣六百餘里。
Zang: that is Rear Tibet, also known as Kaqi. Some five hundred li southwest of Front Tibet lies Tashilhunpo—the ancient land of Zang. It borders Nepal on the south, the Wei region on the east, Ali on the west, and Xinjiang on the north. In Qianlong 15 a resident minister was appointed to guard the region. Its principal city is Tashilhunpo, where the Panchen Erdeni holds his throne seat and assists in governing Tibetan affairs. There are three garrisons: one in the city itself; and two outside, at Gyantse and Tingri. Its western frontier is Pengcuo Ridge. Its northern frontier is Namu Ridge. To the north flows the Yarlung Tsangpo, rising from Ali's southwestern border ranges and flowing east, where the Guoyong joins from the northeast. It also receives the Nawuke Tsangpo, Sabuchu, and Sa'erge Tsangpo, joins the Duokechu north of the city and the southern Dangchu west of the city, passes south of Bukeshili Mountain, and by this point has run over two thousand five hundred li before entering Front Tibet to the east. To the north the Da'ergu Tsangpo flows into Front Tibet and forms Tengri Pool, over six hundred li across.

江孜：在札什倫布城南二百里。 駐守備一。 南有帕裡邊寨，東連布魯克巴，西通哲孟雄，外接西洋部落噶里噶達。 東有千壩，南有宗木小部落，西南有定結，北有拉孜，皆有官寨。 東南：珠布拉大雪山。 西南：喀木巴拉山及薩木嶺。 定結之西有朋出藏布河，源有三，一西出書爾木藏拉山，一東出西爾中馬山，一東南出瓜查嶺。 合而東南流，受西一小水。 又南曰朋必拉，又南，有一水南自綽爾猛通那岡里山來會，折東流，受南北水各一。 又東南流，受西南涿失岡千山、阿巴拉山之水二，又東流，受南一水，又東北至羅西喀爾城南，有一河，即西北拉喀拉布山二水，合東南流，逕城北。 羅楚河自北納三水，南流合焉。 又東北，羅藏布河自西北來注之。 又東繞岡龍前山之北，折南流，受西來之牛藏布河。 又東南，受帕里藏布河。 又西南，牛楚河西自年爾木城合數水來會。 又東南流，出藏南境，過硃拉拉依部落，入厄訥特克國界，下流入雅魯藏布江。 汛西有年楚河，源有二，一出硃母拉母山東北，一出其東順拉嶺下。 泉池十數，匯為一水，北流，名章魯河，又東北，至娘娘廟東，有八水從東北喀魯嶺諸山，又南札木長山、社山來，合而西南流來會。 轉西北流，過江孜城西，又西北過白滿城西，受四水來注。 又北，始名年楚河。 經日喀則城東南，過蘇木佳石橋，長七十丈，有十九洞，為藏地橋樑之冠。 又北流，入雅魯藏布江，源長共八百餘里。 西有帕里藏布河，有一水西南流，匯為噶爾撮池，南流而西，又為查木蘇池，又西南流，折向東南，合東北來一水，又西南，會西北來之噶拉嶺水，又西逕帕里城西，又西南受二水，土人名藏曲大河，西流入朋楚河。
Gyantse lies two hundred li south of Tashilhunpo. One garrison commandant is stationed there. To the south is the Pari frontier stockade; on the east it connects with Bhutan, on the west with Zhemengxiong, and beyond with the Western tribe of Kaligada. To the east lies Qianba; to the south the small Zongmu tribe; to the southwest Dingjie; to the north Lazi—each with an official stockade. To the southeast: Zhubla Great Snow Mountain. To the southwest: Kamubala Mountain and Samu Ridge. West of Dingjie is the Pengchu Tsangpo, with three headwaters: one west from Shuer Muzangla Mountain, one east from Xi'er Zhongma Mountain, and one southeast from Guacha Ridge. They merge and flow southeast, taking a small western tributary. It continues south as Pengbila; farther south a stream joins from Zhuo'ermengtongnagangli Mountain, then it turns east, receiving one tributary each from north and south. It flows southeast, taking waters from Zhuoshigangqian and Abala mountains in the southwest, then east and a southern stream, reaching south of Luoxika'er, where two streams from Lakalabu Mountain in the northwest merge, flow southeast, and pass north of the city. The Luochu joins from the north with three tributaries and flows south to merge. It turns northeast as the Luozangbu enters from the northwest. It flows east around the north of Ganglongqian Mountain, bends south, and receives the Niuzangbu from the west. It continues southeast and receives the Pari Tsangpo. It turns southwest as the Niuchu from west of Nian'ermu City gathers its tributaries and joins. It flows southeast out of southern Tibet, passes the Zhulalayi tribe, enters Enetke territory, and joins the Yarlung Tsangpo downstream. West of the garrison is the Nianchu, with two headwaters: one northeast of Zhumulamu Mountain and one below Dongshunla Ridge to the east. A dozen springs and pools gather into a single stream flowing north as the Zhanglu; northeast of Niangniang Temple, eight streams from Karu Ridge and from Zhamuchang and She mountains unite and flow southwest to join it. It turns northwest past west of Gyantse, then northwest past west of Baiman, receiving four tributaries. Farther north it first takes the name Nianchu. It passes southeast of Shigatse across Sumujia Stone Bridge—seventy zhang long with nineteen arches, the finest bridge in Tibet. It flows north into the Yarlung Tsangpo, its entire course from source exceeding eight hundred li. To the west is the Pari Tsangpo; a stream flows southwest into Gercuo Pool, south then west into Chamusu Pool, then southwest, bends southeast, joins a northeastern stream, then southwest meets waters from Gala Ridge, passes west of Pari, takes two southwestern tributaries—the Zangqu Great River, as locals call it—and flows west into the Pengchu.

定日：在札什倫布城西南七百餘里。 駐守備一。 有汛。 城三面距邊，南有糹戎轄，西南有聶拉木，西有濟嚨，西北有宗喀。 絨轄之東南有喀達，喀達之西南有陽布，俱接廓爾喀界。 宗喀之南有布陵，南近廓爾喀，北接拉達克汗部落。 其西北有薩喀，又西北極邊有阿里。 以上各地俱有營官。 東：崇烏拉山、甲錯山。 西南：嘉汭大山。 西：通拉山。 喀達之西有霞烏拉山。 宗喀之東有鞏塘拉山。 布陵境內有岡底斯山，在阿里之達克喇城東北，直陝西西寧府西南五千五百九十餘里。 其山高五百五十餘丈，周一百四十餘里，四面峰巒陡絕，高出眾山百餘丈，積雪如懸崖，浩然潔白。 頂上有泉，流注至山麓，即伏流地下。 前後環繞諸山，皆巉岩峭峻，奇峰拱列。 按其地勢，出西南徼外，以漸而高，至此而極。 山脈蜿蜒，分幹向西北者，為僧格喀巴布、岡里木孫諸山，繞阿里而北，入西域之和闐南山及蔥嶺諸山。 向東北者，為札布列斜而充、角烏爾充、年前唐拉、薩木坦岡、匝諾莫渾烏巴什、巴顏哈喇諸山。 環衛地，竟青海，連延而下，六千餘里，至陝西西寧等處邊界。 向西南者，為悶那克尼兒、薩木泰岡諸山，亙阿里之南，入厄訥特克國。 向東南者，為達木楚克喀巴布岡、噶爾沙彌、弩金岡蒼諸山，歷藏、衛達喀木，至雲南、四川之境。 康熙五十六年，遣喇嘛楚兒沁藏布蘭木占巴、理籓院主事勝住等，繪畫西海、西藏輿圖，測量地形，以此地為天下之脊，眾山之脈，皆由此起雲。 水經註：「阿耨達山，西南有水名遙奴； 山西南少東，有水名薩罕； 少東，有水名恆伽。 此三水同出一山，俱入恆水。 」今阿里為藏中極西南地，近古天竺境。 此山西出狼楚、拉楚、麻楚三大水皆西流，轉東而南，合為岡噶江，入南海。 疑此即阿耨達山也。 又有打母硃喀巴珀山，山形似馬。 郎千喀巴珀山，山形似象。 生格喀巴珀山，山形似獅。 馬珀家喀巴珀山，山形似孔雀。 皆與岡底斯山相連。 岡噶江即出郎千喀巴珀山北麓，泉出匯為池，西北流，合東北來一水，又西而東北，公生池水伏而復出，合北來三水，西南流來會，為馬品木達賴池。 自西流出為郎噶池，受東北來一水，從西流出，折向西南，曰狼楚河，曲曲二百餘里，有楚噶拉河自東北來注之。 又西折北而東北，逕古格札什魯木布則城之西、則布龍城之東，折西北而西南流，逕則布龍城西南，又折而西北流，拉楚河自西北來會。 三水既會，始名曰岡噶江。 又東南流，出阿里界，逕馬木巴柞木郎部落，至厄訥特克入南海。 朋出藏布河在定日北，東南流，納結楚河、隆崗河，入定結。 有牛楚河，出喜拉岡參山，東南流，合東北岡布紇山水，又東南，逕濟嚨城南境，受北來查母硃山一水，始曰牛楚必拉。 又東流，逕年爾母城北境，折而東南，又轉而東北流，會朋出藏布河。 薩喀境內有鹽池。 阿里東北九百餘里有達魯克池，隆布河與納鞠河皆入焉。
Tingri lies over seven hundred li southwest of Tashilhunpo. One garrison commandant is stationed there. It has a garrison post. The town faces the frontier on three sides; to the south is Rongxia, to the southwest Nyalam, to the west Jilong, and to the northwest Zongka. Southeast of Rongxia is Kada; southwest of Kada is Yangbu—both bordering Gorkha. South of Zongka is Buling, bordering Gorkha to the south and the Ladakh khanate to the north. To its northwest lies Saka; at the far northwestern frontier is Ali. All these places have camp officials. To the east: Chongwula Mountain and Jiacuo Mountain. To the southwest: Jianai Great Mountain. To the west: Tongla Mountain. West of Kada lies Xiawula Mountain. East of Zongka lies Gongtangla Mountain. Within Buling lies Gangdise Mountain, northeast of Dakela in Ali—some five thousand five hundred ninety li southwest of Xining Prefecture in Shaanxi as the crow flies. The peak rises over five hundred fifty zhang, with a circumference of more than one hundred forty li; its slopes are sheer on every side, standing more than a hundred zhang above surrounding ranges, snow-clad like hanging cliffs in vast, pure whiteness. A spring on the summit flows to the foot and then sinks underground. Surrounding peaks before and behind are all jagged cliffs and strange summits arrayed like an arch. By its terrain, beyond the southwestern frontier the land rises gradually until here it reaches its summit. The range winds onward; its northwestern branch forms Sengekabapu, Ganglimusun, and other peaks, circling Ali to the north and entering the southern Khotan ranges and Pamir peaks of the Western Regions. Toward the northeast run Zhabulie Xie'erchong, Jiaowu'erchong, Nianqiantangla, Samutangang, Zhanuomohunwubashi, Bayan Hara, and other ranges. It encircles the Wei region, reaches Qinghai, and extends downward for more than six thousand li to the Xining frontier in Shaanxi. Toward the southwest run Mennakeni'er, Samutaigang, and other ranges, spanning south of Ali into Enetke territory. Toward the southeast run Damuchukekabapugang, Ga'ershami, Nujingangcang, and other ranges through Zang, Wei, and eastern Kham to the borders of Yunnan and Sichuan. In Kangxi 56 the lamas Chuerqin Zangbulanmuzhanba and Lifanyuan director Shengzhu were dispatched to survey and map the Western Sea and Tibet; they identified this place as the backbone of the world, whence all mountain ranges arise. The Shui Jing Zhu records: "Mount Anotada has to the southwest a stream called Yaonu; slightly east of south of the mountain is a stream called Sahan; and slightly east is a stream called Hengjia. These three streams issue from one mountain and all enter the Ganges." Today Ali is the far southwestern corner of Tibet, near ancient India. From this mountain west flow the three great rivers Langchu, Lachu, and Machu, all westward, then east and south, merging as the Gangga and entering the Southern Sea. This is likely Mount Anotada. Also Damuzhukabapo Mountain, shaped like a horse. Langqian Kabapo Mountain is shaped like an elephant. Shengge Kabapo Mountain is shaped like a lion. Mapojia Kabapo Mountain is shaped like a peacock. All are connected to Gangdise Mountain. The Gangga rises at the northern foot of Langqian Kabapo; springs gather into a pool that flows northwest, joins a northeastern stream, then bends west and northeast; Gongsheng Pool's waters sink underground and re-emerge, join three northern streams flowing southwest, and form Mapinmudalai Pool. It flows west as Langga Pool, takes a northeastern stream, exits west, turns southwest as the Langchu, winding over two hundred li, where the Chugala joins from the northeast. It bends west, north, and northeast past west of Guge Zhaxi Lumubuzecheng and east of Zebulong, turns northwest then southwest past southwest of Zebulong, then northwest as the Lachu joins from the northwest. Once the three streams merge, it first takes the name Gangga. It flows southeast out of Ali, passes the Mamubazhamulang tribe, reaches Enetke, and enters the Southern Sea. The Pengchu Tsangpo north of Tingri flows southeast, takes the Jiechu and Longgang, and enters Dingjie. The Niuchu rises on Xilagangcan Mountain, flows southeast, joins Gangbu Heishan waters from the northeast, passes south of Jilong, takes a stream from Zhamuzhu Mountain in the north, and is first called Niuchubila. It flows east along the north of Nian'ermu, turns southeast, then northeast to join the Pengchu Tsangpo. Within Saka territory there is a salt lake. Some nine hundred li northeast of Ali lies Daluke Pool, into which the Longbu and Naju both flow.
