黃體芳，字漱蘭，浙江瑞安人。 同治二年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 日探討掌故，慨然有經世志。 累遷侍讀學士，頻上書言時政得失。 晉、豫飢，請籌急賑，整吏治，清庶獄，稱旨。 時議禁燒鍋裕民食，戶部覈駁，體芳謂燒鍋領帖，部獲歲銀三萬，因上董恂奸邪狀，坐鐫級。
Huang Tifang, whose courtesy name was Shulan, came from Ruian in Zhejiang Province. In 1863 he became a jinshi, entered the Hanlin Academy as a probationer, and received appointment as a compiler. He spent his days studying institutional history and records, and came to hold a strong desire to put his learning at the service of the state. Promoted in stages to Reader of the Hanlin Academy, he often memorialized the throne on what was right and wrong in contemporary governance. During famine in Shanxi and Henan he urged emergency relief, reform of local administration, and review of routine criminal cases, and the court approved his recommendations. When officials debated banning liquor stills to free grain for the people, the Board of Revenue objected. Tifang pointed out that licensed stills paid the Board thirty thousand taels a year; he then memorialized against Dong Xun's corruption and was punished with a one-grade demotion.
2
光緒五年三月，惠陵禮成，主事吳可讀為定大統以屍諫。 詔言：「同治十三年十二月初五日降旨，嗣後皇帝生有皇子，即承繼大行皇帝為嗣。 吳可讀所奏，前旨即是此意。 」於是下群臣議，體芳略言：「'即是此意'一語，止有恪遵，更有何議？ 乃激烈者盛氣力爭，巽畏者囁嚅不吐，或忠或謹，皆人臣盛節，而惜其未明今日事勢也。 譬諸士民之家，長子次子各有孫，而自祖父母視之則無異。 然襲爵職必歸之長房者，嫡長與嫡次之別也。 又如大宗無子，次宗止一嫡子，然小宗以嫡子繼大宗，不聞有所吝者，以仍得兼承本宗故也。 唯君與民微有不同。 民間以嫡子繼大宗，則大宗為主，本宗為兼。 天潢以嫡子繼帝系，則帝係為主，本宗可得而兼，親不可得而兼。 若人君以嫡子繼長支，則固以繼長支為主，而本宗亦不能不兼。 蓋人君無小宗，即稱謂加以區別，亦於本宗恩義無傷。 此兩宮意在嗣子承統，慈愛穆宗，亦即所以慈愛皇上之說也。 今非合兩統為一統，以不定為豫定，就將來承繼者以為承嗣，似亦無策以處之矣。 試思此時即不專為穆宗計，既正名為先帝嗣子，豈有僅封一王貝勒者乎？ 即不專為皇上計，古來天子之嗣子，豈有以不主神器之諸皇子當之者乎？ 即僅為穆宗計，皇上可如民間出繼之子乎？ 即僅為皇上計，穆宗可如前明稱為皇伯考乎？ 夫奉祖訓，禀懿旨，體聖意，非僭。 先帝今上皆無不宜，非悖。 明其統而非其人，非擅。 論統系，辨宗法，正足見國家億萬年無疆之庥，非干犯忌諱。 此固無意氣可逞，亦無功罪可言也。 」疏入，詔存毓慶宮。 自是劾尚書賀壽慈飾奏，俄使崇厚誤國，洪鈞譯地圖舛謬，美使崔國英赴賽會失體，皆人所難言，直聲震中外。
In March 1879, after the burial rites for Emperor Tongzhi were finished, Principal Clerk Wu Kedu used his own death as a memorial to demand a settled line of succession. The throne proclaimed: "By the edict of 5 January 1875, whenever the reigning emperor should have a son, that son is to succeed the late emperor as his heir." Wu Kedu's petition expresses exactly what the prior edict intended. The court then referred the matter to the ministers. Tifang wrote in summary: "The words 'that is the meaning' allow only reverent obedience—what more is there to argue?" The impassioned fought with fierce zeal, while the timid stammered and held back; whether loyal or cautious, each showed a subject's highest integrity, yet none quite grasped the situation of the day. Think of an ordinary family: the eldest and second sons each have grandsons, yet to the grandparents they are all the same. Yet rank and hereditary office must go to the senior branch—that is the difference between the eldest legitimate son and the next legitimate son. If the main line has no heir while a collateral line has a single legitimate son, that son may succeed the main line without objection, since he can still carry on his birth line as well. Between sovereign and subject, however, the case differs slightly. In private life, when a legitimate son succeeds a main line, the main line is primary and the birth line secondary. When the imperial house continues the throne through a legitimate son, the imperial succession is primary: the birth line may be combined with it, but the tie of parent and child cannot be combined. If the sovereign continues the senior branch through a legitimate son, continuing that branch is primary, yet the birth line cannot fail to be joined as well. The sovereign has no minor lineage as such; even if titles are distinguished in address, the moral claims of the birth line remain intact. The two palaces meant that the heir should carry on the succession, cherishing Emperor Tongzhi—and in doing so cherishing the present emperor as well. Unless the two successions are merged into one, the unsettled treated as foreordained, and the future heir regarded as already the heir, there appears to be no workable policy. Even setting Tongzhi aside, once the heir is formally named the late emperor's son, could he be enfeoffed merely as a prince or beile? Even setting the present emperor aside, when has the Son of Heaven's heir ever been anyone other than the one destined for the throne? Even for Tongzhi alone, could the present emperor be treated like a son given out for adoption in a private family? Even for the present emperor alone, could Tongzhi be addressed as the Ming styled an emperor's uncle by birth? To follow ancestral teaching, obey the empress dowager's edict, and embody the emperor's intent is not overreaching. Neither the late emperor nor the present one is slighted; this is not disloyalty. To clarify the line of succession rather than the person is not usurpation. To discuss the succession and clarify clan law is precisely to show the state's blessing for ages to come; it is not to breach taboo. Here there is no room for personal temper, nor any merit or blame to claim. When the memorial was received, the court ordered it filed in the Palace of Nurturing Joy. Afterward he impeached Minister He Shouci for dressing up reports, the Russian envoy Chonghou for betraying the nation, Hong Jun for mistakes in map translation, and the American envoy Cui Guoying for impropriety at an exhibition—matters others would not touch; his blunt reputation resounded at home and abroad.

七年，遷內閣學士，督江蘇學政。 明年，授兵部左侍郎。 中法事起，建索還琉球、經畫越南議。 十一年，還京，劾李鴻章治兵無效，請敕曾紀澤遄歸練師，忤旨，左遷通政使。 兩署左副都御史，奏言自強之本在內治，又歷陳中外交涉得失，後卒如所言。 十七年，乞休。 二十五年，卒。 子紹箕、紹第，並能承家學，而紹箕尤贍雅。
In 1881 he was made Grand Secretary and appointed educational commissioner for Jiangsu. The following year he became Left Vice Minister of War. When war with France broke out, he urged the recovery of Ryukyu and a settled policy toward Vietnam. In 1885 he returned to Beijing, impeached Li Hongzhang for failing to manage the army, and asked that Zeng Jize be recalled immediately to train forces; the court took offense and demoted him to Commissioner of the Court of Transmission. Twice acting Left Vice Censor-in-Chief, he argued that self-strengthening rests on internal order and repeatedly analyzed foreign affairs; later events bore him out. In 1891 he asked to retire. He died in 1899. His sons Shaoji and Shaodi both inherited the family scholarship, and Shaoji was especially refined and generous.
4
紹箕，字仲弢。 光緒六年進士，以編修典試湖北。 晉侍講，擢庶子。 京師立大學堂，充總辦。 究心東西邦學制，手訂章條。 遷侍讀學士。 歷充編書局、譯書局監督。 出為湖北提學使。 東渡日本，與其邦人士論孔教、輒心折。 歸，未幾，卒。
Shaoji, whose courtesy name was Zhongtao. In 1880 he became a jinshi and, while serving as a compiler, supervised the provincial examination in Hubei. He rose to Reader and was then appointed Junior Mentor. When the Imperial University was founded in Beijing, he served as its chief administrator. He studied Eastern and Western school systems in depth and personally drafted the university regulations. He was promoted to Reader of the Hanlin Academy. He served in turn as supervisor of the Compilation Bureau and the Translation Bureau. He was appointed Educational Commissioner of Hubei. In Japan he debated Confucian doctrine with local scholars and was often deeply impressed by them. Shortly after his return he died.
5
宗室寶廷，字竹坡，隸滿洲鑲藍旗，鄭獻親王濟爾哈朗八世孫。 同治七年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 累遷侍讀。 光緒改元，疏請選師保以崇聖德，嚴宦寺以杜干預，覈實內務府以節糜費，訓練神機營以備緩急，懿旨嘉納。 大考三等，降中允，尋授司業。 是時朝廷方銳意求治，詔詢吏治民生用人行政，寶廷力抉其弊，諤諤數百言，至切直。 晉、豫飢，應詔陳言，請罪己，並責臣工。 條上救荒四事，曰：察釐稅，開糧捐，購洋米，增糶局。 復以災廣賑劇，請行分貸法。 畿輔旱，日色赤，市言訛駴，建議內嚴防範，外示鎮定，以安人心。 歷遷侍講學士，以六事進，曰：明黜陟，專責任，詳考詢，嚴程限，去欺矇，慎赦宥，稱旨。 五年，轉侍讀學士。
Baoting of the imperial clan, courtesy name Zhupo, was of the Manchu Bordered Blue Banner and eighth in descent from Prince Jirhalang of Zheng. In 1868 he became a jinshi, entered the Hanlin Academy, and was appointed compiler. He rose in stages to Reader. At the beginning of Guangxu he urged choosing tutors to elevate the emperor's moral cultivation, tightening control over eunuchs, auditing the Imperial Household Department, and drilling the Shenji Camp; the empress dowager approved his recommendations. In the palace examination he placed third class, was demoted to Junior Mentor, and soon received appointment as Vice Director of Education. The court was then eager for reform and asked about administration, livelihood, appointments, and policy; Baoting exposed abuses in several hundred blunt words, with utmost frankness. During famine in Shanxi and Henan he answered the imperial call by asking the emperor to reproach himself and to hold his ministers accountable. He proposed four relief measures: audit tax collection, open grain-donation drives, purchase foreign rice, and expand government grain sales. As the disaster was widespread and relief burdens heavy, he also urged a system of divided loans. Drought in the metropolitan region turned the sun reddish and rumors panicked the markets; he urged tight internal precautions and outward calm to steady public feeling. Rising to Reader of the Hanlin Academy, he submitted six reforms—clarify promotion and demotion, fix responsibility, conduct thorough inquiry, enforce deadlines, remove deceit, and be cautious with amnesties—and won imperial approval. In 1879 he was made Reader of the Hanlin Academy.

初，德宗繼統嗣文宗，懿旨謂將來生有皇子，即繼穆宗為嗣。 內閣侍讀學士廣安請頒鐵券，被訶責。 至是，穆宗奉安惠陵，主事吳可讀堅請為其立後，以屍諫，下廷臣議。 寶廷謂：「恭繹懿旨之意，蓋言穆宗未有儲貳，即以皇上所生之子為嗣，非言生皇子即時承繼也，言嗣而統賅焉矣。 引伸之，蓋言將來即以皇上傳統之皇子繼穆宗為嗣也。 因皇上甫承大統，故渾涵其詞，留待親政日自下明詔，此皇太后不忍歧視之慈心，欲以孝弟仁讓之休歸之皇上也。 廣安不能喻，故生爭於前； 吳可讀不能喻，故死爭於後。 竊痛可讀殉死之忠，而又惜其遺摺之言不盡意也。 可讀未喻懿旨言外之意，而其遺摺未達之意，皇太后早鑑及之，故曰'前降旨時即是此意'也。 而可讀猶以忠佞不齊為慮，誠過慮也。 宋太宗背杜太后，明景帝廢太子見深，雖因佞臣妄進邪說，究由二君有自私之心。 乃者兩宮懿旨懸於上，孤臣遺疏存於下，傳之九州，載之國史，皇上天生聖人，必能以皇太后之心為心。 請將前後懿旨恭呈御覽，明降諭旨，宣示中外，俾天下後世咸知我皇太后至慈，皇上至孝至弟至仁至讓，且以見穆宗至聖至明，付託得人也。 如是，則綱紀正，名分定，天理順，人情安矣。 因赴內閣集議，意微不合，謹以上聞。 」
Earlier, Emperor Guangxu had succeeded as heir to Emperor Xianfeng; the empress dowager's edict held that when the emperor should later have a son, that son would succeed Emperor Tongzhi. Hanlin Reader Guang An petitioned for an iron certificate of assurance and was reprimanded. Now, with Tongzhi's burial at Huiling complete, Wu Kedu demanded an heir for him and used his death as a memorial; the court ordered the ministers to deliberate. Baoting wrote: "Reading the empress dowager's edict reverently, it means that because Tongzhi had no heir, the emperor's own son should be his heir—not that every newborn prince succeeds immediately; 'heir' already embraces the succession." Taken further, it means that the emperor's son who will carry on the throne shall in time succeed Tongzhi as heir. Because the emperor had only just succeeded, the language was deliberately broad, to await the day he took personal rule and issued a clear edict— the empress dowager's compassion unwilling to divide affections, wishing to leave the honor of filial piety, brotherhood, benevolence, and yielding to the emperor. Guang An failed to understand and quarreled in life; Wu Kedu failed to understand and contended in death. I deeply honor Wu Kedu's martyrdom, yet regret that his final memorial did not fully express his intent. Wu Kedu missed the meaning beyond the edict's words, yet what his memorial left unsaid the empress dowager had long seen; hence "the earlier edict already meant this." Yet Wu Kedu still feared loyal and wicked ministers would not stand together—a needless anxiety. Song Taizong defied Empress Dowager Du, and Ming Jingdi deposed the heir Zhu Jianshen; though flatterers urged evil counsel, both emperors in the end acted from selfish motives. The two palaces' edicts hang above and the lone minister's last memorial below, spread through the realm and written into history; the emperor is a born sage and will take the empress dowager's heart as his own. I ask that the earlier and later edicts be presented for review, that a clear edict be proclaimed at home and abroad, so that all ages may know the empress dowager's utmost kindness and the emperor's utmost filial piety, brotherhood, benevolence, and yielding—and see Tongzhi's sagacity in choosing the right successor. Then ritual order is right, titles fixed, principle aligned, and feeling at peace. Finding slight disagreement at the Grand Council deliberation, I respectfully report the above. End of memorial quotation.

又奏：「廷臣謂穆宗繼統之議，已賅於皇太后前降懿旨之中，將來神器所歸，皇上自能斟酌盡善，固也。 然懿旨意深詞簡，不及此引伸明晰，異日皇上生有皇子，將繼穆宗為嗣乎，抑不即繼乎？ 不即繼似違懿旨，即繼又嫌跡近建儲。 就令僅言繼嗣，不標繼統之名，而臣民亦隱以儲貳視之，是不建之建也。 而此皇子賢也，固宗社福； 如其不賢，將來仍傳繼統乎，抑舍而別傳乎？ 別傳之皇子，仍繼穆宗為嗣乎，抑不繼乎？ 即使仍繼穆宗，是亦不廢立之廢立也，豈太平盛事乎？ 至此時即欲皇上斟酌盡善，不亦難乎？ 廷議之意，或以皇上親政，皇子應尚未生，不難豫酌一盡善之規。 然國君十五而生子，皇子誕育如在徹簾之前，又何以處之乎？ 與其留此兩難之局以待皇上，何如及今斟酌盡善乎？ 且懿旨非皇上可改，此時不引伸明晰，將來皇上雖斟酌盡善，何敢自為變通乎？ 此未妥者一也。 廷議又謂繼統與建儲，文義似殊，而事體則一，似也。 然列聖垂訓，原言嗣統之常，今則事屬創局，可讀意在存穆宗之統，與無故擅請建儲者有間，文義之殊，不待言矣。 今廷議不分別詞意，漫謂我朝家法未能深知，則日前懿旨'即是此意'之謂何，臣民不更滋疑乎？ 此未妥者又一也。 」疏入，詔藏毓慶宮。 其他，俄使來議約，朝鮮請通商，均有所獻納。
He also wrote: "Ministers say Tongzhi's succession is already covered by the empress dowager's edict, and that in future the throne will go where the emperor can weigh all and choose wisely—so they hold." Yet the edict is deep in meaning and brief in wording; without this clarification, when the emperor later has a son, will that son succeed Tongzhi at once or not? Not succeeding at once seems to breach the edict; succeeding at once looks too like naming an heir apparent. Even if one speaks only of heirship without naming succession to the line, officials and people will still treat him as heir apparent—an heir apparent established without being established. If that prince proves worthy, the realm is blessed; if unworthy, will the succession still pass to him, or be set aside for another line? Would a prince from another line still succeed Tongzhi, or not? Even if he still succeeded Tongzhi, that would be deposition and installation without calling it so—what great peace is that? By then, even if the emperor wished to weigh all and choose wisely, would it not be hard? Court opinion seems to assume that when the emperor takes personal rule no prince will yet be born, so a perfect plan can easily be made in advance. Yet rulers may father sons at fifteen; if a prince is born before the regency ends, what then? Rather than leave this dilemma for the emperor, why not settle it wisely now? Moreover the edict is not the emperor's to alter; without clarifying it now, how could he later dare change it even with the best judgment? This is the first unsettled point. Court opinion also says succession to the line and establishing an heir differ in wording but are one in substance—or so it seems. Yet successive emperors taught the regular rule of heir and succession; today the case is unprecedented. Wu Kedu meant to preserve Tongzhi's line, unlike those who rashly demand an heir— the verbal difference needs no argument. Now the debate ignores the distinction and vaguely claims our family law is not understood—then what of the edict's "that is the meaning"? Will not doubt only deepen? This is the second unsettled point. When the memorial was received, the court ordered it filed in the Palace of Nurturing Joy. On other matters—the Russian envoy's treaty talks and Korea's request for trade—he likewise offered recommendations.

七年，授內閣學士，出典福建鄉試。 既蕆事，還朝，以在途納妾自劾罷，築室西山，往居之。 是冬，皇太后萬壽祝嘏，賞三品秩。 十六年，卒。
In 1881 he was made Grand Secretary and sent to supervise the Fujian provincial examination. After completing the examination he returned to the capital, resigned on his own motion for having taken a concubine en route, built a house on the Western Hills, and retired there. That winter, on the empress dowager's birthday celebration, he was granted third-rank standing. He died in 1890.

宗室盛昱，字伯熙，隸滿洲鑲白旗，肅武親王豪格七世孫。 祖敬徵，協辦大學士。 父恆恩，左副都御史。 盛昱少慧，十歲時作詩用「特勤」字，據唐闕特勤碑證新唐書突厥「純特勒」為「特勤」之誤，繇是顯名。 光緒二年進士，既，授編修，益厲學，討測經史、輿地及本朝掌故，皆能詳其沿革。 累遷右庶子，充日講起居注官。
Shengyu of the imperial clan, courtesy name Boxi, was of the Manchu Bordered White Banner and seventh in descent from Prince Hooge of Suwu. His grandfather Jingzheng served as Grand Councilor Assistant. His father Hengen was Left Vice Censor-in-Chief. Shengyu was precocious; at ten he used the term Teqin in verse and, citing the Tang Que Teqin stele, showed the New Tang History's Turkic Chun Tele was a mistake for Teqin, and won renown. In 1876 he became a jinshi; appointed compiler, he threw himself into study of the classics, history, geography, and Qing institutions, tracing each in detail. He rose to Right Junior Mentor and served as Daily Lecturer and Recorder.
11
閩浙總督何璟、巡撫劉秉璋收降台匪黃金滿，盛昱劾璟等長惡養奸，請下吏嚴議，發金滿黑龍江、新疆安置。 尚書彭玉麟數辭官不受職，劾其自便身圖，啟功臣驕蹇之漸。 浙江按察使陳寶箴陛見未行，追論官河南聽獄不慎，罷免； 張佩綸劾其留京幹進，寶箴疏辯，盛昱言其嘵嘵失大臣體，請再下吏議。 朝鮮之亂也，提督吳長慶奉北洋大臣張樹聲檄，率師入朝，執大院君李罡應以歸，時詫為奇勳。 盛昱言：「出自誘劫，不足言功，徒令屬國寒心，友邦騰笑。 宜嚴予處分，俾中外知非朝廷本意。 」為講官未半載，數言事，士論推為謇諤。
When Governors He Jing and Liu Bingzhang accepted the surrender of Taiwan bandit Huang Jinman, Shengyu impeached them for harboring evil and urged severe punishment; Jinman was exiled to Heilongjiang and Xinjiang. When Minister Peng Yulin repeatedly refused office, Shengyu impeached him for self-convenience and encouraging the arrogance of meritorious officials. Before Zhejiang Surveillance Commissioner Chen Baozhen could depart for his audience, Shengyu revived charges of mishandled cases in Henan and secured his dismissal; Zhang Peilun impeached him for lingering in the capital to seek advancement; when Chen defended himself, Shengyu said his clamor lacked a senior minister's dignity and asked a second inquiry. During the Korean crisis, Brigadier Wu Changqing entered Korea under Zhang Shusheng's orders and brought back Grand Prince Li Gangying, then hailed as a brilliant exploit. Shengyu wrote: "This came of enticement and abduction, not merit; it only chills our tributary and makes foreign powers laugh." Strict punishment should follow so all may know this was not the court's intent." As lecturer less than half a year, he spoke often on public affairs; opinion ranked him among the blunt remonstrators.

In 1884 he was made Director of the Imperial Academy. When France and Vietnam clashed, Xu Yanxu and Tang Jiong were arrested for losing territory; Shengyu said: "To seize frontier ministers without a clear edict has no precedent in two hundred years." He also impeached the Grand Council for neglect. The empress dowager dismissed Prince Gong Yixin and others and put Prince Chun Yixuan on the Grand Council; Shengyu urged that Prince Chun's exalted standing should not be burdened with routine state affairs. That summer, when ministers were ordered to debate war and peace, Shengyu urged swift war and listed seven advantages, warning: "Miss the moment again and regret will be useless." 13
盛昱為祭酒，與司業治麟究心教士之法，大治學舍，加膏火，定積分日程，懲遊惰，獎樸學，士習為之一變。 十四年，典試山東。 明年，引疾歸。 盛昱家居有清譽，承學之士以得接言論風采為幸。 二十五年，卒。
As Director, Shengyu and Vice Director Zhi Lin reformed teaching, improved the academy, increased stipends, fixed credit schedules, punished idleness, rewarded solid learning, and student habits changed markedly. In 1888 he supervised the Shandong provincial examination. The following year he retired on grounds of illness. In retirement he kept a reputation for integrity; scholars prized contact with his conversation and bearing. He died in 1899.

Zhang Peilun, courtesy name Youqiao, came from Fengrun in Zhili. His father Yintang was Anhui Surveillance Commissioner and died on campaign. Peilun became a jinshi in 1871, rose from compiler to Reader after the palace examination, and served as Daily Lecturer and Recorder. As foreign pressure mounted, he repeatedly memorialized on statecraft, urging strict defense of Xinjiang, the Northeast, and Taiwan against Japanese and Russian designs. During famine in Shanxi and Henan and drought around the capital, he cited ancestral precedent and urged court and realm to take warning, proposing sincere prayer, collective deliberation, cherishing the people, and reducing punishments. When Prince Gong Yixin fell victim to slander, Peilun asked that he be charged to serve loyally with full responsibility; the throne approved. When Huang Tifang described the disaster in heated language and faced official inquiry, Peilun argued forcefully and he was pardoned. He soon entered mourning; when it ended he returned to his former post. Ryukyu had fallen and France was pressing Vietnam; Peilun warned: "Lose Ryukyu and Korea is endangered; abandon Vietnam and Burma will follow." He urged coastal defenses north and south and four great naval commands; he also recommended Xu Yanxu and Tang Jiong as knowing war and fit for the frontier, and as having enlisted Liu Yongfu's Black Flag Army. Wu Dacheng, Chen Baochen, and others then debated policy; with Baoting and Deng Chengxiu they were called the Pure Stream Party, while Peilun was especially famed for impeaching senior ministers. Vice Minister He Shouci and Ministers Wan Qingli and Dong Xun were among those impeached and driven out.

In 1882 the Yunnan accounts scandal arose; Wang Wenshao sat on the Grand Council while heading the Board of Revenue; censors charged bribery though the throne trusted him deeply; citing Qianlong's Liang Shizheng precedent they asked him to withdraw for conflict of interest—without success; after two further impeachments Wenshao was dismissed and Peilun was made acting Left Vice Censor-in-Chief and Reader of the Hanlin Academy. The next year, as France and Vietnam clashed, Peilun submitted more than ten memorials before the court sent troops against local rebels to check the French; the French, finding this inconvenient, feigned peace while secretly taking Nanding. Peilun urged striking before French forces gathered and sending Guangdong troops south; the Grand Council clung to peace, feared his obstruction, and sent him to inspect Shaanxi. France soon attacked Shunhua and forced Vietnam into alliance; the situation worsened. On his return he was ordered to serve in the Zongli Yamen.

十年，法人聲內犯，佩綸謂越難未已，黑旗猶存，萬無分兵東來理，請毋罷戍啟戎心，上韙之。 詔就李鴻章議，遂決戰，令以三品卿銜會辦福建海疆事。 佩綸至船廠，環十一艘自衛，各管帶白非計，斥之。 法艦集，戰書至，眾聞警，謁佩綸亟請備，仍叱出。 比見法艦升火，始大怖，遣學生魏瀚往乞緩，未至而砲聲作，所部五營潰，其三營殲焉。 佩綸遁鼓山麓，鄉人拒之，曰：「我會辦大臣也！ 」拒如初。 翼日，逃至彭田鄉，猶飾詞入告，朝旨發帑犒之，命兼船政。 嗣聞馬尾敗，止奪卿銜，下吏議。 閩人憤甚，於是編修潘炳年、給事中萬培因等先後上其罪狀。 時已坐薦唐尚、徐延旭褫職，至是再論戍。
In 1884, as the French threatened inland attack, Peilun argued Vietnam was unsettled and the Black Flags remained, so they would not divide forces eastward, and urged keeping garrisons; the throne agreed. The court consulted Li Hongzhang and chose war; Peilun received third-rank ministerial rank to oversee Fujian coastal affairs. At the shipyard Peilun surrounded himself with eleven vessels; when commanders said this was not the plan, he rebuked them. When the French fleet gathered and sent a challenge, men rushed to urge preparation; he still drove them away. Only when French ships raised steam did panic set in; he sent student Wei Han to beg delay, but before arrival the guns fired, five camps collapsed, and three were annihilated. Peilun fled to Drum Mountain; villagers refused him, saying: "I am the minister in charge!" They still refused him. The next day he fled to Pengtian village and still dressed up his reports; the court sent treasury funds to comfort him and added ship administration to his duties. When the Mawei defeat became known, only his ministerial rank was stripped and he was referred for judgment. Fujian was furious; Compiler Pan Bingnian, Censor Wan Peiyin, and others memorialized his crimes. He had already lost office for recommending Tang Jiong and Xu Yanxu; now he was sentenced to banishment again.

Released from frontier exile, Li Hongzhang took him into his staff again and gave him his daughter in marriage. When the Sino-Japanese War broke out, Censor Duan Liang impeached him for meddling in public affairs and he was ordered home. At the Boxer settlement Li Hongzhang recommended him for negotiations and the court ordered him to help draft the treaty. When it was done he was promoted to a fourth- or fifth-rank capital post but pleaded illness and did not serve. He died in 1908.

He Ruguan, courtesy name Zijin, was registered in Dapu, Guangdong. In 1868 he became a jinshi, entered the Hanlin Academy, and was appointed compiler. As Reader he was dispatched as envoy to Japan. On return he was made Junior Vice Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and sent to supervise the shipyard. Following Li Hongzhang, he clung to peace; when the enemy came he still ordered each ship not to act rashly. After defeat he claimed he was escorting silver and fled; nowhere would admit him, and he had to seek Peilun at Pengtian village. Peilun feared the enemy would follow and tricked Ruguan into leaving. Opinion held Peilun the chief culprit in Fujian's disaster and Ruguan second. Ruguan was also banished. He later died at home.

Deng Chengxiu, courtesy name Tiexiang, came from Guishan in Guangdong. In 1861 he passed the provincial examination, bought office as a secretary, and was assigned to the Board of Punishments. He became a censor and returned home on mourning. Early in Guangxu, when mourning ended, he resumed his former post. With Zhang Peilun and others he led the Pure Stream faction, impeaching often, and was called the Iron Han. He memorialized against surname lotteries and gambling contributions in examinations as grave abuses; against customs erosion harming the treasury; on examination abuses, listing seven reforms; and on official abuses, listing eight purifications. He also impeached Governor Li Hanzhang, Left Vice Censor Chongxun, Vice Minister Chang Xu, educational commissioners Wu Baoshu and Ye Dachao, administration commissioners Fang Dashen and Gong Yitu, and salt commissioner Zhou Xingyu for misconduct or incompetence. When frontier alarms arose he impeached the court's slackness and asked that Zuo Zongtang be recalled to govern. A year later, when a comet appeared, he said Zongtang had achieved nothing in months and extended the charge to Baojun and Wang Wenshao's incompetence, asking dismissal to appease heaven. Wenshao was then rising in favor; when the Yunnan accounts case arose he impeached him again, still without success. After long service he was made Supervising Secretary.

When Korea was pacified but Ryukyu unsettled, he urged a military minister at Yantai, strong naval patrols, and Wu Changqing's army kept in Korea as a check. When Vietnam erupted and France took Shunhua, he again asked a court debate on national policy—all unanswered. In 1884, as Vietnam worsened, he first impeached Xu Yanxu, Tang Jiong, Zhao Wo, and Huang Guilan for losing territory and bungling command, urging punishment under state law. That summer, as France feigned peace, Chengxiu joined censors in warning that treaties could not be trusted. Soon he and Vice Director Pan Yantong secretly submitted five stratagems against the enemy and impeached Li Hongzhang's peace memorial, resenting Liu Yongfu's willingness to fight, with burning indignation. France soon broke the treaty and attacked Jilong in Taiwan; the Grand Council could not decide between war and peace. Chengxiu then offered three stratagems: "France relies on Saigon and Tokyo for support." If our armies strike Vietnam in three columns, they will be too busy to defend themselves—the best plan." Hold divided forces, fight on contact but do not pursue, aging the army and wasting supplies while sharing risk—that is the middle plan." If fear of funds and transport blocks talk of war, the harm is beyond words—that is no plan at all." He was appointed Minister of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and served in the Zongli Yamen. Thereafter he memorialized on military affairs thirteen times, many of which the court adopted. When Junior Mentor Fan Gongxu was punished, he memorialized in his defense and was demoted one grade. The next year he went to Tianjin to help Li Hongzhang negotiate with the French envoy Patenotre and fix ten articles of a new treaty. On return he asked leave to visit his family.

In 1886 the French sent Dillon and Disère separately to negotiate. Just then Judge Daruignies, who had reached Zhelan, was killed by Vietnamese. The French envoys were afraid and ashamed and hid the matter; they insisted on demarcating by map, and the court agreed. They first proposed ceding Jiangping, Huangzhu, Bailongwei, and other places to Vietnam. Chengxiu cited maps and fought back; the envoys could not prevail and proposed splitting Bailongwei, left to China and right to Vietnam. Chengxiu said the place was Qinzhou's outer gateway; French possession would press Fangcheng inward and sever Dongxing and Sile—Qin and Lian would be lost. Xu Zhixiang, courtesy name Jihe, came from Jiading in Jiangsu. In 1860 he became a jinshi, entered the Hanlin Academy, and was appointed compiler. He rose to Junior Mentor and supervised the Shandong provincial examination. He rose to Grand Secretary and served as educational commissioner for Shuntian. He left on mourning; when it ended he returned to his former post. In 1884, as France and Vietnam fought, Detring urged peace while the court hesitated. Zhixiang offered three policies: fight swiftly, give generals sole command, and unite military strength. When Fujian was in crisis he said He Jing and Zhang Zhaodong lacked ability but recommended Yang Yuebin and Zhang Peilun for heavy duties; the court largely approved. When railways were debated he denounced them, listed eight harms, urged rejecting heterodox schemes and repairing rivers; the throne called him absurd and demoted him three grades. Two years later, when railways were debated again, he blocked them again. He submitted more than ten sealed memorials, earnestly urging restraint of eunuchs and river repair—praised by contemporaries. He supervised the Fujian and Guangdong provincial examinations in turn. In 1892 he became Minister of the Court of Judicial Review, impeaching Grand Councilor Prince Shiduo and Shanxi Governor Akedachun, and attacking Zhang Zhidong without relent. Soon he was ordered to inspect education in Zhejiang and gained a reputation for severity.
24
中日之役，我師敗績，上奕劻、李鴻章誤國狀，請逮葉志超、衛汝貴等寘之法，而畀馮子材、劉永福以征討名號，庶可振國威、作士氣。 會山東教案起，德使海靖勒罷李秉衡職。 致祥曰：「昔歲罷劉秉璋，今茲罷李秉衡，是朝廷黜陟之大權操之敵人也。 為請顧全國體，毋懾敵。 」私念國是不振，亂未有已，乃援引聖祖篤信硃子垂為家法往事，請舉行經筵以輔聖德，皆不報。 秩滿，還朝，遷兵部右侍郎。 二十四年，上違豫，眾情驚疑，復以輔導君德之說進。
After defeat in the Sino-Japanese War he exposed Yikuang and Li Hongzhang's harm to the state, urged arrest of Ye Zhichao and Wei Rugui, and titles for Feng Zicai and Liu Yongfu to revive prestige and morale. When the Shandong missionary case arose, German envoy Heinke forced Li Bingheng's dismissal. Zhixiang said: "Last year Liu Bingzhang was dismissed; this year Li Bingheng—great appointment power now rests with the enemy." He asked that national dignity be preserved and the court not fear the enemy." Privately he thought policy was failing and disorder unending; citing Kangxi's devotion to Zhu Xi as family law, he asked for classics lectures to aid the emperor's virtue—all unanswered. When his term ended he returned and was made Right Vice Minister of War. In 1898, when the emperor fell ill and public alarm grew, he again urged guidance of the ruler's virtue.
25
是時國家多故，聖嗣尚虛，致祥為重國本計，略言：「昔宋真宗取宗室子養之宮中，逮仁宗既生，即遣歸邸； 厥後仁宗、高宗、理宗皆踵行之。 有子而遣養子歸邸者，真宗是也。 無子而即以養子傳授神器者，仁宗之於英宗，高宗之於孝宗，理宗之於度宗是也。 今以宗社系託之重，臣民屬望之切，深維至計，取則前朝，慎選近支宗室兄弟之子數人，擇親擇賢，入侍禁中，止以為子，不以為儲，恪遵家法，既可默察其賢否，徐以俟皇子之生。 則皇上未有子而有子，皇太后未有孫而有孫，而穆宗付託之大業，亦繼承有屬矣。 」乃未幾，果有立溥俊為大阿哥事。 二十五年，卒。
The realm was troubled and the heir still vacant; Zhixiang, weighing the foundation of state, wrote: "Song Zhenzong once reared imperial clansmen in the palace; when Renzong was born they were sent home;" Later Renzong, Gaozong, and Lizong all followed the practice." Sending adopted sons home once a natural son was born—that was Zhenzong's case." Transmitting the throne to an adopted son when there was no heir—that was Renzong to Yingzong, Gaozong to Xiaozong, Lizong to Duzong." Given the weight of the succession and public expectation, he urged choosing several worthy sons of close imperial clansmen to enter the palace as foster sons but not as heir apparent, per family law, until an imperial son should be born." Thus the emperor would have sons in name, the empress dowager grandsons in name, and Tongzhi's entrusted succession would be secured." Before long, indeed, Pujun was established as Great Heir. He died in 1899.
26
論曰：體芳、寶廷、佩綸與張之洞，時稱翰林四諫，有大政事，必具疏論是非，與同時好言事者，又號「清流黨」。 然體芳、寶廷議承大統，惓惓忠愛，非佩綸等所能及也。 承修以搏擊為能，致祥以誕妄受責，君子譏之。 唯盛昱言不妄發，潔身早退，庶超然無負清譽歟？
The historian notes: Tifang, Baoting, Peilun, and Zhang Zhidong were called the Four Hanlin Remonstrators; on great affairs they memorialized on right and wrong, and with like-minded colleagues were called the Pure Stream Party. Yet in debating the imperial succession Tifang and Baoting showed a loyal devotion Peilun and others could not match. Chengxiu excelled at attack; Zhixiang was punished for absurdity—men of judgment mocked both. Only Shengyu spoke cautiously, lived cleanly, and retired early—perhaps he alone did not betray his reputation for integrity.
