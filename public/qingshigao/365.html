覺羅寶興宗室敬徵宗室禧恩陳官俊卓秉恬
Jueluo Baoxing, Zongshi Jingzheng, Zongshi Xi'en, Chen Guanjun, and Zhuo Bingtian
3
覺羅寶興，字獻山，隸鑲黃旗。 嘉慶十五年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 累遷少詹事，入直上書房。 十八年，仁宗幸熱河，林清逆黨突入禁城，寶興散直，至東華門與賊遇，急入告警。 宣宗方在上書房，聞警戒備，賊不得逞。 上還京，擢寶興內閣學士。 十九年，授禮部侍郎。 以事忤旨，詔斥寶興不學，降大理寺卿，罷直書房。 复坐部刊科場條例誤「高宗」為「高祖」，降二級調用。 尋予三等侍衛，充吐魯番領隊大臣。
Jueluo Baoxing, whose style name was Xianshan, belonged to the Bordered Yellow Banner. He passed the civil examinations as a jinshi in the fifteenth year of the Jiaqing reign (1810), entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor, and was appointed a compiler. He rose through successive promotions to Junior Chamberlain for Ceremonials and joined the staff of the Southern Study. In 1813, while Emperor Jiaqing was at Rehe, rebels loyal to Lin Qing stormed the Forbidden City. Baoxing was off duty when he reached the Donghua Gate, met the attackers, and rushed inside to raise the alarm. The future Daoguang Emperor was in the Upper Study; hearing the alarm, he ordered the guards to stand ready, and the rebels were unable to carry out their plot. After the emperor returned to Beijing, Baoxing was promoted to Grand Secretary of the Grand Secretariat. In 1814 he was appointed Vice Minister of Rites. After he gave offence on a matter of policy, an edict denounced Baoxing as unlearned, demoted him to President of the Court of Judicial Review, and stripped him of his posting in the Southern Study. He was later punished again when his ministry's published examination regulations mistook "Gaozong" for "Gaozu," and he was demoted two ranks and reassigned. He was soon given the rank of third-class bodyguard and sent to Turfan as leader of the imperial detachment.
4
道光二年，召為大理寺少卿。 復因事降通政司參議，歷左副都御史、兵部侍郎，出為泰宁鎮總兵。 八年，授理籓院侍郎，調兵部。 迭命偕戶部尚書王鼎察治長蘆、兩淮鹽務，籌議整頓，詳王鼎傳。 十年，出為吉林將軍，疏言：「松花江西岸、輝發河北岸舊例封禁，其餘閒曠山場均設卡倫，惟許兵丁打捕牲畜，以備貢品。 民人無照，私出挖蓡斫木者，查拏治罪。 」又言：「伯都訥珠爾山荒田先後開墾五千二百六十二晌，其租息請自道光十五年為始，以其半分賞兵丁，半存備報修工程。 此外尚有可墾荒地五萬六千餘晌，作為官荒，將來奏請招佃徵租。 烏拉涼水泉已墾七萬三千九百餘晌，請撥二道河東二萬晌，以七成給烏拉總管衙門，三成給協領衙門，資為津貼。 餘未墾地五萬三千餘晌，亦作官荒。 」並從之。 調盛京，又調成都。
In 1822 he was recalled to serve as Vice President of the Court of Judicial Review. He was demoted again on another charge to counselor in the Office of Transmission, then served as Left Vice Censor-in-Chief and Vice Minister of War before being posted out as commander of the Ta'ning Garrison. In 1828 he was made Vice Minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs and then transferred to the Ministry of War. He was repeatedly ordered, together with Minister of Revenue Wang Ding, to investigate and reform the Changlu and Lianghuai salt monopolies; the account appears in Wang Ding's biography. In 1830 he was appointed Military Governor of Jilin. In a memorial he wrote that by long-standing regulation the west bank of the Songhua and the north bank of the Huifa River remained under seal, while elsewhere in the vacant hills guard posts were manned so that only troops might hunt game and livestock to fill tribute quotas. Any civilian who left the zone without a permit to dig ginseng or cut timber was to be hunted down and punished. He further reported that 5,262 shang of wasteland on Borderedun Zhuer Mountain had been brought under the plough in stages. He asked that rent begin in 1835, with half distributed as rewards to troops and half set aside for maintenance and repairs. More than 56,000 additional shang of land could still be opened; he proposed reserving these as state wasteland and bringing tenants in under imperial lease later. At Wula Liangshuiquan more than 73,900 shang had already been reclaimed. He requested assigning 20,000 shang east of Erdao River, with seventy percent going to the Wula commandant's yamen and thirty percent to the assistant commandant's yamen as supplementary income. The remaining 53,000-odd shang of unreclaimed land should likewise be kept as state wasteland. The throne approved these proposals in full. He was transferred to Shengjing, then again to Chengdu.
5
十七年，署四川總督，逾年實授。 時馬邊、越巂邊外夷匪數出為患。 十九年，疏言：「禦邊之策，不外剿、撫、防三者，撫之之道，在施於平時，斷無失利之後轉而就撫之理。 比來勞師糜餉，迄無成功。 為今計者，以修邊防為急務，陳防邊五事：一、增兵額，請於馬邊增兵千二百，雷波、普安、安阜、越巂、寧越各增兵八百，瓘邊、屏山各增兵四百； 一、改營製，請以綏定協副將移駐馬邊廳城，游擊、都司以下各增設移駐有差； 一、築碉堡，飭各廳縣因地制宜，多修堡寨，責令各集團練，官給抬砲，督率教演，擇要隘築砲台，增設大砲； 一、定期巡閱，歲春夏之交，建昌道赴越巂、瓘邊，永寧道赴馬邊、雷波、屏山，週曆巡閱各一次，秋冬責成提督與建昌總兵分赴巡行察勘邊隘； 一、優獎邊吏，馬邊、越巂兩廳同知，請三年俸滿，以題調選缺知府升補。 」疏下議行。 言官論奏四川提督應如湖南例，半年駐越巂等處。 寶興議：「馬邊、越巂相距遼遠，請於春秋夷匪出沒之時，提督往駐馬邊、瓘邊、雷波三廳，建昌總兵往駐越巂、寧越。 」又言：「越巂邊防以大路為重，麥子營、利濟站均應增駐弁兵，乾溝諸汛應酌量移撤，分設於馬日槓諸處。 越巂、寧越兩營相距頗遠，聲勢不能相及。 前請以建昌左營游擊移駐大菩薩地，遠在寧越之東，而越巂營參將復與游擊不相統屬。 請越巂、寧越適中之界牌樓，以建昌鎮右營都司移駐，專管麥子營、利濟站兩汛。 」並從之。
In 1837 he served as acting Governor-General of Sichuan and received the substantive post the year after. At that time Yi raiders beyond the Mabian and Yuexi frontiers were making repeated incursions. In 1839 he memorialized that frontier policy amounted to suppression, pacification, and defense—and that pacification had to be pursued in peacetime, not adopted only after military setbacks. Recent campaigns had exhausted the troops and drained the treasury without achieving a lasting result. What was needed now, he argued, was to strengthen the frontier defenses. He proposed five measures: first, increase troop strength—1,200 more at Mabian, 800 each at Leibo, Pu'an, Anfu, Yuexi, and Ningyue, and 400 each at Qianbian and Pingshan; second, reorganize the garrison system by moving the Suiding Brigade deputy commander to Mabian city and redeploying brigade, battalion, and company officers as required; third, build blockhouses and stockades across the subprefectures and counties, organize local militia, issue swivel guns, drill the men, and erect gun platforms with heavy artillery at key passes; fourth, institute regular inspections—the Jianchang circuit intendant to tour Yuexi and Qianbian each spring, the Yongning circuit intendant to tour Mabian, Leibo, and Pingshan, and in autumn the provincial commander and Jianchang major general to inspect the border passes; fifth, reward frontier officials—the Mabian and Yuexi subprefects to be promoted to prefect after three years of service on the border. The memorial was referred for deliberation and approved for implementation. Memorialists argued that the Sichuan provincial commander should follow the Hunan precedent and spend half the year stationed at Yuexi and neighboring posts. Baoxing countered that Mabian and Yuexi were too far apart for one commander to cover both. He proposed that in spring and autumn, when raiders were most active, the provincial commander should station himself at Mabian, Qianbian, and Leibo while the Jianchang major general took Yuexi and Ningyue. He also urged strengthening the main road defenses at Maiziying and Lijizhan, and redeploying troops from minor posts such as Qiangou to strategic points including Marigang. The Yuexi and Ningyue battalions were too widely separated to support one another. An earlier proposal to move the Jianchang Left Battalion brigade commander to Dapusa would have placed him far east of Ningyue, yet he would not have fallen under the Yuexi battalion colonel's command. He asked instead that the Jianchang Garrison Right Battalion company commander be posted at Jiepailou, midway between Yuexi and Ningyue, with sole responsibility for Maiziying and Lijizhan. The court approved these arrangements as well.

先是寶興以馬邊諸廳縣增設防兵，籌議邊防經費，請按糧津貼，計可徵銀百萬兩，以三十萬為初設防兵之需。 每歲經費，即以餘銀七十萬兩生息，置田供支。 上以津貼病民，撥部帑銀百萬。 翰林院侍讀學士王炳瀛奏：「四川前買義田，遍​​及百餘州縣，若更以數十萬帑銀於各州縣買田收租，膏腴將盡歸公產。 請限於四廳近邊地收買，安置屯防。 」下寶興妥議，疏言：「邊防完竣，用銀二十二萬兩有奇，以三十七萬發鹽茶各商，歲得息三萬七千餘兩，足敷增設練勇餉械之需。 餘銀四十萬，聽部撥別用。 」遂罷買田議，二十一年，拜文淵閣大學士，留四川總督任。 時大學士琦善、協辦大學士伊里布相繼罷，在朝滿洲大臣鮮當上意，故有是授。 二十六年，入覲，命留京管理刑部，充上書房總師傅，兼翰林院掌院學士。 二十八年元旦，加恩年老諸臣，加太保。 十月，卒，年七十二，諡文莊。
Earlier, to fund the new garrisons in the Mabian region, Baoxing had proposed a grain surcharge expected to yield one million taels, thirty thousand of which would cover the initial troop deployments. The remaining seven hundred thousand taels would be invested for interest and used to purchase land whose rents would meet the annual upkeep. The emperor rejected the surcharge as a burden on the people and allocated one million taels from the central treasury instead. Hanlin Reader Wang Bingying objected that Sichuan already held charity fields across more than a hundred jurisdictions; spending hundreds of thousands of taels more to buy land for rent would concentrate the province's best farmland in government hands. He urged limiting purchases to land near the four frontier subprefectures and using it to settle garrison colonists. Baoxing was ordered to revise the plan. He reported that completing the frontier works would cost just over 220,000 taels; lending 370,000 to the salt and tea merchants would yield more than 37,000 taels a year in interest, enough to pay the new militia. The remaining four hundred thousand taels could be reallocated by the ministry for other purposes. The land-purchase scheme was dropped. In 1841 he was made Grand Secretary of the Wenyuan Pavilion while remaining Governor-General of Sichuan. Grand Secretary Qishan and Associate Grand Secretary Yilibu had recently been dismissed, and few Manchu ministers at court enjoyed the emperor's confidence—circumstances that explain this appointment. In 1846 he came to court, was ordered to remain in Beijing to head the Ministry of Justice, and was appointed chief tutor of the Upper Study and chancellor of the Hanlin Academy. On New Year's Day of 1848, as part of a grace for elderly ministers, he was promoted to Grand Tutor. He died in the tenth month at the age of seventy-two and was given the posthumous title Wenzuang.

宗室敬徵，隸鑲白旗，肅親王永錫子。 嘉慶十年，封輔國公，授頭等侍衛，兼委散秩大臣、副都統。 十九年，授內閣學士，兼鑾儀使，充總族長。 二十二年，失察宗室海康等習紅陽教，褫職，謫居盛京。 尋予四等侍衛，乾清門行走。 道光初，累遷工部侍郎，授內務府大臣，調戶部。 八年，偕尚書王鼎察治長蘆鹽務，奏定歸補帑課章程，詳王鼎傳。 十二年，南河奸民陳堂等盜決於家灣官堤，命偕尚書硃士彥往勘。 疏陳：「諸口已合，壩下尚未閉氣，間有蟄陷。 陳堂等聽從逸犯陳端糾眾，以為從例問擬，疏防各官遣戍。 通判張懋祖賠修壩工不實，罰賠枷號。 覆勘湖河各工，請擇要興修，高堰、山盱卑矮石工，分年改砌碎石； 信壩補還石工，智壩、仁河、義河壩改修石底； 里河福興閘塌卸，急築； 揚河西岸加高磚工，改拋碎石。 」並從之。 又會同兩江總督陶澍議定淮鹽票引兼行，言官所論官票運私、侵礙暢岸、爭佔馬頭三者皆可無慮，詔如原議行。
Zongshi Jingzheng belonged to the Bordered White Banner and was a son of Prince Su Yongxi. In 1805 he was enfeoffed as Duke Who Assists the State, appointed a first-class bodyguard, and concurrently served as minister without portfolio and vice commander-in-chief. In 1814 he was appointed Grand Secretary of the Grand Secretariat, made concurrent director of imperial processions, and named chief of the imperial clan. In 1817 he was dismissed for failing to detect that clansmen including Haikang had joined the Hongyang sect, and was banished to Shengjing. He was soon restored as a fourth-class bodyguard with attendance at the Gate of Heavenly Purity. Early in the Daoguang reign he rose to Vice Minister of Works, was appointed minister of the Imperial Household, and was then transferred to the Ministry of Revenue. In 1828 he joined Minister Wang Ding in reforming the Changlu salt monopoly and establishing rules for restoring treasury revenues; see Wang Ding's biography. In 1832, after ruffians led by Chen Tang illicitly breached the official dike at Yujiawan on the Southern Rivers, he was ordered to investigate together with Minister Zhu Shiyan. He reported that although the breaches had been closed, the seal below the dam was not yet complete and hidden collapses still occurred. Chen Tang and his accomplices, who had followed the fugitive Chen Duan, were sentenced as accessories; negligent frontier officials were banished to garrison duty. Subprefect Zhang Maozu was fined and placed in the cangue for failing to make good his promised contribution to the repair. On re-inspection he recommended priority repairs, including rebuilding the low stone revetments at Gaoyan and Shanyu with rubble over several years; restore the stone work at Xin Dam; and rebuild the foundations at Zhi Dam and the Ren and Yi River dams; urgently rebuild the collapsed Fuxing Sluice on the Inner River; raise the brick revetment on the west bank of the Yang River and replace it with rubble stone. The court approved these recommendations. He also worked with Liangjiang Governor-General Tao Zhu to implement joint salt tickets and transport permits for Lianghuai. Memorialists had warned of official tickets used for smuggling, encroachment on shipping lanes, and competition for wharves; Jingzheng argued these concerns were unfounded, and the throne ordered the original plan to proceed.
8
十四年，授左都御史。 偕侍郎吳椿勘浙江海塘，疏言：「念裡亭至尖山柴工尚資禦溜，石塘仍當修整，鎮海及戴家橋汛議改竹簍，塊石不如條石坦水舊法為堅實。 烏龍廟以東，冬工暫緩。 」回京，擢兵部尚書，調工部。 十五年，以孝穆皇后、孝慎皇后梓宮奉安龍泉峪，諏日不慎，罷尚書、都統，仍充內務府大臣。 十六年，署戶部侍郎，累遷工部尚書，兼都統。 東河總督栗毓美多用磚工，御史李蓴言其不便，命敬徵偕蓴往勘。 疏陳：「已辦磚工尚屬整齊，輿論謂保灘護崖可資其力。 水深溜急之處，不及埽工鞏固，搶辦險工，未可深恃。 請停止燒磚，改辦碎石。 」從之。 十八年，調戶部。
In 1834 he was appointed Left Censor-in-Chief. With Vice Minister Wu Chun he inspected the Zhejiang seawall and reported that fascine works from Nianliting to Jianshan still held against the tide and the stone seawall needed repair. Proposals to use bamboo crates at Zhenhai and Daijiaqiao were inferior to the older strip-stone revetment method. Work east of Wulong Temple could be deferred until the next season. After returning to Beijing he was promoted to Minister of War and then transferred to the Ministry of Works. In 1835 he was dismissed as minister and commander-in-chief for carelessness in choosing the date for installing the coffins of Empresses Xiaomu and Xiaoshen at Longquan Valley, but retained his post in the Imperial Household. In 1836 he served as acting Vice Minister of Revenue, rose to Minister of Works, and was made concurrent commander-in-chief. When Eastern Rivers Governor-General Li Yumei favored brick revetments, Censor Li Chun objected, and Jingzheng was sent to investigate with him. He reported that existing brick works were still sound and that public opinion held they could protect banks and cliffs. Where water ran deep and fast, however, brick was less secure than fascine bundles and could not be relied on for emergency repairs. He recommended halting brick production and using rubble stone instead. The court agreed. In 1838 he was transferred to the Ministry of Revenue.
9
二十二年，南河揚河漫口，水由灌河入海。 有議即改新河，河督麟慶以河流未定，遽難決議，命敬徵偕尚書廖鴻荃往勘。 疏言：「改河之議，在因勢利導。 今查灌河海口至蕭莊口門三百六十餘裡。 新河正溜，由六塘出達灌口，其下游東北一百十里，滔滔直注。 惟當潮漲時，黃水相逼，壅閼不前，而上游自口門至響水口二百餘裡，支流忽分忽合，必須兩岸築堤束水，方免汎濫。 計工長三百餘裡，經費難籌。 且中河運道為黃流橫截，不得不移塘灌運。 清水本弱，仍恃借黃以濟。 空船引轉需時，重運更形艱滯。 是移塘乃權宜之計，常年行之，恐妨運道。 舊黃河自蕭莊迄舊海口四百二十餘裡，尾閭寬暢。 自漫口斷流，河身益淤。 若挽歸故道，堵口挑河，共費五六百萬，較改河築堤撙節實多。 請定明歲春融興工，俟軍船回空後築壩合龍。 」詔如議行。 尋以戶部尚書協辦大學士。
In 1842 the Yang River on the Southern Rivers breached its banks, and the flood poured into the sea through the Guan River. Some officials urged an immediate shift to the new channel, but River Commissioner Lin Qing argued the course was still unsettled. Jingzheng was ordered to investigate with Minister Liao Hongquan. He memorialized that redirecting a river meant working with its natural tendencies. He found that from the Guan River estuary to the Xiaozhuang outlet measured more than 360 li. The new channel's main current ran from Liutang to the Guan mouth, then northeast for 110 li in a swift, direct flow. At high tide, however, Yellow River water backed up and stalled the flow. Upstream, over 200 li from the mouth to Xiangshui, channels split and rejoined unpredictably; only dikes on both banks could prevent flooding. Works spanning more than 300 li would be prohibitively expensive. Moreover, the Grand Canal transport route would be cut by Yellow River water, forcing grain traffic onto the Guan River route. Clear water alone was too weak; transport still depended on Yellow River water to boost the current. Turning empty barges would take longer, and laden convoys would face even greater delays. Shifting transport was a temporary expedient; making it permanent would likely cripple the grain route. The old Yellow River channel from Xiaozhuang to the former estuary ran more than 420 li with a broad, open mouth. Since the breach had cut off the flow, the riverbed had silted up further. Returning the river to its old course—blocking the breach and dredging—would cost five or six million taels, far more than building dikes along the new channel. He proposed beginning work the following spring after the ice thawed, closing the breach once the grain fleet had completed its northbound run. The throne ordered the plan carried out as proposed. He was soon appointed Minister of Revenue and Associate Grand Secretary.
10
二十三年，偕侍郎何汝霖赴南河勘工，又赴河南察視中河廳漫口。 疏陳築壩挑河工費需銀五百十八萬兩，較祥符工費為節省，允之。 二十五年，奏：「河南下北河廳廟工，乃北岸七廳適中之所，河臣宜常年駐此，便於控制。 」詔河督每於伏汛前移駐廟工，立冬後仍回濟寧。 尋坐濫保駐藏大臣孟保，降內閣學士。 未幾，复授工部尚書。 又坐濫保科布多參贊大臣果勒明阿，褫職。 三十年，署正白旗滿洲副都統。 咸豐元年，卒，詔念前勞，予一品銜，依尚書例賜卹，諡文★。 子恆恩，左副都御史； 孫盛昱，自有傳。
In 1843 he joined Vice Minister He Rulin to inspect Southern Rivers works and then went to Henan to survey the Middle River Bureau breach. He reported that damming the breach and dredging the channel would cost 5.18 million taels—less than the Xiangfu project—and the plan was approved. In 1845 he argued that the Miaogong works of the Henan Lower North River Bureau lay at the center of the seven north-bank offices and that the river commissioner should be stationed there year-round for effective control. The throne ordered the river commissioner to move to Miaogong before the summer flood season each year and return to Jining after winter began. He was soon demoted to grand secretary for improperly recommending Tibetan resident minister Meng Bao. He was soon reappointed Minister of Works. He was stripped of office again for improperly recommending Kobdo assistant resident minister Guoleming'a. In 1850 he served as acting vice commander-in-chief of the Manchu Bordered White Banner. He died in 1851. The throne recalled his long service, granted him first-rank insignia and ministerial funeral honors, and gave him the posthumous title Wenke. His son Heng'en became Left Vice Censor-in-Chief; his grandson Shengyu has a separate biography.
11
宗室禧恩，字仲蕃，隸正藍旗，睿親王淳穎子。 嘉慶六年，賜頭品頂戴，授頭等侍衛，乾清門行走。 十年，晉御前侍衛，兼副都統、鑾儀使、上駟院卿，轉奉宸院卿，遷內閣學士。 十八年，擢理籓院侍郎。 二十年，授內務府大臣，調戶部侍郎。 二十五年，仁宗崩於熱河避暑山莊，事出倉猝，禧恩以內廷扈從，建議宣宗有定亂勳，當繼位。 樞臣托津、戴均元等猶豫，禧恩抗論，眾不能奪。 會得秘匱硃諭，乃偕諸臣奉宣宗即位，命在御前大臣、領侍衛大臣上行走。
Zongshi Xi'en, whose style name was Zhongfan, belonged to the Plain Blue Banner and was a son of Prince Rui Chunying. In 1801 he was granted the first-rank insignia cap, appointed a first-class bodyguard, and given attendance at the Gate of Heavenly Purity. In 1805 he was promoted to imperial bodyguard and concurrently served as vice commander-in-chief, director of imperial processions, and master of the imperial stud before becoming master of the imperial parks and grand secretary. In 1813 he was promoted to Vice Minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs. In 1815 he was appointed minister of the Imperial Household and transferred to Vice Minister of Revenue. When Emperor Jiaqing died suddenly at the Rehe resort in 1820, Xi'en, who was in the inner-court entourage, argued that the future Daoguang Emperor had earned merit in suppressing the Lin Qing rebellion and should succeed. Grand councilors Tuojin, Dai Junyuan, and others hesitated, but Xi'en argued forcefully and carried the day. When the vermilion rescript was found in the secret casket, he joined the other ministers in installing the Daoguang Emperor and was ordered to attend above the ranks of imperial presence ministers and chief guard ministers.
12
道光二年，擢理籓院尚書。 時哈薩克部眾潛聚烏梁海，議遷徙安置，增設卡倫。 吏部尚書松筠諳習邊事，上每垂詢，禧恩因以諮之。 松筠素坦率，遂代刪改疏稿。 禧恩怒，以上聞，松筠坐越職干預被譴。 尋調工部，仍兼署理籓院尚書。 六年，調戶部。 八年，加太子少保，署吏部尚書。 九年，隨扈盛京，詔念睿親王多爾袞數定大勳，加恩後裔，賜禧恩雙眼花翎。
In 1822 he was promoted to Minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs. Kazakh tribesmen were then gathering secretly at Wulianghai, and officials debated resettling them and adding guard posts. Minister of Personnel Songyun was expert in frontier affairs, and when the emperor consulted him, Xi'en sought his advice. Songyun, who was habitually blunt, took it upon himself to revise Xi'en's memorial draft. Xi'en was furious and reported the matter to the emperor; Songyun was rebuked for overstepping his authority. He was soon transferred to the Ministry of Works while continuing to act as minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs. In 1826 he was transferred to the Ministry of Revenue. In 1828 he was made Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent and acting Minister of Personnel. In 1829, on the imperial visit to Shengjing, an edict honored Prince Rui Dorgon's great services and granted Xi'en the double-eyed peacock feather as a descendant's privilege.
13
十二年，湖南江華瑤趙金龍作亂，命禧恩偕盛京將軍瑚松額督師，未至，總督盧坤、提督羅思舉已平之，殲金龍。 禧恩素貴倨，奉命視師，意氣甚盛，嗛諸將不待而告捷，謂金龍死未可信。 思舉以金龍焚骸及佩物為證，議始息。 廣東瑤匪趙仔青竄入湖南，率提督餘步雲、總兵曾勝追剿之； 偕巡撫吳榮光疏陳善後事。 湖南既定，而兩廣總督李鴻賓剿連山瑤，閱半年，軍屢挫。 詔逮鴻賓，以禧恩署總督，由湖南進兵。 遣步雲、勝等先後破賊，擒首逆鄧三、盤文理，毀其巢。 甫一月，諸瑤乞降。 詔嘉其奏功迅速，賜三眼花翎，封不入八分輔國公。 班師，途次丁母憂，溫諭慰之。
In 1832, when the Yao leader Zhao Jinlong rebelled at Jianghua in Hunan, Xi'en was ordered to command the campaign with Shengjing Military Governor Husong'e—but before they arrived, Governor-General Lu Kun and Provincial Commander Luo Siju had already crushed the rebellion and killed Jinlong. Xi'en, who had always been proud and overbearing, arrived in high spirits to take command. Resentful that the generals had reported victory without him, he declared that Jinlong's death could not yet be confirmed. Luo Siju produced Jinlong's charred remains and personal effects as proof, and the dispute ended. When the Yao bandit Zhao Zaiqing fled from Guangdong into Hunan, he led Provincial Commander Yu Buyun and Major General Zeng Sheng in pursuit; and together with Governor Wu Rongguang memorialized on post-pacification measures. Once Hunan was pacified, Liangguang Governor-General Li Hongbin was still fighting the Lianshan Yao, and after six months his forces had suffered repeated defeats. Li Hongbin was arrested, Xi'en was made acting governor-general, and troops advanced from Hunan. He sent Yu Buyun, Zeng Sheng, and others to defeat the rebels in succession, capturing the ringleaders Deng San and Pan Wenli and destroying their strongholds. Within a month the Yao groups were suing for peace. The throne praised his swift success, granted him the triple-eyed peacock feather, and enfeoffed him as Duke Who Assists the State without income reduction. On the march home he received news of his mother's death; the emperor sent a warm message of condolence.
14
十三年，孝慎皇后薨，命理喪儀，坐議禮徵引違制，褫御前大臣、戶部尚書、內務府大臣。 尋复授理籓院尚書。 以生日受屬員饋送，為御史趙敦詩所劾，疏辯得直，敦詩坐譴。 十四年，因相度龍泉峪萬年吉地，加太子太保。 調兵部尚書，兼署禮部戶部。 十八年，詔以南苑牲畜不蕃，禧恩久管奉宸苑，廢弛疏懈，罷其兼領。 尋得員司積弊狀，盡罷諸兼職，降內閣學士。 二十二年，署盛京將軍，授理籓院侍郎，留將軍署任。 英吉利內犯，海疆戒嚴，命治盛京防務。 既而和議成，疏陳善後十事，並巡洋章程，如議行。
In 1833, when Empress Xiaoshen died, he was put in charge of the funeral rites but was dismissed as imperial presence minister, minister of revenue, and minister of the Imperial Household for citing ritual precedents that violated regulations. He was soon reappointed Minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs. When Censor Zhao Dunshi impeached him for accepting birthday gifts from subordinates, his defense was accepted and Zhao Dunshi was punished instead. In 1834 he was made Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent for surveying the imperial mausoleum site at Longquan Valley. He was transferred to Minister of War and concurrently acted for the Ministries of Rites and Revenue. In 1838 an edict blamed the decline of livestock in the Southern Park on Xi'en's long mismanagement of the Imperial Parks and stripped him of his concurrent posts. When evidence of long-standing abuses by his staff emerged, all his concurrent posts were removed and he was demoted to grand secretary. In 1842 he served as acting Military Governor of Shengjing, was appointed Vice Minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs, and retained the acting governorship. When Britain invaded and the coast was placed on alert, he was ordered to organize Shengjing's defenses. After peace was concluded, he memorialized ten post-war measures and coastal patrol regulations, which were adopted.
15
二十五年，以病解職。 坐失察內地民人越朝鮮界墾地，削公爵，降二等輔國將軍。 三十年，起署馬蘭鎮總兵、密雲副都統。 咸豐元年，召授戶部侍郎。 二年，擢戶部尚書，協辦大學士，管理籓院事。 尋卒，贈太子太保，諡文莊。
In 1845 he resigned due to illness. He was stripped of his ducal title and demoted to assistant general for failing to stop civilians from farming across the Korean border. In 1850 he was recalled to serve as acting commander of Malan Garrison and vice commander-in-chief of Miyun. In 1851 he was summoned and appointed Vice Minister of Revenue. In 1852 he was promoted to Minister of Revenue and Associate Grand Secretary and put in charge of the Court of Colonial Affairs. He died soon after and was posthumously made Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent with the title Wenzuang.
16
禧恩自道光初被恩眷，及孝全皇后被選入宮，家故寒素，賴其資助，遂益用事。 遍膺禁近要職，兼攝諸部，凌轢同列，人皆側目。 後晚寵衰，禧恩亦數獲譴罷斥。 文宗即位，乃復起，不兩年登協揆焉。
Xi'en had enjoyed imperial favor since the early Daoguang years. When Empress Xiaoquan entered the palace, her impoverished family relied on his support, and his influence grew accordingly. He held one key inner-court post after another, concurrently headed multiple ministries, bullied his colleagues, and drew widespread resentment. As the empress's influence waned in her later years, Xi'en was repeatedly reprimanded and dismissed. When Emperor Xianfeng ascended the throne, he was restored and within two years reached the associate grand secretary rank.
17
陳官俊，字偉堂，山東濰縣人。 嘉慶十三年進士，選庶吉士，授編修，遷贊善。 二十一年，入直上書房。 大考二等，擢洗馬，累遷右庶子。 典陝西鄉試，督山西學政。 道光元年，命各省明定陋規，中外臣工多言窒礙，官俊亦疏陳不可行，詔嘉之，予議敘。 會密諭留心察訪官吏賢否、政治得失，官俊恃曾直內廷為宣宗所眷，意氣甚張。 尋遷侍講學士，命回京，仍直上書房。 山西巡撫成格追劾官俊在學政任毆差買妾，妄作威福，大開奔競。 上以官俊於毆差買妾已自承不諱，曾薦舉魏元烺、邱鳴泰，人材尚不繆； 惟所述太監往河東查訪鹽務控案，事出無稽，解職就質，命長齡道出山西，傳旨面詰成格，亦以不能指實引咎，遂兩斥之。
Chen Guanjun, whose style name was Weitang, was a native of Weixian in Shandong. He passed the examinations as a jinshi in 1808, entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor, was appointed a compiler, and was promoted to tutor. In 1816 he joined the Southern Study. After ranking second class in the palace examination, he was promoted to Keeper of the Heir Apparent's Stables and rose to Right Vice Director of Studies. He presided over the Shaanxi provincial examinations and served as educational commissioner of Shanxi. In 1821, when the throne ordered provinces to abolish improper customary fees, many officials objected that the policy would be unworkable. Guanjun agreed in a memorial, won the emperor's praise, and received consideration for promotion. When a secret edict ordered officials to report on local governance, Guanjun, confident in his former inner-court service and the Daoguang Emperor's favor, became increasingly overbearing. He was soon promoted to Hanlin reader, recalled to Beijing, and returned to the Southern Study. Shanxi Governor Cheng'e impeached him for beating runners, buying concubines, abusing his authority, and encouraging corrupt patronage during his term as educational commissioner. The emperor noted that Guanjun had confessed to beating runners and buying concubines, and that his recommendations of Wei Yuanhuang and Qiu Mingtai showed his judgment of talent was not wholly mistaken; but Cheng'e's claim that eunuchs had been sent to Hedong to investigate salt affairs was unfounded. Guanjun was dismissed pending investigation; Changling was ordered to question Cheng'e in Shanxi en route. When Cheng'e could not substantiate his charges, both men were dismissed.
18
官俊降編修，罷直上書房。 連典貴州、江西鄉試，歷中允、祭酒、侍講學士、內閣學士。 十六年，授禮部侍郎，調吏部。 十九年，擢工部尚書。 東陵郎中慶玉侵帑籍沒，主事全孚預告，多所寄頓。 事覺，語由官俊閒談漏洩，回奏复諱飾，詔斥失大臣體，褫職。 二十一年，起為通政使。 歷戶部、吏部侍郎，管理三庫。 擢禮部尚書，調工部。 二十四年，以吏部尚書協辦大學士。
Guanjun was demoted to compiler and removed from the Southern Study. He presided over the Guizhou and Jiangxi provincial examinations in succession and rose through middle supervisor, libationer, Hanlin reader, and grand secretary. In 1836 he was appointed Vice Minister of Rites and transferred to the Ministry of Personnel. In 1839 he was promoted to Minister of Works. When Eastern Mausoleum Bureau Director Qingyu was convicted of embezzlement, Principal Secretary Quanfu, who had warned him in advance, was found to hold many deposits on his behalf. The leak was traced to casual remarks by Guanjun, and when he reported back he tried to cover it up. An edict denounced his conduct as unworthy of a senior minister and stripped him of office. In 1841 he was restored as commissioner of the Office of Transmission. He served as vice minister of revenue and personnel and managed the Three Treasuries. He was promoted to Minister of Rites and transferred to the Ministry of Works. In 1844 he was made Minister of Personnel and Associate Grand Secretary.
19
官俊再起，歷典鄉會試、殿廷禦試，每與衡校。 充上書房總師傅。 編修童福承素無行，直上書房授皇子讀。 給事中陳壇劾之，語及福承為官俊妻作祭文，措詞過當。 福承譴黜，詔斥官俊容隱不奏，罷總師傅，議降三級調用，從寬留任。 二十九年，卒，優詔賜卹，稱其心田坦白，贈太子太保，入祀賢良祠，諡文愨。 賜其孫厚鍾、厚滋並為舉人。
After his restoration, Guanjun repeatedly presided over provincial, metropolitan, and palace examinations as a chief examiner. He served as chief tutor of the Upper Study. Compiler Tong Fucheng, who had a poor reputation, was nevertheless assigned to teach princes in the Upper Study. Supervising Secretary Chen Tan impeached him, citing among other things an overly ornate funeral elegy Tong Fucheng had written for Guanjun's wife. Tong Fucheng was dismissed. An edict rebuked Guanjun for concealing the matter, stripped him of the chief tutorship, and considered demoting him three ranks—but leniently allowed him to keep his post. He died in 1849. A gracious edict granted funeral honors, praised his open character, made him posthumous Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent, enshrined him in the Shrine of Worthy Officials, and gave him the posthumous title Wenque. His grandsons Houzhong and Houzi were both granted provincial graduate status.
20
官俊初直上書房，授宣宗長子奕緯讀，宣宗嘉其訓迪有方。 後皇長子逾冠而薨，上深以為恫，故遇官俊特厚，屢獲咎而恩禮始終不衰。
Guanjun's first posting in the Southern Study was tutoring the Daoguang Emperor's eldest son Yizhi, whom the emperor praised him for instructing well. When the eldest prince died shortly after coming of age, the grieving emperor treated Guanjun with special favor that never waned despite his repeated disgraces.
21
子介祺，道光二十五年進士，官編修。 咸豐中，助軍餉，加侍講學士銜。 後在籍治團練，守城，賑饑，賜二品頂戴。 介祺績學好古，所藏鐘鼎、彝器、金石為近代之冠。
His son Jieqi passed the examinations as a jinshi in 1845 and served as a compiler. During the Xianfeng reign he contributed military funds and was given the rank of Hanlin reader. Later, while at home, he organized militia, defended the city, and relieved famine victims, for which he was granted the second-rank insignia cap. Jieqi was a distinguished scholar-antiquarian whose collection of bronzes and epigraphic stones was unrivaled in his day.
22
卓秉恬，字靜遠，四川華陽人。 嘉慶七年進士，選庶吉士，年甫逾冠，授檢討。 典陝西鄉試。 十八年，改御史，歷給事中，章疏凡數十上。 論盜風未息，由捕役與盜賊因緣為奸，捕役藉盜賊以漁利，盜賊仗捕役為護符，民間控告，官不為理，盜賊結恨，又召荼毒； 直隸之大名、滄州，河南之衛輝、陳州、山東之曹州、東昌、武定，江甦之徐州最甚，請飭實力禁懲。 巡漕山東，履勘泰安、兗州各屬，探濬新泉四十三處，定名勒石。 歷鴻臚寺少卿、順天府丞。
Zhuo Bingtian, whose style name was Jingyuan, was a native of Huayang in Sichuan. He passed the examinations as a jinshi in 1802, entered the Hanlin Academy as a bachelor while still in his early twenties, and was appointed a reviser. He presided over the Shaanxi provincial examinations. In 1813 he was made a censor, rose to supervising secretary, and submitted dozens of memorials. He argued that banditry persisted because constables colluded with thieves—constables profited from protecting criminals, and criminals used constables as shields. When civilians complained, officials ignored them, breeding resentment and further violence; with the worst abuses in Daming and Cangzhou in Zhili, Weihui and Chenzhou in Henan, Caozhou, Dongchang, and Wuding in Shandong, and Xuzhou in Jiangsu. He urged strict enforcement. While inspecting grain transport in Shandong, he surveyed Tai'an, Yanzhou, and neighboring districts, opened forty-three new springs, and had their names inscribed on stone markers. He served as vice president of the Court of Imperial Entertainments and assistant magistrate of Shuntian Prefecture.
23
二十五年，疏言：「由陝西略陽迄東至湖北鄖西，謂之南山老林； 由陝西寧羌迄南而東，經四川境至湖北保康，謂之巴山老林。 地皆磽瘠，糧徭極微。 無業遊民，給地主錢數千，即租種數溝數嶺。 歲薄不收則徙去，謂之棚民。 良莠莫辨，攘奪時聞。 一遇旱澇，一二奸民為之倡，即蟻附蜂起。 州縣以地方遼闊，莫能追捕，遂至互相容隱。 迨釀成大案，即加參劾，事已無濟。 且事連三省，大吏往返諮商，州縣奉文辦理，恆在數月之後。 與其即一隅而專謀之，何如合三省而共議之。 請於扼要之地，專設大員控制。 」宣宗深韙之，詔下三省會議，未果行，僅將邊境文武酌就要地改駐添設。
In 1825 he memorialized that the tract from Lueyang in Shaanxi east to Yunxi in Hubei was known as the Southern Mountains old forest; and from Ningqiang south and east through Sichuan to Baokang in Hubei as the Ba Mountains old forest. The soil was barren and grain taxes and corvée were negligible. Landless migrants paid landlords a few thousand cash to farm a ravine or hillside. They moved on when harvests failed and were known as shed people. Good and bad were indistinguishable, and robbery was common. In drought or flood, a few ringleaders could draw crowds that swarmed like ants. Local officials, unable to police such vast territory, often concealed the problem instead. By the time a major case erupted and officials were impeached, it was too late. Because the region spanned three provinces, high officials exchanged messages while local governments acted months after orders arrived. Rather than addressing each corner separately, he proposed joint deliberation across all three provinces. He asked that a senior official be posted at a strategic point to oversee the region. The Daoguang Emperor strongly approved and ordered the three provinces to confer, but the plan was never fully implemented—only some frontier posts were relocated or added.
24
道光四年，調奉天府丞，丁父憂去。 服闋，歷太僕寺、大理寺少卿，太僕寺卿，宗人府丞，內閣學士，典江南鄉試。 十五年，遷禮部侍郎，調吏部。 督浙江學政。 擢左都御史，召還京，兼管順天府尹事。 歷兵部、戶部、吏部尚書、協辦大學士。 二十四年，拜文淵閣大學士，晉武英殿。 歷管兵部、戶部、工部，賜花翎。 咸豐五年，卒，年七十四，贈太子太保，諡文端。
In 1824 he was transferred to assistant magistrate of Shuntian Prefecture and left office to mourn his father. After mourning he rose through vice president of the Court of the Imperial Stud and Court of Judicial Review, president of the Court of the Imperial Stud, vice director of the Imperial Clan Court, and grand secretary, and presided over the Jiangnan provincial examinations. In 1835 he was made Vice Minister of Rites and transferred to the Ministry of Personnel. He served as educational commissioner of Zhejiang. He was promoted to Left Censor-in-Chief, recalled to Beijing, and put in charge of Shuntian Prefecture affairs. He served as minister of war, revenue, and personnel and as associate grand secretary. In 1844 he was made Grand Secretary of the Wenyuan Pavilion and promoted to the Hall of Military Eminence. He headed the Ministries of War, Revenue, and Works in turn and was granted the peacock feather. He died in 1855 at the age of seventy-four and was posthumously made Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent with the title Wenduan.
25
秉恬兼管京尹最久，凡十有八年。 時九卿會議，一二王公樞相主之，餘率佔位畫諾。 秉恬在列，時有辯論，不為用事者所喜。 子枟，道光二十年進士，官至吏部侍郎。
Bingtian held concurrent charge of the capital magistracy longer than anyone else—a total of eighteen years. At Nine Ministers' conferences, one or two princes or grand councilors dominated while the rest merely nodded assent. Bingtian often spoke up in debate, which won him no favor among those in power. His son Ting passed the examinations as a jinshi in 1840 and rose to Vice Minister of Personnel.
26
論曰：自設軍機處，閣臣不預樞務。 始猶取名德較著者表望中朝，繼則旅進旅退之流，且以年資眷睞，馴躋鼎鉉矣。 寶興號嫺吏事，而蒙簠簋不飭之聲； 敬徵數視河工，差著勞勚； 禧恩、陳官俊並恃恩私，崛而復起； 卓秉恬以言官進，視緘默自安者稍表異焉。
The historian comments: After the Grand Council was established, grand secretaries no longer took part in confidential state affairs. At first men of real reputation still lent the court dignity; later, mediocrities advanced by seniority and favor until they reached the highest office with scarcely a ripple. Baoxing was considered capable in administration yet bore a reputation for personal impropriety; Jingzheng inspected river works repeatedly and earned some credit for his efforts; Xi'en and Chen Guanjun both relied on private favor to rise again after falling; Zhuo Bingtian rose through the censorate and stood out slightly from officials who kept silent and looked only to their own comfort.
