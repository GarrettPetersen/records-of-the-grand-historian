覺羅寶興宗室敬徵宗室禧恩陳官俊卓秉恬
Jueluo Bao Xing, the imperial clansmen Jingzheng and Xien, Chen Guanjun, and Zhuo Bingtian
3
覺羅寶興，字獻山，隸鑲黃旗。 嘉慶十五年進士，選庶吉士，授編修。 累遷少詹事，入直上書房。 十八年，仁宗幸熱河，林清逆黨突入禁城，寶興散直，至東華門與賊遇，急入告警。 宣宗方在上書房，聞警戒備，賊不得逞。 上還京，擢寶興內閣學士。 十九年，授禮部侍郎。 以事忤旨，詔斥寶興不學，降大理寺卿，罷直書房。 复坐部刊科場條例誤「高宗」為「高祖」，降二級調用。 尋予三等侍衛，充吐魯番領隊大臣。
Jueluo Bao Xing, courtesy name Xianshan, was registered in the Bordered Yellow Banner. He took his jinshi degree in Jiaqing 15 (1810), entered the Hanlin as a probationer academician, and was appointed compiler. He rose through the ranks to Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent and was assigned to regular duty in the Imperial Lecture Hall. In Jiaqing 18 (1813), while Emperor Renzong was at Rehe, followers of the rebel Lin Qing broke into the Forbidden City. Bao Xing happened to be off rotation; at the East Flowery Gate he ran into the assailants and rushed inside to sound the warning. The future Emperor Xuanzong was in the Imperial Lecture Hall at the time; on hearing the alarm he put the defenses in order, and the rebels were unable to carry out their plot. After the emperor returned to Beijing, Bao Xing was promoted to Grand Secretary of the Grand Secretariat. In Jiaqing 19 he was made Vice Minister of Rites. He offended the throne over a particular affair; an edict castigated him as poorly educated, reduced him to President of the Court of Judicial Review, and stripped him of his Lecture Hall posting. He was punished again when regulations on the examination grounds published by his ministry misprinted "Gaozong" as "Gaozu"; he was lowered two ranks and reassigned. He was soon made a third-class imperial guardsman and sent as chief commissioner to Turfan.
4
道光二年，召為大理寺少卿。 復因事降通政司參議，歷左副都御史、兵部侍郎，出為泰宁鎮總兵。 八年，授理籓院侍郎，調兵部。 迭命偕戶部尚書王鼎察治長蘆、兩淮鹽務，籌議整頓，詳王鼎傳。 十年，出為吉林將軍，疏言：「松花江西岸、輝發河北岸舊例封禁，其餘閒曠山場均設卡倫，惟許兵丁打捕牲畜，以備貢品。 民人無照，私出挖蓡斫木者，查拏治罪。 」又言：「伯都訥珠爾山荒田先後開墾五千二百六十二晌，其租息請自道光十五年為始，以其半分賞兵丁，半存備報修工程。 此外尚有可墾荒地五萬六千餘晌，作為官荒，將來奏請招佃徵租。 烏拉涼水泉已墾七萬三千九百餘晌，請撥二道河東二萬晌，以七成給烏拉總管衙門，三成給協領衙門，資為津貼。 餘未墾地五萬三千餘晌，亦作官荒。 」並從之。 調盛京，又調成都。
In Daoguang 2 (1822) he was recalled to serve as Vice President of the Court of Judicial Review. He was demoted again over another matter to participating secretary of the Office of Transmission, later held the posts of Left Vice Censor-in-Chief and Vice Minister of War, and was sent out as garrison commander of Taining. In Daoguang 8 he became Vice Minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs and was then moved to the Ministry of War. He was repeatedly assigned with Wang Ding, Minister of Revenue, to inspect and reform the Changlu and Two Huai salt monopolies; the full account appears in Wang Ding's biography. In Daoguang 10 he was appointed general of Jilin and submitted a memorial: "The west bank of the Songhua and the north bank of the Huifa had long been closed to entry by statute; other idle hills and pastures were posted with cordon stations, allowing only garrison troops to hunt game for tribute purposes." Civilians who ventured out without licenses to dig ginseng or fell timber were to be apprehended and punished. " He added: "On the wasteland of Bodunezhu'er Mountain, 5,262 shang had been brought under the plow in stages; he asked that rents commence from Daoguang 15, with half distributed to the troops as bounty and half set aside for upkeep and repair works." Beyond that, more than fifty-six thousand shang of reclaimable wasteland should be held as state land and, in due course, opened to tenants with rents levied by memorial. At Wula Liangshuiquan more than 73,900 shang were already under cultivation; he proposed allotting twenty thousand shang east of the Erdao River—seventy percent to the Wula commandant's office and thirty percent to the assistant commandant's office—as supplementary stipends. The remaining unreclaimed tract of more than fifty-three thousand shang was likewise to be reserved as state wasteland. " The court approved all of it. He was transferred to Mukden and later to Chengdu.
5
十七年，署四川總督，逾年實授。 時馬邊、越巂邊外夷匪數出為患。 十九年，疏言：「禦邊之策，不外剿、撫、防三者，撫之之道，在施於平時，斷無失利之後轉而就撫之理。 比來勞師糜餉，迄無成功。 為今計者，以修邊防為急務，陳防邊五事：一、增兵額，請於馬邊增兵千二百，雷波、普安、安阜、越巂、寧越各增兵八百，瓘邊、屏山各增兵四百； 一、改營製，請以綏定協副將移駐馬邊廳城，游擊、都司以下各增設移駐有差； 一、築碉堡，飭各廳縣因地制宜，多修堡寨，責令各集團練，官給抬砲，督率教演，擇要隘築砲台，增設大砲； 一、定期巡閱，歲春夏之交，建昌道赴越巂、瓘邊，永寧道赴馬邊、雷波、屏山，週曆巡閱各一次，秋冬責成提督與建昌總兵分赴巡行察勘邊隘； 一、優獎邊吏，馬邊、越巂兩廳同知，請三年俸滿，以題調選缺知府升補。 」疏下議行。 言官論奏四川提督應如湖南例，半年駐越巂等處。 寶興議：「馬邊、越巂相距遼遠，請於春秋夷匪出沒之時，提督往駐馬邊、瓘邊、雷波三廳，建昌總兵往駐越巂、寧越。 」又言：「越巂邊防以大路為重，麥子營、利濟站均應增駐弁兵，乾溝諸汛應酌量移撤，分設於馬日槓諸處。 越巂、寧越兩營相距頗遠，聲勢不能相及。 前請以建昌左營游擊移駐大菩薩地，遠在寧越之東，而越巂營參將復與游擊不相統屬。 請越巂、寧越適中之界牌樓，以建昌鎮右營都司移駐，專管麥子營、利濟站兩汛。 」並從之。
In Daoguang 17 he acted as governor-general of Sichuan and received the substantive post the year after. At that time non-Han raiders beyond the Mabian and Yuexi frontiers were raiding with increasing frequency. In Daoguang 19 he memorialized: "Frontier policy comes down to suppression, conciliation, and defense. Conciliation must be practiced in peacetime; there is no sense in resorting to it only after military setbacks." Recent campaigns had worn out the army and drained the treasury without achieving results. His immediate proposal was to make frontier fortification the priority. He outlined five measures: first, raise troop quotas—1,200 more at Mabian, 800 each at Leibo, Pu'an, Anfu, Yuexi, and Ningyue, and 400 each at Qianbian and Pingshan; second, reorganize the garrison structure—station the Suiding brigade deputy commander at Mabian city and redistribute brigade colonels, regimental colonels, and subordinate officers to new posts as needed; third, build blockhouses and stockades—have each district adapt works to local terrain, organize community militia, issue swivel guns from official stores, supervise drill, erect batteries at key passes, and deploy additional artillery; fourth, schedule regular inspections—each spring the Jianchang intendant would tour Yuexi and Qianbian, the Yongning intendant Mabian, Leibo, and Pingshan; in autumn and winter the provincial commander and Jianchang regional commander would divide frontier reconnaissance between them; fifth, reward frontier officials—the vice prefects of Mabian and Yuexi should, after three years in post, be elevated by special nomination to substantive prefectural appointments. " The memorial was referred for deliberation and carried out. Memorialists argued that the Sichuan commander-in-chief, following the Hunan precedent, should spend half the year stationed in Yuexi and neighboring districts. Bao Xing countered: "Mabian and Yuexi lie far apart. In spring and autumn, when raiders are most active, the commander-in-chief should station at Mabian, Qianbian, and Leibo, and the Jianchang regional commander at Yuexi and Ningyue." " He added: "Yuexi defense hinges on the main road. Maiziying and Lijizhan need reinforced garrisons, while posts at Qiangou should be thinned and redeployed to Marigang and similar points." The Yuexi and Ningyue garrisons lay too far apart for mutual support. An earlier proposal had moved the Jianchang Left Brigade colonel to Dapusa, far east of Ningyue, yet left him outside the command of the Yuexi garrison brigade general. He proposed stationing the Jianchang Right Brigade regimental colonel at Jiepailou, midway between Yuexi and Ningyue, with sole responsibility for Maiziying and Lijizhan. " The court approved all of it.
6
先是寶興以馬邊諸廳縣增設防兵，籌議邊防經費，請按糧津貼，計可徵銀百萬兩，以三十萬為初設防兵之需。 每歲經費，即以餘銀七十萬兩生息，置田供支。 上以津貼病民，撥部帑銀百萬。 翰林院侍讀學士王炳瀛奏：「四川前買義田，遍​​及百餘州縣，若更以數十萬帑銀於各州縣買田收租，膏腴將盡歸公產。 請限於四廳近邊地收買，安置屯防。 」下寶興妥議，疏言：「邊防完竣，用銀二十二萬兩有奇，以三十七萬發鹽茶各商，歲得息三萬七千餘兩，足敷增設練勇餉械之需。 餘銀四十萬，聽部撥別用。 」遂罷買田議，二十一年，拜文淵閣大學士，留四川總督任。 時大學士琦善、協辦大學士伊里布相繼罷，在朝滿洲大臣鮮當上意，故有是授。 二十六年，入覲，命留京管理刑部，充上書房總師傅，兼翰林院掌院學士。 二十八年元旦，加恩年老諸臣，加太保。 十月，卒，年七十二，諡文莊。
Earlier, to fund new garrisons in the Mabian districts, Bao Xing had proposed a grain surcharge, estimating a levy of one million taels—three hundred thousand for initial troop costs. Annual upkeep was to come from investing the remaining seven hundred thousand taels and purchasing land whose rents would cover expenses. The emperor judged the surcharge too burdensome on the populace and allotted one million taels from the central treasury instead. Hanlin Reader Wang Bingying warned: "Sichuan already held charity estates in more than a hundred districts; spending hundreds of thousands more in treasury silver to buy land for rent across the province would concentrate the best soil in state hands." He urged limiting purchases to land near the four frontier subprefectures and using it to settle garrison farms. " The court referred the matter to Bao Xing, who reported: "Frontier works cost just over 220,000 taels. He proposed lending 370,000 to salt and tea merchants at interest—more than 37,000 taels a year—enough to pay the new militia and equip them." The remaining 400,000 taels were to be disposed of by the ministry as it saw fit. " The land-purchase scheme was dropped. In Daoguang 21 he was made Grand Secretary of the Hall of Literary Profundity while remaining governor-general of Sichuan. Grand Secretary Qishan and Associate Grand Secretary Yilibu had just been removed in succession, and few Manchu ministers at court enjoyed the emperor's confidence—hence this appointment. In Daoguang 26 he attended court and was ordered to remain in Beijing to head the Ministry of Justice, serve as chief tutor of the Imperial Lecture Hall, and concurrently serve as chancellor of the Hanlin Academy. On New Year's Day of Daoguang 28, as part of a grace for senior officials, he received the honorary rank of Grand Tutor. He died in the tenth month at the age of seventy-two and was given the posthumous name Wenzhuang.
7
宗室敬徵，隸鑲白旗，肅親王永錫子。 嘉慶十年，封輔國公，授頭等侍衛，兼委散秩大臣、副都統。 十九年，授內閣學士，兼鑾儀使，充總族長。 二十二年，失察宗室海康等習紅陽教，褫職，謫居盛京。 尋予四等侍衛，乾清門行走。 道光初，累遷工部侍郎，授內務府大臣，調戶部。 八年，偕尚書王鼎察治長蘆鹽務，奏定歸補帑課章程，詳王鼎傳。 十二年，南河奸民陳堂等盜決於家灣官堤，命偕尚書硃士彥往勘。 疏陳：「諸口已合，壩下尚未閉氣，間有蟄陷。 陳堂等聽從逸犯陳端糾眾，以為從例問擬，疏防各官遣戍。 通判張懋祖賠修壩工不實，罰賠枷號。 覆勘湖河各工，請擇要興修，高堰、山盱卑矮石工，分年改砌碎石； 信壩補還石工，智壩、仁河、義河壩改修石底； 里河福興閘塌卸，急築； 揚河西岸加高磚工，改拋碎石。 」並從之。 又會同兩江總督陶澍議定淮鹽票引兼行，言官所論官票運私、侵礙暢岸、爭佔馬頭三者皆可無慮，詔如原議行。
The imperial clansman Jingzheng was registered in the Bordered White Banner and was a son of Prince Su Yongxi. In Jiaqing 10 he was created Duke of the Second Rank, made a first-class imperial guardsman, and concurrently entrusted as a minister without regular rank and vice president of the Bordered Banner. In Jiaqing 19 he became Grand Secretary of the Grand Secretariat, concurrently Director of Imperial Ceremonial, and was appointed chief head of the clan. In Jiaqing 22 he was dismissed for failing to detect clansmen such as Haikang practicing the Hongyang sect and was exiled to Mukden. He was soon restored as a fourth-class guardsman with attendance at the Gate of Heavenly Purity. Early in the Daoguang reign he rose to Vice Minister of Works, became Minister of the Imperial Household, and was transferred to the Ministry of Revenue. In Daoguang 8 he joined Minister Wang Ding in reforming the Changlu salt monopoly and framed regulations for restoring treasury revenues; see Wang Ding's biography. In Daoguang 12, after ruffians led by Chen Tang illicitly breached the official dike at Yujiawan on the Southern River, he was sent with Minister Zhu Shiyan to inspect the damage. He reported: "The breaches had been closed, but the seal below the dam had not fully set and there were occasional sinkholes." Chen Tang and his accomplices had followed the fugitive Chen Duan in raising a mob; they were sentenced as accessories, and negligent officials were banished to frontier service. Assistant prefect Zhang Maozu had falsified his reimbursement for dike repairs and was fined and placed in the cangue. On reinspection of lake and river works he proposed priority repairs: the low stone revetments at Gaoyan and Shanxu should be rebuilt in rubble over several years; restore stone work at Xinba; rebuild the stone foundations at Zhiba, Renhe, and Yihe dams; urgently rebuild the collapsed Fuxing sluice on the inner channel; raise the brick revetment on the west bank of the Yang River and replace it with dumped rubble. " The court approved all of it. He also worked with Tao Shu, governor-general of the Two Jiangs, on the combined Huai salt ticket-and-certificate system. Memorialists had warned of official tickets used for smuggling, harm to thriving salt banks, and seizure of wharves; Jingzheng judged these concerns unfounded, and the court ordered the original plan carried out.
8
十四年，授左都御史。 偕侍郎吳椿勘浙江海塘，疏言：「念裡亭至尖山柴工尚資禦溜，石塘仍當修整，鎮海及戴家橋汛議改竹簍，塊石不如條石坦水舊法為堅實。 烏龍廟以東，冬工暫緩。 」回京，擢兵部尚書，調工部。 十五年，以孝穆皇后、孝慎皇后梓宮奉安龍泉峪，諏日不慎，罷尚書、都統，仍充內務府大臣。 十六年，署戶部侍郎，累遷工部尚書，兼都統。 東河總督栗毓美多用磚工，御史李蓴言其不便，命敬徵偕蓴往勘。 疏陳：「已辦磚工尚屬整齊，輿論謂保灘護崖可資其力。 水深溜急之處，不及埽工鞏固，搶辦險工，未可深恃。 請停止燒磚，改辦碎石。 」從之。 十八年，調戶部。
In Daoguang 14 he was appointed Left Censor-in-Chief. With Vice Minister Wu Chun he inspected the Zhejiang seawall and reported: "Fascine works from Nianliting to Jianshan still help break the current; the stone seawall needs repair. Proposals to use bamboo crates at Zhenhai and Daijiaqiao were weaker than the older strip-stone revetment." East of Wulong Temple, winter construction could wait. " On returning to the capital he was promoted to Minister of War and then transferred to the Ministry of Works. In Daoguang 15, after an inauspicious date was chosen for installing the coffins of Empresses Xiaomu and Xiaoshen at Longquan Valley, he was stripped of his ministerial and banner-command posts but kept on at the Imperial Household. In Daoguang 16 he acted as Vice Minister of Revenue, rose to Minister of Works, and concurrently commanded a banner. Eastern River governor-general Li Yumei favored brick revetments; Censor Li Chun objected, and Jingzheng was sent with him to inspect. He reported: "Completed brick works were still sound, and local opinion held they helped protect beaches and banks." In deep, fast water they were less secure than fascine bundles, and for emergency repairs they could not be fully trusted. He recommended halting brick production and switching to rubble revetments. " The court agreed. In Daoguang 18 he was moved to the Ministry of Revenue.
9
二十二年，南河揚河漫口，水由灌河入海。 有議即改新河，河督麟慶以河流未定，遽難決議，命敬徵偕尚書廖鴻荃往勘。 疏言：「改河之議，在因勢利導。 今查灌河海口至蕭莊口門三百六十餘裡。 新河正溜，由六塘出達灌口，其下游東北一百十里，滔滔直注。 惟當潮漲時，黃水相逼，壅閼不前，而上游自口門至響水口二百餘裡，支流忽分忽合，必須兩岸築堤束水，方免汎濫。 計工長三百餘裡，經費難籌。 且中河運道為黃流橫截，不得不移塘灌運。 清水本弱，仍恃借黃以濟。 空船引轉需時，重運更形艱滯。 是移塘乃權宜之計，常年行之，恐妨運道。 舊黃河自蕭莊迄舊海口四百二十餘裡，尾閭寬暢。 自漫口斷流，河身益淤。 若挽歸故道，堵口挑河，共費五六百萬，較改河築堤撙節實多。 請定明歲春融興工，俟軍船回空後築壩合龍。 」詔如議行。 尋以戶部尚書協辦大學士。
In Daoguang 22 the Yang River on the Southern River broke its banks and discharged into the sea through the Guan River. Some urged an immediate shift to the new channel; river governor Lin Qing argued the stream was still unsettled and no quick decision was possible. Jingzheng was dispatched with Minister Liao Hongquan to investigate. He memorialized: "Redirecting the river means working with the terrain, not against it." Inspection showed more than 360 li from the Guan River estuary to the Xiaozhuang outlet. The new main current left through Liutang for the Guan mouth and ran northeast for 110 li in a straight, roaring flood. At high tide Yellow River water pressed in and stalled the flow. Upstream, from the mouth to Xiangshuikou for more than 200 li, channels split and rejoined unpredictably; only dikes on both banks could confine the water and prevent flooding. Works would stretch more than 300 li—funding would be hard to secure. Moreover, Yellow River water cut across the Middle River transport route, forcing grain traffic from the Grand Canal onto the Guan. Clear water was already weak and still depended on Yellow River water to keep moving. Empty barges needed long turns; laden convoys would move even more slowly. Shifting the route was an emergency measure; making it permanent would likely cripple the canal. The old Yellow River course from Xiaozhuang to the former mouth ran more than 420 li with a broad, open outlet. Since the breach had cut the flow, the channel had silted heavily. Returning the river to its old course—sealing the breach and dredging—would cost five or six million taels but would save far more than redirecting the river and building new dikes. He proposed starting work after the spring thaw the following year and closing the breach with a cofferdam once the grain fleet had returned empty. " The emperor ordered the plan carried out. He was soon made Associate Grand Secretary while serving as Minister of Revenue.
10
二十三年，偕侍郎何汝霖赴南河勘工，又赴河南察視中河廳漫口。 疏陳築壩挑河工費需銀五百十八萬兩，較祥符工費為節省，允之。 二十五年，奏：「河南下北河廳廟工，乃北岸七廳適中之所，河臣宜常年駐此，便於控制。 」詔河督每於伏汛前移駐廟工，立冬後仍回濟寧。 尋坐濫保駐藏大臣孟保，降內閣學士。 未幾，复授工部尚書。 又坐濫保科布多參贊大臣果勒明阿，褫職。 三十年，署正白旗滿洲副都統。 咸豐元年，卒，詔念前勞，予一品銜，依尚書例賜卹，諡文★。 子恆恩，左副都御史； 孫盛昱，自有傳。
In Daoguang 23 he joined Vice Minister He Rulin to inspect Southern River works and then went to Henan to survey the Middle River breach. He reported that sealing the breach and dredging would cost 5.18 million taels, less than the Xiangfu works, and the court approved. In Daoguang 25 he submitted: "The Miaogong site of the Henan Lower North River office lies midway among the seven north-bank districts; the river commissioner should keep his headquarters there year-round for effective control." " The emperor ordered the river governor to station at Miaogong before the summer flood season and return to Jining after winter set in. He was soon demoted to grand secretary for recklessly recommending Meng Bao as resident minister in Tibet. Before long he was restored as Minister of Works. He was dismissed again for improperly recommending Guoleming'a as assistant resident minister at Kobdo. In Daoguang 30 he acted as vice president of the Plain White Banner Manchu division. He died in Xianfeng 1. The throne honored his past service with first rank, ministerial funeral rites, and the posthumous name Wenqin. His son Heng'en served as Left Vice Censor-in-Chief; his grandson Shengyu has a separate biography.

The imperial clansman Xien, courtesy name Zhongfan, was registered in the Plain Blue Banner and was a son of Prince Rui Chunying. In Jiaqing 6 he received the first-rank cap button, became a first-class guardsman, and was granted attendance at the Gate of Heavenly Purity. In Jiaqing 10 he was promoted to imperial bodyguard and concurrently held posts as banner vice president, Director of Imperial Ceremonial, and Director of the Imperial Stud; he later headed the Imperial Parks and became grand secretary of the Grand Secretariat. In Jiaqing 18 he was promoted to Vice Minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs. In Jiaqing 20 he became Minister of the Imperial Household and was moved to Vice Minister of Revenue. In Jiaqing 25 Emperor Renzong died suddenly at the Rehe summer palace. Xien, attending within the palace, argued that the prince who would become Emperor Xuanzong had quelled the crisis and should take the throne. Grand councillors such as Tuojin and Dai Junyuan hesitated, but Xien pressed his case so firmly that none could gainsay him. Once the vermilion edict was found in the secret casket, he and the other ministers enthroned Emperor Xuanzong and was ordered to walk in precedence above the imperial presence ministers and chief guards ministers.

In Daoguang 2 he was promoted to Minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs. Kazakh tribesmen were then gathering covertly at Wulianghai; the court debated resettling them and adding cordon stations. Minister of Personnel Songyun knew frontier affairs well; the emperor often consulted him, and Xien sought his advice. Songyun was blunt by nature and took it upon himself to revise Xien's memorial draft. Xien was furious and reported the matter; Songyun was reprimanded for overstepping his authority. He was soon moved to the Ministry of Works while continuing to act as Minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs. In Daoguang 6 he was transferred to the Ministry of Revenue. In Daoguang 8 he received the honorary rank of Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent and acted as Minister of Personnel. In Daoguang 9 he accompanied the emperor to Mukden. An edict honored Prince Rui Dorgon's great service and rewarded his descendants with the double-eyed peacock feather for Xien.

In Daoguang 12 the Yao leader Zhao Jinlong of Jianghua, Hunan, rose in revolt. Xien was ordered to take command with Mukden general Husong'e, but before they arrived Governor Lu Kun and commander Luo Siju had already crushed the rebellion and killed Jinlong. Xien had long been proud and overbearing. Sent to take command, he arrived in high spirits, resented the generals' premature victory reports, and declared Jinlong's death unconfirmed. Luo Siju produced Jinlong's charred remains and personal effects as proof, and the dispute ended. The Yao bandit Zhao Zaiqing fled from Guangdong into Hunan; Xien led commander Yu Buyun and regional commander Zeng Sheng in pursuit; and with Governor Wu Rongguang memorialized on post-conflict arrangements. Hunan was pacified, but in Guangdong and Guangxi Governor-General Li Hongbin's campaign against the Lianshan Yao had stalled for half a year with repeated defeats. Li Hongbin was arrested and Xien made acting governor-general, advancing into Guangdong from Hunan. He sent Buyun, Sheng, and others to defeat the rebels in succession, capture the ringleaders Deng San and Pan Wenli, and destroy their strongholds. Within a month the Yao groups surrendered. The court praised his swift victory, granted the triple-eyed peacock feather, and enfeoffed him as Duke of the Second Rank outside the eight-share allotment. While returning from campaign he learned of his mother's death; the emperor sent a warm message of condolence.

In Daoguang 13, after Empress Xiaoshen died, he oversaw the funeral rites but was dismissed from his posts as imperial presence minister, Minister of Revenue, and Minister of the Imperial Household for improper ritual citations. He was soon restored as Minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs. Censor Zhao Dunshi impeached him for accepting birthday gifts from subordinates; Xien's defense was accepted and Zhao Dunshi was punished instead. In Daoguang 14, for surveying the imperial burial site at Longquan Valley, he received the honorary rank of Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent. He was moved to Minister of War and concurrently acted as Minister of Rites and Minister of Revenue. In Daoguang 18 an edict noted declining livestock in the Southern Park; Xien, who had long managed the Imperial Parks, was blamed for negligence and stripped of that concurrent post. Chronic abuses among his staff soon came to light; he lost all concurrent posts and was demoted to grand secretary. In Daoguang 22 he acted as general of Mukden, was appointed Vice Minister of the Court of Colonial Affairs, and kept the acting generalship. When Britain invaded, the coast was placed on alert and he was ordered to organize Mukden's defenses. After peace was made he memorialized ten postwar measures and coastal patrol regulations, which were adopted.

In Daoguang 25 he resigned due to illness. He lost his ducal title and was demoted to general of the state of the second rank for failing to stop Chinese settlers from farming across the Korean border. In Daoguang 30 he was recalled to act as garrison commander at Malan and vice president at Miyun. In Xianfeng 1 he was recalled and appointed Vice Minister of Revenue. In Xianfeng 2 he became Minister of Revenue, Associate Grand Secretary, and superintendent of the Court of Colonial Affairs. He died soon after and was posthumously honored as Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent with the name Wenzhuang.

From early Daoguang Xien enjoyed the emperor's favor. When Empress Xiaoquan entered the palace, her impoverished family relied on his support, and his influence grew accordingly. He held one inner-court post after another, headed multiple ministries concurrently, bullied colleagues, and drew wary glances from all sides. As imperial favor waned in his later years, Xien was repeatedly reprimanded and dismissed. When Emperor Wenzong took the throne he was restored to office and within two years reached the associate grand secretary rank.

Chen Guanjun, courtesy name Weitang, was a native of Weixian in Shandong. He took his jinshi degree in Jiaqing 13, entered the Hanlin as a probationer academician, became a compiler, and was promoted tutor of the heir apparent. In Jiaqing 21 he was assigned to regular duty in the Imperial Lecture Hall. Placing second class in the grand examination, he was promoted to Keeper of the Stud and later to Right Vice Director of the Imperial Clan Court. He presided over the Shaanxi provincial examination and served as educational commissioner of Shanxi. In Daoguang 1 the court ordered provinces to define improper customary fees clearly. Most officials warned the policy would cause gridlock; Guanjun agreed in a memorial. The emperor praised him and granted consideration for promotion. A secret edict then instructed officials to report on merit and misconduct in government. Guanjun, trusting his former inner-court service and the emperor's favor, grew swaggering in manner. He was soon promoted to Hanlin reader, recalled to the capital, and continued in the Imperial Lecture Hall. Shanxi governor Cheng Ge impeached Guanjun for beating runners, buying a concubine, abusing his authority as educational commissioner, and encouraging corrupt patronage. The emperor noted that Guanjun had confessed to beating runners and buying a concubine, and that his recommendations of Wei Yuanliang and Qiu Mingtai showed sound judgment; but his claim that eunuchs had been sent to Hedong to investigate salt affairs was unfounded. Guanjun was removed pending trial; Changling was ordered through Shanxi to question Cheng Ge in person. Cheng Ge could not substantiate his charges and accepted blame, and both men were dismissed.

Guanjun was demoted to compiler and stripped of his Lecture Hall post. He presided over the Guizhou and Jiangxi provincial examinations in succession and held posts as middle secretary, chancellor of the National University, Hanlin reader, and grand secretary. In Daoguang 16 he became Vice Minister of Rites and was moved to the Ministry of Personnel. In Daoguang 19 he was promoted to Minister of Works. Director Qing Yu of the Eastern Tombs embezzled treasury funds and was confiscated; secretary Quan Fu had warned him in advance and helped him hide assets. When the case surfaced, word had leaked through Guanjun's idle talk; he concealed the facts in his memorial reply. An edict condemned his unbecoming conduct and stripped him of office. In Daoguang 21 he was recalled as commissioner of the Office of Transmission. He served as Vice Minister of Revenue and Vice Minister of Personnel and managed the Three Treasuries. He was promoted to Minister of Rites and transferred to the Ministry of Works. In Daoguang 24 he became Associate Grand Secretary while serving as Minister of Personnel.

Restored to office, Guanjun presided over provincial, metropolitan, and palace examinations and took part in grading each time. He served as chief tutor of the Imperial Lecture Hall. Compiler Tong Fucheng, a man of poor character, nevertheless taught the princes in the Imperial Lecture Hall. Supervising secretary Chen Tan impeached him, noting that Fucheng had written an elegy for Guanjun's wife in overwrought language. Fucheng was dismissed. An edict rebuked Guanjun for covering up the matter, stripped him of the chief tutorship, and proposed demoting him three ranks—but allowed him to remain in office as a leniency. He died in Daoguang 29. A gracious edict granted funeral honors, praised his open-hearted character, posthumously made him Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent, enshrined him in the Shrine of Worthy Officials, and gave him the posthumous name Wenque. Guanjun first taught the future Emperor Xuanzong's eldest son Yizhi in the Imperial Lecture Hall, and the emperor praised his instruction. When the eldest prince died after coming of age, the emperor was deeply grieved and treated Guanjun with special favor; despite repeated punishments, imperial grace never faded.

Later, while at home, he organized militia, defended the city, and relieved famine, and was granted the second-rank cap button. Zhuo Bingtian, courtesy name Jingyuan, was a native of Huayang in Sichuan. He took his jinshi degree in Jiaqing 7, entered the Hanlin as a probationer academician while still barely past his capping year, and was appointed reviser. He presided over the Shaanxi provincial examination. In Jiaqing 18 he became a censor, later a supervising secretary, and submitted dozens of memorials. He argued that banditry persisted because constables and thieves colluded: constables profited from thieves, thieves used constables as protection, officials ignored civilian complaints, and resentment bred further violence; the worst areas were Daming and Cangzhou in Zhili, Weihui and Chenzhou in Henan, Caozhou, Dongchang, and Wuding in Shandong, and Xuzhou in Jiangsu; he urged strict enforcement and punishment. While inspecting grain transport in Shandong he surveyed Tai'an and Yanzhou, located and dredged forty-three new springs, and recorded their names on stone steles. He served as Vice President of the Court of Imperial Entertainments and assistant prefect of Shuntian.

In Jiaqing 25 he memorialized: "From Lueyang in Shaanxi east to Yunxi in Hubei lies what is called the Old Forest of the Southern Mountains; from Ningqiang in Shaanxi south and east through Sichuan to Baokang in Hubei is called the Old Forest of the Ba Mountains." The soil was poor and barren, and grain levies and labor obligations were minimal. Landless drifters paid landowners a few thousand cash and rented whole ravines and ridges to farm. When harvests failed they moved on; they were known as shed dwellers. Good and bad elements were indistinguishable, and robbery was common. In drought or flood, a few ringleaders could set off mass uprisings overnight. Prefectures and counties, unable to police such vast territory, often covered for one another instead of pursuing offenders. Impeachments came only after major outbreaks, when it was already too late. Because cases spanned three provinces, senior officials exchanged memos while local officials often received orders months too late. Rather than treating each district in isolation, the three provinces should deliberate together. He proposed posting a senior official at a strategic point with sole authority over the region. " Emperor Xuanzong strongly approved. An edict ordered the three provinces to confer, but the full plan was never implemented; only some frontier posts were relocated or reinforced.

論曰：自設軍機處，閣臣不預樞務。 始猶取名德較著者表望中朝，繼則旅進旅退之流，且以年資眷睞，馴躋鼎鉉矣。 寶興號嫺吏事，而蒙簠簋不飭之聲； 敬徵數視河工，差著勞勚； 禧恩、陳官俊並恃恩私，崛而復起； 卓秉恬以言官進，視緘默自安者稍表異焉。
The historians comment: After the Grand Council was established, grand secretaries no longer took part in confidential state affairs. At first men of real reputation still lent dignity to the court; later the posts went to men who rose by seniority and favor, docilely filling the highest seats. Bao Xing was considered skilled in administration, yet bore a reputation for personal laxity; Jingzheng inspected river works repeatedly and earned some credit for his labors; Xien and Chen Guanjun both relied on private favor to rise again after falls; Zhuo Bingtian entered through the censorate and stood out slightly from officials who kept silent for safety.
