1
李棲筠，字貞一，世為趙人。 幼孤。 有遠度，莊重寡言，體貌軒特。 喜書，多所能曉，為文章，勁迅有體要。 不妄交遊。 族子華每稱有王佐才，士多慕向。 始，居汲共城山下，華固請舉進士，俄擢高第。 調冠氏主簿，太守李峴視若布衣交。 遷安西封常清節度府判官。 常清被召，表攝監察御史，為行軍司馬。 肅宗駐靈武，發安西兵，棲筠料精卒七千赴難，擢殿中侍御史。
Li Qiyun, whose courtesy name was Zhenyi, came from a Zhao family of long standing. He lost his parents while still young. He possessed breadth of vision, was grave and reticent, and cut an imposing figure. A devoted reader, he was versed in many subjects; his prose was forceful and swift yet disciplined in form. He made no friendships lightly. His clansman Li Hua repeatedly praised him as having the makings of a statesman, and men of letters flocked to his side. He first lived below Gongcheng Mountain in Ji; Li Hua kept pressing him to sit for the jinshi, and he soon passed with a high rank. Assigned as recorder of Guanshi, he was received by Prefect Li Xian as an intimate equal, without regard to rank. He was moved to serve as a staff judge under Feng Changqing, military commissioner of Anxi. When Changqing was recalled to court, Qiyun memorialized to have him act as investigating censor and appointed him campaign staff officer. As Emperor Suzong held at Lingwu and called up the Anxi army, Qiyun raised seven thousand elite troops for the emergency and was promoted to palace attendant censor.
2
李峴為大夫，以三司按群臣陷賊者，表棲筠為詳理判官。 推原其人所以脅汙者，輕重以情，悉心助峴，故峴愛恕之，譽一旦出呂諲、崔器上。 三遷吏部員外郎，判南曹。 時大盜後，選簿亡舛，多偽冒，棲筠判析有條，吏氣奪，號神明。 遷山南防禦觀察使。 會峴去相，棲筠坐所善，除太子中允，眾不直，改河南令。
When Li Xian served as grand censor and used the three bureaus to examine officials who had submitted to the rebels, he recommended Qiyun as judge of detailed review. He investigated how each man had been forced into complicity, adjusting penalties to the facts, and gave Xian his unstinting help; Xian cherished and excused him, and overnight his fame eclipsed that of Lü Yin and Cui Qi. Promoted three times, he became vice minister of Personnel with charge of the southern bureau. In the aftermath of the great rebellion the appointment rolls were chaotic and fraud rampant; Qiyun's decisions were lucid and systematic, the clerks were cowed, and men called his judgment uncanny. He was appointed defense and observation commissioner of Shannan. When Xian left the premiership, Qiyun was punished for their association and made crown prince attendant—a posting the public rejected as unfair—then reassigned as magistrate of Henan.
3
李光弼守河陽，高其才，引為行軍司馬，兼糧料使。 改絳州刺史，擢累給事中。 是時，楊綰以進士不鄉舉，但試辭賦浮文，非取士之實，請置五經、秀才科。 詔群臣議，棲筠與賈至、李廙以綰所言為是。 進工部侍郎。 關中舊仰鄭、白二渠溉田，而豪戚壅上遊取硙利，且百所，奪農用十七。 棲筠請皆徹毀，歲得租二百萬，民賴其入，魁然有宰相望。 元載忌之，出為常州刺史。 歲仍旱，編人死徙踵路，棲筠為浚渠，廝江流灌田，遂大稔。 宿賊張度保陽羨西山，累年吏討不克，至是發卒捕斬，支黨皆盡，裏無吠狗。 乃大起學校，堂上畫《孝友傳》示諸生，為鄉飲酒禮，登歌降飲，人人知勸。 以治行進銀青光祿大夫，封贊皇縣子，賜一子官。 人為刻石頌德。
Li Guangbi, holding Heyang, admired his ability and took him on as campaign staff officer while also entrusting him with grain supply. He became prefect of Jiangzhou and was then promoted step by step to palace remonstrator. At that time Yang Wan argued that the jinshi degree no longer rested on local nomination but on flashy literary exercises—a poor test of real ability—and urged new examinations in the Five Classics and the Xiucai category. The court ordered discussion among the ministers; Qiyun, Jia Zhi, and Li Yi agreed with Wan. He was promoted to vice minister of Works. Guanzhong had long depended on the Zheng and Bai canals to water its fields, yet powerful families blocked the upper reaches for milling profit—nearly a hundred such obstructions—depriving the farms of seven parts in ten of their water. Qiyun petitioned to have them all demolished; annual grain rents rose by two million, the people prospered from the yield, and he loomed as a man marked for the highest office. Yuan Zai, jealous of him, had him posted out as prefect of Changzhou. Drought persisted; refugees and corpses lined the roads; Qiyun dredged canals and directed the river to the fields, and the district enjoyed a bumper harvest. The veteran outlaw Zhang Du had held the western hills of Yangxian for years beyond official reach; Qiyun now sent soldiers who captured and executed him, rooted out his network, and left the countryside so quiet that no dog barked. He founded schools on a large scale, hung the Record of Filial Piety and Brotherhood in the hall for his pupils, and held village drinking rites with solemn song and shared cups until custom itself became instruction. On the strength of his administration he was promoted to Silver-Gleam Grand Master of Splendid Happiness, created Viscount of Zanhuang, and granted an office for one son. The people set up stone inscriptions to praise his virtue.
4
蘇州豪士方清因歲兇，誘流殍為盜，積數萬，依黟、歙間，阻山自防，東南厭苦。 詔李光弼分兵討平之。 會平盧行軍司馬許杲恃功，擅留上元，有窺江、吳意，朝廷以創殘，重起兵，即拜棲筠浙西都團練觀察使圖之。 棲筠至，張設武備，遣辯士厚賫金幣抵杲軍賞勞，使士歆愛，奪其謀。 杲懼，悉眾度江，掠楚、泗而潰。 以功進兼御史大夫。 則又增學廬，表宿儒河南褚沖、吳何員等，超拜學官為之師，身執經問義，遠邇趨慕，至徒數百人。 又奏部豪姓多徙貫京兆、河南，規脫徭科，請量產出賦，以杜奸謀。 詔可。
Fang Qing, a Suzhou magnate, exploited famine years to recruit starving refugees into a band of tens of thousands entrenched between Yi and She; the southeast groaned under their depredations. The throne ordered Li Guangbi to detach forces and suppress them. Meanwhile Xu Gao, campaign staff officer of Pinglu, abused his merits by lingering at Shangyuan with an eye on the lower Yangzi; the court, loath to mobilize again while the realm was still bleeding, appointed Qiyun regional commissioner of Zhexi to counter him. On arrival Qiyun arrayed his forces, sent persuasive envoys laden with gold to Gao's camp to reward the troops, won their hearts, and undermined his designs. Gao, alarmed, marched his whole force south of the river, ravaged Chu and Si, and collapsed in flight. For this service he was promoted and also made censor-in-chief. He expanded the schools further, recommended venerable scholars such as Chu Chong of Henan and He Yuan of Wu, elevated them directly as masters, lectured on the classics himself, and drew hundreds of students from far and near. He also reported that great clans were shifting household registration to the capital circuits to evade labor and tax levies, and urged assessments based on actual production to close such fraud. The throne approved.
5
元載當國久，益恣橫，代宗不能堪，陰引剛鯁大臣自助，欲收綱權以黜載。 會御史大夫敬括卒，即召棲筠與河南尹張延賞，擇可為大夫者。 延賞先至，遂代括。 會李少良、陸珽等上書劾載陰事，詔御史問狀，延賞稱疾，不敢鞫，少良、珽覆得罪死。 帝殊失望，出延賞為淮南節度使，引拜棲筠為大夫。 始，棲筠見帝，敷奏明辯，不阿附，帝心善之，故制麻自中以授，朝廷莫知也，中外竦眙。 棲筠素方挺，無所屈。 於是華原尉侯莫陳怤以優補長安尉，當參臺，棲筠物色其勞，怤色動，不能對，乃自言為徐浩、杜濟、薛邕所引，非真優也。 始，浩罷嶺南節度使，以瑰貨數十萬餉載，而濟方為京兆，邕吏部侍郎，三人者，皆載所厚，棲筠並劾之。 帝未決。 會月蝕，帝問其故，棲筠曰：「月蝕脩刑，今罔上行私者未得，天若以儆陛下邪？ 」繇是怤等皆坐貶。 故事，賜百官宴曲江，教坊倡顐雜侍，棲筠以任國風憲，獨不往，臺遂以為法。
Yuan Zai had dominated the government for years and grown ever more overbearing; Daizong, unable to endure it, quietly enlisted upright ministers to recover authority and remove him. When Censor-in-Chief Jing Kuo died, the emperor summoned Qiyun and Zhang Yanshang, intendant of Henan, to choose a successor. Yanshang reached the palace first and was appointed in Kuo's place. When Li Shaoliang and Lu Ting impeached Zai's hidden crimes, the throne ordered the censorate to investigate; Yanshang pleaded illness and shrank from the trial; Shaoliang and Ting were condemned and executed. Deeply disillusioned, the emperor posted Yanshang to Huainan as military commissioner and elevated Qiyun to censor-in-chief. When Qiyun first audienceed the throne, his clear, unflinching memorials won the emperor's regard; the appointment edict was drafted in the inner palace before the court knew, and all were astonished. Qiyun had always been square and unyielding. The Huayuan assistant Hou Mo Chen Fu had been slipped into a Chang'an post through claimed actor privilege; when he was to report to the censorate, Qiyun examined his service record; Fu blanched and confessed that Xu Hao, Du Ji, and Xue Yong had procured the appointment—he was no true actor at all. Hao had lately left the Lingnan commission with a gift of hundreds of thousands in precious goods for Zai; Ji was intendant of Jingzhao and Yong vice minister of Personnel—all three were Zai's protégés—and Qiyun impeached them together. The emperor hesitated. During a lunar eclipse the emperor asked its meaning; Qiyun replied, "Eclipses warn that punishments must be enforced; men who deceive the throne still go free—is Heaven not admonishing Your Majesty?" " Thereupon Fu and his associates were all demoted. By custom the court feasted officials at Qujiang with entertainers in attendance; Qiyun, charged with national discipline, alone stayed away, and the censorate thereafter made that the standard.
6
帝比比欲召相，憚載輒止。 然有進用，皆密訪焉，多所補助。 棲筠見帝猗違不斷，亦內憂憤，卒，年五十八，自為墓誌。 贈吏部尚書，謚曰文獻。
Again and again the emperor meant to make him chancellor, yet fear of Zai stayed his hand. Still, on every important appointment he sought Qiyun's counsel in secret and leaned on him heavily. Seeing the emperor's vacillation, Qiyun brooded in private distress; he died at fifty-eight after composing his own epitaph. He was posthumously honored as minister of Personnel with the posthumous title Literary Contribution.
7
棲筠喜獎善，而樂人攻己短，為天下士歸重，不敢有所斥，稱贊皇公雲。
He delighted in praising merit and welcomed criticism of his faults; scholars everywhere deferred to him, and none spoke ill of the man they called Duke of Zanhuang.
8
子吉甫。 吉甫字弘憲，以蔭補左司禦率府倉曹參軍。 貞元初，為太常博士，年尚少，明練典故。 昭德皇后崩，自天寶後中宮虛，恤禮廢缺。 吉甫草具其儀，德宗稱善。 李泌、竇參器其才，厚遇之。 陸贄疑有黨，出為明州長史。 贄之貶忠州，宰相欲害之，起吉甫為忠州刺史，使甘心焉。 既至，置怨，與結歡，人益重其量，坐是不徙者六歲。 改郴、饒二州。 會前刺史繼死，咸言牙城有物怪，不敢居。 吉甫命菑除其署以視事，吏由是安。 誅破奸盜窟穴，治稱流聞。
His son was Li Jifu. Jifu, courtesy name Hongxian, entered service by yin privilege as a clerk in the Left Imperial Guard granary office. Early in the Zhenyuan era he became an Erudite of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices; though young, he was already expert in ritual precedent. When Empress Zhaode died, the inner palace had stood empty since the Tianbao era and mourning observances had fallen into neglect. Jifu drafted the full rites to the emperor's approval. Li Mi and Dou Can admired his talent and treated him generously. Lu Zhi, suspecting factional ties, posted him out as chief administrator of Mingzhou. When Zhi was banished to Zhongzhou the chancellors meant to destroy him and appointed Jifu prefect there, expecting him to settle old scores. On arrival he put aside enmity and befriended Zhi; men admired his magnanimity all the more, and he remained in place six years without transfer. He was transferred to Chen and Rao prefectures. Successive prefects had died in office, and everyone said the yamen was haunted; none would live there. Jifu had the compound cleared and took up residence; the staff were reassured. He uprooted nests of banditry, and reports of his governance spread far.
9
憲宗立，以考功郎中召，知制誥。 俄入翰林為學士，遷中書舍人。 劉闢拒命，帝意討之，未決。 吉甫獨請無置，宜絕朝貢以折奸謀。 時李錡在浙西，厚賂貴幸，請用韓滉故事領鹽鐵，又求宣、歙。 問吉甫，對曰：「昔韋臯蓄財多，故劉闢因以構亂。 李錡不臣有萌，若益以鹽鐵之饒、采石之險，是趣其反也。 」帝寤，乃以李巽為鹽鐵使。 高崇文圍鹿頭未下，嚴礪請出并州兵，與崇文趨果、閬，以攻渝、合，吉甫以為非是，因言：「漢伐公孫述，晉伐李勢，宋伐譙縱，梁伐劉季連、蕭紀，凡五攻蜀，繇江道者四。 且宣、洪、蘄、鄂強弩，號天下精兵，爭險地兵家所長，請起其兵搗三峽之虛，則賊勢必分，首尾不救，崇文懼舟師成功，人有鬥誌矣。 」帝從之。 礪復請大臣為節度，吉甫諫曰：「崇文功且成，而又命帥，不復盡力矣。 」因請以西川授崇文，而屬礪東川，益資、簡六州，使兩川得以相制。 由是崇文悉力。 劉闢平，吉甫謀居多。
When Xianzong took the throne, Jifu was recalled as director of the Bureau of Evaluations and entrusted with drafting edicts. He soon entered the Hanlin Academy and was promoted to Secretariat drafter. Liu Pi defied the throne; the emperor wished to campaign against him but had not decided. Jifu alone urged that no army be sent and that court tribute be cut off to frustrate his conspiracy. Li Qi then held Zhexi, lavished gifts on the emperor's favorites, and asked—citing Han Huang's precedent—to control the salt and iron monopoly while also seeking Xuan and She prefectures. Asked for his view, Jifu replied, "Wei Gao once hoarded vast wealth, and Liu Pi used that wealth to raise rebellion." Li Qi already shows the seeds of disloyalty; to add the riches of the salt monopoly and the strategic choke point of Caishi would only hasten his revolt. " The emperor took heed and appointed Li Xun salt and iron commissioner instead. Gao Chongwen had invested Lutou without success; Yan Li proposed marching Bingzhou troops with Chongwen toward Guo and Lang to strike Yu and He; Jifu objected, noting that of the five historical campaigns against Shu—from Han against Gongsun Shu to Liang against Liu Jilian and Xiao Ji—four had advanced along the Yangzi. The circuits of Xuan, Hong, Qi, and E field the empire's finest crossbowmen, masters of difficult terrain; raise their forces to strike through the Three Gorges, he argued, and the rebels must split their strength; Chongwen, seeing the river fleet succeed, would fight with renewed zeal. " The emperor agreed. Li again urged appointing a senior minister as commissioner; Jifu remonstrated, "Chongwen is on the verge of victory; name another commander over him and he will slacken." " He proposed granting Western Chuan to Chongwen, placing Li over Eastern Chuan with six additional prefectures, so the two commands could check each other. Chongwen therefore gave the campaign his all. When Liu Pi was suppressed, Jifu's strategy had carried the day.
10
吐蕃遣使請尋盟，吉甫議：「德宗初，未得南詔，故與吐蕃盟。 自異牟尋歸國，吐蕃不敢犯塞，誠許盟，則南詔怨望，邊隙日生。 」帝辭其使。 復請獻濱塞亭障南北數千里求盟，吉甫謀曰：「邊境荒岨，犬牙相吞，邊吏按圖覆視，且不能知。 今吐蕃綿山跨谷，以數番紙而圖千里，起靈武，著劍門，要險之地所亡二三百所，有得地之名，而實喪之，陛下將安用此？ 」帝乃詔謝贊普，不納。
Tibet sent envoys seeking to renew the alliance; Jifu argued, "Early in Dezong's reign, before Nan Zhao submitted, the court allied with Tibet." Since Yi Mouxun returned to the empire Tibet has not dared raid the frontier; grant them a new treaty now and Nan Zhao will turn resentful, with border friction growing daily. " The emperor dismissed their envoys. They again offered maps of frontier forts and beacon towers along thousands of li in exchange for peace; Jifu countered, "The borderlands are rugged and interlocking; frontier officers cannot verify such maps even when they study them." Now they span mountains and valleys on a few sheets of paper claiming a thousand li from Lingwu to Jianmen, erasing two or three hundred strategic posts in name while surrendering them in fact—of what use is such a map to Your Majesty?" " The emperor then politely refused the Tibetan king.
11
張愔既得徐州，帝又欲以濠、泗二州還其軍，吉甫曰：「泗負淮，餉道所會，濠有渦口之險，前日授建封，幾失形勢。 今愔乃兩廊壯士所立，雖有善意，未能制其眾。 又使得淮、渦，厄東南走集，憂未艾也。 」乃止。
After Zhang Yin secured Xuzhou the emperor again meant to restore Hao and Si to his command; Jifu warned, "Si commands the Huai and the junction of supply routes; Hao holds the strategic Wokou crossing—granting it to Jianfeng once nearly cost us the region." Yin was installed by the garrison's own hard men; however well-intentioned he may be, he cannot control his troops. Restore the Huai and Wo crossings and he will choke the southeast's lines of movement—the danger is not past. " The plan was dropped.
12
中書史滑渙素厚中人劉光琦，凡宰相議為光琦持異者，使渙請，常得如素，宦人傳詔，或不至中書，召渙於延英承旨，迎附群意，即為文書，宰相至有不及知者。 由是通四方賂謝，弟泳，官至刺史。 鄭餘慶當國，嘗一責怒，數日即罷去。 吉甫請間，劾其奸，帝使簿渙家，得貲數千萬，貶死雷州。 又建言：「州刺史不得擅見本道使，罷諸道歲終巡句以絕苛斂，命有司舉材堪縣令者，軍國大事以寶書易墨詔。 」由是帝愈倚信。
The Secretariat clerk Hua Huan was intimate with the eunuch Liu Guangqi; whenever the chancellors opposed Liu's interests, Huan would intervene and usually prevail. Eunuchs sometimes bypassed the Secretariat entirely, summoning Huan to Yanying Hall to draft edicts to their liking—chancellors learned of policy only after the fact. Through him bribes flowed from every quarter; his brother Yong rose to prefect. When Zheng Yuqing headed the government he rebuked Huan once; within days Huan was dismissed. Jifu sought a private audience and impeached him; auditors found tens of millions in his household; he was banished and died at Leizhou. He also proposed that prefects not meet circuit commissioners without authorization, that year-end circuit inspections be abolished to end extortion, that qualified magistrates be nominated by the ministries, and that critical military and state orders be issued on imperial treasure documents rather than informal ink edicts. " From that time the emperor trusted him still more.
13
元和二年，杜黃裳罷宰相，乃擢吉甫中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事。 吉甫連蹇外遷十餘年，究知閭裏疾苦，常病方鎮強恣，至是為帝從容言：「使屬郡刺史得自為政，則風化可成。 」帝然之，出郎吏十餘人為刺史。 自王叔文時選任猥冒，吉甫始簿其員，人得敘進，官無留才。 又度錡必反，勸帝召之，使者三往，以病解，而多持金啗權貴，至為錡遊說者。 吉甫曰：「錡，庸材，而所蓄乃亡命群盜，非有鬥誌，討之必克。 」帝意決。 復言：「昔徐州亂，嘗敗吳兵，江南畏之。 若起其眾為先鋒，可以絕徐後患。 韓弘在汴州，多憚其威，誠詔弘子弟率兵為掎角，則賊不戰而潰。 」從之。 詔下，錡眾聞徐、梁兵興，果斬錡降。 以功封贊皇縣侯，徙趙國公。 德宗以來，姑息蕃鎮，有終身不易地者。 吉甫為相歲餘，凡易三十六鎮，殿最分明。
In Yuanhe year 2, after Du Huangchang left office, Jifu was elevated to vice director of the Secretariat and chancellor. After more than a decade of setbacks in provincial posts he knew the people's hardships intimately and had long deplored the arrogance of the regional commands; he now told the emperor calmly, "Let subordinate prefects govern their own districts, and civilizing rule can take root." " The emperor agreed and dispatched more than ten court officials as prefects. Since Wang Shuwen's faction appointments had been chaotic; Jifu began keeping proper registers so men could advance by seniority and talent was no longer stranded in office. He also judged that Li Qi would rebel, urged the emperor to summon him to court; envoys went three times but Qi pleaded illness while bribing the powerful—even hiring lobbyists on his behalf. Jifu said, "Qi is a mediocrity surrounded by desperate outlaws without real fighting spirit; a campaign will surely prevail." " The emperor's mind was made up. He added, "When Xuzhou rebelled before it once defeated Wu armies, and the lower Yangzi still fears that memory." Raise those troops as the vanguard and the lingering threat from Xuzhou can be ended. Han Hong at Bianzhou fears their power; if he is ordered to flank them with his forces, the rebels will collapse without a fight. " The plan was adopted. When the edict went out, Qi's followers heard that Xuzhou and Bianzhou armies were moving and beheaded Qi in surrender. For this service he was created Marquis of Zanhuang, then Duke of Zhao. Since Dezong's reign the court had indulged the regional commands, some holding the same territory for life. In little more than a year as chancellor Jifu rotated thirty-six commands, with clear merit rankings for each.

Pei Jun, as right vice director of the Secretariat with charge of the Revenue Bureau, built a faction to undermine the administration. When Huangfu Shi and others answered the policy examination by denouncing the powerful, the power brokers were furious and the emperor was displeased. Jun's faction spread word that the chancellors were behind it. " Right remembrancers Dugu Yu and Li Zhengchen explained what had happened, and the emperor was satisfied. Jifu had favored Dou Qun, Yang Shie, and Lü Wen and recommended Qun as vice director of the Censorate. Qun at once appointed Shie attendant censor and Wen controller of miscellaneous affairs. Jifu, angry that he had not been consulted, delayed approval for a long time, and Qun's circle bore a grudge. Soon Jifu fell ill and a physician stayed overnight at his house; Qun arrested the physician and impeached Jifu for consorting with occultists. The emperor was alarmed, but investigation found no substance; Qun and his allies were all demoted. The emperor saw him off at Tonghua Gate with a farewell feast and bestowed imperial medicines and secret prescriptions. In three years he secured remission of millions in overdue rents, built the Furen and Guben reservoirs, and irrigated nearly ten thousand qing of farmland. Drought struck the Jianghuai region, worst in eastern and western Zhe; the ministries had not petitioned for relief; Jifu reported the crisis; the emperor was startled and dispatched envoys on separate routes to distribute aid. In year six Pei Ji resigned through illness; Jifu was recalled to his former posts at the helm of government. He audienceed at Yanying Hall for five quarter-hours before the session ended. The emperor honored him so highly that he addressed him by title rather than by name. Jifu deplored the swollen bureaucracy—never since Han and Sui had there been so many officials—and memorialized: "Appointments are careless, ranks chaotic, idle posts abound, and they consume the heaviest taxes, so the people grow poorer daily while useless mouths multiply." Since Tianbao the standing army has held over eight hundred thousand men, yet more than fifteen in a hundred slip away into trade, Buddhism, Daoism, or evasion of corvée. For every three who labor, seven merely wait to be fed. Yet salaried officials inside and outside the capital number nearly ten thousand, with duplicated bureaus and overlapping duties everywhere—revenue shrinks while stipends swell, fixed posts multiply while assignments have no limit. How can ranks and duties not fall into chaos? How can government not become hopelessly burdensome? Early Han had fewer than sixty commanderies, yet Wen and Jing nearly saw the realm split among three kings—fewer districts do not guarantee good order, nor do more districts guarantee effective rule. Today there are three hundred prefectures and fourteen hundred counties—prefectures carved from mere towns, counties from mere townships—costly yet weak in control, not the foundation of good government. I urge a broad review: merge redundant prefectures and counties, suspend unnecessary recruitment cycles—then fewer officials will be easier to find and easier to govern. By statute a first-rank official received three thousand in salary, with duty fields and grain stipends generally not exceeding one thousand shi. Under Dali, powerful ministers drew monthly salaries of nine thousand strings and every prefect one thousand; Chang Gun first imposed limits; Li Mi later adjusted pay by the demands of each post so offices could support one another. Yet many hold titles without duties while drawing full pay, and stipends vary wildly between busy and idle posts—I ask that all salaries be standardized. " The emperor ordered Duan Pingzhong, Wei Guanzhi, Xu Mengrong, and Li Jiang to review and cut redundancies—eight hundred superfluous posts and fourteen hundred clerks were eliminated. He also petitioned to reclaim temple lands and mill revenues around the capital to ease the burden on the poor.

He was soon transferred to Hedong, then recalled as minister of Justice and commissioner for salt and iron transport on all circuits.
25
拜淮南節度使。 王師討蔡方急，李師道謀撓沮之，鄘以兵二萬分壁鄆境，貲餉不仰有司。 是時兵興，天子憂財乏，使程异馳驛江淮，諷諸道輸貨助軍。 鄘素富強，即籍府庫留一歲儲，餘盡納於朝，諸道由是悉索以獻，繄鄘倡之。
He was appointed military commissioner of Huainan. While imperial forces pressed the campaign against Cai, Li Shidao sought to interfere; Yong posted twenty thousand men on the Yan border at his own expense, without drawing on the central ministries. With war underway the emperor feared a shortage of funds and sent Cheng Yi post-haste through Jianghuai to urge the circuits to contribute supplies. Yong's circuit was prosperous; he inventoried the treasury, kept one year's reserves, and sent the rest to the capital—whereupon other circuits followed his example.
26
先是，吐突承璀為監軍，貴寵甚，鄘以剛嚴治，相禮憚，稍厚善。 承璀歸，數稱薦之，召拜門下侍郎、同中書門下平章事。 鄘不喜由宦幸進，及出祖，樂作泣下，謂諸將曰：「吾老安外鎮，宰相豈吾任乎？ 」至京師，不肯視事，引疾固辭，改戶部尚書。 俄檢校尚書左僕射，兼太子賓客，分司東都。 以太子少傅致仕，卒，贈太子太保，謚曰肅。
Earlier the eunuch Tutu Chenghuan had been army supervisor and enjoyed great favor; Yong governed sternly; mutual respect grew into a workable rapport. When Chenghuan returned to court he recommended Yong repeatedly; Yong was summoned as vice director of the Chancellery and chancellor. Yong disliked advancement through eunuch patronage; at his farewell feast he wept as the music played and told his officers, "I am old and content in the provinces—what have I to do with the chancellorship?" " In the capital he refused to take up office, pleaded illness, and was reassigned as minister of Revenue. He soon became acting left vice director of the Secretariat and crown prince guest with detached service in the eastern capital. He retired as junior tutor to the crown prince, died, was posthumously honored as grand guardian of the crown prince, and given the posthumous title Solemn.
27
鄘強直無私，與楊憑、穆質、許孟容、王仲舒友善，皆以氣自任。 而鄘當官，以峭法操下，所至稱治。 猛決少恩，在淮南七年，其生殺禽擿，多委軍吏，而參佐束手不得與，人往往陷非法，議者亦以此少之。
Yong was upright and selfless, friendly with Yang Ping, Mu Zhi, Xu Mengrong, and Wang Zhongshu—men who prided themselves on moral courage. In office he ruled subordinates with stern law and was praised for good governance wherever he served. He was fierce and sparing of mercy; during seven years in Huainan he left life-and-death decisions largely to military staff while his civil aides stood idle; many suffered injustice, and critics held this against him.
28
子拭，仕歷宗正卿、京兆尹、河東鳳翔節度使，以秘書監卒。
His son Shi rose through the posts of director of the Imperial Clan Court, Jingzhao intendant, and commissioner of Hedong and Fengxiang, and died as director of the Secretariat Library.
29
拭子磎，字景望。 大中末，擢進士，累遷戶部郎中，分司東都。 劾奏內園使郝景全不法事，景全反摘磎奏犯順宗嫌名，坐奪俸。 磎上言：「『因事告事，旁訟他人』者，咸通詔語也。 禮，不諱嫌名； 律，廟諱嫌名不坐。 豈臣所引詔書而有司輒論奏？ 臣恐自今用格令者，委曲回避，旁緣為奸也。 」詔不奪俸。
Shi's son was Li Xi, courtesy name Jingwang. Late in the Dazhong era he passed the jinshi and rose to director of the Revenue Bureau with detached service in the eastern capital. He impeached Inner Garden commissioner Hao Jingquan for misconduct; Jingquan retaliated by accusing Xi's memorial of violating Shunzong's taboo name, and Xi was fined by salary confiscation. Xi memorialized, "The phrase 'using one case to report another, dragging in unrelated parties' comes from a Xiantong edict." According to ritual, taboo homophones need not be avoided; and by law, offenses involving temple-taboo homophones are not punishable. How can the ministries punish me for quoting an edict? I fear that hereafter anyone citing regulations will be forced into evasive circumlocution, opening the door to abuse. " The emperor ordered his salary restored.
30
黃巢陷洛，磎挾尚書八印走河陽，時留守劉允章為賊脅，遣人就磎索印，拒不與。 允章悟，亦不臣賊。 嗣襄王之亂，轉側淮南，高駢受偽命，磎苦諫，不納。 入為中書舍人、翰林學士。 辭職歸華陰，復以學士召。
When Huang Chao seized Luoyang, Xi fled to Heyang with the eight seals of the Secretariat; the acting commander Liu Yunzhang, pressured by the rebels, sent men to demand them, but Xi refused. Yunzhang took heart and likewise refused to serve the rebels. During the succession crisis of the Prince of Xiang he moved about in Huainan; Gao Pian accepted a false imperial commission, and Xi's earnest remonstrance went unheeded. He entered court as Secretariat drafter and Hanlin academician. He resigned and retired to Huayin, then was summoned back to the Hanlin Academy.
31
磎好學，家有書至萬卷，世號「李書樓」。 所著文章及註解諸書傳甚多。 子沇，字東濟，有俊才，亦遇害，贈禮部員外郎。
Xi was a devoted scholar; his household held ten thousand scrolls, and he was known as "Li the Book Tower." He wrote extensively, including many commentaries on the classics. His son Yan, courtesy name Dongji, was a gifted man who also died violently and was posthumously honored as vice director of the Board of Rites.
32
贊曰：剛者天德，故孔子稱「剛近仁」。 骨強四支，故君有忠臣，謂之骨鯁。 若棲筠、庸阝二子，其剛者歟！ 棲筠抗權邪，不及相； 庸阝得相，不願拜。 非剛，疇克勝之？ 吉甫踐天宰，謀謨是矣，而鯁正有愧於父雲。
The appraisal says: Firmness is Heaven's virtue; hence Confucius said that firmness borders on humaneness. Strong bones support the limbs; so loyal ministers who speak blunt truth are called the backbone of the state. Of Qiyun and Yong—were these not men of true firmness! Qiyun defied corrupt power yet never became chancellor; Yong was offered the chancellorship and refused to accept it. Without such firmness, who could have done what they did? Jifu held the highest office and his statecraft was sound, yet in blunt integrity he fell short of his father's example.
