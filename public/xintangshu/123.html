◎李蕭盧韋趙和
◎ Li, Xiao, Lu, Wei, Zhao, and He.
3
李嶠，字巨山，趙州贊皇人。 早孤，事母孝。 為兒時，夢人遺雙筆，自是有文辭，十五通《五經》，薛元超稱之。 二十擢進士第，始調安定尉。 舉制策甲科，遷長安。 時畿尉名文章者，駱賓王、劉光業，嶠最少，與等夷。
Li Jiao, styled Jushan, came from Zanhuang in Zhao Prefecture. Orphaned young, he was devoted to his mother. In childhood he dreamed that someone presented him with two brushes; thereafter he showed literary gifts. At fifteen he had mastered the Five Classics, and Xue Yuanchao spoke highly of him. At twenty he took the jinshi degree and was first posted as aide of Anding. He placed in the top grade of the policy examination and was moved to Chang'an. Among the capital aides then renowned for prose were Luo Binwang and Liu Guangye; Jiao was the youngest, yet he stood on equal footing with them.
4
授監察御史。 高宗擊邕、巖二州叛獠，詔監其軍，嶠入洞喻降之，由是罷兵。 稍遷給事中。 會來俊臣構狄仁傑、李嗣真、裴宣禮等獄，將抵死，敕嶠與大理少卿張德裕、侍御史劉憲覆驗，德裕等內知其冤，不敢異。 嶠曰：「知其枉不申，是謂見義不為者。 」卒與二人列其枉，忤武後旨，出為潤州司馬。 久乃召為鳳閣舍人，文冊大號令，多主為之。
He was made investigating censor. When Emperor Gaozong campaigned against the rebel Liao of Yong and Yan, Jiao was ordered to supervise the forces; he entered their mountain strongholds to persuade surrender, and the troops were withdrawn. He was soon promoted to palace attendant. Lai Junchen had framed Di Renjie, Li Sizhen, Pei Xuanli, and others in a capital case; as they were about to be executed, the throne ordered Jiao, Vice Minister of Justice Zhang Deyu, and attendant censor Liu Xian to re-examine the matter. Deyu and his colleagues knew the prisoners were innocent but dared not object. Jiao said, "To know men are wronged and yet not speak is to see what is right and fail to act. " He finally joined the two others in reporting the injustice, ran counter to Empress Wu's wishes, and was demoted to secretary of Run Prefecture. After some time he was recalled as attendant of the Phoenix Pavilion; most of the great edicts and proclamations were composed under his hand.
5
初置右御史臺，察州縣吏善惡、風俗得失，嶠上疏曰：「禁網上疏，法象宜簡，簡則法易行而不煩雜，疏則所羅廣而不苛碎。 伏見垂拱時，諸道巡察使科條四十有四，至別敕令又三十。 而使以三月出，盡十一月奏事，每道所察吏，多者二千，少亦千計，要在品核才行而褒貶之。 今期會迫促，奔逐不暇，欲望詳究所能，不亦艱哉。 此非隳於職，才有限，力不逮耳。 臣願量其功程以為節制，使器周於用，力濟於時，然後得失可以精核矣。 」又言：「今所察按，準漢六條而推廣之，則無不包矣，烏在多張事目也？ 且朝廷萬機非無事，而機事之動，常在四方，故出使者冠蓋相望。 今已置使，則外州之事悉得專之，傳驛減矣。 請率十州置一御史，以期歲為之限，容其身到屬縣，過閭裏，督察奸訛，采風俗，然後可課其成功。 且御史出入天禁，勵己自脩，比他吏相百也。 按劾回庸，糾擿隱欺，比他吏相十也。 陛下誠用臣言，妙擇能者委之，莫不盡力效死矣。 」武後善之，下制析天下為二十道，擇堪使者。 為眾議沮止。
When the Right Censorate was first set up to scrutinize officials' conduct and local customs, Jiao memorialized: "The prohibitory net should be loose above and the law kept simple; simplicity makes enforcement easy and unburdensome, while looseness lets it cast wide without petty entanglement. I note that under the Chui Gong reign touring commissioners already had forty-four regulatory articles, with thirty more added by separate edict. Yet commissioners departed in the third month and had to report by the eleventh; each circuit involved as many as two thousand officials under review, or no fewer than a thousand—the task being to grade conduct and talent for commendation or censure. With deadlines pressing and no respite from the chase, to expect a thorough inquiry into every man's capacity—is that not beyond reach? This is not dereliction but the limit of ability—their strength cannot match the charge. I ask that their workload be measured and bounded, so capacity fits the task and effort matches the season; only then can results be judged with precision. " He added, "If present inspections followed and extended the Han Six Articles, nothing would be left out—why multiply headings? Moreover the court's myriad affairs are never idle, and when urgent business stirs it is usually in the provinces—hence the endless procession of outgoing envoys. With commissioners already in place, outer prefectures' affairs can be entrusted to them alone, and relay traffic is cut. Let one censor oversee every ten prefectures, with a one-year term, time enough to reach subordinate counties and walk the lanes, probe malfeasance, and take the pulse of custom—then his success may be judged. Censors moreover move within the imperial precincts and discipline themselves—a hundred times more than ordinary officers. In impeaching the corrupt and exposing hidden fraud they are ten times more effective than others. If Your Majesty truly adopts this plan and entrusts able men with the charge, none will fail to serve unto death. " Empress Wu approved the proposal and ordered the realm divided into twenty circuits with suitable envoys chosen. Popular opposition in council blocked it.
6
俄知天宮侍郎事，進麟臺少監、同鳳閣鸞臺平章事。 遷鸞臺侍郎。 會張錫輔政，嶠，其出也，罷為成均祭酒。 俄檢校文昌左丞，留守東都。 長安三年，以本官復為平章事，知納言。 遷內史，嶠辭劇，復為成均祭酒、平章事。
Soon he was acting vice minister of the Heavenly Office, then promoted to vice director of the Unicorn Terrace and made associate chief minister of the Phoenix and Phoenix-Parrot Pavilions. He was moved up to vice minister of the Phoenix-Parrot Pavilion. When Zhang Xi entered the government—Jiao being his rival—Jiao was removed and made libationer of the Directorate of Education. Soon he was acting left vice director of the Literary Brilliance Office and left as guardian of the eastern capital. In Chang'an year three he again became associate chief minister in his former capacity and took charge of the chief censorate. Promoted to director of the Secretariat, Jiao declined the onerous post and again served as libationer of the Directorate of Education while remaining associate chief minister.
7
武後將建大像於白司馬阪，嶠諫：「造像雖俾浮屠輸錢，然非州縣承辦不能濟，是名雖不稅而實稅之。 臣計天下編戶，貧弱者眾，有賣舍、帖田供王役者。 今造像錢積十七萬緡，若頒之窮人，家給千錢，則紓十七萬戶饑寒之苦，德無窮矣。 」不納。
When Empress Wu planned a great image at White Sima Slope, Jiao remonstrated: "Although the Buddha image lets clergy donate funds, prefectures and counties must carry it out—so though called no tax, it taxes in fact. I reckon that among registered households the poor are many—some sell homes or pawn fields to meet imperial labor levies. Image funds now total one hundred seventy thousand strings; distributed to the destitute at a thousand per household, one hundred seventy thousand families would be spared hunger and cold—virtue without measure. " She did not heed him.
8
張易之敗，坐附會貶豫州刺史，未行，改通州。 數月，以吏部侍郎召，俄遷尚書。 神龍二年，代韋安石為中書令。
When Zhang Yizhi fell, Jiao was demoted for association to prefect of Yu; before he left he was reassigned to Tong. Within months he was recalled as vice minister of Personnel and soon made minister. In Shenlong year two he replaced Wei Anshi as director of the Secretariat.
9
嶠在吏部時，陰欲藉時望復宰相，乃奏置員外官數千。 既吏眾猥，府庫虛耗，乃上書歸咎於時，因蓋向非，曰：
While at Personnel Jiao secretly hoped to ride popular esteem back to the chancellorship and memorialized for several thousand supernumerary posts. Once offices swelled and the treasury emptied, he memorialized blaming the times and masking his earlier error, saying:
10
元首之尊，居有重門擊柝之衛，出有清警戒道之禁，所以備非常，息異望，誠不可易舉動，慢防閑也。 陛下厭崇邃，輕尊嚴，微服潛遊，閱廛過市，行路私議，朝廷驚懼，如禍產意外，縱不自惜，奈宗廟蒼生何？
The sovereign's dignity requires layered gates and night watches at rest, cleared roads and cordons abroad—to guard against the unexpected and still irregular ambitions. His movements must not be made lightly nor precautions relaxed. Your Majesty disdains lofty seclusion and treats dignity lightly, roaming in disguise through markets; wayfarers murmur and the court trembles as if calamity might strike unawares. Even if you spare yourself, what of the ancestral shrines and the people?
11
又分職建官，不可以濫。 傳曰：「官不必備，惟其人。 」自帝室中興，以不慎爵賞為惠，冒級躐階，朝升夕改，正闕不給，加以員外。 內則府庫為殫，外則黎庶蒙害，非求賢助治之道也。 願愛晙班榮，息匪服之議。 今文武六十以上，而天造含容，皆矜恤之。 老病者已解還授，員外者既遣復留。 恐非所以消敝救時也。 請敕有司料其可用進，不可用退。 又遠方夷人不堪治事，國家向務撫納而官之，非立功酋長，類糜俸祿。 願商度非要者，一切放還。
Moreover offices must not be multiplied without cause. The tradition says, "Posts need not all be filled—only the right men matter. " Since the dynasty's restoration, careless largesse in rank has been mistaken for kindness—men leap grades, promoted at dawn and shifted at dusk; regular posts cannot be filled and supernumeraries pile on. Within the treasury is drained; without the people suffer—this is not the way to seek talent and aid rule. I ask that rank and glory be granted with care and improper appointments cease. Civil and military officials past sixty—the Heaven-made ruler is compassionate toward them all. The old and sick have been released from office, yet dismissed supernumeraries are kept on again. I fear this will not dispel exhaustion nor rescue the times. Let the relevant offices advance the able and retire the unfit. Distant tribesmen cannot govern; the state has long enrolled them for appeasement—yet unless they are meritorious chieftains, most merely consume salaries. Let all nonessential appointees be sent home.
12
又《易》稱：「何以守位曰仁，何以聚人曰財。 」今百姓乏窶，不安居處，不可以守位。 倉儲蕩耗，財力傾殫，不足以聚人。 山東病水潦，江左困輸轉。 國匱於上，人窮於下。 如令邊埸少曌，恐逋亡遂多，盜賊群行，何財召募？ 何眾閑遏乎？ 又崇作寺觀，功費浩廣。 今山東歲饑，糟糠不厭。 而投艱厄之會，收庸、調之半，用籲嗟之物，以榮土木，恐怨結三靈，謗蒙四海。
The Book of Changes also says, "By what does one keep one's throne? By benevolence. By what does one gather the people? By wealth. " The people are impoverished and unsettled—they cannot keep the throne. Granaries are emptied and resources spent—not enough to gather the people. The east suffers floods; the lower Yangtze region is strained by transport levies. The state is depleted above and the people exhausted below. If the frontier stirs even slightly, fugitives will multiply and bandits gather—what wealth remains to raise troops? What force to hold them back? Temples and monasteries are raised at vast expense. East of the mountains famine reigns and even chaff is welcome. Yet in hardship half the corvée and tax levies are taken and the people's lamentations turned to glorify timber and stone—I fear the spirits will resent it and slander will spread across the realm.
13
又比緣征戍，巧詐百情，破役隱身，規脫租賦。 今道人私度者幾數十萬，其中高戶多丁，黠商大賈，詭作臺符，羼名偽度。 且國計軍防，並仰丁口，今丁皆出家，兵悉入道，征行租賦，何以備之？
Lately military campaigns have bred a hundred forms of fraud—evading corvée by concealment and scheming to escape taxes. Privately ordained clergy now number nearly a hundred thousand—wealthy households with many sons, shrewd merchants forging imperial tallies for false ordination. State revenue and defense both depend on registered males; now males take orders and soldiers enter the clergy—how will campaigns and taxes be met?
14
又重賂貴近，補府若史，移沒籍產，以州縣甲等更為下戶。 當道城鎮，至無捉驛者，役逮小弱，即破其家。 願許十道使訪察括取，使奸猾不得而隱。
Heavy bribes to court favorites win prefectural clerkships; registered property is seized and household grades lowered by bribery. Along main routes towns sometimes lack men even for relay stations; when corvée falls on the weak, households are ruined at once. Let commissioners of the ten circuits investigate and register them so the cunning cannot hide.
15
又太常樂戶已多，復求訪散樂，獨持大鼓者已二萬員，願量留之，余勒還籍，以杜妄費。
Court musicians are already numerous, yet scattered performers are sought—drummers alone number twenty thousand. Retain a measured corps and return the rest to registration to stop waste.
16
中宗以嶠身宰相，乃自陳失政，丐罷官，無所嫁非，手詔詰讓。 嶠惶恐，復視事。
Emperor Zhongzong, Jiao being chancellor himself, took his confession of misrule and plea to resign as blame-shifting without cause and rebuked him by handwritten edict. Jiao was alarmed and resumed office.
17
三年，加修文館大學士，封趙國公，以特進同中書門下三品。 睿宗立，罷政事，下除懷州刺史，致仕。 初，中宗崩，嶠嘗密請相王諸子不宜留京師。 及玄宗嗣位，獲其表宮中，或請誅之。 張說曰：「嶠誠懵逆順，然為當時謀，吠非其主，不可追罪。 」天子亦顧數更赦，遂免，貶滁州別駕，聽隨子虔州刺史暢之官。 改廬州別駕，卒，年七十。
In year three he was made grand academician of the Hall for Cultivating Literature, enfeoffed Duke of Zhao, and given third rank equal to the Secretariat and Chancellery as special advancement. When Emperor Ruizong acceded, Jiao left government, was demoted to prefect of Huai, and retired. Earlier, at Emperor Zhongzong's death, Jiao had secretly urged that the Prince of Xiang's sons should not remain in the capital. When Emperor Xuanzong succeeded, the memorial was found in the palace and some called for his execution. Zhang Yue said, "Jiao was indeed confused about loyalty, yet he advised for his own day; a dog barks at whoever is not its master—past fault should not be punished. " The emperor also weighed repeated amnesties and spared him, demoting him to vice prefect of Chu with permission to accompany his son Chang, prefect of Qian, to his post. Reassigned to vice prefect of Lu, he died at seventy.
18
嶠富才思，有所屬綴，人多傳諷。 武後時，汜水獲瑞石，嶠為御史，上《皇符》一篇，為世譏薄。 然其仕前與王勃、楊盈川接，中與崔融、蘇味道齊名，晚諸人沒，而為文章宿老，一時學者取法焉。
Jiao was richly gifted; whatever he wrote people circulated and recited. Under Empress Wu a propitious stone was found at Sishui; Jiao, then censor, submitted "Imperial Talisman"—for which the age mocked him. Yet early he moved in Wang Bo's circle and Yang Yingchuan's; mid-career he stood with Cui Rong and Su Weidao; in old age, after the others had passed, he was the senior master of letters whom scholars emulated.
19
蕭至忠，沂州丞人。 祖德言，為秘書少監。 至忠少與友期諸路，會雨雪，人引避，至忠曰：「寧有與人期可以失信？ 」卒友至乃去，眾嘆服。 仕為伊闕、洛陽尉。 遷監察御史，劾奏鳳閣侍郎蘇味道贓貪，超拜吏部員外郎。 至忠長擊斷，譽聞當時。 中宗神龍初，為御史中丞。 始，至忠為御史，而李承嘉為大夫，嘗讓諸御史曰：「彈事有不咨大夫，可乎？ 」眾不敢對，至忠獨曰：「故事，臺無長官。 御史，天子耳目也，其所請奏當專達，若大夫許而後論，即劾大夫者，又誰白哉？ 」承嘉慚。 至是，承嘉為戶部尚書，至忠劾祝欽明、竇希玠與承嘉等罪，百寮震悚。 遷吏部侍郎，猶兼中丞。
Xiao Zhizhong came from Cheng in Yi Prefecture. His grandfather Deyan was vice director of the Secretariat Archives. As a youth Zhizhong had arranged to meet a friend on the road; when snow fell people urged shelter, but he said, "How can one keep an appointment and break faith? " He left only when his friend arrived; onlookers marveled. He served as aide of Yique and Luoyang. Promoted investigating censor, he impeached Vice Minister Su Weidao of the Phoenix Pavilion for graft and was specially advanced to vice director of Personnel. Zhizhong was famed for decisive judgment. Early in Shenlong under Emperor Zhongzong he became chief investigating censor. Earlier, when Zhizhong was a censor under chief censor Li Chengjia, Chengjia once reproved the censors: "Can impeachments go forward without consulting the chief? " None replied; only Zhizhong said, "By precedent the censorate has no superior officer. Censors are the emperor's eyes and ears; their memorials should reach him directly. If the chief must approve first, who will report the chief when he is impeached? " Chengjia was shamed. Now Chengjia was minister of revenue; Zhizhong impeached Zhu Qinming, Dou Xijie, Chengjia, and others—the whole court trembled. He was promoted to vice minister of Personnel while continuing as chief investigating censor.
20
節湣太子以兵誅武三思而敗，宗楚客等諗侍御史冉祖雍上變，言相王與太子謀。 帝欲按之，至忠泣曰：「往者，天後欲以相王為太子，而王不食累日，獨請迎陛下，其讓德天下莫不聞。 陛下貴為天子，不能容一弟，受人羅織耶？ 竊為陛下不取。 」帝納其言，止。 尋授中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事。 上疏陳時政曰：
When Crown Prince Jiemin failed in his armed attempt to kill Wu Sansi, Zong Chuke and others persuaded attendant censor Ran Zuyong to submit an urgent report alleging that the Prince of Xiang had conspired with the Crown Prince. The emperor wanted to pursue the matter, but Zhizhong wept and said, "In the past, when the empress wished to make the Prince of Xiang crown prince, he refused food for days and insisted on welcoming Your Majesty back. His humility and virtue were known throughout the realm. Your Majesty stands exalted as Son of Heaven—can you not tolerate one younger brother and instead accept others' fabricated charges? I privately believe this would be unworthy of Your Majesty. The emperor accepted his advice and dropped the investigation. He was soon appointed vice director of the Secretariat with the concurrent title of associate chief minister. He submitted a memorial on current affairs, stating:
21
求治之道，首於用賢。 茍非其才則官曠，官曠則事廢，事廢則人殘，歷代所以陵遲者此也。 今授職用人，多因貴要為粉飾，上下相蒙，茍得為是。 夫官爵，公器也； 恩幸，私惠也。 王者正可金帛富之，梁肉食之，以存私澤也。 若公器而私用之，則公義不行而勞人解體，私謁開而正言塞。 日朘月削，卒見雕弊。
The path to good governance begins with appointing worthy men. When the unfit hold office, posts go unfilled by capable men; when capable men are absent, affairs are neglected; when affairs are neglected, the people suffer. This is how dynasties have declined through the ages. Today appointments are made largely to flatter the powerful and well connected; superiors and subordinates deceive one another, and mere self-advancement is treated as enough. For offices and ranks are instruments of the public good; while favor and patronage are private benefits. A ruler may properly enrich favorites with gold and silk and feed them with fine food, thereby maintaining a private bond of goodwill. But if public office is used for private ends, public duty fails and working people lose heart; private petitions flourish while honest counsel is silenced. Resources are eroded day by day and cut away month by month, until the state is utterly drained.
22
今列位已廣，冗員復倍。 陛下降不呰之澤，近戚有無涯之請，臺閣之內，朱紫充滿，官秩益輕，恩賞彌數。 才者不用，用者不才，故人不效力，官匪其人，欲求治固難矣。
The ranks of office are already numerous, and redundant appointees have doubled again. Your Majesty dispenses ungrudging largess, while close relatives make endless demands. Within the halls of government, crimson and purple robes crowd every seat—ranks grow ever cheaper, and rewards ever more frequent. The talented go unused, and those who are used lack talent. Men therefore exert no effort, and offices do not match their holders. Under such conditions, good governance is indeed hard to achieve.
23
又宰相要官子弟，多居美爵，並罕才藝，而更相諉托。 《詩》云：「私人之子，百寮是試。 或以其酒，不以其漿，鞙鞙佩遂，不以其長。 」此言王政不平而眾官廢職，私家子列試榮班，徒長其佩爾。 臣願陛下愛惜爵賞，官無虛授，進大雅以樞近，退小人於閑左，使政令惟一，私不害公，則天下幸甚。 且貞觀故事，宰相子弟多居外職，非直抑強宗，亦以擇賢才爾。 請自宰相及諸司長官子弟，並授外官，共寧百性，表裏相統。
Moreover, the sons of chancellors and high officials mostly hold fine ranks, yet rarely possess real ability, and instead take turns recommending and protecting one another. The Book of Odes says, "The sons of favorites—let all the hundred offices try them. They may offer their wine but not their water; their tinkling girdle-pendants hang full, yet not by their own merit." This describes a time when royal government was unjust and officials neglected their duties—sons of private favorites were placed in honorary ranks, lengthening their sashes and pendants and nothing more. I beg Your Majesty to value offices and rewards and appoint no post in vain: advance men of true integrity to positions near the center of power, and retire petty men to idle posts, so that government commands may be unified and private interest may not harm the public good. Then all under Heaven would be greatly fortunate. Moreover, under the precedent of the Zhenguan reign, the sons of chancellors mostly held posts outside the capital—not only to restrain powerful clans, but also to select men of talent. I ask that the sons of chancellors and department chiefs alike be appointed to outside posts, so that together they may bring peace to the people and inner and outer offices may be properly coordinated.

突厥默啜請尚公主，逢堯以御史中丞攝鴻臚卿，報可。 默啜遣貴近頡利來曰：「詔送金鏤具鞍，乃塗金，非天子意。 使者不可信，雖得公主，猶非實，請罷和親。 」欲馳去，左右色動，逢堯呼曰：「我大國使，不受我辭，可輒去。 」乃牽持其人謂曰：「漢法重女婿而送鞍具，欲安且久，不以金為貴。 可汗乃貪金而不貴信邪？ 」默啜聞曰：「漢使至吾國眾矣，斯食鐵石人，不可易。 」因備禮以見。 逢堯說之曰：「天子昔為單於都護，思與可汗通舊好，可汗當向風慕義，襲冠冕，取重諸蕃。 」默啜信之，為斂發紫衣，南面再拜稱臣，遣子入朝。 逢堯以使有指，擢戶部侍郎。 坐善太平公主，斥朗州司馬，終柘州刺史。 逢堯詼詭，當大事敢僥福，故卒以附麗廢，然唐興奉使者稱逢堯。
When the Türkic khan Mohechu requested marriage to a princess, Xianyao as chief investigating censor acting director of guests for embassies reported approval. Mohechu sent a noble close kin named Jieli, who said, "The edict sent a gold-inlaid saddle, yet it is only gold-plated—not the Son of Heaven's intent. The envoy cannot be trusted; though we obtain the princess it is still not genuine—please stop the marriage alliance." He wished to gallop away; those at his side changed color; Xianyao called out, "I am an envoy of the great state—you will not accept my words yet dare to leave at once!" He then seized the man and said, "By Han law the son-in-law is honored and saddle and fittings are sent for lasting peace—not because gold is prized. Can the khan covet gold and not prize good faith?" Mohechu heard and said, "Han envoys have come to my state in great numbers, but this man eats iron and stone—he cannot be changed." He therefore prepared full rites to receive him. Xianyao persuaded him: "The Son of Heaven formerly served as protector of the khans and wishes to renew old friendship; the khan should face the wind and admire righteousness, don cap and robes, and gain weight among the barbarians." Mohechu believed him, donned purple robes, faced south and twice bowed calling himself subject, and sent his son to court. Because the envoy had achieved his aim, Xianyao was promoted to vice minister of Revenue. Punished for attachment to Princess Taiping, he was demoted to secretary of Lang Prefecture and ended as prefect of Zhe. Xianyao was witty and crafty; on great affairs he dared gamble for fortune—hence he was ruined through clinging to power, yet since Tang's rise envoys abroad have praised Xianyao.
41
贊曰：異哉，玄宗之器蕭至忠也，不亦惑乎！ 至忠本非賢，而寄賢以奸利，失之則邀利以喪賢，姻艷後，挾寵主，取宰相，謀間王室，身誅家破，遺臭無窮。 而帝以乾曜似之，遽使當國，是帝舉不知至忠之不可用，又不知乾曜之所可用也。 或稱帝不以罪掩才，益可怪嘆。 嗚呼！ 力士誠腐夫庸人，不能發擿天子之迷，若曰「至忠賢於初，固不繆於末； 既繆於末，果不賢於初。 惟陛下圖之」，如是，帝且悟往失而精來鑒已。 其後相李林甫、將安祿山，皆基於不明，身播岷陬，信自取之歟。
The commentator says: How strange—Emperor Xuanzong's esteem for Xiao Zhizhong—is it not confused! Zhizhong was originally not worthy, yet he borrowed worth for treacherous profit; when he lost it he courted profit and lost worth; he married into the empress's splendor, relied on her favor, took the chancellorship, and schemed against the royal house—his person was executed, his house destroyed, and his infamy endures without end. Yet the emperor, because Qianyao resembled him, promptly had him direct the state—thus the emperor neither knew Zhizhong could not be used nor knew what in Qianyao could be used. Some praise the emperor for not letting crimes mask talent—this is all the more strange and lamentable. Alas! Lishi was truly a rotten petty man who could not expose the Son of Heaven's confusion; had he said, "Zhizhong was worthy at the start and was not wrong at the end; once wrong at the end, he was truly not worthy at the start. Having been wrong at the end, he was indeed not worthy at the start. May Your Majesty consider this"—then the emperor would have awakened to past error and refined future judgment. Afterward in appointing Li Linfu and entrusting An Lushan, both sprang from lack of discernment; his person was cast to the remote Min mountains—was it not truly self-wrought?
