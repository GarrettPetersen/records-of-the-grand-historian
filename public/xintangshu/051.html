1
食貨一
Finance and Economics, Part 1
2
古之善治其國而愛養斯民者，必立經常簡易之法，使上愛物以養其下，下勉力以事其上，上足而下不困。 故量人之力而授之田，量地之產而取以給公上，量其入而出之以為用度之數。 是三者常相須以濟而不可失，失其一則不能守其二。 及暴君庸主，縱其佚欲，而茍且之吏從之，變制合時以取寵於其上。 故用於上者無節，而取於下者無限，民竭其力而不能供，由是上愈不足而下愈困，則財利之說興，而聚斂之臣用。 《記》曰：「寧畜盜臣。 」盜臣誠可惡，然一人之害爾。 聚斂之臣用，則經常之法壞，而下不勝其弊焉。
In ancient times, rulers who governed well and cared for their people always set up steady, straightforward institutions, so that the court valued resources to support the common folk while the people worked diligently to serve the throne—enough for the state above and no hardship among the people below. They matched each person's capacity when allotting farmland, took from the land's yield only what was needed for public revenue, and balanced intake against outlay to set the budget. These three principles had to work together; if any one failed, the other two could not hold. When tyrants and weak sovereigns gave free rein to their appetites, short-sighted officials went along, changing the rules to suit the moment and curry favor at court. Court spending knew no bounds while levies on the people knew no limit; the populace gave everything they had and still could not meet demand. As the treasury grew emptier and the people poorer, schemes for squeezing wealth proliferated and ministers who specialized in extraction came to power. The 《Classic of Rites》 says: "Better to tolerate a corrupt official." A corrupt official is truly abominable, but his damage falls on a single person alone. When extractive ministers take charge, the standing institutions collapse, and the people cannot endure the harm.
3
唐之始時，授人以口分、世業田，而取之以租、庸、調之法，其用之也有節。 蓋其畜兵以府衛之制，故兵雖多而無所損； 設官有常員之數，故官不濫而易祿。 雖不及三代之盛時，然亦可以為經常之法也。 及其弊也，兵冗官濫，為之大蠹。 自天寶以來，大盜屢起，方鎮數叛，兵革之興，累世不息，而用度之數，不能節矣。 加以驕君昏主，奸吏邪臣，取濟一時，屢更其制，而經常之法，蕩然盡矣。 由是財利之說興，聚斂之臣進。 蓋口分、世業之田壞而為兼並，租、庸、調之法壞而為兩稅。 至於鹽鐵、轉運、屯田、和糴、鑄錢、括苗、榷利、借商、進奉、獻助，無所不為矣。 蓋愈煩而愈弊，以至於亡焉。
When the Tang dynasty was founded, the state allotted per-capita and hereditary farmland to the people and collected the grain tax, corvée levy, and textile tax under the zu-yong-diao system, while keeping spending within bounds. Troops were raised through the fubing garrison system, so although the army was large it did not drain the realm; and offices had fixed headcounts, so the bureaucracy did not swell and salaries remained affordable. It fell short of the golden ages of the Three Dynasties, yet it could still stand as a durable fiscal order. When the system decayed, bloated armies and swollen bureaucracies became its gravest plague. From the Tianbao reign onward, major rebellions broke out again and again, frontier commanders rose in repeated revolt, and war went on from one generation to the next without pause, while state spending could no longer be held in check. Arrogant emperors and muddled courts, together with corrupt clerks and scheming ministers, patched over each crisis with stopgap measures, changing the rules again and again until the standing order vanished entirely. Profit-seeking policies multiplied, and ministers devoted to extraction rose to power. Per-capita and hereditary allotments gave way to land concentration; the zu-yong-diao taxes were replaced by the two-tax system. Salt and iron monopolies, transport offices, military farming colonies, state grain purchases, coinage, cadastral surveys, excise monopolies, loans to merchants, tribute deliveries, and voluntary contributions to the throne—every expedient was tried. The more elaborate the schemes grew, the deeper the rot ran, until the dynasty fell.
4
唐制：度田以步，其闊一步，其長二百四十步為畝，百畝為頃。 凡民始生為黃，四歲為小，十六為中，二十一為丁，六十為老。 授田之制，丁及男年十八以上者，人一頃，其八十畝為口分，二十畝為永業； 老及篤疾、廢疾者，人四十畝，寡妻妾三十畝，當戶者增二十畝，皆以二十畝為永業，其余為口分。 永業之田，樹以榆、棗、桑及所宜之木，皆有數。 田多可以足其人者為寬鄉，少者為狹鄉。 狹鄉授田，減寬鄉之半。 其地有薄厚，歲一易者，倍受之。 寬鄉三易者，不倍授。 工商者，寬鄉減半，狹鄉不給。 凡庶人徙鄉及貧無以葬者，得賣世業田。 自狹鄉而徙寬鄉者，得並賣口分田。 已賣者，不復授。 死者收之，以授無田者。 凡收授皆以歲十月。 授田先貧及有課役者。 凡田，鄉有余以給比鄉，縣有余以給比縣，州有余以給近州。
Under Tang law, land was measured in paces: a field one pace wide and two hundred forty paces long counted as one mu, and one hundred mu made one qing. Commoners were classified by age: newborns as infants, four-year-olds as children, sixteen-year-olds as youths, twenty-one-year-olds as adult males liable for service, and sixty-year-olds as elderly. Under the allotment system, each adult male and every man of eighteen or older received one qing of land: eighty mu as a per-capita grant and twenty mu as a permanent holding; the elderly and the severely disabled received forty mu each, widows and concubines thirty mu, and household heads an extra twenty mu; in each case twenty mu were permanent holdings and the rest per-capita allotments. Permanent holdings had to be planted with prescribed numbers of elm, jujube, mulberry, and other suitable trees. Areas with enough land for everyone were designated broad districts; those with too little land were narrow districts. Narrow districts received allotments half the size of those in broad districts. Where the soil was poor or fields were rotated annually, recipients received double allotments. In broad districts with three-field rotation, no doubling was granted. Merchants and artisans received half allotments in broad districts and none in narrow districts. Commoners who moved to another district or could not afford a funeral were allowed to sell their permanent holdings. Those who moved from a narrow to a broad district could sell their per-capita allotments as well. Land that had been sold was not re-allotted. On the holder's death, the land reverted to the state and was given to the landless. All reclamation and distribution of land took place in the tenth month. Grants went first to the poor and to households liable for tax and labor service. Surplus land in a district went to neighboring districts, surplus in a county to neighboring counties, and surplus in a prefecture to nearby prefectures.
5
凡授田者，丁歲輸粟二斛，稻三斛，謂之租。 丁隨鄉所出，歲輸絹二匹，綾、絁二丈，布加五之一，綿三兩，麻三斤，非蠶鄉則輸銀十四兩，謂之調。 用人之力，歲二十日，閏加二日，不役者日為絹三尺，謂之庸。 有事而加役二十五日者免調，三十日者租、調皆免。 通正役不過五十日。
Every allottee paid annually, per adult male, two hu of millet or three hu of rice; this was the grain tax. Each adult male paid yearly, according to local products, two bolts of silk, two zhang of patterned and coarse silk, cloth with an extra fifth, three liang of cotton floss, and three jin of hemp—or, in districts without sericulture, fourteen liang of silver; this was the textile tax. Labor service was twenty days a year, with two extra days in leap years; those exempt from corvée paid three chi of silk per day instead; this was the corvée levy. If extra labor reached twenty-five days, the textile tax was waived; at thirty days, both grain and textile taxes were waived. Regular corvée service could not exceed fifty days in all.
6
自王公以下，皆有永業田。 太皇太后、皇太后、皇后緦麻以上親，內命婦一品以上親，郡王及五品以上祖父兄弟，職事、勛官三品以上有封者若縣男父子，國子、太學、四門學生、俊士，孝子、順孫、義夫、節婦同籍者，皆免課役。 凡主戶內有課口者為課戶。 若老及男廢疾、篤疾、寡妻妾、部曲、客女、奴婢及視九品以上官，不課。
From princes and dukes down, everyone held permanent land grants. Kin within mourning for the grand empress dowager, empress dowager, and empress; kin of palace ladies of first rank and above; grandfathers and brothers of imperial princes and fifth-rank officials and up; fathers and sons of third-rank officeholders and merit nobles with fiefs such as county barons; students of the national academies and distinguished scholars; and filial sons, obedient grandsons, righteous husbands, and chaste widows on the same household register were all exempt from tax and labor service. Principal households with members liable for tax were classified as taxable households. The elderly, disabled men, the severely ill, widows and concubines, retainers, guest women, slaves, and persons ranked as ninth-rank officials or higher were not liable for tax.
7
凡裏有手實，歲終具民之年與地之闊狹，為鄉帳。 鄉成於縣，縣成於州，州成於戶部。 又有計帳，具來歲課役以報度支。 國有所須，先奏而斂。 凡稅斂之數，書於縣門、村坊，與眾知之。 水、旱、霜、蝗耗十四者，免其租； 桑麻盡者，免其調； 田耗十之六者，免租調； 耗七者，課、役皆免。 凡新附之戶，春以三月免役，夏以六月免課，秋以九月課、役皆免。 徙寬鄉者，縣覆於州，出境則覆於戶部，官以閑月達之。 自畿內徙畿外，自京縣徙余縣，皆有禁。 四夷降戶，附以寬鄉，給復十年。 奴婢縱為良人，給復三年。 沒外蕃人，一年還者給復三年，二年者給復四年，三年者給復五年。 浮民、部曲、客女、奴婢縱為良者附寬鄉。
Each neighborhood kept household registers; at year's end ages and land holdings were compiled into district tax rolls. District rolls were combined at the county, county rolls at the prefecture, and prefectural rolls at the Ministry of Revenue. Projected accounts also listed the coming year's taxes and labor obligations for report to the expenditure office. Whenever the state required revenue, it sought approval before collecting. Tax amounts were posted at county gates and village wards for public notice. If flood, drought, frost, or locusts destroyed fourteen-tenths of the crop, the grain tax was remitted; if mulberry and hemp were wholly destroyed, the textile tax was remitted; if six-tenths of the fields were ruined, both grain and textile taxes were remitted; at seven-tenths loss, all taxes and labor service were waived. Newly registered households were exempt from corvée by the third month, from the grain tax by the sixth month, and from all taxes and service by the ninth month. Migrants to broad districts were verified by the county and reported to the prefecture; if they crossed jurisdictional lines, the Ministry of Revenue was notified, with reports filed in off-season months. Moves from the capital region to the provinces, or from metropolitan counties to other counties, were all restricted. Households of surrendered foreigners were settled in broad districts with ten years' tax and service exemption. Freed slaves received three years' exemption from tax and corvée. Frontier peoples captured abroad who returned within one year received three years' exemption; within two years, four years; within three years, five years. Vagrants, retainers, guest women, and freed slaves were registered in broad districts.
8
貞觀中，初稅草以給諸閑，而驛馬有牧田。
During the Zhenguan reign, a fodder tax was introduced for miscellaneous state needs, and post-station horses were given pasture land.
9
太宗方銳意於治，官吏考課，以鰥寡少者進考，如增戶法； 失勸導者以減戶論。 配租以斂穫早晚、險易、遠近為差。 庸、調輸以八月，發以九月。 同時輸者先遠民。 皆自概量。 州府歲市土所出為貢，其價視絹之上下，無過五十匹。 異物、滋味、口馬、鷹犬，非有詔不獻。 有加配，則以代租賦。 其兇荒則有社倉賑給，不足則徙民就食諸州。 尚書左丞戴胄建議：「自王公以下，計墾田，秋熟，所在為義倉，歲兇以給民。 」太宗善之，乃詔：「畝稅二升，粟、麥、秔、稻，隨土地所宜。 寬鄉斂以所種，狹鄉據青苗簿而督之。 田耗十四者免其半，耗十七者皆免之。 商賈無田者，以其戶為九等，出粟自五石至於五斗為差。 下下戶及夷獠不取焉。 歲不登，則以賑民； 或貸為種子，則至秋而償。 」其後洛、相、幽、徐、齊、並、秦、蒲州又置常平倉，粟藏九年，米藏五年，下濕之地，粟藏五年，米藏三年，皆著於令。
Emperor Taizong was then bent on good government; in performance reviews, officials were promoted for districts with few widowers and orphans, as under the household-growth statute; and those who failed to encourage settlement were penalized as if they had lost population. Grain tax collection was scheduled according to harvest timing, terrain, and distance. Corvée payments and textile taxes were due in the eighth month and forwarded in the ninth. When several districts paid at once, remoter districts went first. Recipients measured the goods themselves. Each year prefectures purchased local products as tribute, valued against silk grades and capped at fifty bolts. Exotic goods, delicacies, riding horses, hawks, and hounds were not sent as tribute without imperial order. Extra tribute assignments could be substituted for regular taxes. In famine, community granaries provided relief; if stores ran short, people were relocated to other prefectures for food. Left Vice Minister Dai Zhou proposed: "From princes and dukes down, tally cultivated land; after the autumn harvest, establish charity granaries in each locality to feed the people in bad years." Taizong approved and decreed: "A levy of two sheng per mu, in millet, wheat, or rice as the soil allowed." Broad districts were taxed on what was actually planted; narrow districts were assessed from the green-crop register. A fourteen-tenths crop loss brought half remission; at seventeen-tenths, full remission. Landless merchants were ranked in nine household grades, contributing from five shi down to five dou of grain. The poorest households and non-Han frontier peoples were exempt. In lean years the grain was used for relief; or lent as seed to be repaid after the autumn harvest. Later Ever-Normal granaries were also set up in Luoyang, Xiangzhou, Youzhou, Xuzhou, Qizhou, Bingzhou, Qinzhou, and Puzhou: millet stored nine years and rice five, or in damp regions millet five years and rice three—all codified in law.
10
貞觀初，戶不及三百萬，絹一匹易米一斗。 至四年，米斗四五錢，外戶不閉者數月，馬牛被野，人行數千里不賫糧，民物蕃息，四夷降附者百二十萬人。 是歲，天下斷獄，死罪者二十九人，號稱太平。 此高祖、太宗致治之大略，及其成效如此。
Early in the Zhenguan era there were fewer than three million households, and one bolt of silk bought only one dou of rice. By the fourth year rice sold for four or five cash per dou; outer gates stood unbarred for months; horses and cattle grazed in the open fields; travelers went thousands of li without provisions; population and wealth prospered; and 1.2 million foreigners submitted. That year only twenty-nine people were executed for capital crimes empire-wide, and the reign was hailed as an age of great peace. Such was the broad fiscal policy by which Gaozu and Taizong brought order, and such were its results.
11
高宗承之，海內艾安。 太尉長孫無忌等輔政，天下未見失德。 數引刺史入閤，問民疾苦。 即位之歲，增戶十五萬。 及中書令李義府、侍中許敬宗既用事，役費並起。 永淳以後，給用益不足。 加以武後之亂，紀綱大壞，民不勝其毒。
Gaozong inherited this order, and the realm remained at peace. With Zhangsun Wuji and others guiding policy, the court showed no serious lapse in governance. He repeatedly summoned prefects to court to ask about the people's hardships. In the year he took the throne, registered households rose by 150,000. Once Chief Minister Li Yifu and Palace Attendant Xu Jingzong gained power, labor levies and state spending both surged. After the Yongchun era, revenue increasingly failed to meet expenses. Then came the chaos of Empress Wu's reign; government collapsed and the people could not endure the oppression.
12
玄宗初立求治，蠲徭役者給蠲符，以流外及九品京官為蠲使，歲再遣之。 開元八年，頒庸調法於天下，好不過精，惡不至濫，闊者一尺八寸，長者四丈。 然是時天下戶未嘗升降。 監察御史宇文融獻策：括籍外羨田、逃戶，自占者給復五年，每丁稅錢千五百，以攝御史分行括實。 陽翟尉皇甫憬上書言其不可。 玄宗方任用融，乃貶憬為盈川尉。 諸道所括得客戶八十余萬，田亦稱是。 州縣希旨張虛數，以正田為羨，編戶為客，歲終，籍錢數百萬緡。
When Xuanzong first sought to restore good government, those exempted from corvée received exemption certificates; junior capital officials served as exemption commissioners and were sent out twice a year. In Kaiyuan 8 the corvée and textile tax standards were issued empire-wide: top quality could not exceed the fine grade, poor quality could not fall below the lowest acceptable grade; width at least one chi eight cun and length no more than four zhang. Yet household registers empire-wide had never been properly updated. Investigating Censor Yuwen Rong proposed registering unreported surplus land and fugitive households: self-reporting households received five years' exemption and paid 1,500 cash per adult male; acting censors were dispatched to verify the rolls. Yangdi District Assistant Huangfu Jing submitted a memorial arguing against the plan. Xuanzong, then relying on Rong, demoted Jing to assistant magistrate of Yingchuan. The circuits registered more than 800,000 client households and a corresponding amount of land. Prefectures and districts, eager to please, inflated the rolls—reporting regular fields as surplus land and registered households as client households—and by year's end had logged several million strings of cash.
13
十六年，乃詔每三歲以九等定籍。 而庸調折租所取華好，州縣長宮勸織，中書門下察濫惡以貶官吏，精者褒賞之。 二十二年，詔男十五、女十三以上得嫁娶。 州縣歲上戶口登耗，采訪使覆實之，刺史、縣令以為課最。
In the sixteenth year an edict required household registers to be ranked in nine grades every three years. Corvée payments, textile taxes, and grain-tax conversions had to be of the finest quality; local officials urged weaving; the Secretariat inspected inferior goods and demoted officials, while rewarding excellence. In the twenty-second year an edict permitted marriage for men of fifteen and women of thirteen and above. Each year prefectures reported household gains and losses; investigating commissioners verified the figures, and prefects and magistrates were chiefly judged on this performance.
14
初，永徽中禁買賣世業、口分田。 其後豪富兼並，貧者失業，於是詔買者還地而罰之。
Initially, during the Yonghui era, the sale of permanent holdings and per-capita allotments was forbidden. Later the rich consolidated land and the poor lost their livelihoods, so an edict required buyers to return the land and imposed penalties.
15
先是楊州租、調以錢，嶺南以米，安南以絲，益州以羅、䌷、綾、絹供春彩。 因詔江南亦以布代租。
Earlier Yangzhou paid grain and textile taxes in cash, Lingnan in rice, Annan in silk, and Yizhou supplied gauze, crepe, damask, and silk for spring tribute cloth. An edict then allowed south of the Yangzi to pay the grain tax in cloth instead.
16
中書令李林甫以租庸、丁防、和糴、春彩、稅草無定法，歲為旨符，遣使一告，費紙五十余萬。 條目既多，覆問逾年，乃與采訪朝集使議革之，為長行旨，以授朝集使及送旨符使，歲有所支，進畫附驛以達，每州不過二紙。
Chief Minister Li Linfu noted that grain tax, corvée, garrison service, government grain purchases, spring tribute cloth, and fodder tax had no fixed rules; each year edict tallies were sent out by messenger at a cost of more than five hundred thousand sheets of paper. With so many entries, verification took more than a year; he then worked with investigating and assembly commissioners to reform the system into standing regulations for assembly commissioners and edict couriers; annual expenditures were listed, sent by sealed relay post, with no more than two sheets per prefecture.
17
凡庸、調、租、資課，皆任土所宜，州縣長官涖定粗良，具上中下三物之樣輸京都。 有濫惡，督中物之直。 二十五年，以江、淮輸運有河、洛之艱，而關中蠶桑少，菽粟常賤，乃命庸、調、資課皆以米，兇年樂輸布絹者亦從之。 河南、北不通運州，租皆為絹，代關中庸、課，詔度支減轉運。
Corvée, textile, grain, and resource levies all followed local products; prefectural and district chiefs set coarse and fine grades and sent samples of upper, middle, and lower quality to the capital. Shoddy goods were charged at middle-grade prices. In the twenty-fifth year, because shipping from the Jiang-Huai region faced the difficulties of the Yellow and Luo routes while Guanzhong had little sericulture and grain was cheap, corvée, textile, and resource levies were ordered paid in rice; in famine years those willing to pay in cloth and silk were also permitted. In Henan and Hebei, prefectures without transport links paid the grain tax entirely in silk to substitute for Guanzhong corvée and levies, and the expenditure office was ordered to cut transport costs.
18
明年，又詔民三歲以下為黃，十五以下為小，二十以下為中。 又以民間戶高丁多者，率與父母別籍異居，以避征戍，乃詔十丁以上免二丁，五丁以上免一丁，侍丁孝者免徭役。 天寶三載，更民十八以上為中男，二十三以上成丁。 五載，詔貧不能自濟者，每鄉免三十丁租庸。 男子七十五以上、婦人七十以上，中男一人為侍； 八十以上以令式從事。 是時，海內富實，米鬥之價錢十三，青、齊間鬥才三錢，絹一匹錢二百。 道路列肆，具酒食以待行人，店有驛驢，行千里不持尺兵。 天下歲入之物，租錢二百余萬緡，粟千九百八十余萬斛，庸、調絹七百四十萬匹，綿百八十余萬屯，布千三十五萬余端。 天子驕於佚樂而用不知節，大抵用物之數，常過其所入。 於是錢谷之臣，始事朘刻。 太府卿楊崇禮句剝分銖，有欠折漬損者，州縣督送，歷年不止。 其子慎矜專知太府，次子慎名知京倉，亦以苛刻結主恩。 王鉷為戶口色役使，歲進錢百億萬緡，非租庸正額者，積百寶大盈庫，以供天子燕私。 及安祿山反，司空楊國忠以為正庫物不可以給士，遣侍御史崔眾至太原納錢度僧尼道士，旬日得百萬緡而已。 自兩京陷沒，民物耗弊，天下蕭然。
The next year another edict classified those under three as infants, under fifteen as children, and under twenty as youths. Because wealthy households with many adult sons commonly split registers and lived apart from parents to avoid conscription, an edict exempted two adult males from households of ten or more and one from households of five or more; filial attendants were exempt from corvée. In Tianbao 3 males of eighteen and above were reclassified as youths, and those of twenty-three and above as adult males liable for service. In the fifth year an edict exempted thirty adult males per district from grain tax and corvée among those too poor to support themselves. Men seventy-five and above and women seventy and above were granted one youth as attendant; those eighty and above were treated according to statute. At this time the realm was prosperous: rice sold for thirteen cash per dou, only three cash in Qing and Qi, and two hundred cash per bolt of silk. Shops lined the roads with wine and food for travelers; inns kept relay donkeys; one could travel a thousand li unarmed. Annual revenue empire-wide included more than two million strings in grain-tax cash, more than 19.8 million hu of grain, 7.4 million bolts of silk for corvée and textile taxes, more than 1.8 million bales of cotton, and more than 10.35 million bolts of cloth. The emperor grew arrogant in pleasure and knew no restraint in spending; consumption routinely exceeded revenue. Revenue officials then began to practice extortion and petty exaction. Grand Treasury Director Yang Chongli wrung out every penny; shortages, spoilage, or damage were chased down by prefectures and districts year after year without end. His son Shenjin ran the Grand Treasury and his second son Shenming the capital granaries; both also curried favor through harsh exaction. Wang Hong served as commissioner for household registers and corvée categories, annually presenting ten billion strings; sums outside regular grain and corvée quotas went to the Great Abundance Treasury for the emperor's private use. When An Lushan rebelled, Minister of Works Yang Guozhong argued that regular treasury goods could not supply the army; he sent Attending Censor Cui Zhong to Taiyuan to collect cash by ordaining monks and priests, raising only one million strings in ten days. After the two capitals fell, people and goods were devastated and the realm lay in ruin.
19
肅宗即位，遣御史鄭叔清等籍江淮、蜀漢富商右族訾畜，十收其二，謂之率貸。 諸道亦稅商賈以贍軍，錢一千者有稅。 於是北海郡錄事參軍第五琦以錢谷得見，請於江淮置租庸使，吳鹽、蜀麻、銅冶皆有稅，市輕貨繇江陵、襄陽、上津路轉至鳳翔。 明年，鄭叔清與宰相裴冕建議，以天下用度不充，諸道得召人納錢，給空名告身，授官勛邑號； 度道士僧尼不可勝計； 納錢百千，賜明經出身； 商賈助軍者，給復。 及兩京平，又於關輔諸州，納錢度道士僧尼萬人。 而百姓殘於兵盜，米鬥至錢七千，鬻籺為糧，民行乞食者屬路。 乃詔能賑貧乏者，寵以爵衤失。
When Suzong took the throne, he sent Censor Zheng Shuqing and others to register the assets of wealthy merchants and great families in the Jiang-Huai, Shu, and Han regions, taking two-tenths—a levy called "assessed lending." The circuits also taxed merchants to support the army, levying on every thousand cash. Beihai District Recorder Diwu Qi then gained audience through fiscal expertise and proposed zu-yong commissioners in the Jiang-Huai; Wu salt, Shu hemp, and copper smelting were taxed; light goods were shipped via Jiangling, Xiangyang, and the Shangjin route to Fengxiang. The next year Zheng Shuqing and Chief Minister Pei Mian proposed that because state revenue was insufficient, circuits could accept cash for blank commission credentials granting offices, merit titles, and fief names; ordained Daoist priests and Buddhist monks and nuns beyond counting; those paying a hundred thousand cash received mingjing examination credentials; merchants who aided the army received tax and service exemption. When the two capitals were recovered, ten thousand monks and priests were ordained for cash in the Guanfu prefectures. Yet the people were ravaged by war and banditry; rice reached seven thousand cash per dou; chaff was sold as grain; and beggars lined the roads. An edict then honored those who relieved the poor with titles and robes of rank.
20
故事，天下財賦歸左藏，而太府以時上其數，尚書比部覆其出入。 是時，京師豪將假取不能禁，第五琦為度支鹽鐵使，請皆歸大盈庫，供天子給賜，主以中官。 自是天下之財為人君私藏，有司不得程其多少。
By precedent empire-wide revenue went to the Left Vault; the Grand Treasury reported figures on schedule and the Ministry of Revenue audited receipts and disbursements. Capital military grandees then borrowed funds without restraint; Diwu Qi as expenditure and salt commissioner proposed that all revenue go to the Great Abundance Treasury for imperial gifts, managed by palace eunuchs. From then on the realm's wealth became the emperor's private hoard, beyond official accounting.
21
廣德元年，詔一戶三丁者免一丁，凡畝稅二升，男子二十五為成丁，五十五為老，以優民。 而強寇未夷，民耗斂重。 及吐蕃逼京師，近甸屯兵數萬，百官進俸錢，又率戶以給軍糧。 至大歷元年，詔流民還者，給復二年，田園盡，則授以逃田。 天下苗一畝稅錢十五，市輕貨給百官手力課。 以國用急，不及秋，方苗青即征之，號「青苗錢」又有「地頭錢」，每畝二十，通名為青苗錢。 又詔上都秋稅分二等，上等畝稅一斗，下等六升，荒田畝稅二升。 五年，始定法：夏，上田畝稅六升，下田畝四升； 秋，上田畝稅五升，下田畝三升； 荒田如故； 青苗錢畝加一倍，而地頭錢不在焉。
In Guangde 1 an edict exempted one adult male from households of three; set a general levy of two sheng per mu; made twenty-five the age of full liability and fifty-five the age of elder exemption—to ease the people's burden. Yet powerful rebels were not pacified and levies still weighed heavily on the people. When Tibetans threatened the capital, tens of thousands of troops were garrisoned nearby; officials contributed salary funds and households were levied for army grain. By Dali 1 returning refugees received two years' exemption; if their land was gone, abandoned fields were granted. Empire-wide a seedling tax of fifteen cash per mu was levied; light goods were sold to fund officials' labor allowances. Because revenue was urgent, tax was collected while seedlings were still green before autumn—called "green-crop money"; there was also "field-head money" of twenty per mu; both were known as green-crop money. Another edict divided the capital's autumn tax into two grades: upper fields one dou per mu, lower six sheng, wasteland two sheng. In the fifth year the law was fixed: in summer, upper fields six sheng per mu, lower fields four; in autumn, upper fields five sheng per mu, lower three; wasteland as before; green-crop money was doubled per mu, but field-head money was excluded.
22
初，轉運使掌外，度支使掌內。 永泰二年，分天下財賦、鑄錢、常平、轉運、鹽鐵，置二使。 東都畿內、河南、淮南、江東西、湖南、荊南、山南東道，以轉運使劉晏領之； 京畿、關內、河東、劍南、山南西道，以京兆尹、判度支第五琦領之。 及琦貶，以戶部侍郎、判度支韓滉與晏分治。
Initially the transport commissioner managed external revenue and the expenditure commissioner internal revenue. In Yongtai 2 empire-wide revenue, coinage, Ever-Normal granaries, transport, and salt and iron were split between two commissioners. The Eastern Capital region, Henan, Huainan, Jiangdong and Jiangxi, Hunan, Jingnan, and Shannan East were assigned to Transport Commissioner Liu Yan; the Capital Metropolitan, Guannei, Hedong, Jiannan, and Shannan West circuits to Jingzhao Governor and acting expenditure commissioner Diwu Qi. When Qi was demoted, Vice Minister of Revenue and acting expenditure commissioner Han Huang shared administration with Yan.
23
時回紇有助收西京功，代宗厚遇之，與中國婚姻，歲送馬十萬匹，酬以縑帛百余萬匹。 而中國財力屈竭，歲負馬價。 河、湟六鎮既陷，歲發防秋兵三萬戍京西，資糧百五十余萬緡。 而中官魚朝恩方恃恩擅權，代宗與宰相元載日夜圖之。 及朝恩誅，帝復與載貳，君臣猜間不協，邊計兵食，置而不議者幾十年。 而諸鎮擅地，結為表裏，日治兵繕壘，天子不能繩以法，顓留意祠禱、焚幣玉、寫浮屠書，度支稟賜僧巫，歲以鉅萬計。 然帝性儉約，身所禦衣，必浣染至再三，欲以先天下。 然生日、端午，四方貢獻至數千萬者，加以恩澤，而諸道尚侈麗以自媚。 朝多留事，經歲不能遣，置客省以居，上封事不足采者、蕃夷貢獻未報及失職未敘者，食度支數千百人。 德宗即位，用宰相崔祐甫，拘客省者出之，食度支者遣之，歲省費萬計。
The Uyghurs had helped recover the Western Capital; Daizong treated them generously, intermarried with them, and each year sent one hundred thousand horses, repaid with more than a million bolts of silk. Yet the empire's finances were exhausted and each year fell short of the horse payments. After the six Hehuang garrisons fell, thirty thousand autumn-defense troops were sent yearly to garrison western Jing at a cost of more than 1.5 million strings in provisions. Meanwhile the eunuch Yu Chaoen monopolized power through imperial favor; Daizong and Chief Minister Yuan Zai plotted against him day and night. After Chaoen was executed, the emperor again turned against Zai; ruler and minister grew suspicious and estranged; frontier strategy and army provisions went undiscussed for nearly ten years. Military governors seized territory and acted in concert, daily drilling troops and repairing fortifications; the emperor could not restrain them by law and devoted himself to temple prayers, burning jade offerings, and copying Buddhist sutras; the expenditure office supplied monks and shamans at a cost of tens of thousands each year. Yet the emperor was personally frugal; he wore his robes washed and dyed again and again, hoping to set an example for the realm. Yet on his birthday and the Dragon Boat Festival tribute from the provinces reached tens of millions; combined with his largesse, the circuits still competed in extravagant display to curry favor. The court left many matters unresolved for years; a Guest Reception Office housed rejected memorials, unanswered foreign tribute, and officials awaiting reassignment—thousands fed at state expense. When Dezong took the throne, he employed Chief Minister Cui Youfu, released those detained in the Guest Reception Office, dismissed those on state stipends, and saved tens of thousands in annual costs.
