1
食貨一
Finance and Economics, Part One
2
古之善治其國而愛養斯民者，必立經常簡易之法，使上愛物以養其下，下勉力以事其上，上足而下不困。 故量人之力而授之田，量地之產而取以給公上，量其入而出之以為用度之數。 是三者常相須以濟而不可失，失其一則不能守其二。 及暴君庸主，縱其佚欲，而茍且之吏從之，變制合時以取寵於其上。 故用於上者無節，而取於下者無限，民竭其力而不能供，由是上愈不足而下愈困，則財利之說興，而聚斂之臣用。 《記》曰：「寧畜盜臣。 」盜臣誠可惡，然一人之害爾。 聚斂之臣用，則經常之法壞，而下不勝其弊焉。
In antiquity, rulers who governed well and truly cared for their people always set up regular, straightforward laws, so that the court valued resources to support the populace, the people labored diligently to serve the throne, the state had enough, and the common folk were not ground down. They gauged each person's capacity when allotting land, gauged what the soil could produce when levying tribute for the state, and gauged revenue against outlays to set the budget. These three measures always had to work together; lose any one of them and the other two could not hold. But when tyrants and mediocre sovereigns indulged every whim, servile officials went along, twisting the rules to suit the moment and curry favor at court. Spending at the top knew no bounds while exactions from below knew no limit; the people gave all they had and still could not meet demand. The court grew poorer even as the masses grew poorer still, until talk of maximizing revenue flourished and extortionist ministers came to power. The 《Record of Rites》 says, "Better to harbor a thieving minister." A thieving minister is detestable, to be sure, but the harm is only that of one man. When extortionist ministers are put in charge, the regular laws collapse, and the people cannot survive the damage.
3
唐之始時，授人以口分、世業田，而取之以租、庸、調之法，其用之也有節。 蓋其畜兵以府衛之制，故兵雖多而無所損； 設官有常員之數，故官不濫而易祿。 雖不及三代之盛時，然亦可以為經常之法也。 及其弊也，兵冗官濫，為之大蠹。 自天寶以來，大盜屢起，方鎮數叛，兵革之興，累世不息，而用度之數，不能節矣。 加以驕君昏主，奸吏邪臣，取濟一時，屢更其制，而經常之法，蕩然盡矣。 由是財利之說興，聚斂之臣進。 蓋口分、世業之田壞而為兼並，租、庸、調之法壞而為兩稅。 至於鹽鐵、轉運、屯田、和糴、鑄錢、括苗、榷利、借商、進奉、獻助，無所不為矣。 蓋愈煩而愈弊，以至於亡焉。
At the founding of the Tang, the state granted koufen and hereditary estate fields and collected revenue through the zu, yong, and diao levies, and spending was kept within bounds. Troops were maintained through the fubing militia and guard system, so a large army did not drain the treasury; and offices had fixed quotas, so posts were not multiplied beyond need and salaries remained manageable. It fell short of the golden ages of the Three Dynasties, yet it could still stand as a durable, regular system. When the system decayed, bloated armies and swollen bureaucracies became its greatest curse. From the Tianbao reign onward, major rebellions broke out again and again, military governors rose in repeated revolt, and warfare raged across generations without pause, until the budget could no longer be kept in check. Arrogant emperors and muddled sovereigns, corrupt clerks and scheming ministers, scrambling for stopgap revenue and rewriting the rules again and again, until the regular system was gone altogether. Then the gospel of maximizing revenue spread, and extortionist ministers rose to power. Koufen and hereditary fields were swallowed up in consolidation; the zu, yong, and diao levies gave way to the two-tax system. Salt and iron monopolies, transport commissions, garrison farms, government grain purchases, coinage, seedling surveys, profit monopolies, loans to merchants, tribute deliveries, and voluntary contributions—every expedient was tried. The more elaborate the schemes grew, the worse the harm, until the dynasty itself collapsed.
4
唐制：度田以步，其闊一步，其長二百四十步為畝，百畝為頃。 凡民始生為黃，四歲為小，十六為中，二十一為丁，六十為老。 授田之制，丁及男年十八以上者，人一頃，其八十畝為口分，二十畝為永業； 老及篤疾、廢疾者，人四十畝，寡妻妾三十畝，當戶者增二十畝，皆以二十畝為永業，其余為口分。 永業之田，樹以榆、棗、桑及所宜之木，皆有數。 田多可以足其人者為寬鄉，少者為狹鄉。 狹鄉授田，減寬鄉之半。 其地有薄厚，歲一易者，倍受之。 寬鄉三易者，不倍授。 工商者，寬鄉減半，狹鄉不給。 凡庶人徙鄉及貧無以葬者，得賣世業田。 自狹鄉而徙寬鄉者，得並賣口分田。 已賣者，不復授。 死者收之，以授無田者。 凡收授皆以歲十月。 授田先貧及有課役者。 凡田，鄉有余以給比鄉，縣有余以給比縣，州有余以給近州。
Under Tang law, land was measured in bu: one bu wide by two hundred forty bu long constituted one mu, and one hundred mu made one qing. Commoners were classified at birth as infants (huang), at four as children (xiao), at sixteen as youths (zhong), at twenty-one as adult males liable for labor (ding), and at sixty as elderly (lao). Under the land-grant system, each adult male or man of eighteen and above received one qing: eighty mu as koufen (allotment land) and twenty mu as yongye (hereditary estate); the elderly and the severely or permanently disabled received forty mu each; widows and concubines thirty mu; heads of household an extra twenty mu—with twenty mu always set aside as yongye and the remainder as koufen. Yongye plots had to be planted with specified numbers of elms, jujubes, mulberries, and other suitable trees. Districts with enough land to go around were termed broad districts (kuānxiāng); those with too little were narrow districts (xiáxiāng). In narrow districts, allotments were cut to half the broad-district standard. Where soil was poor or fields were rotated annually, allotments were doubled. In broad districts with three-year rotation, no doubling was granted. Artisans and merchants received half the broad-district allotment and none at all in narrow districts. Commoners relocating to another district, or too poor to afford burial, were permitted to sell their hereditary estate land. Those moving from a narrow to a broad district could sell their koufen land as well. Land that had been sold was never re-allotted. On the holder's death, the land reverted to the state and was reassigned to the landless. All recoveries and grants were carried out in the tenth month. Land was allotted first to the poor and to households with tax and labor obligations. Surplus land in a district went to neighboring districts; county surpluses to neighboring counties; prefectural surpluses to nearby prefectures.
5
凡授田者，丁歲輸粟二斛，稻三斛，謂之租。 丁隨鄉所出，歲輸絹二匹，綾、絁二丈，布加五之一，綿三兩，麻三斤，非蠶鄉則輸銀十四兩，謂之調。 用人之力，歲二十日，閏加二日，不役者日為絹三尺，謂之庸。 有事而加役二十五日者免調，三十日者租、調皆免。 通正役不過五十日。
Recipients of land paid two hu of grain or three hu of rice per adult male each year—this was the land tax (zu). Each adult male paid yearly, according to local products, two bolts of silk, two zhang of ling and shi fabric, cloth with a one-fifth surcharge, three liang of cotton, and three jin of hemp—or, in districts without sericulture, fourteen liang of silver. This was the diao levy. Labor service ran twenty days a year, with two extra days in intercalary years; those exempt from corvée paid three chi of silk per day instead. This was the yong levy. Extra service of twenty-five days exempted the diao levy; thirty days exempted both zu and diao. Total regular corvée never exceeded fifty days.
6
自王公以下，皆有永業田。 太皇太后、皇太后、皇后緦麻以上親，內命婦一品以上親，郡王及五品以上祖父兄弟，職事、勛官三品以上有封者若縣男父子，國子、太學、四門學生、俊士，孝子、順孫、義夫、節婦同籍者，皆免課役。 凡主戶內有課口者為課戶。 若老及男廢疾、篤疾、寡妻妾、部曲、客女、奴婢及視九品以上官，不課。
From princes and dukes down, everyone held yongye estate land. Kin of the grand empress dowager, empress dowager, and empress within the sizui mourning circle; kin of inner consorts of first rank and above; grandfathers and brothers of commandery princes and officials of fifth rank and above; active-duty and merit officials of third rank and above who held fiefs, including county marquises and their sons; students of the Directorate, Imperial University, and Four Gates schools and jinshi candidates; and filial sons, obedient grandsons, righteous husbands, and chaste wives on the same household register—all were exempt from taxes and labor service. Chief households containing persons liable for tax were classified as taxable households (kèhù). The elderly, men with crippling or severe illness, widows and concubines, retainers, guest women, slaves, and persons treated as officials of ninth rank and above were not taxed.
7
凡裏有手實，歲終具民之年與地之闊狹，為鄉帳。 鄉成於縣，縣成於州，州成於戶部。 又有計帳，具來歲課役以報度支。 國有所須，先奏而斂。 凡稅斂之數，書於縣門、村坊，與眾知之。 水、旱、霜、蝗耗十四者，免其租； 桑麻盡者，免其調； 田耗十之六者，免租調； 耗七者，課、役皆免。 凡新附之戶，春以三月免役，夏以六月免課，秋以九月課、役皆免。 徙寬鄉者，縣覆於州，出境則覆於戶部，官以閑月達之。 自畿內徙畿外，自京縣徙余縣，皆有禁。 四夷降戶，附以寬鄉，給復十年。 奴婢縱為良人，給復三年。 沒外蕃人，一年還者給復三年，二年者給復四年，三年者給復五年。 浮民、部曲、客女、奴婢縱為良者附寬鄉。
Each li maintained a household register (shoushi); at year's end it compiled ages and land measurements into a district roll. District rolls were consolidated at the county, county rolls at the prefecture, and prefectural rolls at the Ministry of Revenue. A planning account (jizhang) also listed the next year's taxes and labor obligations for report to the Directorate of Expenditure. Whenever the state required revenue, it sought approval before levying. Tax amounts were posted at county gates and village wards for public notice. If flood, drought, frost, or locusts destroyed fourteen-tenths of the crop, the land tax was waived; if mulberry and hemp were entirely lost, the diao levy was waived; if six-tenths of the fields were ruined, both zu and diao were waived; at seven-tenths loss, all taxes and labor service were waived. Newly registered households were exempt from corvée for three months if enrolled in spring, from taxes for six months if in summer, and from both for nine months if in autumn. Those relocating to broad districts were verified by the county and reported to the prefecture; cross-prefecture moves required Ministry of Revenue approval, with reports filed in off-season months. Relocation from the metropolitan circuit to the provinces, or from capital counties to ordinary counties, was subject to restrictions. Surrendered households from the frontier peoples were settled in broad districts and granted ten years of tax exemption. Manumitted slaves received three years of tax exemption. Frontier people captured overseas who returned within one year received three years' exemption; within two years, four years; within three years, five years. Vagrants, retainers, guest women, and manumitted slaves were registered in broad districts.
8
貞觀中，初稅草以給諸閑，而驛馬有牧田。
During the Zhenguan reign, a fodder tax was first imposed to supply government stables, and relay horses were given pasture land.
9
太宗方銳意於治，官吏考課，以鰥寡少者進考，如增戶法； 失勸導者以減戶論。 配租以斂穫早晚、險易、遠近為差。 庸、調輸以八月，發以九月。 同時輸者先遠民。 皆自概量。 州府歲市土所出為貢，其價視絹之上下，無過五十匹。 異物、滋味、口馬、鷹犬，非有詔不獻。 有加配，則以代租賦。 其兇荒則有社倉賑給，不足則徙民就食諸州。 尚書左丞戴胄建議：「自王公以下，計墾田，秋熟，所在為義倉，歲兇以給民。 」太宗善之，乃詔：「畝稅二升，粟、麥、秔、稻，隨土地所宜。 寬鄉斂以所種，狹鄉據青苗簿而督之。 田耗十四者免其半，耗十七者皆免之。 商賈無田者，以其戶為九等，出粟自五石至於五斗為差。 下下戶及夷獠不取焉。 歲不登，則以賑民； 或貸為種子，則至秋而償。 」其後洛、相、幽、徐、齊、並、秦、蒲州又置常平倉，粟藏九年，米藏五年，下濕之地，粟藏五年，米藏三年，皆著於令。
Taizong was then intent on good government; in performance reviews, officials were rated up for reducing the numbers of widowers and orphans, as under the household-growth statute; and officials who failed to guide and encourage the people were penalized for declining household counts. Land tax collection was scheduled according to harvest timing, terrain difficulty, and distance. Yong and diao levies were due in the eighth month and forwarded in the ninth. When deliveries coincided, distant districts were served first. All goods were measured by the taxpayers themselves. Each prefecture yearly purchased local products as tribute, valued against silk grades, not to exceed fifty bolts. Exotic goods, delicacies, riding horses, hawks, and hunting dogs were not presented without imperial command. Extra tribute assignments could be substituted for land tax and other levies. In famine, community granaries provided relief; when stores ran short, people were relocated to other prefectures for food. Left Vice Director of the Secretariat Dai Zhou proposed: "From princes and dukes down, based on reclaimed acreage, charity granaries should be set up wherever the autumn harvest came in, to feed the people in years of dearth." Taizong approved and decreed: "A levy of two sheng per mu, in millet, wheat, jing, or rice as the soil allowed." In broad districts the levy followed actual planting; in narrow districts it was assessed from the green-sprout register. A fourteen-tenths crop loss halved the levy; at seventeen-tenths, the levy was waived entirely. Merchants without land were graded in nine household ranks, contributing grain on a scale from five shi down to five dou. The lowest-rank households and Yi and Liao peoples were exempt. In lean years, grain was distributed in relief; or lent as seed grain, to be repaid at harvest. Later, Luoyang, Xiangzhou, Youzhou, Xuzhou, Qizhou, Bingzhou, Qinzhou, and Puzhou also set up Ever-Normal Granaries—grain stored nine years, rice five; in damp lowlands, grain five years and rice three—all codified in statute.
10
貞觀初，戶不及三百萬，絹一匹易米一斗。 至四年，米斗四五錢，外戶不閉者數月，馬牛被野，人行數千里不賫糧，民物蕃息，四夷降附者百二十萬人。 是歲，天下斷獄，死罪者二十九人，號稱太平。 此高祖、太宗致治之大略，及其成效如此。
Early in the Zhenguan era, registered households numbered fewer than three million, and one bolt of silk bought only one dou of rice. By the fourth year, a dou of rice cost four or five cash; outer gates stood unbarred for months on end; horses and cattle roamed the open country; travelers journeyed thousands of li without carrying provisions; population and wealth flourished; and 1.2 million people from the frontier peoples submitted. That year, courts throughout the empire handed down only twenty-nine death sentences; the age was hailed as the Great Peace. Such was the broad strategy by which Gaozu and Taizong brought order to the realm, and such were its results.
11
高宗承之，海內艾安。 太尉長孫無忌等輔政，天下未見失德。 數引刺史入閤，問民疾苦。 即位之歲，增戶十五萬。 及中書令李義府、侍中許敬宗既用事，役費並起。 永淳以後，給用益不足。 加以武後之亂，紀綱大壞，民不勝其毒。
Gaozong carried on the legacy, and the realm within the seas knew peace. With Grand Mentor Zhangsun Wuji and others guiding policy, the court showed no lapse in virtue. He repeatedly summoned prefectural governors to the palace to inquire after the people's hardships. In the year of his accession, registered households rose by 150,000. Once Chief Minister Li Yifu and Attendant-in-Attendance Xu Jingzong gained power, corvée and spending rose together. After the Yongchun reign, revenue increasingly fell short of need. Then came the turmoil of Empress Wu; institutions collapsed, and the people could not endure the oppression.
12
玄宗初立求治，蠲徭役者給蠲符，以流外及九品京官為蠲使，歲再遣之。 開元八年，頒庸調法於天下，好不過精，惡不至濫，闊者一尺八寸，長者四丈。 然是時天下戶未嘗升降。 監察御史宇文融獻策：括籍外羨田、逃戶，自占者給復五年，每丁稅錢千五百，以攝御史分行括實。 陽翟尉皇甫憬上書言其不可。 玄宗方任用融，乃貶憬為盈川尉。 諸道所括得客戶八十余萬，田亦稱是。 州縣希旨張虛數，以正田為羨，編戶為客，歲終，籍錢數百萬緡。
When Xuanzong first ascended the throne intent on reform, those exempted from corvée received exemption tallies; low-ranking capital officials served as exemption commissioners and were dispatched twice a year. In Kaiyuan year 8, standards for yong and diao levies were issued empire-wide: finest grades no better than jing quality, coarsest no worse than lan; width no more than one chi eight cun, length no more than four zhang. Yet household registers empire-wide had scarcely been updated. Investigating Censor Yuwen Rong proposed surveying unregistered surplus land and hidden households: self-reporters received five years' tax exemption; each adult male paid 1,500 cash; and acting censors were dispatched by circuit to verify the rolls. Assistant Magistrate of Yangdi Huangfu Jing submitted a memorial arguing the plan was unworkable. Xuanzong, then favoring Rong, demoted Jing to assistant magistrate of Yingchuan. The circuits registered more than 800,000 client households and a corresponding acreage of land. Prefectures and counties, eager to please their superiors, inflated the rolls—reclassifying regular fields as surplus land and registered households as client households—and by year's end had logged several million strings in revenue.
13
十六年，乃詔每三歲以九等定籍。 而庸調折租所取華好，州縣長宮勸織，中書門下察濫惡以貶官吏，精者褒賞之。 二十二年，詔男十五、女十三以上得嫁娶。 州縣歲上戶口登耗，采訪使覆實之，刺史、縣令以為課最。
In year 16, an edict ordered household registers to be reassessed on a nine-grade scale every three years. Yong, diao, and grain-for-rent payments were required in the finest quality; local magistrates urged weaving; the Secretariat inspected for shoddy goods and demoted negligent officials, while rewarding excellence. In year 22, an edict permitted marriage for males aged fifteen and females aged thirteen and above. Each year prefectures and counties reported household growth and decline; investigating commissioners verified the figures; and prefects and magistrates were rated on the results.
14
初，永徽中禁買賣世業、口分田。 其後豪富兼並，貧者失業，於是詔買者還地而罰之。
Early in the Yonghui reign, the sale of hereditary estate and koufen land was prohibited. Later, as the rich consolidated holdings and the poor lost their livelihoods, an edict required buyers to return the land and face penalties.
15
先是楊州租、調以錢，嶺南以米，安南以絲，益州以羅、䌷、綾、絹供春彩。 因詔江南亦以布代租。
Previously Yangzhou paid land tax and diao in cash, Lingnan in rice, Annan in silk, and Yizhou furnished gauze, chou, ling, and silk for spring tribute fabrics. An edict then extended the practice to Jiangnan, allowing cloth to substitute for the land tax.
16
中書令李林甫以租庸、丁防、和糴、春彩、稅草無定法，歲為旨符，遣使一告，費紙五十余萬。 條目既多，覆問逾年，乃與采訪朝集使議革之，為長行旨，以授朝集使及送旨符使，歲有所支，進畫附驛以達，每州不過二紙。
Chief Minister Li Linfu noted that land tax, yong, frontier garrison duty, government grain purchases, spring silks, and fodder tax lacked fixed rules; each year produced directive tallies dispatched by envoys in a single notice, consuming more than five hundred thousand sheets of paper. With so many entries, follow-up inquiries dragged on for more than a year. He then worked with investigating and assembly commissioners to reform the system into standing directives for assembly commissioners and dispatch envoys; annual expenditures were listed, submitted in draft, and relayed by post—no more than two sheets per prefecture.
17
凡庸、調、租、資課，皆任土所宜，州縣長官涖定粗良，具上中下三物之樣輸京都。 有濫惡，督中物之直。 二十五年，以江、淮輸運有河、洛之艱，而關中蠶桑少，菽粟常賤，乃命庸、調、資課皆以米，兇年樂輸布絹者亦從之。 河南、北不通運州，租皆為絹，代關中庸、課，詔度支減轉運。
Yong, diao, land tax, and material levies all followed local products; magistrates set coarse and fine grades and sent samples of upper, middle, and lower quality to the capital. Shoddy goods were charged at middle-grade prices. In year 25, because transport from the Jiang-Huai region faced the obstacles of the Yellow and Luo rivers, while Guanzhong produced little silk and grain prices were chronically low, yong, diao, and material levies were ordered payable in rice; in famine years cloth and silk payments were also accepted. In north and south Henan, prefectures without transport links paid the land tax entirely in silk, substituting for Guanzhong yong and levies, and the Directorate of Expenditure was ordered to cut transport costs.
18
明年，又詔民三歲以下為黃，十五以下為小，二十以下為中。 又以民間戶高丁多者，率與父母別籍異居，以避征戍，乃詔十丁以上免二丁，五丁以上免一丁，侍丁孝者免徭役。 天寶三載，更民十八以上為中男，二十三以上成丁。 五載，詔貧不能自濟者，每鄉免三十丁租庸。 男子七十五以上、婦人七十以上，中男一人為侍； 八十以上以令式從事。 是時，海內富實，米鬥之價錢十三，青、齊間鬥才三錢，絹一匹錢二百。 道路列肆，具酒食以待行人，店有驛驢，行千里不持尺兵。 天下歲入之物，租錢二百余萬緡，粟千九百八十余萬斛，庸、調絹七百四十萬匹，綿百八十余萬屯，布千三十五萬余端。 天子驕於佚樂而用不知節，大抵用物之數，常過其所入。 於是錢谷之臣，始事朘刻。 太府卿楊崇禮句剝分銖，有欠折漬損者，州縣督送，歷年不止。 其子慎矜專知太府，次子慎名知京倉，亦以苛刻結主恩。 王鉷為戶口色役使，歲進錢百億萬緡，非租庸正額者，積百寶大盈庫，以供天子燕私。 及安祿山反，司空楊國忠以為正庫物不可以給士，遣侍御史崔眾至太原納錢度僧尼道士，旬日得百萬緡而已。 自兩京陷沒，民物耗弊，天下蕭然。
The following year, another edict classified those under three as infants, under fifteen as children, and under twenty as youths. Because wealthy households with many adult sons commonly split registers and lived apart from parents to evade taxes and garrison duty, an edict exempted two adult males from households of ten or more and one from households of five or more, and filial sons caring for parents were exempt from corvée. In Tianbao year 3, males eighteen and above were classified as zhongnan, and those twenty-three and above as full adult males liable for labor. In year 5, an edict exempted thirty adult males per district from land tax and yong for households too poor to support themselves. For men aged seventy-five and above and women aged seventy and above, one zhongnan was assigned as attendant; for those eighty and above, the matter followed statutory regulations. At this time the realm was prosperous: a dou of rice cost thirteen cash; in Qing and Qi only three; a bolt of silk two hundred. Shops lined the highways offering food and drink to travelers; relay stations kept post mules; one could travel a thousand li without carrying weapons. Annual revenue empire-wide amounted to more than two million strings in land tax cash, 19.8 million hu of grain, 7.4 million bolts of yong and diao silk, 1.8 million tun of cotton, and 10.35 million duan of cloth. The emperor grew arrogant in pleasure and knew no restraint in spending; expenditures routinely exceeded revenue. Ministers of finance then turned to squeezing and scraping. Grand Steward Yang Chongli parsed accounts to the last fen and zhu; any shortage, damage, or spoilage was pursued by prefectures and counties year after year without end. His son Shenjin took charge of the Grand Steward's office; his second son Shenming managed the capital granaries; both curried imperial favor through harsh exaction. Wang Hong served as commissioner for household registers and corvée levies, reporting ten billion strings of cash yearly; revenue outside regular land tax and yong quotas went to the Great Abundance Treasury to fund the emperor's private entertainments. When An Lushan rebelled, Minister of Works Yang Guozhong argued that regular treasury goods could not supply the army; he sent Attending Censor Cui Zhong to Taiyuan to sell ordination licenses to monks and Taoist priests, raising only one million strings in ten days. After the two capitals fell, people and goods were devastated and the realm lay in ruin.
19
肅宗即位，遣御史鄭叔清等籍江淮、蜀漢富商右族訾畜，十收其二，謂之率貸。 諸道亦稅商賈以贍軍，錢一千者有稅。 於是北海郡錄事參軍第五琦以錢谷得見，請於江淮置租庸使，吳鹽、蜀麻、銅冶皆有稅，市輕貨繇江陵、襄陽、上津路轉至鳳翔。 明年，鄭叔清與宰相裴冕建議，以天下用度不充，諸道得召人納錢，給空名告身，授官勛邑號； 度道士僧尼不可勝計； 納錢百千，賜明經出身； 商賈助軍者，給復。 及兩京平，又於關輔諸州，納錢度道士僧尼萬人。 而百姓殘於兵盜，米鬥至錢七千，鬻籺為糧，民行乞食者屬路。 乃詔能賑貧乏者，寵以爵衤失。
When Suzong ascended the throne, he sent Censor Zheng Shuqing and others to register the wealth of great merchants and powerful families in Jiang-Huai and Shu-Han, levying two-tenths of declared assets—a tax called the proportional levy. Circuits also taxed merchants to support the armies; any transaction of a thousand cash was taxed. Then Di Wuqi, recording officer of Beihai Commandery, gained an audience on fiscal matters and proposed zu-yong commissioners for Jiang-Huai; taxes were imposed on Wu salt, Shu hemp, and copper smelting; light goods were purchased and routed through Jiangling, Xiangyang, and Shangjin to Fengxiang. The next year, Zheng Shuqing and Chancellor Pei Mian proposed that, with revenue insufficient empire-wide, circuits might sell blank commission documents for cash, granting offices, merit titles, and fief designations; ordaining Taoist priests and Buddhist nuns beyond counting; those paying one hundred or one thousand strings received mingjing examination credentials; merchants who contributed to the armies received tax exemptions. After the two capitals were recovered, another ten thousand ordination licenses for Taoist priests and Buddhist nuns were sold in the Guanfu prefectures. Yet the people were ravaged by war and banditry; a dou of rice cost seven thousand cash; bran cakes were sold for food; and beggars lined the roads. An edict then honored those who could relieve the poor and destitute with noble rank and official robes.
20
故事，天下財賦歸左藏，而太府以時上其數，尚書比部覆其出入。 是時，京師豪將假取不能禁，第五琦為度支鹽鐵使，請皆歸大盈庫，供天子給賜，主以中官。 自是天下之財為人君私藏，有司不得程其多少。
By precedent, imperial revenue went to the Left Treasury; the Grand Steward reported figures on schedule; and the Secretariat's Comparison Department audited receipts and disbursements. Powerful generals in the capital borrowed from the treasuries without restraint; Di Wuqi, as expenditure and salt-and-iron commissioner, proposed routing all revenue to the Great Abundance Treasury for imperial gifts, managed by palace eunuchs. From then on, imperial wealth became the emperor's private hoard, beyond the oversight of regular officials.
21
廣德元年，詔一戶三丁者免一丁，凡畝稅二升，男子二十五為成丁，五十五為老，以優民。 而強寇未夷，民耗斂重。 及吐蕃逼京師，近甸屯兵數萬，百官進俸錢，又率戶以給軍糧。 至大歷元年，詔流民還者，給復二年，田園盡，則授以逃田。 天下苗一畝稅錢十五，市輕貨給百官手力課。 以國用急，不及秋，方苗青即征之，號「青苗錢」又有「地頭錢」，每畝二十，通名為青苗錢。 又詔上都秋稅分二等，上等畝稅一斗，下等六升，荒田畝稅二升。 五年，始定法：夏，上田畝稅六升，下田畝四升； 秋，上田畝稅五升，下田畝三升； 荒田如故； 青苗錢畝加一倍，而地頭錢不在焉。
In Guangde year 1, an edict exempted one adult male from households of three; set a general levy of two sheng per mu; made twenty-five the age of full liability and fifty-five the age of elder status, to ease the burden on the people. Yet powerful enemies remained unpacified, the people were exhausted, and levies remained heavy. When Tibetans threatened the capital, tens of thousands of troops were stationed in nearby districts; officials contributed salary funds; and households were levied by quota for army grain. By Dali year 1, returning refugees received two years' tax exemption; where fields were entirely lost, abandoned land was reassigned. A seedling tax of fifteen cash per mu was levied empire-wide; light goods were purchased to fund officials' labor allowances. With state finances urgent, collection began before autumn while seedlings were still green—called green-sprout money; a field-head levy of twenty cash per mu was also added; both were known as green-sprout money. Another edict set the capital's autumn tax in two grades: upper fields one dou per mu, lower fields six sheng, wasteland two sheng. In year 5 the rates were fixed: for summer harvest, upper fields six sheng per mu, lower fields four sheng; for autumn harvest, upper fields five sheng per mu, lower fields three sheng; wasteland unchanged; green-sprout money per mu was doubled, but the field-head levy was dropped.
22
初，轉運使掌外，度支使掌內。 永泰二年，分天下財賦、鑄錢、常平、轉運、鹽鐵，置二使。 東都畿內、河南、淮南、江東西、湖南、荊南、山南東道，以轉運使劉晏領之； 京畿、關內、河東、劍南、山南西道，以京兆尹、判度支第五琦領之。 及琦貶，以戶部侍郎、判度支韓滉與晏分治。
Initially, the transport commissioner managed external revenue and the expenditure commissioner internal finances. In Yongtai year 2, imperial revenue, coinage, ever-normal granaries, transport, and salt and iron were split between two commissioners. The Eastern Capital metropolitan circuit, Henan, Huainan, Jiangdong east and west, Hunan, Jingnan, and Shannan east circuit fell under Transport Commissioner Liu Yan; the metropolitan circuit, Guannei, Hedong, Jiannan, and Shannan west circuit under Jingzhao Prefect and Acting Expenditure Commissioner Di Wuqi. When Wuqi was demoted, Vice Minister of Revenue and Acting Expenditure Commissioner Han Huang shared jurisdiction with Yan.
23
時回紇有助收西京功，代宗厚遇之，與中國婚姻，歲送馬十萬匹，酬以縑帛百余萬匹。 而中國財力屈竭，歲負馬價。 河、湟六鎮既陷，歲發防秋兵三萬戍京西，資糧百五十余萬緡。 而中官魚朝恩方恃恩擅權，代宗與宰相元載日夜圖之。 及朝恩誅，帝復與載貳，君臣猜間不協，邊計兵食，置而不議者幾十年。 而諸鎮擅地，結為表裏，日治兵繕壘，天子不能繩以法，顓留意祠禱、焚幣玉、寫浮屠書，度支稟賜僧巫，歲以鉅萬計。 然帝性儉約，身所禦衣，必浣染至再三，欲以先天下。 然生日、端午，四方貢獻至數千萬者，加以恩澤，而諸道尚侈麗以自媚。 朝多留事，經歲不能遣，置客省以居，上封事不足采者、蕃夷貢獻未報及失職未敘者，食度支數千百人。 德宗即位，用宰相崔祐甫，拘客省者出之，食度支者遣之，歲省費萬計。
The Uyghurs had helped recover the western capital; Daizong treated them generously, arranged marriage alliances, and each year received one hundred thousand horses in exchange for more than a million bolts of silk. Yet China's finances were exhausted, and each year the empire fell short of the horse payments. After the six He-Huang garrisons fell, thirty thousand autumn-defense troops were posted yearly to western Jing at a cost of more than 1.5 million strings in provisions. Meanwhile the eunuch Yu Chaoen monopolized power through imperial favor; Daizong and Chancellor Yuan Zai plotted against him day and night. After Chaoen was killed, the emperor again turned against Zai; ruler and minister grew mutually suspicious; and frontier plans for troops and supplies went undiscussed for nearly ten years. Military governors seized territory and acted in concert, drilling troops and repairing fortifications daily; the emperor could not restrain them by law, but devoted himself to temple prayers, burning jade and silk offerings, and copying Buddhist scriptures, while the expenditure office granted monks and shamans gifts totaling tens of thousands each year. Yet the emperor was personally frugal, wearing his robes washed and dyed again and again, hoping to set an example for the realm. Yet on his birthday and at the Dragon Boat Festival, regional tribute reached tens of millions; favors multiplied; and circuits still competed in lavish display to curry favor. Court business piled up, some cases undecided for a year; a Guest Reception Office housed petitioners whose memorials were rejected, foreign envoys awaiting response, and officials awaiting reassignment—thousands fed at state expense. When Dezong ascended the throne, he appointed Chancellor Cui Youfu, released those held in the Guest Reception Office, dismissed hangers-on at state expense, and saved tens of thousands each year.
