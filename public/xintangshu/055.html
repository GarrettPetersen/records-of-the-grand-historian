1
食貨五
Food and Commodities 5
2
武德元年，文武官給祿，頗減隋制，一品七百石，從一品六百石，二品五百石，從二品四百六十石，三品四百石，從三品三百六十石，四品三百石，從四品二百六十石，五品二百石，從五品百六十石，六品百石，從六品九十石，七品八十石，從七品七十石，八品六十石，從八品五十石，九品四十石，從九品三十石，皆以歲給之。 外官則否。
In 618 the court set grain salaries for civil and military officials well below Sui levels: seven hundred shi for first rank, six hundred for deputy first rank, five hundred for second rank, and so on down to thirty shi for deputy ninth rank, all disbursed once a year. Local officials outside the capital received no such salaries.
3
一品有職分田十二頃，二品十頃，三品九頃，四品七頃，五品六頃，六品四頃，七品三頃，五十畝，八品二頃五十畝，九品二頃，皆給百里內之地。 諸州都督、都護、親王府官二品十二頃，三品十頃，四品八頃，五品七頃，六品五頃，七品四頃，八品三頃，九品二頃五十畝。 鎮戍、關津、嶽瀆官五品五頃，六品三頃五十畝，七品三頃，八品二頃，九品一頃五十畝。 三衛中郎將、上府折沖都尉六頃，中府五頃五十畝，下府及郎將五頃； 上府果毅都尉四頃，中府三頃五十畝，下府三頃； 上府長史、別將三頃，中府、下府二頃五十畝； 親王府典軍五頃五十畝，副典軍四頃； 千牛備身左右、太子千牛備身三頃； 折沖上府兵曹二頃，中府、下府一頃五十畝。 外軍校尉一頃二十畝，旅帥一頃，隊正、副八十畝。
Capital officials received duty-allotment land by rank: twelve qing for first rank, ten for second, down to two qing for ninth rank, all assigned within a hundred li of the capital. Regional commissioners, protectorate officials, and staff of princely establishments received duty land by rank from twelve qing at second rank down to two qing fifty mu at ninth. Officials at frontier posts, passes, and sacred mountains received five qing at fifth rank, scaling down to one qing fifty mu at ninth. Central Guard middle generals and upper-tier assault-and-resist commanders received six qing; middle-tier commands five qing fifty mu; lower-tier commands and commandant-generals five qing; Brave-steadfast commanders of upper commands received four qing; those of middle commands three qing fifty mu; lower commands three qing; Chief administrators and deputy generals of upper commands received three qing; those of middle and lower commands two qing fifty mu; Princely establishment commandants received five qing fifty mu; deputy commandants four qing; Thousand-Ox bodyguards of the emperor and crown prince each received three qing; Military aides of upper assault-and-resist commands received two qing; those of middle and lower commands one qing fifty mu. Field army company commanders received one qing twenty mu; brigade leaders one qing; squad leaders and their deputies eighty mu.
4
親王以下又有永業田百頃，職事官一品六十頃，郡王、職事官從一品五十頃，國公、職事官從二品三十五頃，縣公、職事官三品二十五頃，職事官從三品二十頃，侯、職事官四品十二頃，子、職事官五品八頃，男、職事官從五品五頃，六品、七品二頃五十畝，八品、九品二頃。 上柱國三十頃，柱國二十五頃，上護軍二十頃，護軍十五頃，上輕車都尉十頃，輕車都尉七頃，上騎都尉六頃，騎都尉四頃，驍騎、飛騎尉八十畝，雲騎、武騎尉六十畝。 散官五品以上給同職事官。 五品以上受田寬鄉，六品以下受於本鄉。 解免者追田，除名者受口分之田，襲爵者不別給。 流內九品以上口分田終其身，六十以上停私乃收。
Imperial princes and below also held permanent-estate land of one hundred qing; substantive appointees received graded allotments from sixty qing at first rank down to two qing for eighth and ninth ranks, with noble titles matched to corresponding ranks. Merit ranks received permanent land from thirty qing for Upper Pillar of State down to sixty mu for cloud and martial cavalry commandants. Honorary officials of fifth rank and above received the same allotments as substantive appointees. Fifth rank and above drew land from underpopulated districts; sixth rank and below from their home districts. Dismissed officials forfeited their land; those removed from the registers kept only per-capita allotments; heirs who succeeded to titles received no additional grant. Exiled officials of ninth rank and above held per-capita land for life; after sixty they ceased private use and the state reclaimed it.
5
凡給田而無地者，畝給粟二斗。
When an allotment could not be met with land, the recipient was paid two dou of grain per mu instead.
6
京司及州縣皆有公廨田，供公私之費。 其後以用度不足，京官有俸賜而巳。 諸司置公廨本錢，以番官貿易取息，計員多少為月料。
Capital agencies and local governments each held public-office land to cover official expenses. Later, as revenues fell short, capital officials received only cash salaries and no longer drew on office land. Offices then established capital funds and had rotating clerks trade for profit, distributing monthly allowances according to staff size.
7
貞觀初，百官得上考者，給祿一季。 未幾，又詔得上下考給祿一年，出使者稟其家，新至官者計日給糧。 中書舍人高季輔言：「外官卑品貧匱，宜給祿養親。 」自後以地租春秋給京官，歲凡五十萬一千五百余斛。 外官降京官一等，一品以五十石為一等，二品、三品以三十石為一等，四品、五品以二十石為一等，六品、七品以五石為一等，八品、九品以二石五斗為一等。 無粟則以鹽為祿。
Early in the Zhenguan era, officials rated highest in annual review received an extra season's salary. Soon afterward an edict extended the bonus to a full year for top and near-top ratings; envoys were paid through their households, and new appointees received daily grain rations until regular salary began. Supervising Secretary Gao Jifu argued that low-ranking local officials were impoverished and ought to receive salaries to support their families. Thereafter capital officials were paid from land tax collected in spring and autumn, totaling more than five hundred ten thousand hu a year. Local officials were paid one grade below capital ranks, in steps of fifty shi for first rank, thirty for second and third, twenty for fourth and fifth, five for sixth and seventh, and two shi five dou for eighth and ninth. Where grain could not be supplied, salaries were paid in salt.
8
十一年，以職田侵漁百姓，詔給逃還貧戶，視職田多少，每畝給粟二升，謂之「地子」。 是歲，以水旱復罷之。
In the eleventh year, because officials' duty land had encroached on commoners, the court compensated returning refugees at two sheng of grain per mu of duty land, a payment called "land dues." That same year flood and drought led the court to suspend the measure.
9
十二年，罷諸司公廨本錢，以天下上戶七千人為胥士，視防閤制而收其課，計官多少而給之。 十五年，復置公廨本錢，以諸司令史主之，號「捉錢令史」。 每司九人，補於吏部，所主才五萬錢以下，市肆販易，月納息錢四千，歲滿受官。 諫議大夫褚遂良上疏：「京七十余司，更一二載，捉錢令史六百余人受職。 太學高第，諸州進士，拔十取五，猶有犯禁罹法者，況廛肆之人，茍得無恥，不可使其居職。 」太宗乃罷捉錢令史，復詔給百官俸。
In the twelfth year the court abolished office capital funds and enrolled seven thousand wealthy households empire-wide as retainers, collecting tribute on the gate-guard model and distributing allowances by headcount. In the fifteenth year the funds were restored under office clerks known as "money-collecting clerks." Each office had nine such clerks appointed through the Ministry of Personnel, each managing up to fifty thousand cash in market trade, paying four thousand in monthly interest, and qualifying for office after one year. Remonstrance Grandee Chu Suiliang memorialized: "Within a year or two more than six hundred money-collecting clerks from over seventy capital offices will have bought their way into office. Even among top academy graduates and provincial candidates chosen at a rate of five in ten, some still break the law; shopkeepers who pursue profit without shame should never be allowed to hold office. " Emperor Taizong abolished the money-collecting clerks and restored regular salaries for all officials.
10
十八年，以京兆府、岐、同、華、邠、坊州隙地陂澤可墾者，復給京官職田。
In the eighteenth year reclaimable waste land in Jingzhao and neighboring prefectures was again assigned as duty land for capital officials.
11
二十二年，置京諸司公廨本錢，捉以令史、府史、胥士。 永徽元年，廢之，以天下租腳直為京官俸料。 其後又薄斂一歲稅，以高戶主之，月收息給俸。 尋顓以稅錢給之，歲總十五萬二千七百三十緡。
In the twenty-second year capital offices again received capital funds managed by clerks, prefectural clerks, and retainers. In 650 the funds were abolished again, and capital salaries were drawn from empire-wide rent transport surcharges. Later the court levied a modest one-year tax surcharge, assigned wealthy households to manage it, and paid salaries from monthly interest. Soon the entire tax levy was devoted to salaries, totaling 152,730 strings of cash a year.
12
一品月俸八千，食料一千八百，雜用一千二百。 二品月俸六千五百，食料一千五百，雜用一千。 三品月俸五千一百，雜用九百。 四品月俸三千五百，食料、雜用七百。 五品月俸三千，食料、雜用六百。 六品月俸二千，食料、雜用四百。 七品月俸一千七百五十，食料、雜用三百五十。 八品月俸一千三百，食料三百，雜用二百五十。 九品月俸一千五十，食料二百五十，雜用二百。 行署月俸一百四十，食料三十。
First rank: eight thousand cash monthly salary, eighteen hundred for food, twelve hundred for miscellaneous expenses. Second rank: six thousand five hundred salary, fifteen hundred food, one thousand miscellaneous. Third rank: five thousand one hundred salary and nine hundred miscellaneous. Fourth rank: three thousand five hundred salary, seven hundred each for food and miscellaneous. Fifth rank: three thousand salary, six hundred each for food and miscellaneous. Sixth rank: two thousand salary, four hundred each for food and miscellaneous. Seventh rank: one thousand seven hundred fifty salary, three hundred fifty each for food and miscellaneous. Eighth rank: one thousand three hundred salary, three hundred food, two hundred fifty miscellaneous. Ninth rank: one thousand fifty salary, two hundred fifty food, two hundred miscellaneous. Acting appointees received one hundred forty cash monthly and thirty for food.
13
職事官又有防閤、庶仆：一品防閤九十六人，二品七十二人，三品四十八人，四品三十二人，五品二十四人； 六品庶仆十五人，七品四人，八品三人，九品二人。 公主有邑士八十人，郡主六十人，縣主四十人。 外官以州、府、縣上下中為差，少尹、長史、司馬及丞減長官之半，參軍、博士減判司三之二，主簿、縣尉減丞三之二，錄事、市令以參軍職田為輕重，京縣錄事以縣尉職田為輕重。 羈縻州官，給以土物。 關監官，給以年支輕貨。 折沖府官則有仗身：上府折沖都尉六人，果毅四人，長史、別將三人，兵曹二人，中、下府各減一人，皆十五日而代。 開府儀同三司、特進、光祿大夫同職事官，公廨、雜用不給。 員外官、檢校、判、試、知給祿料食糧之半，散官、勛官、衛官減四之一，致仕五品以上給半祿，解官充侍亦如之。 四夷宿衛同京官。
Substantive officials also received gate guards and household servants: ninety-six guards for first rank, seventy-two for second, and so on down to twenty-four for fifth; sixth rank fifteen household servants, seventh four, eighth three, ninth two. Imperial princesses received eighty fief retainers, commandery princesses sixty, and county princesses forty. Local allotments varied with prefectural, fu, and county grade; deputies and assistants received half the chief's share, military aides and erudites two-thirds of supervising secretaries', and registrars and defenders two-thirds of assistants', with recorders scaled to comparable posts. Officials of loose-rein prefectures were paid in local products. Pass and market officials received annual allowances in light goods. Assault-and-resist commands provided personal guards: six for upper-tier commanders, four for brave-steadfast officers, three for administrators and deputies, two for military aides, with middle and lower commands one fewer each, all rotating every fifteen days. Honorary grand ministers of state received the same salaries as substantive appointees but no office allowances or miscellaneous expenses. Extra-status, acting, provisional, trial, and temporary appointees received half salary and grain; honorary, merit, and guard officials three-quarters; retired officials of fifth rank and above half pay; dismissed officials on attendant duty the same. Barbarian palace guards received the same treatment as capital officials.
14
天下置公廨本錢，以典史主之，收贏十之七，以供佐史以下不賦粟者常食，余為百官俸料。 京兆、河南府錢三百八十萬，太原及四大都督府二百七十五萬，中都督府、上州二百四十二萬，下都督、中州一百五十四萬，下州八十八萬； 京兆、河南府京縣一百四十三萬，太原府京縣九十一萬三千，京兆、河南府畿縣八十二萬五千，太原府畿縣、諸州上縣七十七萬，中縣五十五萬，中下縣、下縣三十八萬五千； 折沖上府二十萬，中府減四之一，下府十萬。
Empire-wide office capital funds were managed by clerks, who retained seven-tenths of profits to feed lower clerks without grain salaries and applied the rest to officials' pay. Jingzhao and Henan received 3.8 million cash; Taiyuan and the four great area commands 2.75 million; middle commands and upper prefectures 2.42 million; lower commands and middle prefectures 1.54 million; lower prefectures 880,000; Capital counties of Jingzhao and Henan received 1.43 million; Taiyuan capital counties 913,000; metropolitan counties 825,000; upper counties 770,000; middle counties 550,000; lower-middle and lower counties 385,000; Upper assault-and-resist commands received 200,000 cash; middle commands one-fourth less; lower commands 100,000.
15
麟德二年，給文官五品以上仗身，以掌閑、幕士為之。 咸亨元年，與職事官皆罷。 乾封元年，京文武官視職事品給防閤、庶仆。
In 665 civil officials of fifth rank and above received personal guards drawn from leisure attendants and tent guards. In 670 the practice was abolished for substantive officials as well. In 666 capital civil and military officials again received gate guards and servants according to substantive rank.
16
百官俸出於租調，運送之費甚廣。 公廨出舉，典史有徹垣墉、鬻田宅以免責者。 又以雜職供薪炭，納直倍於正丁。 儀鳳三年，王公以下率口出錢，以充百官俸食防閤、庶仆、邑士、仗身、封戶。
Official salaries came from rent and allocation taxes, and transport costs were enormous. Office funds were lent at interest, and some clerks demolished walls or sold property to escape debt. Miscellaneous laborers also supplied fuel and charcoal, paying twice the levy of regular adult males. In 678 princes and nobles downward paid a per-capita levy to fund officials' salaries, food, retainers, guards, and enfeoffed households.
17
調露元年，職事五品以上復給仗身。 光宅元年，以京官八品、九品俸薄，詔八品歲給庶仆三人，九品二人。 文武職事三品以上給親事、帳內。 以六品、七品子為親事，以八品、九品子為帳內，歲納錢千五百，謂之「品子課錢」。 三師、三公、開府儀同三司百三十人； 嗣王、郡王百八人； 上柱國領二品以上職事九十五人，領三品職事六十九人； 柱國領二品以上職事七十三人，領三品職事五十五人； 護軍領二品以上職事六十二人，領三品職事三十六人。 二品以下又有白直、執衣：二品白直四十人，三品三十二人，四品二十四人，五品十六人，六品十人，七品七人，八品五人，九品四人； 二品執衣十八人，三品十五人，四品十三人，五品九人，六品、七品各六人，八品、九品各三人。 皆中男為之。 防閤、庶仆，皆滿歲而代。 外官五品以上亦有執衣。 都護府不治州事亦有仗身：都護四人，副都護、長史、司馬三人，諸曹參軍事二人，上鎮將四人，中下鎮將、上鎮副三人，中、下鎮副各二人，鎮倉曹、關令丞、戍主副各一人，皆取於防人衛士，十五日而代。 宿衛官三品以上仗身三人，五品以上二人，六品以下及散官五品以上各一人，取於番上衛士，役而不收課。 親王出籓者，府佐史、典軍、副典軍有事力人，數如白直。 諸司、諸使有守當及廳子，以兵及勛官為之。 白直、執衣以下分三番，周歲而代，供役不逾境。 後皆納課：仗身錢六百四十，防閤、庶仆、白直錢二千五百，執衣錢一千。 其後親事、帳內亦納課如品子之數。
In 679 substantive officials of fifth rank and above again received personal guards. In 690, because eighth- and ninth-rank capital salaries were meager, the court granted three household servants a year to eighth rank and two to ninth. Substantive civil and military officials of third rank and above received personal attendants and tent insiders. Sons of sixth- and seventh-rank families served as personal attendants and sons of eighth- and ninth-rank families as tent insiders, each paying 1,500 cash a year in "rank-son tribute." Three preceptors, Three Excellencies, and honorary grand ministers of state: 130 attendants; heir princes and commandery princes: 108; Upper Pillar of State with second-rank substantive posts: 95; with third-rank posts: 69; Pillar of State with second-rank substantive posts: 73; with third-rank posts: 55; Guardian General with second-rank substantive posts: 62; with third-rank posts: 36. Second rank and below also had white-clad runners and robe bearers, from forty runners for second rank down to four for ninth; robe bearers numbered eighteen for second rank, fifteen for third, down to three each for eighth and ninth. All were drawn from registered middle youths. Gate guards and household servants all rotated annually. Local officials of fifth rank and above also received robe bearers. Protectorate offices without prefectural duties also had personal guards, from four for the protector down to one for frontier deputies, all drawn from garrison and palace guards and rotated every fifteen days. Palace guards of third rank and above received three personal guards, fifth rank and above two, lower ranks and honorary fifth rank and above one each, drawn from rotating guards without paying tribute. Imperial princes assigned to fiefs gave their staff laborers in numbers matching white-clad runners. Offices and commissioners employed soldiers and merit officials as guards and hall attendants. White-clad runners and robe bearers served in three shifts, rotating yearly, and were not sent beyond their district. Later all paid cash in lieu of service: 640 for personal guards, 2,500 for gate guards, servants, and white-clad runners, 1,000 for robe bearers. Later personal attendants and tent insiders also paid the same rank-son tribute.
18
州縣典史捉公廨本錢者，收利十之七。 富戶幸免徭役，貧者破產甚眾。 秘書少監崔沔請計戶均出，每丁加升尺，所增蓋少； 流亡漸復，倉庫充實，然後取於正賦，罷新加者。
Prefectural and county clerks who managed office capital funds kept seven-tenths of the profits. Wealthy households escaped corvée while many poor families were ruined. Secretariat Vice Director Cui Hao proposed spreading the burden equally per household, adding only one sheng one chi per adult male; once refugees returned and granaries were full, salaries could be drawn from regular taxes and the surcharge abolished.
19
開元十年，中書舍人張嘉貞又陳其不便，遂罷天下公廨本錢，復稅戶以給百官； 籍內外職田，賦逃還戶及貧民； 罷職事五品以上仗身。
In 722 Supervising Secretary Zhang Jiazhen again protested the system; the court abolished empire-wide office capital funds and again funded officials from tax households; registered all duty land inside and outside the capital and assigned it to returning refugees and the poor; The court abolished personal guards for office-holding officials of fifth rank and above.
20
十八年，復給京官職田。 州縣籍一歲稅錢為本，以高戶捉之，月收贏以給外官。 復置天下公廨本錢，收贏十之六。 十九年，初置職田頃畝簿，租價無過六斗，地不毛者畝給二斗。
In Kaiyuan 18 (730), capital officials again received duty land. Prefectures and counties set aside one year's tax revenue as operating capital, assigned wealthy households to manage it, and used the monthly profits to pay local officials. Public-office capital funds were restored throughout the empire, with six-tenths of the profits taken in. In Kaiyuan 19 (731), the court first compiled duty-land acreage registers, capped rent at six dou, and allotted only two dou per mu on poor soil.
21
二十四年，令百官防閤、庶仆俸食雜用以月給之，總稱月俸：一品錢三萬一千，二品二萬四千，三品萬七千，四品萬一千五百六十七，五品九千二百，六品五千三百，七品四千一百，八品二千四百七十五，九品千九百一十七。 祿米則歲再給之：一品七百斛，從一品六百斛，二品五百斛，從二品四百六十斛，三品四百斛，從三品三百六十斛，四品三百斛，從四品二百五十斛，五品二百斛，從五品百六十斛，六品百斛，自此十斛為率，至從七品七十斛，八品六十七斛，自此五斛為率，至從九品五十二斛。 外官降一等。
In Kaiyuan 24 (736), gate guards, household servants, provisions, and miscellaneous expenses for all officials were paid monthly as a single "monthly salary": 31,000 cash for first rank, 24,000 for second, 17,000 for third, 11,567 for fourth, 9,200 for fifth, 5,300 for sixth, 4,100 for seventh, 2,475 for eighth, and 1,917 for ninth. Salary grain was issued twice a year: 700 hu for first rank down to 100 hu for sixth; from sixth rank onward the allowance fell by ten hu per step to 70 hu for vice-seventh rank, then by five hu per step from eighth rank to 52 hu for vice-ninth rank. Local officials were paid at one rank below their nominal grade.
22
先是州縣無防人者，籍十八以上中男及殘疾以守城門及倉庫門，謂之門夫。 番上不至者，閑月督課，為錢百七十，忙月二百。 至是以門夫資課給州縣官。
Earlier, in prefectures and counties without garrison guards, registered middle youths aged eighteen or older and disabled men were assigned to guard city and granary gates; they were called gate laborers. Gate laborers who missed their rotating shifts paid a commutation fee of 170 cash in slack months and 200 in busy months. These gate-laborer commutation fees were now used to pay prefectural and county officials.
23
二十九年，以京畿地狹，計丁給田猶不足，於是分諸司官在都者，給職田於都畿，以京師地給貧民。 是時河南、北職田兼稅桑，有詔公廨、職田有桑者，毋督絲課。
In Kaiyuan 29 (741), because land around the capital was scarce and per-capita allotments still fell short, officials of various offices stationed in the capital received duty land in the metropolitan region while capital land was reassigned to the poor. At the time, duty land north and south of the Yellow River also carried mulberry taxes; an edict exempted public-office and duty land with mulberry trees from silk tribute assessments.
24
天寶初，給員外郎料，天下白直歲役丁十萬，有詔罷之，計數加稅以供用，人皆以為便。
Early in the Tianbao era the court paid outside directors' stipends; 100,000 white-clad corvée laborers served annually empire-wide until an edict abolished the service and replaced it with a head tax, which people widely welcomed.
25
自開元後，置使甚眾，每使各給雜錢。 宰相楊國忠身兼數官，堂封外月給錢百萬。 幽州平盧節度使安祿山、隴右節度使哥舒翰兼使所給，亦不下百萬。
After the Kaiyuan era the court created a profusion of commissioner posts, each with its own miscellaneous cash allowance. Chancellor Yang Guozhong held several posts at once and, on top of his hall stipend, received one million cash a month. Pinglu Military Commissioner An Lushu of Youzhou and Longyou Military Commissioner Geshu Han likewise drew no less than one million cash from their concurrent commissioner allowances.
26
十二載，國忠以兩京百官職田送租勞民，請五十里外輸於縣倉，鬥納直二錢，百里外納直三錢，使百官就請於縣，然縣吏欺盜蓋多，而閑司有不能自直者。 十四載，兩京九品以上月給俸加十之二，同正員加十之一。 兵興，權臣增領諸使，月給厚俸，比開元制祿數倍。
In Tianbao 12 (753), Guozhong argued that delivering duty-land rents for officials of both capitals overburdened the people and proposed that rent beyond fifty li be delivered to county granaries for a fee of two cash per dou, or three beyond one hundred li, with officials drawing payment at the county — but county clerks embezzled heavily, and some idle offices could not even cover their expenses. In Tianbao 14 (755), monthly salaries for officials of ninth rank and above in both capitals rose by twenty percent, and those matching regular incumbents by ten percent. Once war broke out, powerful ministers accumulated commissioner posts and drew lavish monthly salaries several times the Kaiyuan standard.
27
至德初，以用物不足，內外官不給料錢，郡府縣官給半祿及白直、品子課。 乾元元年，亦給外官半料及職田，京官給手力課而已。 上元元年，復令京官職田以時輸送，受加耗者以枉法贓論。 其後籍以為軍糧矣。 永泰末，取州縣官及折沖府官職田苗子三之一，市輕貨以賑京官。
Early in the Zhide era, supplies ran short and stipend money ceased for officials inside and outside the capital; prefectural, fu, and county officials received half salary plus white-clad and rank-son commutation fees. In 758 local officials again received half stipends and duty land, while capital officials received only laborer commutation fees. In 760 the court again required capital officials to deliver duty-land rents on time and treated acceptance of illegal shrinkage surcharges as corrupt bribe-taking. It was later requisitioned altogether as military grain. Late in the Yongtai era the court seized one-third of duty-land crops from prefectural, county, and militia garrison officials, sold them for portable goods, and used the proceeds to aid capital officials.
28
大歷元年，斂天下青苗錢，得錢四百九十萬緡，輸大盈庫，封太府左、右藏，鐍而不發者累歲。 二年，復給京兆府及畿縣官職田，以三之一供軍饟。 增稅青苗錢，一畝至三十。 權臣月俸有至九十萬者，刺史亦至十萬。 楊綰、常袞為相，增京官正員官及諸道觀察使、都團練使、副使以下料錢。 初，檢校官同中書門下平章事者，月給錢十二萬。 至是戶部侍郎判度支韓滉請同正官，從高而給之。 文官一千八百五十四員，武官九百四十二員，月俸二十六萬緡，而增給者居三之一。
In 766 the court collected green-sprout tax empire-wide, raising 4.9 million strings of cash for the Great Surplus Treasury; the left and right storehouses of the Grand Treasury were sealed and went unopened for years. In 767 duty land was restored to officials of Jingzhao fu and the capital districts, with one-third of its yield earmarked for military provisions. The green-sprout tax was raised to thirty cash per mu. Monthly salaries for powerful ministers reached nine hundred thousand cash; prefects could receive as much as one hundred thousand. When Yang Guan and Chang Gun became chancellors, they raised stipends for regular capital officials, circuit observation commissioners, regional training commissioners, and their deputies and subordinates. Previously, acting officials who also served as Grand Councilors received 120,000 cash a month. Han Huang, Vice Minister of Revenue overseeing fiscal affairs, then asked to be paid like a regular incumbent, at whichever rate was higher. The rolls listed 1,854 civil and 942 military officials, with monthly salaries totaling 260,000 strings — and one-third of them received the new increases.
29
先是，州縣職田、公廨田，每歲六月以白簿上尚書省覆實； 至十月輸送，則有黃籍，歲一易之。 後不復簿上，唯授祖清望要官，而職卑者稽留不付，黃籍亦不復更矣。 德宗即位，詔黃籍與白簿皆上有司。
Previously, each June prefectures and counties submitted white registers of duty land and public-office land to the Ministry of Revenue for verification; and by October, when rents were delivered, a yellow register was compiled and renewed annually. Later the registers lapsed: rent went only to eminent high officials, while low-ranking ones delayed or withheld payment, and the yellow register ceased to be updated. When Emperor Dezong took the throne, he ordered both yellow and white registers submitted to the proper offices.
30
建中三年，復減百官料錢以助軍。 李泌為相，又增百官及畿內官月俸，復置手力資課，歲給錢六十一萬六千余緡，文官千八百九十二員，武官八百九十六員。 左右衛上將軍以下又有六雜給：一曰糧米，二曰鹽，三曰私馬，四曰手力，五曰隨身，六曰春冬服。 私馬則有芻豆，手力則有資錢，隨身則有糧米、鹽，春冬服則有布、絹、絁、䌷、綿，射生、神策軍大將軍以下增以鞋，比大歷制祿又厚矣。 州縣官有手力雜給錢，然俸最薄者也。 李泌以度支有兩稅錢，鹽鐵使有筦榷錢，可以擬經費，中外給用，每貫墊二十，號「戶部除陌錢」。 復有闕官俸料、職田錢，積戶部，號「戶部別貯錢」。 御史中丞專掌之，皆以給京官，歲費不及五十五萬緡。 京兆和糴，度支給諸軍冬衣，亦往往取之。 減王公以下永業田：郡王、職事官從一品田五十頃，國公、職事官正二品田四十頃，郡公、職事官從二品田三十頃，縣公、職事官正四品田十四頃，職事官從四品田十一頃。 尚郡主檢校四品京官者月給料錢三十萬，祿百二十石。 尚縣主檢校五品京官者料錢二十萬，祿百石。
In 782 officials' stipends were cut again to fund the army. As chancellor, Li Bi again raised monthly salaries for all officials and those in the capital districts, restored laborer commutation fees, and spent over 616,000 strings a year on 1,892 civil and 896 military officials. Generals of the Left and Right Guard and below also received six miscellaneous allowances: grain, salt, private horses, laborers, personal attendants, and spring and winter clothing. Horse allowances included fodder; laborer allowances included cash; attendant allowances included grain and salt; clothing allowances included cloth, silk, gauze, thin silk, and cotton; major generals of the archer corps and Shence Army and below also received shoes — all more generous than the Dali salary scale. Prefectural and county officials received laborer allowances, but their salaries remained the lowest. Li Bi funded inner and outer expenses from two-tax revenue and salt-and-iron monopoly funds, deducting twenty cash from every string disbursed under the name "Ministry of Revenue deduction charge." Vacant-post stipends and duty-land funds also accumulated at the Ministry of Revenue in a pool called "Ministry of Revenue separately stored money." The Censor Vice Director managed this fund exclusively for capital officials, spending less than 550,000 strings a year. Jingzhao grain purchases and army winter-clothing payments from the fiscal commission also frequently came from this fund. Hereditary land allotments below princely rank were cut: fifty qing for a Prince of a Commandery or a vice-first-rank office holder, forty for a State Duke or regular second rank, thirty for a Prince of a Prefecture or vice-second rank, fourteen for a Prince of a County or regular fourth rank, and eleven for vice-fourth rank. A fourth-rank capital official serving as consort to a Princess of a Commandery received 300,000 cash in monthly stipends and 120 shi of salary grain. A fifth-rank capital official serving as consort to a Princess of a County received 200,000 cash in stipends and 100 shi of salary grain.
31
自李泌增百官俸，當時以為不可朘削矣，然有名存而職廢、額去而俸在者。 宰相李吉甫建議減之，遂為常法。
After Li Bi raised officials' salaries, contemporaries believed the increases could never be rolled back — yet titles survived after offices were abolished, and salaries continued after authorized posts were eliminated. Chancellor Li Jifu urged cutting those payments, and the reduction became permanent policy.
32
於時祠祭、蕃夷賜宴、別設，皆長安、萬年人吏主辦，二縣置本錢，配納質積戶收息以供費。 諸使捉錢者，給牒免徭役，有罪府縣不敢劾治。 民間有不取本錢，立虛契，子孫相承為之。 嘗有毆人破首，詣閑廄使納利錢受牒貸罪。 御史中丞柳公綽奏諸使捉錢戶，府縣得捕役，給牒者毀之，自是不得錢者不納利矣。 議者以兩省、尚書省、御史臺總樞機，正百寮，而倍稱息利，非馭官之體。
Sacrificial rites, feasts for foreign envoys, and other special entertainments were handled by clerks from Chang'an and Wannian Counties. Each county maintained a capital fund and assigned collateral households to collect interest for the costs. Commissioners' money-collectors carried certificates exempting them from corvée labor, and prefectural and county magistrates were too intimidated to prosecute them even when they broke the law. Some families never touched the principal at all: they drew up sham contracts and handed the arrangement down from generation to generation. In one case a man who had beaten another and split his skull went to the Commissioner of the Idle Stables, paid interest, received an exemption document, and walked free. Vice Censor-in-Chief Liu Gongchuo petitioned that commissioners' money-collectors could be pressed into corvée by local government, that exemption documents already issued be voided, and that henceforth no interest would be collected from men who had never received capital in the first place. Critics argued that the Two Departments, the Department of State Affairs, and the Censorate together steered the government and set the standard for all officials, yet they doubled lending rates — conduct unworthy of those who govern.
33
元和九年，戶部除陌錢每緡增墊五錢，四時給諸司諸使之餐，置驅使官督之，御史一人核其侵漁，起明年正月，收息五之一，號「元和十年新收置公廨本錢」。
In 814 the Ministry of Revenue raised its document-stamping surcharge by five cash per string to fund seasonal meals for every office and commissioner, appointing dispatch officers to oversee disbursements and a censor to check graft. Interest at one-fifth of principal was to be collected from the first month of 815 under the title "New Public-Office Capital Funds of the Yuanhe Reign, Year Ten."
34
初，捉錢者私增公廨本，以防耗失，而富人乘以為奸，可督者私之，外以逋官錢迫蹙閭裏，民不堪其擾。 御史中丞崔從奏增錢者不得逾官本。 其後兩省捉錢，官給牒逐利江淮之間，鬻茶鹽以橈法。 十三年，以職田多少不均，每司收草粟以多少為差。 其後宰相李玨、楊嗣復奏堂廚食利錢擾民煩碎，於是罷堂廚捉錢官，置庫量入計費。
At first collectors had padded office capital to cover expected losses, but wealthy men turned the practice into a racket. Those placed under supervision pocketed the surplus for themselves while using delinquent official debts to squeeze whole neighborhoods until the people could endure it no longer. Vice Censor-in-Chief Cui Cong petitioned that no collector might lend out more than the capital officially allotted. Later the Two Departments sent out licensed money-collectors to seek profit along the Jiang-Huai region, trading tea and salt in defiance of monopoly law. In 818, because duty-field holdings varied widely, each office was assessed grass and grain levies in proportion to what it actually held. Chancellors Li Jue and Yang Sifu later complained that the Hall Kitchen's interest scheme burdened the people with petty exactions. The Hall Kitchen money-collectors were abolished and a treasury established with expenses pegged to receipts.

After the Huichang era the Tang fixed cash salaries for all officials without further adjustment. The amounts were as follows: the Grand Preceptor, Grand Tutor, and Grand Protector each received two million cash. The Grand Commandant, Minister of Education, and Minister of Works each received 1.6 million cash. The Palace Counselor received 1.5 million cash. The Secretariat Director; Chancellery and Secretariat Vice Directors; Left and Right Vice Grand Counselors; and the Crown Prince's Grand Preceptor, Grand Tutor, and Grand Protector each received 1.4 million cash. Ministers, the Censor-in-Chief, and the Crown Prince's Junior Preceptor, Junior Tutor, and Junior Protector each received one million cash.
