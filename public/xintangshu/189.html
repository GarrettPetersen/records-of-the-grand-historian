1
高趙田朱
Gao, Zhao, Tian, and Zhu.
2
高仁厚，亡其系出。 初事劍南西川節度使陳敬瑄為營使。 黃巢陷京師，天子出居成都，敬瑄遣黃頭軍部將李鋋鞏鹹以兵萬五千戍興平，數敗巢軍。 賊號蜀兵為「鵶兒」，每戰，輒戒曰：「毋與鴉兒鬥。 」敬瑄喜其兵可用，益選卒二千，使仁厚將而東。
Gao Renhou's family origins are unknown. He first served Chen Jingxuan, governor of Jiannan West Circuit, as a camp commissioner. When Huang Chao seized the capital, the emperor withdrew to Chengdu. Chen Jingxuan sent Li Xin and Gong Xian of the Yellow-Head Army with fifteen thousand men to hold Xingping, and they defeated Chao's forces again and again. The rebels nicknamed the Shu troops "Crow Boys," and before every battle they would warn one another: "Do not fight the crow boys." Chen Jingxuan, delighted with their effectiveness, picked out two thousand more troops and sent Renhou east to command them.
3
先是，京師有不肖子，皆著疊帶冒，持梃剽閭裏，號「閑子」。 京兆尹始視事，輒殺尤者以怖其餘。 竇潏治京兆，至殺數十百人，稍稍憚戢。 巢入京師，人多避難寶雞，閑子掠之，吏不能制。 仁厚素知狀，下約入邑閭縱擊。 軍入，閑子聚觀嗤侮，於是殺數千人，坊門反閉，欲亡不得，故皆死，自是閭裏乃安。
Earlier, the capital had its share of worthless young men who wore layered headwraps, carried clubs, and robbed their neighborhoods—they were known as "idlers." Whenever a new Jingzhao governor took office, he would execute the worst offenders to intimidate the rest. When Dou Yun governed Jingzhao, he put several hundred to death, and the idlers gradually grew afraid and quieted down. After Chao took the capital, many people fled to Baoji for safety, but the idlers preyed on them and the authorities could not stop it. Renhou had long known how things stood, and he issued orders that his men were free to attack the idlers wherever they found them in the towns. When the troops marched in, the idlers gathered to jeer at them—whereupon thousands were cut down. The lane gates were barred from inside; none could escape, and all perished. From that time the neighborhoods were secure.
4
會邛州賊阡能眾數萬略諸縣，列壁數十，涪州刺史韓秀升等亂峽中，韓求反蜀州，諸將不能定。 敬瑄召仁厚還，使督兵四討，屯永安。 阡能遣諜者入軍中，吏執以獻，諜自言父母妻子囚於賊，約不得軍虛實且死。 仁厚哀之，曰：「為我報賊，明日我且戰，有能釋甲迎我者，署背曰『歸順』，皆得復農矣。 」縱諜去，命諸將毀柵，鼓而前。 賊渠羅渾擎設伏詐降，仁厚遣將不持兵入諭其眾，皆真降。 渾擎詐窮而逸，吏執之，仁厚曰：「愚人不足語。 」降眾署，皆得免，則告諸壁：「大軍至。 」賊帥句胡僧大驚，斬之，莫能禁，眾執胡僧以降。 韓求知大賊已禽，徇諸壁曰：「敢出者斬！ 」眾罵之，求赴水死，眾鉤出，斬以徇，余柵皆下。 仁厚按轡徘徊視賊壘，吏請焚之，仁厚命取財糧，乃縱火，屍賊成都。 仁厚還，天子禦樓勞軍，授仁厚檢校尚書左仆射、眉州刺史。
About this time the Qiongzhou rebel Qian Neng led tens of thousands in plundering the counties and building dozens of fortified camps. Han Xiushan, prefect of Fuzhou, and others raised havoc in the gorges, while Han Qiu rebelled at Shuzhou, and none of the generals could put them down. Chen Jingxuan recalled Renhou and put him in charge of forces on four fronts, with headquarters at Yong'an. Qian Neng sent spies into the camp. When officers captured them and brought them in, the spies explained that the rebels held their families hostage and had sworn them to report on the army's movements—or face death. Renhou took pity on them and said, "Tell the rebels for me: I attack tomorrow. Whoever lays down his arms to welcome me—let him have 'Submitted' marked on his back—and he may go home to his fields." He let the spies go, ordered his generals to tear down the stockades, and marched forward to the beat of the drums. The rebel leader Luo Hunqing laid an ambush and pretended to surrender. Renhou sent officers in unarmed to reason with the men, and they submitted in earnest. Hunqing pretended to be at his last gasp and slipped away, but officers caught him. Renhou said, "This fool is not worth talking to." He registered those who had surrendered and granted them all amnesty, then sent word to the other camps: "The main army is here." The rebel chief Gou Huseng panicked and began executing men, but could not hold them back; the troops seized Huseng and surrendered. When Han Qiu learned that the chief rebel had been taken, he rode through the camps proclaiming, "Anyone who comes out will be killed!" The men cursed him. Qiu tried to drown himself, but they hauled him out with hooks, beheaded him as an example, and the rest of the stockades surrendered. Renhou rode slowly about the rebel strongholds. When his officers asked to burn them, he ordered the spoils and grain removed first, then set them ablaze. The dead were heaped at Chengdu. On Renhou's return, the emperor appeared on the gate-tower to honor the army and appointed him Acting Minister of the Left and governor of Meizhou.
5
敬瑄與仁厚謀曰：「秀升未禽，貢輸梗奪，百官乏奉，民不鹽食。 公能破賊，當以東川待公。 」仁厚許之。 詔拜行軍司馬。 仁厚聞賊儲械、子女皆在屯，乃以銳兵瀕江，伐木頹水礙舟道，負岸而陣。 使遊軍逼賊，久不戰，則夜以千卒持短刀、強弩直薄營，火而噪之。 秀升率舟兵救火，仁厚遣人鶩沒鑿舟，皆沈，眾懼，多潰。 秀升斬潰兵，欲脅止之，眾怒，執秀升以降。 仁厚問狀，對曰：「天子蒙塵，反者何獨我？ 」仁厚檻車送行在，斬於市。
Chen Jingxuan said to Renhou in counsel, "Xiushan is still at large; tribute and supplies are cut off; the court officials go unpaid; the people cannot even get salt." If you can break the rebels, Eastern Chuan is yours. Renhou agreed. The court appointed him mobile army inspector. Learning that the rebels had stockpiled arms and families in their camp, Renhou took elite troops to the riverbank, felled trees into the stream to block the boats, and drew up his line along the shore. He sent skirmishers to harry the enemy. When they held back from battle, he sent a thousand men at night with short swords and powerful crossbows to rush the camp, set it ablaze, and raise a great uproar. Xiushan led his river troops to fight the blaze, but Renhou had divers bore holes in the boats until every vessel sank. The rebels panicked and broke in flight. Xiushan began killing fleeing soldiers to force the rest to stand firm, but his men grew furious, seized him, and surrendered. When Renhou questioned him, Xiushan answered, "The emperor is driven into exile—why should I alone be called a rebel?" Renhou sent him to the emperor's camp in a cage and had him beheaded in the marketplace.
6
東川節度使楊師立初隸神策軍，累遷檢校司空、同中書門下平章事。 聞敬瑄仁厚代己，有望言。 敬瑄諷帝召師立以本官兼尚書右仆射，師立益怒，移檄言敬瑄十罪，殺監軍田繪，屯涪城，遣兵攻綿州，不克。 又檄劍州刺史姚卓文共攻成都，假卓文為指揮應接使，卓文不應。 帝乃下詔削官爵。 敬瑄即表仁厚為東川節度留後，楊茂言為行軍副使，楊棠為諸軍都虞候，率兵三萬討之。 師立遣大將張士安、鄭君雄守鹿頭關。 仁厚次漢州，前軍戰德陽，師立嬰城，閱四旬，夜出兵擾北柵，仁厚設兩翼而伏，披柵門列炬，賊不敢進，伏發，擊走之。 楊茂言謂仁厚且敗，引兵走，久乃還。 明日，會諸將，仁厚曰：「副使當以死報天子。 」斬而徇。 於是士安不敢出，師立自督士，十戰皆北。 仁厚約城中斬首惡者賞，君雄呼於軍曰：「天子所討，反者耳，吾等何與？ 」乃與士安嘩而進，以仁厚書示師立曰：「請以死謝眾。 」自沈於池死。 君雄悉誅其家，獻首天子。 仁厚入府，縱系囚，賑貧絕。 詔拜劍南東川節度使。
Yang Shili, governor of Eastern Chuan, had begun in the Divine Strategy Army and risen through repeated promotions to Acting Grand Preceptor and co-equal with the chief ministers. When he heard that Chen Jingxuan intended to replace him with Renhou, he spoke bitterly of it. Chen Jingxuan persuaded the emperor to recall Shili with his present rank plus the post of Acting Minister of the Right. Shili grew still angrier, issued a manifesto listing ten crimes against Chen Jingxuan, killed the supervising commissioner Tian Hui, encamped at Fucheng, and sent troops against Mianzhou without success. He also summoned Yao Zhuowen, prefect of Jianzhou, to join in an attack on Chengdu and named him liaison commander, but Zhuowen refused. The emperor then issued an edict stripping him of rank and title. Chen Jingxuan at once recommended Renhou as acting governor of Eastern Chuan, Yang Maoyan as mobile deputy, and Yang Tang as chief inspector of all armies, and marched thirty thousand men against Shili. Shili sent his chief generals Zhang Shi'an and Zheng Junxiong to defend Lutou Pass. Renhou encamped at Hanzhou while his vanguard fought at Deyang. Shili shut himself in the city. After forty days he sent a night raid against the northern camp. Renhou posted wings in ambush, threw open the stockade gates, and lined torches along the way. The enemy dared not advance; when the ambush struck, they were driven off. Yang Maoyan decided Renhou was about to lose and withdrew with his troops, returning only after a long delay. The next day he convened his generals and said, "The deputy must pay with his life for his failure to serve the emperor." He had him beheaded as an example to the army. After that Shi'an dared not come out, and though Shili personally led his men in ten engagements, he was beaten every time. Renhou promised rewards to anyone in the city who killed the ringleaders. Junxiong cried out to the troops, "The emperor is punishing rebels—what is that to us?" He and Shi'an then stormed forward with an uproar, showed Shili Renhou's letter, and said, "You must die to satisfy the men." Shili drowned himself in a pool. Junxiong put Shili's entire family to death and sent his head to the emperor. Renhou entered the prefectural seat, freed the prisoners, and gave relief to the poorest families. The court appointed him military governor of Jiannan East Circuit.
7
光啟二年，遂據梓州，絕敬瑄。 君雄時為遂州刺史，亦陷漢州，攻成都。 敬瑄使部將李順之逆戰，君雄死。 又發維、茂州羌軍擊仁厚，斬之。 乾寧中，皆追贈司徒。
In the second year of Guangqi (886) he seized Zizhou and cut off relations with Chen Jingxuan. Junxiong, who was now governor of Suizhou, also seized Hanzhou and marched on Chengdu. Chen Jingxuan sent his officer Li Shunzhi to meet him in battle, and Junxiong was killed. Chen Jingxuan also sent Qiang troops from Wei and Mao prefectures against Renhou and had him killed. During the Qianning era, they were all posthumously honored as Grand Preceptor.
8
趙犨，陳州宛丘人，世為忠武軍牙將。 犨資警健，兒弄時好為營陣行列，自號令指顧，群兒無敢亂。 父叔文見之，曰：「是當大吾門。 」稍長，喜書，學擊劍，善射。 會昌中，從伐潞州，收天井關，又從征蠻，忠武軍功多，遷大校。
Zhao Chou was a native of Wancheng in Chen Prefecture. For generations his family had served as officers of the Loyal and Martial Army. Chou was quick-witted and robust. Even as a boy at play he liked to drill his companions in battle formations, giving orders and directing them, and none of the others dared disobey. His father Shuwén saw this and said, "This boy will bring greatness to our clan." As he grew older he took to books, studied swordsmanship, and became an expert archer. During the Huichang era he joined the campaign against Luzhou and helped take Tianjing Pass, then served in the southern campaigns. The Loyal and Martial Army won great distinction, and he was promoted to senior commander.
9
黃巢入長安，所在盜興，陳人詣節度府，請犨為刺史，表於朝，授之。 既視事，會官屬計曰：「巢若不死長安，必東出關，陳其沖也。 」乃培城疏塹，實倉庫，峙槁薪，為守計。 民有貲者悉內之，繕甲兵，募悍勇，悉補子弟領兵。 巢敗，果東奔。 賊將孟楷以萬人寇項，犨擊禽之。 僖宗嘉其功，遷累檢校司空。 巢聞楷死，驚且怒，悉軍據溵水，與秦宗權合兵數十萬，繚長壕五周，百道攻之。 州人大恐，犨令曰：「士貴建功立名節，今雖眾寡不敵，男子當死地求生，徒懼無益也。 且死國，不愈生為賊乎？ 吾家食陳祿，誓破賊以保陳，異議者斬！ 」眾聽命。 引銳士出戰，屢破賊。 巢益怒，將必屠之，乃起八仙營於州左，僭象宮闕，列百官曹署，儲糧為持久計。 宗權輸鎧仗軍須，賊益張。 犨小大數百戰，勝負相當，故人心固，乃間道乞師於朱全忠。 未幾，汴軍至，壁西北，陳人思奮，犨引兵急擊賊，破之。 圍凡三百日而解。
When Huang Chao entered Chang'an, brigandage broke out everywhere. The people of Chen went to the circuit headquarters and asked that Chou be made their prefect. The request was sent to court, and he was appointed. As soon as he took office he gathered his staff and said, "If Chao is not destroyed in Chang'an, he will surely march east through the passes—and Chen lies directly in his path." He raised the walls, deepened the moats, filled the granaries, piled up fuel, and made every preparation for a siege. He brought every wealthy household inside the walls, repaired arms and armor, recruited the bravest men, and put his sons and kinsmen in command of them all. When Chao was defeated, he fled east as expected. The rebel general Meng Kai invaded Xiang with ten thousand men, but Chou attacked and took him prisoner. Emperor Xizong commended his achievement and promoted him in stages to Acting Grand Preceptor. When Chao learned of Kai's death he was stunned and furious. He massed his entire army on the Yin River, joined Qin Zongquan for a force of several hundred thousand, dug five rings of trenches around the city, and attacked on every side. The people of the prefecture were terrified. Chou proclaimed, "A true soldier wins fame by deeds of valor. We are outnumbered, but a man finds life by fighting where death seems certain. Fear alone will save no one." Is it not better to die for one's country than to live on as a rebel's slave? My family has lived on Chen's bounty. I swear to break the rebels and save this city—and anyone who disagrees will be executed! The men obeyed. He led his best troops out to fight and defeated the rebels again and again. Chao grew still angrier and resolved to slaughter the city. He built the Eight Immortals Camp east of the walls, laid out quarters in imitation of the imperial palace, set up offices for his officials, and stockpiled grain for a long siege. Qin Zongquan sent armor, weapons, and supplies, and the rebels grew bolder still. Chou fought hundreds of engagements large and small, winning about as often as he lost, and the people's resolve held firm. He sent a secret messenger to Zhu Quanzhong begging for reinforcements. Before long the Bian troops arrived and camped to the northwest. The people of Chen were eager to fight. Chou led a fierce assault and routed the rebels. The siege lasted three hundred days before it was finally lifted.
10
中和五年，擢彰義軍節度使。 巢雖敗，宗權始熾，略地數千里，屠二十餘州，唯陳賴犨獨完，以功檢校司徒，加泰寧、浙西兩節度，皆在陳並領之。 龍紀初，進同中書門下平章事、忠武軍節度，仍治陳州，流亡踵還。 與弟昶至友愛，後將老，悉以軍事付之，乃卒，贈太尉。
In the fifth year of Zhonghe (885) he was appointed military governor of the Zhangyi Army. Although Chao was defeated, Qin Zongquan rose to power, ravaging thousands of li of territory and laying waste to more than twenty prefectures. Chen alone, thanks to Chou, stood intact. For this he was made Acting Grand Preceptor and given the additional commands of Taining and Zhexi, all administered from Chen. At the beginning of the Longji era (889) he was made co-equal with the chief ministers and military governor of the Loyal and Martial Army, still ruling from Chenzhou, and refugees began streaming home. He and his younger brother Chang were devoted to one another. In his later years, as age overtook him, he handed all military affairs to Chang, then died and was posthumously honored as Grand Marshal.
11
犨悉忠力以孤城抗賊，巢卒敗亡。 然附全忠，亦賴其力復振，故委輸調發助全忠，常先它鎮雲。
Chou had poured all his loyalty and strength into holding a single city against the rebels, and in the end Chao was destroyed. Yet he had thrown in his lot with Zhu Quanzhong and depended on Zhu's power to recover his position, so he sent supplies and troops to aid him, often ahead of every other circuit.
12
昶，字大東，神采軒異，而內沈厚，有法度。 破孟楷功多。 巢之圍，昶夜掫師，疲而寢，如有神相之者。 犁曙決戰，士爭奮死鬥，禽賊酋數人，斬級千餘。 犨領泰寧，以昶為州刺史、檢校尚書右仆射。 當時，方鎮言忠壯吏治，舉言犨、昶。 犨之老，乃授留後，遷忠武節度使，亦留陳。 進檢校司徒。 劭勸農桑，於人有恩惠。 加同中書門下平章事。 乾寧二年卒，年五十三，贈太尉。
Chang, styled Dadong, had a striking presence, yet inwardly he was grave and steady and governed by strict discipline. He won great distinction in the defeat of Meng Kai. During Chao's siege Chang led the troops by night. Exhausted, he fell asleep on watch, yet it was as though spirits watched over him. At dawn he fought a decisive battle. The men fought with desperate valor, captured several rebel chiefs, and took more than a thousand heads. While Chou held Taining, he appointed Chang prefect of the circuit and Acting Minister of the Right. At the time, whenever the regional governors spoke of loyalty, courage, and good administration, they named Chou and Chang. When Chou grew old he made Chang his acting governor, then transferred the Loyal and Martial command to him while remaining at Chen. Chang was promoted to Acting Grand Preceptor. He encouraged farming and sericulture and treated the people with kindness. He was made co-equal with the chief ministers. He died in the second year of Qianning (895), aged fifty-three, and was posthumously honored as Grand Marshal.
13
犨子珝，字有節。 雄毅喜書，善騎射。 巢之難，激勵麾下，約皆死。 以先冢邇賊，畏見殘齮，即夜縋死士取柩以入。 庫有巨弩，機牙壞，不能張，珝以意調治，激矢至五百步，人馬皆洞，賊畏不敢逼。 以勞檢校尚書右仆射，遙領處州刺史。
Chou's son Xu, styled Youjie. Bold and resolute, he loved learning and excelled at horsemanship and archery. During Huang Chao's siege he roused his men and bound them by oath to fight to the death. The family graves lay close to the enemy lines, and he feared they would be defiled. That night he sent daredevils down on ropes to bring the coffins within the walls. In the arsenal stood a great crossbow with a broken mechanism that no one could draw. Xu restored it by instinct alone. Its bolts pierced men and horses five hundred paces away, and the rebels dared not come near. For his service he was appointed Acting Minister of the Right and given the governorship of Chuzhou in absentia.
14
昶帥忠武，珝遷行軍司馬。 昶之喪，知忠武留後，政簡濟，上下安之。 全忠表為忠武軍節度使。 陳土惡善圮，珝疊甓表墉，遂無患。 三加檢校太保。 光化三年，同中書門下平章事，進兼侍中，封天水郡公。 按鄧艾故跡，決翟王渠溉稻以利農。 一家三節度，相繼二十餘年，陳人宜之。
When Chang took command of the Loyal and Martial Army, Xu was made mobile army inspector. After Chang's death he became acting governor of the Loyal and Martial Army. His rule was simple and effective, and officers and men alike were content. Zhu Quanzhong recommended him for the post of military governor of the Loyal and Martial Army. The soil of Chen was weak and the walls often crumbled. Xu faced them with layered brick and the problem ceased. He was three times promoted to Acting Grand Guardian. In the third year of Guanghua he was made co-equal with the chief ministers, promoted to Palace Attendant, and enfeoffed as Duke of Tianshui. Following the route of Deng Ai's old canal, he opened the Zhaiwang Channel to irrigate the rice fields and benefit the farmers. One family held three governorships in succession for more than twenty years, and the people of Chen thought it only right.
15
天復初，韓建帥忠武，以珝知同州節度留後。 昭宗還長安，詔入朝，賜號「迎鑾功臣」。 以檢校太傅為右金吾衛上將軍，從東遷。 歲餘，以疾免。 卒，年五十五，贈侍中，陳人為罷市。
At the beginning of the Tianfu era, Han Jian took command of the Loyal and Martial Army and appointed Xu acting governor of Tongzhou. When Emperor Zhaozong returned to Chang'an, Xu was summoned to court and given the title Meritorious Minister Who Welcomed the Imperial Carriage. As Acting Grand Tutor he was appointed commanding general of the Right Golden Guard and accompanied the court's move east. After a year he resigned on grounds of illness. He died at fifty-five and was posthumously made Palace Attendant. The people of Chen closed their markets in mourning.
16
田頵，字德臣，廬州合肥人。 略通書傳，沈果有大誌。 與楊行密同裏，約為兄弟。 應州募屯邊，遷主將。 行密據廬州，頵謀為多。 攻趙锽於宣州，锽出東溪，乘暴流以逸，阻水解甲，謂追騎不能及。 頵乘輕舠追之，锽驚，遂見禽。 行密表頵為馬步軍都虞候。
Tian Jun, styled Dechen, was a native of Hefei in Luz Prefecture. He had some learning in the classics, was grave and resolute, and harbored great ambition. He came from the same village as Yang Xingmi, and the two pledged themselves as brothers. He answered the prefecture's call to garrison the frontier and rose to chief commander. When Xingmi seized Luzhou, Jun's counsel proved the most valuable. Attacking Zhao Chou at Xuanzhou, Chou fled down the East Stream and rode the raging current to safety. He dammed the water, laid aside his armor, and thought his pursuers could not catch him. Jun pursued in a light skiff. Chou panicked and was taken. Xingmi recommended Jun for the post of chief inspector of horse and foot.
17
沙陀叛將安仁義奔淮南，行密大喜，屬以騎兵，使在頵右，兩人名冠軍中，共攻常州，殺刺史杜棱。 錢镠方屯潤州，一夕潰。 會孫儒南略，頵等屯丹陽，儒火揚州，壁廣德，頵破其屯。 與戰，頵走，行密怒，奪其兵。 或諫行密曰：「強敵傅壘，不用頵，非計也。 」行密復將頵。 儒詒書仁義通好，以疑行密，行密待益厚，署行軍副使，卒用此二人功禽儒。 乃表仁義為潤州刺史，頵寧國軍節度使。 累遷檢校太保、同中書門下平章事。 仁義至檢校太保。
The Shatuo defector An Renyi fled to Huainan. Xingmi was delighted and gave him command of the cavalry, posting him at Jun's right hand. The two were the foremost warriors in the army. Together they attacked Changzhou and killed the prefect Du Ling. Qian Liu was encamped at Runzhou, but in a single night his army was routed. When Sun Ru marched south, Jun and the others encamped at Danyang. Ru burned Yangzhou and fortified Guangde, but Jun overran his camp. In the fighting Jun fled. Xingmi was furious and stripped him of his command. Someone urged Xingmi, saying, "With a powerful enemy at the gates, to leave Jun idle is no strategy." Xingmi put Jun back in command. Ru sent a forged letter to Renyi proposing an alliance, hoping to sow suspicion. Xingmi treated Renyi all the more generously and made him mobile deputy. In the end it was these two men's valor that captured Ru. He then recommended Renyi as governor of Runzhou and Jun as military governor of the Ningguo Army. Jun was repeatedly promoted to Acting Grand Guardian and co-equal with the chief ministers. Renyi rose to Acting Grand Guardian.
18
頵已平馮弘鐸，至揚州謝行密。 左右求貲不已，獄吏亦有請，頵怒曰：「吏覬吾入獄邪！ 」又求池、歙為屬州，行密不許，頵始怨。 將還，指府門曰：「吾不復入此。」
After Jun had pacified Feng Hongduo, he came to Yangzhou to pay his respects to Xingmi. His attendants kept demanding gifts without end, and even the prison officers asked for bribes. Jun snapped, "Do these clerks expect to see me behind bars?" He also asked that Chizhou and Shezhou be made his subordinate prefectures. Xingmi refused, and Jun began to harbor resentment. As he was about to leave, he pointed at the prefectural gate and said, "I shall never set foot in here again."
19
是時，錢镠部將徐綰叛，镠入杭州逐綰，綰屯靈隱山迎頵。
About this time Qian Liu's officer Xu Wan rebelled. Liu entered Hangzhou to drive him out, but Wan encamped on Lingyin Mountain to welcome Jun.
20
頵遣客何曉見镠曰：「王宜東保會稽，無為虛屠士眾也。 」镠曰：「軍中小叛常然，公為人長，何助逆耶？ 」頵攻北門，镠登城與語，射中麾下。 頵築壘絕往來道，镠患之，出金幣十輿，募能奪地者。 陳璋以死士三百，免胄馳擊，奪其地，镠授璋衢州刺史。 頵攻城未能克，將濟江絕西陵，為镠將所卻，圍益急。
Jun sent his envoy He Xiao to tell Liu, "Your Highness should withdraw east to Kuaiji and spare your men a needless slaughter." Liu replied, "Petty mutinies are common enough in any army. As a man of standing, why do you aid a traitor?" Jun attacked the north gate. Liu mounted the wall to parley with him and shot one of Jun's officers. Jun built fortifications to cut off all traffic. Troubled, Liu offered ten cartloads of gold and coin to anyone who could break the blockade. Chen Zhang led three hundred daredevils in a bareheaded charge and recaptured the ground. Liu appointed him governor of Quzhou. Jun could not take the city by assault. He tried to cross the river and cut off Xiling, but Liu's generals drove him back and the siege grew tighter still.
21
先是，行密欲女镠子，镠急，乃遣元迎女，且告行密曰：「頵得誌，為患必大，請以子為質，願召還頵。 」行密使人謂頵曰：「不還，我遣人代守宣州。 」頵不從。 镠輸錢二百萬緡犒軍，頵又請镠子元瓘出質，乃與綰引兵還。 然內怨行密與镠，因移書曰：「侯王守方以奉天子，譬百川不朝於海，雖狂奔澶漫，終為涸土，不若順流無窮也。 東南揚為大，刀布金玉積如阜，願公上天子常賦，頵請悉儲峙，單車以從。 」行密答曰：「貢賦繇汴而達，適足資敵爾。 」於是頵絕行密，大募兵。 李神福白行密：「頵必叛，宜先圖之。 」行密曰：「頵有大功，而反狀未明，殺之，諸將不為用。 」頵遣其佐杜荀鶴至汴通好，全忠喜，屯宿州須變。 行密以康儒在頵所，故授廬州刺史以間之。 頵怒，族其家，儒曰：「公不用吾謀，死無地矣。」
Earlier Xingmi had wished to marry his daughter to Liu's son. Liu, anxious, sent Yuan Guangzan to fetch the bride and told Xingmi, "If Jun prevails, the harm will be grave. Keep my son as hostage and recall Jun." Xingmi sent word to Jun: "If you do not return, I shall send someone else to hold Xuanzhou." Jun refused. Liu sent two million strings of cash to reward Jun's army. Jun also demanded that Liu's son Yuan Guangzan be left as hostage. Only then did he and Wan withdraw. Yet he nursed resentment against Xingmi and Liu and wrote, "Princes and lords hold their territories to serve the Son of Heaven, as the hundred streams must flow to the sea. However wildly they may rush and spread, in the end they dry to dust. Better to follow the current without end." Yangzhou is the greatest city in the southeast, its stores of goods and gold piled like hills. I urge you to send the emperor his regular tribute—I will store it all and follow behind with a single cart. Xingmi answered, "Tribute must pass through Bian to reach the capital—that only feeds our enemy." Thereupon Jun broke with Xingmi and raised a great army. Li Shenfu warned Xingmi, "Jun is certain to rebel. We should strike first." Xingmi said, "Jun has rendered great service, and his treason is not yet proven. If we kill him, the other generals will not follow us." Jun sent his aide Du Xunhe to Bian to propose an alliance. Zhu Quanzhong was pleased and encamped at Suzhou to await developments. Because Kang Ru was with Jun, Xingmi appointed him governor of Luzhou to sow discord between them. Jun was furious and exterminated Kang's entire family. Kang said, "You would not heed my counsel—now I have nowhere to die."
22
頵與安仁義連和攻升州，劫刺史李神福妻息厚養之。 神福方與劉存攻鄂州，行密召之。 神福謂諸將曰：「頵反，此心腹疾，宜速攻之。 」頵遣李臯詒書神福曰：「公家在此，茍從我，當分地以王。 」答曰：「吾以一卒從吳王，任上將，終不以妻子易意。 」乃斬臯，破頵兵於曷山。 始，頵將王壇等以舟師躡神福後，至吉陽磯，不戰。 會日暮，壇掩神福軍半濟，神福反舟順流急擊，大破之，因縱火，士多死。 明日，壇復戰，敗於皖口，頵乃自將來戰。 神福曰：「賊棄城而來，此天亡也。 」乃瀕水堅壁不出，請行密以兵塞頵走道。
Jun allied with An Renyi to attack Shengzhou and seized the wife and children of the prefect Li Shenfu, treating them with every consideration. Shenfu was then besieging Ezhou with Liu Cun when Xingmi summoned him back. Shenfu told his officers, "Jun has rebelled. This is a mortal wound—we must attack at once." Jun sent Li Gao with a forged letter to Shenfu: "Your family is in my hands. Follow me and I will divide the realm and make you a prince." Shenfu answered, "I followed the King of Wu as a common soldier and rose to high command. I will never betray him for my wife and children." He beheaded Gao and routed Jun's army at Mount Yi. At first Jun's generals Wang Tan and others followed with the fleet. At Jiyang Ford they held back from battle. At dusk Tan ambushed Shenfu's force mid-crossing. Shenfu turned his boats downstream and struck hard, routing them completely. He then set the fleet ablaze, and many men perished. The next day Tan fought again and was beaten at Wan Estuary. Jun then took the field in person. Shenfu said, "The rebels have abandoned their city to come fight us—Heaven has doomed them." He entrenched along the river and refused to give battle, asking Xingmi to send troops to cut off Jun's retreat.
23
仁義焚東塘戰艦，夜攻常州，不克，轉戰至夾岡，立二幟，解甲而息，追兵莫敢向。 頵陳舟蕪湖。 行密遣將王茂章攻潤州。 仁義以善射冠軍中，當時稱朱瑾槊，米誌誠弩，皆為第一。 仁義常曰：「誌誠弩十，不當瑾槊之一； 瑾槊十，不當吾弓之一。 」人以為然。 又其治軍嚴，善得士心。 戰卒數百，濠梁不毀，開門鬥，先告所當中，然後射之。 茂章等不敢與確。 行密遣使謂曰：「吾不忘公功，能自歸，當復為行軍副使，但不可處兵。 」仁義欲降，其子固諫，乃止。
Renyi burned his warships at Dongtang and attacked Changzhou by night without success. Fighting on to Jiagang, he set up two banners, laid aside his armor to rest, and his pursuers dared not approach. Jun stationed his fleet at Wuhu. Xingmi sent his general Wang Maozhang to attack Runzhou. Renyi was the finest archer in the army. At the time Zhu Jin's spear and Mi Zhicheng's crossbow were each called the best in the realm. Renyi used to say, "Ten of Zhicheng's crossbows are not worth one thrust of Jin's spear;" and ten of Jin's spears are not worth one shot from my bow." People believed him. He also governed his troops with strict discipline and knew how to win their loyalty. In battle, though hundreds of paces separated the armies and the walls stood intact, he would open the gate to fight. He would announce his target first, then shoot—and never miss. Maozhang and the others dared not meet him in open combat. Xingmi sent a messenger saying, "I have not forgotten your service. If you surrender of your own accord, you shall again be mobile deputy—but you may not keep command of troops." Renyi wished to surrender, but his son pleaded with him urgently, and he desisted.
24
行密召其將臺濛泣語曰：「人嘗告頵必反，我不忍負人，頵果負我。 吾思為將者非公莫可。 」濛頓首謝，率騎度江，為陣以行。 士笑其怯，濛曰：「頵宿將多謀，備之何害？ 」與王壇等戰廣德，濛以行密書遺壇諸將，皆再拜氣奪。 濛麾兵擊之，壇走。 神福既以不戰困頵，頵紿言母病，還至蕪湖。 聞壇敗，留精兵二萬屬郭行琮，身走城。 濛之行，為狹營小舍，覘者以為才容二千人，頵輕之，不復召兵。 與戰黃池，矢石始交而濛遁，兵爭逐北，遇伏，頵大敗，召蕪湖兵，不得入。 行琮及壇皆歸行密，頵恚，自料死士數百，號「爪牙都」，身薄戰。 濛退軍示弱，士超隍，濛殊死戰，軍潰。 頵奔城，橋陷，為亂兵所殺，年四十六。 其下猶鬥，示頵首，乃潰。
Xingmi summoned his general Tai Meng and said through tears, "People warned me again and again that Jun would rebel. I could not bring myself to wrong a loyal man—and Jun has wronged me." "I can think of no general but you for this task." Meng kowtowed and led his cavalry across the river in battle formation. His men laughed at his caution. Meng said, "Jun is a veteran of many battles and much guile—what harm is there in being prepared?" At Guangde he fought Wang Tan and the others. Meng sent Xingmi's letters to Tan's generals, and they all bowed twice, their spirits broken. Meng waved his troops forward and Tan fled. Shenfu had worn Jun down by refusing battle. Jun pretended his mother was ill and withdrew to Wuhu. When he heard Tan had been defeated, he left twenty thousand elite troops with Guo Xingzhen and fled to the city himself. As Meng advanced he built narrow camps and small shelters. Scouts thought he had no more than two thousand men. Jun despised him and did not call up reinforcements. At Huangchi the exchange of arrows had barely begun when Meng feigned flight. Jun's men rushed in pursuit and walked into an ambush. Jun was routed utterly. He called for his troops at Wuhu, but they could not reach him. Xingzhen and Tan both went over to Xingmi. Jun, enraged, gathered several hundred daredevils he called the Claw-and-Fang Division and led the charge himself. Meng withdrew as if in defeat. Jun's men scaled the ramparts. Meng turned and fought with desperate fury, and Jun's army collapsed. Jun fled for the city, but the bridge collapsed beneath him and he was killed by his own disorderly troops. He was forty-six. His men fought on until Jun's head was displayed; then they broke and fled.
25
頵始以元瓘歸，戰不勝，輒欲殺之，頵母護免。 及镠與行密合，頵曰：「今日不勝，必殺元瓘。 」已而頵死，傳首至淮南，行密泣下，葬以庶人禮，亦葬康儒，還元瓘於杭。
When Yuan Guangzan was first sent as hostage, Jun tried to kill him after every defeat, but Jun's mother always intervened to save him. When Liu and Xingmi joined forces, Jun said, "If we do not win today, I will kill Yuan Guangzan." Then Jun died. His head was sent to Huainan. Xingmi wept, buried him with the rites due a commoner, also buried Kang Ru, and returned Yuan Guangzan to Hangzhou.
26
頵善為治，資寬厚，通利商賈，民愛之。 善遇士，若楊夔、康軿、夏侯淑、殷文圭、王希羽等皆為上客。 文圭有美名，全忠、镠交辟不應。 頵置田宅，迎其母，以甥事之，故文圭為盡力。 夔知頵不足亢行密，著《溺賦》以戒，頵不用。
Jun was a capable administrator, generous in disposition, favorable to merchants, and beloved by the people. He treated men of letters with great respect. Yang Kui, Kang Zhen, Xiahou Shu, Yin Wengui, Wang Xiyu, and others were all his honored guests. Wengui enjoyed a fine reputation. Zhu Quanzhong and Qian Liu both invited him repeatedly, but he refused. Jun gave him land and a house, welcomed his mother, and treated him as a kinsman by marriage. Wengui therefore served him with all his strength. Kui knew Jun was no match for Xingmi and wrote the Fu on Drowning as a warning, but Jun paid no heed.
27
行密使王茂章穴地取潤州，安仁義以家屬保城樓，兵不敢登。 召李德誠曰：「汝可以委命。 」乃抵弓矢就縛，父子斬揚州市。
Xingmi had Wang Maozhang tunnel under the walls to take Runzhou. An Renyi placed his family on the city tower as a shield, and the attackers dared not climb up. He summoned Li Decheng and said, "You may answer for my life." He then laid down his bow and arrows and submitted to bonds. Father and son were beheaded in the market at Yangzhou.
28
濛，字頂雲，亦合肥人。 頵破，行密表為檢校太保、宣州觀察使。 天祐初卒。
Meng, styled Dingyun, was also a native of Hefei. After Jun's defeat, Xingmi recommended Meng as Acting Grand Guardian and inspector of Xuanzhou. He died at the beginning of the Tianyou era.
29
朱延壽者，廬州舒城人。 事行密，破秦彥、畢師鐸、趙锽、孫儒功居多。 行密欲以寬恕結人心，而延壽敢殺。 時揚州多盜，捕得者，行密輒賜所盜遣之，戒曰：「勿使延壽知。 」已而陰許延壽殺之。
Zhu Yanhou was a native of Shucheng in Luz Prefecture. He served Xingmi and won the greatest distinction in the defeats of Qin Yan, Bi Shiduo, Zhao Chou, and Sun Ru. Xingmi wished to win hearts through mercy, but Yanhou was quick to kill. Bandits were numerous in Yangzhou. When one was caught, Xingmi would return what he had stolen and send him away, warning, "Do not let Yanhou know." Then secretly he allowed Yanhou to execute them.
30
初，壽州刺史高彥溫舉州入朱全忠，行密襲之，諸將憚城堅不可拔，延壽鼓之，拔其城即表為淮南節度副使。 全忠猶屯壽春，延壽以新軍出，每旗五伍為列，遣李厚以十旗擊西偏，不勝，將斬之，厚請益五旗，殊死戰，全忠引去。 於是取黃、蘄、光三州，以功遷壽州團練使。
Earlier the prefect of Shouzhou, Gao Yanwen, had surrendered his prefecture to Zhu Quanzhong. Xingmi attacked it, but his generals feared the strong walls could not be taken. Yanhou roused them, captured the city, and was at once recommended as deputy military governor of Huainan. Quanzhong was still encamped at Shouchun. Yanhou led his fresh troops out with five men to each banner. He sent Li Hou with ten banners against the western flank. When Hou was beaten, Yanhou was about to execute him, but Hou begged for five more banners and fought to the death until Quanzhong withdrew. He then took Huang, Qi, and Guang prefectures and, for his merit, was made defense commissioner of Shouzhou.
31
昭宗在鳳翔，詔延壽圍蔡以披全忠勢，擢奉國軍節度使。 全忠兵每至，延壽開門不設備，而不敢逼也。 延壽用軍常以寡鬥眾，敗還者盡斬之。
While Emperor Zhaozong was at Fengxiang, he ordered Yanhou to besiege Cai and relieve the pressure on Quanzhong, and promoted him to military governor of the Fengguo Army. Whenever Quanzhong's troops appeared, Yanhou would open his gates without taking precautions, yet they dared not press him. Yanhou often fought with few against many, and any soldier who returned defeated was beheaded.
32
田頵之附全忠，延壽陰約曰：「公有所為，我願執鞭。 」頵喜，二人謀絕行密。 行密憂甚，紿病目，行觸柱僵。 妻，延壽姊也，掖之。 行密泣曰：「吾喪明，諸子幼，得舅代我，無憂矣。 」遣辯士召之，延壽疑，不肯赴。 姊遣婢報故，延壽疾走揚州，拜未訖，士禽殺之，而廢其妻。
When Tian Jun threw in his lot with Quanzhong, Yanhou secretly pledged, "Whatever you undertake, I am ready to serve at your side." Jun was delighted, and the two plotted to break with Xingmi. Xingmi was deeply troubled. He pretended to be blind, walked into a pillar, and collapsed as if stunned. His wife, who was Yanhou's elder sister, helped him up. Xingmi wept and said, "I have gone blind, my sons are still young—if my uncle-in-law will take my place, I need fear nothing." He sent an eloquent envoy to summon him, but Yanhou was suspicious and refused to come. His sister sent a maid to tell him the truth. Yanhou raced to Yangzhou, but before his bow was complete soldiers seized and killed him, and his wife was cast aside.
33
贊曰：全忠，唐之盜也，行密誌梟其元而後已。 田頵使出軍賦而助之，此其謀責難而絕之，非忠於唐也。 棄所附而覬尊大，亦已妄矣。 孔子稱孟公綽為趙、魏老則優，不可以為滕、薛大夫。 如仁厚、田、朱，材不足為吳、蜀之老，可與事天子哉！
The commentator says: Quanzhong was a robber of the Tang. Xingmi was resolved not to rest until he had taken Quanzhong's head. When Tian Jun sent troops and supplies to aid Quanzhong, his aim was to reproach and break with Xingmi—not loyalty to the Tang. To abandon one's patron and reach for supremacy was folly enough. Confucius said that Meng Gongchuo would excel as steward for the great houses of Zhao and Wei, but could not serve as minister in small states like Teng and Xue. Men like Renhou, Tian, and Zhu lacked the talent to be chief stewards even in Wu and Shu—how could they serve the Son of Heaven!
