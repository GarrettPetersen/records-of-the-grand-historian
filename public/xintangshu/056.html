1
25%古之為國者，議事以制，不為刑辟，懼民之知爭端也。 後世作為刑書，惟恐不備，俾民之知所避也。 其為法雖殊，而用心則一，蓋皆欲民之無犯也。 然未知夫導之以德、齊之以禮，而可使民遷善遠罪而不自知也。
In ancient times, rulers governed by deliberating affairs and setting norms rather than drafting criminal codes, because they feared teaching the people where disputes might arise. Later generations wrote penal codes and worried chiefly that nothing be left out, so that the people might know what to shun. The methods differed, but the aim was the same: both approaches sought to keep the people from breaking the law. They did not yet grasp that guiding people with virtue and harmonizing them through ritual could move them toward goodness and away from crime before they even realized it.
2
唐之刑書有四，曰：律、令、格、式。 令者，尊卑貴賤之等數，國家之制度也； 格者，百官有司之所常行之事也； 式者，其所常守之法也。 凡邦國之政，必從事於此三者。 其有所違及人之為惡而入于罪戾者，一斷以律。 律之為書，因隋之舊，為十有二篇：一曰名例，二曰衞禁，三曰職制，四曰戶婚，五曰廄庫，六曰擅興，七曰賊盜，八曰鬬訟，九曰詐偽，十曰雜律，十一曰捕亡，十二曰斷獄。
Tang criminal law comprised four bodies of text: the Statutes, the Ordinances, the Administrative Precedents, and the Formulae. Ordinances defined the ranks of high and low and the institutions of the realm. Administrative Precedents recorded the routine duties of the hundred officials and their bureaus. Formulae set out the standard procedures they were to follow. All governance of the realm had to proceed through these three categories. Any breach of them, or any act of malice that brought a person within the scope of punishment, was resolved solely under the Statutes. The Statutes followed the Sui model in twelve chapters: General Principles; Frontier Security and Prohibitions; Official Duties and Regulations; Households and Marriage; Stables and Storehouses; Unauthorized Mobilization; Banditry and Theft; Assault and Litigation; Fraud and Forgery; Miscellaneous Statutes; Apprehension of Fugitives; and Trial and Punishment.
3
其用刑有五：一曰笞。 笞之為言恥也； 凡過之小者，捶撻以恥之。 漢用竹，後世更以楚。 書曰「扑作教刑」是也。 二曰杖。 杖者，持也：可持以擊也。 書曰「鞭作官刑」是也。 三曰徒。 徒者，奴也； 蓋奴辱之。 周禮曰：其奴，男子入于罪隸，任之以事，寘之圜土而教之，量其罪之輕重，有年數而捨。 四曰流。 書云「流宥五刑」，謂不忍刑殺，宥之于遠也。 五曰死。 乃古大辟之刑也。
Five punishments were applied: the first was beating with the rod. The term for rod-beating denotes shame. Minor offenses were punished with flogging meant to humiliate the offender. The Han used bamboo rods; later dynasties switched to birch switches. This is what the Book of Documents means by "flogging serves as corrective punishment." The second was the staff. Staff means "to hold" — something one can grasp to strike with. This corresponds to the Book of Documents line, "the whip serves as official punishment." The third was penal servitude. Servitude meant enslavement. It reduced offenders to servile humiliation. The Rites of Zhou state that male offenders became criminal bondsmen, were put to labor, confined in the Round Mound for instruction, and released after a term set according to the severity of the offense. The fourth was exile. The Classic says, "exile pardons the five punishments," meaning that when execution was too harsh to bear, the offender was spared by banishment to a distant place. The fifth was death. This was the ancient capital punishment.
4
自隋以前，死刑有五，曰：罄、絞、斬、梟、裂。 而流、徒之刑，鞭笞兼用，數皆踰百。 至隋始定為：笞刑五，自十至于五十； 杖刑五，自六十至于百； 徒刑五，自一年至于三年； 流刑三，自一千里至于二千里； 死刑二，絞、斬。 除其鞭刑及梟首、轘裂之酷。 又有議、請、減、贖、當、免之法。 唐皆因之。 然隋文帝性刻深，而煬帝昏亂，民不勝其毒。
Before the Sui, five forms of capital punishment were recognized: exposure of the corpse, strangulation, decapitation, display of the severed head, and dismemberment. Exile and penal servitude were often combined with flogging and rod-beating, with stroke counts sometimes exceeding one hundred. The Sui first standardized five grades of rod-beating, ranging from ten to fifty strokes. Five grades of staff punishment, from sixty to one hundred strokes. Five grades of penal servitude, from one to three years. Three grades of exile, from one thousand to two thousand li. Two forms of capital punishment: strangulation and decapitation. The Sui abolished flogging with the whip and the cruel punishments of displaying severed heads and cart-sundering. They also established procedures for deliberation, petition, reduction, commutation, offset, and exemption. The Tang adopted all of these provisions. Yet Emperor Wen of Sui was harsh by nature, and Emperor Yang was corrupt and erratic, so the people could not endure their cruelty.
5
唐興，高祖入京師，約法十二條，惟殺人、劫盜、背軍、叛逆者死。 及受禪，命納言劉文靜等損益律令。 武德二年，頒新格五十三條，唯吏受賕、犯盜、詐冒府庫物，赦不原。 凡斷屠日及正月、五月、九月不行刑。 四年，高祖躬錄囚徒，以人因亂冒法者眾，盜非劫傷其主及征人逃亡、官吏枉法，皆原之。 已而又詔僕射裴寂等十五人更撰律令，凡律五百，麗以五十三條。 流罪三，皆加千里； 居作三歲至二歲半者悉為一歲。 餘無改焉。
When the Tang rose, Gaozu entered the capital and proclaimed a twelve-article compact law: only murder, robbery, desertion, and rebellion were capital crimes. After accepting the abdication, he ordered Counselor-in-chief Liu Wenjing and others to revise the statutes and ordinances. In the second year of Wude, he issued fifty-three new administrative precedents, with amnesty withheld only for officials who took bribes, committed theft, or fraudulently claimed government storehouse goods. Executions were suspended on slaughter-prohibition days and during the first, fifth, and ninth months. In the fourth year, Gaozu personally reviewed prisoners. Because many had broken the law amid the chaos, he pardoned theft without robbery injuring the owner, desertion by conscripts, and official malfeasance. He then ordered Vice Director Pei Ji and fourteen others to draft new statutes and ordinances: five hundred statutory articles supplemented by fifty-three additional provisions. All three grades of exile were increased by one thousand li. Terms of labor service between three years and two and a half years were all reduced to one year. Everything else remained unchanged.
6
太宗即位，詔長孫无忌、房玄齡等復定舊令，議絞刑之屬五十，皆免死而斷右趾。 既而又哀其斷毀支體，謂侍臣曰：「肉刑，前代除之久矣，今復斷人趾，吾不忍也。 」王珪、蕭瑀、陳叔達對曰：「受刑者當死而獲生，豈憚去一趾？ 去趾，所以使見者知懼。 今以死刑為斷趾，蓋寬之也。 」帝曰：「公等更思之。 」其後蜀王法曹參軍裴弘獻駮律令四十餘事，乃詔房玄齡與弘獻等重加刪定。 玄齡等以謂「古者五刑，刖居其一。 及肉刑既廢，今以笞、杖、徒、流、死為五刑，而又刖足，是六刑也」。 於是除斷趾法，為加役流三千里，居作二年。
When Taizong ascended the throne, he ordered Zhangsun Wuji, Fang Xuanling, and others to revise the old ordinances. They proposed that the fifty offenses punishable by strangulation should be commuted from death to amputation of the right foot. He soon grew troubled by the mutilation involved and told his ministers, "Corporal punishments were abolished long ago. To cut off men's toes again — I cannot bear it. " Wang Gui, Xiao Yu, and Chen Shuda replied, "Offenders who would have died are allowed to live — would they hesitate to lose a toe? Amputation of the toe is meant to instill fear in those who witness it. Replacing execution with toe amputation is in fact a lenient measure. " The emperor said, "Think it over again. " Later Pei Hongxian, a legal aide under the Prince of Shu, challenged more than forty provisions in the statutes and ordinances, and the emperor ordered Fang Xuanling, Hongxian, and others to revise them again. Fang Xuanling and his colleagues argued, "In antiquity the five punishments included mutilation as one of them. After corporal punishments were abolished, the five punishments became rod-beating, staff, servitude, exile, and death. To add foot amputation would make six punishments." They therefore abolished toe amputation and substituted augmented penal servitude: exile for three thousand li with two years of labor service.
7
太宗嘗覽明堂針灸圖，見人之五藏皆近背，針灸失所，則其害致死，歎曰：「夫箠者，五刑之輕； 死者，人之所重。 安得犯至輕之刑而或致死？ 」遂詔罪人無得鞭背。
Taizong once studied the Hall of Enlightenment acupuncture chart and saw that the five viscera lie close to the back; misplaced needling could prove fatal. He sighed and said, "Flogging is the lightest of the five punishments. Death is what people hold most dear. How can the lightest punishment sometimes bring death? " He then decreed that criminals must not be flogged on the back.
8
五年，河內人李好德坐妖言下獄，大理丞張蘊古以為好德病狂瞀，法不當坐。 治書侍御史權萬紀劾蘊古相州人，好德兄厚德方為相州刺史，故蘊古奏不以實。 太宗怒，遽斬蘊古，既而大悔，因詔「死刑雖令即決，皆三覆奏」。 久之，謂羣臣曰：「死者不可復生。 昔王世充殺鄭頲而猶能悔，近有府史取賕不多，朕殺之，是思之不審也。 決囚雖三覆奏，而頃刻之間，何暇思慮？ 自今宜二日五覆奏。 決日，尚食勿進酒肉，教坊太常輟教習，諸州死罪三覆奏，其日亦蔬食，務合禮撤樂、減膳之意。 」
In the fifth year, Li Haode of Henei was imprisoned for sorcerous speech. Assistant Director Zhang Yungu of the Court of Judicial Review argued that Haode was deranged and should not be punished under the law. Supervising Secretary Quan Wanji impeached Yungu, noting that he was from Xiangzhou while Haode's elder brother Houde was then prefect of Xiangzhou, and therefore Yungu had filed a false report. Taizong was enraged and had Yungu executed at once. He soon deeply regretted it and decreed, "Even when capital punishment is ordered for immediate execution, it must receive threefold review and memorial." Some time later he told the assembled ministers, "The dead cannot be brought back to life. Wang Shichong once killed Zheng Ting and still came to regret it. Recently a bureau clerk took only a small bribe, yet I had him killed — I had not thought the matter through. Even with threefold review, who has time to reflect in the space of a moment? Henceforth there should be fivefold review over two days. On execution day the Imperial Provisioners were to serve no wine or meat, the Directorate of Education and the Court of Imperial Sacrifices were to suspend performances, and prefectures reviewing capital cases were to observe threefold memorial on that day and eat only vegetables, in keeping with the ritual meaning of withdrawing music and reducing meals. "
9
故時律，兄弟分居，蔭不相及，而連坐則俱死。 同州人房彊以弟謀反當從坐，帝因錄囚為之動容，曰：「反逆有二：興師動眾一也，惡言犯法二也。 輕重固異，而鈞謂之反，連坐皆死，豈定法耶？ 」玄齡等議曰：「禮，孫為父尸，故祖有蔭孫令，是祖孫重而兄弟輕。 」於是令：反逆者，祖孫與兄弟緣坐，皆配沒； 惡言犯法者，兄弟配流而已。 玄齡等遂與法司增損隋律，降大辟為流者九十二，流為徒者七十一，以為律； 定令一千五百四十六條，以為令； 又刪武德以來敕三千餘條為七百條，以為格； 又取尚書省列曹及諸寺、監、十六衞計帳以為式。
Under the law then in force, brothers who lived apart did not share privileges of official protection, yet collective punishment still required that all be executed together. Fang Qiang of Tongzhou was to be punished by association because his younger brother had plotted rebellion. While reviewing prisoners, the emperor was moved and said, "Rebellion takes two forms: raising troops is one, and seditious speech violating the law is the other. Their gravity clearly differs, yet both are called rebellion and collective punishment brings death to all. Is that a proper fixed law? " Fang Xuanling and his colleagues argued, "By ritual the grandson represents the father's person, which is why the grandfather's privilege extends to the grandson. Grandfather and grandson weigh more heavily than brothers. " An ordinance was then issued: in cases of rebellion, grandfathers, grandsons, and brothers punished by association were all sentenced to confiscation and exile. For seditious speech alone, brothers were sentenced only to exile. Fang Xuanling and the legal offices then revised the Sui statutes, commuting capital punishment to exile in ninety-two cases and exile to penal servitude in seventy-one, and compiled these as the Statutes. They fixed 1,546 articles as the Ordinances. They also reduced more than three thousand edicts issued since Wude to seven hundred articles as the Administrative Precedents. They further compiled account ledgers from the Department of State Affairs bureaus, the directorates, supervisorates, and the Sixteen Guards as the Formulae.
10
凡州縣皆有獄，而京兆、河南獄治京師，其諸司有罪及金吾捕者又有大理獄。 京師之囚，刑部月一奏，御史巡行之。 每歲立春至秋及大祭祀、致齊，朔望、上下弦、二十四氣、雨及夜未明，假日、斷屠月，皆停死刑。
Every prefecture and county had a prison; the Jingzhao and Henan prisons handled the capital. Offenses by government offices and arrests by the Gold Crow Guard were held in the Court of Judicial Review prison. Capital prisoners were reported monthly to the Ministry of Justice, and censors conducted inspection tours. Each year from Establishment of Spring through autumn, and during major sacrifices, fasting periods, new and full moons, quarter moons, the twenty-four seasonal nodes, rain, the hours before dawn, holidays, and slaughter-prohibition months, capital punishment was suspended.
11
京師決死，涖以御史、金吾，在外則上佐，餘皆判官涖之。 五品以上罪論死，乘車就刑，大理正涖之，或賜死于家。 凡囚已刑，無親屬者，將作給棺，瘞于京城七里外，壙有甎銘，上揭以榜，家人得取以葬。
Capital executions in the capital were supervised by censors and the Gold Crow Guard; in the provinces by the chief aide; elsewhere by judicial reviewers. Officials of fifth rank and above who were sentenced to death went to execution by carriage under supervision of the Court of Judicial Review director, or were granted death at home. Executed prisoners without kin received coffins from the Directorate of Palace Buildings and were buried seven li outside the capital. Brick inscriptions marked the tomb, a placard was posted above, and relatives could claim the body for burial.
12
諸獄之長官，五日一慮囚。 夏置漿飲，月一沐之； 疾病給醫藥，重者釋械，其家一人入侍，職事散官三品以上，婦女子孫二人入侍。
Prison chiefs reviewed cases every five days. In summer they were given drink and gruel; once a month they were bathed. The sick received medicine; the seriously ill were unfettered. One family member could attend; for active-duty and honorary officials of third rank and above, two female relatives, sons, or grandsons could attend.
13
天下疑獄讞大理寺不能決，尚書省眾議之，錄可為法者送祕書省。 奏報不馳驛。 經覆而決者，刑部歲以正月遣使巡覆，所至，閱獄囚杻校、糧餉，治不如法者。
Cases throughout the realm that the Court of Judicial Review could not decide were deliberated collectively by the Department of State Affairs; precedents suitable as law were recorded and sent to the Palace Library. Memorial reports were not sent by urgent courier relay. Each year in the first month the Ministry of Justice dispatched envoys to review cases decided after appeal. Wherever they went they inspected prisoners' fetters and provisions and punished officials who failed to follow procedure.
14
杻校鉗鎖皆有長短廣狹之制，量囚輕重用之。
Fetters, cangues, shackles, and chains were all regulated in length and width according to the severity of the offense.
15
囚二十日一訊，三訊而止，數不過二百。
Prisoners were interrogated every twenty days, with interrogation ending after three sessions and the total not exceeding two hundred blows.
16
凡杖，皆長三尺五寸，削去節目。 訊杖，大頭徑三分二釐，小頭二分二釐。 常行杖，大頭二分七釐，小頭一分七釐。 笞杖，大頭二分，小頭一分有半。
All staffs were three feet five inches long, with knots and eyes trimmed away. Interrogation staffs measured three fen two li at the thick end and two fen two li at the thin end. Regular execution staffs measured two fen seven li at the thick end and one fen seven li at the thin end. Rod-beating staffs measured two fen at the thick end and one and a half fen at the thin end.
17
死罪校而加杻，官品勳階第七者，鎖禁之。 輕罪及十歲以下至八十以上者、廢疾、侏儒、懷姙皆頌繫以待斷。
Capital offenders wore the cangue with added fetters; those of seventh official rank or merit grade were confined with locks. Light offenders, children under ten, the elderly over eighty, the disabled, dwarfs, and pregnant women were all detained without fetters pending final judgment.
18
居作者著鉗若校，京師隸將作，女子隸少府縫作。 旬給假一日，臘、寒食二日，毋出役院。 病者釋鉗校、給假，疾差陪役。 謀反者男女奴婢沒為官奴婢，隸司農，七十者免之。 凡役，男子入于蔬圃，女子入于廚饎。
Convicts at labor wore manacles or the cangue. In the capital they served under the Director of Palace Construction; women were assigned to the Minister of the Lesser Treasury for sewing. They received one day of leave each ten-day period, and two days at the La and Cold Food festivals — though they were not permitted to leave the labor compound. The sick had their manacles and cangues removed and were granted leave; once recovered, they returned to labor. When someone plotted rebellion, his male and female slaves were confiscated and made government slaves under the Minister of Agriculture; those aged seventy or above were exempted. As a general rule of penal service, men were sent to vegetable gardens and women to kitchen work.
19
流移人在道疾病，婦人免乳，祖父母、父母喪，男女奴婢死，皆給假，授程糧。
Exiles who fell ill on the road, women in postpartum nursing exemption, those mourning grandparents or parents, and the death of male or female slaves — in all such cases leave was granted and journey rations issued.
20
非反逆緣坐，六歲縱之，特流者三歲縱之，有官者得復仕。
Those not implicated through collective guilt in treason or rebellion were released after six years; those under special exile after three years; and those who had previously held office were allowed to return to service.
21
初，太宗以古者斷獄，訊於三槐、九棘，乃詔：「死罪，中書、門下五品以上及尚書等平議之； 三品以上犯公罪流，私罪徒，皆不追身。 」凡所以纖悉條目，必本於仁恕。 然自張蘊古之死也，法官以失出為誡，有失入者，又不加罪，自是吏法稍密。 帝以問大理卿劉德威，對曰：「律，失入減三等，失出減五等。 今失入無辜，而失出為大罪，故吏皆深文。 」帝矍然，遂命失出入者皆如律，自此吏亦持平。
Early in his reign, Taizong recalled that in antiquity criminal cases were tried at the Three Paulownias and Nine Thorn Trees, and decreed: "In capital cases, officials of fifth rank and above in the Secretariat and Chancellery, together with the Department of State Affairs and others, shall jointly review the sentence; Officials of third rank and above guilty of public offenses meriting exile or private offenses meriting penal servitude were not subject to personal arrest. " Every fine provision in the code was grounded in humanity and forbearance. Yet after Zhang Yungu's execution, judges treated erroneous acquittal as the gravest warning, while erroneous conviction went unpunished; from that point the law grew steadily harsher in officials' hands. The emperor asked Liu Dewei, chief of the Court of Judicial Review, who answered: "The statutes reduce punishment by three degrees for erroneous conviction and by five for erroneous acquittal. Yet now convicting the innocent carries no penalty, while acquitting the guilty is treated as a grave offense — so officials everywhere distort the law to severity. " The emperor was startled, and ordered that both kinds of judicial error be punished as the statutes prescribed; thereafter officials applied the law with greater balance.
22
十四年，詔流罪無遠近皆徙邊要州。 後犯者寖少。 十六年，又徙死罪以實西州，流者戍之，以罪輕重為更限。
In the fourteenth year, an edict ordered that all exiles, whatever the original distance prescribed, were to be sent to strategic border prefectures. Thereafter the number of offenders gradually declined. In the sixteenth year, capital offenders were sent to populate Western Prefecture, with exiles assigned to garrison duty there; terms of service varied according to the severity of the offense.
23
廣州都督瓽仁弘嘗率鄉兵二千助高祖起，封長沙郡公。 仁弘交通豪酋，納金寶，沒降獠為奴婢，又擅賦夷人。 既還，有舟七十。 或告其贓，法當死。 帝哀其老且有功，因貸為庶人，乃召五品以上，謂曰：「賞罰所以代天行法，今朕寬仁弘死，是自弄法以負天也。 人臣有過，請罪於君，君有過，宜請罪於天。 其令有司設藳席于南郊三日，朕將請罪。 」房玄齡等曰：「寬仁弘不以私而以功，何罪之請？ 」百僚頓首三請，乃止。
Qu Renhong, military governor of Guangzhou, had once raised two thousand local troops to help Gaozu seize power and was enfeoffed Duke of Changsha. Renhong colluded with powerful tribal chiefs, accepted bribes in gold and jewels, enslaved surrendered tribal peoples, and levied unauthorized taxes on indigenous populations. On his return he came with seventy boats. Someone reported his corruption, and by law the offense merited death. The emperor, moved by Renhong's age and former service, commuted his sentence and reduced him to commoner status. He then summoned officials of fifth rank and above and said: "Reward and punishment exist so that the ruler may execute Heaven's law. By sparing Renhong from death, I have twisted the law and failed Heaven. When a subject errs, he should beg forgiveness of his ruler; when a ruler errs, he should beg forgiveness of Heaven. Let the relevant offices prepare a straw mat at the southern suburb for three days, and I shall there beg Heaven's forgiveness. " Fang Xuanling and others said: "Your Majesty spared Renhong not from private favor but for his past service — what offense requires begging forgiveness? " The assembled officials kowtowed and thrice entreated him, and at last he desisted.
24
太宗以英武定天下，然其天姿仁恕。 初即位，有勸以威刑肅天下者，魏徵以為不可，因為上言王政本於仁恩，所以愛民厚俗之意，太宗欣然納之，遂以寬仁治天下，而於刑法尤慎。 四年，天下斷死罪二十九人。 六年，親錄囚徒，閔死罪者三百九十人，縱之還家，期以明年秋即刑； 及期，囚皆詣朝堂，無後者，太宗嘉其誠信，悉原之。 然嘗謂羣臣曰：「吾聞語曰『一歲再赦，好人喑啞』。 吾有天下未嘗數赦者，不欲誘民於幸免也。 」自房玄齡等更定律、令、格、式，訖太宗世，用之無所變改。
Taizong had conquered the realm through martial prowess, yet his nature was humane and forgiving. Soon after his accession, some urged him to awe the realm with harsh punishments. Wei Zheng objected, arguing that kingship rests on humaneness and grace — the very purpose of cherishing the people and strengthening custom. Taizong gladly accepted this counsel, governed with leniency and mercy, and was especially cautious in matters of criminal law. In the fourth year of his reign, only twenty-nine death sentences were passed empire-wide. In the sixth year he personally reviewed the prisons, took pity on three hundred ninety condemned prisoners, released them to return home, and appointed the following autumn for their execution; When the appointed time came, every prisoner returned to the court; none absconded. Taizong praised their good faith and pardoned them all. Yet he once told his ministers: "I have heard it said, 'Pardon twice in a single year, and the virtuous will no longer speak out.' Since taking the throne I have never issued frequent amnesties, for I do not wish to teach the people to count on escaping punishment. " From the revision of the Statutes, Ordinances, Administrative Precedents, and Formulae by Fang Xuanling and others through the end of Taizong's reign, the code remained unchanged.
25
高宗初即位，詔律學之士撰律疏。 又詔長孫无忌等增損格敕，其曹司常務曰留司格，頒之天下曰散頒格。 龍朔、儀鳳中，司刑太常伯李敬玄、左僕射劉仁軌相繼又加刊正。
At the beginning of Gaozong's reign, he ordered scholars of statutory law to compile a commentary on the penal code. He also ordered Zhangsun Wuji and others to revise administrative edicts: those governing the routine duties of the various offices were called Retained Department Precedents; those issued empire-wide were called Broadly Distributed Precedents. During the Longshuo and Yifeng periods, Li Jingxuan, Minister of Justice, and Liu Ren'gui, Left Vice Director, successively undertook further revisions.
26
武后時，內史裴居道、鳳閣侍郎韋方質等又刪武德以後至于垂拱詔敕為新格，藏於有司，曰垂拱留司格。 神龍元年，中書令韋安石又續其後至於神龍，為散頒格。 睿宗即位，戶部尚書岑羲等又著太極格。
Under Empress Wu, Pei Judao, Palace Secretary, Wei Fangzhi, Vice Minister of the Phoenix Pavilion, and others compiled edicts and decrees from the Wude era through Chuigong into new precedents, deposited with the relevant offices as the Chuigong Retained Department Precedents. In the first year of Shenlong, Wei Anshi, Chief of the Secretariat, continued the compilation through that reign, producing Broadly Distributed Precedents. When Ruizong acceded, Cen Yi, Minister of Revenue, and others compiled the Taizi Precedents.
27
玄宗開元三年，黃門監盧懷慎等又著開元格。 至二十五年，中書令李林甫又著新格，凡所損益數千條，明年，吏部尚書宋璟又著後格，皆以開元名書。 天寶四載，又詔刑部尚書蕭炅稍復增損之。
In the third year of Kaiyuan, Lu Huaishen, Supervisor of the Yellow Gate, and others compiled the Kaiyuan Precedents. By the twenty-fifth year of Kaiyuan, Li Linfu, Chief of the Secretariat, compiled new precedents comprising several thousand revisions; the following year Song Jing, Minister of Personnel, compiled supplementary precedents — all bearing the Kaiyuan name. In the fourth year of Tianbao, an edict ordered Xiao Jiong, Minister of Justice, to revise the code once more.
28
肅宗、代宗無所改造。 至德宗時，詔中書門下選律學之士，取至德以來制敕奏讞，掇其可為法者藏之，而不名書。
Under Suzong and Daizong the code underwent no further revision. Under Dezong, an edict ordered the Secretariat and Chancellery to select scholars of statutory law, collect decrees and memorialized judgments since the Zhide era, preserve what could serve as law, and leave the compilation untitled.
29
憲宗時，刑部侍郎許孟容等刪天寶以後敕為開元格後敕。
Under Xianzong, Xu Mengrong, Vice Minister of Justice, and others compiled post-Tianbao edicts into the Supplementary Edicts to the Kaiyuan Precedents.
30
文宗命尚書省郎官各刪本司敕，而丞與侍郎覆視，中書門下參其可否而奏之，為大和格後敕。 開成三年，刑部侍郎狄兼謩採開元二十六年以後至于開成制敕，刪其繁者，為開成詳定格。
Wenzong ordered secretaries in the Department of State Affairs each to compile edicts from their own bureau; assistants and vice ministers reviewed the selections, and the Secretariat and Chancellery evaluated and memorialized them, producing the Supplementary Edicts to the Dahe Precedents. In the third year of Kaicheng, Di Jianmo, Vice Minister of Justice, collected decrees from the twenty-sixth year of Kaiyuan through Kaicheng, stripped away redundancies, and compiled the Detailed Kaicheng Precedents.
31
宣宗時，左衞率府倉曹參軍張戣以刑律分類為門，而附以格敕，為大中刑律統類，詔刑部頒行之。
Under Xuanzong, Zhang Wei, army store clerk of the Left Guard, arranged the penal statutes by category with administrative edicts appended, producing the Comprehensive Penal Categories of the Dazhong era, which the Ministry of Justice was ordered to promulgate.
32
此其當世所施行而著見者，其餘有其書而不常行者，不足紀也。 書曰：「慎乃出令。 」蓋法令在簡，簡則明，行之在久，久則信，而中材之主，庸愚之吏，常莫克守之，而喜為變革。 至其繁積，則雖有精明之士不能徧習，而吏得上下以為姦，此刑書之弊也。 蓋自高宗以來，其大節鮮可紀，而格令之書，不勝其繁也。
These were the codes actually enforced and clearly attested in their day; others existed in writing but were not regularly applied, and are not worth recording. The Book of Documents says: "Be careful when issuing commands. " Laws succeed through brevity, for brevity brings clarity; through endurance, for endurance brings trust. Yet mediocre rulers and dull officials commonly fail to uphold this, and delight in constant revision. When codes accumulate into impenetrable bulk, even the sharpest minds cannot master them, and officials manipulate them at every level for private gain — such is the curse of penal law. From Gaozong's reign onward, little of grand principle remained worth recording, while the volumes of precedents and ordinances grew beyond reckoning.
33
高宗既昏懦，而繼以武氏之亂，毒流天下，幾至於亡。 自永徽以後，武氏已得志，而刑濫矣。 當時大獄，以尚書刑部、御史臺、大理寺雜按，謂之「三司」，而法吏以慘酷為能，至不釋枷而笞棰以死者，皆不禁。 律有杖百，凡五十九條，犯者或至死而杖未畢，乃詔除其四十九條，然無益也。 武后已稱制，懼天下不服，欲制以威，乃修後周告密之法，詔官司受訊，有言密事者，馳驛奏之。 自徐敬業、越王貞、琅邪王沖等起兵討亂，武氏益恐。 乃引酷吏周興、來俊臣輩典大獄，與侯思止、王弘義、郭弘霸、李敬仁、康暐、衞遂忠等集告事數百人，共為羅織，構陷無辜。 自唐之宗室與朝廷之士，日被告捕，不可勝數，天下之人，為之仄足，如狄仁傑、魏元忠等皆幾不免。 左臺御史周矩上疏曰：「比姦憸告訐，習以為常。 推劾之吏，以深刻為功，鑿空爭能，相矜以虐。 泥耳囊頭，摺脅籤爪，縣髮燻耳，臥鄰穢溺，刻害支體，糜爛獄中，號曰『獄持』； 閉絕食飲，晝夜使不得眠，號曰『宿囚』。 殘賊威暴，取快目前。 被誣者苟求得死，何所不至？ 為國者以仁為宗，以刑為助，周用仁而昌，秦用刑而亡。 願陛下緩刑用仁，天下幸甚！ 」武后不納。 麟臺正字陳子昂亦上書切諫，不省。 及周興、來俊臣等誅死，后亦老，其意少衰，而狄仁傑、姚崇、宋璟、王及善相與論垂拱以來酷濫之冤，太后感寤，由是不復殺戮。 然其毒虐所被，自古未之有也。 大足元年，乃詔法司及推事使敢多作辯狀而加語者，以故入論。 中宗、韋后繼以亂敗。
Gaozong was weak and indecisive, and the Wu clan's reign of terror followed; its poison spread across the realm and nearly brought the dynasty to ruin. From the Yonghui era onward, once the Wu clan had gained power, punishment degenerated into abuse. Major cases were jointly tried by the Ministry of Justice, the Censorate, and the Court of Judicial Review — the so-called "Three Offices" — while judicial officers treated cruelty as accomplishment; beating prisoners to death without even removing the cangue went unpunished. The code prescribed one hundred blows in fifty-nine articles, and offenders sometimes died before the full count was delivered. An edict removed forty-nine of those articles — yet it did no good. Once Empress Wu had seized power, fearing the realm would not submit, she sought to rule through terror and revived the Later Zhou system of secret denunciation, decreeing that any secret report heard during an interrogation be sent to her by urgent courier. After Xu Jingye, the Prince of Yue, Zhen, the Prince of Langye, Chong, and others took up arms against her, the Wu clan's fear only deepened. She then put cruel officials such as Zhou Xing and Lai Junchen in charge of major cases. Together with Hou Sizhi, Wang Hongyi, Guo Hongba, Li Jingren, Kang Wei, Wei Suizhong, and others, they gathered hundreds of informers, fabricated charges, and framed the innocent. Day after day members of the imperial clan and court officials were denounced and arrested beyond count; the whole realm lived in dread; even men of the stature of Di Renjie and Wei Yuanzhong nearly perished. Left Censor Zhou Ju submitted a memorial saying: "Of late, treacherous denunciations and malicious accusations have become routine. Investigating officers treat severity as merit, invent charges to outdo one another, and compete in cruelty. They stop the ears and hood the head, break ribs and drive splinters under the nails, hang prisoners by the hair and smoke their ears, force them to lie beside filth and ordure, mutilate the body until it rots in the cell — a technique called 'Prison Torment'; They cut off food and drink and allow no sleep day or night — a technique called 'Sleepless Detention.' They are savage and brutal, gratifying themselves in the moment. When the falsely accused would confess to anything just to die and end their suffering, what limit remains? A state should take humaneness as its foundation and punishment as its instrument: Zhou relied on humaneness and prospered; Qin relied on punishment and was destroyed. I beg Your Majesty to soften punishment and govern with humaneness — the realm would be immeasurably blessed! " Empress Wu paid no heed. Chen Zi'ang, proofreader of the Lin'tai Pavilion, also submitted an urgent memorial of remonstrance; she ignored it. After Zhou Xing, Lai Junchen, and others were put to death, the Empress had also grown old and her zeal somewhat faded. Di Renjie, Yao Chong, Song Jing, and Wang Jishan together reviewed the cruel excesses and wrongful convictions since the Chuigong era; the Empress Dowager was moved to repentance, and thereafter ceased the killing. Yet the poison and cruelty of her reign had no precedent in history. In the first year of Dazu, an edict declared that judicial offices and investigation commissioners who fabricated elaborate confessions or inserted incriminating language would be prosecuted for intentional false conviction. Zhongzong and Empress Wei followed, and the court sank again into chaos and ruin.
34
玄宗自初即位，勵精政事，常自選太守、縣令，告戒以言，而良吏布州縣，民獲安樂； 二十年間，號稱治平，衣食富足，人罕犯法。 是歲刑部所斷天下死罪五十八人，往時大理獄，相傳鳥雀不栖，至是有鵲巢其庭樹，羣臣稱賀，以為幾致刑錯。 然而李林甫用事矣，自來俊臣誅後，至此始復起大獄，以誣陷所殺數十百人，如韋堅、李邕等皆一時名臣，天下冤之。 而天子亦自喜邊功，遣將分出以擊蠻夷，兵數大敗，士卒死傷以萬計，國用耗乏，而轉漕輸送，遠近煩費，民力既弊，盜賊起而獄訟繁矣。 天子方惻然，詔曰：「徒非重刑，而役者寒暑不釋械繫。 杖，古以代肉刑也，或犯非巨蠧而棰以至死，其皆免，以配諸軍自效。 民年八十以上及重疾有罪，皆勿坐。 侍丁犯法，原之俾終養。 」以此施德其民。 然巨盜起，天下被其毒，民莫蒙其賜也。
From the start of his reign Xuanzong applied himself diligently to governance, personally selecting prefects and magistrates and instructing them by edict; capable officials filled the provinces and counties, and the people lived in peace; Within twenty years the age was hailed as one of good order; food and clothing were plentiful, and crime was rare. That year the Ministry of Justice condemned only fifty-eight persons to death empire-wide. It was once said that not even birds would perch in the Court of Judicial Review prison; now magpies nested in its courtyard trees. Ministers congratulated the throne, declaring that the age had nearly reached the ideal in which punishments fall unused. Yet Li Linfu was already in power, and for the first time since Lai Junchen's execution major political trials resumed; dozens or hundreds died through fabricated charges. Men such as Wei Jian and Li Yong were among the foremost ministers of the day, and the realm mourned their wrongful deaths. The Son of Heaven also craved frontier glory, dispatching generals on multiple campaigns against southern peoples; the armies suffered repeated defeats, with tens of thousands dead and wounded; state revenues were drained by supply lines stretching across the realm; once the people were exhausted, banditry spread and the courts overflowed with litigation. The emperor, moved with compassion, decreed: "Penal servitude is not a grave punishment, yet labor convicts remain shackled through winter and summer alike. The staff was anciently a substitute for mutilation, yet some offenders of no grave corruption were beaten to death — let all such punishments be remitted, and the offenders assigned to military service to redeem themselves. Commoners over eighty and the gravely ill who had committed offenses were not to be punished. Filial-support conscripts who broke the law were pardoned so they could finish caring for their parents. " Thus he sought to show mercy to the people. Yet great bandits ravaged the realm, and the people never tasted his mercy.
35
安、史之亂，偽官陸大鈞等背賊來歸，及慶緒奔河北，脅從者相率待罪闕下，自大臣陳希烈等合數百人。 以御史大夫李峴、中丞崔器等為三司使，而肅宗方喜刑名，器亦刻深，乃以河南尹達奚珣等三十九人為重罪，斬于獨柳樹者十一人，珣及韋恆腰斬，陳希烈等賜自盡於獄中者七人，其餘決重杖死者二十一人。 以歲除日行刑，集百官臨視，家屬流竄。 初，史思明、高秀巖等皆自拔歸命，聞珣等被誅，懼不自安，乃復叛。 而三司用刑連年，流貶相繼。 及王璵為相，請詔三司推覈未已者，一切免之。 然河北叛人畏誅不降，兵連不解，朝廷屢起大獄。 肅宗後亦悔，歎曰：「朕為三司所悞。 」臨崩，詔天下流人皆釋之。
During the An-Shi rebellion, Lu Dajun and other officials who had served the rebel regime defected to the throne; when Shi Qingxu fled to Hebei, those who had followed under duress came in succession to await judgment before the palace gates — from senior ministers such as Chen Xilie on down, several hundred in all. Li Xian, Censor-in-Chief, and Vice Censor Cui Qi were appointed Three Offices commissioners. Suzong then favored harsh justice, and Cui Qi was himself severe. Thirty-nine men including Daxi Xun, Intendant of Henan, were judged guilty of grave offenses: eleven were beheaded at the Lone Willow Tree; Xun and Wei Heng were cut in two at the waist; seven including Chen Xilie were granted suicide in prison; the remaining twenty-one were beaten to death. The executions were carried out on New Year's Eve before an assembly of officials; the condemned men's families were exiled. Shi Siming, Gao Xiuyan, and others had earlier defected of their own accord; when they learned that Xun and the others had been executed, fearing for their own lives, they rebelled again. The Three Offices continued to apply harsh justice year after year, with exile and demotion following one upon another. When Wang Yu became chief minister, he petitioned for an edict pardoning all cases still under investigation by the Three Offices. Yet rebels in Hebei, fearing execution, refused to surrender; the fighting dragged on, and the court repeatedly opened major political trials. Suzong later repented as well, sighing: "I was led astray by the Three Offices. " On his deathbed he decreed the release of all exiles throughout the realm.
36
代宗性仁恕，常以至德以來用刑為戒。 及河、洛平，下詔河北、河南吏民任偽官者，一切不問。 得史朝義將士妻子四百餘人，皆赦之。 僕固懷恩反，免其家，不緣坐。 劇賊高玉聚徒南山，啗人數千，後擒獲，會赦，代宗將貸其死，公卿議請為葅醢，帝不從，卒杖殺之。 諫者常諷帝政寬，故朝廷不肅。 帝笑曰：「艱難時無以逮下，顧刑法峻急，有威無恩，朕不忍也。 」即位五年，府縣寺獄無重囚。 故時，別敕決人捶無數。 寶應元年，詔曰：「凡制敕與一頓杖者，其數止四十； 至到與一頓及重杖一頓、痛杖一頓者，皆止六十。 」
Daizong was humane and forgiving by nature, and constantly held up the excesses of punishment since the Zhide era as a warning. Once the He-Luo region was pacified, he decreed that officials and commoners in Hebei and Henan who had served the rebel regime would not be prosecuted. He captured more than four hundred wives and children of Shi Chaoyi's officers and soldiers and pardoned them all. When Pugu Huai'en rebelled, he exempted Huai'en's family from collective punishment. The ferocious bandit Gao Yu gathered a following in the Southern Mountains and devoured thousands of people. After his capture, when an amnesty was declared, Daizong was inclined to spare him, but the high officials urged that he be minced alive. The emperor refused and had him beaten to death instead. Remonstrators often criticized the emperor's leniency, complaining that the court lacked discipline. The emperor laughed and said, "In hard times one cannot reach down to the people; yet if the law is harsh and urgent, there is awe but no grace — I cannot bear that." " Within five years of his accession, the prisons of prefectures, counties, and directorates held no serious offenders. In former times, special edicts ordering beatings imposed no limit on the number of strokes. In the first year of Baoying, an edict declared, "Whenever an ordinance or edict orders a single session of beating, the count shall not exceed forty strokes; for a single session, a heavy beating in one session, or a painful beating in one session, the count shall not exceed sixty strokes. "
37
德宗性猜忌少恩，然用刑無大濫。 刑部侍郎班宏言：「謀反、大逆及叛、惡逆四者，十惡之大也，犯者宜如律。 其餘當斬、絞刑者，決重杖一頓處死，以代極法。 」故時，死罪皆先決杖，其數或百或六十，於是悉罷之。
Dezong was suspicious and sparing of grace by nature, yet he did not greatly abuse punishment. Vice Minister of Justice Ban Hong said, "Plotting rebellion, great treason, rebellion, and wicked treason are among the ten abominations; offenders should be handled according to statute. For all other offenses meriting decapitation or strangulation, one session of heavy staff beating to death should substitute for capital punishment." In former times, capital offenders were first beaten, sometimes one hundred or sixty strokes; this practice was now entirely abolished.
38
憲宗英果明斷，自即位數誅方鎮，欲治僭叛，一以法度，然於用刑喜寬仁。 是時，李吉甫、李絳為相。 吉甫言：「治天下必任賞罰，陛下頻降赦令，蠲逋負，賑飢民，恩德至矣。 然典刑未舉，中外有懈怠心。 」絳曰：「今天下雖未大治，亦未甚亂，乃古平國用中典之時。 自古欲治之君，必先德化，至暴亂之世，始專任刑法。 吉甫之言過矣。 」憲宗以為然。 司空于頔亦諷帝用刑以收威柄，帝謂宰相曰：「頔懷姦謀，欲朕失人心也。 」元和八年，詔：「兩京、關內、河東、河北、淮南、山南東西道死罪十惡、殺人、鑄錢、造印，若彊盜持仗劫京兆界中及它盜贓踰三匹者，論如故。 其餘死罪皆流天德五城，父祖子孫欲隨者，勿禁。 」蓋刑者，政之輔也。 政得其道，仁義興行，而禮讓成俗，然猶不敢廢刑，所以為民防也，寬之而已。 今不隆其本、顧風俗謂何而廢常刑，是弛民之禁，啟其姦，由積水而決其防。 故自玄宗廢徒杖刑，至是又廢死刑，民未知德，而徒以為幸也。
Xianzong was heroic, resolute, and clear in judgment. From his accession he repeatedly executed regional commanders, seeking to curb rebellion through law alone, yet in applying punishment he favored leniency and mercy. At this time Li Jifu and Li Jiang served as chief ministers. Li Jifu said, "To govern the realm one must employ reward and punishment. Your Majesty has frequently issued amnesties, remitted arrears, and relieved the hungry — Your grace has reached its utmost. Yet standard punishments have not been enforced, and slackness has spread through the court and the realm." Li Jiang replied, "The realm today is not greatly well governed, yet it is not greatly chaotic either — this is the time when a peaceful state applies the middle standard of punishments. Throughout history, rulers who sought good order began with moral transformation; only in ages of violent disorder did they rely solely on criminal law. Li Jifu's argument goes too far." Xianzong agreed. Minister of Works Yu Di also urged the emperor to use harsh punishments to recover authority. The emperor told his chief ministers, "Yu Di harbors treacherous designs and wishes me to lose the people's hearts." In the eighth year of Yuanhe, an edict declared that in the two capitals, Guannei, Hedong, Hebei, Huainan, and the eastern and western circuits of Shannan, capital crimes among the ten abominations, murder, coining, seal forging, armed robbery within the Jingzhao jurisdiction, and theft of booty exceeding three bolts of silk would be punished as before. All other capital crimes were commuted to exile at the Five Cities of Tiande; fathers, grandfathers, sons, and grandsons who wished to accompany the exile were not forbidden. Punishment is the auxiliary of government. When government attains the Way, humaneness and righteousness flourish and ritual and deference become custom — yet punishment is not abolished, for it safeguards the people; one only moderates its application. Now, without elevating moral foundations and with customs as they are, what does it mean to abolish regular punishments? This relaxes the people's restraints and opens the way to wrongdoing — like storing water and then breaking the dam. From the time Xuanzong abolished penal servitude and staff punishments down to this reign, capital punishment was again effectively abolished; the people had not yet learned virtue and merely counted themselves fortunate.
39
穆宗童昏，然頗知慎刑法，每有司斷大獄，令中書舍人一人參酌而輕重之，號「參酌院」。 大理少卿崔𣏌奏曰：「國家法度，高祖、太宗定制二百餘年矣。 周禮正月布刑，張之門閭及都鄙邦國，所以屢丁寧，使四方謹行之。 大理寺，陛下守法之司也。 今別設參酌之官，有司定罪，乃議其出入，是與奪繫於人情，而法官不得守其職。 昔子路問政，孔子曰：『必也正名乎。 』臣以為參酌之名不正，宜廢。 」乃罷之。
Muzong was childish and dull-witted, yet he knew to be cautious in criminal law. Whenever the offices tried major cases, he ordered one Secretariat drafter to participate in deliberating severity and leniency — the so-called "Participation and Deliberation Office." Assistant Director Cui Gong of the Court of Judicial Review memorialized, "The state's laws and institutions were established by Gaozu and Taizong more than two hundred years ago. The Rites of Zhou prescribe that in the first month punishments are promulgated and posted at gates and lanes and in capitals and states, so that through repeated admonition all quarters may carefully observe them. The Court of Judicial Review is Your Majesty's office for upholding the law. Now to establish a separate participation-and-deliberation office, so that after the relevant office has determined guilt another body deliberates increase or decrease — this ties outcomes to human feeling, and judges cannot uphold their duty. Confucius told Zilu, when he asked about government, 'One must first rectify names.' I consider that the name participation-and-deliberation is incorrect and should be abolished." It was then abolished.
40
大和六年，興平縣民上官興以醉殺人而逃，聞械其父，乃自歸。 京兆尹杜悰、御史中丞宇文鼎以其就刑免父，請減死。 詔兩省議，以為殺人者死，百王所守； 若許以生，是誘之殺人也。 諫官亦以為言。 文宗以興免父囚，近於義，杖流靈州，君子以為失刑。 文宗好治，躬自謹畏，然閹宦肆孽不能制。 至誅殺大臣，夷滅其族，濫及者不可勝數，心知其冤，為之飲恨流涕，而莫能救止。 蓋仁者制亂，而弱者縱之，然則剛彊非不仁，而柔弱者仁之賊也。
In the sixth year of Dahe, Shangguan Xing of Xingping county killed a man while drunk and fled; when he learned his father had been put in fetters, he surrendered voluntarily. Jingzhao Intendant Du Cong and Vice Censor-in-Chief Yuwen Ding, because Xing had surrendered to spare his father, petitioned to reduce the death sentence. An edict ordered deliberation by the two departments, which held that murderers deserve death — a principle all kings uphold; to spare him would be to entice people to kill. Remonstrating officials also spoke against leniency. Wenzong, moved that Xing had spared his father imprisonment, considered this close to righteousness and had him beaten and exiled to Lingzhou; men of discernment held that justice had been compromised. Wenzong loved good governance and personally practiced caution and restraint, yet he could not control the eunuchs' wanton crimes. When great ministers were executed and their clans exterminated, with countless innocents implicated, he knew in his heart they were wronged, wept in bitter resentment, yet could not rescue them. The humane govern disorder; the weak indulge it — yet firmness is not inhumaneness, while weakness is the enemy of humaneness.
41
武宗用李德裕誅劉稹等，大刑舉矣，而性嚴刻。 故時，竊盜無死，所以原民情迫於飢寒也，至是贓滿千錢者死，至宣宗乃罷之。 而宣宗亦自喜刑名，常曰：「犯我法，雖子弟不宥也。 」然少仁恩，唐德自是衰矣。
Wuzong, through Li Deyu, executed Liu Zhen and others; great punishments were carried out, yet his nature was harsh and severe. In former times petty theft was not capital, to excuse the people's plight under hunger and cold; now theft of a thousand cash or more was capital; only under Xuanzong was this abolished. Xuanzong also delighted in criminal law, often saying, "Those who violate my law — even kinsmen are not spared." Yet he had little humaneness and grace; from this point Tang virtue declined.
42
蓋自高祖、太宗除隋虐亂，治以寬平，民樂其安，重於犯法，致治之美，幾乎三代之盛時。 考其推心惻物，其可謂仁矣！ 自高宗、武后以來，毒流邦家，唐祚絕而復續。 玄宗初勵精為政，二十年間，刑獄減省，歲斷死罪纔五十八人。 以此見致治雖難，勉之則易，未有為而不至者。 自此以後，兵革遂興，國家多故，而人主規規，無復太宗之志。 其雖有心於治者，亦不能講考大法，而性有寬猛，凡所更革，一切臨時苟且，或重或輕，徒為繁文，不足以示後世。 而高祖、太宗之法，僅守而存。 故自肅宗以來，所可書者幾希矣； 懿宗以後，無所稱焉。
From Gaozu and Taizong, who removed the Sui's cruel disorder and governed with leniency and peace, the people rejoiced in security and dreaded offending the law — their governance nearly approached the flourishing age of the Three Dynasties. Considering how they extended their hearts in compassion toward all beneath Heaven, they may truly be called humane! From Gaozong and Empress Wu onward, poison flowed through the state; the Tang mandate was broken and then restored. At the beginning Xuanzong applied himself diligently to government; within twenty years prisons and punishments were reduced, and annual death sentences amounted to only fifty-eight persons. From this one sees that though good governance is difficult, if one strives for it, it is easy — nothing attempted fails to reach completion. From this time onward warfare arose in succession, the state faced endless troubles, and rulers fussed over small matters without recovering Taizong's ambition. Even those with some heart for governance could not examine and apply the great laws; their natures swung between leniency and severity, and all reforms were temporary makeshifts, sometimes heavy, sometimes light — mere redundant text insufficient to instruct later ages. The laws of Gaozu and Taizong were only barely preserved. Therefore from Suzong onward, little is worth recording; from Emperor Yizong onward, there is nothing to praise.
