陳三李曹劉
Chen, the three Lis, Cao, and Liu
2
◎陳夷行李紳李讓夷曹確劉瞻 （助） 李蔚
◎ Chen Yixing, Li Shen, Li Rangyi, Cao Que, and Liu Zhan (Liu Zhu) Li Wei
3
陳夷行，字周道，其先江左諸陳也，世客潁川。 由進士第，擢累起居郎、史館修撰。 以勞遷司封員外郎，凡再歲，以吏部郎中為翰林學士。 莊恪太子在東宮，夷行兼侍讀，五日一謁，為太子講說。 數遷至工部侍郎。
Chen Yixing, courtesy name Zhoudao, was descended from the Chen clans of the lower Yangtze; his family had long resided as guests in Yingchuan. Having passed the jinshi examination, he rose through successive appointments as Attendant of the Heir Apparent and Compiler in the Historiography Institute. For his service he was moved to Vice Director of the Bureau of Enfeoffments; after two years he entered the Hanlin Academy as a Bachelor while serving as Director of Personnel in the Ministry of Civil Office. While the Zhuangke Heir Apparent resided in the Eastern Palace, Yixing also served as Lecturer, attending every five days to instruct the crown prince. He was promoted in stages until he reached Vice Minister of Works.
4
開成二年，進同中書門下平章事。 而楊嗣復、李玨相次輔政，夷行介特，雅不與合，每議論天子前，往往語相侵短。 夷行不能堪，輒引疾求去，文宗遣使者尉勞起之。 會以王彥威為忠武節度使，史孝章領邠寧，議皆出嗣復。 及夷行對延英，帝問：「除二鎮當否？ 」對曰：「茍自聖擇，無不當者。 」嗣復曰：「若用人盡出上意而當，固善。 如小不稱，下安得嘿然？ 」夷行曰：「比奸臣數幹權，願陛下無倒持大阿，以鐏授人。 」嗣復曰：「古者任則不疑，齊桓公器管仲於讎虜，豈有倒持慮邪？ 」帝以其面相觸，頗不悅。 仙韶樂工尉遲璋授王府率，右拾遺竇洵直當衙論奏，鄭覃、嗣復嫌以細故，謂洵直近名。 夷行曰：「諫官當衙，正須論宰相得失，彼賤工安足言者？ 然亦不可置不用。 」帝即徙璋光州長史，以百縑賜洵直。 進門下侍郎。
In 837 he was appointed Associate Grand Councillor of the Secretariat-Chancellery. Yang Sifu and Li Jue were then serving in turn as chief ministers. Yixing was stiff and uncompromising by nature and had little use for them; whenever they debated policy before the emperor their exchanges often turned into personal attacks. Unable to endure this, Yixing repeatedly pleaded illness to resign, whereupon Emperor Wenzong sent envoys to console him and recall him to office. About this time Wang Yanwei was appointed Military Commissioner of Zhongwu and Shi Xiaozhang was put in charge of Binning—decisions that had all originated with Sifu. When Yixing appeared for audience in the Yanying Hall, the emperor asked, "Were these two frontier appointments appropriate? " He answered, "If Your Majesty chose them yourself, they cannot fail to be right. " Sifu said, "If every appointment came straight from Your Majesty's will and proved correct, that would be fine. But if they are even slightly unsuitable, how can your ministers remain silent? " Yixing said, "Treacherous ministers have repeatedly seized power of late. I beg Your Majesty not to hold the great axe by the blade and hand the haft to someone else. " Sifu replied, "In antiquity to appoint was to trust without doubt. Duke Huan of Qi prized Guan Zhong even though he had been a captive of his enemies—why should there be any fear of handing over authority? " The emperor, seeing them clash face to face, was deeply displeased. A musician of the Xianzhao Office, Yuchi Zhang, was appointed Commandant of the Princely Household. Remonstrance Official Dou Xunzhi memorialized at court against the appointment. Zheng Tan and Sifu dismissed it as trivial and accused Xunzhi of seeking renown. Yixing said, "It is precisely a remonstrance official's duty at court to discuss the chancellors' merits and faults. What business has a lowly artisan here? Yet he should not be ignored altogether. " The emperor immediately transferred Zhang to the post of Administrator of Guang Prefecture and rewarded Xunzhi with a hundred bolts of silk. He was promoted to Vice Minister of the Chancellery.
5
帝常怪天寶政事不善，問：「姚元崇、宋璟於時在否？ 」李玨曰：「姚亡而宋罷。 」玨因推言：「玄宗自謂未嘗殺一不辜，而任李林甫，種夷數十族，不亦惑乎？ 」夷行曰：「陛下今亦宜戒以權屬人。 」嗣復曰：「夷行失言，太宗易暴亂為仁義，用房玄齡十有六年，任魏徵十有五年，未嘗失道。 人主用忠良久益治，用邪佞一日多矣。 」時用郭薳為坊州刺史，右拾遺宋元阝論不可，果坐贓敗。 帝欲賞邧，夷行曰：「諫官論事是其職，若一事善輒進官，恐後不免有私。 」夷行蓋專詆嗣復。 又素善覃，陰助其力，以排折朋黨。 是時，雖天子亦惡其太過，恩禮遂衰，罷為吏部尚書，尋拜華州刺史。
The emperor often lamented that government under the Tianbao era had gone wrong and asked, "Were Yao Chong and Song Jing still in office then? " Li Jue said, "Yao was dead and Song had already been removed. " Jue went on, "Emperor Xuanzong claimed he had never put an innocent man to death, yet he entrusted Li Linfu, who wiped out dozens of clans root and branch—is that not delusion? " Yixing said, "Your Majesty today should likewise take warning against handing power to others. " Sifu said, "Yixing has spoken wrongly. Taizong turned violence and chaos into benevolent rule; he kept Fang Xuanling for sixteen years and Wei Zheng for fifteen, and never strayed from the right path. When a ruler keeps loyal men long, government grows steadier day by day; employ the wicked and fawning for even a day and harm multiplies. " At that time Guo Shen was appointed Prefect of Fang Prefecture. Remonstrance Official Song Kuang argued against it, and Guo was indeed later ruined by a corruption conviction. The emperor wished to reward Kuang. Yixing said, "It is a remonstrance official's duty to speak on policy. If every sound memorial earns a promotion, later remonstrators will hardly avoid self-interest. " In truth Yixing was chiefly aiming his barbs at Sifu. He had long been friendly with Zheng Tan as well and secretly bolstered him in order to break the power of the factional cliques. By then even the emperor had come to dislike his excesses. Imperial favor waned, and he was removed to the post of Minister of Civil Office, then soon made Governor of Hua Prefecture.
6
武宗即位，召為御史大夫，俄還門下侍郎平章事，進位尚書左仆射。 夷行與崔珙俱拜，乃奏：「仆射始視事，受四品官拜，無著令。 比日左右丞、吏部侍郎、御史中丞皆為仆射拜階下，謂之『隔品致敬』。 準禮，皇太子見上臺群官，群官先拜而後答，以無二上也。 仆射與四品官並列朝廷，不容獨優。 前日鄭餘慶著《仆射上儀》，謂隔品官無亢禮。 時竇易直任御史中丞，議不可。 及易直自為仆射，乃忘前議，當時鄙厭之。 臣等不願以失禮速誚於時。 且開元元年，以左右仆射為左右丞相，位次三公，三公上日答拜，而仆射受之，非是。 望敕所司約《三公上儀》，著定令。 」詔可。 始，累朝紛議不決，至夷行遂定。 以足疾乞身，罷為太子太保，以檢校司空為河中節度使，卒。
When Emperor Wuzong ascended the throne, Yixing was recalled as Censor-in-Chief, soon restored as Vice Minister of the Chancellery and Grand Councillor, and promoted to Left Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs. When Yixing and Cui Gong took office together they submitted a memorial: "When a Vice Director first assumes his duties and receives bows from fourth-rank officials, there is no statute on the books for it. Lately the Left and Right Assistant Directors, the Vice Ministers of Civil Office, and the Vice Censor-in-Chief have all bowed to the Vice Director at the foot of the steps—what is called 'homage across ranks.' By ritual, when the crown prince receives officials of the upper terrace, the officials bow first and he responds afterward, for there cannot be two superiors. A Vice Director stands in court on equal footing with fourth-rank officials; he cannot be granted privilege alone. Earlier Zheng Yuqing had written Ceremonial for Vice Directors at Court, holding that officials of different ranks owed no deferential rite to one another. At that time Dou Yizhi was serving as Vice Censor-in-Chief and argued that this was improper. But when Yizhi himself became Vice Director he forgot his earlier position, and contemporaries despised him for it. We do not wish to invite swift reproach for breach of ritual. Moreover, in 713 the Left and Right Vice Directors were renamed Left and Right Chancellors and ranked just below the Three Dukes. On the Three Dukes' court days they return bows, yet Vice Directors receive them—that is wrong. We ask that Your Majesty order the responsible offices to follow Ceremonial for the Three Dukes at Court and codify the rule. " The edict approved their request. For generations successive courts had debated the issue without resolution; Yixing finally settled it. He pleaded illness in his feet and asked to retire, was relieved as Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent, and served as Acting Minister of Works and Military Commissioner of Hedong until his death.
7
李紳，字公垂，中書令敬玄曾孫。 世宦南方，客潤州。 紳六歲而孤，哀等成人。 母盧，躬授之學。 為人短小精悍，於詩最有名，時號「短李」。 蘇州刺史韋夏卿數稱之。 葬母，有烏銜芝墜輔車。
Li Shen, courtesy name Gongchui, was the great-grandson of Grand Councillor Li Jingxuan. His family had served in office in the south for generations and lived as guests in Run Prefecture. Shen was orphaned at six and mourned with the gravity of a grown man. His mother, née Lu, taught him herself. He was short in stature but keen and forceful, and was most renowned for his poetry; contemporaries called him 'Short Li.' Wei Xiaqing, Prefect of Suzhou, often praised him. At his mother's burial a crow carrying a fungus in its beak dropped it onto the funeral carriage.
8
元和初，擢進士第，補國子助教，不樂，輒去。 客金陵，李锜愛其才，辟掌書記。 锜浸不法，賓客莫敢言，紳數諫，不入； 欲去，不許。 會使者召锜，稱疾，留後王淡為具行，锜怒，陰教士臠食之，即脅使者為眾奏天子，幸得留。 锜召紳作疏，坐锜前，紳陽怖栗，至不能為字，下筆輒塗去，盡數紙。 锜怒罵曰：「何敢爾，不憚死邪？ 」對曰：「生未嘗見金革，今得死為幸。 」即註以刃，令易紙，復然。 或言許縱能軍中書，紳不足用。 召縱至，操書如所欲，即囚紳獄中，锜誅，乃免。 或欲以聞，謝曰：「本激於義，非市名也。 」乃止。
Early in the Yuanhe era he passed the jinshi examination and was appointed an assistant in the Directorate of Education, but he was unhappy in the post and soon resigned. While living in Jinling he was admired by Li Qi, who engaged him as Chief Secretary. Qi grew increasingly lawless. None of his retainers dared speak up, though Shen remonstrated repeatedly to no effect; when he tried to leave, Qi would not let him go. When imperial envoys summoned Qi to court, he pleaded illness and had his deputy Wang Dan prepare to go in his stead. Qi, enraged, secretly ordered soldiers to hack Dan apart and devour him, then forced the envoy before the assembled troops to memorialize the throne on his behalf—and by luck was allowed to stay. Qi ordered Shen to draft the memorial and seated him before himself. Shen feigned such terror that he could not form a single character; every stroke he made he smeared away until he had ruined sheet after sheet. Qi raged and cursed, "How dare you behave like this—are you not afraid to die? " He replied, "I have never known war in my life; to die now would be my good fortune. " Qi had a blade pressed to him and ordered fresh paper, but Shen did the same again. Someone said Xu Zong could handle military documents and that Shen was useless. Zong was summoned and wrote the memorial as Qi wished. Shen was thrown into prison and not released until Qi was executed. Some wished to report his conduct to the court, but he declined, saying, "I acted from duty, not to buy a reputation. " And so they desisted.
9
久之，從辟山南觀察府。 穆宗召為右拾遺、翰林學士，與李德裕、元稹同時，號「三俊」。 累擢中書舍人。 稹為宰相，而李逢吉教人告於方事，稹遂罷； 欲引牛僧孺，懼紳等在禁近沮解，乃授德裕浙西觀察使。 僧孺輔政，以紳為御史中丞，顧其氣剛卞，易疵累，而韓愈勁直，乃以愈為京兆尹，兼御史大夫，免臺參以激紳。 紳、愈果不相下，更持臺府故事，論詰往反，詆訐紛然，繇是皆罷之，以紳為江西觀察使。 帝素厚遇紳，遣使者就第勞賜，以為樂外遷，紳泣言為逢吉中傷。 入謝，又自陳所以然，帝悟，改戶部侍郎。 逢吉終欲陷之。 紳族子虞，有文學名，隱居華陽，自言不願仕，時來省紳，雅與柏耆、程昔範善。 及耆為拾遺，虞以書求薦，紳惡其無立操，痛誚之。 虞失望，後至京師，悉暴紳所言於逢吉。 逢吉滋怒，乃用張又新、李續等計，擢虞、昔範與劉棲楚皆為拾遺，以伺紳隙，內結中人王守澄自助。 會敬宗立，逢吉知紳失勢可乘，使守澄從容奏言：「先帝始議立太子，杜元穎、李紳勸立深王，獨宰相逢吉請立陛下，而李續、李虞助之。 」逢吉乘間言紳嘗不利於陛下，請逐之。 帝初即位，不能辨，乃貶紳為端州司馬。 棲楚等怒得善地，皆切齒。 詔下，百官賀逢吉，唯右拾遺吳思不往，逢吉斥思，令告大行喪於吐蕃。 此時，人無敢言者，惟韋處厚屢言紳枉，折逢吉之奸。 後天子於禁中得先帝手緘書一笥，發之，見裴度、元穎、紳三疏請立帝為嗣，始大感悟，悉焚逢吉黨所上謗書。
After some time he entered the staff of the Shannan Observation Commission. Emperor Muzong summoned him as Right Remonstrance Official and Hanlin Academician. He served alongside Li Deyu and Yuan Zhen and was known as one of the 'Three Outstanding Men.' He rose in stages to Secretariat Drafter. When Yuan Zhen became chancellor, Li Fengji had someone lodge charges against him in the Fang affair, and Zhen was removed; Fengji wished to bring in Niu Sengru but feared that Shen and others near the throne would block the move, so he sent Li Deyu out as Military Commissioner of Zhexi. When Sengru took power he appointed Shen Vice Censor-in-Chief, reckoning his temper stiff and abrasive and easy to trip up. Han Yu, however, was blunt and upright, so Sengru made Yu Metropolitan Governor of Jingzhao and concurrent Censor-in-Chief, exempting him from Censorate attendance in order to provoke Shen. Shen and Yu would not yield to each other. They traded on Censorate precedent, arguing back and forth until mutual slander filled the court. Both were dismissed, and Shen was sent out as Military Commissioner of Jiangxi. The emperor had always favored Shen and sent envoys to his home with gifts and words of comfort, assuming he welcomed an outside posting. Shen wept and said he had been struck down by Fengji's machinations. When he came to give thanks he explained the whole affair again, and the emperor understood; Shen was reassigned as Vice Minister of Revenue. Fengji still meant to destroy him in the end. Shen's clansman Li Yu had a literary reputation and lived in seclusion on Mount Hua, claiming he had no wish for office, though he visited Shen from time to time and was close to Bai Qi and Cheng Xifan. When Bai Qi became a remonstrance official, Yu wrote asking Shen to recommend him. Shen despised his lack of principle and rebuked him harshly. Disappointed, Yu later came to the capital and repeated everything Shen had said to Fengji. Fengji grew angrier still and, following a plan of Zhang Youxin and Li Xu, promoted Yu, Xifan, and Liu Qichu as remonstrance officials to watch for openings against Shen, while secretly allying with the eunuch Wang Shoucheng. When Emperor Jingzong ascended the throne, Fengji saw that Shen had lost power and could be struck. He had Shoucheng calmly report to the throne, "When the late emperor first debated the succession, Du Yuanying and Li Shen urged naming the Prince of Shen. Only Chancellor Fengji asked that Your Majesty be named heir, with Li Xu and Li Yu supporting him. " Seizing the moment, Fengji added that Shen had once been hostile to the emperor and asked that he be driven away. The new emperor could not tell truth from slander and demoted Shen to Administrator of Duan Prefecture. Qichu and his allies, furious that Shen had landed in so mild a place, gnashed their teeth. When the edict was issued the officials congratulated Fengji—only Right Remonstrance Official Wu Si refused to attend. Fengji banished Si, ordering him to announce the grand mourning in Tibet. At that time no one dared speak up except Wei Chuhou, who repeatedly declared Shen's innocence and exposed Fengji's treachery. Later, in the inner palace, the emperor found a case of his father's sealed writings. Opening it, he discovered three memorials by Pei Du, Yuan Ying, and Shen asking that he be named heir. He was deeply moved and burned every slanderous document Fengji's faction had submitted.
10
始，紳南逐，歷封、康間，湍瀨險澀，惟乘漲流乃濟。 康州有媼龍祠，舊傳能致雲雨，紳以書禱，俄而大漲。 寶歷赦令不言左降官與量移，處厚執爭，詔為追定，得徙江州長史，遷滁、壽二州刺史。 霍山多虎，擷茶者病之，治機阱，發民跡射，不能止。 紳至，盡去之，虎不為暴。 以太子賓客分司東都。 太和中，李德裕當國，擢紳浙東觀察使。 李宗閔方得君，復以太子賓客分司。 開成初，鄭覃以紳為河南尹。 河南多惡少，或危帽散衣，擊大毯，屍官道，車馬不敢前。 紳治剛嚴，皆望風遁去。 遷宣武節度使。 大旱，蝗不入境。
When Shen was first banished south he traveled between Feng and Kang, where rapids and shoals were treacherous and could be crossed only when the current ran high. Kang Prefecture had a shrine to the Dragon Mother, said since old times to summon rain. Shen prayed there in writing, and soon the river swelled greatly. The Baoli amnesty did not mention demoted officials eligible for transfer. Chuhou argued the point firmly until an edict was issued to correct the omission, allowing Shen to move to Administrator of Jiang Prefecture and later to serve as Prefect of Chu and Shou. Mount Huo teemed with tigers that plagued the tea pickers. Traps were set and locals organized to track and shoot them, yet the attacks did not cease. When Shen arrived he had all the traps removed, and the tigers ceased their depredations. He was appointed Honored Guest of the Heir Apparent with nominal duty at the Eastern Capital. During the Taihe era, when Li Deyu held power, Shen was promoted to Military Commissioner of Zhedong. When Li Zongmin gained the emperor's ear, Shen was again reduced to Honored Guest of the Heir Apparent with nominal duty. Early in the Kaicheng era Zheng Tan appointed him Metropolitan Governor of Henan. Henan teemed with hoodlums. Some wore tall hats and disheveled dress, beating large balls and sprawling across the highway until traffic dared not pass. Shen governed with stern rigor, and they all fled at the mere report of him. He was made Military Commissioner of Xuanwu. During a severe drought, locusts did not cross into his circuit.
11
武宗即位，徙淮南，召拜中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事，進尚書右仆射、門下侍郎，封趙郡公。 居位四年，以足緩不任朝謁，辭位，以檢校右仆射平章事，復節度淮南。 卒，贈太尉，謚文肅。
When Wuzong ascended the throne, Shen was transferred to Huainan, then recalled as Vice Minister of the Secretariat and Associate Grand Councillor, promoted to Right Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs and Vice Minister of the Chancellery, and enfeoffed Duke of Zhao. He held office four years, then pleaded that his feet were too weak for court attendance, resigned the chief post, and served as Acting Right Vice Director and Grand Councillor while returning to command Huainan. He died and was posthumously honored as Grand Marshal with the posthumous title Wensu.
12
始，灃人吳汝納者，韶州刺史武陵兄子也。 武陵坐贓貶潘州司戶參軍死，汝納家被逐，久不調。 時李吉甫任宰相，汝納怨之，後遂附宗閔黨中。 會昌時，為永寧尉，弟湘為江都尉。 部人訟湘受贓狼籍，身娶民顏悅女。 紳使觀察判官魏铏鞫湘，罪明白，論報殺之。 時，議者謂吳氏世與宰相有嫌，疑紳內顧望，織成其罪。 諫官屢論列，詔遣御史崔元藻覆按，元藻言湘盜用程糧錢有狀，娶部人女不實，按悅嘗為青州衙推，而妻王故衣冠女，不應坐。 德裕惡元藻持兩端，奏貶崖州司戶參軍。 宣宗立，德裕去位，紳已卒。 崔鉉等久不得誌，導汝納使為湘訟，言：「湘素直，為人誣蔑，大校重牢，五木被體，吏至以娶妻資媵結贓。 」且言：「顏悅故士族，湘罪皆不當死，紳枉殺之。 」又言：「湘死，紳令即瘞，不得歸葬。 按紳以舊宰相鎮一方，恣威權。 凡戮有罪，猶待秋分； 湘無辜，盛夏被殺。 」崔元藻銜德裕斥己，即翻其辭，因言：「御史覆獄還，皆對天子別白是非，德裕權軋天下，使不得對，具獄不付有司，但用紳奏而寘湘死。 」是時，德裕已失權，而宗閔故黨令狐、崔鉉、白敏中皆當路，因是逞憾，以利誘動元藻等，使三司結紳杖鉞作藩，虐殺良平，準神龍詔書，酷吏歿者官爵皆奪，子孫不得進宦，紳雖亡，請從《春秋》戮死者之比。 詔削紳三官，子孫不得仕。 貶德裕等，擢汝納左拾遺，元藻武功令。
At the outset there was Wu Runa of Feng, nephew of Wu Ling, Prefect of Shao. Wu Ling was convicted of corruption, demoted to army administrator of Pan Prefecture, and died. Runa's family was expelled and he long remained without office. When Li Jifu was chancellor, Runa bore a grudge against him and later joined Li Zongmin's faction. During Huichang he served as commandant of Yongning; his younger brother Wu Xiang was commandant of Jiangdu. Locals accused Xiang of rampant corruption and of taking a commoner's daughter, Yan Yue's daughter, as his wife. Shen had the observation commission's judge Wei Tong try Xiang. The guilt was clear and Xiang was sentenced to death. Commentators said the Wu family had long feuded with chancellors and suspected Shen of partiality in fabricating the case. Remonstrance officials repeatedly protested until an edict sent Censor Cui Yuanyao to reinvestigate. Yuanyao reported that Xiang had indeed embezzled transport funds, that the charge of marrying a local woman was false, and that Yan Yue had been a Qingzhou yamen aide while his wife Wang came from an old gentry family and should not be punished. Deyu hated Yuanyao's wavering and had him demoted to army administrator of Ya Prefecture. When Xuanzong ascended the throne, Deyu fell from power and Shen was already dead. Cui Xuan and others, long thwarted in their designs, coached Runa to sue for Xiang, claiming Xiang was upright, had been slandered, was shackled in a major prison and subjected to the five punishments, while officials fabricated bribery from his bride-price and dowry. " They added that Yan Yue was of an old gentry house, that none of Xiang's offenses warranted death, and that Shen had killed him unjustly. " They also said that when Xiang died Shen ordered immediate burial and would not permit his body to be returned home. They charged that Shen, a former chancellor ruling a circuit, had wielded power willfully. Even when executing the guilty he still waited for the autumn equinox; yet Xiang, though innocent, was killed in midsummer. " Cui Yuanyao, nursing resentment at Deyu's dismissal, reversed his testimony and said censors returning from reinvestigation should explain verdicts to the throne, but Deyu's power had crushed the realm, prevented such audiences, withheld the full dossier from the courts, and relied solely on Shen's memorial to kill Xiang. " Deyu had already fallen, but Zongmin's old allies Linghu, Cui Xuan, and Bai Minzhong held power. Venting old rancor, they bribed Yuanyao and others until the Three Offices ruled that Shen, as a regional commander, had cruelly killed the innocent. Citing the Shenlong edict on dead cruel officials, they stripped three ranks from Shen and barred his descendants from office, invoking the Spring and Autumn precedent for punishing the dead. An edict stripped three ranks from Shen and barred his descendants from office. Deyu and others were demoted, Runa was promoted to Left Remonstrance Official, and Yuanyao was made magistrate of Wugong.

始，紳以文藝節操見用，而屢為怨仇所拫卻，卒能自伸其才，以名位終。 所至務為威烈，或陷暴刻，故雖沒而坐湘冤雲。
Shen had risen on literary talent and integrity, was repeatedly struck down by enemies, yet in the end extended his talents and finished in high office. Wherever he served he pursued stern authority and sometimes harsh severity; hence even after death the injustice of Xiang's case lingered.
14
李讓夷，字達心，系本隴西。 擢進士第，辟鎮國李絳府判官。 又從西川杜元穎幕府。 與宋申錫善，申錫為翰林學士，薦讓夷右拾遺，俄召拜學士。 素善薛廷老，廷老不飭細檢，數飲酒不治職，罷去，坐是亦奪職。 累進諫議大夫。
Li Rangyi, courtesy name Daxin, was of Longxi descent. He passed the jinshi examination and entered Li Jiang's Zhenguo headquarters as a judge. He later served on Du Yuanying's staff in western Sichuan. He was close to Song Shenxi. When Shenxi entered the Hanlin Academy he recommended Rangyi as Right Remonstrance Official, and Rangyi was soon summoned as an academician. He was also close to Xue Tinglao. Tinglao was careless and often drunk on duty; when Tinglao was dismissed Rangyi lost his post as well. He rose to Remonstrance Grandee.
15

Early in Kaicheng, when Attendant of the Heir Apparent Li Bao was removed, Wenzong asked Li Shi, "Chu Suiliang once combined remonstrance grandee with heir-apparent attendant—who holds that remonstrance post now? Name someone fit for it." " Shi named Feng Ding, Sun Jian, Xiao Ju, and Li Rangyi. The emperor said, "Rangyi will do." " Li Guyuan urged Cui Qiu and Zhang Cizong. " Zheng Tan said, "Qiu was once close to Li Zongmin, and the court recorder's brush below the red steps becomes a model for posterity—a partisan must not hold it. As for Pei Zhongru or Li Rangyi, I have nothing to say against them." " Rangyi was appointed and promoted to Secretariat Drafter. Thereafter Li Jue and Yang Sifu, resenting Tan's recommendation, blocked his advancement for the rest of Wenzong's reign.

武宗初，李德裕復入，三遷至尚書右丞，拜中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事。 潞州平，檢校尚書右仆射。 宣宗立，進司空、門下侍郎，為大行山陵使。 未復土，拜淮南節度使。 以疾願還，卒於道，贈司徒。 讓夷廉介不妄交，位雖顯劇，以儉約自將，為世咨美。
Early under Wuzong, Li Deyu returned to court, rose thrice to Right Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs, and became Vice Minister of the Secretariat and Associate Grand Councillor. When Luzhou was pacified he was made Acting Right Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs. When Xuanzong ascended the throne he was promoted to Minister of Works and Vice Minister of the Chancellery and made commissioner for the late emperor's tomb. Before the mourning earth was restored he was appointed Military Commissioner of Huainan. He pleaded illness to return, died on the road, and was posthumously made Minister of Education. Rangyi was incorruptible and cautious in friendship. Though his posts were weighty, he lived frugally and was praised by his contemporaries.
17
曹確，字剛中，河南河南人。 擢進士第，歷踐中外官，累拜兵部侍郎。 懿宗咸通中，以本官同中書門下平章事，俄進中書侍郎。
Cao Que, courtesy name Gangzhong, was from Henan in the Henan region. He passed the jinshi examination, served in central and local posts, and rose to Vice Minister of War. Under Yizong in the Xiantong era he became Associate Grand Councillor in his existing post and soon Vice Minister of the Secretariat.
18
確邃儒術，器識方重，動循法度。 時帝薄於德，昵寵優人李可及。 可及者，能新聲，自度曲，辭調悽折，京師俞薄少年爭慕之，號為「拍彈」。 同昌公主喪畢，帝與郭淑妃悼念不已，可及為帝造曲，曰《嘆百年》，教舞者數百，皆珠翠襐飾，刻畫魚龍地衣，度用繒五千，倚曲作辭，哀思裴回，聞者皆涕下。 舞闋，珠寶覆地，帝以為天下之至悲，愈寵之。 家嘗娶婦，帝曰：「第去，吾當賜酒。 」俄而使者負二銀珰與之，皆珠珍也。 可及憑恩橫甚，人無敢斥，遂擢為威衛將軍。 確曰：「太宗著令，文武官六百四十三，謂房玄齡曰：『朕設此待天下賢士。 工商雜流，假使技出等夷，正當厚給以財，不可假以官，與賢者比肩立、同坐食也。 』文宗欲以樂工尉遲璋為王府率，拾遺竇洵直固爭，卒授光州長史。 今而位將軍，不可。 」帝不聽。 至僖宗立，始貶死。 方幸時，惟確屢言之。 而神策中尉西門季玄者，亦剛鯁，謂可及曰：「汝以巧佞惑天子，當族滅！ 」嘗見其受賜，謂曰：「今載以官車，後籍沒亦當爾。」
Que was steeped in Confucian learning, upright in judgment, and measured in conduct. The emperor was weak in virtue and doted on the actor Li Keji. Keji invented new music, composed his own tunes with plaintive lyrics, and frivolous youths of the capital admired him under the name 'patting and strumming.' After Princess Tongchang's mourning ended the emperor and Consort Guo still grieved. Keji composed 'Lament for a Century' for him, trained hundreds of dancers in pearls and kingfisher trim on carpets carved with fish and dragons, using five thousand bolts of silk; the lyrics were so mournful that listeners wept. When the dance ended jewels carpeted the ground. The emperor called it the world's supreme sorrow and favored Keji all the more. When his household took a bride the emperor said, "Go home—I shall send wine." " Envoys soon arrived bearing two silver pendants filled with pearls and gems. Keji grew insolent on imperial favor; none dared reprove him, and he was made a general of the imperial guard. Que said, "Taizong fixed six hundred forty-three civil and military posts and told Fang Xuanling, 'I established these to await worthy men under heaven. Merchants and artisans, even if skilled beyond their peers, should receive wealth, not office to stand and dine beside the worthy." Wenzong wished to make the musician Yuchi Zhang commandant of the princely household; Remonstrance Official Dou Xunzhi protested until Yuchi was made administrator of Guang Prefecture. To make Keji a general now is impermissible." The emperor would not listen. Only when Xizong ascended the throne was Keji demoted and died. While favor lasted, only Que spoke against it repeatedly. Shence commandant Ximen Jixuan was likewise stern and told Keji, "You bewitch the Son of Heaven with clever flattery—your clan should be exterminated!" Seeing him receive gifts, Jixuan said, "Today it rides on an official cart; when your property is confiscated it will be the same."

After six years Que became Right Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs, left court as grand councillor to command Zhenhai, was transferred to Hedong, and died. Earlier Bi Xian and Que had served together as chancellors with fine reputations; people called them 'Cao and Bi.' His younger brother Fen came to court as Vice Minister of Revenue and finance commissioner from Military Commissioner of Zhongwu and died.

劉瞻，字幾之，其先出彭城，後徙桂陽。 舉進士、博學宏詞，皆中。 徐商辟署鹽鐵府，累遷太常博士。 劉彖執政，薦為翰林學士，拜中書舍人，進承旨。 出為河東節度使。
Liu Zhan, courtesy name Jizhi, was descended from Pengcheng and later moved to Guiyang. He passed both the jinshi and the erudite examinations in expansive learning. Xu Shang recruited him to the Salt and Iron Commission; he rose to Erudite of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. When Liu Zhan held power he recommended Zhan as Hanlin academician, made him Secretariat Drafter, and advanced him to Chief Drafter. He was sent out as Military Commissioner of Hedong.

In 870 he became Vice Minister of the Secretariat and Associate Grand Councillor. When Princess Tongchang died Yizong arrested imperial physician Han Shaozong and others, sent them to the edict prison, and seized hundreds of their clansmen. Zhan urged the remonstrance officials, but all hedged. He then memorialized firmly, "Shaozong exhausted his arts without success—he deserves pity. Your Majesty indulges love for a daughter, imprisons commoners, vents rage heedlessly, and invites slander for arbitrary cruelty." The emperor was furious and dismissed him the same day, appointing him Acting Minister of Punishments, Grand Councillor, and Military Commissioner of Jingnan. Lu Yan and Wei Baoheng whispered slanders to the emperor, and soon Zhan was demoted to Prefect of Lian. Hanlin Academician Zheng Tian was punished because the reprimand edict was too mild. Vice Censor-in-Chief Sun Huang, Remonstrance Grandee Gao Xiang, and others were demoted to Lingnan for friendship with Zhan. Yan and his allies were still unsatisfied. Seeing on the map that the post lay ten thousand li away, they demoted Zhan to army administrator of the prefecture, had Li Yu draft a savage edict, and were about to kill him. The realm held that Zhan was upright and had been squeezed out by slander; all deemed it injustice. Zhang Gongsu, Military Commissioner of You, memorialized in his defense; Yan and the others did not dare harm him. When Xizong ascended the throne Zhan was moved to Kang and Guo, recalled as Minister of Punishments, again made Vice Minister of the Secretariat and Grand Councillor, and died after three months.

Zhan was frugal, using surplus salary to aid poor kin and friends and keeping no stores at home. He owned no mansion; gifts never reached his door; his conduct remained pure start to finish.

Li Wei, courtesy name Maoxiu, was of Longxi descent. He passed the jinshi and the distinguished writings examination. He was appointed investigating censor and rose to Right Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs.
25
懿宗惑浮屠，常飯萬僧禁中，自為贊唄。 蔚上疏切諫，引狄仁傑、姚元崇、辛替否所言，譏病時弊。 帝不聽，但以虛禮褒答。 俄拜京兆尹、太常卿。 出為宣武節度使，徙淮南。 代還，民詣闕請留，詔許一歲。 僖宗乾符初，以吏部尚書同中書門下平章事。 罷為東都留守。 河東亂，殺其帥崔季康，用邠寧李侃代之，士不附，以蔚嘗在太原府有惠政，為人所懷，拜河東節度使，同平章事。 至鎮三日，卒。
Yizong was deluded by Buddhism and regularly fed ten thousand monks in the palace while chanting hymns himself. Wei submitted a sharp memorial citing Di Renjie, Yao Chong, and Xin Tifou and ridiculing the age's failings. The emperor would not listen and answered only with empty courtesy. Soon he was made Metropolitan Governor of Jingzhao and Minister of Imperial Sacrifices. He was sent out as Military Commissioner of Xuanwu, then transferred to Huainan. When his replacement arrived the people petitioned the throne to keep him; an edict allowed him one more year. Early in the Qianfu era under Xizong he became Minister of Civil Office and Associate Grand Councillor. He was removed to serve as Defender of the Eastern Capital. Hedong rebelled and killed its commander Cui Jikang. Li Kan of Binning was sent to replace him, but the troops would not accept him. Because Wei had once served in Taiyuan with benevolent rule and was still beloved, he was appointed Military Commissioner of Hedong and Associate Grand Councillor. 始，懿宗成安國祠，賜寶坐二，度高二丈，構以沈檀，塗髹，鏤龍鳳葩蘤，金扣之，上施復坐，陳經幾其前，四隅立瑞鳥神人，高數尺，磴道以升，前被繡囊錦襜，珍麗精絕。 咸通十四年春，詔迎佛骨鳳翔，或言：「昔憲宗嘗為此，俄晏駕。 」帝曰：「使朕生見之，死無恨！ 」乃以金銀為剎，珠玉為帳，孔鷸周飾之，小者尋丈，高至倍，刻檀為檐註，陛墄塗黃金，每一剎，數百人舉之。 香輿前後系道，綴珠瑟瑟幡蓋，殘彩以為幢節，費無貲限。 夏四月，至長安，彩觀夾路，其徒導衛。 天子禦安福樓迎拜，至泣下。 詔賜兩街僧金幣，京師耆老及見元和事者，悉厚賜之。 不逞小人至斷臂指，流血滿道。 所過鄉聚，皆裒土為剎，相望於塗，爭以金翠抆飾。 傳言剎悉震搖，若有光景雲。 京師高貲相與集大衢，作繒臺縵闕，註水銀為池，金玉為樹木，聚桑門羅像，考鼓鳴螺繼日夜。 錦車繡輿，載歌舞從之。 秋七月，帝崩。 方人主甘心篤向，如蔚言者甚多，皆不能救。 僖宗立，詔歸其骨，都人耆耋辭餞，或嗚咽流涕。
Earlier Yizong completed the Chengan State shrine and bestowed two jeweled thrones two zhang high, built of heavy sandalwood, lacquered and carved with dragons, phoenixes, and flowers, trimmed in gold, topped with canopy seats. Sutra tables stood before them; at the four corners rose auspicious birds and divine figures several feet tall, with steps leading up. Embroidered bags and brocade curtains hung before them—splendor without equal. In the spring of 873 an edict ordered the Buddha's relic brought from Fengxiang. Some warned, "When Xianzong did this he soon died. " The emperor said, "If I can see it while I live, I shall die without regret! " He had reliquary towers made of gold and silver, canopies of pearl and jade, trimmed with kingfisher feathers. The smaller towers stood a xun high, the larger twice that. Sandalwood eaves and pillars were carved, steps gilded, and each tower required hundreds of men to carry it. Incense chariots lined the road for miles, hung with pearl and jadeite banners and canopies, pennants fashioned from leftover brocades—cost beyond counting. In the fourth month it reached Chang'an. Colored pavilions lined the road while monks guided the procession. The emperor received it at Anfu Tower, bowing until he wept. An edict rewarded monks of both precincts with gold and cash, and richly compensated every elder in the capital who had lived through the Yuanhe era. Desperate men even cut off their own arms and fingers until blood filled the streets. Every village along the route piled earth into reliquary towers that faced one another along the road, each competing to adorn them with gold and kingfisher trim. Rumor held that the towers trembled and glowed as if wreathed in light and cloud. Wealthy families of the capital gathered on the main avenues, erecting brocade platforms and curtained towers, pouring mercury into pools, fashioning trees of gold and jade, assembling monks and images, beating drums and sounding conchs day and night without cease. Brocade carts and embroidered palanquins followed with singers and dancers. In the seventh month the emperor died. Just as the ruler had willingly thrown himself into devotion, many who spoke as Wei had could not save him. When Xizong ascended the throne an edict sent the relic back. The aged of the capital came to bid it farewell, many weeping aloud.

贊曰：人之惑怪神也，甚哉！ 若佛者，特西域一槁人耳。 裸顛露足，以乞食自資，臒辱其身，屏營山樊，行一概之苦，本無求於人，徒屬稍稍從之。 然其言荒茫漫靡，夷幻變現，善推不驗無實之事，以鬼神死生貫為一條，據之不疑。 掊嗜欲，棄親屬，大抵與黃老相出入。 至漢十四葉，書入中國。 跡夫生人之情，以耳目不際為奇，以不可知為神，以物理之外為畏，以變化無方為聖，以生而死、死復生、回復償報、歆艷其間為或然，以賤近貴遠為。 鞮譯差殊，不可研詰。 華人之譎誕者，又攘莊周、列禦寇之說佐其高，層累架騰，直出其表，以無上不可加為勝，妄相誇脅而倡其風。 於是，自天子逮庶人，皆震動而祠奉之。
The commentator says: How deeply people are deluded by the supernatural! The Buddha was nothing more than a gaunt man from the Western Regions. Bareheaded, feet exposed, he lived by begging, emaciating and humbling himself in mountain seclusion, practicing uniform austerity without asking anything of others—only slowly did followers gather. Yet his teachings were vast and vague, leveling illusions and transformations, skilled at inferring what cannot be verified, threading ghosts, spirits, death, and life into one chain—and holding it without doubt. He rejected desire and abandoned kin, doctrines that largely overlap with the teachings of the Yellow Emperor and Laozi. Not until the fourteenth generation of Han did the texts enter China. Tracing human nature: what eyes and ears cannot reach seems marvelous; what cannot be known seems divine; what lies beyond physical law seems fearful; unbounded change seems holy; life into death, death into life, cyclic retribution seems plausible; what is near is despised and what is far exalted. Barbarian transliteration varied so widely it could not be rigorously examined. Crafty Chinese literati seized on Zhuang Zhou and Lie Yukou to raise it higher still, piling doctrine upon doctrine until it soared beyond measure, taking 'nothing higher can be added' as triumph, and falsely boasting and bullying one another to spread its fashion. Thereupon, from the Son of Heaven to commoners, all were stirred to worship it.
28
初，宰相王縉以緣業事佐代宗，於是始作內道場，晝夜梵唄，冀禳寇戎，大作盂蘭，肖祖宗像，分供塔廟，為賊臣嘻笑。 至憲宗世，遂迎佛骨於鳳翔，內之宮中。 韓愈指言其弊，帝怒，竄愈瀕死，憲亦弗獲天年。 幸福而禍，無亦左乎！ 懿宗不君，精爽奪迷，復蹈前車而覆之。 興哀無知之場，丐庇百解之胔，以死自誓，無有顧藉，流淚拜伏，雖事宗廟上帝，無以進焉。 屈萬乘之貴，自等於古胡，數千載而遠，以身為徇。 嗚呼，運祚殫，天告之矣！ 懿不三月而徂，唐德之不競，厥有來哉，悲夫！
Early on Chancellor Wang Jin, pursuing karmic rites under Daizong, first established inner cloister chapels with day-and-night chanting to ward off invaders, staged great Ullambana rites, fashioned ancestral images, and distributed offerings to pagodas and temples—much to the amusement of rebel ministers. By Xianzong's reign the Buddha's relic was brought from Fengxiang into the palace. Han Yu pointed out the harm; the emperor in rage exiled him nearly to death, and Xianzong likewise did not live out his years. Fortune turning to calamity—is that not perverse! Yizong was no true ruler; his spirit was seized by delusion, and he repeated the overturned precedent of his predecessors. He raised mourning in ignorance, begged shelter from a relic of a hundred joints, swore himself to death without restraint, wept and prostrated himself—yet could not advance even the rites owed to the ancestral temple and Heaven. He humbled the dignity of the throne, equating himself with barbarians of ages past, and offered his person as sacrifice. Alas—the mandate was spent; Heaven had announced it! Yizong was gone within three months. That Tang virtue could no longer contend had its source here—how lamentable!
