楊戴陽二李韓杜邢
Yang, Dai, Yang, the two Lis, Han, Du, and Xing.
2
楊朝晟
Yang Chaosheng
3
楊朝晟，字叔明，夏州朔方人。 興行間，以先鋒功授甘泉府果毅。 建中初，從李懷光討劉文喜涇州，斬獲多，加驃騎大將軍。 李納寇徐州，從唐朝臣往討，常冠軍。 懷光赴難奉天，屬朝晟兵千人下咸陽，賜實封百五十戶。
Yang Chaosheng, whose courtesy name was Shuming, came from Shuofang in Xia Prefecture. During the Xingxing operations he distinguished himself in the vanguard and was appointed defender of Ganquan-fu. Early in the Jianzhong era he campaigned with Li Huai'guang against Liu Wenxi at Jingzhou, winning heavy slayings and captures, and was promoted to Grand General of the Agile Cavalry. When Li Na raided Xuzhou, he marched with Tang Chaochen to drive him off and repeatedly took first place in the fighting. When Li Huai'guang hurried to the rescue at Fengtian, Chaosheng was left in command of a thousand men below Xianyang and granted an actual fief of one hundred fifty households.
4
懷光反，韓遊瑰退保邠、寧，賊黨張昕守邠州，大索軍實，多募士，欲潛歸之。 朝晟父懷賓為遊瑰將，夜以數十騎斬昕及同謀者。 遊瑰遣懷賓告行在，德宗勞問，授兼御史中丞。 朝晟泣見懷光曰：「父立功於國，子當誅，不可以主兵。 」懷光縶之。 及諸軍圍河中，遊瑰營長春宮，而懷賓戰甚力。 懷光平，帝原朝晟，因為遊瑰都虞候。 父子皆開府、賓客、御史中丞，軍中以為榮。
After Huai'guang rebelled, Han Yougui fell back to defend Bin and Ning, while the rebel partisan Zhang Xin held Binzhou, scoured the armies for supplies, drafted many men, and plotted to defect to Huai'guang in secret. Chaosheng's father Huaiyin served under Yougui as a general; one night he rode out with several dozen horsemen and killed Xin and the other conspirators. Yougui dispatched Huaiyin to report to the imperial camp; Dezong received him warmly and named him concurrent Vice Censor-in-Chief. Chaosheng came before Huai'guang in tears and said, "My father has won merit for the realm, and as his son I deserve execution — I must not be allowed to lead soldiers." Huai'guang had him bound. When the allied armies besieged Hezhong, Yougui pitched camp at Changchun Palace while Huaiyin fought with relentless vigor. After Huai'guang's rebellion was crushed, the emperor pardoned Chaosheng and appointed him chief adjutant under Yougui. Father and son alike held honorary grand-secretary ranks, guest-minister titles, and Vice Censor-in-Chief posts — a distinction the army took as a signal honor.
5
吐蕃犯邊，遊瑰自將守寧州，而禦士寬，軍驕。 及張獻甫來代，軍遂亂，朝晟逃於郊。 眾脅監軍，請以范希朝為節度使。 希朝時已在京師。 明日，朝晟出，紿眾曰：「予來賀所請之當也。 」眾稍定。 朝晟結諸將謀誅首惡者。 居三日，紿遣人自邠來，曰：「前請報罷，張公已舍邠矣，反者皆當死，吾不願盡誅也，第取首惡者。 」眾所讙指，斬二百餘人，獻甫遂入於軍。 帝以希朝為節度副使，而朝晟加御史大夫。
When Tibetans raided the border, Yougui took personal command at Ningzhou, but his discipline was lax and the troops grew insubordinate. When Zhang Xianfu arrived to replace him, the army mutinied, and Chaosheng fled into the countryside. The mutineers seized the army supervisor and demanded that Fan Xichao be made military commissioner. Xichao was already in the capital at the time. The next day Chaosheng appeared and told the troops, "I have come to congratulate you on making the right choice." The men gradually settled down. Chaosheng rallied the other generals to plot the execution of the ringleaders. Three days later he staged a ruse, sending a messenger from Bin who announced, "Your petition has been rejected; Lord Zhang has already left Binzhou. All rebels deserve death, but I do not wish to slaughter everyone — only the ringleaders need die." The men singled out more than two hundred ringleaders for execution, and Xianfu was then able to take command of the army. The emperor appointed Xichao deputy military commissioner and promoted Chaosheng to Censor-in-Chief.
6
貞元九年，城鹽州，發卒護境，朝晟屯木波堡。 會獻甫卒，有詔代為邠寧節度使。 朝晟請城方渠，合道，木波以遏吐蕃路。 詔問：「須兵幾何？ 」報曰：「部兵可辦。 」帝問：「前日城五原，興師七萬，今何易邪？ 」對曰：「鹽州之役，虜先知之。 今薄戎而城，虜料王師不十萬，勢難輕入。 若發部兵，十日至塞下，未三旬城畢，積芻聚糧，留卒守之，寇至不可拔，萊野翦夷，虜且走，此萬全計也。 若大發兵，閱月乃至，虜亦來，來必戰，戰則不暇城矣。 」帝納其策。 師次方渠，水乏。 有青蛇降險下走，視其跡，水從而流，朝晟使築防環之，遂為渟淵，士飲仰足，圖其事以聞。 有詔置祠，命泉曰應聖。 已城，吐蕃悉眾至，度不能害，乃引去。 復城馬嶺而歸，開地三百里。 十七年，卒於屯。
In Zhenyuan 9, when Salt Prefecture was fortified and troops were sent to guard the frontier, Chaosheng encamped at Mubo Fort. When Xianfu died, an edict named Chaosheng military commissioner of Binning in his place. Chaosheng proposed building fortresses at Fangqu, Hedao, and Mubo to choke off the Tibetan advance routes. The throne asked, "How many troops will you need?" He replied, "My own command can handle it." The emperor asked, "When we fortified Wuyuan earlier we mobilized seventy thousand men — why is this so easy now?" He answered, "At Salt Prefecture the enemy learned of our plans in advance." This time we will build close to their territory; they will assume the imperial forces number fewer than a hundred thousand and will not dare to attack lightly. If we send only my command troops, they can reach the frontier in ten days and finish the walls in under thirty. We stockpile fodder and grain, leave garrisons behind, and when the enemy arrives they cannot dislodge us. Once the countryside is cleared the barbarians will withdraw — that is a plan without flaw. If we mobilize a huge army, it takes a month to arrive — by then the enemy comes too, and once they come we must fight; if we are fighting we have no time to build walls. The emperor accepted his plan. When the army reached Fangqu, water ran short. A green serpent was seen slithering down a cliff; following its trail, water began to flow. Chaosheng had a retaining wall built around the spring, forming a deep pool from which the troops drank their fill. He sent a report illustrated with an account of the event. An edict ordered a shrine erected and named the spring Responsive to the Sage. Once the walls were finished, the Tibetans came in full force, saw they could not overcome the position, and withdrew. He fortified Maling as well and returned, opening three hundred li of new territory. In the seventeenth year he died at his post.
7
戴休顏弟戴休璿戴休晏
Dai Xiuyan; his younger brothers Dai Xiuxuan and Dai Xiuyan
8
戴休顏，字休顏，夏州人。 家世尚武，誌膽不常。 郭子儀引為大將，諭平党項羌，以安河曲。 試太常卿，封濟陰郡公，進封咸寧郡王，兼朔方節度副使。 城邠州功最，遷鹽州刺史。 朱泚反，率兵三千晝夜馳，奔問行在，德宗嘉之，賜實戶二百。 與渾瑊、杜希全、韓遊瑰等扞禦有勞。 帝進狩梁、洋，留守奉天。 李懷光屯咸陽，使人誘之，休顏斬其使，勒兵自守。 懷光眙駭，自涇陽夜走。 遷檢校工部尚書、奉天行營節度使。 合渾瑊兵破泚偏師，斬首三千級，追至中渭橋。 京師平，又與瑊率兵趨岐陽，邀泚殘黨。 加檢校尚書右僕射，進戶四百。 從乘輿至京師，賜女樂、甲第，拜左龍武軍統軍。 卒，贈揚州大都督。
Dai Xiuyan, whose courtesy name was Xiuyan, came from Xia Prefecture. His family had long been martial in tradition, and his resolve and daring were out of the ordinary. Guo Ziyi took him on as a senior commander and charged him with pacifying the Tangut Qiang to secure the Hequ frontier. He served provisionally as Grand Master of Splendid Happiness, was created Duke of Jiyin, then advanced to Prince of Xianning and appointed deputy military commissioner of Shuofang. His work on the Binzhou fortifications ranked first, and he was transferred to regional inspector of Salt Prefecture. When Zhu Ci rebelled, he raced day and night with three thousand men to reach the imperial camp. Dezong praised him and granted an actual fief of two hundred households. Together with Hun Jian, Du Xiquan, Han Yougui, and others he distinguished himself in the defense. When the emperor withdrew to Liang and Yang, Xiuyan stayed behind to hold Fengtian. Li Huai'guang, encamped at Xianyang, sent envoys to win him over; Xiuyan executed the messengers and mobilized his troops to hold his ground. Huai'guang was struck with dismay and fled Jingyang by night. He was promoted to acting Minister of Works and military commissioner of the Fengtian field army. Joining Hun Jian's forces he routed a detached column of Zhu Ci's army, taking three thousand heads, and pursued as far as the Middle Wei Bridge. After the capital was recovered, he marched with Jian toward Qiyang to cut off Zhu Ci's remaining followers. He was promoted to acting Right Vice Director of the Imperial Secretariat and his fief was increased by four hundred households. He accompanied the emperor back to the capital, received female musicians and a grand residence, and was named commander of the Left Dragon Martial Army. He died and was posthumously made Grand Protector of Yang Prefecture.
9
弟休璿，歷開府儀同三司，封東陽郡王； 休晏，歷輔國大將軍，封彭城郡公。 俱以將略稱。
His younger brother Xiuxuan rose to Grand Master with Establishment Equal to the Three Excellencies and was created Prince of Dongyang; Xiuyan rose to Grand General Who Assists the State and was created Duke of Pengcheng. Both were renowned for their military ability.
10
陽惠元
Yang Huiyuan
11
陽惠元，平州人。 以趫勇奮，事平盧軍。 從田神功、李忠臣浮海入青州。 詔以兵隸神策，為京西兵馬使，鎮奉天。
Yang Huiyuan came from Ping Prefecture. Known for his swift courage, he served in the Pinglu Army. He followed Tian Shenggong and Li Zhongchen by sea into Qingzhou. An edict transferred his troops to the Shence Army; he was made Capital-West army supervisor and stationed at Fengtian.
12
德宗初立，稍繩諸節度跋扈者。 於是李正己屯曹州，田悅增河上兵，河南大擾。 詔移兵萬二千戍關東，帝御望春樓誓師，因勞遣諸將。 酒至神策，將士不敢飲。 帝問故，惠元曰：「初發奉天，臣之帥張巨濟與眾約：『是役也，不立功，毋飲酒！ 』臣不敢食其言。 」既行，有饋於道，惟惠元軍瓶罍不發。 帝咨嘆不已，璽書慰勞。 俄以兵三千會諸將擊田悅，戰御河，奪三橋，惠元功多。 以兵屬李懷光。
When Dezong first took the throne, he began reining in the overbearing military commissioners. Li Zhengyi then encamped at Caozhou, Tian Yue reinforced his troops along the Yellow River, and Henan fell into turmoil. An edict ordered twelve thousand troops east to garrison Guandong. The emperor reviewed them at Wangchun Tower, took the army oath, and then sent the generals off with words of encouragement. When the wine reached the Shence troops, the officers and men refused to drink. The emperor asked why. Huiyuan said, "When we left Fengtian, my commander Zhang Juji made a pact with the men: 'In this campaign, whoever wins no merit shall not drink wine!'" I dare not break that vow." On the march, gifts of wine were offered along the road, but only Huiyuan's men kept their flasks sealed. The emperor marveled at this repeatedly and sent an imperial letter of praise and consolation. Soon afterward he joined the other generals with three thousand men to attack Tian Yue, fought at the Yu River, captured three bridges, and won the greatest share of the credit. His troops were placed under Li Huai'guang's command.
13
及朱泚反，自河朔赴難，解奉天圍，加檢校工部尚書，攝貝州刺史。 詔惠元與神策行營節度使李晟、鄜坊節度使李建徽及懷光聯營便橋。 晟知懷光且叛，移屯東渭橋。 翰林學士陸贄諫帝曰：「四將接壘，晟等兵寡位下，為懷光所易，勢不兩完。 晟既慮變，請與惠元東徙，則建徽孤立。 宜因晟行，合兩軍皆往，以備賊為解，趣裝進道，則懷光計無所施。 」帝不從，使神策將李升往伺。 還奏：「懷光反明甚。 」是夕，奪二軍，惠元、建徽走奉天，懷光遣將冉宗馳騎追及於好畤。 惠元被髮呼天，血流出眥，袒裼戰而死。 二子晟、暠匿井中，皆及害。 建徽獨免。 詔贈惠元尚書左僕射，晟殿中監，暠邠州刺史。
When Zhu Ci rebelled, he marched from Hebei to the rescue, lifted the siege of Fengtian, was promoted to acting Minister of Works, and named acting regional inspector of Bei Prefecture. An edict ordered Huiyuan to camp jointly with Li Sheng, military commissioner of the Shence field army, Li Jianhui, military commissioner of Zheng and Fang, and Huai'guang at Bian Bridge. Sheng, knowing Huai'guang was on the verge of rebellion, shifted his camp to the Eastern Wei Bridge. Hanlin academician Lu Zhi urged the emperor, "With four generals camped side by side, Sheng and the others have fewer troops and lower rank; Huai'guang can easily overpower them, and both sides cannot survive intact." If Sheng, fearing treachery, moves east with Huiyuan, Jianhui will be left isolated. You should use Sheng's move as the occasion to send both armies east together, citing the need to guard against the rebels, and order them to pack up and march at once — then Huai'guang's plot will come to nothing. The emperor would not heed this advice and sent the Shence general Li Sheng to investigate. On his return he reported, "Huai'guang's rebellion is unmistakable." That night Huai'guang seized both armies. Huiyuan and Jianhui fled toward Fengtian, and Huai'guang sent the general Ran Zong in pursuit; he overtook them at Haozhi. Huiyuan tore loose his hair and cried to heaven, blood streaming from his eyes; stripped to the waist he fought until he was killed. His two sons Sheng and Gao hid in a well but were both killed. Jianhui alone escaped. An edict posthumously made Huiyuan Left Vice Director of the Imperial Secretariat, Sheng palace supervisor, and Gao regional inspector of Bin Prefecture.
14
少子旻，字公素。 惠元之死，被八創，墮別井，或救得免。 歷邢州刺史。 盧從史既縛，潞軍潰，有驍卒五千，從史嘗以子視者，奔於旻，旻閉城不內。 眾皆哭曰：「奴失帥，今公有完城，又度支錢百萬在府，少賜之，為表天子求旌節。 」旻開諭禍福遣之，眾感悟，遂還軍。 憲宗嘉之，遷易州刺史。
His youngest son Min, whose courtesy name was Gongsu, when Huiyuan was killed, suffered eight wounds, fell into another well, and was pulled out alive. He later served as regional inspector of Xing Prefecture. After Lu Congshi was captured, the Lu army collapsed. Five thousand elite troops whom Congshi had treated like sons fled to Min, but Min shut the gates and refused them entry. They all wept and said, "We have lost our commander, but you hold an intact city, and a million strings of revenue funds are stored in the prefecture. Give us a little, and we will petition the throne on your behalf for a military commission." Min opened the gates, explained the consequences to them, and sent them away. Moved by his words, they returned to their units. Xianzong praised him and transferred him to regional inspector of Yi Prefecture.
15
王師討吳元濟，以唐州刺史提兵深入二百里，薄申州，拔外郛，殘其垣。 以功加御史中丞。 容州西原蠻反，授本州經略招討使，擊定之。 進御史大夫，合邕、容兩管為一道。 卒，贈左散騎常侍。
When the imperial army campaigned against Wu Yuanji, he led his Tang Prefecture troops two hundred li into enemy territory, pressed Shenzhou, captured its outer suburbs, and tore down its walls. For this he was promoted to Vice Censor-in-Chief. When the Western Yuan tribes of Rong Prefecture rebelled, he was made military commissioner and pacification commissioner of the prefecture and crushed the uprising. He was promoted to Censor-in-Chief and merged the Yong and Rong circuits into a single command. He died and was posthumously made Left Regular Attendant of the Palace.
16
李元諒
Li Yuanliang
17
李元諒，安息人。 本安氏，少為宦官駱奉先養息，冒姓駱，名元光。 美須髯，鷙敢有謀。 以宿衛積勞試太子詹事。 李懷讓節度鎮國，署奏以自副。 居軍十年，士心憚服。
Li Yuanliang was of Parthian descent. He was originally of the An clan; as a youth the eunuch Luo Fengxian adopted him, and he took the surname Luo and the name Yuanguang. Handsome and full-bearded, he was fierce, daring, and resourceful. Long service in the palace guard earned him appointment as acting Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent. When Li Huairang served as military commissioner of Zhenguo, he named Yuanliang his deputy in a memorial to the throne. He spent ten years with the army, and the troops feared and respected him.
18
德宗出奉天，賊遣將何望之襲華州，於是刺史董晉棄城走。 望之欲聚兵以絕東道，元諒自潼關引兵徑薄其城，拔之。 時兵興倉卒，裹罽為鎧，剡蒿為矢，募兵數日至萬餘，軍氣乃振。 賊來攻，輒卻。 時尚可孤守藍田，元諒屯昭應，王權壁中渭橋，賊兵不能逾渭南。 未幾，遷鎮國軍節度使，封武康郡王。 先是，詔發豳、隴兵東討李希烈。 師方出關，泚使劉忠孝召還； 至華陰，華陰尉李夷簡說驛官捕之，追及關，元諒斬以徇，所召兵不得入，由是華州獨完。 俄詔元諒與李晟收京師，次浐西。 元諒先奮鏖賊，敗之，進屯苑東，晟使壞苑垣入。 泚連戰皆北，遂大潰，京師平。 讓功於晟，退壁近郊。 加檢校尚書左僕射，實封戶五百，賜甲第、女樂、一子六品官。
When Dezong withdrew to Fengtian, the rebels sent the general He Wangzhi to strike Hua Prefecture, and the regional inspector Dong Jin abandoned the city and fled. Wangzhi planned to mass troops and sever the eastern route, but Yuanliang marched from Tong Pass straight against his position and recaptured the city. The army had been raised in haste: felt was wrapped for armor and artemisia stalks carved into arrows. Within days he recruited more than ten thousand men, and the troops' morale revived. Whenever the rebels attacked, he drove them back. Shang Ke'gu held Lantian, Yuanliang encamped at Zhaoying, and Wang Quan fortified the Middle Wei Bridge, so rebel forces could not cross south of the Wei. Soon afterward he was made military commissioner of the Zhenguo Army and created Prince of Wukang. Earlier an edict had ordered Bin and Long troops east to campaign against Li Xilie. The army had just left the pass when Zhu Ci sent Liu Zhongxiao to recall them; but at Huayin the assistant Li Yijian persuaded the post officials to seize the messenger. Yuanliang overtook them at the pass, executed the envoy, and displayed his head, so the recalled troops could not enter. Hua Prefecture alone remained intact. Soon an edict ordered Yuanliang and Li Sheng to retake the capital; they encamped west of Chan. Yuanliang led the fierce assault, routed the rebels, and advanced to camp east of the imperial park while Sheng breached the park wall to enter. Zhu Ci lost battle after battle and finally broke in full rout; the capital was recovered. He yielded the credit to Sheng and withdrew his forces to camp in the suburbs. He was promoted to acting Left Vice Director of the Imperial Secretariat, granted an actual fief of five hundred households, and given a grand residence, female musicians, and a sixth-rank post for one son.
19
李懷光反，與馬燧、渾瑊討之。 其將徐廷光素易元諒，數嫚罵，為優胡戲，斥侮其祖。 又使約降，曰：「我降漢將耳。 」及馬燧至，降於燧。 元諒見韓遊瑰曰：「彼詬吾祖，今日斬之，子助我乎？ 」許諾。 既而遇諸道，即數其罪，叱左右斬之，詣燧謝。 燧大怒，將殺元諒，遊瑰見曰：「殺一偏裨尚爾，即殺一節度，法宜如何？ 」燧默然。 元諒請輸錢百萬勞軍自贖，瑊亦為請，燧赦之。 帝以專殺，恐有司劾治，前詔勿論。
When Li Huai'guang rebelled, he campaigned against him with Ma Sui and Hun Jian. Huai'guang's general Xu Tingguang had long despised Yuanliang, repeatedly insulted him, staged comic performances mocking him, and reviled his ancestors. He also sent to negotiate surrender, saying, "I will surrender only to a Han general." When Ma Sui arrived, he surrendered to Sui instead. Yuanliang went to Han Yougui and said, "He insulted my ancestors. I mean to kill him today — will you help me?" Yougui agreed. When the allied commanders met, he at once listed Xu's crimes, ordered his attendants to execute him, and then went to apologize to Ma Sui. Sui was furious and was about to execute Yuanliang. Yougui intervened: "If you react this way to the killing of a subordinate, what would the law say if you killed a military commissioner?" Sui fell silent. Yuanliang offered a million strings of cash to reward the army in atonement; Jian also pleaded for him, and Sui pardoned him. Fearing the authorities would prosecute him for unauthorized execution, the emperor issued an advance edict that the matter was not to be pursued.
20
貞元三年，吐蕃請盟，詔以軍從瑊會平涼，元諒軍潘原、遊瑰軍洛口以為援。 元諒曰：「潘原去平涼七十里，虜詐不情，如有急，何以赴？ 請與公連屯。 」瑊以違詔，不聽。 瑊壁盟所二十里，元諒密徙營次之。 既會，元諒望雲物曰：「不詳，虜必有變！ 」傳令約部伍出陣。 俄而虜劫盟，瑊奔還，元諒兵成列出，而涇原節度使李觀亦以精兵五千伏險，與元諒相表裏，虜騎乃解。 元諒遣車重先，而與瑊振旅徐還，時以為有古良將風。 是會也，微元諒、觀二人，瑊且不免。 帝嘉嘆，賜善馬金幣良厚，因賜姓及名。
In Zhenyuan 3, when Tibet requested a treaty, an edict ordered troops to accompany Jian to Pingliang, with Yuanliang's army at Panyuan and Yougui's at Luokou as reserves. Yuanliang said, "Panyuan is seventy li from Pingliang. The Tibetans are treacherous. If trouble comes, how can we reach you in time?" I ask that we camp together." Jian refused, citing the edict's orders. Jian encamped twenty li from the treaty site, but Yuanliang quietly moved his camp beside him. At the meeting Yuanliang studied the sky and said, "This is ill-omened — the Tibetans must be plotting something!" He ordered his units to form up for battle. Soon the Tibetans broke the treaty. Jian fled, but Yuanliang's troops were already in battle order. Li Guan, military commissioner of Jingyuan, had five thousand elite troops in ambush, coordinating with Yuanliang from both sides, and the Tibetan cavalry withdrew. Yuanliang sent the baggage train ahead and withdrew slowly with Jian in good order; contemporaries said he showed the bearing of the great generals of old. At that conference, had it not been for Yuanliang and Guan, Jian would scarcely have escaped alive. The emperor praised him warmly and gave him fine horses and generous gifts of gold and coin, and bestowed on him the imperial surname and the name Yuanliang.
21
更節度隴右，治良原。 良原隍堞湮圮，旁皆平林薦草，虜入寇，常牧馬休徒於此。 元諒培高浚淵，身執苦與士卒均，菑翳榛莽，辟美田數十里，勸士墾藝，歲入粟菽數十萬斛，什具畢給。 又築連弩臺，遠烽偵，為守備，進據勢勝，列新壁。 虜至無所掠，戰又輒北，由是涇、隴以安，西戎憚之。 卒，年六十二，贈司空，謚曰莊威。
He was reassigned as military commissioner of Longyou with his headquarters at Liangyuan. Liangyuan's walls and moats were in ruins, and level woods and tall grass surrounded the site where invaders often pastured horses and rested their men. Yuanliang raised the ramparts and deepened the moats, sharing the hard labor with his men. He cleared the brush, opened fine fields for miles around, and urged the troops to farm. Annual harvests reached hundreds of thousands of hu of grain and beans, and all equipment was fully supplied. He built repeating-crossbow towers, extended beacon and scout lines, strengthened the defenses, seized commanding ground, and erected new walls. When the enemy came they found nothing to plunder and were repeatedly driven back. Jing and Long were secured, and the western tribes feared him. He died at sixty-two and was posthumously made Minister of Works with the posthumous title Zhuangwei.

李觀，其先自趙郡徙洛陽，故為洛陽人。 少沈厚寡言。 以策干朔方節度使郭子儀，子儀遣佐坊州刺史吳伷為防遏使。 以親喪解。 吐蕃內寇，代宗幸陜，觀隱盩厔，率鄉里子姓千人守黑水，虜不敢侵。 嶺南節度使楊慎微奏為偏將，徐浩、李勉代節度，常倚以軍政，數捕平劇賊。 遷大將，試殿中監，召為右龍武將軍。
Li Guan's family had moved from Zhao Commandery to Luoyang, so he was counted as a man of Luoyang. As a youth he was grave, steady, and sparing of words. He presented a policy memorial to Guo Ziyi, military commissioner of Shuofang, who sent him to assist Wu Xian, regional inspector of Fang Prefecture, as defense commissioner. He left office to observe mourning for a parent. When Tibetans raided the interior, Emperor Daizong withdrew to Shan. Guan remained at Zhaozhi and led a thousand local clansmen to hold Heishui, and the enemy did not dare attack. Yang Shenwei, military commissioner of Lingnan, recommended him as a subordinate general. Xu Hao and Li Mian, who succeeded as commissioners, relied on him for military affairs and repeatedly crushed major bandit gangs. He was promoted to senior commander, named acting palace supervisor, and summoned as Right Dragon Martial General.
23
涇師叛，觀適番上，即領兵千餘扈德宗奉天。 詔盡察諸軍，整飭誰邏，增募五千人，鼙旝歡豎，士氣益振。 賜封戶二百，授二子八品官。 從至梁州。 帝還，詔總後軍。 擢四鎮、北庭行軍涇原節度使。 在屯四年，訓部伍，儲藏饒衍。 平涼之盟，吐蕃不得志。 是年，觀入朝，前一日就道，虜至期出精騎狙擊，不及，去。 以少府監檢校工部尚書。 卒，贈太子少傅。
When the Jingzhou troops mutinied, Guan happened to be on palace guard duty and at once led more than a thousand men to escort Dezong to Fengtian. An edict put him in charge of inspecting all armies, tightening night patrols and recruiting five thousand more men. Drums and banners were raised in celebration, and morale surged. He received a fief of two hundred households and eighth-rank posts for two sons. He accompanied the emperor to Liang Prefecture. When the emperor returned to the capital, an edict named him commander of the rear guard. He was promoted to military commissioner of Jingyuan for the Four Garrisons and Beiting field army. He spent four years at his post, drilling the ranks until stores overflowed. At the Pingliang treaty the Tibetans failed to achieve their aims. That year, as Guan set out for court, the Tibetans sent elite cavalry to ambush him on the road the day before he departed, but they arrived too late and withdrew. He was named acting Minister of Works while serving as Director of the Palace Parks. He died and was posthumously made Junior Tutor of the Heir Apparent.

韓遊瑰
Han Yougui
25
韓遊瑰，靈州靈武人，始為郭子儀裨將。 安祿山反，使阿史那從禮將同羅、突厥五千騎偽降於朔方，出塞門，誘河曲九蕃府、六胡叛，部落凡五十萬。 子儀使遊瑰率辛京杲擊破之，九蕃府還附。 累進邠寧節度留後。
Han Yougui came from Lingwu in Ling Prefecture and began his career as a subordinate general under Guo Ziyi. When An Lushan rebelled, he sent Ashina Congli with five thousand Tongluo and Turk cavalry in a feigned surrender to Shuofang. They left through Saimen and incited the Nine Tribal Offices and Six Hu of Hequ to revolt — five hundred thousand tribesmen in all. Ziyi sent Yougui with Xin Jinggao to rout them, and the Nine Tribal Offices returned to allegiance. He rose to acting military commissioner of Binning.
26
奉天之狩，兵未集，遊瑰與慶州刺史論惟明以兵三千來赴，自乾陵北趨醴泉，未至，有詔引軍屯便橋。 次泥泉，與泚兵值，遊瑰欲還奉天，監軍翟文秀曰：「吾壁於此，賊敢逾我而西，可夾攻取之。 今入奉天，賊亦隨至，是引賊迫天子也。 」遊瑰曰：「不然，我寡賊眾。 彼分以亢我，餘眾猶能鼓而西也，不如先入衛天子。 且奉天無強卒，安得夾攻？ 吾士乏且寒，賊以利誘之，眾且潰。 」遂還奉天。 泚兵躡攻之，戰不利； 泚兵奪門，遊瑰殊死戰，乃解。 泚大治戰棚、雲橋，士皆懼，遊瑰曰：「賊取佛祠幹木為攻具，可以火之。 」既而賊大噪攻南雉，遊瑰曰：「是分吾力也。 」趨北雉，遣將郭詢、郭廷玉以銳士三百傅滿直出，火其棚，投薪於中，風返，棚皆燼，賊氣沮。 故諸將推遊瑰赴難功第一。 帝以衛軍無職局，軍置統軍一員，以遊瑰、惟明、賈隱林處之。
When the emperor withdrew to Fengtian before his forces had assembled, Yougui and Lun Weiming, regional inspector of Qing Prefecture, marched three thousand men from north of Qianling toward Liquan. Before they arrived, an edict ordered them to encamp at Bian Bridge. At Niquan they met Zhu Ci's army. Yougui wanted to fall back to Fengtian, but the army supervisor Zhai Wenxiu said, "If we hold here and the rebels try to pass us westward, we can take them in a pincer." If we enter Fengtian now, the rebels will follow and drive straight at the emperor." Yougui replied, "No. We are few and they are many." If they detach a force to block us, the rest can still march west. We should enter Fengtian first and protect the emperor." Besides, Fengtian has no strong garrison — how could we mount a pincer attack?" Our men are exhausted and cold. If the enemy tempts them with rewards, the ranks will break." They turned back to Fengtian. Zhu Ci's troops pursued and attacked; the fighting went badly. The rebels seized a gate, but Yougui fought with desperate valor until the attack was beaten off. Zhu Ci built siege towers and assault bridges, and the troops were terrified. Yougui said, "The rebels are using dry timber from Buddhist shrines for their engines — we can burn them." When the rebels launched a major assault on the southern bastion, Yougui said, "They are dividing our strength." He rushed to the northern bastion and sent Guo Xun and Guo Tingyu with three hundred picked men carrying fuel straight at the enemy works. They set the sheds ablaze and threw in more fuel; the wind shifted back, the structures burned to ash, and rebel morale collapsed. The generals therefore ranked Yougui first in merit for the relief of Fengtian. Because the palace guard lacked a command structure, the emperor created a post of army commander and appointed Yougui, Weiming, and Jia Yinlin to it.
27
李懷光叛，誘遊瑰為變，遊瑰白髮其書。 帝曰：「卿可謂忠義矣！ 」對曰：「臣安知忠義？ 但懷光誤臣，使震驚乘輿，後持臣自解。 」帝嘉其誠，從問：「計欲安出？ 」對曰：「懷光總諸府兵，怙以為亂。 今邠有張昕，靈武有寧薍景璿，河中有呂鳴嶽，振武有杜從政，潼關有李朝臣，渭北有竇覦，皆守將也。 陛下以其眾與地授之，罷懷光權，而尊以元功，諸將仰首，各聽其帥，彼安能以亂？ 」帝曰：「罷懷光權而泚益張，若何？ 」對曰：「陛下約士以不次之賞，今貢賦方至，發而酬之，其守自固。 邠有萬精甲，臣得將之，可以誅賊。 四方杖義而起，賊不足慮。 」帝美其言。
When Li Huai'guang rebelled, he tried to win Yougui to his side; Yougui reported the plot and produced Huai'guang's letter. The emperor said, "You have shown true loyalty!" He replied, "How would I know loyalty?" Huai'guang misled me into alarming the imperial carriage, and would later have used me to excuse himself." The emperor admired his candor and asked, "What do you propose?" He answered, "Huai'guang commands the troops of many prefectures and relies on them to rebel." Zhang Xin holds Bin, Jingxuan holds Lingwu, Lü Mingyue holds Hezhong, Du Congzheng holds Zhenwu, Li Zhaochen holds Tong Pass, and Dou Kan holds Weibei — all loyal garrison commanders. Give each commander his troops and territory, strip Huai'guang of power but honor him as foremost in merit. The generals will look to their own leaders — how could they follow him in rebellion?" The emperor asked, "If we strip Huai'guang of power, Zhu Ci will grow stronger — what then?" He answered, "Your Majesty has promised extraordinary rewards. Tribute is arriving now — distribute it at once, and the garrisons will hold firm on their own." Bin has ten thousand elite armored troops. Give me command of them, and I can destroy the rebels." Loyal forces are rising everywhere on principle — the rebels are nothing to fear. The emperor praised his counsel.
28
會懷光誘復至，渾瑊得書，稍嚴卒以警。 遊瑰不知，發怒，嫚罵瑊。 帝疑有變，即日幸梁州，遊瑰使子從帝。 懷光檄假遊瑰邠州刺史，欲因張昕殺之。 遊瑰既失兵，不知所圖。 有客劉南金說曰：「邠有留甲，可以立功，殆天假也！ 」遊瑰悟，誘舊部兵八百馳入邠，說昕曰：「懷光自蹈禍機，公今可取富貴，無共汙不義也。 我願以麾下為公先驅。 」昕不聽。 遊瑰移疾不出，陰結其將高固等。 昕欲殺遊瑰，戒左右衷甲入。 昕小史李岌潛白遊瑰，伏甲先起，高固等應之，斬昕首以聞。 時懷光子玫在邠，遊瑰衛出之，曰：「殺之只以怒敵，至必遽，不如舍之。 」玫至涇陽，懷光遂走蒲州。
When Huai'guang's overtures arrived again, Hun Jian received the letter and tightened discipline among his troops. Yougui knew nothing of this and flew into a rage, hurling abuse at Jian. Suspecting treachery, the emperor withdrew to Liang Prefecture the same day; Yougui sent his son to accompany him. Huai'guang issued a proclamation naming Yougui regional inspector of Bin Prefecture, planning to have Zhang Xin kill him. Stripped of his army, Yougui did not know what to do. A retainer named Liu Nanjin said, "There are troops still at Bin. You can win merit there — perhaps Heaven is giving you the chance!" Yougui took the hint, rallied eight hundred of his old troops, and rode into Bin. He told Xin, "Huai'guang has walked into disaster. You can seize fortune now — do not share in his treason." I will lead my men as your vanguard." Xin refused. Yougui claimed illness and stayed indoors while secretly winning over Xin's generals Gao Gu and others. Xin plotted to kill Yougui and ordered his attendants to wear armor under their robes. Xin's clerk Li Ji warned Yougui in secret. Yougui's men struck first; Gao Gu and the others joined in, beheaded Xin, and reported to the throne. Huai'guang's son Mei was at Bin. Yougui escorted him out, saying, "Killing him would only enrage the enemy. When Huai'guang hears of it he will flee all the faster if we spare the boy." When Mei reached Jingyang, Huai'guang fled to Pu Prefecture.
29
遊瑰屯七盤，受李晟節度。 詔拜邠寧節度使，遂會渾瑊於奉天，與瑊、戴休顏分扼京西要險。 李晟入長安，遊瑰破泚兵咸陽。 泚走涇州，遊瑰使諭涇將楊澄，澄拒不納，泚遂敗。 京師平，遷檢校尚書左僕射，實封戶四百。 帝至自興元，遊瑰及瑊、休顏從，而李晟、尚可孤、李元諒奉迎，論功與瑊等皆第一。 遊瑰還屯邠寧。 懷光寇同州，瑊、元諒敗於乾坑。 詔遊瑰率兵並力，敗賊眾五千於屯。 遂會瑊、馬燧圍蒲城。 師次焦籬堡，守將尉珪降。 懷光見勢單蹙，乃縊死。
Yougui encamped at Qipan and placed himself under Li Sheng's command. An edict named him military commissioner of Binning. He joined Hun Jian at Fengtian and, with Jian and Dai Xiuyan, held the key passes west of the capital. When Li Sheng entered Chang'an, Yougui routed Zhu Ci's forces at Xianyang. Zhu Ci fled to Jingzhou. Yougui sent envoys to the Jing commander Yang Cheng, who refused him entry, and Zhu Ci was destroyed. After the capital was recovered, he was promoted to acting Left Vice Director of the Imperial Secretariat with an actual fief of four hundred households. When the emperor returned from Xingyuan, Yougui accompanied him with Jian and Xiuyan, while Li Sheng, Shang Ke'gu, and Li Yuanliang came out to welcome him. In the reckoning of merit they all ranked first with Jian. Yougui returned to his post at Binning. Huai'guang raided Tong Prefecture, and Jian and Yuanliang were defeated at Qiankeng. An edict ordered Yougui to join the campaign; he defeated five thousand rebels at their camp. He then joined Jian and Ma Sui in besieging Pucheng. At Jiaoli Fort the defending general Wei Gui surrendered. Seeing his position hopeless, Huai'guang hanged himself.
30
貞元二年，吐蕃入涇、隴、邠、寧，遊瑰追至安化，虜營合水北。 遊瑰策曰：「賊行無人地，必怠，可襲取之。 」使將史履澄夜領兵五百入其營，斬數百級，取馬五千。 遲明，虜以兵尾擊，遊瑰羅幟自衛，鼙鼓四發，虜驚潰去。 是歲，復圍鹽州，刺史杜彥光約與之城，吐蕃許之，又取銀、夏、麟等州。 遊瑰請收鹽州以斷戎人走集：「虜入漢，食禾菽，方春而病，此天亡時也。 」有詔李元諒、韓全義率師一萬，會遊瑰收鹽州。 吐蕃請修清水盟，以歸侵地，馬燧為之請。 詔問遊瑰，答曰：「西戎弱則請盟，強則入寇。 今侵地益深而乞盟，詐我也！ 」帝不從。 會盟平涼，詔遊瑰以軍屯洛口。 盟之日，遊瑰以勁騎五百待非常，令曰：「即有變，急趨柏泉以分虜勢。 」瑊被劫，馳以免，虜見兵出，即解去。 後吐蕃寇大回原，遊瑰方壁長武，即選騎八百迎擊，自引兵繼之。 監軍以為戎不可易，答曰：「賊攻豐義，今遊騎先破，則彼大眾不敢前，豐義全矣！ 」戰南原，敗之，吐蕃夜遁。
In Zhenyuan 2 the Tibetans raided Jing, Long, Bin, and Ning. Yougui pursued them to Anhua, where they camped north of the He River. Yougui said, "They are marching through empty country and will grow careless. We can take them by surprise." He sent the general Shi Lücheng with five hundred men into the enemy camp by night. They killed several hundred Tibetans and captured five thousand horses. At dawn the Tibetans counterattacked from the rear. Yougui drew up his banners, drums sounded on every side, and the enemy broke and fled. That year they besieged Salt Prefecture again. The regional inspector Du Yanguang agreed to surrender the city, and Tibet also seized Yin, Xia, Lin, and other prefectures. Yougui urged recovering Salt Prefecture to cut the enemy's lines of retreat: "When they invade Han territory they eat our grain and beans. It is spring and they fall sick — Heaven is destroying them." An edict ordered Li Yuanliang and Han Quanyi each to bring ten thousand men and join Yougui in retaking Salt Prefecture. Tibet asked to renew the Qingshui treaty and return conquered land; Ma Sui supported the request. The throne asked Yougui, who answered, "When the western tribes are weak they sue for peace; when strong they raid." They have seized more land than ever and now beg for a treaty — this is a trick!" The emperor would not listen. At the Pingliang conference an edict ordered Yougui to station his army at Luokou. On the day of the treaty Yougui held five hundred elite horsemen in reserve and ordered, "If anything goes wrong, ride at once for Baiquan to split the enemy force." Jian was ambushed but rode clear; when the Tibetans saw troops advancing they withdrew. Later, when Tibet raided Dayuan Plain, Yougui was fortifying at Changwu. He sent eight hundred cavalry to intercept them and followed with the main force. The army supervisor said the enemy could not be challenged lightly. Yougui answered, "They are attacking Fengyi. If our cavalry defeat their scouts first, their main force will not dare advance and Fengyi will be saved." He fought on the southern plain, routed them, and the Tibetans fled by night.
31
會子欽緒以射生將衛京師，與妖人李廣弘謀反，謀泄，奔邠州，中人捕斬，以狀示遊瑰。 遊瑰懼，求歸死京師，帝不許。 又執欽緒二息送京師，帝亦原之。 未幾，入朝，素服聽命，有詔復位，勞遇如故。
His son Qinxu, a bowman-captain guarding the capital, plotted rebellion with the charlatan Li Guanghong. The plot was exposed; he fled to Binzhou, was captured and executed by palace agents, and the report was sent to Yougui. Yougui was terrified and asked to return to the capital to accept punishment; the emperor refused. He also sent Qinxu's two sons to the capital; the emperor pardoned them as well. Soon afterward he came to court in plain dress to await judgment. An edict restored him to office, and he was received with the same honors as before.
32
遊瑰盛言城豐義以遏虜侵。 帝悅，趣還軍。 初，遊瑰之朝，眾謂且得罪，故賫送殊薄。 既還，舉軍不自安。 大將范希朝善兵，遊瑰畏其逼，欲誅之，希朝奔鳳翔，帝聞，召入宿衛。 遊瑰遣兵築豐義，才二板而潰，寧卒數百大掠，遊瑰不能禁。 詔用張獻甫代之。 遊瑰畏亂，委軍輕出，還京師，拜右龍武統軍。 卒，謚曰襄。
Yougui urged building a fortress at Fengyi to block enemy raids. The emperor was pleased and ordered him back to his command. When Yougui first went to court, his men expected him to be punished, so their parting gifts were meager. When he returned, the whole army was uneasy. His senior commander Fan Xichao was a capable soldier. Yougui feared him and plotted his death; Xichao fled to Fengxiang and was summoned to the capital guard. Yougui sent men to build Fengyi, but the wall collapsed after only two courses. Hundreds of Ning troops looted wildly, and he could not stop them. An edict replaced him with Zhang Xianfu. Fearing mutiny, Yougui left his army in haste, returned to the capital, and was named commander of the Right Dragon Martial Army. He died and was given the posthumous title Xiang.
33
廣弘者，自言宗室子。 始為浮屠，妄曰：「我嘗見嶽、瀆神，當作天子，可復冠。 」男子董昌舍廣弘於資敬寺，召相工唐郛視之，教郛告人曰：「廣弘且大貴。 」乃誘欽緒、神策將魏循、李傪、越州參軍事劉昉等作亂。 昉家數具酒大會廣弘所，陰相署置。 又妄曰：「神戒我十月十日趣舉。 」約欽緒夜擊鼓，噪淩霄門，焚飛龍廄，循等以神策兵迎廣弘，事捷，大剽三日。 循、傪上變，乃禽廣弘及支黨鞫仗內，付三司訊實，皆殊死。 廣弘臨刑，色自如。 由是禁人不得入觀、祠。
Guanghong claimed to be a member of the imperial clan. He had been a Buddhist monk and declared, "I have seen the gods of Mount Heng and the sacred rivers. I am destined to be emperor and may wear the cap of office again." A man named Dong Chang housed Guanghong at Zijing Temple and brought in the physiognomist Tang Fu, whom he instructed to proclaim that Guanghong was destined for greatness. He then recruited Qinxu, the Shence generals Wei Xun and Li Song, and the Yue Prefecture staff member Liu Fang and others to rebel. Liu Fang's household held repeated banquets at Guanghong's lodging, secretly assigning ranks among the conspirators. He also declared, "The gods have told me to act on the tenth day of the tenth month." They agreed that Qinxu would beat drums at night, storm Lingxiao Gate, and burn the Flying Dragon stables while Xun and the others brought Shence troops to install Guanghong. If they succeeded, they would loot for three days. Xun and Song reported the plot. Guanghong and his followers were seized, interrogated in the palace, handed to the Three Offices, and all sentenced to death. At execution Guanghong's face showed no change. After this the public was forbidden to enter temples and shrines freely.
34
杜希全
Du Xiquan
35
杜希全，京兆醴泉人。 以裨將隸郭子儀，積功勞至朔方節度使。 軍令整嚴，士畏其威。 奉天之狩，希全與鄜坊節度使李建徽、鹽州刺史戴休顏、夏州刺史時常春引兵赴難。 次漠谷，為賊邀擊，乘高縱石下之，強弩雜發。 德宗使援之，不克，還保邠州。 賊平，遷檢校尚書左僕射、靈鹽豐夏節度使，封餘姚郡王。 將即屯，獻《體要》八章，砭切政病。 帝嘉納，賜《君臣箴》一篇。
Du Xiquan came from Liquan in Jingzhao. He served Guo Ziyi as a subordinate general and rose through merit to military commissioner of Shuofang. His discipline was severe and his troops feared him. When the emperor withdrew to Fengtian, Xiquan marched with Li Jianhui, military commissioner of Zheng and Fang, Dai Xiuyan, regional inspector of Salt Prefecture, and Shi Changchun, regional inspector of Xia Prefecture. At Mogu Valley they were ambushed; the enemy rolled stones down from the heights and poured crossbow fire into them. Dezong sent reinforcements but they could not break through; the column fell back to Binzhou. After the rebellion was crushed he was made acting Left Vice Director of the Imperial Secretariat and military commissioner of Ling, Yan, Feng, and Xia, and created Prince of Yuyao. Before leaving for his post he submitted "Essentials" in eight chapters, sharply criticizing flaws in government. The emperor praised and accepted it and granted him a copy of "Admonitions for Ruler and Minister."
36
尋兼夏綏銀節度都統，建言：「鹽州據要會，為塞保鄣。 自平涼背盟，城陷於虜，於是靈武勢縣，鄜坊單逼，為邊深患，請復城鹽州。 」乃詔希全及朔方、邠寧、銀夏、鄜坊、振武及神策行營諸節度合選士三萬五千屯鹽州，又敕涇原、劍南、山南軍深入吐蕃，牽撓其力，使不得犯塞。 執築凡六千人，閱二旬畢。 由是虜憚，不輕入。
Soon he was also made overall commander of the Xia, Sui, and Yin circuits and memorialized, "Salt Prefecture occupies a strategic junction and is the frontier's chief bulwark." Since the Pingliang betrayal the city had fallen to the enemy, leaving Lingwu exposed and Zheng-Fang isolated — a grave frontier crisis. He asked to rebuild Salt Prefecture. An edict ordered Xiquan and the commissioners of Shuofang, Binning, Yin-Xia, Zheng-Fang, Zhenwu, and the Shence field army to assemble thirty-five thousand men at Salt Prefecture, and ordered Jingyuan, Jiannan, and Shannan troops to raid deep into Tibet to pin down enemy forces. Six thousand laborers finished the work in twenty days. The enemy thereafter feared to raid lightly.
37
希全居河西久，頗越法橫肆，帝數容掩其短。 豐州刺史李景略名出希全上，疑逼己，遂排劾之。 帝為斥以答其意。 素苦風眩，稍劇，益忌忍，遂誣殺判官李起，吏下累息。 卒，贈司空。
Xiquan had long governed Hexi and often acted above the law; the emperor repeatedly overlooked his excesses. Li Jinglue, regional inspector of Feng Prefecture, outshone him in reputation. Xiquan, fearing a rival, impeached and drove him out. The emperor dismissed Jinglue to satisfy him. He had long suffered dizzy spells; as they worsened he grew more suspicious and cruel, falsely executed his aide Li Qi, and reduced his staff to terrified silence. He died and was posthumously made Minister of Works.
38
邢君牙
Xing Junya
39
邢君牙，瀛州樂壽人。 少從幽薊、平盧軍，以戰功歷果毅、折沖郎將。 安祿山反，從侯希逸涉海入青州。 田神功為兗鄆節度使，使君牙將兵屯好畤防盛秋。 吐蕃犯京師，代宗出陜，以扈從功，累封河間郡公。
Xing Junya came from Leshou in Ying Prefecture. As a youth he served in the Youji and Pinglu armies and rose through battle merit to defender and assault-resistance commandant. When An Lushan rebelled, he followed Hou Xiyi by sea into Qingzhou. When Tian Shenggong was military commissioner of Yan and Yun, he stationed Junya at Haozhi to guard against autumn raids. When Tibet raided the capital, Emperor Daizong withdrew to Shan. For his escort service Junya was repeatedly enfeoffed as Duke of Hejian.
40
建中初，李晟從馬燧討田悅，以君牙為都將，在武安、襄國間凡五戰，斬馘功最。 德宗出奉天，晟率君牙倍道赴難，徙屯渭橋，軍中便宜，惟君牙得豫。 晟在鳳翔，數行邊，常以君牙守。 晟入朝，代為鳳翔觀察使。 俄領節度，檢校尚書右僕射。 吐蕃歲犯邊，君牙劭耕講戰以為備，戎不能侵。 又城隴州平戎川，號永信城。 卒官，贈司空。
Early in the Jianzhong era Li Sheng campaigned with Ma Sui against Tian Yue and made Junya his chief commander. In five battles between Wu'an and Xiangguo he won the greatest share of kills and captures. When Dezong withdrew to Fengtian, Sheng raced Junya to the rescue and shifted camp to Wei Bridge. Of Sheng's staff only Junya was consulted on urgent military decisions. When Sheng was at Fengxiang he often toured the frontier and regularly left Junya in command. When Sheng went to court, Junya succeeded him as observation commissioner of Fengxiang. Soon afterward he took the military commission and was named acting Right Vice Director of the Imperial Secretariat. Tibet raided the border every year; Junya urged farming and drilled his troops for war, and the enemy could not break through. He also fortified Pingrongchuan in Long Prefecture and named the stronghold Yongxin Fort. He died in office and was posthumously made Minister of Works.
41
初，布衣張汾者，無紹而干君牙，軒然坐客上。 會吏擿簿書，以盜沒宴錢五萬，君牙怒其欺，汾不謝去，曰：「吾在京師，聞邢君牙一時豪俊，今乃與設吏論錢，云何？ 」君牙慚，遽釋吏，引為上客，留月餘，以五百縑為謝。 其屈己好士類此。
Early on a commoner named Zhang Fen sought out Junya without any introduction and took a seat above the other guests as if he belonged there. When a clerk found in the accounts that fifty thousand strings of banquet funds had been stolen, Junya was furious at the fraud. Fen offered no apology but walked out, saying, "In the capital I heard that Xing Junya was a hero of his generation — and now you haggle over money with a petty clerk? What kind of man is that?" Junya was ashamed. He at once released the clerk, treated Fen as an honored guest, kept him for more than a month, and sent him off with five hundred bolts of silk in gratitude. His willingness to humble himself and his love of worthy men were like this.
