1
于王二杜範
Yu, Wang, the two Dus, and Fan.
2
于頔，字允元，後周太師謹七世孫。 蔭補千牛，調華陰尉，累勞遷侍御史。 為吐蕃計會使，有專對材。 擢長安令、駕部郎中。
Yu Di, whose style name was Yun'yuan, was a seventh-generation descendant of Jin, Grand Tutor of the Later Zhou. He entered service through hereditary appointment as a Qianniu guardsman, was posted as magistrate of Huayin, and rose through merit to attendant censor. As envoy for Tibetan accounts he showed a gift for handling delicate negotiations on his own authority. He was then promoted to mayor of Chang'an and director in the Ministry of Works for chariots.
3
出為湖州刺史。 部有湖陂，異時溉田三千頃，久廞廢，頔行縣，命脩復堤閼，歲獲粳稻蒲魚無慮萬計。 州地庳薄，葬者不掩柩，頔為坎，瘞枯骨千餘，人賴以安。
He was appointed prefect of Huzhou. His jurisdiction held a lake embankment that had once watered three thousand qing of farmland but had long fallen into ruin. Touring the counties, Di ordered the dikes and sluice gates restored, and thereafter the district yielded tens of thousands in japonica rice, cattails, and fish each year. The land was low and impoverished, and the dead were often buried without even covering the coffins. Di dug burial pits and interred more than a thousand exposed bones, to the great relief of the people.
4
未幾，改蘇州。 罷淫祠，浚溝澮，端路衢，為政有績。 然暴橫少恩，杖前部尉以逞憾，觀察使王緯以聞，德宗不省。 俄遷大理卿，為陜虢觀察使，慢言謝緯曰：「始足下劾我，三進官矣！ 」益自肆。 峻罰苛懲，官吏惴恐，皆重足一跡。 參軍事姚峴不勝虐，自沈於河。
Before long he was transferred to Suzhou. He abolished improper shrines, dredged the irrigation canals, and straightened the highways, and his administration won a solid record of achievement. Yet he was brutal and sparing of mercy, once beating a former prefect to settle a grudge. Surveillance commissioner Wang Wei reported him, but Emperor Dezong took no notice. Soon he was made director of the Court of Judicial Review and surveillance commissioner of Shanzhou and Guozhou, and he told Wei with insolent mock gratitude: "When you first impeached me, I had only risen three ranks since then!" " He grew even more unrestrained. His penalties were severe and his punishments harsh; officials lived in dread and scarcely dared move except on tiptoe. His military adjutant Yao Xian could not bear the abuse and drowned himself in the river.
5
貞元十四年，拜山南東道節度使。 是時，吳少誠叛，頔率兵自唐州戰吳房朗山，取之，禽其將李璨，又勝之濯神溝。 於是請升襄州為大都督府，廣募戰士，儲良械，撊然有專漢南意，所牾者類治以軍法。 帝晚務姑息，頔所奏建，無不開允。 公斂私輸，持下益急，而慢於奉上。 誣劾鄧州刺史元洪，朝廷重違，為流端州，命中人護送至棗陽。 頔遣兵劫洪還，拘之，表責洪太重，改吉州長史，遣使厚諭乃已。 嘗怒判官薛正倫，奏貶陜州長史，比詔下，頔中悔，奏復署舊職。 正倫死，以兵圍其居，強使孽子與婚。 昵吏高洪，縱使剝下，別將陳儀不勝忿，刺殺洪，一府驚潰。 累遷檢校尚書左僕射、同中書門下平章事，封燕國公。 俄擅以兵取鄧州，天子未始誰何。 初，襄有髹器，天下以為法。 至頔驕蹇，故方帥不法者號「襄樣節度」。
In the fourteenth year of Zhenyuan he was appointed military commissioner of the eastern Shannan circuit. At that time Wu Shaocheng rebelled. Di led troops from Tangzhou, fought Wu at Langshan in Fang, captured the place, took his general Li Can prisoner, and defeated him again at Zhushen Gully. He then petitioned to elevate Xiangzhou to a great metropolitan prefecture, recruited warriors on a wide scale, and stockpiled fine arms, swaggering as though he meant to dominate the entire Han south; most who crossed him were punished under military law. In his later years the emperor pursued a policy of indulgence, and none of Di's proposals was refused. He diverted public revenue to private ends, grew ever harsher toward those below him, and showed little respect toward the throne above. He brought a false charge against Dengzhou prefect Yuan Hong. Reluctant to defy him, the court exiled Hong to Duanzhou and ordered eunuchs to escort him as far as Zaoyang. Di sent troops to seize Hong on the road and bring him back, then detained him. He memorialized that Hong's sentence was too harsh, had it reduced to chief minister of Jizhou, and only dropped the affair after sending envoys with lavish reassurances. Once, enraged at his judicial assistant Xue Zhenglun, he memorialized to demote him to chief minister of Shanzhou. When the edict arrived, Di had already changed his mind and petitioned to restore Zhenglun to his former post. After Zhenglun died, Di surrounded his house with soldiers and compelled Zhenglun's illegitimate son to marry into his family. He favored his clerk Gao Hong and allowed him to fleece the populace. Vice-commander Chen Yi, unable to contain his fury, stabbed Hong to death, throwing the whole prefectural staff into panic. He rose in stages to acting chief minister of the left ministry and co-equal with the Secretariat, and was enfeoffed as Duke of Yan. Before long he seized Dengzhou by force on his own authority, and the emperor never held him to account. In earlier days Xiang's lacquerware had set the standard for the empire. Once Di grew arrogant and overbearing, lawless regional commissioners came to be called "commissioners in the Xiang mold."
6
憲宗立，權綱自出，頔稍懼，願以子尚主，帝許之。 遂入朝，拜司空、同中書門下平章事。 請準杜佑，月三奉朝，詔可。
When Xianzong took the throne and began asserting imperial authority, Di grew uneasy and offered his son in marriage to a princess; the emperor consented. He then came to court and was appointed Minister of Works and co-equal with the Secretariat. He asked to follow Du You's precedent of attending court three times a month, and the request was granted.
7
時宦者梁守謙幸於帝，頗用事。 有梁正言者，與頔子敏善，敏因正言厚賂守謙，求頔出鎮。 久不報，敏怒其紿，責所饋，誘正言家奴支解之，棄混中。 家童上變，詔捕頔吏沈壁及它奴送御史獄，命中丞薛存誠、刑部侍郎王播、大理卿武少儀雜問之。 頔與諸子素服待罪建福門，門史不內，屏營負墻立，更遣人上章，有司拒不聞。 翌日復往，宰相諭使還第。 貶為恩王傅，子敏竄雷州，至商山，賜死。 次子季友奪二官，正及方免官。 流壁封州，正言誅死。
At that time the eunuch Liang Shouqian enjoyed the emperor's favor and wielded great influence. A man named Liang Zhengyan was close to Di's son Min. Through Zhengyan, Min lavished bribes on Shouqian to secure his father's return to a regional command. When no answer came for a long time, Min concluded he had been cheated, demanded his gifts back, and induced Zhengyan's household slave to dismember him and throw the remains into a privy pit. A household servant reported the crime. An edict ordered Di's clerk Shen Bi and the other slaves sent to the censorate prison, and directed Vice Censor Xue Cuncheng, Vice Minister of Punishments Wang Bo, and Court Director Wu Shaoyi to conduct a joint investigation. Di and his sons waited in plain dress to accept punishment at the Jianfu Gate, but the gate attendants would not let them in. They stood in anguish with their backs to the wall, sent in another memorial, and were turned away by the authorities. They returned the next day, but the chancellor told them to go back to their residence. Di was demoted to tutor of the Prince of En. His son Min was exiled to Leizhou and, at Shangshan, was granted death by the throne. His second son Jiyou was stripped of two ranks, while Zheng and Fang were removed from office. Shen Bi was exiled to Fengzhou, and Zhengyan was put to death.
8
久之，拜戶部尚書。 帝討蔡，頔獻家財以助國，帝卻之。 又坐季友居喪荒宴，削金紫光祿大夫。 帝初欲頔告老，宰相李逢吉謂得謝乃優禮，非所以示責。 明年，乃致仕。 宰司將以太子少保官之，帝改署賓客。 郁郁不得意卒，贈太保，太常謚曰厲。
After a long interval he was appointed Minister of Revenue. When the emperor campaigned against Cai, Di offered his family fortune to support the state, but the emperor refused it. He was punished again because Jiyou held a riotous feast during mourning, and Di was stripped of his rank as Golden-girdled Grand Mentor of the Bright Hall. The emperor at first meant to let Di request retirement, but Chancellor Li Fengji argued that granting leave would look like favor, not punishment. The following year he retired from office. The chief ministers were about to appoint him Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent, but the emperor reassigned him as Guest of the Crown Prince instead. He died embittered and dissatisfied. Posthumously he was given the title Grand Tutor, and the Court of Imperial Sacrifices assigned him the posthumous name Vicious.
9
頔嘗制《順聖樂舞》獻諸朝。 又教女伎為八佾，聲態雄侈，號《孫吳順聖樂》雲。
Di once composed the "Shun Sage Music and Dance" and presented it at court. He also trained female performers in the eight-row dance. The sound and spectacle were grand and extravagant, and it was called the "Sun Wu Shun Sage Music."
10
季友尚憲宗永昌公主，拜駙馬都尉。 從穆宗獵苑中，求改頔謚，會徐泗節度使李亦為請，更賜謚曰思。 尚書右丞張正甫封還詔書，右補闕高釴、博士王彥威持不可，謂：「頔文吏，倔強犯命，擅軍襄、鄧，欲脅制朝廷； 殺不辜，留制囚，遮使者，僭正樂。 勢迫而朝，非其宿心，得全腰領而歿，猶以為幸，不宜更謚。 」帝不從。
Jiyou married Emperor Xianzong's Princess Yongchang and was appointed Grand Commandant of the Imperial Son-in-law. While accompanying Muzong on a hunt in the imperial park, he asked to change Di's posthumous name. Xu-Si commissioner Li made the same request, and the posthumous name was changed to Reflective. Vice Director of the Right Ministry Zhang Zhengfu returned the edict unopened. Remonstrator Gao Yi and academician Wang Yanwei objected, saying: "Di was a civil official who defied orders, seized armies at Xiang and Deng on his own authority, and sought to intimidate the court; he killed the innocent, held prisoners under his own authority, obstructed imperial envoys, and presumptuously adopted court music. He came to court only because force left him no choice, not from any change of heart. That he died with his head still on his shoulders was already more than he deserved; his posthumous name ought not be changed again. The emperor would not agree.
11
方，長慶時以勛家子通豪俠，欲事河朔，以策幹宰相元稹。 而李逢吉黨謀傾執政，乃告稹結客刺裴度，事下有司，驗無狀，方坐誅。
Fang, in the Changqing era, moved among bold spirits as the son of a meritorious house and wished to throw in his lot with the Hebei commanders. He presented his plans to Chancellor Yuan Zhen. Li Fengji's faction, plotting to bring down the administration, accused Zhen of hiring assassins to kill Pei Du. The case went to the authorities; investigation found no proof, but Fang was executed nonetheless.
12
王智興，字匡諫，懷州溫人。 少驍銳，為徐州牙兵，事刺史李洧。 洧棄李納，挈州自歸。 納怒，急攻洧。 智興能駛步，奉表，不數日至京師告急，德宗出朔方軍五千擊納，解去，自是為徐特將。
Wang Zhixing, whose style name was Kuangjian, was a native of Wen in Huaizhou. In youth he was fierce and daring, and served as a soldier in the Xuzhou yamen under prefect Li Wei. Wei broke with Li Na and brought the prefecture back to imperial allegiance on his own. Na was furious and pressed the attack on Wei. Zhixing was a swift runner. He carried the memorial and reached the capital within days to report the emergency. Dezong sent five thousand troops from the northern frontier to strike Na and lift the siege. From then on Zhixing was made a special general of Xu.
13
討吳元濟也。 李師道謀撓王師，數侵徐救蔡。 節度使李願遣智興率步騎拒賊。 其將王朝晏方攻沛，智興逆擊，敗之，朝晏脫身保沂州。 進破姚海兵五萬於豐北，獲美妾三人，智興曰：「軍中有女子，安得不敗？ 」即斬以徇。 朝晏自沂以輕兵襲沛，夜戰狄丘，復破之。 累遷侍御史。
This was during the campaign against Wu Yuanji. Li Shidao sought to disrupt the imperial campaign and repeatedly raided Xu to relieve Cai. Commissioner Li Yuan sent Zhixing with infantry and cavalry to hold the rebels off. Shidao's general Wang Chaoyan was attacking Pei when Zhixing met him head-on, defeated him, and drove Chaoyan to flee and hold Yizhou. Pressing forward, he routed Yao Hai's army of fifty thousand north of Feng and seized three beautiful concubines. Zhixing said, "An army that keeps women in its ranks—how can it fail to lose?" " He had them beheaded at once as a warning to the troops. Chaoyan led light troops from Yi to raid Pei again. Zhixing fought him at night at Diqiu and defeated him once more. He was promoted in stages to attendant censor.
14
元和十三年，伐師道，智興以步騎八千次胡陵，與忠武軍會，以騎畀其子晏平、晏宰為先鋒，自率軍繼之。 壞河橋，收黃隊，攻金鄉，拔魚臺，俘斬萬計。 賊平，進御史中丞。 明年，召還，為沂州刺史。
In the thirteenth year of Yuanhe, during the campaign against Li Shidao, Zhixing camped at Huling with eight thousand infantry and cavalry, joined the Zhongwu army, placed his sons Yanping and Yanzai at the head of the cavalry vanguard, and followed with the main force. He destroyed the river bridge, seized Huangdui, attacked Jinxiang, and took Yutai, capturing and killing enemies by the tens of thousands. When the rebels were pacified, he was promoted to vice director of the censorate. The following year he was recalled and appointed prefect of Yizhou.
15
長慶初，河朔用兵，加檢校左散騎常侍，充武寧軍副使、河北行營諸軍都知兵馬使，帥兵三千度河。 屬朝廷用崔群為武寧節度使，群畏智興難制，密請追還京師，未報。 會赦王廷湊，諸節度班師。 智興還，群遣寮屬迎之，令士季甲而入。 智興心不悅，因勒兵斬關入，殺異己者十餘輩，然後謁群謝曰：「此軍情也！ 」群乃治裝去，智興以兵衛送還朝； 至埇橋，掠鹽鐵院及貢物，劫商旅，逐濠州刺史侯弘度。 朝廷甫罷兵，不能討，即詔檢校工部尚書，充本軍節度使。 智興由是揫索財賂，交權幸以賈虛名，用度不足，始稅泗口以佐軍須。
Early in Changqing, when troops were mobilized in Hebei, he was given the acting rank of left regular attendant, made deputy commissioner of the Wuning army and overall commander of the Hebei expedition forces, and led three thousand men across the Yellow River. The court then appointed Cui Qun commissioner of Wuning. Qun feared Zhixing would be hard to control and secretly petitioned to have him recalled to the capital, but no answer had yet come. When Wang Tingcou was granted amnesty, the various commissioners withdrew their armies. When Zhixing returned, Qun sent his staff to receive him and ordered the troops to enter the city in full armor by season. Zhixing took this as an insult. He led his troops through the gate by force, killed more than ten men who opposed him, then went to Qun and apologized, saying, "This was the army's doing!" Qun thereupon packed to leave. Zhixing escorted him toward the capital under armed guard; but at Yongqiao he plundered the Salt and Iron commission and tribute stores, robbed traveling merchants, and drove out Haozhou prefect Hou Hongdu. The court had only just ended the war and could not punish him, so it immediately appointed him acting Minister of Works and commissioner of his own army. Zhixing then extorted wealth and bribes, curried favor with powerful courtiers to buy empty prestige, and, finding his budget insufficient, began levying taxes at Sikou to meet military expenses.
16
李攻宋州，智興悉銳師出宋西鄙，破之漳口。 平，加檢校尚書左僕射。 李同捷以滄德叛，智興請悉師三萬賫五月糧討賊，詔拜檢校司徒、同中書門下平章事、滄德行營招撫使。 既戰，降其將十輩、銳士三千，遂拔棣州。 諸將聞，戰愈力，遂有功。 入朝，燕麟德殿，賜予備厚。 冊拜太傅，封雁門郡王，進兼侍中。 改忠武、河中、宣武三節度。 卒，年七十九，贈太尉。
When Li attacked Songzhou, Zhixing led his best troops out along Song's western border and defeated him at Zhangkou. After the victory he was given the acting rank of chief minister of the left ministry. When Li Tongjie rebelled in Cang and De, Zhixing asked to lead his entire force of thirty thousand with five months' provisions to crush the rebels. He was appointed acting Minister of Scriptorium, co-equal with the Secretariat, and pacification commissioner of the Cang-De expedition. In the fighting that followed, ten of the enemy commanders and three thousand crack troops surrendered, and he then took Di prefecture. When the other generals heard of this, they fought all the harder, and the campaign succeeded. He came to court and was feasted in the Qilinde Hall with lavish gifts. He was enfeoffed as Grand Tutor and Duke of Yanmen, and advanced to concurrent Palace Attendant. He was transferred in turn to the Zhongwu, Hezhong, and Xuanwu commands. He died at the age of seventy-nine and was posthumously given the title Grand Tutor.
17
子九人，晏平、宰知名。
He had nine sons; Yanping and Yanzai were the best known.
18
晏平幼從父軍，以討同捷功，檢校右散騎常侍、朔方靈鹽節度使。 父喪，擅取馬四百、兵械七千自衛歸洛陽。 御史劾之，有詔流康州，不即行，陰求援於河北三鎮。 三鎮表其困，改撫州司馬。 給事中韋溫、薛廷老、盧弘宣等還詔不敢下，改永州司戶參軍。 溫固執，文宗諭而止。
Yanping had followed his father into the army from boyhood. For his merit against Tongjie he was made acting right regular attendant and commissioner of Shuofang, Ling, and Yan. When his father died, he arbitrarily seized four hundred horses and seven thousand weapons and marched back to Luoyang under armed escort. The censors impeached him, and an edict exiled him to Kangzhou. He did not set out at once, but secretly sought help from the three Hebei commissioners. The three commissioners memorialized on his behalf, and his sentence was reduced to marshal of Fuzhou. Attendants-in-ordinary Wei Wen, Xue Tinglao, and Lu Hongxuan returned the edict unissued. The sentence was changed again to census registrar of Yongzhou. Wen held firm in his objection until Wenzong personally instructed him to desist.
19
晏宰，後去「晏」，獨名宰。 少拳果，長隸神策軍。 甘露之變，以功兼御史大夫，為光州刺史。 有美政，觀察使段文昌薦之朝，除鹽州刺史。 持法嚴，人不甚便。 累擢邠寧慶節度使。 回鶻平，徙忠武軍。
Yan Zai later dropped the surname Yan and was known simply as Zai. As a youth he was skilled with his fists and daring; in manhood he served in the Shence Army. For his service in the Sweet Dew incident he was made concurrent vice director of the censorate and appointed prefect of Guangzhou. His administration won praise, and surveillance commissioner Duan Wenchang recommended him to court. He was then appointed prefect of Yanzhou. He enforced the law with great severity, to the discomfort of the populace. He rose in stages to commissioner of Bin, Ning, and Qing. After the Uyghurs were pacified, he was transferred to the Zhongwu command.
20
討劉稹也，詔宰以兵出魏博，趨磁州。 當是時，何弘敬陰首鼠，聞宰至，大懼，即引軍濟漳水。 宰相李德裕建言：「河陽兵寡，以忠武為援，既以捍洛，則並制魏博。 」遂詔宰以兵五千椎鋒，兼統河陽行營。 進取天井關，賊黨離沮。 德裕以宰乘破竹勢不遂取澤州，以其子晏實守磁，為顧望計，帝有詔切責。 宰懼，急攻陵川，破賊石會關，進攻澤州。 其將郭誼殺稹降。 宰傳稹首京師，遂節度太原。
During the campaign against Liu Zhen, an edict ordered Zai to lead troops out of Weibo toward Cizhou. At that time He Hongjing was secretly sitting on the fence. When he heard Zai had arrived, he was terrified and at once led his army across the Zhang River. Chancellor Li Deyu proposed: "Heyang has too few troops. Use Zhongwu as reinforcement. Once Luoyang is secured, Weibo can be controlled as well." An edict then appointed Zai with five thousand men as the assault vanguard and placed him in overall command of the Heyang expedition camp. He advanced and seized Tianjing Pass, and the rebel ranks split in dismay. Deyu believed that Zai, with victory in easy reach, deliberately delayed taking Zezhou while leaving his son Yanshi to hold Ci as a hedge. The emperor issued a stern edict of rebuke. Alarmed, Zai pressed the attack on Lingchuan, broke the rebel stronghold at Shi Hui Pass, and advanced on Zezhou. His general Guo Yi killed Zhen and surrendered. Zai sent Zhen's head to the capital and was then appointed commissioner of Taiyuan.
21
宣宗初，入朝，厚結權幸求宰相，周墀劾之，乃還軍。 吐蕃引党項、回鶻寇河西，詔統代北諸軍進擊。 以疾不任事，徙河陽。 罷為太子少保，分司東都。 進少傅，卒。
Early in Xuanzong's reign he came to court and cultivated powerful favorites in hopes of becoming chancellor. Zhou Chi impeached him, and he returned to his command. When Tibet incited the Tangut and Uyghurs to raid Hexi, he was ordered to command the northern frontier armies in a counterattack. Illness left him unable to perform his duties, and he was transferred to Heyang. He was removed as Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent and assigned to the branch office at the eastern capital. He was promoted to Junior Tutor and died.
22
晏實幼機警，智興自養之，故名與諸父齒。 稹平，擢淄州刺史，終天雄節度使。
Yanshi was clever from childhood. Zhixing raised him himself, and so his name was ranked with those of his uncles. After Zhen's defeat he was promoted to prefect of Zi and eventually became commissioner of Tianxiong.
23
杜兼，字處弘，中書令正倫五世孫。 初，正倫無子，故以兄子誌靜為後。 父廙，為鄭州錄事參事軍事。 安祿山亂，逃去，賊索之急，宋州刺史李岑以兵迎之，為追騎所害。 兼尚幼，逃入終南山。 伯父存介為賊執，臨刑，兼號呼願為奴以贖，遂皆免。
Du Jian, whose style name was Chuhong, was a fifth-generation descendant of Chief Minister Xue Zhenglun. Zhenglun had no sons, so he adopted his elder brother's son Zhijing as his heir. His father Yi served as recording secretary and military adjutant in Zhengzhou. During the An Lushan rebellion he fled, and the rebels hunted him relentlessly. Songzhou prefect Li Cen sent troops to escort him to safety, but he was killed by pursuing horsemen. Jian was still a child and fled into the Zhongnan Mountains. His uncle Cunjie was captured by the rebels. As execution neared, Jian cried out that he would become a slave to ransom him, and both were spared.
24
建中初，進士高第，徐泗節度使張建封表置其府。 積勞為濠州刺史。 性浮險，尚豪侈。 德宗既厭兵，大抵刺史重代易，至歷年不徙。 兼探帝意，謀自固，即脩武備，募占勁兵三千。 帝以為才，遂橫恣。 僚官韋賞、陸楚皆聞家子，有美譽，論事忤兼，誣劾以罪。 帝遣中人至，兼廷勞畢，出詔執賞等殺之，二人無罪死，眾莫不冤。 又妄系令狐運而陷李藩，欲殺之，不克。
Early in the Jianzhong era he passed the civil service examination with high honors, and Xu-Si commissioner Zhang Jianfeng had him appointed to his staff. Through accumulated service he was appointed prefect of Haozhou. He was frivolous and treacherous by nature and loved extravagance. Emperor Dezong had grown weary of war, and prefects were seldom rotated; some held their posts for years without transfer. Reading the emperor's mood, Jian sought to entrench himself. He repaired the defenses and conscripted three thousand crack troops. The emperor took this as proof of his ability, and Jian thereupon ruled with arrogant license. His staff officers Wei Shang and Lu Chu were both sons of distinguished families with fine reputations. When their counsel offended Jian, he brought false charges against them. The emperor sent a eunuch. After Jian had entertained him in the hall, he produced an edict ordering Shang and the others seized and executed. Both men died though innocent, and the public regarded it as a gross injustice. He also seized Linghu Yun without cause to frame Li Fan and tried to have him killed, but failed.
25
元和初，入為刑部郎中，改蘇州刺史。 比行，上書言李锜必反，留為吏部郎中。 尋擢河南尹。 杜佑素善兼，終始倚為助力。 所至大殺戮，裒蓺財貲，極耆欲。 適幸其時，未嘗敗。 卒，年七十。 家聚書至萬卷，署其末，以墜鬻為不孝，戒子孫雲。
Early in Yuanhe he was appointed director in the Ministry of Punishments, then transferred to prefect of Suzhou. Just before he was to leave, he memorialized that Li Qi would certainly rebel. He was kept at court as director in the Ministry of Personnel. He was soon promoted to mayor of Henan. Du You had long been on good terms with Jian and consistently relied on him for support. Wherever he served he carried out mass executions, amassed wealth, and indulged his desires to the full. Fortune favored his timing, and he never suffered a downfall. He died at the age of seventy. His household collected more than ten thousand scrolls of books. He inscribed at the end that to let them fall into ruin or sell them off was unfilial, and thus admonished his descendants.
26
從弟羔，貞元初及進士第，有至性。 父死河北，母更兵亂，不知所之，羔憂號終日。 及兼為澤潞判官，鞫獄，有媼辨對不凡，乃羔母，因得奉養。 而不知父墓區處，晝夜哀慟； 它日舍佛祠，觀柱間有文字，乃其父臨死記墓所在。 羔奔往，亦有耆老識其壟，因是得葬。 元和中，為萬年令，時許季同為長安令，京兆尹元義方責租賦不時，系二縣吏，將罪之。 羔等辯列尤苦，尹不為縱。 羔乃謁宰相，請移散官。 憲宗遣中使問狀，具對府政苛細，力不堪奉。 詔皆免官，奪尹三月俸。 議者以羔為直。 未幾，授戶部郎中，後歷振武節度使，以工部尚書致仕。 卒，贈尚書右僕射，謚曰敬。
His cousin Gao passed the civil service examination early in the Zhenyuan era and was renowned for his filial devotion. His father had died in Hebei, and his mother was lost again in the chaos of war. Not knowing where she was, Gao grieved and wept all day long. When Jian served as judicial assistant in Ze-Lu and was hearing a case, an old woman's replies were unusually sharp. She proved to be Gao's mother, and he was thus able to support her again. Yet he still did not know where his father's grave lay, and grieved day and night. One day he stayed at a Buddhist temple and noticed writing between the pillars. It was his father's deathbed record of where the tomb lay. Gao hurried there, and an old man also recognized the mound. He was thus able to give his father a proper burial. During Yuanhe he served as magistrate of Wannian while Xu Jitong was magistrate of Chang'an. Metropolitan governor Yuan Yifang blamed them for late tax collection, detained the clerks of both counties, and was about to punish them. Gao and the others pleaded their case with great anguish, but the governor would not relent. Gao then went to the chancellor and asked to be transferred to an honorary post. Xianzong sent a palace envoy to inquire. Gao explained in full that the metropolitan administration was oppressively exacting and that he could no longer bear to serve under it. An edict removed all of them from office and stripped the governor of three months' salary. Commentators regarded Gao as a man of integrity. Before long he was appointed director in the Ministry of Revenue, later served as commissioner of Zhenwu, and retired as Minister of Works. He died and was posthumously given the title chief minister of the right ministry, with the posthumous name Respectful.
27
子中立，字無為，以門蔭歷太子通事舍人。 開成初，文宗欲以真源、臨真二公主降士族，謂宰相曰：「民間脩婚姻，不計官品而上閥閱。 我家二百年天子，顧不及崔、盧耶？ 」詔宗正卿取世家子以聞。 中立及校書郎衛洙得召見禁中，拜著作郎。 月中，遷光祿少卿、駙馬都尉，尚真源長公主。
His son Zhongli, whose style name was Wuwei, entered service through hereditary privilege as communications officer of the Heir Apparent. Early in the Kaicheng era, Wenzong wished to marry the Princesses Zhenyuan and Linzhen to gentry families and told the chancellor: "Among common people arranging marriages, rank counts for less than pedigree. Our house has ruled as Son of Heaven for two hundred years — are we really beneath the Cui and Lu clans?" An edict ordered the Director of the Imperial Clan to select sons of eminent families and report back. Zhongli and proofreader Wei Zhu were summoned to an audience in the palace and appointed compilers. Within the month he was made Vice Director of the Bright Hall and Grand Commandant of the Imperial Son-in-law and married the Eldest Princess Zhenyuan.
28
中立數求自試，憒憒不樂，因言：「朝廷法令備具，吾若不任事，何賴貴戚撓天下法耶？ 」帝聞異之，轉太仆、衛尉二少卿，歷左右金吾大將軍。 京師惡少優戲道中，具騶唱呵衛，自謂「盧言京兆」，驅放自如。 中立部從吏捕系，立箠死。 遷司農卿。 繩吏急，反為中傷，左徙慶王傅。
Zhongli repeatedly asked for real duties and grew restless in idleness. He said, "The court's laws are complete. If I do not take on real work, why should I rely on noble birth to bend the laws of the realm?" The emperor was struck by this and transferred him to the vice directorships of the Stables and Guards, then successively to general of the left and right Golden Crow guards. Ruffians in the capital staged street performances with mounted escorts shouting guard commands, calling themselves "Lu Speaks for the Capital" and driving people about at will. Zhongli's attendants seized them and beat them to death on the spot. He was transferred to Director of Agriculture. His harsh discipline of the clerks drew slander against him, and he was demoted to tutor of the Prince of Qing.
29
久之，復拜司農卿，入謝，帝曰：「卿用法深，信乎？ 」答曰：「轂下百司養名不肯事，如司農尤叢劇。 陛下無遽信流言，假臣數月，事可濟。 」帝許之。 初，度支度六宮飧錢移司農，司農季一出付吏，大吏盡舉所給於人，權其子錢以給之，既不以時，黃門來督責慢罵。 中立取錢納帑舍，率五日一出，吏不得為奸，後遂以為法。 加檢校右散騎常侍。
After a long interval he was again appointed Director of Agriculture. When he came to give thanks, the emperor said, "They say you enforce the law with great severity. Is that true?" He replied, "Under the capital the hundred offices cultivate reputation and shirk their duties. Agriculture is especially tangled and burdensome. Your Majesty should not hastily believe rumors. Grant me a few months, and the work can be done." The emperor agreed. Previously the Revenue Bureau allocated the six palaces' meal funds to Agriculture, which disbursed them once per season to the clerks. Senior clerks lent out the entire sum and used the interest to make payments. Because payments were late, eunuchs came to supervise and berated them. Zhongli took the funds into the treasury storehouse and disbursed them every five days on average, so the clerks could no longer cheat. The practice later became standard procedure. He was given the acting rank of right regular attendant.
30
京兆尹缺，宣宗將用之，宰相以年少，欲歷試其能，更出為義武節度使。 舊傜車三千乘，歲免鹽海瀕，民苦之。 中立置「飛雪將」數百人，具舟以載，自是民不勞，軍食足矣。 大中十二年，大水泛徐、兗、青、鄆，而滄地積卑，中立自按行，引禦水入之毛河，東註海，州無水災。 卒，年四十八，贈工部尚書。
When the metropolitan governorship fell vacant, Xuanzong meant to appoint him, but the chancellor, thinking him too young, wished to test his abilities first and sent him out again as commissioner of Yiwu. Formerly three thousand corvée carts were levied each year along the salt coast, to the great distress of the people. Zhongli established several hundred "Flying Snow" transport troops and equipped boats for carriage. Thereafter the people were spared excessive labor and military provisions were ample. In the twelfth year of Dazhong, great floods spread over Xu, Yan, Qing, and Yun. Because Cang's terrain was low, Zhongli personally surveyed the works, diverted the imperial flood-control channel into the Mao River, and sent the water eastward to the sea, sparing the prefecture from disaster. He died at the age of forty-eight and was posthumously given the title Minister of Works.
31
中立居官精明，吏下寒栗畏伏。 中雖坐累免，及復用，亦不為寬假，其天資所長雲。
Zhongli was sharp and exacting in office, and his subordinates trembled before him. Though he was once removed under accumulated charges, when restored to office he still showed no leniency. Such was his natural bent.
32
杜亞，字次公，自雲本京兆人。 肅宗在靈武，上書論當世事，擢校書郎。 杜鴻漸節度河西，奏署幕府。 入朝，歷吏部員外郎。 鴻漸為山南、劍南副元帥，亞與楊炎並為判官。 再遷諫議大夫。
Du Ya, whose style name was Cigong, claimed descent from Jingzhao. When Suzong was at Lingwu, he submitted a memorial on current affairs and was promoted to proofreader. When Du Hongjian was commissioner of Hexi, he had Ya appointed to his staff. He came to court and served as vice director in the Ministry of Personnel. When Hongjian served as vice commander of Shannan and Jiannan, Ya and Yang Yan both served as his judicial assistants. He was again promoted to remonstrance grandee.
33
亞自以當衡柄，悒悒不悅。 李棲筠風望高，時謂當宰相，故亞厚結納。 元載得罪，亞與劉晏等劾治。 載死，遷給事中。 常袞惡之，出為江西觀察使。 德宗立，召還。 亞意必任臺宰，倍道進。 與人語，皆天下大政。 或以事祈謁，輒相然可。 帝知，不悅也。 既又建奏疏闊，不稱旨，罷為陜虢觀察兼轉運使。 徙河中。 劉晏抵罪，貶睦州刺史。
Ya believed himself destined for high office and brooded in discontent. Li Qiyun enjoyed high standing, and many expected him to become chancellor, so Ya cultivated close ties with him. When Yuan Zai fell from power, Ya joined Liu Yan and others in impeaching and prosecuting him. After Zai's death, Ya was transferred to attendant-in-ordinary. Chang Gun disliked him and sent him out as surveillance commissioner of Jiangxi. When Dezong took the throne, Ya was recalled. Ya was certain he would be made a chief minister and hurried to the capital at double speed. In conversation with others, he spoke only of great affairs of state. When people came to him with requests, he would readily agree to whatever they asked. When the emperor learned of this, he was displeased. His memorials grew broad and unfocused and failed to please the emperor. He was demoted to surveillance commissioner of Shanzhou and Guozhou and concurrent transport commissioner. He was then transferred to Hezhong. When Liu Yan was convicted, Ya was demoted to prefect of Muzhou.
34
興元初，入遷刑部侍郎，又拜淮西節度使。 至則治漕渠，引湖陂，築防庸，入之渠中，以通大舟，夾堤高卬，田因得溉灌。 疏啟道衢，徹壅通堙，人皆悅賴。 然承陳少遊後，裒率煩重，用度無藝，人冀有所矯革，而亞雅意丞弼，厭外官，往往不親事，日夜召賓客言噱流連。 方春，南民為競度戲，亞欲輕駛，乃髹船底，使篙人衣油彩衣，沒水不濡，觀沼華邃，費皆千萬。 隴西李衡在坐，曰：「使桀、紂為之，不是過也！ 」既泛九曲池，曳繡為帆，詫曰：「要當稱是林沼。 」衡曰：「未有錦纜，雲何？ 」亞大慚。 自是府財耗竭。
Early in the Xingyuan era he was recalled and made vice minister of punishments, then appointed military commissioner of Huaixi. On taking up his post he repaired the transport canals, drew water from lake embankments, and built protective works so large vessels could pass. He raised the dikes on both sides, bringing irrigation to the fields. He cleared the highways, removed obstructions, and opened silted passages, to the great satisfaction of the people. Yet following Chen Shaoyou's example, he imposed heavy and burdensome levies and spent without restraint. The people hoped for reform, but Ya secretly aspired to the chancellorship, disliked provincial office, often neglected his duties, and spent his days and nights entertaining guests with idle talk. In spring, when the southern people held boat-racing festivals, Ya wished to put on a show. He lacquered the boat bottoms and had the polemen wear oil-painted garments that stayed dry underwater. The spectacle on the pond was lavish beyond measure, costing tens of millions. Li Heng of Longxi, who was present, remarked, "If Jie and Zhou had done this, they could scarcely have surpassed it!" He then sailed on the Nine-Bend Pool with brocade rigged as sails and exclaimed, "This is what befits such woods and waters." Heng said, "There is no brocade mooring cable — how can that be?" Ya was deeply ashamed. From then on the prefectural treasury was drained dry.
35
貞元中，罷歸。 宰相竇參憚其宿望，以檢校吏部尚書留守東都。 病風痹且廢，猶欲固寵，奏墾苑中為營田，可減度支歲稟。 詔許之。 先是，苑地可耕者，皆留司中人及屯士占假。 亞計窘，更舉軍帑錢與甸人，至秋取菽粟償息輸軍中，貧不能償者發囷窖略盡，流亡過半。 又賂中人求兼河南尹。 帝審其妄，使禮部尚書董晉代之，賜亞還。 病不能謁。 卒，年七十四，贈太子少傅，謚曰肅。
During the Zhenyuan era he was removed and returned to the capital. Chancellor Dou Can, wary of his long-standing reputation, appointed him acting Minister of Personnel to remain and guard the eastern capital. Though crippled by wind paralysis, he still sought to curry favor and memorialized to reclaim land in the imperial park as military farms, claiming this would reduce the Revenue Bureau's annual grain allotment. The edict approved his plan. Previously, all arable land in the park had been occupied on loan by central-office retainers and garrison soldiers. Hard pressed, Ya advanced army treasury funds to the park tenants, who were to repay with interest in beans and grain each autumn. Those too poor to repay had their granaries stripped nearly bare, and more than half the tenants fled as refugees. He also bribed eunuchs to secure a concurrent appointment as mayor of Henan. The emperor saw through his presumption and sent Minister of Rites Dong Jin to replace him, permitting Ya to return home. He was too ill to attend audience. He died at the age of seventy-four and was posthumously given the title Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent, with the posthumous name Solemn.
36
範傳正，字西老，鄧州順陽人。 父惀，為戶部員外郎，與趙郡李華善，有當世名。 傳正舉進士、宏辭，皆高第，授集賢殿校書郎。 歷歙、湖、蘇三州刺史，有殊政，進拜宣歙觀察使。 代還，坐治第過制，憲宗薄不用，改光祿卿。 以風痹卒，贈左散騎常侍。
Fan Chuanzheng, whose style name was Xilao, was a native of Shunyang in Dengzhou. His father Lun served as vice director in the Ministry of Revenue, was on friendly terms with Li Hua of Zhao, and enjoyed renown in his day. Chuanzheng passed both the civil service examination and the broad learning examination with high honors and was appointed collator in the Hall of Assembled Talents. He served in turn as prefect of She, Hu, and Su with distinguished administrations and was promoted to surveillance commissioner of Xuan and She. On returning to the capital he was punished because his residence exceeded regulations. Xianzong held him in low regard and transferred him to Director of the Bright Hall. He died of wind paralysis and was posthumously given the title left regular attendant.
37
傳正好古，性精悍，初自整飭。 宦益達，用度益奢侈，傾貲貨市權貴歡，私公府如家帑，亦幸素有名，得不敗雲。
Chuanzheng loved antiquity and was by nature sharp and forceful; in his early career he kept himself tightly disciplined. As he rose higher in office, his spending grew ever more lavish. He poured out his wealth to win favor with the powerful and treated public offices like his private purse. Yet because he had long enjoyed a good reputation, he escaped ruin.
