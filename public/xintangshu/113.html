唐張徐
Tang, Zhang, and Xu
2
◎唐臨 （交昭） 張文瓘 （文琮錫文收） 徐有功 （商彥若）
Tang Lin (Jiaozhao) Zhang Wenjin (Wencong, Xi, and Wenshou) Xu Yougong (Shang Yanruo)
3
唐臨，字本德，京兆長安人。 周內史瑾之孫。 其先自北海內徙。 武德初，隱太子討王世充，臨以策進說，太子引直典書坊，授右衛率府鎧曹參軍。 太子廢，出為萬泉丞。 有輕囚久系，方春，農事興，臨說令可且出囚，使就畎畝。 不許。 臨曰：「有所疑，丞執其罪。 」令移疾，臨悉縱歸，與之約，囚如期還。
Tang Lin, whose style name was Bende, came from Chang'an in Jingzhao. He was the grandson of Jin, who had served Zhou as interior secretary. His family had earlier moved inland from Beihai. Early in the Wude era, when the Hidden Crown Prince marched against Wang Shichong, Lin offered strategic advice and was brought onto his staff in the Palace Library, then appointed registrar of army equipment in the Right Guard commandant's office. After the crown prince was deposed, he was sent out as magistrate of Wanquan. Some minor offenders had been held a long time. With spring at hand and farming underway, Lin urged the magistrate to release them temporarily so they could work their fields. The magistrate refused. Lin said, "If there is any doubt, the aide will bear the responsibility." The magistrate then pleaded illness, and Lin released them all on their promise to return; every prisoner came back on time.
4
再遷侍御史。 大夫韋挺責著位不肅，明日，挺越次與江夏王道宗語，臨進曰：「王亂班。 」道宗曰：「與大夫語，何至爾！ 」臨曰：「大夫亦亂班。 」挺失色，眾皆悚伏。 俄持節按獄交州，出冤系三千人。 累遷大理卿。 高宗嘗錄囚，臨占對無不盡，帝喜曰：「為國之要在用法，刻則人殘，寬則失有罪，惟是折中，以稱朕意。 」它日復訊，余司斷者輒紛訴不已，獨臨所訊無一言。 帝問故，答曰：「唐卿斷囚不冤，所以絕意。 」帝嘆曰：「為獄者固當若是。 」乃自述其考曰「形如死灰，心若鐵石」雲。
He was soon promoted again to attending censor. Senior censor Wei Ting had rebuked an official for failing to observe proper court decorum. The next day Ting himself stepped out of rank to converse with Prince Daozong of Jiangxia, and Lin stepped forward and said, "Your Highness has disturbed the ranks." Daozong replied, "I am only speaking with the senior censor—why make so much of it!" Lin said, "The senior censor has disturbed the ranks as well." Ting turned pale, and everyone present shrank back in awe. Before long he was commissioned to review cases in Jiaozhou and released three thousand people who had been wrongfully imprisoned. He rose through successive appointments to minister of justice. Emperor Gaozong once held a prison review, and Lin answered every case without leaving anything unresolved. The emperor was pleased and said, "Governing a state depends on how the law is applied: if it is harsh, people are ruined; if it is lenient, the guilty go free. Only by striking the right balance can it satisfy my intent." On another review day, prisoners judged by other officials kept appealing without end, but not one of those Lin had tried spoke up. The emperor asked why, and he answered, "Minister Tang's judgments are never unjust, so they have given up any thought of appealing." The emperor sighed and said, "A man who runs the prisons ought indeed to be like this." He then wrote his own assessment of Lin: "His bearing is like dead ashes, his heart like iron and stone."
5
臨累遷吏部尚書。 初，來濟謫臺州，李義府謫普州，臨奏許祎為江南巡察使，張倫劍南巡察使。 祎與濟善，而倫與義府有隙。 武後常右義府，察知之，謂臨遣所私督其過，坐免官。 起為潮州刺史，卒，年六十。
Lin was eventually promoted to minister of the civil office. Earlier Lai Ji had been demoted to Taizhou and Li Yifu to Puzhou. Lin memorialized appointing Xu Yi as Jiangnan inspection commissioner and Zhang Lun as Jiannan inspection commissioner. Yi was friendly with Ji, whereas Lun bore a grudge against Yifu. Empress Wu habitually favored Yifu. When she perceived this arrangement, she accused Lin of sending his own men to hunt for Yifu's faults, and he was dismissed from office. He was later appointed prefect of Chaozhou and died at the age of sixty.
6
臨儉薄寡欲，不好治第宅。 性旁通，專務掩人過。 見妻子，必正衣冠。
Lin lived frugally with few wants and never cared to build a fine residence. By nature he was broad-minded and made a point of concealing others' faults. Even when receiving his wife and children he always dressed in full formal attire.
7
兄皎，武德初，為秦王府記室，從王征討，掌書檄。 貞觀中，官吏部侍郎。 先是，選集四時補擬，不為限。 皎請以冬初集，盡季春止，後遂為法。 終益州長史，贈太常卿。
His elder brother Jiao, early in the Wude era, served as recorder in the Prince of Qin's household, accompanied the prince on campaign, and handled documents and proclamations. During the Zhenguan reign he served as vice minister of the civil office. Previously, selection assemblies for appointments had been held in all four seasons without restriction. Jiao proposed that assemblies begin at the start of winter and end by late spring, and this afterward became standard practice. He ended his career as chief administrator of Yizhou and was posthumously enfeoffed as grand master of splendid happiness.
8
子之奇，給事中。 坐章懷太子屬徙邊。 後除括蒼令，與徐敬業起兵，誅。
His son Zhiqi served as supervising secretary. He was implicated as an associate of Crown Prince Zhanghuai and banished to the frontier. He was later appointed magistrate of Kuocang, joined Xu Jingye's rebellion, and was executed.
9
臨孫紹紹，神龍時為太常博士。 遷左臺侍御史、度支員外郎，常兼博士。 韋庶人請妃、公主、命婦以上葬給鼓吹，詔可。 紹曰：「鼓吹本軍容，黃帝戰涿鹿，以為警衛，故曲有《靈夔吼》、《雕鶚爭》、《石墜崖》、《壯士怒》之類。 惟功臣詔葬，得兼用之。 男子有四方功，所以加寵。 雖郊祀天地，不參設，容得接閨閫哉？ 在令，五品官昏葬，無給鼓吹者，唯京官五品則假四品，蓋班秩在夫若子。 請置前詔，用舊典。 」不省。
Lin's grandson Shaoshao served in the Shenlong era as an erudite of the grand imperial sacrifices. He was promoted to attending censor of the left bureau and vice director of revenue, and often held the erudite post concurrently. Consort Wei requested that imperial consorts, princesses, and titled ladies of rank and above be granted martial music at burial, and the edict approved it. Shao said, "Martial music was originally military regalia. When the Yellow Emperor fought at Zhuolu it served as guard and escort, which is why its pieces include titles such as 'Spirit Kui Roars,' 'Eagle and Goshawk Contend,' 'Stone Falls from the Cliff,' and 'Bold Warrior's Wrath.'" Only meritorious ministers granted burial by imperial decree were permitted to use it. It was bestowed on men who had earned merit on all sides as a mark of special honor. Even at suburban sacrifices to Heaven and Earth it was not deployed alongside other rites—how then could it be extended to the women's quarters? Under statute, fifth-rank officials were not granted martial music at burial. Only capital officials of fifth rank borrowed fourth-rank honors, because ceremonial rank followed that of husband or son. He asked that the recent edict be set aside and the old regulations restored. His plea went unheeded.
10
中宗始郊，國子祭酒祝欽明等知韋後能制天子，欲迎諂之，即奏以皇后亞獻，安樂公主終獻，又四時及列帝誕日，遣使者詣陵如事生。 紹以為非禮，引正誼固爭。 帝又詔武氏陵及諸武墓皆置守戶，紹謂：「昊、順二陵守戶五百，與昭陵同。 在令，先世帝王陵戶二十，今雖崇奉外家，宜準附常典。 又親王墓戶十，梁、魯乃追贈，不可逾真王。 褒德衛卒，至逾宗廟，不可明甚，請罷之。 」又言：「比群臣務厚葬，以俑人象驂眩耀相矜，下逮眾庶，流宕成俗。 願按令切敕裁損，凡明器不許列衢路，惟陳墓所。 昏家盛設障車，擁道為戲樂，邀貨捐貲動萬計，甚傷化紊禮，不可示天下。 」事雖不從，議者美嘆。
When Emperor Zhongzong first performed the suburban sacrifice, National University rector Zhu Qinming and others, knowing Consort Wei could dominate the emperor, sought to flatter her. They memorialized that the empress perform the secondary offering and Princess Anle the final offering, and that in all four seasons and on successive emperors' birthdays envoys be sent to the tombs as though the dead were still living. Shao held these measures contrary to ritual propriety and argued against them on principle with unwavering firmness. The emperor also ordered guard households for the Wu clan tombs and all Wu family graves. Shao said, "The tombs of Emperors Hao and Shun have five hundred guard households, the same as Zhaoling." Under statute, tombs of former dynasties' emperors and kings have twenty guard households. Though the maternal kin are now honored, they should conform to the usual regulations. Princes' tombs have ten guard households. Liang and Lu were posthumous enfeoffments and cannot exceed true princes. The guard honoring virtue at Wei's tomb goes so far as to exceed what is due the ancestral temple. The impropriety could not be clearer. I ask that it be abolished. He also said, "Recently officials have competed in lavish burials, using figurines and images of outriders to dazzle and outdo one another, a practice that has spread even among common people and become a widespread custom." I ask that a strict edict be issued under statute to curb this excess: spirit objects must not be displayed along public roads but only at the grave site. Wedding families lavishly deploy barrier carts, blocking the roads for spectacle and amusement and soliciting gifts and donations that run into tens of thousands. This gravely harms public morals and disrupts ritual order and must not be displayed before the realm. Though his proposals were not adopted, commentators praised him with admiration.
11
睿宗即位，數言政損益，再遷給事中，兼太常少卿。 先天二年，玄宗講武驪山，紹以典儀坐失軍容，當斬。 帝怒甚，執纛下，左右猶冀少貸，金吾將軍李邈遽傳詔斬之。 時深咎邈，帝亦悔，俄詔罷邈官，擯於家。
After Ruizong took the throne, he repeatedly offered advice on improving government and was promoted again to supervising secretary, concurrently serving as vice minister of the grand imperial sacrifices. In the second year of Xiantian, when Xuanzong held military exercises at Lishan, Shao, as master of ceremonies, was charged with neglecting military decorum and sentenced to death. The emperor was furious and had him seized below the command banner. Those around him still hoped for some leniency, but General of the Golden Crow Li Miao hastily transmitted the edict and had him beheaded. At the time many blamed Miao severely, and the emperor himself soon regretted the matter. Before long he issued an edict stripping Miao of office and banishing him to his home.
12
張文瓘，字稚圭，貝州武城人。 隋大業末，徙家魏州之昌樂。 幼孤，事母、兄以孝友聞。 貞觀初，第明經，補并州參軍。 時李勣為長史，嘗嘆曰：「稚圭，今之管、蕭，吾所不及。 」勣入朝，文瓘與屬僚二人皆餞，勣贈二人以佩刀、玉帶，而不及文瓘。 文瓘以疑請，勣曰：「子無為嫌。 若某，豫少決，故贈以刀，欲其果於斷； 某放誕少檢，故贈以帶，俾其守約束。 若子才，無施不可，焉用贈？ 」因極推引。 再遷水部員外郎。 時兄文琮為戶部侍郎，於制，兄弟不並臺閣，出為雲陽令。 累授東西臺舍人，參知政事。 乾封二年，遷東臺侍郎、同東西臺三品，遂與勣同為宰相。 俄知左史事。
Zhang Wenjin, whose style name was Zhigui, came from Wucheng in Beizhou. Late in the Sui Daye era his family moved to Changle in Weizhou. Orphaned in youth, he cared for his mother and elder brother and was known for filial devotion and brotherly affection. Early in the Zhenguan reign he passed the Mingjing examination and was appointed military aide in Bingzhou. At the time Li Ji served as chief administrator and once sighed and said, "Zhigui is a Guan Zhong or Xiao He for our age—I cannot match him." When Ji went to court, Wenjin and two subordinate officials all saw him off. Ji gave the two men a girdle knife and a jade belt but gave Wenjin nothing. Wenjin asked about the omission, and Ji said, "Do not take offense, sir." One of them is by nature hesitant in judgment, so I gave him a knife, hoping he would learn to decide firmly; the other is unrestrained and lax in conduct, so I gave him a belt to help him keep himself in check. As for your talent, there is nothing you cannot do well—what need is there for a gift? He then recommended Wenjin to the fullest extent. He was soon promoted again to vice director of waterways. At the time his elder brother Wencong was vice minister of revenue. By regulation brothers could not serve together in the secretariat, so Wenjin was sent out as magistrate of Yunyang. He was repeatedly appointed secretariat drafter of the eastern and western bureaus and given a role in governing affairs. In the second year of Qianfeng he was promoted to vice director of the eastern bureau with third rank equal to the eastern and western bureaus, and thus became chancellor alongside Ji. Before long he was put in charge of the left historiographer's office.
13
時高宗造蓬萊、上陽、合璧等宮，復征討四夷，京師養廄馬萬匹，帑瓘浸虛。 文瓘諫曰：「王者養民，逸則富以康，勞則怨以叛。 秦、漢廣事四夷，造宮室，至二世土崩，武帝末年戶口減半。 夫制治於未亂，保邦於未危。 人罔常懷，懷於有仁。 臣願撫之，無使勞而生怨。 隋監未遠，不可不察。 」帝善其言，賜繒錦百段，為減廄馬數千。
At the time Emperor Gaozong was building the Penglai, Shangyang, Hebi, and other palaces, campaigning again against the frontier peoples, and maintaining ten thousand horses in the capital stables, while the treasury was steadily drained. Wenjin remonstrated, saying, "A ruler nourishes his people: when they are at ease they grow prosperous and secure; when they are overworked they grow resentful and rebel." Qin and Han both lavished effort on the frontier peoples and built palaces on a vast scale, until the Qin collapsed in the reign of the Second Emperor and in Emperor Wu's late years the registered population was cut in half. Order must be established before chaos arises, and the state preserved before danger appears. The people have no constant loyalty; they give their hearts to whoever shows benevolence. I beg that Your Majesty comfort them and not let overwork breed resentment. The lesson of Sui is still close at hand and must not be overlooked. The emperor approved his advice, granted him a hundred lengths of silk brocade, and reduced the stable herds by several thousand horses.
14
改黃門侍郎，兼太子右庶子，又兼大理卿。 不旬日，斷疑獄四百，抵罪者無怨言。 嘗有小疾，囚相與齋禱，願亟視事。 時以執法平恕方戴胄。 後拜侍中，兼太子賓客。 諸囚聞其遷，皆垂泣，其得人心如此。 性嚴正，未嘗回容，諸司奏議，悉心糾駁，故帝委之。 或時移疾，佗宰相奏事，帝必問與文瓘議未。 若不者，曰：「往共籌之。 」或曰：「已議。 」即皆報可。
He was transferred to vice director of the yellow gate, concurrently right vice guardian of the heir apparent, and again concurrently minister of justice. In less than ten days he resolved four hundred doubtful cases, and not one of those punished lodged a complaint. Once when he fell slightly ill, the prisoners together fasted and prayed that he would soon return to his duties. At the time he was ranked alongside Dai Zhou for impartial and lenient enforcement of the law. He was later appointed palace attendant, concurrently guest of the heir apparent. When the prisoners heard of his transfer they all wept—such was the depth of regard he had won. By nature he was stern and upright and never showed favoritism. On memorials from every office he scrutinized and corrected errors with care, and for this the emperor relied on him. Whenever he was ill, if other chancellors presented business the emperor always asked whether Wenjin had been consulted. If they had not, he would say, "Go discuss it with him together." Or they would say, "It has already been discussed." Then all reported their approval.
15
新羅叛，帝將出兵討之。 時文瓘病臥家，自力請見，曰：「吐蕃盜邊，兵屯境未解，新羅復叛，議者欲出師，二虜俱事，臣恐人不堪弊，請息兵修德，以懷異俗。 」詔可。
Silla rebelled, and the emperor was preparing to send an army to punish them. At that time Wenjin was ill and confined at home, but he forced himself to seek an audience and said, "The Tibetans are raiding our borders and troops are still stationed on the frontier without having been withdrawn. Silla has rebelled again, and those deliberating wish to send out an army. With both frontier foes engaged at once, I fear the people cannot bear the burden. I beg that we halt the armies and cultivate virtue, so as to win over foreign peoples by kindness." An edict approved his proposal.
16
初，同列以堂饌豐余，欲少損。 文瓘曰：「此天子所以重樞務、待賢才也，吾等若不任職，當自引避，不宜節減，以自取名。 」眾乃止。 卒，年七十三，贈幽州都督，謚曰懿。 以嘗事孝敬皇帝，詔陪葬恭陵。
At first his colleagues, finding the hall provisions abundant, wished to cut them back somewhat. Wenjin said, "This is how the Son of Heaven shows the weight of pivot affairs and treats worthy talent. If we are not equal to our duties we should withdraw on our own; it is not fitting to pare them down in order to win a reputation for thrift." The others then stopped. He died at seventy-three; posthumously he was made military governor of Youzhou, with the posthumous title Resolute. Because he had once served the Filial and Respectful Emperor, an edict ordered him buried at Gong Mausoleum.
17
四子：潛，為魏州刺史； 沛，同州刺史； 洽，衛尉卿； 涉，殿中監。 父子皆至三品，時謂「萬石張家」。 韋溫誅，涉為亂兵所殺。
He had four sons: Qian, who was prefect of Wei Prefecture; Pei, prefect of Tong Prefecture; He, minister of the guard; She, director of the palace secretariat. Father and sons all reached the third rank; the age called them "the Zhang family of ten thousand piculs." When Wei Wen was executed, She was killed by mutinous troops.
18
文琮，好自寫書，筆不釋手。 子弟諫止，曰：「吾好此，不為倦。 」貞觀中，為治書侍御史，遷亳州刺史。 永徽初，獻《文皇帝頌》，優制褒美，拜戶部侍郎。 坐房遺愛從母弟，出為建州刺史。 州尚淫祀，不立社稷，文琮下教曰：「春秋二社本於農，今此州廢不立，尚何觀？ 比歲田畝卒荒，或未之思乎！ 神在於敬，可以致福。 」於是始建祀場，民悅從之。 卒於官。
Wenzong loved to copy books himself and never put down his brush. His sons and younger kin admonished him to stop, but he said, "I delight in this and am not wearied by it." In the Zhenguan era he served as director of the imperial library on punishments, then was transferred to prefect of Bo Prefecture. At the beginning of Yonghui he presented "A Eulogy to Emperor Wen," received an excellent edict of praise, and was appointed vice minister of revenue. Because he was a maternal cousin of Fang Yi'ai, he was sent out as prefect of Jian Prefecture. The prefecture still favored licentious cults and had not established the altars of soil and grain. Wenzong issued an instruction: "The spring and autumn twin sacrifices have their root in agriculture; now this prefecture has abandoned them and not set them up—what is there to look to? In recent years the fields have ended in desolation—has no one thought of this? The spirits dwell in reverence; through reverence blessing may be attained. Thereupon they first built the sacrifice grounds, and the people gladly followed. He died in office.
19
子錫，久視初，為鳳閣侍郎、同鳳閣鸞臺平章事，代其甥李嶠為宰相。 請還廬陵王，不為張易之所右。 與鄭杲俱知選，坐泄禁中語，又賕謝鉅萬，時蘇味道亦坐事，同被訊，系鳳閣，俄徙司刑三品院。 錫按轡專道，神氣不懾，日膳豐鮮，無損貶。 味道徒步赴逮，席地菜食。 武後聞之，釋味道，將斬錫，既而流循州。 神龍中，累遷工部尚書，兼修國史，東都留守。 韋後臨朝，詔同中書門下三品，旬日，出為絳州刺史。 累封平原郡公，卒。
His son Xi: at the beginning of the Jiushi era he was vice director of the phoenix pavilion and associate director of the phoenix pavilion and crane terrace, with the status of grand councillor, succeeding his nephew Li Jiao as chancellor. He petitioned for the return of the Prince of Luling and would not yield to Zhang Yizhi. Together with Zheng Gao he oversaw selection. He was convicted of divulging palace matters and also of accepting bribes in the tens of thousands. At the time Su Weidao was also implicated; both were interrogated, detained at the phoenix pavilion, and soon moved to the third-rank courtyard of the ministry of justice. Xi rode holding the reins on the privileged lane, his bearing unshaken; his daily meals remained rich and fresh, without any curtailment. Weidao went on foot to his arrest and ate plain vegetables sitting on the ground. When Empress Wu heard of it she released Weidao and was about to execute Xi, but in the end exiled him to Xun Prefecture. In the Shenlong era he was repeatedly promoted to minister of works, concurrently revising the national history, and left in charge of the eastern capital. When Empress Wei held court, an edict made him of the same rank as the three departments at the secretariat and chancellery; within ten days he was sent out as prefect of Jiang Prefecture. He was repeatedly enfeoffed as Duke of Pingyuan Commandery and died.
20
文琮從父弟文收，終太子率更令。 善音律，著《新樂書》十餘篇。
Wenzong's paternal cousin Wenshou ended as chief commandant of rate revision for the heir apparent. Skilled in pitch and regulation, he authored more than ten chapters of the New Book of Music.
21
徐有功，名弘敏，避孝敬皇帝諱，以字行，國子博士文遠孫也。 舉明經，累補蒲州司法參軍，襲封東莞縣男。 為政仁，不忍杖罰，民服其恩，更相約曰：「犯徐參軍杖者，必斥之。 」訖代不辱一人。 累遷司刑丞。 時武後僭位，畏唐大臣謀己。 於是周興、來俊臣、丘神勣、王弘義等揣識後指，置總監牧院諸獄，捕將相，俾相鉤逮，掩搦護送，楚掠凝慘。 又汙引天下豪桀，馳使者即按，一切以反論。 吏爭以周內窮詆相高，後輒勸以官賞，於是以急變相告言者無虛日。 朝野震恐，莫敢正言，獨有功數犯顏爭枉直，後厲語折抑，有功爭益牢。 時博州刺史瑯邪王沖，責息錢於貴鄉，遣家奴督斂，與尉顏餘慶相聞知，奴自市弓矢還。 會沖坐逆誅，魏州人告餘慶豫沖謀，後令俊臣鞫治，以反狀聞。 有司議：「餘慶更永昌赦，法當流。 」侍御史魏元忠謂：「餘慶為沖督償、通書，合謀明甚，非曰支黨，請殊死，籍其家。 」詔可。 有功曰：「永昌赦令：『與虺貞同惡，魁首已伏誅，支黨未發者原之。 』《書》曰：『殲厥渠魁』，律以『造意為首』，尋赦已伏語，則魁首無遺。 餘慶赦後被言，是謂支黨。 今以支為首，是以生入死。 赦而復罪，不如勿赦； 生而復殺，不如勿生。 竊謂朝廷不當爾。 」後怒曰：「何謂魁首？ 」答曰：「魁者，大帥； 首者，元謀。 」後曰：「餘慶安得不為魁首？ 」答曰：「若魁首者，虺貞是已。 既已伏誅，餘慶今方論罪，非支黨何？ 」後意解，乃曰：「公更思之。 」遂免死。 當此時，左右及衛仗在廷陛者數百人，皆縮項不敢息，而有功氣定言詳，然不橈。
Xu Yougong—his name Hongmin; to avoid the taboo of the Filial and Respectful Emperor he went by his style-name—was grandson of the erudite of the imperial academy Xu Wenyuan. He passed the classics examination, was repeatedly appointed judicial administrator of Pu Prefecture, and inherited enfeoffment as baron of Dongguan County. His governance was humane and he could not bear the rod. The people submitted to his kindness and made a pact among themselves: "Whoever would have Administrative Assistant Xu flogged must be driven away." By the end of his tenure not a single person had been disgraced by beating. He was repeatedly promoted to vice director of the ministry of justice. At the time Empress Wu usurped the throne and feared that Tang grandees would plot against her. Thereupon Zhou Xing, Lai Junchen, Qiu Shenji, Wang Hongyi, and others divined the empress's intent and established the various prisons of the general supervisor's pasture court. They seized generals and chancellors, made them implicate one another, covered and seized them for escort, and torture grew ever more cruel and ghastly. They also defiled and implicated the empire's bold spirits, galloping envoys to investigate on the spot; everything was judged as treason. Officials vied to outdo one another in exhaustive investigation and slander; the empress would then encourage them with offices and rewards—thus there was no day without urgent denunciations and mutual informing. Court and countryside trembled in terror and none dared speak plainly. Only Yougong repeatedly offended her countenance to dispute wrong from right. The empress rebuked him with harsh words, yet Yougong argued all the more firmly. At the time the Prince of Langye, Wang Chong, as prefect of Bo, was collecting interest money at Gui Township and sent a household slave to press for payment. He had exchanges with the assistant magistrate Yan Yuqing, and the slave returned after buying bows and arrows on his own. When Chong was executed for rebellion, men of Wei Prefecture reported that Yuqing had foreknown Chong's plot. The empress ordered Junchen to interrogate and investigate, and he reported the facts of treason. The relevant offices deliberated: "Yuqing again falls under the Yongchang amnesty; by law he should be banished." Remonstrance secretary Wei Yuanzhong said, "Yuqing for Chong pressed repayment and exchanged letters—the conspiracy is abundantly clear. He is not called a branch follower; I request extraordinary death and confiscation of his household." The edict approved. Yougong said, "The Yongchang amnesty order states: 'Those who shared evil with Hui Zhen—if the ringleaders have already been put to death, branch followers who have not yet been exposed are forgiven.'" The Documents say, 'Exterminate their leader'; the code takes 'the one who originated the intent as chief.' If we follow the amnesty's words 'have already submitted,' then no ringleader remains. Yuqing was denounced after the amnesty—that is what is called a branch follower. Now to take a branch follower as the chief is to bring the living into death. To pardon and then punish again would be better not to have pardoned at all; to give life and then kill again would be better not to have given life at all. I venture to say the court should not act in this way. The empress said angrily, "What do you mean by ringleader?" He answered, "Kui means the great commander; shou means the prime plotter." The empress said, "How can Yuqing not be the ringleader?" He answered, "If there is a ringleader, it is Hui Zhen." Since they have already been executed, Yuqing is only now being judged for his crime—if he is not a branch follower, what is he? The empress's mind eased and she said, "Sir, think again." He was then spared from death. At this time the attendants at left and right and the guard troops on the court steps numbered in the hundreds; all shrank their necks and dared not breathe, yet Yougong's bearing was calm, his words measured, and he did not bend.
22
有韓紀孝者，受徐敬業偽官，前已物故，推事使顧仲琰籍其家，詔已報可。 有功追議曰：「律，謀反者斬。 身亡即無斬法，無斬法則不得相緣。 所緣之人亡，則所因之罪減。 」詔從之，皆以更赦免，如此獲宥者數十百姓。
There was a Han Jixiao who had accepted a false office from Xu Jingye and had already died. The commissioner of investigation Gu Zhongyan was registering his household, and the edict had already reported approval. Yougong pursued deliberation and said, "In the code, those who plot rebellion are beheaded." When the person is dead there is no statute for beheading; without a statute for beheading there can be no successive punishment. When the person connected in the chain is dead, the crime from which it derives is reduced. The edict followed his view; all were spared through the next amnesty—thus several tens of families gained pardon.
23
累轉秋官郎中。 鳳閣侍郎任知古、冬官尚書裴行本等七人被誣當死，後謂宰相曰：「古人以殺止殺，我今以恩止殺，就群公丐知古等，賜以再生，可乎？ 」俊臣、張知默固請如法，後不許。 俊臣獨引行本更驗前罪。 有功奏曰：「俊臣違陛下再生之賜，不可以示信。 」於是悉免死。
He was repeatedly transferred to director of the autumn office. Phoenix pavilion vice director Ren Zhigu, minister of winter works Pei Xingben, and seven others were falsely accused and condemned to death. The empress said to the chancellor, "The ancients stopped killing with killing; I today will stop killing with grace. From you gentlemen I beg Zhigu and the rest—grant them rebirth—is that possible?" Junchen and Zhang Zhimuo firmly requested execution according to law, but the empress would not permit it. Junchen alone drew Xingben back to verify prior crimes. Yougong memorialized, "Junchen violates Your Majesty's gift of rebirth and cannot be shown as trustworthy." Thereupon all were spared from death.
24
道州刺史李仁褒兄弟為人誣構，有功爭不能得。 秋官侍郎周興劾之曰：「漢法，附下罔上者斬，面欺者亦斬。 在古，析言破律者殺。 有功故出反囚，罪當誅，請按之。 」後不許，猶坐史官。
The prefect of Dao Prefecture Li Renbao and his brothers were falsely framed by others; Yougong disputed on their behalf but could not prevail. Autumn office vice director Zhou Xing impeached him, saying, "Han law: those who attach below and deceive above are beheaded; those who deceive to one's face are also beheaded." In antiquity, those who split words to break the code were killed. Yougong deliberately released treason prisoners; the crime merits execution—I request investigation. The empress did not permit it, but he still suffered demotion as a history official.
25
俄起為左肅政臺侍御史，辭曰：「臣聞鹿走山林而命系庖廚者，勢固自然。 陛下以法官用臣，臣守正行法，必坐此死矣。 」後固授之。 天下聞有功復進，灑然相賀。 時有詔：「公坐流、私坐徒以上會赦免，逾百日不首者，復論。 」有功奏曰：「陛下寬殊死罪，已發者原之，是通改過之心、自新之路。 故律，告赦前事，以其罪坐之。 若無告言，所犯終不自發； 如告言赦前事，則與律乖。 今赦前之罪，不自言者，還以法論，即恩雖布天下，而一罪不能貸，臣竊為陛下不取。 」後更詔五品以上議可。
Soon he was raised as attending censor of the left office for scrutinizing government, but he declined, saying, "Your servant has heard that when a deer runs in mountain forests yet its life is tied to the kitchen, the tendency is of itself so." Your Majesty employs your servant as a judge; your servant keeps to the upright and executes the law—I am certain to die for this. The empress firmly appointed him anyway. When all under Heaven heard that Yougong had been promoted again, they rejoiced together in relief. At the time there was an edict: "For public crimes punished by exile and private crimes punished by hard labor and above, meeting an amnesty grants exemption; those who do not confess within one hundred days are prosecuted again." Yougong memorialized, "Your Majesty has eased extraordinary death sentences and forgives those already exposed—that opens the mind to reform wrongdoing and the road to renewal." Therefore in the code, reporting pre-amnesty matters assigns guilt according to the crime. If there is no report, the offense in the end will not expose itself; if one reports pre-amnesty matters, it clashes with the code. Now pre-amnesty crimes for those who do not confess themselves are still judged by law—then though grace is spread over all under Heaven, a single crime cannot be remitted. Your servant ventures that Your Majesty should not take this course. The empress then ordered officials of the fifth rank and above to deliberate and approve.
26
又上疏曰：「天下員有定，比選者日多，選曹諉囑公行，囂謗滿路。 唐季人多逆節，鞫訊結斷，刑慘獄嚴，革命歲久，其流弗改。 事表生情，法外構理，而刻薄吏驅扇成奸。 雖朝堂進表，列匭內牒，叫閽弗聽，叩鼓弗聞，使申其冤，正增其枉。 誠令天官銓註有所不平、法司推斷舞法深詆、三司理匭受所上章擁塞不白者皆許臣按驗劾發，奪祿貶勞，不越月逾時，可致刑措。 」後納之。
He also submitted a memorial, saying, "Empire-wide posts have a fixed count, yet daily the candidates for selection grow. The selection bureau shrugs off its duties in public, and clamor and slander fill the roads." In the troubled close of Tang many men turned against propriety. Interrogation and closing of cases—the punishments were cruel and the prisons severe. Since the revolution many years have passed, yet the drift has not changed. Matters show emotion on the surface; outside the code they construct reasoning, while harsh petty officials drive and fan them into treachery. Though they advance memorials in the court hall, file petitions in the accusation box, cry at the gate and go unheard, beat the drum and go unheeded—if they are allowed to state their grievances, they only add to their injustice. If Your Majesty truly permits me to investigate and impeach wherever the heavenly office's selections are unfair, wherever judicial offices twist the law in deep calumny, and wherever the three offices' petition box blocks memorials from being reported—stripping salaries and demoting offenders to labor service—then within a month or season punishments may be set aside altogether. The empress accepted his proposal.
27
竇孝諶妻龐為其奴怖以妖祟，教為夜解，因告以厭詛。 給事中薛季昶鞫之，龐當死。 子希瑊訟冤，有功明其枉。 季昶劾有功黨惡逆，當棄市。 有功方視事，令史泣以告。 有功曰：「豈吾獨死，而諸人長不死邪？ 」安步去。 後召詰曰：「公比斷獄多失出，何耶？ 」對曰：「失出，臣小過； 好生，陛下大德。 」後默然。 龐得減死，有功免為民。
Pang, wife of Dou Xiaochen, was frightened by her slave with talk of demonic portents. The slave taught her night rituals of release and then reported her for practicing curse-magic. Supervising secretary Xue Jichang interrogated the case, and Pang was sentenced to death. Her son Xizhen appealed the injustice, and Yougong showed that she had been wronged. Jichang impeached Yougong as partisan to wicked rebellion and demanded he be executed in the market. Yougong was just attending to business when a clerk came weeping to tell him. Yougong said, "How is it that I alone should die while all others live on?" He walked away at an easy pace. The empress summoned and questioned him, saying, "Sir, of late in judging cases you often err on the side of release—why is that?" He answered, "Erring on the side of release is your servant's small fault; cherishing life is Your Majesty's great virtue." The empress fell silent. Pang's sentence was reduced from death, and Yougong was spared and reduced to commoner status.
28
起拜左司郎中，轉司刑少卿。 與皇甫文備同按獄，誣有功縱逆黨。 久之，文備坐事下獄，有功出之。 或曰：「彼嘗陷君於死，今生之，何也？ 」對曰：「爾所言者私忿，我所守者公法，不可以私害公。」
He was raised and appointed director of the left bureau, then transferred to vice minister of justice. Together with Huangfu Wenbei he reviewed cases and falsely accused Yougong of releasing rebel partisans. Before long Wenbei was implicated and cast into prison, and Yougong secured his release. Someone said, "He once tried to have you killed—why do you now save his life?" He answered, "What you speak of is private resentment; what I uphold is public law—private feeling must not harm the public good."
29
嘗謂所親曰：「大理，人命所系，不可阿旨詭辭，以求茍免。 」故有功為獄，常持平守正，以執據冤罔，凡三坐大辟，將死，泰然不憂，赦之，亦不喜，後以此重之。 所全活甚眾，酷吏為少衰，然疾之如仇矣。 改司仆少卿。 卒，年六十八，贈司刑卿。 中宗即位，加贈越州都督，遣使就第吊祭，賜物百段，授一子官。 開元初，竇希瑊等請以己官讓有功子惀，以報舊德，由是自大理司直遷恭陵令。 會昌中，追謚忠正。
He once said to those close to him, "The court of review is where human lives are bound. One must not bend to intent with twisted words merely to seek a shabby escape." Therefore in handling prisons Yougong constantly held the balance and kept to the upright, seizing on the basis of wrongful imprisonment. Three times he was liable to capital punishment; facing death he was calm and untroubled, and when pardoned he was not glad. The empress thereafter valued him all the more for this. The number he preserved alive was very great, and harsh officials somewhat declined, yet they hated him as an enemy. He was transferred to vice minister of the court of the imperial stud. He died at sixty-eight and was posthumously enfeoffed as minister of justice. When Emperor Zhongzong took the throne he was further posthumously made military governor of Yuezhou. Envoys were sent to his residence for mourning sacrifices, a hundred lengths of goods were granted, and one son was given office. At the beginning of the Kaiyuan era Dou Xizhen and others asked to yield their own offices to Yougong's son Lun in repayment of his old kindness. Thereby Lun rose from straight officer of the court of review to magistrate of Gong Mausoleum. In the Huichang era he was posthumously titled Loyal and Upright.
30
初，鹿城主簿潘好禮慕有功為人，論之曰：「昔稱張釋之為廷尉，天下無冤人，今有功斷獄，亦天下無冤人。 然釋之當漢文帝時，中外無事，守法而已。 有功居革命之際，周興、來俊臣等掩義隱賊，崇飾惡言，以誣盛德，有功守死明道，身濱殆者數矣，此其賢於釋之明甚。 」或稱有功仁恕過漢於、張。 起居舍人盧若虛曰：「徐公當雷霆之震，而能全仁恕，雖千載未見其比。 」五世孫商。
Initially Pan Haoli, chief clerk of Lucheng, admired Yougong's character and said, "In the past Zhang Shizhi as court commandant was said to leave the realm without wronged men; now Yougong in judging cases also leaves the realm without wronged men." Yet Shizhi served under Emperor Wen of Han, when within and without the realm was at peace—he merely kept the law. Yougong lived in an age of revolution, when Zhou Xing, Lai Junchen, and others covered righteousness and hid villainy, exalting evil words to slander great virtue. Yougong held to death in defense of the bright Way and several times came to the brink of peril—here his worth clearly far exceeds Shizhi's. Some said Yougong's benevolence and forbearance surpassed those of the Han officials Yu Ding and Zhang Tang. Diarist Lu Ruoxu said, "Lord Xu faced the thunderbolt's shock yet preserved benevolence and forbearance whole—in a thousand years his like has not been seen." His fifth-generation descendant was Shang.
31
贊曰：「徐有功不以唐、周貳其心，惟一於法，身蹈死以救人之死，故能處猜後、酷吏之間，以恕自將，內挫虐焰，不使天下殘於燎，可謂仁人也哉！ 議者謂過漢於、張，渠不信夫！
The encomium says, "Xu Yougong did not divide his heart between Tang and Zhou—he was single-minded in the law and himself trod death to save others from death. Thus he could dwell between a suspicious empress and harsh officials, taking forbearance as his guide, inwardly blunting the flame of cruelty so that the realm was not maimed by the blaze. He may truly be called a man of benevolence!" Commentators say he surpassed the Han officials Yu and Zhang—how can one not believe it!
32
商，字義聲，或字秋卿，客新鄭再世，因為新鄭人。 幼隱中條山。 擢進士第。 大中時，擢累尚書左丞。 宣宗詔為巡邊使，使有指，拜河中節度使。 突厥殘種保特峨山，以千帳度河自歸，詔商綏定。 商表處山東寬鄉，置備征軍，凡千人，襞紙為鎧，勁矢不能洞。 徙節山南東道，襄多山棚，為票賊，商取材卒為捕盜將，別為屯營，寇所發，輒跡捕，捕必得，遂為精兵。 江西都將反，韋宙乘傳抵山南發兵，商命部將韓季友以捕盜營士往。 賊平，宙表留季友所部為綱紀。 咸通初，以刑部尚書為諸道鹽鐵轉運使，封東莞縣子。 四年，進同中書門下平章事，出為荊南節度使。 累進太子太保，卒。
Shang, whose style name was Yisheng or also Qiuqing, had been a guest in Xinzheng for two generations and thus came to be regarded as a man of Xinzheng. In youth he lived in seclusion on Zhongtiao Mountain. He passed the jinshi examination. During the Dazhong era he was repeatedly promoted to left vice director of the secretariat. Emperor Xuanzong appointed him frontier inspection commissioner, and when his mission proved successful he was made military commissioner of Hezhong. Remnant Turks held Mount E. With a thousand tents they crossed the river to submit voluntarily, and an edict ordered Shang to pacify and settle them. Shang memorialized to settle them in broad townships east of the mountains and established a reserve expeditionary army of a thousand men who folded paper into armor that even strong arrows could not pierce. He was transferred to command Shannan East Circuit. Xiang had many mountain stockade communities who became fierce bandits. Shang selected able soldiers as bandit-capture generals and set up separate encampments; wherever bandits struck he traced and seized them, capture was certain, and they became elite troops. A chief general of Jiangxi rebelled. Wei Zhou took relay post-horses to Shannan to raise troops, and Shang ordered his subordinate general Han Jiyou to go with the bandit-capture camp soldiers. When the rebels were pacified, Zhou memorialized to retain Jiyou's command as a local discipline force. At the beginning of the Xiantong era, as minister of punishments he was made salt and iron transport commissioner for all circuits and enfeoffed as viscount of Dongguan County. In the fourth year he was advanced to associate director of the secretariat and chancellery and sent out as military commissioner of Jingnan. He was repeatedly advanced to grand guardian of the heir apparent and died.
33
子彥若，事僖宗為中書舍人。 昭宗立，再用為御史中丞。 張浚師敗太原，以彥若為戶部侍郎、同中書門下平章事。 俄代李茂貞為鳳翔節度使，不得入，還為御史大夫。 乾寧初，復當國，進位太保、齊國公。 崔胤專政，以彥若位己右，不悅，以平章事為清海軍節度使，卒於鎮，而行軍司馬劉隱因主留務。 方時多難，彥若最見信於帝，有以事自陳者，帝曰：「汝當問彥若。 」其所倚任如此。
His son Yanruo served Emperor Xizong as secretariat drafter. When Emperor Zhaozong took the throne he was again appointed censor-in-chief. When Zhang Jun's army was defeated at Taiyuan, Yanruo was made vice minister of revenue and associate director of the secretariat and chancellery. Before long he replaced Li Maozhen as military commissioner of Fengxiang but could not take up the post and returned as grand censor. At the beginning of the Qianning era he again held power, advanced to grand guardian and Duke of Qi. Cui Yin monopolized government and, displeased that Yanruo outranked him, sent him out as military commissioner of the Qinghai army while retaining his grand councillor title. Yanruo died in the command, and the army marching marshal Liu Yin thereby took charge of remaining affairs. In that troubled age Yanruo was most trusted by the emperor. When someone presented a matter on his own behalf the emperor said, "You should ask Yanruo." Such was the reliance the emperor placed in him.
