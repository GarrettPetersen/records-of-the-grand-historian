__FORCETOC__崔融崔融，字安成，齊州全節人。 擢八科高第。 累補宮門丞、崇文館學士。 中宗為太子時，選侍讀，典東朝章疏。 武后幸嵩高，見融銘《啟母碣》，歎美之。 及已封，即命銘《朝覲碑》。 授著作佐郎，遷右史，進鳳閣舍人。 時有司議關市，行人盡征之，融上疏謂：「《周官》九賦，其七曰關市。 以市多淫巧，而關通末遊，欲止抑之，故加稅耳。 然唯斂工商，而不及往來。 今一切通取，則事不師古。 且四人異業舊矣，復動而搖之。 市者，兼受善惡也。 若甚，則細人無所容，細人無所容，久必為亂。 天下之關必險道，市必要津，豪宗、惡少在焉，聞一旦變法，或致騷動，恐南走蠻，北走狄。 今江津、河滸列鋪率稅，檢覆稽留，加主司僦略邀丐，則商人廢業。 魏、晉、齊、隋所不行，況陛下乎？ 有如師興費廣，雖倍算商旅、加斂齊人可也。 」後納之。
Cui Rong — Cui Rong, styled Ancheng, came from Quanjie in Qizhou. He passed the eight-categories examination with the highest standing. He rose through posts as Palace Gate Deputy and academician of the Chongwen Hall. While Zhongzong was still crown prince, he was selected as a study companion and oversaw the eastern palace's memorials and reports. When Empress Wu visited Mount Song, she read Rong's inscription on the Qimu stele and marveled at it. Once she had received her enfeoffment, she at once commissioned him to inscribe the Chaojing stele. He was made Assistant in the Bureau of Historiography, then promoted to Right Historian and advanced to Drafting Attendant of the Fengge Office. When officials proposed levying market tolls on all travelers without exception, Rong submitted a memorial: "In the Zhou Officials, of the Nine Levies, the seventh is market tolls. Markets abound in clever luxuries, and the passes draw off into minor trades; the tax was meant to curb and suppress that, not to burden everyone who passed through. Yet it was levied only on artisans and merchants, not on travelers passing through. To collect from everyone alike now would be to abandon the ancient precedent. Moreover the four classes of people have long had their separate trades; to stir them up again would unsettle them. A market takes in both the worthy and the base. Press it too hard and the lowly have nowhere to stand; when they have nowhere to stand, disorder will follow in time. The empire's passes stand on dangerous roads and its markets on vital crossings, where powerful clans and rowdies gather. Word of an abrupt change in the law could spark unrest, and they might flee south to the barbarians or north to the Di. Today at river crossings and along the banks, tax posts levy at a fixed rate, inspections hold travelers up, and supervising officials extort and beg on the side—until merchants give up their trade altogether. Wei, Jin, Qi, and Sui never went this far—how much less should Your Majesty? If armies must be raised and costs mount, then even doubling tolls on traveling merchants and levying extra on the people of Qi would be tolerable. " The court later adopted his advice.
2
張易之兄弟頗延文學士，融與李嶠、蘇味道、麟台少監王紹宗降節佞附。 易之誅，貶袁州刺史。 召授國子司業。 與脩《武后實錄》勞，封清河縣子。 融為文華婉，當時未有輩者。 朝廷大筆，多手敕委之，其《洛出寶圖頌》尤工。 譔《武后哀冊》最高麗，絕筆而死，時謂思苦神竭雲。 年五十四。 贈衛州刺史，諡曰文。 膳部員外郎杜審言為融所獎引，為服緦麻。
The Zhang Yizhi brothers cultivated literary men about them; Rong, Li Jiao, Su Weidao, and Vice Director of the Lin Terrace Wang Shaozong all humbled themselves to flatter and court them. After Yizhi was put to death, Rong was demoted to prefect of Yuanzhou. He was recalled and appointed Vice Director of the Directorate of Education. For helping compile the Veritable Records of Empress Wu, he was enfeoffed as Viscount of Qinghe. Rong wrote in a florid, graceful style unmatched in his day. The court's weightiest compositions were often assigned to him by autograph edict; his "Eulogy on the Treasure Chart Emerging from the Luo" was especially fine. He composed the Lament for Empress Wu in the loftiest style and died the moment he set down his brush; people said his mind had been wrung dry and his spirit spent. He was fifty-four. He was posthumously appointed Prefect of Weizhou and given the posthumous title Wen. Du Shenyan of the Board of Provisions, whom Rong had championed, wore the finest grade of mourning for him.

六子，其聞者禹錫、翹。 禹錫，開元中，中書舍人，贈定州刺史，諡曰貞。 翹，禮部尚書，贈荊州大都督，諡曰成。
He had six sons; those who became known were Yuxi and Qiao. Yuxi served as Secretariat Drafting Attendant under Kaiyuan and was posthumously made Prefect of Dingzhou with the posthumous title Zhen. Qiao rose to Minister of Rites and was posthumously made Grand Protector-General of Jingzhou with the posthumous title Cheng.
4
孫巨，右補闕，亦有文。 曾孫從曾孫從。 從字子乂，少孤貧，與兄能偕隱太原山中。 會歲饑，拾橡實以飯，講學不廢。 擢進士第。 從山南嚴震府為推官，以母喪免。 兄弟廬墓，手藝松柏。 喪闋，不應辟命。 久之，韋皋引為西山運務使。 奏遷判官，攝守邛州。 前刺史有以盜繫獄，辭已具。 從疑其冤，縱不治，俄得真盜。 皋卒，劉辟反，欲並東川。 從以書諭止辟，辟怒，從乃募兵嬰城守。 辟方悉兵拒高崇文，戰而敗，從完州自如。 盧坦表宣州副使。
His grandson Ju served as Right Remonstrance Censor and was likewise accomplished in letters. His great-grandson was Cong. Cong, styled Ziyi, lost his father early and grew up poor; he and his elder brother Neng withdrew together to the mountains of Taiyuan. In a year of famine they lived on gathered acorns yet never stopped their lectures and study. He passed the jinshi examination. Cong served on Yan Zhen's staff in Shannan as investigating officer until he left office to mourn his mother. The brothers kept vigil by the tomb and planted pines and cypresses with their own hands. When mourning ended, he declined every summons to office. Eventually Wei Gao brought him in as Commissioner for Western Mountain transport. He was promoted to staff judge and served as acting prefect of Qiongzhou. The former prefect sat in prison on a theft charge with a confession already on record. Cong suspected a miscarriage of justice, released the man without proceeding, and soon caught the real thief. After Gao died, Liu Pi rebelled and sought to annex Dongchuan. Cong wrote urging Pi to desist; when Pi flew into a rage, Cong raised troops and held the city. Pi had thrown all his forces against Gao Chongwen, lost the battle, and Cong's prefecture emerged unscathed. Lu Tan recommended him for the post of Vice Commissioner of Xuanzhou.
5
入為殿中侍御史，遷吏部員外郎。 異時，史給選者成牒，以先後丐賕，從一限出之，後遂為法。 裴度為御史中丞，奏以右司郎中知雜事。 度已相，代為中丞。 所彈治，不屈權幸。 事系台閣而付仗內者，必請還有司。 薦引御史，務取質重廉退者。 李翛以寵得京兆尹，為莊憲太后山陵橋道使，務以減末徭費為功，至不治道輴車留渭橋，久不得進。 從三劾之，無少貸。
He entered the capital as Palace Attending Censor and was promoted to Vice Director of the Ministry of Personnel. Formerly clerks handing out appointment documents solicited bribes according to who came first; Cong released them all on a single deadline, and the practice thereafter became law. When Pei Du served as Censor-in-Chief, he had Cong, as Right Department Director, take charge of miscellaneous censorial business. Once Du had become chancellor, Cong succeeded him as Censor-in-Chief. In impeachment and prosecution he never bowed to the powerful or the favored. Whenever a case belonged to the Censorate but was handed to the inner palace guards, he insisted it be returned to the proper offices. In recommending censors he sought men of solid character who were retiring and incorrupt. Li Xiao won favor and became Metropolitan Governor of Jingzhao and Commissioner for the bridges and roads at Empress Zhuangxian's mausoleum; he prided himself on trimming minor corvée costs until he neglected the road and the imperial hearse was stranded at Wei Bridge for a long time. Cong impeached him three times and showed no leniency whatsoever.
6
俄授陝虢觀察使。 遷尚書右丞。 王承宗請割德、棣而遣子入侍也，憲宗選堪使者，以命從。 議者謂承宗狠譎，非單使可屈。 次魏，田弘正請以五百騎從，辭之，惟童騎十數，疾趨鎮。 集軍士球場宣詔，為陳逆順大節禍福之效，音辭暢厲，士感動，承宗自失，貌愈恭，至泣下，即按二州戶口、符印上之。 還為山南西道節度使。 帝欲遂相，監軍使揣知，為用事者求金，從不肯答，用是不得相。 長慶初，繇尚書左丞領鄜坊節度。 屬部多神策屯軍，數亂法驕橫，吏不能制，從一繩以法，下皆重足畏之。 党項互市羊馬，類先遺帥守，從獨不取，而厚慰待之，羌不敢盜境。 寶曆初，為東都留守。 故事，留司官入宮城門列晨衙見留守。 吏誕傲，久廢，至是復行。
He was soon appointed Observation Commissioner of Shan-Guo. He was promoted to Right Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs. When Wang Chengzong offered to surrender De and Di and send his son to court, Xianzong chose a capable envoy and sent Cong. Critics said Chengzong was fierce and treacherous and could not be swayed by a lone envoy. At Wei, Tian Hongzheng offered five hundred horsemen as escort; Cong declined and rode on with only a dozen youths, pressing swiftly to the commandery seat. He gathered the troops on the drill ground to proclaim the edict and set forth the great moral of loyalty and rebellion and the wages of fortune and ruin; his voice rang clear and stern, the soldiers were stirred, Chengzong lost his composure, grew ever more deferential, even to tears, and at once surrendered the household registers and seals of the two prefectures. On his return he was made Military Governor of Shannan West Circuit. The emperor meant to make him chancellor; the army supervisory commissioner guessed as much and solicited gold on behalf of the power-holders; Cong would not comply, and for that reason never reached the chancellorship. Early in Changqing he moved from Left Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs to command the Bian-Fang circuit. His jurisdiction held many encamped Shence troops who repeatedly broke the law in arrogant defiance and whom local officials could not restrain; Cong applied the law without exception, and all below walked in fear of him. When Tanguts traded sheep and horses at the border markets, prefects and garrison commanders usually took gifts first; Cong alone refused gifts and treated them generously instead, and the Qiang did not dare raid his frontier. Early in Baoli he was appointed Eastern Capital Regent. By precedent, resident officials entered the palace gate and lined up for the morning audience with the regent. Clerks had grown arrogant and the practice had long lapsed; Cong restored it.
7
召拜戶部尚書。 宰相李宗閔以從裴度、李德裕所善，內不喜。 從求致仕，除太子賓客，分司東都，告滿百日去。 於是眾嘩語不平，宗閔懼，復授檢校尚書左僕射、淮南節度副大使，知節度事。 揚州凡交易貲產、奴婢有貫率錢，畜羊有口算，又貿曲牟其贏，以佐用度，從皆蠲除之。 官吏俸帛常加倍以給，獨節度使則否，從皆與之同。 大和六年卒，年七十二。 下有刲股肉以祭者。 贈司空，諡曰貞。
He was summoned and appointed Minister of Revenue. Chancellor Li Zongmin disliked Cong inwardly because Pei Du and Li Deyu favored him. Cong asked to retire and was made Guest of the Heir Apparent with a commission at the Eastern Capital; after the hundred-day notice he left office. The public clamored in protest; Zongmin grew alarmed and reappointed him Acting Left Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs and Vice Military Governor of Huainan with charge of circuit affairs. At Yangzhou every transaction in goods or property and every sale of slaves carried a standard levy, sheep were taxed by the head, and officials traded in yeast to skim profits for expenses; Cong abolished all of it. Officials' salary silks were commonly paid at double the standard, but military governors alone were not; Cong paid himself the same as everyone else. He died in the sixth year of Dahe, at seventy-two. Some of his subordinates cut flesh from their thighs for funeral sacrifice. He was posthumously appointed Minister of Works with the posthumous title Zhen.
8
從為人嚴偉，立朝棱棱有風望，不喜交權利，忠厚而讓。 階品當立門戟，終不請。 位方鎮，內無聲妓娛玩。 士大夫賢之。 曾孫能能，字子才。 硃泚之亂，渾瑊以朔方軍戰武功，引佐幕府。 進累侍御史。 河東鄭儋表為判官。 累遷黔中觀察使，以讒坐貶。 從為中丞，奏以自代。 繇將作監授嶺南節度使，與從皆秉節居鎮，世傳為榮。 卒，年六十八，贈禮部尚書。
Cong was stern and imposing; in court he carried himself with sharp dignity, shunned dealings in power and profit, and was loyal, generous, and self-effacing. His rank entitled him to ceremonial gate halberds, but he never asked for them. Though he held a frontier command, he kept no singing girls or diversions within his household. Scholar-officials held him in esteem. His great-grandson was Neng — Neng, styled Zicai. During Zhu Ci's rebellion, Hun Jian fought at Wugong with the Shuofang army and brought Neng onto his staff. He rose through successive posts as Attending Censor. Zheng Dan of Hedong recommended him as staff judge. He rose to Observation Commissioner of Qianzhong but was demoted on a slander charge. When Cong became Censor-in-Chief, he memorialized recommending Neng as his successor. From Director of the Directorate for Palace Buildings he was made Military Governor of Lingnan; he and Cong both held frontier commands, a distinction the family passed down with pride. He died at sixty-eight and was posthumously appointed Minister of Rites.
9
從子慎由、安潛。 能子彥曾。 能從子慎由慎由，字敬止。 聰警強記，資端厚，有父風采。 繇進士第擢賢良方正異等。 鄭滑高銖辟府判官。 入為右拾遺，進翰林學士。 授湖南觀察使。 召還，由刑部侍郎領浙西。 入遷戶部侍郎，判戶部。 始，慎由苦目疾，不得視，醫為治刮，適愈而召。
Cong's nephews were Shenyou and An Qian. Neng's son was Yanzeng. Neng's nephew Shenyou — Shenyou, styled Jingzhi. Clever and alert with a formidable memory, upright and generous in character, he bore his father's presence. After taking the jinshi degree, he was advanced to outstanding grade in the Worthy and Upright examination. Gao Chu of Zheng-Hua recruited him as staff judge. He entered the capital as Right Reminder and was advanced to Hanlin Academician. He was appointed Observation Commissioner of Hunan. He was recalled and, as Vice Minister of Justice, took charge of Zhexi. He entered the capital as Vice Minister of Revenue with charge of the ministry. Shenyou had long suffered from eye disease and could not see; physicians scraped his eyes to treat him, and he had just recovered when the summons came.
10
俄進工部尚書、同中書門下平章事。 與蕭鄴有隙，鄴輔政，引劉瑑，而出慎由為東川節度使。 初，宣宗餌長年藥，病渴且中躁，而國嗣未立。 帝對宰相欲肆赦，患無其端。 慎由曰：「太子，天下本。 若立之，赦為有名。 」帝惡之，不答。 鄴等乘是譖去之，時大中十二年也。
He was soon promoted to Minister of Works and Associate Chief Councilor. He feuded with Xiao Ye; when Ye took power he brought in Liu Zhuan and posted Shenyou out as Military Governor of Dongchuan. Xuanzong had been taking longevity elixirs, suffered raging thirst and inner agitation, and still had not named an heir. The emperor told his chancellors he wished to proclaim a general amnesty but could find no suitable occasion. Shenyou said, "The crown prince is the foundation of the realm. If you invest him, the amnesty will have a proper justification. " The emperor took offense and made no reply. Ye and his allies used the remark to slander him out of office; this was the twelfth year of Dazhong.
11
咸通初，徙華州刺史，改河中節度使。 以吏部尚書請老，授太子太保，分司東都。 卒，贈司空，諡曰貞。 子胤，別傳。 能從子安潛安潛，字進之。 進士擢第。 咸通中，曆江西觀察、忠武節度使。 乾符初，王仙芝寇河南，安潛募人增陴繕械，不以力費仰朝廷。 首請會兵討捕，號令精明，賊畏之，不犯陳許境。 使大將張自勉將兵七千援宋州。 時宋威屯曹州，而官軍數卻，賊圍宋益急。 自勉收南月城，斬賊二千級，仙芝夜解去。 宰相鄭畋建言：「請以陳許兵三千隸宋威。 」而威忌自勉，乞盡得安潛軍，使自勉隸麾下。 畋謂威有疑忿，必殺自勉，奏言：「今以兵悉畀威，是自勉以功受辱。 安潛抗賊有功，乃取銳兵付威，後有緩急，何以戰？ 是勞不蒙賞，無以示天下。 」詔止以四千付威，餘還自勉。
Early in Xiantong he was transferred to Prefect of Huazhou, then made Military Governor of Hezhong. As Minister of Personnel he asked to retire and was made Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent with a commission at the Eastern Capital. He died and was posthumously appointed Minister of Works with the posthumous title Zhen. His son Yin is treated in a separate biography. Neng's nephew An Qian — An Qian, styled Jinzhi. He passed the jinshi examination. Under Xiantong he served as Observation Commissioner of Jiangxi and then Military Governor of Zhongwu. Early in Qianfu, when Wang Xianzhi raided Henan, An Qian recruited troops, raised the walls, and repaired arms without asking the court to bear the cost. He was first to call for combined forces to hunt the rebels; his orders were crisp and exact, the bandits feared him, and they did not cross into Chen-Xu. He sent his chief general Zhang Zimian with seven thousand men to relieve Songzhou. Song Wei was then encamped at Caozhou, but government troops had fallen back repeatedly and the rebels tightened their siege of Songzhou. Zimian retook Nanyue city and killed two thousand rebels; Xianzhi raised the siege overnight and withdrew. Chancellor Zheng Tian proposed, "Assign three thousand Chen-Xu troops to Song Wei. " But Wei resented Zimian and asked to take all of An Qian's troops and place Zimian under his own command. Tian said Wei nursed suspicion and would surely kill Zimian; he memorialized, "To hand him every soldier now is to let Zimian be disgraced for his victory. An Qian has fought the rebels with success, yet you would strip him of his best troops for Wei—when the next crisis comes, how will he fight? Toil would go unrewarded, and the court would have no example to show the realm. " The edict gave Wei only four thousand men and returned the rest to Zimian.
12
俄代高駢領西川節度。 吏倚駢為奸利者，安潛皆誅之，數更除繆政，於是盜賊衰，蜀民以安。 宰相盧攜素厚駢，乃誣以罪，罷為太子賓客，分司東都。
He soon replaced Gao Pian as Military Governor of Xichuan. He executed every official who had profited through Pian's patronage, repealed one bad policy after another, and banditry waned until the people of Shu lived in peace. Chancellor Lu Xie, who had long favored Pian, framed An Qian on false charges and dismissed him as Guest of the Heir Apparent with a commission at the Eastern Capital.
13
僖宗避賊劍南，召為太子少師。 王鐸任都統，表以自副。 鐸解兵，安潛復為少師、東都留守。 青州王敬武卒，詔拜平盧節度使，檢校太師兼侍中。 會敬武子師範專地，不得入而還。 後遷太子太傅。 卒，贈太子太師，諡貞孝。
When Xizong fled the rebels to Jiannan, An Qian was summoned as Junior Tutor of the Heir Apparent. When Wang Duo served as supreme commander, he recommended An Qian as his deputy. When Duo laid down command, An Qian again became Junior Tutor and Eastern Capital Regent. When Wang Jingwu of Qingzhou died, the court appointed An Qian Military Governor of Pinglu with the acting titles Grand Preceptor and Palace Attendant. But Jingwu's son Shifan held the territory himself; unable to take up the post, An Qian turned back. He was later transferred to Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent. He died and was posthumously appointed Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent with the posthumous title Zhenxiao.
14
安潛于吏事尤長，雖位將相，閱具獄，未嘗不身聽之。 能子彥曾彥曾，咸通初，繇太僕卿為徐州觀察使。 曉律令，然卞急，為政剛猛。 徐軍素驕，而彥曾長於撫民，短治軍，士多怨之。
An Qian excelled at administrative work; even at ministerial rank he personally heard every completed case brought before him. Neng's son Yanzeng — early in Xiantong Yanzeng moved from Director of the Imperial Stud to Observation Commissioner of Xuzhou. He knew the law codes well but was impetuous and governed with harsh severity. The Xuzhou army had long been unruly; Yanzeng was good with civilians but poor with troops, and the soldiers resented him.
15
初，蠻寇五管，陷交趾，詔節度使孟球募兵三千往屯，以八百人戍桂林。 舊制，三年一更。 至期請代，而彥曾親吏尹戡、徐行儉貪不恤士，乃議稟賜乏，請無發兵，復留屯一年。 戍者怒，殺都將王仲甫，脅糧料判官龐勳為將，取庫兵，剽湘、衡，虜丁壯，合眾千餘北還，自浙西趨淮南，達泗口。 所過先遣俳兒弄木偶，伺人情，以防邀遏。 彥曾命牙將田厚簡慰勞，而用都虞候元密伏甲任山館擊賊。 勳遣吏紿言士思歸，不敢遏，請至府解甲自歸，彥曾斬其吏。 勳陷宿州，發廥錢募兵，亡命者從亂如歸，船千艘，與騎夾岸，噪而進。 彥曾料丁男乘城。 或勸率眾奔兗州，彥曾曰：「我，方帥也，奉命守此，惟有死爾。 」斬議者一人號於眾。 俄而勳傅城，城中大霧如墮。 彥曾悉誅賊家屬，勳眾四面超墉入，囚彥曾大彭館。 有曹君長者說勳曰：「貴者不並處，今朝廷未以留後命公，蓋觀察使存爾。 」勳乃殺彥曾於寢，自監軍使逮官屬皆死。 始，彥曾治第鄭州，引水灌沼，水十步忽化為血。 署張佛筵，液蜜為人，一昔鼠齧皆斷首。 徐有子亭，下瀦水為沱，彥曾導清河灌之，鐫石龍首注溜，蔽以屋。 徐人謂屋覆龍，于文為「龐」； 清河，崔望也，為吞噬雲。 贈刑部尚書。 乾符中，錄其子祐之為滎陽尉。
When barbarians raided the Five Circuits and took Jiaozhi, the court ordered Military Governor Meng Qiu to raise three thousand men for the frontier and post eight hundred at Guilin. By regulation they were rotated every three years. When their term ended they asked to be relieved, but Yanzeng's favorites Yin Kan and Xu Xingjian, greedy and indifferent to the men, claimed stipends were exhausted, refused to send replacements, and kept the garrison another year. The garrison rose in fury, killed Commandant Wang Zhongfu, and forced Commissary Judge Pang Xun to lead them; they seized arsenal arms, raided Xiang and Heng, impressed men, and with more than a thousand followers marched north through Zhexi into Huainan as far as Sikou. Wherever they marched they sent jesters with puppets ahead to gauge local sentiment and avoid ambush. Yanzeng sent his yamen general Tian Houjian with gifts of comfort while Chief Commandant Yuan Mi lay in ambush at Renshan lodge to attack the rebels. Xun sent an envoy claiming his men only wanted to go home and asking to surrender at the prefectural seat; Yanzeng had the envoy executed. Xun took Suzhou, opened the treasury to recruit troops, and outlaws flocked to him; a thousand boats and horsemen along both banks advanced with a roar. Yanzeng drafted every able-bodied man onto the walls. Some urged him to flee with his followers to Yanzhou; Yanzeng said, "I am a frontier commander ordered to hold this city—death is all that remains. " He beheaded one adviser and displayed the head before the troops. Soon Xun assaulted the walls while a dense fog seemed to fall over the city. Yanzeng had every rebel family executed; Xun's men scaled the walls on all sides, seized the city, and imprisoned Yanzeng in the Great Peng lodge. A man named Cao Junzhang told Xun, "Two men of rank cannot share one seat; the court has not yet made you acting commissioner—that is why the observation commissioner still lives. " Xun then killed Yanzeng in his bedchamber, and from the army supervisory commissioner down every official was slain. Earlier, while building a mansion in Zhengzhou, he channeled water into a pond that within ten paces suddenly turned to blood. He laid out a Buddhist feast with honey figures of men; overnight rats gnawed off every head. Xuzhou had the Ziting pavilion with a pool below it; Yanzeng diverted the Clear River to fill it, carved a stone dragon head for the outlet, and roofed it over. Locals said a roof over a dragon, written out, spells "Pang"; and the Clear River is the Cui clan's domain, signifying devouring and swallowing. He was posthumously appointed Minister of Justice. During Qianfu his son Youzhi was given office as magistrate of Xingyang.
16
徐吏有路審中者，彥曾知其能，頗任之。 既遇害，賂守卒，斂藏其屍。 張玄稔攻徐州； 審中率死士應官軍，開南白門，官兵入，因得破勳。 後位嵐州刺史。 鄭畋謂審中節貫神明，請擢為右羽林將軍，詔可。
Among Yanzeng's Xu subordinates was Lu Shenzhong, whom he recognized as capable and relied on heavily. After Yanzeng was killed, he bribed the guards and secretly recovered and hid the body. When Zhang Xuanchen attacked Xuzhou, Shenzhong led dare-to-die troops to the government side, opened the South White Gate, let the imperial army in, and Pang Xun was overthrown. He later served as Prefect of Lanzhou. Zheng Tian said Shenzhong's integrity touched the divine and asked that he be promoted to Right General of the Feathered Forest; the edict approved.
17
有許鐸者，罷武城令，客于徐，勳脅以官，不從。 彥曾官屬被囚，鐸潛饋資糧，及死，為收瘞，匿免其子弟，賊平，乃皆歸其喪。 詔拜石首令，賜銀緋。 僚官焦璐、溫廷皓、李棁、崔蘊、柳秦、盧崇嗣、韋廷範贈官有差，錄其子官之。 徐彥伯徐彥伯，兗州瑕丘人，名洪，以字顯。 七歲能為文。 結廬太行山下。 薛元超安撫河北，表其賢，對策高第。 調永壽尉、蒲州司兵參軍。 時司戶韋皓善判，司士李亙工書，而彥伯屬辭，時稱「河東三絕」。 遷職方員外郎，奉迎中宗房州，進給事中。 武后撰《三教珠英》，取文辭士，皆天下選，而彥伯、李嶠居首。 遷宗正卿，出為齊州刺史。 帝復位，改太常少卿。 以脩《武后實錄》勞，封高平縣子。 為衛州刺史，政善狀，璽書嘉勞。 移蒲州，以近畿，會郊祭，上《南郊賦》一篇，辭致黃縟。 擢脩文館學士、工部侍郎。 曆太子賓客。 以疾乞骸骨，許之。 開元二年卒。
Xu Duo, a dismissed magistrate of Wucheng staying in Xu, refused office when Pang Xun tried to coerce him. When Yanzeng's staff were imprisoned, Duo secretly fed them; after they were killed he buried them, hid their sons and brothers, and when the rebellion ended returned every coffin to the families. An edict made him magistrate of Shishou and bestowed silver seals and crimson robes. Staff officials Jiao Lu, Wen Tinghao, Li Zhuo, Cui Yun, Liu Qin, Lu Chongsi, and Wei Tingfan received posthumous offices of varying rank, and their sons were given government posts. Xu Yanbo — Xu Yanbo of Xiaqiu in Yanzhou, personal name Hong, known by his style. At seven he could already write. He built a hut beneath Mount Taihang. When Xue Yuanchao pacified Hebei he recommended Yanbo's talent; Yanbo took top standing in the policy-response examination. He was posted as magistrate of Yongshou and Military Affairs Officer of Puzhou. Revenue Registrar Wei Hao excelled at legal judgments, Records Officer Li Gen at calligraphy, and Yanbo at literary composition—they were called the "Three Unmatched of Hedong." He was promoted to Vice Director of the Bureau of Personnel Assignments, escorted Zhongzong back from Fangzhou, and advanced to Drafting Attendant. When Empress Wu compiled the "Pearls of the Three Teachings," she chose literary scholars from across the empire; Yanbo and Li Jiao ranked first. He became Director of the Imperial Clan Court and then Prefect of Qizhou. When the emperor was restored, he was made Vice Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. For helping compile the Veritable Records of Empress Wu, he was enfeoffed as Viscount of Gaoping. As Prefect of Weizhou he governed so well that an autograph edict praised and rewarded him. Transferred to Puzhou near the capital, he submitted a "Rhapsody on the Southern Suburb" for the suburban sacrifice, written in ornate, stately prose. He was advanced to Xiūwén Hall academician and Vice Minister of Works. He later served as Guest of the Heir Apparent. He asked to retire on grounds of illness and was permitted. He died in the second year of Kaiyuan.
18
彥伯事寡嫂謹，撫諸侄同己子。 秉筆累朝，後來翕然慕仿。 晚為文稍強澀，然當時不及也。
Yanbo tended his widowed sister-in-law with scrupulous care and raised his nephews as his own sons. He wrote for successive reigns, and later writers imitated him wholesale. His late style grew somewhat forceful and austere, yet none in his day could match him.
19
始，武后時，大獄興，王公卿士以語言為酷吏所引，死徙不可計。 彥伯著《樞機論》以謂：「言者，德之柄，行之主，志之端，身之文也。 君子之樞機，動則物應，得失之見也。 可以濟身，亦以覆身，否泰榮辱一系之。 能審思而應，精慮而動，擇其交以後談，則悔吝何由而生？ 怨惡何由而至？ 如此乃可以言也。 」以為戒世雲。 蘇味道蘇味道，趙州欒城人。 九歲能屬辭，與裏人李嶠俱以文翰顯，時號「蘇李」。 逮冠，州舉進士，中第。 累調咸陽尉。 吏部侍郎裴行儉才之，會征突厥，引管書記。 裴居道為左金吾衛將軍，倩味道作章，攬筆而具，閑徹清密，當時盛傳。
Under Empress Wu great prosecutions arose; princes and officials were ensnared by their own words through cruel officials, and deaths and banishments were beyond counting. Yanbo wrote the "Treatise on the Pivot of Power," arguing, "Speech is the handle of virtue, the master of conduct, the edge of intent, the ornament of the self. A gentleman's pivot of power, once moved, draws response from the world; gain and loss stand revealed. It can save a man or destroy him; fortune and ruin, honor and shame all hang upon it. Reflect carefully before you answer, deliberate finely before you act, and choose your company before you speak—how then could regret ever arise? How then could resentment and hatred find their way to you? Only then is speech safe. " He meant it as a warning to the age. Su Weidao — Su Weidao of Luancheng in Zhaozhou. At nine he could already write; he and his townsman Li Jiao won fame for their letters and were known as "Su and Li." When he came of age the prefecture nominated him for the jinshi examination and he passed. He was posted successively as magistrate of Xianyang. Vice Minister of Personnel Pei Xingjian recognized his talent and, when the campaign against the Turks began, brought him on as secretary. When Pei Judao was General of the Left Golden Crow Guard, he asked Weidao to draft a memorial; Weidao took up the brush and finished at once in prose at once leisurely and precise, and the piece was widely admired.
20
延載中，以鳳閣舍人檢校侍郎、同鳳閣鸞台平章事，歲餘為真。 證聖元年，與張錫俱坐法系司刑獄。 錫雖下吏，氣象自如，味道獨席地飯蔬，為危惴可憐者。 武后聞，放錫嶺南，才降味道集州刺史。 召為天官侍郎。 聖曆初，復以鳳閣侍郎、同鳳閣鸞台三品。 更葬其親，有詔州縣治喪事。 味道因役庸過程，遂侵毀鄉人墓田，蕭至忠劾之，貶坊州刺史。 遷益州大都督府長史。 張易之敗，坐黨附，貶眉州刺史。 復還益州長史，未就道卒，年五十八，贈冀州刺史。
During Yanzai he served as Fengge Drafting Attendant, Acting Vice Director, and Associate Chief Councilor; after a year he received the regular appointment. In the first year of Zhengsheng he and Zhang Xi were both implicated in legal cases and imprisoned by the Ministry of Justice. Xi kept his composure even in custody, but Weidao sat on the bare ground eating vegetables, a picture of terrified misery. Empress Wu heard of it, sent Xi to Lingnan, and demoted Weidao only to Prefect of Jizhou. He was recalled as Vice Director of the Bureau of the Heavenly Office. Early in Shenglì he again became Fengge Vice Director and Third Rank Associate of the Secretariat-Chancellery. When he reburied his parents, an edict ordered local officials to manage the funeral. Corvée workers passing through his parents' reburial encroached on a neighbor's grave land; Xiao Zhizhong impeached him and he was demoted to Prefect of Fangzhou. He was transferred to Chief Administrator of the Yizhou Grand Protectorate-General. When Zhang Yizhi fell, Weidao was punished for associating with his faction and demoted to Prefect of Meizhou. He was restored to Chief Administrator of Yizhou but died before taking up the post, at fifty-eight; he was posthumously appointed Prefect of Jizhou.
21
味道練台閣故事，善占奏。 然其為相，特具位，未嘗有所發明，脂韋自營而已。 常謂人曰：「決事不欲明白，誤則有悔，摸棱持兩端可也。 」故世號「摸棱手」。 性友愛。 其弟味元，味元嘗請托不遂，因慢折之，味道怡然不屑。 所論著行于時。 豆盧欽望豆盧欽望，雍州萬年人。 祖寬，隋文帝外孫，為梁泉令。 高祖定關中，與郡守蕭瑀率豪姓進款。 擢累殿中監。 子懷讓，尚萬春公主。 詔寬用魏太和詔，去「豆」姓，著「盧」。 貞觀中，遷禮部尚書、左衛大將軍，芮國公。 卒，贈特進、并州都督，陪葬昭陵，諡曰定。 復其舊姓。
Weidao knew Secretariat-Chancellery precedent inside out and excelled at memorials. As chancellor he merely filled the seat, advanced no policy, and greased every wheel for his own safety. He often said, "Do not decide matters too clearly; mistakes bring regret—it is safer to stay ambiguous and keep both sides open." " Hence his nickname, "the edge-feeler." He was affectionate by nature. His younger brother Weiyuan, when a request went unfulfilled, treated him with contempt; Weidao remained unruffled. His writings circulated widely in his day. Dou Lu Qinwang — Dou Lu Qinwang of Wannian in Yongzhou. His grandfather Kuan was a grandson-in-law of Emperor Wen of Sui and served as magistrate of Liangquan. When Gaozu secured Guanzhong, he and Prefect Xiao Yu led the great clans in submission. He rose through successive posts to Supervisor of the Palace. His son Huairang married Princess Wanchun. An edict followed the Northern Wei Taihe precedent: Kuan dropped the surname Dou and adopted Lu. Under Zhenguan he became Minister of Rites and Great General of the Left Guard with the title Duke of Ruiguo. He died and was posthumously made Special Advancement and Protector-General of Bingzhou, buried with attendant honor at Zhaoling with the posthumous title Ding. His original surname was restored.
22
欽望累官越州都督、司賓卿。 長壽二年，拜內史，封芮國公。 李昭德被罪，有司劾奏欽望阿順昭德不執正，附臣罔君，貶趙州刺史。 入為司府卿，遷秋官尚書。 中宗還東宮，拜太子宮尹。 進文昌右相、同鳳閣鸞台三品。 罷為太子賓客。 帝復位，擢尚書左僕射、平章軍國重事。 欽望居宰相積十餘年，方易之、三思等怙勢宣烝，窺間王室，戮忠戚，觖冀非常，不能有所裁抑，獨謹身諄諄自全。 進開府儀同三司，檢校安國相王府長史。 卒，年八十，贈司空、并州大都督，陪葬乾陵，諡曰元。
Qinwang successively served as Protector-General of Yuezhou and Director of the Court for Imperial Guests. In the second year of Changshou he was appointed Inner Scribe and enfeoffed as Duke of Ruiguo. When Li Zhaode was condemned, officials impeached Qinwang for flattering him rather than upholding justice, for a subject who clung to a minister and misled the throne; he was demoted to Prefect of Zhaozhou. He entered the capital as Director of the Court of the Imperial Treasury and was promoted to Minister of Autumnal Affairs. When Zhongzong returned to the Eastern Palace, Qinwang was appointed Palace Intendant of the Heir Apparent. He was promoted to Right Chancellor of the Secretariat and Third Rank Associate of the Secretariat-Chancellery. He was dismissed and made Guest of the Heir Apparent. When the emperor was restored, he was made Left Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs with charge of weighty military and state affairs. Qinwang served as chancellor for more than ten years while Yizhi, Sansi, and their faction abused power, probed the throne's weakness, killed loyal kin, and reached for the extraordinary; he could not check them and only guarded himself with painstaking caution. He was promoted to Grand Master with the Golden Pouch and Acting Chief Administrator of the Anguo Prince of Xiang's establishment. He died at eighty and was posthumously appointed Minister of Works and Grand Protector-General of Bingzhou, buried with attendant honor at Qianling with the posthumous title Yuan.
23
武后時，宰相又有史務滋、崔元綜、周允元，略可述者附左方。 附史務滋史務滋，宣州溧陽人。 累吏勞，遷司賓卿，進拜納言。 後革命，詔務滋等十人分行天下。 雅州刺史劉行實兄弟為侍御史來子珣誣其反，詔務滋與來俊臣雜治，俊臣言務滋與囚善，掩其反狀，後命俊臣並治，遂自殺。 附崔元綜崔元綜，鄭州新鄭人。 祖君肅，武德中為黃門侍郎、鴻臚卿。 元綜，天授初以鸞台侍郎、同鳳閣鸞台平章事。 性恪慎，坐政事堂，束帶，終日不休偃，尤護細概。 外若謹厚，而中刻薄。 每受制鞫獄，必澡垢索疵，不入死不肯止，人畏鄙之。 未幾，坐事流振州，搢紳為慶。 會赦還，除監察御史。 遷蒲州刺史，致仕。 善攝生，年九十餘卒。 附周允元周允元字汝良，豫州安城人。 自右肅政御史中丞，拜檢校鳳閣侍郎、同鳳閣鸞台平章事。 武后宴宰相，詔陳書傳善言，允元曰：「恥其君不如堯、舜。 」武三思劾奏語指斥，後曰：「聞其言足以誡，安得為過？ 」卒，贈貝州刺史。
Under Empress Wu there were also chancellors Shi Wuzi, Cui Yuanzong, and Zhou Yun'yuan; brief accounts of those worth noting follow. Appendix: Shi Wuzi — Shi Wuzi of Liyang in Xuanzhou. Through long clerical service he became Director of the Court for Imperial Guests and then Supplicator. After the dynastic revolution, an edict sent Wuzi and nine others on separate tours of the realm. Prefect Liu Xingshi and his brother were accused of rebellion by Attending Censor Lai Zixun; Wuzi and Lai Jun'chen were ordered to try the case together. Jun'chen claimed Wuzi favored the prisoners and hid evidence of rebellion; when the court left Jun'chen in sole charge, Wuzi killed himself. Appendix: Cui Yuanzong — Cui Yuanzong of Xinzheng in Zhengzhou. His grandfather Junsu served under Wude as Vice Director of the Yellow Gate and Director of the Court for Dependencies. Early in Tianshou Yuanzong served as Luan Terrace Vice Director and Associate Chief Councilor. Scrupulous by nature, he sat in the Hall of Government Affairs with belt fastened and never rested all day, guarding every minute detail. Outwardly he seemed careful and generous, but inwardly he was harsh and mean. Whenever ordered to interrogate a case, he hunted for every flaw and would not stop short of a death sentence; men feared and despised him. Soon he was banished to Zhenzhou on a charge, to the relief of the gentry. An amnesty brought him back, and he was made Investigating Censor. He was transferred to Prefect of Puzhou and retired from office. Skilled at nurturing his health, he died in his nineties. Appendix: Zhou Yun'yuan — Zhou Yun'yuan, styled Ruliang, of Ancheng in Yuzhou. From Censor-in-Chief of the Right Office for Scrutinizing Governance he was appointed Acting Fengge Vice Director and Associate Chief Councilor. At a feast for her chancellors, Empress Wu asked each to cite a fine saying from the classics; Yun'yuan said, "It is shameful when one's ruler falls short of Yao and Shun." " Wu Sansi accused the remark of being a pointed rebuke; the empress said, "His words are admonition enough—how can that be fault?" " He died and was posthumously appointed Prefect of Beizhou.
