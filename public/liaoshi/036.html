Military Guards, Part 3 — Five Capitals Militia Levies
2
遼建五京：臨潢，契丹故壤； 遼陽，漢之遼東，為渤海故國； 中京，漢遼西地，自唐以來契丹有之。 三京丁籍可紀者二十二萬六千一百，蕃漢轉戶為多。 析津、大同、故漢地，籍丁八十萬六千七百。 契丹本戶多隸宮帳、部族，其餘蕃漢戶丁分隸者，皆不與焉。
The Liao founded five capitals—Linhuang in the old Khitan heartland; Liaoyang, the Han Liaodong region and former Bohai state; Zhongjing in Han Liaoxi, which the Khitan had held since Tang. Among the three capitals, registrable levies came to 226,100—mostly Fan and Han rotating households. Former Han districts such as Xijin and Datong listed 806,700 militia levies. Khitan core families mostly fell under palace tents and tribes, so other Fan and Han levies assigned elsewhere were excluded from these rolls.
3

Taizu founded the imperial capital at Linhuang Prefecture. When Taizong pacified Jin, Shi Jingtang of Later Jin ceded sixteen prefectures; the court then fixed four capitals and renamed the imperial seat Shangjing. Shangjing mustered 167,200 levies.

30
遼屬國可紀者五十有九，朝貢無常。 有事則遣使征兵，或下詔專征； 不從者討之。 助軍眾寡，各從其便，無常額。 又有鐵不得國者，興宗重熙十七年乞以兵助攻夏國，詔不許。
Fifty-nine subject states appear in the records, though tribute missions had no fixed schedule. In emergencies the court dispatched envoys to raise troops or issued edicts ordering separate expeditions; States that refused were punished by force. Contributed forces followed each realm's means, with no standing quota. The Iron-Forbidden State once asked in Chongxi year 17 to join an attack on Western Xia; the court denied the request.

遼之為國，鄰於梁、唐、晉、漢、周、宋。 晉以恩故，始則父子一家，終則寇讎相攻； 梁、唐、周隱然一敵國； 宋惟太宗征北漢，遼不能救，餘多敗衄，縱得亦不償失。 良由石晉獻土，中國失五關之固然也。 高麗小邦，屢喪遼兵，非以險阻足恃故歟。 西夏彈丸之地，南敗宋，東抗遼。 雖西北士馬雄勁，元昊、諒祚智勇過人，能使党項、阻卜掣肘大國，蓋亦襟山帶河，有以助其勢耳。 雖然，宋久失地利，而舊《志》言兵，唯以敵宋為務。 逾三關，聚議北京，猶不敢輕進。 豈不以大河在前，三鎮在後，臨事好謀之審，不容不然歟。
Liao bordered in turn Liang, Tang, Jin, Han, Zhou, and Song. With Later Jin the bond began as kin-by-grace yet closed in mutual raids; Liang, Tang, and Zhou each stood as a full rival power. Only when Song's Taizong attacked Northern Han could Liao not relieve the target; otherwise Liao mostly lost, and wins rarely paid for themselves. The root cause was Shi Jingtang's land grant and the Central States' forfeiture of the five passes' natural defenses. Small Goryeo still drained Liao forces again and again—perhaps its rugged ground alone could explain it. Western Xia, though minute, beat Song to the south and held Liao at bay in the east. Even with strong northwestern cavalry, Yuanhao and Liangzuo's talent let Tangut and Zubu allies tie down a great empire, aided by mountains and rivers at their flank. Yet Song had lost terrain for years while older records still framed warfare chiefly against Song. Even after crossing the three passes and gathering councils at Beijing, Liao hesitated to push forward. With the Yellow River ahead and the three prefectures behind, cautious planning at the crisis point left no other choice.

Two imperial camps, twelve palaces, one office, and five capitals together fielded 1,642,800 troops. Palace levies, great chieftains, tribal hosts, Zhongjing, head-of-household domains, and subject troops lay outside this tally. Restraint in committing them was how the dynasty lasted.
