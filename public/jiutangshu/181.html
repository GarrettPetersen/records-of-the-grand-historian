1
列傳第一百三十一史憲誠史憲誠，其先出於奚虜，今爲靈武建康人。 祖道德，開府儀同三司、試太常卿、上柱國、懷澤郡王。 父周洛，爲魏博軍校，事田季安，至兵馬大使、銀青光祿大夫、檢校太子賓客、兼御史中丞、柱國、北海郡王。 憲誠始以材勇，隨父歷軍中右職，兼監察御史。 元和中，田弘正討李師道，令憲誠以先鋒四千人濟河，累下其城柵。 復以大軍齊進，乘勢逐北，魏之全師迫於鄆之城下。 師道窮蹙，劉悟斬首投魏軍。 錄功，超授憲誠兼中丞。
Biography 131 — Shi Xiancheng. His forebears were Xi tribesmen; by his day his household was counted among the people of Jiankang in Lingwu. His grandfather Daode was Grand Preceptor of the Palace with the honorary rank equal to the Three Excellencies, acting Grand Master of Ceremonies, Senior Pillar of State, and Prince of Huaize commandery. His father Zhouluo was an officer in the Weibo army under Tian Ji'an, rising to Military Commissioner, Silver-Green-Light Grandee, honorary Crown Prince's Guest, concurrent Censor-in-Chief, Pillar of State, and Prince of Beihai commandery. Xiancheng first distinguished himself by ability and valor; he followed his father through senior army posts and also held the post of Supervising Censor. During the Yuanhe period Tian Hongzheng attacked Li Shidao and sent Xiancheng with four thousand vanguard troops across the Yellow River; he took one enemy strongpoint after another. The main army then advanced in concert, pressing the pursuit northward until the whole Wei host stood beneath the walls of Yan. Shidao was cornered; Liu Wu cut off his head and presented it to the Wei army. His achievements were entered on the rolls and Xiancheng was promoted out of turn to concurrent Censor-in-Chief.
2
鎮州王承宗死，弘正自魏移領鎮州。 居數月，爲王廷湊所殺，遂以兵叛。 朝廷以弘正子布爲魏博節度使，領兵討伐，俾復父冤。 時幽州硃克融援助廷湊，布不能制，因自引決，軍情囂然。
Wang Chengzong of Zhenzhou died, and Hongzheng transferred from Wei to take command of Zhenzhou. Within a few months he was murdered by Wang Tingcou, who then rose in armed rebellion. The court made Hongzheng's son Bu military commissioner of Weibo and sent him to campaign, that he might avenge his father. Zhu Kerong of Youzhou then aided Tingcou; Bu could not master the situation and took his own life, and the army's temper grew unruly.
3
憲誠爲中軍都知兵馬使，乘亂以河朔舊事動其人心，諸軍即擁而歸魏，共立爲帥，國家因而命之。 時克融、廷湊並據兵爲亂，憲誠喜得旄節，雖外順朝旨，而中與硃、王爲輔車之勢，長慶二年正月也。
Xiancheng was commander of the central army in charge of military affairs; seizing the disorder, he stirred the men with old Hebei precedents, and the armies at once rallied to him and returned to Wei, jointly setting him up as commander — whereupon the court confirmed him in office. Kerong and Tingcou both held troops in rebellion at the time; Xiancheng rejoiced to receive the commission and insignia; though outwardly he obeyed the court, inwardly he and Zhu and Wang stood in mutual support — this was in the first month of Changqing year 2.
4
尋遣司門郎中韋文恪宣慰。 時李㝏爲亂，與憲誠書問交通。 憲誠表請與㝏節鉞，仍於黎陽艤舟，示欲渡河。 及見文恪，舉止驕倨，其言甚悖。 旋聞絺爲帳下所殺，乃從改過，謂文恪曰：「憲誠蕃人，猶狗也，唯能識主。 雖被棒打，終不忍離。 」其狡譎如此。 朝廷每爲優容，尋加左僕射。 敬宗即位，進秩司空。
Soon the court sent Wei Wenke, a director in the Ministry of Rites' Department of Credentials, to offer consolation and reassurance. At that time Li Tongjie was in rebellion and exchanged messages with Xiancheng. Xiancheng memorialized asking that he and Tongjie each receive military commissions, and moreover moored boats at Liyang as if preparing to cross the river. When he met Wenke his bearing was proud and his words deeply insubordinate. Before long he heard that Li Ge had been killed by his own tent guards; he then changed his manner and told Wenke, "Xiancheng is a barbarian, no better than a dog — he knows only to recognize his master." "Even when beaten with a stick, he cannot bear to leave his master." Such was the depth of his cunning. The court repeatedly indulged him; he was soon made Left Vice Minister of Works. When Emperor Jingzong took the throne, Xiancheng was promoted to Grand Minister of Works.
5
太和二年，滄景節度使李全略卒，其子同捷竊據軍城，表邀符節，舉兵伐之。 先是，憲誠與全略婚媾，及同捷叛，復潛以糧餉爲助。 上屢發使申諭，尋又就加平章事。 憲誠嘗遣驍將至闕下，恣爲張大，宰相韋處厚以語折剉之，憲誠不敢復與同捷爲應。 時憲誠示出師共討同捷。 及滄景平，加司徒。 憲誠心不自安，乃遣子孝章入覲，又飛章願以所管奉命。 上嘉之。 乃加侍中，移鎮河中。 憲誠素懷向背，不能以忠誠感激其眾。 未及出城，太和三年六月二十六日夜，爲軍眾所害，冊贈太尉。 憲誠子孝章孝章，幼聰悟好學。 元和中，李醞爲魏帥，取大將子弟列於軍籍。 孝章倡言願效文職，醞奇之，令攝府參軍。 及憲誠領節鉞，改士曹參軍、兼監察御史，賜緋。 孝章以父在鎮多違朝旨，嘗雪涕極諫，備陳逆順之理。 朝廷聞而嘉之，乃授檢校太子左諭德、兼侍御史，充節度副使。 累遷至散騎常侍、兼御史大夫，賜紫。 領本道兵同平滄景，加工部尚書。 尋請赴闕，文宗慰勞甚厚，憲誠亦因懇乞朝覲。 上知憲誠之入覲，自孝章之謀，遂加禮部尚書，分相、衞、澶三州別爲一鎮，俾孝章領之。 孝章未到鎮，憲誠遇害。 上以孝章有忠節，起復爲右金吾衞將軍。 間歲，授鄜坊節度使。 居四年，遷於滑。 一歲，入爲右領軍大將軍，改右金吾大將軍，俄授邠寧節度。
In Taihe year 2 Li Quanlue, military commissioner of Cangjing, died; his son Tongjie seized the garrison and petitioned for the commission and insignia, and Xiancheng raised troops to attack him. Xiancheng had earlier been joined to Quanlue by marriage; when Tongjie rebelled he again secretly sent grain and supplies as aid. The emperor repeatedly sent envoys to instruct him; he was then given the additional title of Grand Councilor. Xiancheng had once sent fierce generals to the capital to swagger and boast; the chief minister Wei Chuhou cut him down with sharp words, and Xiancheng dared not again act in concert with Tongjie. At that time Xiancheng made a show of marching out to join in the campaign against Tongjie. When Cangjing was pacified, he was made Grand Mentor of State. Xiancheng felt ill at ease in his heart; he sent his son Xiaozhang to court for audience and also sent urgent memorials offering to place his command under the court's orders. The emperor commended this. He was then made Palace Attendant and transferred to command at Hezhong. Xiancheng had long wavered in loyalty and could not move his men with sincere devotion. Before he had even left the city, on the night of the twenty-sixth day of the sixth month of Taihe year 3, the troops killed him; he was posthumously enfeoffed as Grand Preceptor of State. Xiancheng's son Xiaozhang. Xiaozhang was clever and fond of study from boyhood. In the Yuanhe era, when Li Yun was military commissioner of Wei, he enrolled the sons of senior generals on the army rolls. Xiaozhang spoke up saying he wished to serve in a civil post; Yun was impressed and had him serve as acting headquarters adjutant. When Xiancheng received the military commission, Xiaozhang was made registrar in the Bureau of Personnel and concurrent Supervising Censor, and was granted crimson robes. Because his father in his command often defied the court, Xiaozhang once wept as he remonstrated to the utmost, setting forth in full the logic of loyalty and rebellion. The court heard of this and commended him; he was appointed honorary Left Mentor to the Crown Prince and concurrent Attending Censor, and made deputy military commissioner. He rose in stages to Regular Attendant and concurrent Censor-in-Chief, and was granted purple robes. Leading troops of his circuit he helped pacify Cangjing and was given the additional post of Minister of Revenue. Soon he asked to go to court; Emperor Wenzong comforted and rewarded him generously, and Xiancheng also earnestly begged to attend court because of this. The emperor knew Xiancheng's coming to court sprang from Xiaozhang's counsel; he then promoted Xiaozhang to Minister of Rites and carved Xiang, Wei, and Chan into a separate command for Xiaozhang to govern. Before Xiaozhang reached his post, Xiancheng was killed. The emperor, considering Xiaozhang loyal, recalled him from mourning to serve as general of the Right Imperial Insignia Guard. A year later he was appointed military commissioner of Bin and Fang. After four years in office he was transferred to command Hua. One year later he entered court as grand general of the Right Army, then was changed to grand general of the Right Imperial Insignia Guard, and soon received the Binning command.
6
孝章歷三鎮，雖無異績，而謹身畏法，以保初終。 開成三年十月卒，贈右僕射。 何進滔何進滔，靈武人也。 曾祖孝物，祖俊，並本州軍校。 父默，夏州衙前兵馬使，檢校太子賓客，試太常卿。 以進滔之貴，贈左散騎常侍。 進滔客寄於魏，委質軍門，事節度使田弘正。 弘正奉詔討鄆州，破李師道，時進滔爲衙內都知兵馬使，以功授兼侍御史。 太和三年，軍眾害史憲誠，連聲而呼曰：「得衙內都知兵馬使何端公知留後，即三軍安矣。 」推而立之。 朝廷因授進滔左散騎常侍、魏博等州節度觀察處置等使。 爲魏帥十餘年，大得民情，累官至司徒、平章事卒。 進滔子弘敬子弘敬襲其位。 朝廷時遣河中帥李執方、滄州帥劉約各遣使勸令歸闕，別俟朝旨。 弘敬不從，竟就加節制。 及劉稹反，不時起兵。 鎮州王元逵下邢、洺二州，兵次上黨，弘敬方出師壓境。 大中後，宣宗務其姑息，繼加官爵，亦至使相。 咸通初，卒。 子全皞嗣之。 朝廷尋降符節，累官亦至同平章事。 十一年，爲軍人所害。 子孫相繼，四十餘年。 韓允忠韓允忠，魏州人也。 舊名君雄，懿宗改賜今名。 父國昌，歷本州右職。 會昌中，從何弘敬破劉稹，以功爲貝州刺史、兼御史中丞。 以允忠故，累贈兵部尚書。 允忠少仕軍門，繼升裨校。 潞州之役，亦與其行。 咸通十一年，何全皞爲軍眾所殺，推允忠爲帥。 時僖宗爲普王，即降詔遙領節度，授允忠左散騎常侍、兼御史中丞，充節度觀察留後。 不數月，轉檢校工部尚書、魏州大都督府長史、充魏博節度觀察等使。 累加至檢校司空、同平章事。 乾符元年十一月卒，年六十一。 累贈太尉。 允忠子簡子簡，自允忠初授戎帥，便爲節度副使。 乾符初，累官至檢校工部尚書。 允忠卒，即起復爲節度觀察留後。 逾月，加檢校右僕射。 其後累加至侍中，封昌黎郡王。
Xiaozhang held three commands; though he achieved nothing remarkable, he kept himself within the law and preserved his position from first to last. In the tenth month of Kaicheng year 3 he died and was posthumously enfeoffed as Right Vice Minister of Works. He Jintao. He Jintao was a man of Lingwu. His great-grandfather Xiaowu and grandfather Jun had both been military officers of the prefecture. His father Mo was military commissioner in the yamen of Xia prefecture, honorary Crown Prince's Guest, and acting Grand Master of Ceremonies. When Jintao rose to eminence, Mo was posthumously given Left Regular Attendant. Jintao lodged in Wei and pledged himself at the military gate, serving the military commissioner Tian Hongzheng. Hongzheng carried out the imperial order to attack Yanzhou and defeat Li Shidao; at that time Jintao was commander of the inner yamen in charge of military affairs, and for his merit was given the additional post of Attending Censor. In Taihe year 3 the troops killed Shi Xiancheng; they shouted together, "If the commander of the inner yamen, Master He the Duke, will act as acting commander, the three armies will be secure!" They pushed him forward and set him up. The court thereupon appointed Jintao Left Regular Attendant and military commissioner of Weibo and other prefectures, with authority to observe and dispose. As commander of Wei for more than ten years he won the people's hearts; he rose in rank to Grand Mentor of State and Grand Councilor and died. Jintao's son Hongjing. Hongjing inherited his position. The court at that time sent Li Zhifang, commander of Hezhong, and Liu Yue, commander of Cangzhou, each to dispatch envoys urging him to return to court and await separate imperial orders. Hongjing did not comply and nevertheless received the commission and military control outright. When Liu Zhen rebelled he did not raise troops promptly. Wang Yuankui of Zhenzhou took Xing and Ming prefectures and advanced to Shangdang; only then did Hongjing march out to press the border. After the Dazhong era Emperor Xuanzong pursued appeasement and piled offices and enfeoffments upon him until Hongjing too reached commissioner and councilor. He died early in the Xiantong era. His son Quanhao succeeded to his post. The court soon bestowed the commission and insignia; he too rose to concurrent Grand Councilor. In the eleventh year the troops killed him. Father and son held power in succession for more than forty years. Han Yunzhong. Han Yunzhong was a man of Weizhou. His original name was Junxiong; Emperor Yizong changed it and bestowed the present name. His father Guochang held senior posts on the right in the prefecture. In the Huichang era he followed He Hongjing in defeating Liu Zhen; for merit he was made prefect of Bei prefecture and concurrent Censor-in-Chief. Because of Yunzhong, Guochang was repeatedly promoted posthumously to Minister of War. Yunzhong entered military service young and rose step by step to staff officer. In the Lu prefecture campaign he marched with them as well. In Xiantong year 11, when He Quanhao was killed by the troops, they pushed Yunzhong forward as commander. At that time Emperor Xizong was Prince of Pu; an edict was promptly issued putting him in remote charge of the command; Yunzhong was given Left Regular Attendant and concurrent Censor-in-Chief as acting military commissioner in observation. Within a few months he was made honorary Minister of Works, chief administrator of the great capital prefecture of Weizhou, and full military commissioner of Weibo in observation, and so on. He was promoted repeatedly to honorary Grand Minister of Works and concurrent Grand Councilor. In the eleventh month of Qianfu year 1 he died, aged sixty-one. Posthumously he was repeatedly enfeoffed as Grand Preceptor of State. Yunzhong's son Jian. Jian, from the time Yunzhong first received the military command, served as deputy military commissioner. At the beginning of Qianfu he had risen repeatedly to honorary Minister of Works. When Yunzhong died he was recalled from mourning to serve as acting military commissioner in observation. After more than a month he was given the additional title of honorary Right Vice Minister of Works. Later he rose to Palace Attendant and was enfeoffed Prince of Changli commandery.
7
賊巢之亂，諸葛爽受其偽命河陽節度使。 時僖宗在蜀，寇盜蜂起，簡據有六州，甲兵強盛，竊懷僭亂之志，且欲啟其封疆，乃舉兵攻河陽，爽棄城而走。 簡遂留兵保守，因北掠邢、洺而歸，遂移軍攻鄆。 鄆帥曹全晸出戰，爲簡所敗，死之。 鄆將崔君裕收合殘眾，保鄆州。 簡進攻其城，半年不下，河陽復爲諸葛爽所襲。 簡因欲先討君裕，次及河陽，乃舉兵至鄆，君裕請降。 尋移軍復攻河陽，行及新鄕，爲爽軍逆擊，敗之。 簡單騎奔回，憂憤。 疽發背而卒，時中和元年十一月也。 樂彥禎樂彥禎，魏州人也。 父少寂，歷澶、博、貝三州刺史，贈工部尚書。 彥禎少爲本州軍校。 韓簡之領節旄也，以彥禎爲馬歩軍都虞候，轉博州刺史。 下河陽，走諸葛爽。 有功，遷澶州刺史。 簡再討河陽之敗也。 彥禎以一軍先歸，魏人遂共立之。 朝廷尋授檢校工部尚書，知魏博留後。 俄加戸部尚書，充節度觀察處置等使。 中和四年，累加至尚書左僕射、同平章事。 僖宗自蜀回，加開府儀同三司，冊拜司徒。
During the upheaval of Huang Chao's rebels, Zhuge Shuang received the false appointment as military commissioner of Heyang. At that time Emperor Xizong was in Shu and bandits swarmed everywhere; Jian held six prefectures with strong armor and troops, secretly harbored ambitions of usurpation, and wished besides to enlarge his domain; he raised troops to attack Heyang, and Shuang abandoned the city and fled. Jian left troops to hold it, then raided north through Xing and Ming and returned, then shifted his army to attack Yan. Cao Quanzhen, military commissioner of Yan, went out to fight, was defeated by Jian, and was killed. The Yan general Cui Junyu gathered the remnant forces and held Yan prefecture. Jian pressed the attack on the city for half a year without taking it; Heyang was again seized in a raid by Shuang. Jian therefore wished first to subdue Junyu and then turn to Heyang; he raised troops to Yan and Junyu asked to surrender. He soon shifted troops to attack Heyang again; at Xinxiang he was met by Shuang's army, struck, and defeated. Jian fled back alone on horseback, full of grief and resentment. A carbuncle broke out on his back and he died — this was the eleventh month of Zhonghe year 1. Li Yanzhen. Li Yanzhen was a man of Weizhou. His father Shaoji had served as prefect of Chan, Bo, and Bei prefectures and was posthumously made Minister of Works. In youth Yanzhen was a military officer of the prefecture. When Han Jian received the military commission, Yanzhen was made chief commandant of the horse and foot army and then prefect of Bo prefecture. He attacked Heyang and drove Zhuge Shuang to flight. For merit he was transferred to prefect of Chan prefecture. This was when Jian again attacked Heyang and was defeated. Yanzhen returned first with one army, and the people of Wei thereupon jointly set him up. The court soon appointed him honorary Minister of Works and acting commander of Weibo. Shortly he was made Minister of Revenue and full military commissioner in observation and disposal, and so on. In Zhonghe year 4 he was promoted repeatedly to Left Vice Minister of Works and concurrent Grand Councilor. When Emperor Xizong returned from Shu he was given Grand Preceptor of the Palace with honorary rank equal to the Three Excellencies and was enfeoffed by edict as Grand Mentor of State.
8
彥禎志滿驕大，動多不法。 一旦征六州之眾，板築羅城，約河門舊堤，周八十里，月餘而畢，人用怨咨。 彥禎子從訓又其子從訓天資悖逆。 王鐸自滑移鎮滄州，過魏郊，從訓見其女妓，利之，先伏兵於漳南高雞泊，俟鐸之至，圍而害之，掠其所有。 時朝廷微弱，不能詰。 魏人素知鐸名望，議者惜之，而罪從訓。 從訓又召亡命之徒五百餘輩，出入臥內，號爲「子將」，委以腹心。 軍人籍籍，各有異議。 從訓聞而忌之，易服遁出，止於近縣。 彥禎因命爲六州都指揮使。 未幾，又兼相州刺史。 到任之後，般輦軍器，取索錢帛，使人來往，交午塗路，軍府疑貳。
Yanzhen's heart swelled with pride; his conduct was often unlawful. Once he mobilized the people of the six prefectures to build the outer wall with rammed earth along the old embankment at Hemen, eighty li around; it was finished in more than a month, and the people groaned in resentment. Yanzhen's son Congxun. His son Congxun was by nature rebellious and perverse. Wang Duo moved from Hua to command Cangzhou and passed through Wei territory; Congxun saw his daughter's singing girls and coveted them; he first hid troops at Gaojipo south of Zhang, and when Duo arrived he surrounded and killed him and plundered everything he had. The court was weak at the time and could not call him to account. The people of Wei had long known Duo's reputation; commentators regretted the affair and blamed Congxun. Congxun also summoned more than five hundred fugitives who came and went within his sleeping quarters; they were called the "Junior Generals," and he entrusted them with his innermost confidence. The soldiers murmured among themselves; each held his own view. When Congxun heard of this he grew jealous, changed clothes, slipped out, and stayed in a nearby county. Yanzhen thereupon appointed him commander-in-chief of the six prefectures. Before long he was also made concurrent prefect of Xiang prefecture. After taking office he carted arms, extorted money and silk, and sent messengers back and forth until the roads were crowded; the headquarters grew divided.
9
彥禎危憤而卒，眾推都將趙文㺹知留後事。 從訓自相州領兵三萬餘人至城下，文㺹按兵不出。 眾懷疑懼，復害文㺹，推羅弘信爲帥。 弘信以兵出戰，敗之。 從訓招集餘眾，次於洹水。 弘信遣將程公佐領兵討擊，大敗之，梟從訓首於軍門，時文德元年春也。 羅弘信羅弘信，字德孚，魏州貴鄕人。 曾祖秀，祖珍，父讓，皆爲本州軍校。 弘信少從戎役，歷事節度使韓簡、樂彥禎。 光啟末，彥禎子從訓忌牙軍，出居於外，軍眾廢彥禎，推趙文㺹權主軍州事。 眾復以爲不便，因推弘信爲帥。 先是，有鄰人密謂弘信曰：「某嘗夜遇一白鬚翁，相告雲，君當爲土地主。 如是者再三。 」弘信竊異之。 及廢文㺹，軍人聚呼曰：「孰願爲節度使者？ 」弘信即應之曰：「白鬚翁早以命我。 」眾乃環而視之，曰：「可也。 」由是立之。 僖宗聞之，文德元年四月，詔加工部尚書，權知節度留後。 七月，復加金紫光祿大夫、檢校尚書右僕射，充魏博節度觀察處置等使。 龍紀中，加檢校司空、同平章事，封豫章郡公。
Yanzhen died in peril and resentment; the masses pushed forward the chief commander Zhao Wenbi to handle acting commander affairs. Congxun led more than thirty thousand men from Xiang prefecture to the foot of the city; Wenbi held his troops and would not come out. The men grew fearful; they killed Wenbi again and set up Luo Hongxin as commander. Hongxin led troops out to fight and routed him. Congxun gathered the remnant forces and encamped at the Huan River. Hongxin sent the general Cheng Gongzuo to lead troops in pursuit and attack; he inflicted a great defeat and displayed Congxun's head at the army gate — this was spring of Wende year 1. Luo Hongxin. Luo Hongxin, courtesy name Defu, was a man of Guixiang in Weizhou. His great-grandfather Xiu, grandfather Zhen, and father Rang had all been military officers of the prefecture. Hongxin entered military service young and served the commissioners Han Jian and Li Yanzhen in turn. At the end of Guangqi, Congxun feared the yamen troops and lived outside; the army deposed Yanzhen and put Zhao Wenbi in charge. The men again found this unsatisfactory and therefore set up Hongxin as commander. Earlier a neighbor had secretly told Hongxin, "I once met a white-bearded old man at night who told me you are to be lord of this land." This happened three times. Hongxin privately wondered at it. When Wenbi was deposed the soldiers gathered and shouted, "Who wishes to be military commissioner?" Hongxin at once answered, "The white-bearded old man long ago commanded me." The masses then surrounded and looked at him, saying, "He will do." On that basis he was established in command. When Emperor Xizong heard of it, in the fourth month of Wende year 1 an edict added the title Minister of Works and put him in authority as acting military commissioner. In the seventh month he was again given Golden-Green-Light Grandee and honorary Right Vice Minister of Works and made full military commissioner of Weibo in observation and disposal, and so on. In the Longji era he was given honorary Grand Minister of Works and concurrent Grand Councilor and enfeoffed as Duke of Yuzhang commandery.
10
乾寧中，朱全忠急攻兗鄆，朱瑄求援於太原。 太原發軍，假道於魏，令大將李存信屯莘縣。 存信御軍無法，侵魏之芻牧，弘信不平之。 全忠復遣人謂之曰：「太原志吞河朔，回戈之日，貴道堪憂。 」弘信乃托好於汴，出師三萬攻存信，敗之。 太原怒，舉兵攻魏，營於觀音門外。 汴將葛從周援之，屯於洹水。 李克用子落落時爲鐵林軍使，爲從周所擒，乃退歸。 自是太原之師，每歲侵擾相、魏，魏人患之。
During Qianning, Zhu Quanzhong pressed Yan and Yun hard; Zhu Xuan sought help from Taiyuan. Taiyuan sent an army through Wei and ordered Li Cunxin to camp at Shen county. Cunxin disciplined his troops poorly and seized Wei's fodder and pasture; Hongxin resented it. Quanzhong again sent someone to tell him, "Taiyuan intends to swallow Hebei; on the day it turns its spears back, your territory will be in peril." Hongxin then pretended friendship with Bian, marched out thirty thousand men against Cunxin, and defeated him. Taiyuan was furious, raised troops against Wei, and encamped outside the Guanyin Gate. The Bian general Ge Congzhou came to his aid and camped on the Huan River. Li Keyong's son Luoluo was at that time commander of the Iron Forest Army; Congzhou captured him and they then withdrew. From this time forward the Taiyuan army raided Xiang and Wei every year; the people of Wei suffered from it.
11
朱全忠方事兗鄆，懼弘信離貳，每歲時賂遺，必卑辭厚禮答貺。 全忠對魏使北面拜而受之，曰：「六兄比予倍年已上，兄弟之國，安得以常鄰遇之。 」弘信以爲厚己，亦推心焉。 弘信累官至檢校太師、守侍中、臨清王。 光化元年九月卒，年六十三，贈太師，追封北平王，謚曰莊肅。 子威。 弘信子威威，字端己。 文德初，授左散騎常侍，充天雄軍節度副使。 自龍紀至乾寧，十年之中，累加官爵。 弘信卒，襲父位爲留後，朝廷從而命之。 天復末，累加至檢校太傅、兼侍中、長沙王。 天祐初，授檢校太尉、守侍中，進封鄴王，賜號「忠勤宣力致理功臣」。
Quanzhong was then fighting Yan and Yun and feared Hongxin would turn; every season he sent gifts, and Hongxin always answered with humble, lavish courtesy. Quanzhong toward envoys from Wei bowed facing north to receive them, saying, "Sixth Elder Brother is more than twice my years; as brother states, how can we be treated as ordinary neighbors?" Hongxin took this as deep regard for himself and gave Quanzhong his full trust. Hongxin rose in office repeatedly to honorary Grand Preceptor of State, defender Palace Attendant, and Prince of Linqing. In the ninth month of Guanghua year 1 he died, aged sixty-three; he was posthumously enfeoffed as Grand Preceptor of State, posthumously titled Prince of Beiping, with the posthumous name Zhuangsu. He had a son named Wei. Hongxin's son Wei, courtesy name Duanji. At the start of Wende he received Left Regular Attendant and served as deputy commissioner of the Tianxiong Army. From Longji through Qianning, over ten years his offices and titles mounted steadily. When Hongxin died, Wei inherited the acting command; the court confirmed him. At the end of Tianfu he was promoted repeatedly to honorary Grand Tutor of State, concurrent Palace Attendant, and Prince of Changsha. At the beginning of Tianyou he was given honorary Grand Preceptor of State, defender Palace Attendant, advanced to Prince of Ye, and granted the title Meritorious Minister Diligent in Loyal Service and Effective in Governance.
12
魏之牙中軍者，自至德中，田承嗣盜據相、魏、澶、博、衞、貝等六州，召募軍中子弟置之部下，遂以爲號。 皆豐給厚賜，不勝驕寵。 年代浸遠，父子相襲，親黨膠固。 其凶戾者，強買豪奪，逾法犯令，長吏不能禁。 變易主帥，有同兒戲，如史憲誠、何進滔、韓君雄、樂彥禎，皆爲其所立。 優獎小不如意，則舉族被害。 威懲其往弊，雖以貨賂姑息，而心銜之。
Wei's yamen army: since Zhide, when Tian Chengsi seized Xiang, Wei, Chan, Bo, Wei, and Bei, he enrolled soldiers' sons in his command — and that became their name. They were richly paid and heavily favored, grown insufferably proud. As time passed, fathers and sons succeeded one another and kin networks hardened. Those who were fierce and cruel forcibly bought and plundered, transgressed law and violated orders, and senior officials could not restrain them. Replacing commanders was child's play; Shi Xiancheng, He Jintao, Han Junxiong, and Li Yanzhen were all put up by them. If rewards fell short even slightly, whole clans were destroyed. Wei meant to end their old abuses; though he bought them off, he nursed hatred in his heart.
13
威嗣世之明年，正月，幽州劉仁恭擁兵十萬，謀亂河朔，進陷貝州，長驅攻魏。 威求援於汴。 朱全忠遣將李思安屯於洹水。 葛從周自邢、洺引軍入魏。 燕將劉守文、單可及攻汴軍於內黃。 思安逆戰，大敗之，乘勝追躡。 從周出會掩擊，覆敗燕軍，斬首三萬。 三年，威引汴軍攻滄州以報之。 自是，威感全忠援助之恩，合從景附。
The year after Wei succeeded, in the first month Liu Rengong of Youzhou mustered a hundred thousand men, plotted to overturn Hebei, took Bei, and drove on Wei. Wei asked Bian for help. Quanzhong sent Li Si'an to camp on the Huan River. Ge Congzhou led troops in from Xing and Ming. The Yan generals Liu Shouwen and Shan Keji attacked the Bian army at Neihuang. Si'an met them, won a great victory, and pursued hotly. Congzhou joined the attack from another angle and crushed the Yan army, taking thirty thousand heads. In the third year Wei led Bian troops against Cangzhou in revenge. From then on Wei was grateful for Quanzhong's help and clung to his alliance.
14
天祐二年七月十三日夜，牙軍裨校李公佺作亂，威僅以身免。 公佺出奔滄州。 自是愈懼，遣使求援於全忠，密謀破之。 全忠遣李思安會魏博軍，再攻滄州。 全忠女妻威子廷規，先是卒。 全忠遣長直軍校馬嗣勳選兵千人，密於輿中實兵甲入魏，言助女葬事。 三年正月五日，嗣勳至，全忠親率大軍濟河，言視行營於滄景。 威欲因而出迎，至期，即假全忠帳下銳卒入而夾攻之。 牙軍頗疑，堅請不出。 威恐洩其事，慰納之。 是月十四日夜，率廝養百十輩，與嗣勳合攻之。 時宿於牙城者千人，遲明殺之殆盡； 凡八千家，皆破其族。 魏軍攻滄州者，在歷亭聞有變，其將史仁遇擁之，保於高唐。 六州之內，皆爲讎敵，累月平之。 威仕梁數年後卒，年三十四，位至守太師、兼中書令，贈尚書令，謚曰貞壯。
On the night of the thirteenth day of the seventh month of Tianyou year 2 the yamen army staff officer Li Gongxian made disorder; Wei barely escaped with his life. Gongxian fled to Cangzhou. Wei grew still more afraid, sent envoys to Quanzhong for aid, and secretly plotted to break the yamen army. Quanzhong sent Li Si'an to join the Weibo army in a second attack on Cangzhou. Quanzhong's daughter had married Wei's son Tinggui and had already died. Quanzhong sent Ma Sixun to pick a thousand men and hide arms in funeral carts entering Wei, claiming he came to help bury his daughter. On the fifth day of the first month of the third year Sixun arrived; Quanzhong crossed the river at the head of a great host, saying he would inspect the Cangjing front. Wei planned to go out and welcome him, then borrow Quanzhong's picked troops for a pincer strike. The yamen troops were suspicious and begged him firmly not to leave the city. Fearing a leak, Wei soothed them and agreed. On the fourteenth night of that month he led a hundred-odd attendants and joined Sixun in the assault. A thousand yamen troops were billeted in the inner city; by dawn nearly all were killed. Eight thousand households in all had their lineages wiped out. The Wei force besieging Cangzhou was at Liting; hearing of the upheaval, Shi Renyu rallied them and held Gaotang. Throughout the six prefectures everyone became an enemy; pacification took months. Wei served Liang for several years and then died, aged thirty-four, reaching defender Grand Preceptor of State and concurrent Director of the Department of State Affairs; he was posthumously given Minister of the Imperial Secretariat, with the posthumous name Zhenzhuang.
15
威性明敏，達於吏道。 伏膺儒術，招納文人，聚書至萬卷。 每花朝月夕，與賓佐賦詠，甚有情致。 錢塘人羅隱者，有當世詩名，自號「江東生」。 威遣使賂遺，敘其宗姓，推爲叔父。 隱亦集其詩寄之。 威酷嗜其作，目己所爲曰《偷江東集》，凡五卷，今鄴中人士諷詠之。 史臣曰史臣曰：魏、鎮、燕三鎮，不能制之也久矣。 兵強地廣，合從連衡。 爵命雖假於朝廷，群臣自謀於元帥。 如史憲誠等五家，其初皆因此而得之，其後亦因此而失之。 蓋不知取之以權，守之以仁，則遠矣。 若善繼者，史氏、羅氏之二子有焉，其餘不足觀也。 贊贊曰：逆取順守，古亦有之。 如其逆守，滅亡必隨。 史、何、韓、樂，世數盛衰。 足以爲鑒，念茲在茲。
Wei was quick-witted and skilled in administration. He honored Confucian learning, gathered literati, and amassed ten thousand scrolls. On every flower festival and moonlit evening he composed and recited poetry with his guests and staff, with much grace and feeling. There was Luo Yin of Qiantang, who enjoyed a contemporary reputation in poetry and styled himself the Man of Jiangdong. Wei sent envoys with gifts, claimed kinship through their clan name, and honored him as uncle. Yin in turn assembled his own poems and sent them as a gift. Wei adored his poetry, called his own work The Collection Stolen from Jiangdong in five scrolls, and men of Ye still recite it. The historian writes: For the three commands of Wei, Zhen, and Yan, the court has long since lost the power to control them. Their armies are strong and their territories broad; they form alliances and counter-alliances. Titles came from the court, but the ministers schemed under the commander's banner. The five houses such as Shi Xiancheng first rose this way — and later fell the same way. They did not know to seize power by expedience and keep it through benevolence; how far they were from wisdom. Only the Shi and Luo heirs truly carried on well; the rest are hardly worth notice. The encomium says: Seizing power through rebellion yet ruling with obedience to the court — even the ancients knew such cases. Hold power through rebellion, and ruin follows inevitably. Shi, He, Han, and Le — their houses rose and fell with the generations. They are warning enough; keep this lesson close.
