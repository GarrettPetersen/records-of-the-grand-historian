列傳第一百三十一史憲誠史憲誠，其先出於奚虜，今爲靈武建康人。 祖道德，開府儀同三司、試太常卿、上柱國、懷澤郡王。 父周洛，爲魏博軍校，事田季安，至兵馬大使、銀青光祿大夫、檢校太子賓客、兼御史中丞、柱國、北海郡王。 憲誠始以材勇，隨父歷軍中右職，兼監察御史。 元和中，田弘正討李師道，令憲誠以先鋒四千人濟河，累下其城柵。 復以大軍齊進，乘勢逐北，魏之全師迫於鄆之城下。 師道窮蹙，劉悟斬首投魏軍。 錄功，超授憲誠兼中丞。
[Biography 131] Shi Xiancheng. Shi Xiancheng was descended from the Xi people; his family were now registered natives of Jiankang in Lingwu prefecture. His grandfather Shi Daode had held the titles of Grand Prefect of ceremonially equal rank to the Three Excellencies, provisional Grand Minister of Ceremonies, Supreme Pillar of the State, and Prince of Huaize. His father Shi Zhouluo had been a Weibo army officer under Tian Ji'an, rising to Military Affairs Commissioner, Silver-Gleam Grand Master for Splendid Happiness, acting Crown Prince's Guest, concurrent Censor-in-Chief, Pillar of the State, and Prince of Beihai. Xiancheng first distinguished himself by talent and valor, following his father through senior army posts while also holding the concurrent post of Investigating Censor. During the Yuanhe reign, when Tian Hongzheng campaigned against Li Shidao, he sent Xiancheng across the river at the head of four thousand vanguard troops, who took one walled camp after another. The main army then advanced in concert, pressing the pursuit north until Weibo's entire force had Yan city under siege. With Shidao cornered, Liu Wu cut off his head and presented it to the Weibo forces. His achievements were entered in the records, and Xiancheng was promoted out of turn to concurrent Censor-in-Chief.
2
鎮州王承宗死，弘正自魏移領鎮州。 居數月，爲王廷湊所殺，遂以兵叛。 朝廷以弘正子布爲魏博節度使，領兵討伐，俾復父冤。 時幽州硃克融援助廷湊，布不能制，因自引決，軍情囂然。
When Wang Chengzong of Zhenzhou died, Hongzheng transferred from Wei to assume command of Zhenzhou. Within a few months he was murdered by Wang Tingcou, who then rose in armed rebellion. The court appointed Hongzheng's son Tian Bu military governor of Weibo and sent him to campaign against Tingcou so that he might avenge his father. Zhu Kerong of Youzhou was then supporting Tingcou; unable to gain the upper hand, Bu took his own life, and the army fell into uproar.
3
憲誠爲中軍都知兵馬使，乘亂以河朔舊事動其人心，諸軍即擁而歸魏，共立爲帥，國家因而命之。 時克融、廷湊並據兵爲亂，憲誠喜得旄節，雖外順朝旨，而中與硃、王爲輔車之勢，長慶二年正月也。
Xiancheng, who was Central Army Commissioner for Military Affairs, exploited the chaos to appeal to old Hebei customs; the troops promptly rallied back to Wei and installed him as their leader, and the court thereupon confirmed the appointment. Kerong and Tingcou were both in armed rebellion at the time. Delighted to receive the commissioner's baton, Xiancheng outwardly complied with the court while secretly aligning with Zhu and Wang in mutual support — this was in the first month of Changqing 2.
4
尋遣司門郎中韋文恪宣慰。 時李㝏爲亂，與憲誠書問交通。 憲誠表請與㝏節鉞，仍於黎陽艤舟，示欲渡河。 及見文恪，舉止驕倨，其言甚悖。 旋聞絺爲帳下所殺，乃從改過，謂文恪曰：「憲誠蕃人，猶狗也，唯能識主。 雖被棒打，終不忍離。 」其狡譎如此。 朝廷每爲優容，尋加左僕射。 敬宗即位，進秩司空。
The court soon sent Wei Wenke of the Bureau of Passes as an emissary to offer reassurance. Li Xi was then in rebellion and was exchanging correspondence with Xiancheng. Xiancheng petitioned that Xi be granted a commissioner's baton as well, and moored boats at Liyang as if preparing to cross the Yellow River. When he received Wenke, his manner was arrogant and his words openly defiant. When he soon learned that Xi had been killed by his own officers, he changed his tune and told Wenke, "Xiancheng is a barbarian — no better than a dog — and knows only how to recognize his master." Even when beaten with clubs, he cannot bring himself to leave his master's side. Such was the depth of his cunning and deceit. The court repeatedly indulged him, and soon promoted him to Left Vice Director of the Secretariat. When Emperor Jingzong ascended the throne, Xiancheng was promoted to Minister of Works.
5
太和二年，滄景節度使李全略卒，其子同捷竊據軍城，表邀符節，舉兵伐之。 先是，憲誠與全略婚媾，及同捷叛，復潛以糧餉爲助。 上屢發使申諭，尋又就加平章事。 憲誠嘗遣驍將至闕下，恣爲張大，宰相韋處厚以語折剉之，憲誠不敢復與同捷爲應。 時憲誠示出師共討同捷。 及滄景平，加司徒。 憲誠心不自安，乃遣子孝章入覲，又飛章願以所管奉命。 上嘉之。 乃加侍中，移鎮河中。 憲誠素懷向背，不能以忠誠感激其眾。 未及出城，太和三年六月二十六日夜，爲軍眾所害，冊贈太尉。 憲誠子孝章孝章，幼聰悟好學。 元和中，李醞爲魏帥，取大將子弟列於軍籍。 孝章倡言願效文職，醞奇之，令攝府參軍。 及憲誠領節鉞，改士曹參軍、兼監察御史，賜緋。 孝章以父在鎮多違朝旨，嘗雪涕極諫，備陳逆順之理。 朝廷聞而嘉之，乃授檢校太子左諭德、兼侍御史，充節度副使。 累遷至散騎常侍、兼御史大夫，賜紫。 領本道兵同平滄景，加工部尚書。 尋請赴闕，文宗慰勞甚厚，憲誠亦因懇乞朝覲。 上知憲誠之入覲，自孝章之謀，遂加禮部尚書，分相、衞、澶三州別爲一鎮，俾孝章領之。 孝章未到鎮，憲誠遇害。 上以孝章有忠節，起復爲右金吾衞將軍。 間歲，授鄜坊節度使。 居四年，遷於滑。 一歲，入爲右領軍大將軍，改右金吾大將軍，俄授邠寧節度。
In Taihe 2, when Li Quanlue, military governor of Cangjing, died, his son Tongjie seized the garrison city and petitioned for the commissioner's baton; Xiancheng marched against him. Xiancheng had earlier been connected to Quanlue by marriage; when Tongjie rebelled, he again secretly sent grain and supplies to aid him. The emperor repeatedly sent envoys to remonstrate with him, and soon afterward also named him Grand Councilor. Xiancheng once sent bold generals to the capital to boast openly; Grand Councilor Wei Chuhou rebuked them sharply, and Xiancheng dared not coordinate with Tongjie again. For a time Xiancheng made a show of taking the field jointly against Tongjie. When Cangjing was pacified, he was promoted to Minister of Education. Uneasy in his own mind, Xiancheng sent his son Xiaozhang to the capital for an audience and urgently petitioned to place his command under direct court authority. The emperor praised his loyalty. He was made Palace Attendant and transferred to command Hedong circuit. Xiancheng had long been of two minds toward the court and could not win his troops over with genuine loyalty. Before he could depart the city, on the night of the twenty-sixth day of the sixth month, Taihe 3, the troops killed him; he was posthumously enfeoffed as Grand Preceptor. Xiancheng's son, Xiaozhang. From childhood Xiaozhang was quick-witted and devoted to learning. During the Yuanhe reign, when Li Yun commanded Wei, he enrolled the sons of senior generals on the army rolls. Xiaozhang was the first to declare that he wished to serve in a civil capacity; Yun was impressed and appointed him acting staff adjutant. When Xiancheng received the commissioner's baton, Xiaozhang was made Staff Section Adjutant and concurrent Investigating Censor, with crimson robes conferred. Seeing that his father repeatedly defied the court while in command, Xiaozhang once wept as he remonstrated with him at length on the difference between loyalty and treason. When the court learned of this, it praised his conduct and appointed him acting Left Mentor of the Crown Prince, concurrent Attending Censor, and deputy military commissioner. He rose in succession to Regular Attendant, concurrent Censor-in-Chief, and was granted purple robes. Leading troops of his circuit, he helped pacify Cangjing and was made Minister of Works. He soon requested leave to attend court; Emperor Wenzong received him with generous praise, and Xiancheng also earnestly petitioned for an audience. Knowing that Xiancheng's audience at court had come about through Xiaozhang's counsel, the emperor made him Minister of Rites, carved out Xiang, Wei, and Chan as a separate command, and placed Xiaozhang in charge of it. Before Xiaozhang could take up his new command, Xiancheng was killed. Because Xiaozhang had shown loyal integrity, the emperor recalled him from mourning and appointed him General of the Right Gold Crow Guard. Within a year he was appointed military governor of Fufang. After four years he was transferred to Hua circuit. After one year he was recalled to court as Grand General of the Right Leading Army, then made Grand General of the Right Gold Crow Guard, and soon afterward given command of Binning.
6
孝章歷三鎮，雖無異績，而謹身畏法，以保初終。 開成三年十月卒，贈右僕射。 何進滔何進滔，靈武人也。 曾祖孝物，祖俊，並本州軍校。 父默，夏州衙前兵馬使，檢校太子賓客，試太常卿。 以進滔之貴，贈左散騎常侍。 進滔客寄於魏，委質軍門，事節度使田弘正。 弘正奉詔討鄆州，破李師道，時進滔爲衙內都知兵馬使，以功授兼侍御史。 太和三年，軍眾害史憲誠，連聲而呼曰：「得衙內都知兵馬使何端公知留後，即三軍安矣。 」推而立之。 朝廷因授進滔左散騎常侍、魏博等州節度觀察處置等使。 爲魏帥十餘年，大得民情，累官至司徒、平章事卒。 進滔子弘敬子弘敬襲其位。 朝廷時遣河中帥李執方、滄州帥劉約各遣使勸令歸闕，別俟朝旨。 弘敬不從，竟就加節制。 及劉稹反，不時起兵。 鎮州王元逵下邢、洺二州，兵次上黨，弘敬方出師壓境。 大中後，宣宗務其姑息，繼加官爵，亦至使相。 咸通初，卒。 子全皞嗣之。 朝廷尋降符節，累官亦至同平章事。 十一年，爲軍人所害。 子孫相繼，四十餘年。 韓允忠韓允忠，魏州人也。 舊名君雄，懿宗改賜今名。 父國昌，歷本州右職。 會昌中，從何弘敬破劉稹，以功爲貝州刺史、兼御史中丞。 以允忠故，累贈兵部尚書。 允忠少仕軍門，繼升裨校。 潞州之役，亦與其行。 咸通十一年，何全皞爲軍眾所殺，推允忠爲帥。 時僖宗爲普王，即降詔遙領節度，授允忠左散騎常侍、兼御史中丞，充節度觀察留後。 不數月，轉檢校工部尚書、魏州大都督府長史、充魏博節度觀察等使。 累加至檢校司空、同平章事。 乾符元年十一月卒，年六十一。 累贈太尉。 允忠子簡子簡，自允忠初授戎帥，便爲節度副使。 乾符初，累官至檢校工部尚書。 允忠卒，即起復爲節度觀察留後。 逾月，加檢校右僕射。 其後累加至侍中，封昌黎郡王。
Xiaozhang held three commands. Though he won no striking distinction, he conducted himself with scrupulous regard for the law and preserved his position to the end. He died in the tenth month of Kaicheng 3 and was posthumously made Right Vice Director of the Secretariat. He Jintao. He Jintao was a native of Lingwu. His great-grandfather He Xiaowu and grandfather He Jun had both been army officers in their home prefecture. His father He Mo had been Military Affairs Commissioner on the staff of Xiazhou, acting Crown Prince's Guest, and provisional Grand Minister of Ceremonies. When Jintao rose to prominence, his father was posthumously made Left Regular Attendant. Jintao had come to Wei as a guest and entered military service under Tian Hongzheng, military governor of the circuit. When Hongzheng campaigned against Yan at imperial order and defeated Li Shidao, Jintao was Yamen Inner Commissioner for Military Affairs and was made concurrent Attending Censor for his service. In Taihe 3 the troops killed Shi Xiancheng and shouted as one, "If Yamen Inner Commissioner Lord He takes charge as acting military governor, the three armies will be secure." They pressed him forward and installed him as their leader. The court thereupon appointed him Left Regular Attendant and military commissioner for observation and disposition of Weibo and associated prefectures. He commanded Wei for more than ten years and won broad popular support, rising to Minister of Education and Grand Councilor before he died. Jintao's son, Hongjing. Hongjing succeeded to his father's post. The court repeatedly had Li Zhifang of Hedong and Liu Yue of Cangzhou send envoys urging him to come to court and await further imperial orders. Hongjing refused, and in the end the court simply confirmed him in the commission. When Liu Zhen rebelled, he failed to take the field promptly. Wang Yuankui of Zhenzhou had already taken Xing and Ming and halted his army at Shangdang before Hongjing finally marched out to menace the border. After the Dazhong reign, Emperor Xuanzong pursued a conciliatory policy toward him, heaping honors upon him until he too held commissioner-councilor rank. He died early in the Xiantong reign. His son Quanhao succeeded him. The court soon conferred the commissioner's baton on him, and his accumulated honors likewise reached concurrent Grand Councilor. In the eleventh year of his rule he was killed by his own troops. Father and son held the command in succession for more than forty years. Han Yunzhong. Han Yunzhong was a native of Weizhou. His original name was Junxiong; Emperor Yizong granted him his present name. His father Han Guochang had held senior posts in the prefecture. During the Huichang reign he followed He Hongjing in defeating Liu Zhen and, for his service, was made prefect of Beizhou and concurrent Censor-in-Chief. Because of Yunzhong's eminence, his father was repeatedly posthumously promoted, ultimately to Minister of War. Yunzhong entered army service in his youth and rose in succession to adjutant. He also took part in the Luzhou campaign. In Xiantong 11, when He Quanhao was killed by the troops, they installed Yunzhong as their commander. Emperor Xizong was then still Prince of Pu; the court at once issued an edict confirming remote command of the circuit and appointed Yunzhong Left Regular Attendant and concurrent Censor-in-Chief as acting military commissioner. Within a few months he was made acting Minister of Works, chief administrator of the Weizhou metropolitan prefecture, and full military commissioner of Weibo. He was promoted in succession to acting Minister of Works and concurrent Grand Councilor. He died in the eleventh month of Qianfu 1, at the age of sixty-one. He was posthumously enfeoffed as Grand Preceptor. Yunzhong's son, Jian. From the moment Yunzhong first received military command, Jian served as his deputy commissioner. By the beginning of the Qianfu reign he had risen to acting Minister of Works. When Yunzhong died, Jian was immediately recalled from mourning and made acting military commissioner. Within a month he was made acting Right Vice Director of the Secretariat. He was later promoted in succession to Palace Attendant and enfeoffed as Prince of Changli.
7
賊巢之亂，諸葛爽受其偽命河陽節度使。 時僖宗在蜀，寇盜蜂起，簡據有六州，甲兵強盛，竊懷僭亂之志，且欲啟其封疆，乃舉兵攻河陽，爽棄城而走。 簡遂留兵保守，因北掠邢、洺而歸，遂移軍攻鄆。 鄆帥曹全晸出戰，爲簡所敗，死之。 鄆將崔君裕收合殘眾，保鄆州。 簡進攻其城，半年不下，河陽復爲諸葛爽所襲。 簡因欲先討君裕，次及河陽，乃舉兵至鄆，君裕請降。 尋移軍復攻河陽，行及新鄕，爲爽軍逆擊，敗之。 簡單騎奔回，憂憤。 疽發背而卒，時中和元年十一月也。 樂彥禎樂彥禎，魏州人也。 父少寂，歷澶、博、貝三州刺史，贈工部尚書。 彥禎少爲本州軍校。 韓簡之領節旄也，以彥禎爲馬歩軍都虞候，轉博州刺史。 下河陽，走諸葛爽。 有功，遷澶州刺史。 簡再討河陽之敗也。 彥禎以一軍先歸，魏人遂共立之。 朝廷尋授檢校工部尚書，知魏博留後。 俄加戸部尚書，充節度觀察處置等使。 中和四年，累加至尚書左僕射、同平章事。 僖宗自蜀回，加開府儀同三司，冊拜司徒。
During Huang Chao's rebellion, Zhuge Shuang accepted a bogus appointment from him as military governor of Heyang. With Emperor Xizong in exile in Shu and rebels rising everywhere, Jian held six prefectures and commanded a powerful army. Secretly nursing ambitions of rebellion and eager to enlarge his domain, he marched on Heyang; Shuang abandoned the city and fled. Jian left troops to hold Heyang, raided north through Xing and Ming, then turned his army against Yan. Cao Quanbian, military governor of Yan, took the field and was defeated and killed by Jian. The Yan officer Cui Junyu rallied the remnant forces and held Yanzhou. Jian besieged the city for half a year without success, while Heyang was seized again by Zhuge Shuang. Wishing first to deal with Junyu and then with Heyang, Jian marched on Yan, where Junyu offered to surrender. He soon marched again on Heyang, but at Xinxiang Shuang's forces met him in a counterattack and routed his army. Jian fled back alone on horseback, sick with grief and anger. A carbuncle broke out on his back and he died, in the eleventh month of Zhonghe 1. Le Yanzhen. Le Yanzhen was a native of Weizhou. His father Le Shaoji had served as prefect of Chan, Bo, and Bei and was posthumously made Minister of Works. Yanzhen entered service in his youth as an army officer in his home prefecture. When Han Jian held military command, he appointed Yanzhen Chief Commandant of the Horse and Foot Army and later made him prefect of Bo. He took Heyang and put Zhuge Shuang to flight. For this service he was promoted to prefect of Chan. This occurred during Han Jian's second failed campaign against Heyang. Yanzhen was the first to return with his troops, and the men of Wei promptly installed him as their leader. The court soon appointed him acting Minister of Works and acting military governor of Weibo. He was soon made Minister of Revenue and full military commissioner for observation and disposition. In Zhonghe 4 he was promoted in succession to Left Vice Director of the Secretariat and concurrent Grand Councilor. When Emperor Xizong returned from exile in Shu, Yanzhen was made Grand Prefect of ceremonially equal rank and enfeoffed as Minister of Education.
8
彥禎志滿驕大，動多不法。 一旦征六州之眾，板築羅城，約河門舊堤，周八十里，月餘而畢，人用怨咨。 彥禎子從訓又其子從訓天資悖逆。 王鐸自滑移鎮滄州，過魏郊，從訓見其女妓，利之，先伏兵於漳南高雞泊，俟鐸之至，圍而害之，掠其所有。 時朝廷微弱，不能詰。 魏人素知鐸名望，議者惜之，而罪從訓。 從訓又召亡命之徒五百餘輩，出入臥內，號爲「子將」，委以腹心。 軍人籍籍，各有異議。 從訓聞而忌之，易服遁出，止於近縣。 彥禎因命爲六州都指揮使。 未幾，又兼相州刺史。 到任之後，般輦軍器，取索錢帛，使人來往，交午塗路，軍府疑貳。
Sated with success and increasingly arrogant, Yanzhen frequently acted outside the law. He once conscripted labor from all six prefectures to build an outer walled city along the old Hemen embankment, eighty li around; though finished in little more than a month, the people groaned under the burden. Yanzhen's son, Congxun. His son Congxun was rebellious and violent by nature. When Wang Duo was transferred from Hua to Cangzhou and passed through Wei's outskirts, Congxun coveted his female entertainers, ambushed him at Gaojibo south of the Zhang River, killed him, and plundered everything he had. The court was then too weak to hold him accountable. The people of Wei had long respected Duo's standing; public opinion lamented his death and condemned Congxun. Congxun also gathered more than five hundred outlaws who moved freely in and out of his private quarters, called them "Son-Generals," and entrusted them with his closest secrets. Dissatisfaction spread through the ranks, and many voiced objections. Hearing of this, Congxun grew wary, disguised himself, slipped out of the city, and lodged in a nearby county. Yanzhen then appointed him supreme commander of the six prefectures. Before long he was also made prefect of Xiang. Once in office he shipped arms and extorted money and silk; his agents clogged the roads, and the military headquarters grew suspicious of him.
9
彥禎危憤而卒，眾推都將趙文㺹知留後事。 從訓自相州領兵三萬餘人至城下，文㺹按兵不出。 眾懷疑懼，復害文㺹，推羅弘信爲帥。 弘信以兵出戰，敗之。 從訓招集餘眾，次於洹水。 弘信遣將程公佐領兵討擊，大敗之，梟從訓首於軍門，時文德元年春也。 羅弘信羅弘信，字德孚，魏州貴鄕人。 曾祖秀，祖珍，父讓，皆爲本州軍校。 弘信少從戎役，歷事節度使韓簡、樂彥禎。 光啟末，彥禎子從訓忌牙軍，出居於外，軍眾廢彥禎，推趙文㺹權主軍州事。 眾復以爲不便，因推弘信爲帥。 先是，有鄰人密謂弘信曰：「某嘗夜遇一白鬚翁，相告雲，君當爲土地主。 如是者再三。 」弘信竊異之。 及廢文㺹，軍人聚呼曰：「孰願爲節度使者？ 」弘信即應之曰：「白鬚翁早以命我。 」眾乃環而視之，曰：「可也。 」由是立之。 僖宗聞之，文德元年四月，詔加工部尚書，權知節度留後。 七月，復加金紫光祿大夫、檢校尚書右僕射，充魏博節度觀察處置等使。 龍紀中，加檢校司空、同平章事，封豫章郡公。
Yanzhen died, sick with fear and anger; the troops installed the commandant Zhao Wenjue as acting military governor. Congxun marched from Xiangzhou with more than thirty thousand men and halted before the city; Wenjue refused to give battle. The troops, uneasy and afraid, killed Wenjue and installed Luo Hongxin as their commander. Hongxin took the field and defeated him. Congxun rallied his remnant forces and encamped on the Huan River. Hongxin sent Cheng Gongzuo against him, routed his army, and displayed Congxun's head before the camp gate — this was in the spring of Wende 1. Luo Hongxin. Luo Hongxin, courtesy name Defu, was a native of Guixiang in Weizhou. His great-grandfather Luo Xiu, grandfather Luo Zhen, and father Luo Rang had all been army officers in their home prefecture. Hongxin entered military service in his youth and served under the military governors Han Jian and Le Yanzhen. Late in the Guangqi reign, Congxun, fearing the yazi troops, moved outside the city; the army deposed Yanzhen and installed Zhao Wenjue to govern the circuit provisionally. Finding this arrangement unsatisfactory, the troops then installed Hongxin as commander. Earlier a neighbor had told him in secret, "I once met a white-bearded old man at night who said you would become lord of this land." He said this had happened again and again. Hongxin had privately wondered at the omen. When Wenjue was removed, the soldiers called out together, "Who will be military commissioner?" Hongxin answered at once, "The white-bearded old man long ago destined me for it." The men surrounded him and said, "He will do." On that basis they installed him. When Emperor Xizong learned of this, in the fourth month of Wende 1 he was ordered to serve as Minister of Works and acting military governor. In the seventh month he was further made Golden-Gleam Grand Master for Splendid Happiness, acting Right Vice Director, and full military commissioner of Weibo. During the Longji reign he was made acting Minister of Works, concurrent Grand Councilor, and enfeoffed as Duke of Yuzhang.
10
乾寧中，朱全忠急攻兗鄆，朱瑄求援於太原。 太原發軍，假道於魏，令大將李存信屯莘縣。 存信御軍無法，侵魏之芻牧，弘信不平之。 全忠復遣人謂之曰：「太原志吞河朔，回戈之日，貴道堪憂。 」弘信乃托好於汴，出師三萬攻存信，敗之。 太原怒，舉兵攻魏，營於觀音門外。 汴將葛從周援之，屯於洹水。 李克用子落落時爲鐵林軍使，爲從周所擒，乃退歸。 自是太原之師，每歲侵擾相、魏，魏人患之。
During the Qianning reign Zhu Quanzhong pressed hard against Yan and Yanzhou, and Zhu Xuan sought help from Taiyuan. Taiyuan sent an army through Wei by permission and stationed the general Li Cunxin at Shen county. Cunxin disciplined his troops poorly and seized Wei's fodder and pasturelands, which angered Hongxin. Quanzhong sent word again: "Taiyuan means to swallow Hebei; when they turn homeward, your circuit will be in grave danger." Hongxin therefore professed friendship with Bian, marched out thirty thousand men against Cunxin, and defeated him. Enraged, Taiyuan attacked Wei and encamped outside the Guanyin Gate. The Bian general Ge Congzhou came to his aid and encamped on the Huan River. Li Keyong's son Luoluo, then commander of the Iron Forest Army, was captured by Congzhou, and the Taiyuan forces withdrew. Thereafter Taiyuan raided Xiang and Wei year after year, to the great distress of the Wei command.
11
朱全忠方事兗鄆，懼弘信離貳，每歲時賂遺，必卑辭厚禮答貺。 全忠對魏使北面拜而受之，曰：「六兄比予倍年已上，兄弟之國，安得以常鄰遇之。 」弘信以爲厚己，亦推心焉。 弘信累官至檢校太師、守侍中、臨清王。 光化元年九月卒，年六十三，贈太師，追封北平王，謚曰莊肅。 子威。 弘信子威威，字端己。 文德初，授左散騎常侍，充天雄軍節度副使。 自龍紀至乾寧，十年之中，累加官爵。 弘信卒，襲父位爲留後，朝廷從而命之。 天復末，累加至檢校太傅、兼侍中、長沙王。 天祐初，授檢校太尉、守侍中，進封鄴王，賜號「忠勤宣力致理功臣」。
While Quanzhong was campaigning in Yan and Yanzhou, he feared Hongxin might turn against him and every year sent lavish seasonal gifts, which Hongxin always answered with humble words and generous courtesy. Quanzhong would bow northward to receive Wei's envoys and say, "Sixth Elder Brother is more than twice my age; between brother states, how can one treat a neighbor as an ordinary acquaintance?" Hongxin took this as deep respect for himself and gave Quanzhong his full trust in return. Hongxin rose to acting Grand Preceptor, Guardian Palace Attendant, and Prince of Linqing. He died in the ninth month of Guanghua 1, at the age of sixty-three; he was posthumously made Grand Preceptor, enfeoffed as Prince of Northern Peace, and given the posthumous title Zhuangsu. His son was Wei. Hongxin's son, Wei. Wei, courtesy name Duanji. At the beginning of the Wende reign he was appointed Left Regular Attendant and deputy military commissioner of the Tianxiong Army. From the Longji through the Qianning reigns, over ten years he accumulated honors and titles. When Hongxin died, Wei succeeded him as acting military governor, and the court confirmed the appointment. By the end of the Tianfu reign he had risen to acting Grand Mentor, concurrent Palace Attendant, and Prince of Changsha. Early in the Tianyou reign he was made acting Grand Commandant and Guardian Palace Attendant, advanced to Prince of Ye, and granted the title Meritorious Minister of Loyal Diligence Who Expands Power and Brings Order.
12
魏之牙中軍者，自至德中，田承嗣盜據相、魏、澶、博、衞、貝等六州，召募軍中子弟置之部下，遂以爲號。 皆豐給厚賜，不勝驕寵。 年代浸遠，父子相襲，親黨膠固。 其凶戾者，強買豪奪，逾法犯令，長吏不能禁。 變易主帥，有同兒戲，如史憲誠、何進滔、韓君雄、樂彥禎，皆爲其所立。 優獎小不如意，則舉族被害。 威懲其往弊，雖以貨賂姑息，而心銜之。
Wei's yazi central army dated from the Zhide era, when Tian Chengsi seized the six prefectures of Xiang, Wei, Chan, Bo, Wei, and Bei, enrolled sons of army families under his command, and gave them this name. They were lavishly paid and richly rewarded until pride and indulgence knew no bounds. Over the generations father succeeded son, and kin and allies grew ever more tightly bound. The most brutal among them seized property by force, broke the law with impunity, and local officials could not restrain them. Replacing commanders was child's play to them; Shi Xiancheng, He Jintao, Han Junxiong, and Le Yanzhen had all been installed by the yazi. If rewards fell even slightly short of their expectations, entire clans were slaughtered. Wei set himself to curb their abuses; though he bought them off with gifts, he nursed deep resentment against them.
13
威嗣世之明年，正月，幽州劉仁恭擁兵十萬，謀亂河朔，進陷貝州，長驅攻魏。 威求援於汴。 朱全忠遣將李思安屯於洹水。 葛從周自邢、洺引軍入魏。 燕將劉守文、單可及攻汴軍於內黃。 思安逆戰，大敗之，乘勝追躡。 從周出會掩擊，覆敗燕軍，斬首三萬。 三年，威引汴軍攻滄州以報之。 自是，威感全忠援助之恩，合從景附。
In the first month of the year following Wei's succession, Liu Rengong of Youzhou mustered one hundred thousand men, plotted to throw Hebei into chaos, took Bei prefecture, and marched straight on Wei. Wei sought help from Bian. Zhu Quanzhong sent Li Si'an to encamp on the Huan River. Ge Congzhou led his army from Xing and Ming into Wei. The Yan generals Liu Shouwen and Shan Keyi attacked the Bian army at Neihuang. Si'an met them in battle, routed them, and pressed the pursuit. Congzhou joined the attack from another direction, crushed the Yan army, and took thirty thousand heads. In the third year Wei led Bian troops against Cangzhou in reprisal. From that time Wei, grateful for Quanzhong's aid, entered into close alliance with him.
14
天祐二年七月十三日夜，牙軍裨校李公佺作亂，威僅以身免。 公佺出奔滄州。 自是愈懼，遣使求援於全忠，密謀破之。 全忠遣李思安會魏博軍，再攻滄州。 全忠女妻威子廷規，先是卒。 全忠遣長直軍校馬嗣勳選兵千人，密於輿中實兵甲入魏，言助女葬事。 三年正月五日，嗣勳至，全忠親率大軍濟河，言視行營於滄景。 威欲因而出迎，至期，即假全忠帳下銳卒入而夾攻之。 牙軍頗疑，堅請不出。 威恐洩其事，慰納之。 是月十四日夜，率廝養百十輩，與嗣勳合攻之。 時宿於牙城者千人，遲明殺之殆盡； 凡八千家，皆破其族。 魏軍攻滄州者，在歷亭聞有變，其將史仁遇擁之，保於高唐。 六州之內，皆爲讎敵，累月平之。 威仕梁數年後卒，年三十四，位至守太師、兼中書令，贈尚書令，謚曰貞壯。
On the night of the thirteenth day of the seventh month, Tianyou 2, the yazi adjutant Li Gongquan rebelled, and Wei barely escaped with his life. Gongquan fled to Cangzhou. Thereafter ever more afraid, he sent envoys to Quanzhong for aid and secretly plotted their destruction. Quanzhong sent Li Si'an with Weibo troops to attack Cangzhou again. Quanzhong's daughter had been married to Wei's son Tinggui, who had died earlier. Quanzhong sent the veteran officer Ma Sixun with a thousand picked men, arms hidden in funeral litters, on the pretext of assisting with his daughter's burial. On the fifth day of the first month of the third year Sixun arrived; Quanzhong personally led a great army across the river, claiming he was going to inspect the Cangjing front. Wei planned to go out to welcome him and, when the day came, use Quanzhong's crack troops to enter the city and attack the yazi from within and without. The yazi grew suspicious and firmly urged him not to leave the city. Fearing his plot would leak out, Wei reassured them. On the night of the fourteenth he led a little more than a hundred personal retainers and joined Sixun in the assault. About a thousand yazi were quartered in the inner city; by dawn nearly all had been killed; eight thousand households in all had their entire clans destroyed. The Wei troops besieging Cangzhou, hearing of the upheaval at Liting, were rallied by their general Shi Renyu, who held Gaotang. Throughout the six prefectures they were hunted as enemies, and months passed before order was restored. After several years in Liang service Wei died at thirty-four, having reached Guardian Grand Preceptor and concurrent Director of the Secretariat; he was posthumously made Director of the Secretariat with the posthumous title Zhenzhuang.
15
威性明敏，達於吏道。 伏膺儒術，招納文人，聚書至萬卷。 每花朝月夕，與賓佐賦詠，甚有情致。 錢塘人羅隱者，有當世詩名，自號「江東生」。 威遣使賂遺，敘其宗姓，推爲叔父。 隱亦集其詩寄之。 威酷嗜其作，目己所爲曰《偷江東集》，凡五卷，今鄴中人士諷詠之。 史臣曰史臣曰：魏、鎮、燕三鎮，不能制之也久矣。 兵強地廣，合從連衡。 爵命雖假於朝廷，群臣自謀於元帥。 如史憲誠等五家，其初皆因此而得之，其後亦因此而失之。 蓋不知取之以權，守之以仁，則遠矣。 若善繼者，史氏、羅氏之二子有焉，其餘不足觀也。 贊贊曰：逆取順守，古亦有之。 如其逆守，滅亡必隨。 史、何、韓、樂，世數盛衰。 足以爲鑒，念茲在茲。
Wei was quick-witted and thoroughly versed in administration. He devoted himself to Confucian learning, gathered scholars about him, and collected ten thousand scrolls of books. On spring mornings and moonlit evenings he would compose and recite poetry with his guests and staff, with real grace and feeling. There was Luo Yin of Qiantang, a poet of contemporary renown who styled himself "Born East of the River." Wei sent envoys with gifts, traced their shared clan name, and treated him as an uncle. Yin in turn assembled his poems and sent them to Wei. Wei was devoted to Yin's work and titled his own compositions Collected Poems Pilfered from East of the River in five scrolls; people in Ye still recite them today. The historian writes: The commands of Wei, Zhen, and Yan have long defied central control. Their armies are strong and their territories vast; they league together in shifting alliances. Though their titles come from the court on loan, their officers plot among themselves under their commanders. The five houses represented here, beginning with Shi Xiancheng, first won power in this way and later lost it by the same means. They did not understand that power seized by expedient means must be held by humane governance — a lesson far beyond them. Among those who ruled well, only the sons of the Shi and Luo houses merit notice; the rest are scarcely worth mention. In summary: Seizing power by force yet holding it through loyal service — history offers precedents even for that. But to hold power through continued rebellion is to invite certain ruin. The houses of Shi, He, Han, and Le rose and fell across the generations. Their story is warning enough — let it be kept always in mind.
