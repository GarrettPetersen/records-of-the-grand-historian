許敬宗
Xu Jingzong
2
許敬宗，杭州新城人，隋禮部侍郎善心子也。 其先自高陽南渡，世仕江左。 敬宗幼善屬文，舉秀才，授淮陽郡司法書佐，俄直謁者台，奏通事舍人事。 江都之難，善心為宇文化及所害。 敬宗流轉，投於李密，密以為元帥府記室，與魏徵同為管記。 武德初，赤牒擬漣州別駕。 太宗聞其名，召補秦府學士。 貞觀八年，累除著作郎，兼修國史，遷中書舍人。 十年，文德皇后崩，百官縗絰。 率更令歐陽詢狀貌醜異，眾或指之，敬宗見而大笑，為御史所劾，左授洪州都督府司馬。 累遷給事中，兼修國史。 十七年，以修《武德》、《貞觀實錄》成，封高陽縣男，賜物八百段，權檢校黃門侍郎。 高宗在春宮，遷太子右庶子。 十九年，太宗親伐高麗，皇太子定州監國，敬宗與高士廉等共知機要。 中書令岑文本卒於行所，令敬宗以本官檢校中書侍郎。 太宗大破遼賊於駐蹕山，敬宗立於馬前受旨草詔書，詞彩甚麗，深見嗟賞。 先是，庶人承乾廢黜，宮僚多被除削，久未收敘。 敬宗上表曰：「臣聞先王慎罰，務在於恤刑，往哲寬仁，義在於宥過。 聖人之道，莫尚於茲。 竊見廢官，五品以上，除名棄斥，頗歷歲時。 但庶人疇昔之年，身處不疑之地，苞藏悖逆，陰結宰臣，所預奸謀，多連宗戚。 禍生慮表，非可防萌，宮內官僚，迥無關預。 今乃投鼠及器，孰謂無冤？ 焚山毀玉，稍同遷怒。 伏尋先典，例有可原。 昔吳國陪臣，則爰絲不坐於劉濞； 昌邑中尉，則王吉免緣於海昏。 譬諸欒布，乃策名於彭越； 比乎田叔，亦委質於張敖。 主以凶逆，陷其誅夷； 臣以賢良，荷彼收擢。 歷觀往代，此類尤多。 近者有隋，又遵斯義。 楊勇之廢，罪止加於佞人，李綱之徒，皆不預於刑網。 古今裁其折衷，史籍稱為美談。 而今張玄素、令狐德棻、趙弘智、裴宣機、蕭鈞等，並砥節勵操，有雅望於當朝； 經明行修，播令名於天下。 或以直言而遭箠撲，或以忤意而見猜嫌，一概雷同，並罹天憲，恐於王道，傷在未弘。 」由是玄素等稍得敘用。 二十一年，加銀青光祿大夫。
Xu Jingzong, a native of Xincheng in Hangzhou, was the son of Shanshan, who had served the Sui as Vice Minister of Rites. His family had crossed south from Gaoyang and for generations held office in the lands south of the Yangtze. As a youth Jingzong excelled at literary composition. He passed the xiucai examination and was appointed judicial clerk of Huaiyang commandery; before long he served on the Direct Petitioners Board and petitioned for appointment as Master of Ceremonies Concierge. During the upheaval at Jiangdu, Shanshan was killed by Yuwen Huaji. Jingzong wandered from place to place until he joined Li Mi, who appointed him secretary in the Marshal's Headquarters, where he and Wei Zheng shared duties as record-keepers. Early in the Wude reign a red patent named him acting prefect of Lianzhou. When the future Emperor Taizong heard of him, he summoned Jingzong to serve as a scholar in the Prince of Qin's household. In the eighth year of the Zhenguan era he rose through successive posts to Gentleman of the Writing Office, served concurrently as compiler of the national history, and was promoted to Secretariat Drafter. In the tenth year Empress Wende died, and the entire bureaucracy went into mourning dress. Ouyang Xun, Director of the Bureau of Weights and Measures, had a grotesque appearance, and people in the crowd would sometimes point at him. When Jingzong saw this he laughed aloud. The censorate impeached him, and he was demoted to army adjutant under the Hongzhou area command. He was promoted in stages to Attendant Within the Gates while continuing to compile the national history. In the seventeenth year, when the Veritable Records of the Wude and Zhenguan reigns were finished, he was enfeoffed as Baron of Gaoyang, rewarded with eight hundred bolts of silk and goods, and given acting authority as Vice Minister of the Palace Secretariat. While Gaozong was still crown prince, Jingzong was appointed Right Vice Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent. In the nineteenth year Emperor Taizong led a campaign against Goguryeo in person. The crown prince remained at Dingzhou to oversee the realm, and Jingzong joined Gao Shilian and others in managing confidential state affairs. Chief Secretariat Counselor Cen Wende died on campaign, and Jingzong was ordered to act as Vice Minister of the Secretariat while keeping his existing rank. When Emperor Taizong routed the Liaodong forces at Zhubi Mountain, Jingzong stood at his horse's head to receive orders and draft an edict on the spot. The prose was so polished that the emperor admired it warmly. Earlier, after the deposed heir Chengqian was reduced to commoner status, many of his former palace staff had been dismissed and stripped of rank, and for years none had been restored to office. Jingzong submitted a memorial that read: "I have heard that the ancient kings were cautious in imposing punishments and strove above all to temper justice with mercy, and that the sages of old were lenient and humane because their purpose was to pardon faults." Nothing in the Way of the sages stands higher than this principle. I observe that dismissed officials of the fifth rank and above, whose names were struck from the rolls and who were cast out in disgrace, have already endured many years in exile. Yet in years past the deposed heir, though he stood in a position that ought to have been above suspicion, harbored treason and secretly conspired with chief ministers. The plots in which he took part mostly involved his own kin and affines. The disaster arose beyond what anyone could have foreseen at its first stirrings and could not have been stopped in advance. The palace officials had no part in it whatsoever. Yet now the rat is thrown and the vessel is struck as well. Who could call this free of injustice? To burn the mountain and destroy the jade along with it is much like venting one's anger on the innocent. When I examine the precedents of antiquity, I find case after case in which pardon was granted. In former times Yuan Si, an attendant minister of Wu, was not punished for the crime of Liu Pi; and Wang Ji, commandant of Changyi, was spared any connection with the Marquis of Haihun's guilt. Consider Luan Bu, who nevertheless won his place under Peng Yue; or Tian Shu, who likewise pledged his service to Zhang Ao. Their lords, convicted of treason, were executed and their houses destroyed; yet the subjects themselves, judged worthy, were taken up and promoted. Looking back through the ages, examples of this kind are especially numerous. The Sui dynasty, not long ago, followed the same principle. When Yang Yong was deposed, punishment fell only on the flatterers around him. Men such as Li Gang were never caught in the penal net. Ancient and modern alike have weighed such cases in the balance, and the histories praise them as exemplary acts of justice. Yet today Zhang Xuansu, Linghu Defen, Zhao Hongzhi, Pei Xuanji, Xiao Jun, and others have all polished their integrity and strengthened their conduct, and enjoy high repute at court; they are versed in the classics and exemplary in conduct, and their good names are known throughout the realm. Some were beaten for speaking bluntly, others were suspected for giving offense. Treated all alike without distinction, they have alike fallen under imperial punishment. I fear that for the kingly Way the harm lies in its not being broad enough. " As a result Xuansu and the others were gradually restored to office. In the twenty-first year he was given the additional title Silver-Gleam Grand Master of Splendid Happiness.

高宗嗣位，代於志寧為禮部尚書。 敬宗嫁女與蠻酋馮盎之子，多納金寶，為有司所劾，左授鄭州刺史。 永徽三年，入為衛尉卿，加弘文館學士，兼修國史。 六年，復拜禮部尚書，高宗將廢皇后王氏而立武昭儀，敬宗特贊成其計。 長孫無忌、褚遂良、韓瑗等並直言忤旨，敬宗與李義府潛加誣構，並流死於嶺外。 顯慶元年，加太子賓客，尋冊拜侍中，監修國史。 三年，進封郡公，尋贈其父善心為冀州刺史。 高宗因於古長安城遊覽，問侍臣曰：「朕觀故城舊基，宮室似與百姓雜居，自秦、漢已來，幾代都此？ 」敬宗對曰：「秦都咸陽，郭邑連跨渭水，故云『渭水貫都，以象天河。 』至漢惠帝始築此城，其後苻堅、姚萇、後周並都之。 」帝又問：「昆明池是漢武帝何年中開鑿？ 」敬宗對曰：「武帝遣使通西南夷，而為昆明滇池所閉，欲伐昆明國，故因鎬之舊澤，以穿此池，用習水戰，元狩三年事也。 」帝因令敬宗與弘文館學士具檢秦、漢已來歷代宮室處所以奏。 其年，代李義府為中書令，任遇之重，當朝莫比。 龍朔二年，從新令改為右相，加光祿大夫。 三年，冊拜太子少師、同東西台三品，並依舊監修國史。 乾封初，以敬宗年老，不能行步，特令與司空李勣，每朝日各乘小馬入禁門至內省。
When Emperor Gaozong succeeded to the throne, Jingzong replaced Yu Zhining as Minister of Rites. Jingzong gave his daughter in marriage to the son of the tribal chieftain Feng Ang and accepted a large payment in gold and jewels. The responsible offices impeached him, and he was demoted to prefect of Zhengzhou. In the third year of the Yonghui era he was recalled to the capital as Minister of the Guard, made a Scholar of the Hongwen Institute, and again charged with compiling the national history. In the sixth year he was again appointed Minister of Rites. When Emperor Gaozong planned to depose Empress Wang and install Lady Wu as consort, Jingzong strongly backed the scheme. Zhangsun Wuji, Chu Suiliang, Han Yuan, and others all spoke bluntly against the emperor's will. Jingzong and Li Yifu secretly fabricated charges against them, and they were all exiled to the far south, where they died. In the first year of the Xianqing era he was made Guest of the Heir Apparent, and soon afterward was appointed Palace Attendant and put in charge of compiling the national history. In the third year he was advanced to Duke of a commandery, and soon afterward his father Shanshan was posthumously appointed prefect of Jizhou. While touring the old Chang'an city, Emperor Gaozong asked his ministers: "I see the old foundations here. The palaces seem to have stood mingled with ordinary dwellings. Since Qin and Han times, how many dynasties have used this place as their capital?" " Jingzong replied: "The Qin capital was at Xianyang, and its outer wards stretched across the Wei River. Hence the saying, 'The Wei River runs through the capital, like the River of Heaven across the sky.'" " Emperor Hui of Han was the first to build this city. Later Fu Jian, Yao Chang, and the Northern Zhou all made it their capital as well." " The emperor then asked: "In which year of Emperor Wu of Han was Kunming Pool excavated?" " Jingzong replied: "Emperor Wu sent envoys to open relations with the peoples of the southwest, but they were blocked by the waters of Lake Dian near Kunming. Wishing to campaign against the Kunming state, he followed the old marshlands of the Jing River basin to cut this pool and train his forces in naval warfare. That was in the third year of the Yuanshuo era." " The emperor then ordered Jingzong and the scholars of the Hongwen Institute to research and report on the palace sites of every dynasty since Qin and Han times. That year he replaced Li Yifu as Chief Secretariat Counselor. No one at court enjoyed greater favor or heavier responsibility. In the second year of the Longshuo era, under the new administrative statutes he was redesignated Right Chancellor and given the additional title Grand Master of Splendid Happiness. In the third year he was appointed Junior Tutor of the Heir Apparent and Third Rank with equal standing on the Eastern and Western Offices, while continuing as before to supervise compilation of the national history. Early in the Qianfeng era, because Jingzong was elderly and could no longer walk easily, a special order was issued that he and Minister of Works Li Ji might each ride a pony through the forbidden gate to the inner offices on days of court audience.

敬宗自掌知國史，記事阿曲。 初，虞世基與敬宗父善心同為宇文化及所害，封德彝時為內史舍人，備見其事，因謂人曰：「世基被誅，世南匍匐而請代； 善心之死，敬宗舞蹈以求生。 」人以為口實，敬宗深銜之，及為德彝立傳，盛加其罪惡。 敬宗嫁女與左監門大將軍錢九隴，本皇家隸人，敬宗貪財與婚，乃為九隴曲敘門閥，妄加功績，並升與劉文靜、長孫順德同卷。 敬宗為子娶尉遲寶琳孫女為妻，多得賂遺，及作寶琳父敬德傳，悉為隱諸過咎。 太宗作《威鳳賦》以賜長孫無忌，敬宗改雲賜敬德。 白州人龐孝泰，蠻酋凡品，率兵從征高麗，賊知其懦，襲破之。 敬宗又納其寶貨，稱孝泰頻破賊徒，斬獲數萬。 漢將驍健者，唯蘇定方與龐孝泰耳，曹繼叔、劉伯英皆出其下。 虛美隱惡如此！ 初，高祖、太宗兩朝實錄，其敬播所修者，頗多詳直，敬宗又輒以己愛憎曲事刪改，論者尤之。 然自貞觀已來，朝廷所修《五代史》及《晉書》、《東殿新書》、《西域圖志》、《文思博要》、《文館詞林》、《累璧》、《瑤山玉彩》《姓氏錄》、《新禮》，皆總知其事，前後賞賚，不可勝紀。 敬宗好色無度。 其長子昂頗有才藻，歷位太子舍人。 母裴氏早卒。 裴侍婢有姿色，敬宗嬖之，以為繼室，假姓虞氏。 昂素與通，烝之不絕，敬宗怒黜虞氏，加昂以不孝，奏請流於嶺外。 顯慶中，表乞昂還，除虔化令，尋卒。 咸亨元年，抗表乞骸骨，詔聽致仕，仍加特進，俸祿如舊。 三年薨，年八十一。 高宗為之舉哀，廢朝三日，詔文武百官就第赴哭，冊贈開府儀同三司、揚州大都督，陪葬昭陵。 文集八十捲。 太常將定謚，博士袁思古議曰：「敬宗位以才升，歷居清級，然棄長子於荒徼，嫁少女於夷落。 聞《詩》學《禮》，事絕於趨庭； 納采問名，唯聞於黷貨。 白圭斯玷，有累清塵，易名之典，須憑實行。 按謚法『名與實爽曰繆』，請謚為『繆』。 」敬宗孫、太子舍人彥伯不勝其恥，與思古大相忿競，又稱思古與許氏先有嫌隙，請改謚官。 太常博士王福畤議曰：「謚者，飾終之稱也，得失一朝，榮辱千載。 若使嫌隙是實，即合據法推繩； 如其不虧直道，義不可奪，官不可侵。 二三其德，何以言禮？ 福畤忝當官守，匪躬之故。 若順風阿意，背直從曲，更是甲令虛設，將謂禮院無人，何以激揚雅道，顧視同列！ 請依思古謚議為定。 」戶部尚書戴至德謂福畤曰：「高陽公任遇如此，何以定謚為『繆』？ 」答曰：「昔晉司空何曾薨，太常博士秦秀謚為繆丑公。 何曾既忠且孝，徒以日食萬錢，所以貶為繆丑。 況敬宗忠孝不逮於曾，飲食男女之累，有逾於何氏，而謚之為『繆』，無負於許氏矣。 」時有詔令尚書省五品已上重議，禮部尚書袁思敬議稱：「按謚法『既過能改曰恭』，請謚曰『恭』。 」詔從其議。 彥伯，昂之子，起家著作郎。 敬宗末年文筆，多令彥伯代作。 又納婢妾讒言，奏流於嶺表，後遇赦得還，除太子舍人。 早卒，有集十捲。
Once Jingzong took personal charge of the national history, he recorded events in a flattering and distorted manner. Earlier, Yu Shiji and Jingzong's father Shanshan had both been killed by Yuwen Huaji. Feng Deyi was then a Secretariat drafter and witnessed the whole affair. He later told others: "When Shiji was condemned to death, his brother Shinan crawled forward on his knees to beg to die in his place; when Shanshan faced death, Jingzong danced and capered to save his own life." " People repeated this story for years. Jingzong bore a deep grudge against Feng Deyi, and when he came to write Deyi's biography he piled on accusations of crime and wickedness. Jingzong gave his daughter in marriage to Qian Jiulong, General of the Left Gate Guards, who had originally been a bondservant of the imperial house. Greedy for the bride-price, Jingzong bent Jiulong's genealogy to flatter him, invented achievements for him, and placed his biography in the same fascicle as those of Liu Wenjing and Zhangsun Shunde. He arranged for his son to marry the granddaughter of Yuchi Baolin and accepted a large bride-price. When he wrote the biography of Baolin's father Jingde, he concealed every fault and failing. Emperor Taizong had composed the "Rhapsody on the Majestic Phoenix" as a gift to Zhangsun Wuji, but Jingzong altered the record to say it had been given to Jingde instead. Pang Xiaotai of Baizhou, a tribal chieftain of no distinction, led troops on the campaign against Goguryeo. The enemy knew he was timid and routed his force. Jingzong accepted bribes from him and recorded that Xiaotai had repeatedly routed the enemy and taken tens of thousands of heads and captives. Among the valiant Han commanders of the day, he wrote, only Su Dingfang and Pang Xiaotai stood at the top, while Cao Jishu and Liu Boying ranked below them. Such was the way he inflated merit and concealed wrongdoing! The Veritable Records of the Gaozu and Taizong reigns, in the portions compiled by Jing Bo, had been detailed and forthright in many places. Jingzong then arbitrarily cut and altered events to suit his own likes and dislikes, and critics especially condemned him for it. Yet from the Zhenguan era onward he had overall charge of such court compilations as the History of the Five Dynasties, the Book of Jin, the New Book of the Eastern Hall, the Gazetteer of the Western Regions, the Literary Thoughts Broad Compendium, the Forest of Literary Phrases of the Literary Institute, the Accumulated Jade Tablets, the Jade Splendor of Mount Yao, the Record of Surnames, and the New Rites. The rewards he received over the years were beyond counting. Jingzong was insatiably lustful. His eldest son Ang was gifted in letters and had served as Attendant of the Heir Apparent. Ang's mother, of the Pei clan, had died young. A maid of the Pei household was beautiful, and Jingzong took her as a favorite and made her his successor wife under the false surname Yu. Ang had long been intimate with her and continued the affair without cease. Jingzong in anger dismissed the woman of the Yu clan, charged Ang with filial impiety, and memorialized to have him exiled beyond the Lingnan passes. During the Xianqing era he memorialized to have Ang recalled, appointed him magistrate of Qianhua, and Ang soon died. In the first year of the Xianheng era he submitted a memorial requesting retirement. An edict granted his request while still conferring Special Advancement on him, with his salary unchanged. In the third year he died, at the age of eighty-one. Emperor Gaozong mourned him in person, suspended court for three days, and ordered civil and military officials of every rank to attend the lamentation at his residence. By patent he was posthumously granted Honorary Grand Master of the Palace with Golden Bells and Grand Area Commander of Yangzhou, with the honor of burial accompaniment at Zhaoling. His collected writings ran to eighty fascicles. When the Court of Imperial Sacrifices was about to fix his posthumous name, Erudite Yuan Sigu proposed: "Jingzong rose through talent and held high office, yet he abandoned his eldest son in a remote frontier and gave his young daughter in marriage to a barbarian settlement." "He had heard the Odes and studied the Rites, yet in practice he failed the test of instruction in his own courtyard; and when betrothal gifts were exchanged and the bride's name asked, one heard only of bribes." "A stain on white jade mars a man's pure reputation. The canon for fixing a posthumous name must rest on actual conduct." "By the canon of posthumous names, 'when name and reality diverge, the name is Miu.' I therefore propose the posthumous name Miu." " Jingzong's grandson Yanbo, who served as Attendant of the Heir Apparent, could not bear the shame. He quarreled fiercely with Sigu and claimed that Sigu had long borne a grudge against the Xu clan, requesting that the officer responsible for the posthumous name be replaced. Erudite Wang Fuchou of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices argued: "A posthumous name is the title that adorns a man's end. What is decided in a single morning may bring honor or disgrace for a thousand years." "If the grudge is real, then the law should be invoked and an investigation pursued; but if he has not failed the straight Way, then righteousness cannot be set aside and his office cannot be infringed." "To waver in one's principles—how can one speak of ritual propriety?" "I hold this office in humility and act as duty requires, without bending to please others." "If I followed the prevailing wind and flattered power, turning my back on what is straight to follow what is crooked, the statutes would become an empty show and people would say the Ritual Office had no men of principle. How then could the refined Way be upheld, or one's colleagues faced with a clear conscience?" "I ask that Sigu's proposal for the posthumous name be adopted as final." " Minister of Revenue Dai Zhide said to Fuchou: "The Duke of Gaoyang enjoyed such favor at court. How can his posthumous name be fixed as Miu?" " He replied: "In former times, when He Zeng, Minister of Works of Jin, died, Erudite Qin Xiu of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices gave him the posthumous name Duke Miu the Ugly." "He Zeng was loyal and filial in every respect, yet he was demoted to Miu the Ugly solely because he spent ten thousand coins a day on food." "Jingzong's loyalty and filial piety fell far short of He Zeng's, while his indulgence in food, drink, and women exceeded the He's. To give him the posthumous name Miu does the Xu clan no injustice at all." " At that time an edict ordered officials of the fifth rank and above in the Department of State Affairs to deliberate again. Minister of Rites Yuan Sijing proposed: "By the canon of posthumous names, 'having faults yet able to reform is called Gong.' I therefore request the posthumous name Gong." " An edict adopted his proposal. Yanbo, Ang's son, began his career as Gentleman of the Writing Office. In his later years Jingzong had Yanbo write most of his literary compositions for him. Jingzong again listened to slander from maids and concubines and memorialized to exile Yanbo to the far south. Yanbo was later recalled under an amnesty and appointed Attendant of the Heir Apparent. He died young. His collected writings ran to ten fascicles.

Li Yifu was a native of Raoyang in Yingzhou. His grandfather had served as assistant magistrate of Shehong county in Zizhou, and the family thereafter made its home at Yongtai. In the eighth year of the Zhenguan era, Li Daliang, inspection commissioner of the Jiannan circuit, recommended Yifu to the throne because of his skill at literary composition. He placed at the top of the policy-response examination and was appointed Master of Ceremonies in the Secretariat. Liu Ji, Vice Minister of the Palace Secretariat, and Ma Zhou, Attendant Censor for Documents, both praised and recommended him, and he was soon appointed Investigating Censor. An edict further ordered him to attend the Prince of Jin while retaining his original post. When the prince entered the Eastern Palace, Yifu was appointed Attendant of the Heir Apparent and made a Direct Scholar of the Chongxian Institute. He and the Heir Apparent's Secretariat Drafter Lai Ji were both known for their literary gifts, and at court they were spoken of as Lai and Li. 邃初冥昧，元氣氤氳。 二儀始闡，三才既分。 司乾立宰，出《震》為君。 化昭淳樸，道映典墳。 功成揖讓，事極華、勳。 肇興夏啟，降及姬文。 咸資繼德，永樹高芬。 百代沿襲，千齡奉聖。 粵若我後，丕承寶命。 允穆三階，爰齊七政。 時雍化洽，風移俗盛。 載崇國本，式延家慶。 《震》維標德，《離》警體正。 寄切宗祧，事隆監撫。 思皇茂則，敬詢端輔。 業光啟、誦，藝優干羽。 九載崇儒，三朝問豎，歷選儲儀，遺文在斯。 望試登俎，高諭喬枝。 俯容思順，非禮無施。 前修盛業，來哲通規。 飭躬是蹈，則叡問風馳； 立志或爽，則玄猷日虧。 無恃尊極，修途難測； 無恃親賢，失德靡全。 勿輕小善，積小而名自聞； 勿輕微行，累微而身自正。 佞諛有類，邪巧多方。 其萌不絕，其害必彰。 監言斯屏，儲業攸昌。 竊惟令嗣，有殊前事。 雖以貴以賢，而非長非次。 皇明眷德，超倫作貳。 匪懋聲華，莫酬恩異。 匪崇徽烈，莫符天志。 勉之又勉，光茲守器。 下臣司箴，敢告近侍。
In the remote beginning all was dark and obscure, and primordial qi swirled in mist. The two cosmic principles first unfolded, and heaven, earth, and humanity took their separate forms. He who governs Heaven established the ruler, and from the hexagram Zhen the sovereign emerged. Transformation revealed the pure and plain way of antiquity, and the Way shone upon the canonical texts. When merit was achieved came yielding and abdication, and affairs reached their height with Yao and Shun. It rose first with Xia Qi and descended to King Wen of Zhou. All relied on inherited virtue and planted a lasting fragrance of renown. For a hundred generations the pattern was handed down, and for a thousand ages the sage was served. Thus our sovereign greatly received the precious mandate of Heaven. He harmonized the three ranks of society and aligned the seven celestial regulators. The age was harmonious and transformation complete; customs shifted and manners flourished. He exalted the foundation of the state and extended the rejoicing of the imperial house. The hexagram Zhen marks virtue; the hexagram Li warns that the person must be upright. His charge touches closely on the ancestral temple, and his affairs are weighty in supervision and nurture. Think deeply on the sovereign's abundant standards and respectfully consult upright assistants. His accomplishments shine like the schools of Qi and Song; his arts excel like the shield-dance and feather-dance. For nine years he honored Confucian learning; for three reigns he questioned the young attendants. Through successive selections of heir-apparent rites, the legacy texts stand here. Look to the trial of ascending the sacrificial stand; lofty instruction is like the tall branch of a tree. Bow in bearing and think on compliance; what is not ritual, do not enact. Former worthies cultivated flourishing achievements; later sages grasped the universal rule. If one disciplines the person and treads this path, sagely inquiry comes like the wind; but if one sets the will awry, the profound design daily declines. Do not rely on supreme eminence, for the cultivated path is hard to measure. Do not rely on kin and worthies; lose virtue and nothing remains whole. Do not slight small acts of goodness; accumulate them and fame will make itself heard. Do not slight slight conduct; accumulate it and the person will right itself. Flattery and sycophancy have their kinds, and wicked craft takes many forms. If their sprouts are not cut off, their harm will surely be manifest. Screen off such words of oversight, and the heir's enterprise will flourish. I reflect that the worthy heir differs from former cases. Although noble and worthy, he is neither the eldest son nor in proper order of succession. Imperial brightness favors his virtue and sets him above his peers as heir. Unless he makes his reputation flourish, he cannot repay the extraordinary favor shown him. Unless he exalts his fine achievements, he cannot match Heaven's intent. Exert yourself again and again, and make bright this guarding of the vessel of state. Your lowly servant holds the office of admonition and ventures to inform those close at hand.

太子表上其文，優詔賜帛四十匹，又令預撰《晉書》。 高宗嗣位，遷中書舍人。 永徽二年，兼修國史，加弘文館學士。 高宗將立武昭儀為皇后，義府嘗密申協贊，尋擢拜中書侍郎、同中書門下三品，監修國史，賜爵廣平縣男。
The crown prince submitted the text in a memorial. An edict of praise granted forty bolts of silk, and Yifu was ordered to take part in compiling the Book of Jin. When Emperor Gaozong succeeded to the throne, Yifu was promoted to Secretariat Drafter. In the second year of the Yonghui era he was charged with compiling the national history and made a Scholar of the Hongwen Institute. When Emperor Gaozong planned to install Lady Wu as empress, Yifu had secretly memorialized in support. He was soon promoted to Vice Minister of the Secretariat and Third Rank with equal standing in the Secretariat and Chancellery, put in charge of compiling the national history, and enfeoffed as Baron of Guangping.
9
義府貌狀溫恭，與人語必嬉怡微笑，而褊忌陰賊。 既處權要，欲人附己，微忤意者，輒加傾陷。 故時人言義府笑中有刀，又以其柔而害物，亦謂之「李貓。 」顯慶元年，以本官兼太子右庶子，進爵為侯。 有洛州婦人淳于氏，坐奸繫於大理，義府聞其姿色，囑大理丞畢正義求為別宅婦，特為雪其罪。 卿段寶玄疑其故，遽以狀聞，詔令按其事，正義惶懼自縊而死。 侍御史王義方廷奏義府犯狀，因言其初容貌為劉洎、馬周所幸，由此得進，言詞猥褻。 帝怒，出義方為萊州司戶，而不問義府奸濫之罪。 義府云：「王御史妄相彈奏，得無愧乎？ 」義方對云：「仲尼為魯司寇七日，誅少正卯於兩觀之下； 義方任御史旬有六日，不能去奸邪於雙闕之前，實以為愧。 」尋兼太子左庶子。 二年，代崔敦禮為中書令，兼檢校御史大夫，監修國史、學士並如故。 尋加太子賓客，進封河間郡公。 三年，又追贈其父德晟為魏州刺史，諸子孩抱者並列清官，詔為造甲第，榮寵莫之能比。 而義府貪冒無厭，與母、妻及諸子、女婿賣官鬻獄，其門如市。 多引腹心，廣樹朋黨，傾動朝野。 初，杜正倫為中書侍郎，義府時任典儀，至是乃與正倫同為中書令。 正倫每以先進自處，不下義府，而中書侍郎李友益密與正倫共圖議義府，更相伺察。 義府知而密令人封奏其事。 正倫與義府訟於上前，各有曲直。 上以大臣不和，兩責之，左貶義府為普州刺史，正倫為橫州刺史，友益配流峰州。 四年，復召義府兼吏部尚書、同中書門下三品，自余官封如故。 龍朔元年，丁母憂去職。 二年，起復為司列太常伯、同東西台三品。 義府尋請改葬其祖父，營墓於永康陵側。 三原令李孝節私課丁夫車牛，為其載土築墳，晝夜不息。 於是高陵、櫟陽、富平、雲陽、華原、同官、涇陽等七縣，以孝節之故，懼不得已，悉課丁車赴役。 高陵令張敬業恭勤怯懦，不堪其勞，死於作所。 王公已下，爭致贈遺，其羽儀、導從、轜輶、器服，並窮極奢侈。 又會葬車馬祖奠供帳，自灞橋屬於三原，七十里間，相繼不絕。 武德已來，王公葬送之盛，未始有也。 義府本無藻鑑才，怙武後之勢，專以賣官為事。 銓序失次，人多怨讟。 時殷王初出閣，又以義府兼王府長史。 三年，遷右相，殷王府長史仍知選事並如故。 義府入則諂言自媚，出則肆其奸宄，百僚畏之，無敢言其過者。 帝頗知其罪失，從容誡義府云：「聞卿兒子、女婿皆不謹慎，多作罪過，我亦為卿掩覆，未即公言，卿可誡勖，勿令如此。 」義府勃然變色，腮頸俱起，徐曰：「誰向陛下道此？ 」上曰：「但我言如是，何須問我所從得耶！ 」義府睆然，殊不引咎，緩步而去。 上亦優容之。 初，五禮儀注，自前代相沿，吉凶畢舉。 太常博士蕭楚材、孔志約以皇室凶禮為預備凶事，非臣子所宜言之。 義府深然之。 於是悉刪而焚焉。 義府既貴之後，又自言本出趙郡，始與諸李敘昭穆，而無賴之徒苟合，藉其權勢，拜伏為兄叔者甚眾。 給事中李崇德初亦與同譜敘昭穆，及義府出為普州刺史，遂即除削。 義府聞而銜之，及重為宰相，乃令人誣構其罪，竟下獄自殺。 初，貞觀中，太宗命吏部尚書高士廉、御史大夫韋挺、中書侍郎岑文本、禮部侍郎令狐德棻等及四方士大夫諳練門閥者修《氏族志》，勒成百卷，升降去取，時稱允當，頒下諸州，藏為永式。 義府恥其家代無名，乃奏改此書，專委禮部郎中孔志約、著作郎楊仁卿、太子洗馬史玄道、太常丞呂才重修。 志約等遂立格云：「皇朝得五品官者，皆升士流。 」於是兵卒以軍功致五品者，盡入書限，更名為《姓氏錄》。 由是搢紳士大夫多恥被甄敘，皆號此書為「勳格」。 義府仍奏收天下《氏族志》本焚之。 關東魏、齊舊姓，雖皆淪替，猶相矜尚，自為婚姻。 義府為子求婚不得，乃奏隴西李等七家，不得相與為婚。
Yifu had a mild and respectful appearance and always smiled pleasantly when he spoke with others, yet he was narrow-minded, jealous, and secretly treacherous. Once he held power, he demanded that others attach themselves to him. Anyone who slightly offended him was at once overthrown and entrapped. People of the time therefore said that Yifu had a knife within his smile, and because he was soft yet harmed others, they also called him "Li the Cat." " In the first year of the Xianqing era he was made Right Vice Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent while retaining his original post, and was advanced in rank to Marquis. A woman of the Chunyu clan in Luozhou was imprisoned in the Court of Judicial Review on a charge of adultery. When Yifu heard of her beauty, he instructed Vice Director Bi Zhengyi to obtain her for his separate residence and had her crime cleared. Senior Director Duan Baoxuan suspected what was happening and hastily reported the matter. An edict ordered an investigation, and Zhengyi, in fear and panic, hanged himself. Attendant Censor Wang Yifang memorialized in court on Yifu's offenses and said that at first his appearance had won the favor of Liu Ji and Ma Zhou, through which he had advanced. His language was obscene and lewd. The emperor was angry and sent Yifang out as revenue clerk of Laizhou, but did not inquire into Yifu's crimes of wickedness and excess. Yifu said: "Censor Wang recklessly impeached me. Should he not feel ashamed?" " Yifang replied: "Confucius served as Minister of Crime of Lu for seven days and executed Shaozheng Mao beneath the two towers of the gate." I have served as censor for sixteen days and could not remove the wicked and treacherous before the paired towers of the palace gate. That is what truly shames me." " He was soon made Left Vice Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent as well. In the second year he replaced Cui Dunli as Chief Secretariat Counselor, was given acting authority as Censor-in-Chief, and continued as before to supervise compilation of the national history and hold his scholar post. He was soon made Guest of the Heir Apparent and advanced to Duke of Hejian commandery. In the third year his father Desheng was posthumously appointed prefect of Weizhou. All his sons still in infancy were given pure offices, and an edict ordered a grand mansion built for him. No one at court enjoyed honor and favor to match his. Yet Yifu was insatiably greedy. With his mother, wife, sons, and sons-in-law he sold offices and traded in judicial cases, and his gate was thronged like a market. He drew in many trusted intimates, built broad networks of faction, and swayed court and countryside alike. Earlier Du Zhenglun had been Vice Minister of the Secretariat while Yifu held the post of Master of Ceremonies. Now the two men served together as Chief Secretariat Counselor. Zhenglun always treated himself as the senior man and would not defer to Yifu. Vice Minister Li Youyi secretly joined Zhenglun in plotting against Yifu, and the two men watched each other closely. When Yifu learned of this, he secretly had someone seal and memorialize the matter to the throne. Zhenglun and Yifu argued their case before the emperor, and each had some right and some wrong on his side. Because the great ministers were at odds, the emperor blamed both men and demoted Yifu to prefect of Puzhou and Zhenglun to prefect of Hengzhou. Youyi was sentenced to exile at Fengzhou. In the fourth year Yifu was recalled and made Minister of the Civil Service with Third Rank equal standing in the Secretariat and Chancellery. His other offices and enfeoffment remained unchanged. In the first year of the Longshuo era he left office to observe mourning for his mother. In the second year he was recalled from mourning as Minister of Rites in charge of ranks and Third Rank with equal standing on the Eastern and Western Offices. Yifu soon requested permission to rebury his grandfather and built a tomb beside Yongkang Mausoleum. Li Xiaojie, magistrate of Sanyuan, privately levied corvée laborers, carts, and oxen to haul earth and build the mound for him, working day and night without cease. Thereupon the seven counties of Gaoling, Liyang, Fuping, Yunyang, Huayuan, Tongguan, and Jingyang, fearing Xiaojie's power, had no choice but to levy corvée laborers and carts for the work. Zhang Jingye, magistrate of Gaoling, was respectful and diligent but timid and frail. He could not bear the labor and died at the work site. From princes and dukes downward, all vied to send gifts. His escort, attendants, carriages, and vessels and garments were pushed to the utmost luxury. At the joint burial the carriages, horses, roadside offerings, and supply tents stretched from Ba Bridge to Sanyuan, and for seventy li they followed one after another without a break. Since the Wude era there had never been such magnificence in the burial procession of a prince or duke. Yifu had no real talent for judging men. Relying on Empress Wu's power, he devoted himself solely to selling offices. The order of selection and ranking was thrown into chaos, and resentment spread widely. At that time the Prince of Yin had just left the palace quarters, and Yifu was again made Chief Secretary of the princely establishment. In the third year he was transferred to Right Chancellor while continuing as Chief Secretary of the Prince of Yin's establishment and retaining charge of selection affairs. Within the palace Yifu spoke flattering words to please those in power; outside it he unleashed his treachery and wickedness. The officials feared him, and none dared speak of his faults. The emperor was well aware of his crimes and faults and calmly admonished him: "I hear that your sons and sons-in-law are all careless and commit many offenses. I have covered this for you and have not yet spoken of it publicly. Warn and encourage them. Do not let things go on this way." " Yifu flushed with anger, his cheeks and neck swelling, and said slowly: "Who told Your Majesty this?" " The emperor said: "It is simply that I speak thus. Why must you ask where I learned it?" " Yifu stared blankly, showed no sign of accepting blame, and walked away with slow steps. The emperor treated him with forbearance nonetheless. At first the annotated rituals of the Five Rites, handed down from former ages, included both auspicious and inauspicious rites in full. Erudites Xiao Chucai and Kong Zhiyue of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices argued that inauspicious rites of the imperial house were preparations for inauspicious events and were not what subjects should speak of. Yifu strongly agreed. He then deleted them all and had them burned. After Yifu had risen to power, he declared that his family originally came from Zhao commandery and began to arrange senior and junior kinship with the various Li clans. Worthless men who joined him opportunistically, borrowing his power, were very many in bowing to him as elder brother or uncle. Attendant Within the Gates Li Chongde had at first also arranged senior and junior kinship with Yifu in the same genealogy. When Yifu was sent out as prefect of Puzhou, Chongde was at once struck from the rolls. Yifu heard of this and bore a deep grudge. When he again became chief minister, he had someone fabricate charges against Chongde, who was eventually imprisoned and took his own life. Earlier, in the Zhenguan era, Emperor Taizong ordered Gao Shilian, Minister of the Civil Service, Wei Ting, Censor-in-Chief, Cen Wende, Vice Minister of the Secretariat, Linghu Defen, Vice Minister of Rites, and other scholar-officials from across the realm skilled in lineage and family standing to compile the Genealogies of Clans. The work ran to a hundred fascicles. Its promotions and demotions were widely judged fair, distributed to every prefecture, and preserved as a permanent standard. Ashamed that his family had no distinguished name for generations, Yifu memorialized to revise the book and entrusted the work solely to Kong Zhiyue, Section Director of Rites, Yang Renqing, Gentleman of the Writing Office, Shi Xuandao, Groom of the Heir Apparent, and Lü Cai, Vice Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. Zhiyue and the others thereupon established a rule stating: "In our dynasty all who obtain office of the fifth rank are to be entered into the gentry stream." " Thereupon soldiers who through military merit reached the fifth rank all entered the book's limits, and it was renamed the Record of Surnames. Because of this many gentry scholar-officials were ashamed to be entered in the register, and all called the book the "Merit Register." Yifu also memorialized to collect every copy of the Genealogies of Clans in the realm and have them burned. The old surnames of Wei and Qi east of the Pass, though all had fallen into decline, still prized one another and made marriages only among themselves. When Yifu failed to obtain a marriage alliance for his son, he memorialized that the seven great families, including the Li of Longxi, might not intermarry with one another.

陰陽占候人杜元紀為義府望氣，雲「所居宅有獄氣，發積錢二千萬乃可厭勝。 」義府信之，聚斂更急切。 義府居母服，有制朔望給哭假，義府輒微服與元紀凌晨共出城東，登古冢候望，哀禮都廢。 由是人皆言其窺覘災眚，陰懷異圖。 義府又遣其子右司議郎津，召長孫無忌之孫延，謂曰：「相為得一官，數日詔書當出。 」居五日，果授延司津監，乃取延錢七百貫。 於是右金吾倉曹參軍楊行穎表言義府罪狀，制下司刑太常伯劉祥道與侍御詳刑對推其事，仍令司空李勣監焉。 按皆有實，乃下制曰：「右相、行殷王府長史、河間郡公李義府，洩禁中之語，鬻寵授之朝恩； 交占候之人，輕朔望之哀禮。 蓄邪黷貨，實玷衣冠； 稔惡嫉賢，載虧政道。 特以任使多年，未忍便加重罰，宜從遐棄，以肅朝倫。 可除名長流巂州。 其子太子右司議郎津，專恃權門，罕懷忌憚，姦淫是務，賄賂無厭，交遊非所，潛報機密，亦宜明罰，屏跡荒裔。 可除名長流振州。 」義府次子率府長史洽、千牛備身洋、子婿少府主簿柳元貞等，皆憑恃受贓，併除名長流延州。 朝野莫不稱慶，時人為之語曰：「今日巨唐年，還誅四凶族。 」四凶者，謂洽及柳元貞等四人也。 或作《河間道行軍元帥劉祥道破銅山大賊李義府露布》，榜之通衢。 義府先多取人奴婢，及敗，一時奔散，各歸其家。 《露布》稱「混奴婢而亂放，各識家而競入」者，謂此也。 乾封元年，大赦，長流人不許還，義府憂憤發疾卒，年五十餘。 文集三十捲，傳於代； 又著《宦遊記》二十捲，尋亡失。 自義府流放後，朝士常憂懼，恐其復來，及聞其死，於是始安。
The yin-yang diviner Du Yuanji performed qi-watching for Yifu and said: "Your residence bears the qi of prison. You must disburse twenty million in stored cash before the omen can be overcome." " Yifu believed him and gathered wealth even more urgently. While Yifu was in mourning for his mother, regulations allowed leave for lamentation on the first and fifteenth of each month. Yet he would put on ordinary dress and at dawn go out east of the city with Yuanji to climb an ancient mound and observe the heavens, abandoning mourning rites entirely. Because of this everyone said he was spying on calamities and portents and secretly harbored treasonous designs. Yifu also sent his son Jin, Right Secretariat Drafter of the Heir Apparent, to summon Zhangsun Wuji's grandson Yan and tell him: "I can get you an office. The edict will issue in a few days." " Five days later Yan was indeed appointed Director of the Office of River Transport, and Jin then took seven hundred strings of cash from him. Thereupon Yang Xingying, Right Golden Guard Army Revenue Clerk, memorialized on Yifu's crimes. An edict ordered Liu Xiangdao, Minister of Punishments, and the Attendant Censor for Detailed Punishment jointly to investigate the case, with Minister of Works Li Ji appointed to supervise. The investigation substantiated every charge, and an edict was issued: "Right Chancellor, acting Chief Secretary of the Prince of Yin's establishment, Duke of Hejian commandery Li Yifu leaked words from within the forbidden precinct and sold favor in exchange for court grace; associated with diviners of portents and treated lightly the mourning rites due on the first and fifteenth of the month; hoarded wickedness and amassed illicit goods, truly staining the robes and caps of office; ripened in evil and envied the worthy, repeatedly harming the way of government. Only because he has served for many years can we not bear to impose heavy punishment at once. He should be cast to a distant place to restore order at court. His name is to be struck from the rolls and he is long exiled to Xizhou. His son Jin, Right Secretariat Drafter of the Heir Apparent, relied solely on his father's power, rarely showed fear, pursued lewdness, was insatiable in bribes, associated with improper persons, and secretly reported confidential matters. He too should receive clear punishment and be banished to the wild borderlands. His name is to be struck from the rolls and he is long exiled to Zhenzhou. " Yifu's second son Xia, Chief Secretary of the Palace Guard Office, his son Yang, Thousand-Ox Guard Attendant, and his son-in-law Liu Yuanzhen, Assistant Magistrate of the Palace Workshop, and others all relied on his power to accept bribes. Together their names were struck from the rolls and they were long exiled to Yanzhou. " Court and countryside alike celebrated, and people of the time made a saying: "In this great Tang year, the four wicked clans are put to death once again." " The four wicked ones referred to Xia, Liu Yuanzhen, and four others. Someone composed a battle report titled "Marshal Liu Xiangdao of the Hejian Route Army Crushes the Great Bandit Li Yifu of Copper Mountain" and posted it at the crossroads. Yifu had earlier taken many people's slaves. When he fell, they all fled at once and scattered, each returning to his own household. The battle report's phrase about "mixing male and female slaves and releasing them in disorder, each recognizing his household and racing home" referred to this. In the first year of the Qianfeng era a great amnesty was proclaimed, but long exiles were not permitted to return. Yifu fell ill from grief and anger and died, aged more than fifty. His collected writings ran to thirty fascicles and were transmitted in his age; he also wrote the Records of Official Travel in twenty fascicles, which were soon lost. After Yifu was exiled, court gentlemen lived in constant anxiety, fearing he might return. Only when they heard of his death did they at last feel calm.
11
少子湛
The Younger Son Zhan
12
義府少子湛，年六歲時，以父貴授周王文學。 神龍初，累遷右散騎常侍，襲封河間郡公。 時鳳閣侍郎張柬之將誅張易之兄弟，遂引湛為左羽林將軍，令與敬暉等啟請皇太子，備陳將誅易之兄弟意，太子許之。 及兵發，湛與右羽林大將軍李多祚等詣東宮迎皇太子，拒而不時出，湛進啟曰：「逆豎反道亂常，將圖不軌，宗社危敗，實在須臾。 湛等諸將與南衙執事剋期誅翦，伏願殿下暫至玄武門，以副眾望。 」太子曰：「凶豎悖亂，誠合誅夷，然聖躬不豫，慮有驚動。 公等且止，以俟後圖。 」湛曰：「諸將棄家族，共宰相同心戮力，匡輔社稷，殿下奈何不哀其懇誠而欲陷之鼎鑊？ 湛等微命，雖不足惜，殿下速出自止遏。 」太子乃馳馬就路。 湛從至玄武門，斬關而入，率所部兵直至則天所寢長生殿，環繞侍衛。 因奏：「臣等奉令誅逆賊易之、昌宗，恐有漏洩，遂不獲預奏，輒陳兵禁掖，是臣等死罪。 」則天謂湛曰：「卿亦是誅易之軍將耶？ 我於汝父子恩不少，何至是也！ 」則天移就上陽宮，因留湛宿衛。 中宗即位，拜右羽林大將軍，進封趙國公，加實封通前滿五百戶。 頃之，復授左散騎常侍，累轉左領軍衛大將軍。 開元初卒。 崔義玄別有傳。
Yifu's younger son Zhan, at the age of six, was appointed Literary Scholar of the Prince of Zhou because of his father's rank. Early in the Shenlong era he rose through successive posts to Right Regular Attendant of the Cavalry and inherited the enfeoffment as Duke of Hejian commandery. At that time Zhang Jianzhi, Vice Minister of the Phoenix Pavilion, was planning to execute Zhang Yizhi and his brothers. He brought in Zhan as General of the Left Forest of Feathers and ordered him, together with Jing Hui and others, to petition the crown prince and explain fully their plan to kill the Yizhi brothers. The crown prince assented. When the troops set out, Zhan went with Li Duozuo, Right General of the Forest of Feathers, and others to the Eastern Palace to welcome the crown prince, who refused to come out promptly. Zhan stepped forward and said: "The rebellious villains violate the Way and disorder the norm and plot treason. The altars of state stand on the brink of ruin, and the danger is truly a matter of moments." We generals, together with the duty officers of the Southern Yamen, have set a date to cut them down. We humbly beg Your Highness to come for a time to the Xuanwu Gate and answer the hopes of all. " The crown prince said: "The wicked villains are rebellious and truly ought to be executed, yet the sacred person is unwell, and I fear there may be disturbance." You gentlemen must stop for now and wait for a later plan." " Zhan said: "We generals have abandoned our families and, together with the chief minister, exert our strength with one heart to support the altars of state. Why does Your Highness not pity our earnest sincerity and instead wish to cast us into the cauldron?" Our slight lives are not worth cherishing, but Your Highness must quickly come forth yourself to stop and restrain the plot." " The crown prince thereupon spurred his horse onto the road. Zhan followed to the Xuanwu Gate, cut through the barrier and entered, and led his troops straight to the Ever-Vital Hall where Empress Zetian slept, encircling it in guard service. He then memorialized: "We received orders to execute the rebellious villains Yizhi and Changzong. Fearing leakage, we could not memorialize in advance and rashly arrayed troops in the forbidden inner quarters. This is our capital crime." " Empress Zetian said to Zhan: "Are you also one of the generals executing Yizhi?" The favor I have shown your father and you is not small. How could it come to this!" " Empress Zetian was moved to the Shangyang Palace, and Zhan was left to guard her overnight. When Emperor Zhongzong succeeded to the throne, Zhan was appointed Right General of the Forest of Feathers, advanced to Duke of Zhao, and given actual enfeoffment bringing his total household grant to five hundred. Soon he was again appointed Left Regular Attendant of the Cavalry and rose in stages to Left General of the Army Guard. He died early in the Kaiyuan era. Cui Yixuan has a separate biography.
13
史臣曰
The Historiographer's Comment
14
史臣曰：許高陽武德之際，已為文皇入館之賓，垂三十年，位不過列曹尹； 而馬周、劉洎起羈旅徒步，六七年間，皆登宰執。 考其行實，則高陽之文學宏奧，周、洎無以過之，然而太宗任遇相殊者，良以高陽才優而行薄故也。 及屬嗣君沖暗，嬖妾奸邪，阿附豺狼，窺圖權軸，人之凶險，一至於斯。 仲尼所謂「雖有周公之才，不足觀也。 」義府才思精密，所謂「猩猩能言」，鄙哉！
The historiographer writes: Xu of Gaoyang in the Wude period was already a guest whom the future Emperor Taizong brought into his household. For nearly thirty years his rank never rose above director of a bureau or magistrate; yet Ma Zhou and Liu Ji rose from travelers on foot in humble circumstances, and within six or seven years both reached chief minister. Examining their conduct and achievements, Gaoyang's literary learning was vast and profound, and Zhou and Ji could not surpass him. Yet the reason Emperor Taizong favored them so differently was truly that Gaoyang's talent was superior but his conduct was thin. When it fell to a successor who was dull and dark, with favorite concubines and wicked ministers, he fawned on wolves and jackals and spied on the pivot of power. Human viciousness reached this point. What Confucius called "though he has the talent of the Duke of Zhou, he is not worth observing." " Yifu's literary thought was precise and subtle—what people call "the orangutan can speak"—how base!
15
贊曰：貞觀文士，高陽、河間。 圖形學館，染翰書山。 進身以筆，得位由奸。 為虎傅翼，即又胡顏。
In praise: The literary gentlemen of the Zhenguan era—Gaoyang and Hejian. Their portraits hung in the Academy; they stained their brushes on the mountain of books. They advanced themselves by the brush and obtained position through wickedness. To give wings to a tiger—then how can one show a human face?
