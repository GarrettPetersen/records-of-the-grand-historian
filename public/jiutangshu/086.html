許敬宗
Xu Jingzong
2
許敬宗，杭州新城人，隋禮部侍郎善心子也。 其先自高陽南渡，世仕江左。 敬宗幼善屬文，舉秀才，授淮陽郡司法書佐，俄直謁者台，奏通事舍人事。 江都之難，善心為宇文化及所害。 敬宗流轉，投於李密，密以為元帥府記室，與魏徵同為管記。 武德初，赤牒擬漣州別駕。 太宗聞其名，召補秦府學士。 貞觀八年，累除著作郎，兼修國史，遷中書舍人。 十年，文德皇后崩，百官縗絰。 率更令歐陽詢狀貌醜異，眾或指之，敬宗見而大笑，為御史所劾，左授洪州都督府司馬。 累遷給事中，兼修國史。 十七年，以修《武德》、《貞觀實錄》成，封高陽縣男，賜物八百段，權檢校黃門侍郎。 高宗在春宮，遷太子右庶子。 十九年，太宗親伐高麗，皇太子定州監國，敬宗與高士廉等共知機要。 中書令岑文本卒於行所，令敬宗以本官檢校中書侍郎。 太宗大破遼賊於駐蹕山，敬宗立於馬前受旨草詔書，詞彩甚麗，深見嗟賞。 先是，庶人承乾廢黜，宮僚多被除削，久未收敘。 敬宗上表曰：「臣聞先王慎罰，務在於恤刑，往哲寬仁，義在於宥過。 聖人之道，莫尚於茲。 竊見廢官，五品以上，除名棄斥，頗歷歲時。 但庶人疇昔之年，身處不疑之地，苞藏悖逆，陰結宰臣，所預奸謀，多連宗戚。 禍生慮表，非可防萌，宮內官僚，迥無關預。 今乃投鼠及器，孰謂無冤？ 焚山毀玉，稍同遷怒。 伏尋先典，例有可原。 昔吳國陪臣，則爰絲不坐於劉濞； 昌邑中尉，則王吉免緣於海昏。 譬諸欒布，乃策名於彭越； 比乎田叔，亦委質於張敖。 主以凶逆，陷其誅夷； 臣以賢良，荷彼收擢。 歷觀往代，此類尤多。 近者有隋，又遵斯義。 楊勇之廢，罪止加於佞人，李綱之徒，皆不預於刑網。 古今裁其折衷，史籍稱為美談。 而今張玄素、令狐德棻、趙弘智、裴宣機、蕭鈞等，並砥節勵操，有雅望於當朝； 經明行修，播令名於天下。 或以直言而遭箠撲，或以忤意而見猜嫌，一概雷同，並罹天憲，恐於王道，傷在未弘。 」由是玄素等稍得敘用。 二十一年，加銀青光祿大夫。
Xu Jingzong came from Xincheng in Hangzhou; he was the son of Shanzin, who had served the Sui as Vice Minister of Rites. His clan had moved south from Gaoyang and held office in the lower Yangzi region for generations. As a youth Jingzong showed a gift for writing. After passing the provincial examination he became a judicial clerk in Huaiyang commandery, then soon joined the Directorate of Audiences and was posted as Master of Ceremonies for foreign envoys. During the Jiangdu coup Shanzin was killed by Yuwen Huaji. Jingzong drifted through the turmoil until he joined Li Mi, who appointed him secretary on his staff alongside Wei Zheng as co-recorder. Early in the Wude era a formal nomination slated him for assistant administrator of Lianzhou. When Taizong heard of him he summoned him as a literary adviser to the Prince of Qin's household. In Zhenguan 8 he rose to Gentleman of the Palace Library while also compiling the national history, then was promoted to Drafting Secretary of the Secretariat. In the tenth year Empress Wende died, and the entire bureaucracy went into hemp mourning. Ouyang Xun, Director of the Imperial Workshops, had a grotesque appearance, and people in the crowd would point at him. Jingzong saw this and laughed aloud. The censors impeached him, and he was demoted to military adjutant at the Hongzhou headquarters. He was promoted in turn to Attendant Censor while continuing to compile the national history. In the seventeenth year, when the Veritable Records of the Wude and Zhenguan reigns were finished, he was enfeoffed as Baron of Gaoyang, given eight hundred rolls of goods, and appointed acting Vice Director of the Chancellery. While Gaozong was still crown prince, Jingzong was made Right Vice Director of his household. In the nineteenth year Taizong led the campaign against Goguryeo in person. The crown prince remained at Dingzhou to oversee the realm, and Jingzong joined Gao Shilian and others in managing state secrets. Chief Minister Cen Wuben died on campaign, and Jingzong was ordered to serve as acting Vice Director of the Secretariat while keeping his existing post. Taizong won a great victory over the Liaodong forces at Mount Zhubi. Jingzong stood at his horse's head to take dictation and draft the edict; the prose was so fine that the emperor greatly praised him. Earlier, when the deposed heir Chengqian was cast aside, many of his household officials had been dismissed and stripped of rank, and for years had not been reappointed. Jingzong submitted a memorial: "I have heard that the ancient kings were cautious in imposing punishment and sought to temper the law with mercy, and that the sages of old were lenient and humane because their purpose was to forgive faults. In the way of the sage, nothing ranks higher than this. I observe that officials of the fifth rank and above who were dismissed when Chengqian fell—struck from the rolls and cast out—have already waited many years. Yet in those years the deposed heir, though he stood in a position of unquestioned trust, harbored treason, secretly colluded with chief ministers, and drew many kinsmen of the imperial house into his plots. The disaster arose beyond what anyone could foresee and could not be stopped at the first sign; the palace staff had nothing to do with it at all. Now you strike at the rat and break the vessel beside it—who can say there is no injustice? To burn the mountain and ruin the jade within is much like punishing the innocent in a fit of anger. When I consult the ancient statutes, I find precedents for pardon. In antiquity Yuan Si, a minister of Wu, was not punished for Liu Pi's rebellion; and Wang Ji, commandant of Changyi, was spared when the Marquis of Haihun was deposed. Luan Bu entered Peng Yue's service; and Tian Shu pledged himself to Zhang Ao— yet when their lords were executed for treason, the ministers themselves, for their worth, were taken up and promoted. Looking back through the ages, such cases are especially common. The Sui, in our own recent past, followed the same principle. When Yang Yong was deposed, only the flatterers were punished; men such as Li Gang were never caught in the penal net. Ancient and modern historians have praised such balanced judgments as a fine example. Yet today Zhang Xuansu, Linghu Defen, Zhao Hongzhi, Pei Xuanji, Xiao Jun, and others have all polished their integrity and enjoy high repute at court; they are learned in the classics and exemplary in conduct, and their good names are known throughout the realm. Some were beaten for speaking frankly, others fell under suspicion for displeasing the throne—yet all were punished alike. I fear this harms the kingly way and shows that Your Majesty's magnanimity has not yet been fully shown. " As a result Xuansu and the others were gradually restored to office. In the twenty-first year he was given the additional title Silver-Gleam Grand Master of Splendid Happiness.
3
高宗嗣位，代於志寧為禮部尚書。 敬宗嫁女與蠻酋馮盎之子，多納金寶，為有司所劾，左授鄭州刺史。 永徽三年，入為衛尉卿，加弘文館學士，兼修國史。 六年，復拜禮部尚書，高宗將廢皇后王氏而立武昭儀，敬宗特贊成其計。 長孫無忌、褚遂良、韓瑗等並直言忤旨，敬宗與李義府潛加誣構，並流死於嶺外。 顯慶元年，加太子賓客，尋冊拜侍中，監修國史。 三年，進封郡公，尋贈其父善心為冀州刺史。 高宗因於古長安城遊覽，問侍臣曰：「朕觀故城舊基，宮室似與百姓雜居，自秦、漢已來，幾代都此？ 」敬宗對曰：「秦都咸陽，郭邑連跨渭水，故云『渭水貫都，以象天河。 』至漢惠帝始築此城，其後苻堅、姚萇、後周並都之。 」帝又問：「昆明池是漢武帝何年中開鑿？ 」敬宗對曰：「武帝遣使通西南夷，而為昆明滇池所閉，欲伐昆明國，故因鎬之舊澤，以穿此池，用習水戰，元狩三年事也。 」帝因令敬宗與弘文館學士具檢秦、漢已來歷代宮室處所以奏。 其年，代李義府為中書令，任遇之重，當朝莫比。 龍朔二年，從新令改為右相，加光祿大夫。 三年，冊拜太子少師、同東西台三品，並依舊監修國史。 乾封初，以敬宗年老，不能行步，特令與司空李勣，每朝日各乘小馬入禁門至內省。
When Gaozong came to the throne, Jingzong replaced Yu Zhining as Minister of Rites. Jingzong married his daughter to the son of the tribal chieftain Feng Ang and accepted a large bride-price in gold and jewels. The authorities impeached him, and he was demoted to prefect of Zhengzhou. In Yonghui 3 he returned to the capital as Minister of the Imperial Stud, was made an academician of the Hongwen Institute, and again took charge of compiling the national history. In the sixth year he was again made Minister of Rites. When Gaozong planned to depose Empress Wang and install Consort Wu, Jingzong strongly backed the scheme. Zhangsun Wuji, Chu Suiliang, Han Yuan, and others spoke out against the plan; Jingzong and Li Yifu secretly fabricated charges against them, and all died in exile beyond the passes. In Xianqing 1 he was made Guest of the Crown Prince; soon afterward he was appointed Palace Attendant and put in charge of compiling the national history. In the third year he was enfeoffed as Duke of a commandery; soon afterward his father Shanzin was posthumously named prefect of Jizhou. While touring the old city of Chang'an, Gaozong asked his ministers: "Looking at these old foundations, the palaces seem to have stood among ordinary houses. Since Qin and Han times, how many dynasties have used this as their capital? " Jingzong replied: "The Qin capital was at Xianyang, with the outer walls spanning the Wei River—hence the saying, 'The Wei runs through the capital like the River of Heaven. ' Emperor Hui of Han was the first to build this city; later Fu Jian, Yao Chang, and the Northern Zhou all made it their capital. " The emperor then asked: "In which year of Emperor Wu of Han was Kunming Pool dug? " Jingzong replied: "Emperor Wu sent envoys to the southwestern tribes, but they were blocked at Dianchi by the Kunming people. He wished to attack the state of Kunming, so he used the old marshes near the capital to dig this pool for naval drill—it was in Yuanshuo 3. " The emperor then ordered Jingzong and the Hongwen academicians to research and report on the palace sites of every dynasty since Qin and Han. That year he replaced Li Yifu as Chief Minister of the Secretariat; no one at court enjoyed greater favor. In Longshuo 2, under the new administrative code, he became Right Chancellor and was given the additional title Grand Master of Splendid Happiness. In the third year he was formally appointed Junior Tutor of the Crown Prince and Third-Rank Associate of both Chancelleries, and continued to supervise the national history as before. Early in the Qianfeng era, because Jingzong was too old to walk, a special order allowed him and Minister of Works Li Ji each to ride a pony through the palace gate to the inner offices on court days.
4
敬宗自掌知國史，記事阿曲。 初，虞世基與敬宗父善心同為宇文化及所害，封德彝時為內史舍人，備見其事，因謂人曰：「世基被誅，世南匍匐而請代； 善心之死，敬宗舞蹈以求生。 」人以為口實，敬宗深銜之，及為德彝立傳，盛加其罪惡。 敬宗嫁女與左監門大將軍錢九隴，本皇家隸人，敬宗貪財與婚，乃為九隴曲敘門閥，妄加功績，並升與劉文靜、長孫順德同卷。 敬宗為子娶尉遲寶琳孫女為妻，多得賂遺，及作寶琳父敬德傳，悉為隱諸過咎。 太宗作《威鳳賦》以賜長孫無忌，敬宗改雲賜敬德。 白州人龐孝泰，蠻酋凡品，率兵從征高麗，賊知其懦，襲破之。 敬宗又納其寶貨，稱孝泰頻破賊徒，斬獲數萬。 漢將驍健者，唯蘇定方與龐孝泰耳，曹繼叔、劉伯英皆出其下。 虛美隱惡如此！ 初，高祖、太宗兩朝實錄，其敬播所修者，頗多詳直，敬宗又輒以己愛憎曲事刪改，論者尤之。 然自貞觀已來，朝廷所修《五代史》及《晉書》、《東殿新書》、《西域圖志》、《文思博要》、《文館詞林》、《累璧》、《瑤山玉彩》《姓氏錄》、《新禮》，皆總知其事，前後賞賚，不可勝紀。 敬宗好色無度。 其長子昂頗有才藻，歷位太子舍人。 母裴氏早卒。 裴侍婢有姿色，敬宗嬖之，以為繼室，假姓虞氏。 昂素與通，烝之不絕，敬宗怒黜虞氏，加昂以不孝，奏請流於嶺外。 顯慶中，表乞昂還，除虔化令，尋卒。 咸亨元年，抗表乞骸骨，詔聽致仕，仍加特進，俸祿如舊。 三年薨，年八十一。 高宗為之舉哀，廢朝三日，詔文武百官就第赴哭，冊贈開府儀同三司、揚州大都督，陪葬昭陵。 文集八十捲。 太常將定謚，博士袁思古議曰：「敬宗位以才升，歷居清級，然棄長子於荒徼，嫁少女於夷落。 聞《詩》學《禮》，事絕於趨庭； 納采問名，唯聞於黷貨。 白圭斯玷，有累清塵，易名之典，須憑實行。 按謚法『名與實爽曰繆』，請謚為『繆』。 」敬宗孫、太子舍人彥伯不勝其恥，與思古大相忿競，又稱思古與許氏先有嫌隙，請改謚官。 太常博士王福畤議曰：「謚者，飾終之稱也，得失一朝，榮辱千載。 若使嫌隙是實，即合據法推繩； 如其不虧直道，義不可奪，官不可侵。 二三其德，何以言禮？ 福畤忝當官守，匪躬之故。 若順風阿意，背直從曲，更是甲令虛設，將謂禮院無人，何以激揚雅道，顧視同列！ 請依思古謚議為定。 」戶部尚書戴至德謂福畤曰：「高陽公任遇如此，何以定謚為『繆』？ 」答曰：「昔晉司空何曾薨，太常博士秦秀謚為繆丑公。 何曾既忠且孝，徒以日食萬錢，所以貶為繆丑。 況敬宗忠孝不逮於曾，飲食男女之累，有逾於何氏，而謚之為『繆』，無負於許氏矣。 」時有詔令尚書省五品已上重議，禮部尚書袁思敬議稱：「按謚法『既過能改曰恭』，請謚曰『恭』。 」詔從其議。 彥伯，昂之子，起家著作郎。 敬宗末年文筆，多令彥伯代作。 又納婢妾讒言，奏流於嶺表，後遇赦得還，除太子舍人。 早卒，有集十捲。
Once Jingzong took charge of the national history himself, he recorded events in a flattering and distorted way. Earlier Yu Shiji and Jingzong's father Shanzin had both been killed by Yuwen Huaji. Feng Deyi, then a drafting secretary, witnessed the whole affair and remarked: "When Shiji was condemned, his brother Shinan crawled forward and begged to die in his place; but when Shanzin was to die, Jingzong danced and capered to save his own life." " People repeated this story against him. Jingzong deeply resented it, and when he wrote Deyi's biography he greatly exaggerated his faults. He married his daughter to Qian Jiulong, General of the Left Gate Guards, who had originally been an imperial bondservant. Greedy for the bride-price, Jingzong falsified Jiulong's pedigree, invented achievements for him, and placed his biography in the same volume as those of Liu Wenjing and Zhangsun Shunde. He arranged for his son to marry the granddaughter of Yuchi Baolin and accepted many bribes. When he wrote the biography of Baolin's father Jingde, he concealed every fault. Taizong had written the "Ode to the Majestic Phoenix" as a gift to Zhangsun Wuji; Jingzong changed the record to say it had been given to Jingde. Pang Xiaotai of Baizhou was a tribal chieftain of no distinction who led troops against Goguryeo. The enemy knew he was timid, attacked him, and destroyed his force. Jingzong took bribes from him and recorded that Xiaotai had repeatedly routed the enemy and killed or captured tens of thousands. He wrote that among valiant Han generals only Su Dingfang and Pang Xiaotai stood out, with Cao Jishu and Liu Boying beneath them. Such was the way he invented praise and concealed wrongdoing! The Veritable Records of the Gaozu and Taizong reigns compiled by Xu Jingbo had been detailed and straightforward; Jingzong repeatedly altered and deleted passages according to his personal likes and dislikes, which critics especially condemned. Yet from the Zhenguan era onward he oversaw such major court compilations as the History of the Five Dynasties, the Book of Jin, the New Books of the Eastern Hall, the Gazetteer of the Western Regions, the Literary Essence Compendium, the Forest of Literary Phrases, the Accumulated Jade Tablets, the Jade Mountain Splendor, the Register of Surnames, and the New Rites, and the rewards he received are beyond counting. Jingzong was licentious beyond measure. His eldest son Ang was gifted in letters and served as Attendant of the Crown Prince. Ang's mother, of the Pei clan, had died young. A maid of the Pei household was beautiful; Jingzong favored her, took her as his new wife, and gave her the false surname Yu. Ang had long been intimate with her and the affair continued. Jingzong in anger dismissed the Yu woman, charged Ang with unfilial conduct, and memorialized to have him exiled beyond the passes. During Xianqing he memorialized to have Ang recalled; Ang was appointed magistrate of Qianhua and soon died. In Xianheng 1 he submitted a memorial asking to retire; the emperor granted his request, gave him the special advancement rank, and kept his salary unchanged. In the third year he died, at the age of eighty-one. Gaozong mourned him, suspended court for three days, and ordered all officials to attend the mourning at his residence. He was posthumously given the titles Grand Master of the Palace with Golden Bells and Grand Protector General of Yangzhou, and was buried with honor at Zhaoling. His collected writings ran to eighty fascicles. When the Court of Imperial Sacrifices was about to fix his posthumous name, Erudite Yuan Sigu proposed: "Jingzong rose through talent and held high office, yet he abandoned his eldest son in the frontier wilds and married his young daughter to a barbarian chief. He never taught his son the Odes and Rites in the family courtyard; betrothal and marriage were known only through bribes. A white jade tablet is stained—his reputation is tarnished. The canon for posthumous names must rest on actual conduct. By the canon of posthumous names, 'when name and reality diverge, the name is Miu [False].' I request the posthumous name Miu." " Jingzong's grandson Yanbo, Attendant of the Crown Prince, could not bear the shame and quarreled fiercely with Sigu, claiming that Sigu had long held a grudge against the Xu family and asking that another officer be assigned to fix the name. Erudite Wang Fuchou of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices proposed: "The posthumous name adorns a man's end: a moment's judgment, a reputation for a thousand years. If the grudge is real, he should be prosecuted by law; but if he has not violated the straight path, his judgment cannot be set aside and his office cannot be taken from him. To change one's principles—how can one speak of ritual propriety? I hold this office unworthily, but I must not betray my duty. If I bow to pressure and abandon the straight path for the crooked, the statutes become empty words and the Ritual Office is shown to have no integrity. How then can we uphold the refined way or face our colleagues? I ask that Sigu's proposal be adopted as final. " Minister of Revenue Dai Zhide said to Fuchou: "The Duke of Gaoyang enjoyed such favor at court—how can you fix his posthumous name as Miu? " He replied: "When He Zeng, Minister of Works of Jin, died, Erudite Qin Xiu gave him the posthumous name Duke Miu-Chou [False and Ugly]. He Zeng was loyal and filial, yet solely because he spent ten thousand cash a day on food, he was given the name Miu-Chou. Jingzong's loyalty and filial piety did not equal He Zeng's, and his indulgence in food, drink, and women exceeded He Zeng's—yet to give him the name Miu does the Xu family no injustice. " An edict then ordered officials of the fifth rank and above in the Secretariat to deliberate again. Minister of Rites Yuan Sijing proposed: "By the canon, 'having faults yet able to reform is called Gong [Respectful].' I request the posthumous name Gong." " The edict followed his proposal. Yanbo was Ang's son; he began his career as Gentleman of the Palace Library. In Jingzong's later years Yanbo ghost-wrote most of his compositions. Maidservants and concubines slandered him, and he was exiled beyond the passes. After a general amnesty he returned and was appointed Attendant of the Crown Prince. He died young, leaving collected writings of ten fascicles.

李義府
Li Yifu

李義府，瀛州饒陽人也。 其祖為梓州射洪縣丞，因家於永泰。 貞觀八年，劍南道巡察大使李大亮以義府善屬文，表薦之。 對策擢第，補門下省典儀。 黃門侍郎劉洎、侍書御史馬周皆稱薦之，尋除監察御史。 又敕義府以本官兼侍晉王。 及升春宮，除太子舍人，加崇賢館直學士，與太子司議郎來濟俱以文翰見知，時稱來、李。 義府嘗獻《承華箴》，其辭曰：
Li Yifu came from Raoyang in Yingzhou. His grandfather had served as assistant magistrate of Shehong in Zizhou, and the family made its home in Yongtai. In Zhenguan 8 Li Daliang, inspector of the Jiannan circuit, recommended Yifu to the throne for his skill in writing. He placed at the top in the policy-response examination and was appointed master of ceremonial forms in the Chancellery. Vice Minister Liu Ji and Palace Censor Ma Zhou both recommended him, and he was soon appointed Investigating Censor. The emperor also ordered Yifu to serve Prince Jin concurrently while keeping his existing post. When the prince entered the Eastern Palace, Yifu became Attendant of the Crown Prince and resident academician of the Chongxian Hall. He and Lai Ji, secretary for discussion of the crown prince, were both known for their writing, and people spoke of them as Lai and Li. The Younger Son Zhan

義府少子湛，年六歲時，以父貴授周王文學。 神龍初，累遷右散騎常侍，襲封河間郡公。 時鳳閣侍郎張柬之將誅張易之兄弟，遂引湛為左羽林將軍，令與敬暉等啟請皇太子，備陳將誅易之兄弟意，太子許之。 及兵發，湛與右羽林大將軍李多祚等詣東宮迎皇太子，拒而不時出，湛進啟曰：「逆豎反道亂常，將圖不軌，宗社危敗，實在須臾。 湛等諸將與南衙執事剋期誅翦，伏願殿下暫至玄武門，以副眾望。 」太子曰：「凶豎悖亂，誠合誅夷，然聖躬不豫，慮有驚動。 公等且止，以俟後圖。 」湛曰：「諸將棄家族，共宰相同心戮力，匡輔社稷，殿下奈何不哀其懇誠而欲陷之鼎鑊？ 湛等微命，雖不足惜，殿下速出自止遏。 」太子乃馳馬就路。 湛從至玄武門，斬關而入，率所部兵直至則天所寢長生殿，環繞侍衛。 因奏：「臣等奉令誅逆賊易之、昌宗，恐有漏洩，遂不獲預奏，輒陳兵禁掖，是臣等死罪。 」則天謂湛曰：「卿亦是誅易之軍將耶？ 我於汝父子恩不少，何至是也！ 」則天移就上陽宮，因留湛宿衛。 中宗即位，拜右羽林大將軍，進封趙國公，加實封通前滿五百戶。 頃之，復授左散騎常侍，累轉左領軍衛大將軍。 開元初卒。 崔義玄別有傳。
Yifu's youngest son Zhan was only six when his father's rank won him appointment as literary master to the Prince of Zhou. At the outset of the Shenlong reign he rose through successive posts to Right Regular Attendant and inherited the dukedom of Hejian. When Vice Director Zhang Jianzhi of the Fengge Secretariat prepared to kill the Zhang Yizhi brothers, he brought Zhan in as general of the Left Feathered Forest Guard and had him join Jing Hui and the others in asking audience with the crown prince. They laid out their plan to kill the Yizhi brothers, and the prince agreed. When the troops moved, Zhan went with General Li Duozuo of the Right Feathered Forest Guard and others to the Eastern Palace to fetch the crown prince. The prince refused and lingered indoors. Zhan stepped forward and said, "These traitorous villains have overturned the natural order and mean to rebel. The realm hangs by a thread. The generals and the officials of the Southern Yamen have fixed a date to cut them down. I beg Your Highness to come at once to the Xuanwu Gate and answer the army's expectation. " The crown prince said, "Those villains deserve death, but the emperor is ill, and I fear the shock would harm him. Stop for now and wait for a better moment. " Zhan said, "These generals have abandoned their families to stand with the chief ministers and save the throne. How can Your Highness ignore their earnest plea and send them to the cauldron instead? Our lives are nothing—but Your Highness must come out now and take command. " The crown prince then spurred his horse and rode out. Zhan followed him to the Xuanwu Gate, broke through the barrier, and led his troops straight to the Hall of Eternal Life where Empress Wu slept, forming a ring of guards around her. He then reported, "We received orders to execute the traitors Yizhi and Changzong. Fearing a leak, we could not report in advance and have rashly brought troops into the inner palace—this is a capital offense for us. " Empress Wu said to Zhan, "So you too command the troops sent to kill Yizhi? I have done much for you and your father—how did it come to this! " Empress Wu moved to the Shangyang Palace and kept Zhan as her night guard. When Emperor Zhongzong came to the throne, Zhan was made general of the Right Feathered Forest Guard, enfeoffed as Duke of Zhao with total income fiefs of five hundred households. Soon he was again named Left Regular Attendant and later rose to general of the Left Court Guard. He died early in the Kaiyuan reign. Cui Yixuan is treated in a separate biography.

The Historiographer Comments
14
史臣曰：許高陽武德之際，已為文皇入館之賓，垂三十年，位不過列曹尹； 而馬周、劉洎起羈旅徒步，六七年間，皆登宰執。 考其行實，則高陽之文學宏奧，周、洎無以過之，然而太宗任遇相殊者，良以高陽才優而行薄故也。 及屬嗣君沖暗，嬖妾奸邪，阿附豺狼，窺圖權軸，人之凶險，一至於斯。 仲尼所謂「雖有周公之才，不足觀也。 」義府才思精密，所謂「猩猩能言」，鄙哉！
The historiographer writes: Xu of Gaoyang entered Emperor Taizong's palace library as a companion as early as the Wude era, yet for nearly thirty years rose no higher than a department director; yet Ma Zhou and Liu Ji, men who had arrived on foot with nothing, reached the chancellorship within six or seven years. Judged by their deeds, Gaoyang's literary attainments exceeded anything Zhou or Ji could match; yet the reason Taizong favored them so differently was simply that Gaoyang's talent was great but his character was small. Under a dull successor surrounded by favored women and wicked flatterers, he fawned on wolves and jackals and schemed for the heart of power. That human depravity could go so far— This is what Confucius meant when he said, "Even if one had the talent of the Duke of Zhou, he would not be worth watching. " Yifu was clever and polished—precisely the sort of wit Confucius dismissed when he said, "Even an orangutan can talk"—contemptible!
15
贊曰：貞觀文士，高陽、河間。 圖形學館，染翰書山。 進身以筆，得位由奸。 為虎傅翼，即又胡顏。
In summary: Among Zhenguan's men of letters were Gaoyang and Hejian. Their portraits hung in the Academy; their brushes steeped in the mountain of books. They rose by the pen and won office through treachery. Having given wings to a tiger, they still had the shamelessness to show their faces.
