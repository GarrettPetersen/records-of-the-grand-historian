1
朱克融朱克融，賊泚之從孫也。 祖滔，父洄。 克融少為幽州軍校，事節度使劉總。 總將歸朝，慮其有變，籍軍中素有異志者，薦之闕下，時克融亦在籍中。 宰相崔植、杜元穎不知兵，且無遠略，謂兩河無虞，遂奏勒歸鎮。 長慶初，幽州軍亂，囚其帥張弘靖。 時洄廢疾於家，軍中素伏其謀略，至是眾欲立之。 洄自以老且病，推克融統軍務焉。 朝廷尋加檢校左散騎常侍，授以符節。
Zhu Kerong was a grandnephew of the rebel Zhu Ci. His grandfather was Zhu Tao, and his father was Zhu Hui. In his youth Kerong served as an officer in the Youzhou army under Military Governor Liu Zong. When Zong was preparing to return to court, fearing unrest he registered soldiers who had long harbored disloyal ambitions and sent their names to the capital; Kerong was among those listed. Chief Ministers Cui Zhi and Du Yuanying knew nothing of warfare and lacked long-range vision; assuming the two Hebei circuits were secure, they memorialized that the men be sent back to their commands. Early in the Changqing reign the Youzhou army mutinied and imprisoned its commander Zhang Hongjing. Hui was then laid up at home with illness; the troops had long respected his stratagems, and now they wished to install him as leader. Hui, considering himself old and infirm, yielded command of military affairs to Kerong. The court soon appointed him Acting Left Regular Attendant and conferred the seals of command.
2
，遣使送方鎮及三軍時服，克融怒所賜疏弱，執中使以聞。 上特優容，別命中使宣諭，仍改賜衣物，流其使楊文端等。 先是，克融執中使，奏稱：「竊聞陛下欲幸東都，請將兵馬並丁匠五千人，修理宮闕，迎候車駕。 」又上言無衣，擬於朝廷請三十萬端疋，以備一歲所費，不然則三軍不安。 天子怒其悖慢，取宰臣裴度謀，優容之，語見別卷。 克融官至檢校司空、吳興郡王。
The court sent envoys bearing seasonal gifts for the frontier command and the three armies; Kerong, angered that the gifts were meager, seized the imperial envoy and reported the matter to the throne. The Emperor showed him special forbearance, ordered a separate envoy to announce the edict, sent different garments, and banished his envoy Yang Wenduan and others. Earlier, when Kerong had seized the imperial envoy, he had memorialized: "I have heard that Your Majesty intends to visit the Eastern Capital. I ask leave to lead troops and five thousand craftsmen to repair the palaces and await the imperial progress." He also petitioned that the armies lacked clothing and proposed to request three hundred thousand bolts from the court for a year's supply — otherwise the three armies would be unsettled. The Emperor was angered by his insolence and consulted Chief Minister Pei Du, who counselled indulgence; the full account appears in another fascicle. Kerong eventually rose to Acting Minister of Works and was enfeoffed as Prince of Wuxing.
3
其年五月，本州軍亂，殺之，子延齡亦遇害。 次子延嗣竊立，尋為大將李載義所殺。 李載義李載義，字方谷，常山湣王之後。 代以武力稱，繼為幽州屬郡守。 載義少孤，與鄉曲之不令者遊。 有勇力，善挽強角牴。 劉濟為幽州節度使，見而偉之，致於親軍，從征伐。 以功遷衙前都知兵馬使，檢校光祿大夫、兼監察御史。 寶歷中，幽師殺硃克融。 其子延嗣竊襲父位，不遵朝旨，虐用其人； 載義遂殺之，數其罪以聞。 敬宗嘉之，拜檢校戶部尚書、兼御史大夫，封武威郡王，充幽州盧龍等軍節度副大使，知節度事。
In the fifth month of that year the local army mutinied and killed him; his son Yanling was also killed. The second son Yansi usurped command but was soon killed by the great general Li Zaiyi. Li Zaiyi, courtesy name Fanggu, was a descendant of Prince Min of Changshan. His family had risen through military prowess, and he in turn became a prefect in Youzhou's jurisdiction. Zaiyi was orphaned in youth and kept company with unruly men of his district. He was brave and strong and excelled at drawing the heavy bow and at wrestling. When Liu Ji became Military Governor of Youzhou, he was impressed by him, enrolled him in his personal guard, and took him on campaigns. For his merit he was promoted to commander of the vanguard troops, with the titular posts of Acting Grand Master for Splendid Happiness and Concurrent Investigating Censor. During the Baoli reign the Youzhou troops killed Zhu Kerong. His son Yansi usurped his father's position, defied court directives, and cruelly mistreated his men; Zaiyi then killed him and reported his crimes to the throne in detail. Emperor Jingzong commended him, appointing him Acting Minister of Revenue and Concurrent Censor-in-Chief, enfeoffing him as Prince of Wuwei, and making him deputy military commissioner of the Youzhou-Lulong armies with authority over frontier affairs.
4
未幾，李同捷據滄景以邀襲父爵。 載義上表，請討同捷以自效。 上嘉其誠懇，特加檢校右僕射。 累破賊軍，以功加司空，進階金紫。 ，平滄景，策勛加平章事，仍賜實封三百戶。 四年，契丹寇邊，以兵擊走之，仍虜其名王，就加太保。 五年春，為其部下楊志誠所逐，因入覲。 上以載義有平滄景之功，又能恭順朝旨，再拜太保、同平章事。 其年，改山南西道節度、觀察等使，兼興元尹。 七年，遷北都留守，兼太原尹，充河東節度觀察處置等使。 尋加開府儀同三司。 丁母憂，起復驃騎大將軍，餘如故。
Before long Li Tongjie seized Cangzhou and Jingzhou to claim his father's fief. Zaiyi submitted a memorial asking to campaign against Tongjie to prove his loyalty. The Emperor praised his sincerity and specially appointed him Acting Vice Director of the Right Department. He repeatedly defeated the rebel armies; for his merit he was made Minister of Works and advanced to the Golden Purple grade. When Cangzhou and Jingzhou were pacified, he was made Grand Counsellor for his merit and granted three hundred households of substantive fief. In the fourth year the Khitans raided the border; he drove them off and captured one of their princes, and was thereupon made Grand Mentor. In the spring of the fifth year he was driven out by his subordinate Yang Zhicheng and came to court to pay homage. Because Zaiyi had merit in pacifying Cang-Jing and could respectfully follow court directives, the Emperor again appointed him Grand Mentor and Grand Counsellor. That year he was transferred to military commissioner and grand coordinator of the Shanxi West Circuit, concurrently governor of Xingyuan. In the seventh year he was moved to Northern Capital Protector, concurrently governor of Taiyuan and commissioner for the Hedong military, observation, and administration circuits. Soon after he was given the insignia of Grand Master Opening the Office with privileges equal to the Three Excellencies. When his mother's mourning began he was recalled from mourning as Grand General of Valiant Cavalry; his other posts remained unchanged.
5
回鶻每遣使入朝，所至強暴。 邊城長吏多務茍安，不敢制之以法。 但嚴兵防守，虜益驕悍，或突入市肆，暴橫無所憚。 至是，有回鶻將軍李暢者，曉習中國事，知不能以法制馭，益驕恣。 鞭捶驛吏，貪求無已。 載義因召李暢與語曰：「可汗使將軍朝貢，以固舅甥之好，不當使將軍暴踐中華。 今朝廷饔餼至厚，所以禮蕃客也。 茍有不至，吏當坐死。 若將軍之部伍不戢，淩侮上國，剽掠廬舍，載義必殺為盜者。 將軍勿以法令可輕而不戒勵之！ 」遂罷防守之兵，而使兩卒司其門。 虜知其心為下，無敢犯令。 九年，加侍中。 卒，年五十，贈太尉。
Whenever the Uyghurs sent envoys to court, they were violent wherever they went. Senior officials of frontier cities mostly sought a quiet life and did not dare restrain them by law. They only mounted strict military guard, and the barbarians grew ever more arrogant — sometimes bursting into market streets and rampaging without restraint. On this occasion there was a Uyghur general named Li Chang who understood Chinese affairs and knew the court could not control them by law; he grew still more overbearing. He flogged relay-station clerks and demanded gifts without end. Zaiyi therefore summoned Li Chang and said to him: "The qaghan sent you, General, to pay tribute and strengthen the bond of uncle and nephew — you ought not to let the General trample over the Central Domain." The court's provisions for you are very generous — this is how foreign guests are treated. If anything falls short, the clerks would face execution. If the men under the General's command are undisciplined, insult the empire, and plunder dwellings, Zaiyi will surely kill anyone who acts as a bandit. General, do not think the laws light and fail to warn and restrain your men! He then dismissed the guard troops and had two soldiers watch his gate. The barbarians knew his resolve was firm and did not dare violate his orders. In the ninth year he was made Palace Attendant. He died at age fifty and was posthumously honoured as Grand Preceptor.
6
載義晚年驕恣，慘暴一方。 以楊志誠復為部下所逐，過太原，載義躬身毆擊，遂欲殺之，賴從事救解以免。 然而擅殺志誠之妻孥及將卒。 朝廷錄其功，屈法不問。 楊志誠楊志誠，為幽州後院副兵馬使，事李載義。 時朝廷賜載義德政碑文。 載義延中使擊鞠，志誠亦與焉，遂於鞠場叫呼謀亂。 載義奔於易州，志誠乃為本道馬步都知兵馬使。
In his later years Zaiyi grew arrogant and cruelly terrorized the region. When Yang Zhicheng was in turn driven out by his subordinates and passed through Taiyuan, Zaiyi personally beat him and nearly killed him — only a staff member's intervention saved him. Yet he killed Zhicheng's wife, children, and officers on his own authority. The court credited his earlier merit and bent the law without prosecuting him. Yang Zhicheng was deputy commander of the rear-guard troops in Youzhou and served Li Zaiyi. At that time the court had granted Zaiyi a stele commemorating his virtuous governance. Zaiyi entertained the imperial envoy at cuju; Zhicheng was also present and suddenly shouted at the playing field, plotting mutiny. Zaiyi fled to Yizhou; Zhicheng then became commander of infantry and cavalry for the circuit.
7
文宗聞之驚，急召宰臣。 時牛僧孺先至，上謂曰：「幽州今日之事可奈何？ 」僧孺曰：「此不足煩聖慮，臣被召疾趨氣促，容臣稍緩息以對。 」上良久曰：「卿以為不足憂，何也？ 」僧孺對曰：「陛下以范陽得失系國家休戚耶？ 且自安、史之後，范陽非國家所有。 前時劉總向化，以土地歸闕，朝廷約用錢八十萬貫，而未嘗得范陽尺布鬥粟上供天府； 則今日志誠之得，猶前日載義之得也。 陛下但因而撫之，亦事之宜也。 且范陽，國家所賴者，以其北捍突厥，不令南寇。 今若假志誠節鉞，惜其土地，必自為力。 則爪牙之用，固不計於逆順。 臣固曰不足煩聖慮。 」上大喜曰：「如卿之言，吾洗然矣。 」尋以嘉王運遙領節度，以志誠為節度觀察留後，檢校左散騎常侍，兼幽州左司馬。 尋改檢校工部尚書、節度副大使，知節度事。
When Emperor Wenzong heard of this he was alarmed and urgently summoned the chief ministers. Niu Sengru arrived first; the Emperor said to him: "What is to be done about today's events in Youzhou?" Sengru said: "This is not worth troubling Your Sacred Majesty. I was summoned in haste and am out of breath — allow me a moment to recover before answering." After a long pause the Emperor said: "You say it is not worth worry — why?" Sengru replied: "Does Your Majesty believe the gain or loss of Fanyang affects the nation's weal and woe?" Since the An-Shi Rebellion, Fanyang has not truly belonged to the state. When Liu Zong earlier submitted to the court and returned his territory, the court pledged eight hundred thousand strings of cash yet never received a single foot of Fanyang cloth or a dipper of grain for the imperial treasury; then Zhicheng's gain today is the same as Zaiyi's gain was before. Your Majesty need only accept this and soothe him — that is also what the situation calls for. Moreover Fanyang is what the state relies upon because it shields the north against the Turks and keeps them from raiding south. Now if you grant Zhicheng the seals of command but withhold his territory, he will surely fight on his own behalf. Then his claws and teeth will be used regardless of loyalty or rebellion. I repeat that it is not worth troubling Your Sacred Majesty. The Emperor was greatly pleased and said: "As you say, my mind is cleared." Soon the Prince of Jia, Li Yun, was made titular military commissioner while Zhicheng was made acting military commissioner and observation commissioner, Acting Left Regular Attendant, and concurrent Left Marshal of Youzhou. Soon he was changed to Acting Minister of Works, deputy military commissioner, with authority over frontier affairs.
8
七年，轉檢校吏部尚書。 詔下，進奏官徐迪詣中書白宰相曰：「軍中不識朝廷體位，只知自尚書改僕射為遷，何知工部轉吏部為美？ 且軍士盛飾以待新恩，一旦復為尚書，軍中必慚。 今中使往彼，其勢恐不得出。 」及使至，其傔奔還，奏曰：「楊志誠怒不得僕射，三軍亦有怨言。 春衣使魏寶義、兼他使焦奉鸞，尹士恭，並為志誠縶留矣。 」志誠遣將王文穎謝恩，並讓官，復賜官告批答，文穎不受而歸。 朝廷納裴度言，務以含垢，下詔諭之，因再遣使加尚書右僕射。
In the seventh year he was transferred to Acting Minister of Personnel. When the edict was issued, the memorial envoy Xu Di went to the Secretariat and told the chief ministers: "The army does not understand the court's hierarchy of offices — they only know that a change from Vice Director to Director is a promotion; how would they know that transfer from Works to Personnel is an honour?" The troops have prepared splendid dress awaiting the new favour; if they suddenly become Vice Director again the army will be shamed. Now if an imperial envoy goes there, I fear he will not be allowed to leave. When the envoy arrived his attendants fled back and reported: "Yang Zhicheng is angry at not receiving the directorship; the three armies also have complaints." The spring-clothing envoy Wei Baoyi, together with other envoys Jiao Fengluan and Yin Shigong, have all been detained by Zhicheng. Zhicheng sent his general Wang Wenying to thank the throne and decline the appointment; the court again sent patent letters of appointment with replies, but Wenying would not accept them and returned. The court adopted Pei Du's advice to swallow the affront, issued an edict reassuring him, and sent envoys again to appoint him Acting Vice Director of the Right Department.
9
八年，為三軍所逐，則立史元忠。 元忠進志誠所造袞龍衣二副及被服鞍韉，皆繡飾鸞鳳日月之形，或為王字。 因付御史臺按問，流嶺南。 行至商州，殺之。
In the eighth year he was driven out by the three armies and Shi Yuanzhong was installed. Yuanzhong presented two sets of dragon robes and saddle trappings that Zhicheng had made, all embroidered with phoenixes, sun and moon, or the character for "king." He handed them to the Censorate for investigation and Zhicheng was banished to Lingnan. When he reached Shangzhou he was killed.
10
初，元忠既逐志誠，詔以通王淳遙領節度，授元忠左散騎常侍、幽州大都督府左司馬、知府事，充節度留後。 明年，轉檢校工部尚書、節度副大使，知節度事。 後為偏將陳行泰所殺。 張仲武張仲武，范陽人也。 仲武少業《左氏春秋》，擲筆為薊北雄武軍使。 會昌初，陳行泰殺節度使史元忠，權主留後。 俄而，行泰又為次將張絳所殺，令三軍上表，請降符節。 時仲武遣軍吏吳仲舒表請以本軍伐叛。 上遣宰臣詢其事，仲舒曰：「絳與行泰，皆是遊客，主軍人心不附。 仲武是軍中舊將張光朝之子，年五十餘，兼曉儒書，老於戎事，性抱忠義，願歸心闕廷。 」李德裕因奏：「陳行泰、張絳皆令大將上奏，邀求節旄，所以必不可與。 今仲武上表布誠，先陳密款，因而拔用，即似有名。 」許之，乃授兵馬留後，詔撫王纮遙領節度。 尋改仲武節度副大使、知節度事，檢校工部尚書、幽州大都督府長史、兼御史大夫、蘭陵郡王。 俄而回鶻擾邊。
When Yuanzhong had first driven out Zhicheng, the edict made Prince of Tong Li Chun titular military commissioner and appointed Yuanzhong Left Regular Attendant, Left Marshal of the Youzhou Grand Protectorate with authority over prefectural affairs, and acting military commissioner. The next year he was transferred to Acting Minister of Works, deputy military commissioner, with authority over frontier affairs. Later he was killed by the junior general Chen Xingtai. Zhang Zhongwu was a native of Fanyang. In youth Zhongwu studied the Zuozhuan, then cast aside his brush and became commander of the Xiongwei army in northern Ji. In the early Huichang era Chen Xingtai killed Military Governor Shi Yuanzhong and held power as acting commissioner. Before long Xingtai was in turn killed by the next-ranking general Zhang Jiang, who had the three armies submit a memorial requesting the grant of credentials. At that time Zhongwu sent the army clerk Wu Zhongshu to memorialize asking that this army be used to suppress the rebels. The Emperor sent chief ministers to inquire; Zhongshu said: "Jiang and Xingtai are both outsiders — the men leading the army do not trust them." Zhongwu is an old officer of the army, son of Zhang Guangchao, over fifty years old, versed in Confucian learning, long experienced in military affairs, and by nature loyal and righteous — he wishes to submit his heart to the court." Li Deyu thereupon memorialized: "Both Chen Xingtai and Zhang Jiang have had their great generals submit memorials demanding the insignia of command — therefore they must absolutely not be granted." Zhongwu now memorialized the throne with professions of loyalty, laying out secret pledges first—and on that basis was elevated to office, as though the appointment had always been meant for him. The request was granted. Zhongwu was made acting military commissioner, while Wang Hong of Zhaofu was ordered to hold the governorship in name from a distance. Before long he was made deputy military governor with full charge of the circuit, acting Minister of Works, chief administrator of Youzhou, Censor-in-Chief, and Prince of Lanling. Soon afterward the Uyghurs began raiding the frontier.
11
時回鶻有將勒那頡啜擁赤心宰相一族七千帳，東逼漁陽。 仲武遣其弟仲至與裨將遊奉寰、王如清等，率銳兵三萬人大破之。 前後收其侯王貴族千餘人，降三萬人，獲牛馬、橐駝、旗纛、罽幕不可勝計。 遣從事李周瞳、牙門將國從，相次獻捷。 詔加檢校兵部尚書，兼東面招撫回鶻使。 先是，奚、契丹皆有回鶻監護使，督以歲貢，且為漢諜。 至是，遣裨將石公緒等諭意兩部，凡戮八百餘人。 又回鶻初遣宣門將軍等四十七人，詭詞結歡，潛伺邊隙。 仲武使密賂其下，盡得陰謀。 且欲馳入五原，驅掠雜虜。 遂逗遛其使，緩彼師期。 人馬病死，竟不遣之。 回鶻烏介可汗既敗，不敢近邊，乃依康居求活，盡徙餘種，寄托黑車子部。
A Uyghur general named Le Nazhichuo had gathered seven thousand tents of the Chixin Chancellor's clan and was driving east against Yuyang. Zhongwu dispatched his brother Zhongzhi with the subordinate generals You Fenghuan and Wang Ruqing to take thirty thousand elite troops and break them utterly. In the fighting they took more than a thousand Uyghur princes and nobles, accepted the surrender of thirty thousand men, and captured cattle, horses, camels, banners, standards, and felt tents beyond numbering. He sent his aide Li Zhoutong and gate officer Guo Cong, one after another, to the capital with news of victory. The throne then promoted him to acting Minister of War and made him commissioner for winning over the Uyghurs on the eastern frontier. Earlier, the Xi and Khitan had each been assigned Uyghur overseers to collect annual tribute and report back as Tang spies. Zhongwu now sent subordinates such as Shi Gongxu to make his will known to both peoples, and in all more than eight hundred were put to death. The Uyghurs had also first sent forty-seven envoys, among them gate guards, speaking fair words to win favor while secretly probing the frontier for weakness. Zhongwu secretly bribed men in their party and learned the whole of their hidden design. They planned besides to ride hard into Wuyuan and sweep the mixed frontier peoples before them. Zhongwu therefore kept their envoys in hand and put off the date they had set to march. Their men and horses sickened and died, and in the end the envoys were never released. When the Uyghur khan Wujie had been broken, he no longer dared come near the border. He turned toward Kangju to save himself, moved every remnant of his people, and took shelter with the Black Cart tribe.

仲武歷官至司徒、中書門下平章事。 大中年卒，謚曰莊。 子直方子直方，以幽州節度副使襲父位。 動多不法，慮為將卒所圖。 三年冬，托以遊獵，奔赴闕庭，尋授金吾將軍。 直方性率暴，行豪奪之事，以罪累貶柳州司馬。 十一年，遷右驍衛將軍，分司東都。 咸通中，位至羽林統軍。 中和歲，賊巢犯闕，公卿恃其豪，多隱藏於第。 直方納招亡命，謀欲劫巢。 或有告者，由是以兵圍而害之。 張允伸張允伸，字逢昌，范陽人也。 曾祖秀，檀州刺史。 祖巖，納降軍使。 父朝掖，贈太尉。 允申世仕幽州軍門，累職至押衙，兼馬步都知兵馬使。 大中四年，戎帥周綝寢疾，表允伸為留後，朝廷可其奏，加右散騎常侍。 其年冬，詔賜旌節，遷檢校工部尚書。 ，累加至光祿大夫、檢校司徒、兼太傅、同中書門下平章事、燕國公。
In the course of his career Zhongwu rose to Minister of Education and Grand Councellor. He died during the Dazhong reign and was posthumously titled Zhuang. His son Zhifang inherited the post, serving as deputy military governor of Youzhou in his father's stead. He behaved lawlessly in many matters and grew afraid the troops might turn on him. In the third year, during winter, he pleaded a hunting trip, fled to the capital, and was soon made a general of the imperial guard. Zhifang was rough and overbearing by nature and lived by seizure and force; repeated offenses eventually got him demoted to military advisor at Liuzhou. In the eleventh year he was moved to general of the Right Xiaowei Guard with duty at the Eastern Capital branch court. Under Xiantong he rose to command the Forest of Feathers guard. In the Zhonghe years, when Huang Chao's rebels stormed the capital, high officials put their faith in Zhifang's fighting name and many took refuge in his house. Zhifang gathered runaways and outlaws and plotted a strike against Huang Chao. When word of it got out, armed men surrounded him and put him to death. Zhang Yunshen, courtesy name Fengchang, came from Fanyang. His great-grandfather Xiu had been prefect of Tanzhou. His grandfather Yan had served as commissioner of the surrendered army. His father Chaoye was posthumously honored as Grand Preceptor. The Zhang family had long served in the Youzhou armies; Yunshen himself worked his way up to chief of staff and commander of all horse and foot. In Dazhong 4 the frontier commander Zhou Can fell seriously ill and recommended Yunshen as acting governor. The court assented and made Yunshen Right Regular Mounted Escort. That winter the throne bestowed the banner and commission of full authority and promoted him to acting Minister of Works. Further honors followed in turn: Grand Master of Splendid Happiness, acting Minister of Education, Grand Tutor, Grand Councillor, and Duke of Yan.
14
十年，徐人作亂，請以弟允臯領兵伐叛，懿宗不允。 進助軍米五十萬石，鹽二萬石。 詔嘉之，賜以錦彩、玉帶、金銀器等。 冬，又加特進，兼侍中。 十二年，以風恙拜章請就醫藥，詔許之。 以子簡會檢校工部尚書，充節度副大使。 十三年，允伸再上表進納所賜旌節。 朝命未至，其年正月二十五日卒，年八十八。 再贈太尉，謚曰忠烈。
In the tenth year, when Xu rose in revolt, he asked leave to send his brother Yungang against the rebels; Emperor Yizong refused. Instead he sent fifty thousand shi of grain and twenty thousand shi of salt to support the campaign. The court praised the gift and rewarded him with brocades, a jade belt, gold and silver ware, and other gifts. That winter he was given the rank of Special Advancement and made Palace Attendant as well. In the twelfth year he memorialized, pleading illness brought on by wind, to seek medical leave; the throne allowed it. His son Jianhui was made acting Minister of Works and deputy military governor in his place. In the thirteenth year Yunshen memorialized again, offering back the banner and commission the throne had given him. The court's reply had not yet come when, on the twenty-fifth day of the first month that year, he died at eighty-eight. He was again honored after death as Grand Preceptor, with the posthumous name Zhonglie.
15
允伸領鎮凡二十三年，克勤克儉，比歲豐登。 邊鄙無虞，軍民用乂。 至今談者美之。 有子十四人。
Yunshen governed the frontier twenty-three years in all, tireless and sparing; harvest after harvest ran rich. The marches knew no trouble, and soldiers and people alike lived at ease. Men still speak of him with admiration. He left fourteen sons.
16
簡真，幽府左司馬，先允伸卒。 簡壽，右領軍衛大將軍。 餘或升朝籍，或為刺史、郡佐。 張公素張公素，范陽人。 咸通中，為幽州軍校。 事張允伸，累遷至平州刺史。 允伸卒，子簡會權主留後事，公素領本郡兵赴焉。 三軍素畏公素威望，簡會知力不能制，即時出奔，遂立為帥。 朝廷尋授旌節，累加至中書門下平章事。 無幾，李茂勛奪其位，公素歸闕，貶復州司戶參軍。 李可舉李可舉，本回鶻阿布思之族也。 張仲武破回鶻，可舉父茂勛與本部侯王降焉。 茂勛善騎射，性沈毅，仲武器之。 常遣拓邊，以功封郡王，賜姓名。
Jian Zhen served as Left Assistant Administrator on the Youzhou prefectural staff and died before Zhang Yunshen. Jian Shou held the post of Grand General of the Right Victorious Army Guard. The others either rose into court ranks or served as prefects and county administrators. Zhang Gongsu was a native of Fanyang. In the Xiantong era he served as an officer in the Youzhou army. Under Zhang Yunshen he rose through successive promotions to Prefect of Pingzhou. After Yunshen's death, his son Jian Hui provisionally handled the military governor's affairs, and Gongsu marched there at the head of his home command's troops. The three armies had long stood in awe of Gongsu's authority; realizing he lacked the power to control them, Jian Hui fled on the spot, and the troops thereupon installed Gongsu as their commander. The court soon invested him with banners and credentials of command, and he was promoted in stages to Chief Minister of the Secretariat and Chancellery. Not long afterward Li Maoxun displaced him; Gongsu returned to the capital and was demoted to Recorder of Fuzhou. Li Keju was originally a member of the Uyghur Abusi clan. After Zhang Zhongwu routed the Uyghurs, Keju's father Maoxun and the tribal chiefs of his band submitted to him. Maoxun was skilled in horsemanship and archery and steady by temperament, and Zhongwu held him in high regard. Repeatedly dispatched to expand the frontier, he was enfeoffed as Prince of a commandery for his achievements and given a formal Chinese name.
17
咸通末，納降軍使陳貢言者，幽之宿將，人所信服。 茂勛密謀劫而殺之，聲雲貢言舉兵。 張公素以兵逆擊不利，公素走，茂勛入城，軍民方知其非貢言也。 既有其眾，遂推而立之，朝廷即降符節。 無幾，以疾告老，授右僕射致仕，表可舉自節度副使、幽州左司馬加右散騎常侍，為節度留後。 中和中，累官至檢校太尉。
Near the end of the Xiantong reign, Chen Gongyan, Commissioner for Surrendered Troops, was a seasoned Youzhou general in whom the men placed their trust. Maoxun secretly plotted to seize and kill him, putting about the story that Gongyan had risen in rebellion. Zhang Gongsu marched out to oppose him but was beaten; Gongsu fled, and only when Maoxun entered the city did soldiers and civilians realize the revolt had not been Gongyan's doing. Having secured the army, they thereupon raised him up as leader, and the court promptly granted him the seals of command. Not long after, pleading illness he asked to retire; he was granted the title of Right Vice Director and allowed to leave office, and he memorialized that Keju be promoted from Deputy Military Governor and Left Assistant Administrator of Youzhou to Right Regular Attendant and appointed Acting Military Governor. In the Zhonghe period he advanced through successive appointments to Acting Grand General.
18
中和末，以太原李克用兵勢方盛，與定州王處存密相締結。 可舉慮其窺伺山東，終為己患，遂遣使構雲中赫連鐸乘其背，則與鎮州合謀舉兵，兼言易、定是燕、趙之餘，雲得其地則正其疆理而分之。 時可舉遣將李全忠攻易州。 有次將劉仁恭者，多權數； 攻之彌月不下，乃穴地道以入。 其城既下，易州士卒稍驕。 王處存引輕軍三千，以羊皮蒙之，夜伏於城外，仍別於間道以騎士伺之。 燕軍望見，謂之群羊，爭趨焉。 處存乘其無部伍，一擊大敗之，尋復其城。 全忠遁歸，懼可舉罪之，收其餘眾，反攻幽州。 可舉危急，收集其族，登樓自燔而死。 李全忠李全忠，范陽人。 廣明中，為棣州司馬。 有蘆生於室，一尺三節，心惡之。 謂別駕張建曰：「吾室生蘆，無乃怪歟？ 」建曰：「蘆，茅類，得澤而滋，公家有茅土之慶，殆天意乎！ 其生三節，必傳節鉞者三人。 公勉樹功名，無忘斯言。 」全忠秩滿還鄉里，事節度使李可舉為牙將。 時可舉兵鋒方盛，欲與鎮人分易、定，遣全忠將兵攻之，為定州軍大敗於易水。 全忠懼，率其餘眾掩攻幽州。 可舉死。 三軍推全忠為留後，朝廷因以節鉞授之，春也。
Near the end of the Zhonghe reign, with Li Keyong of Taiyuan at the height of his military power, Keju entered into a secret alliance with Wang Chucun of Dingzhou. Keju feared that Keyong would eye Shandong and eventually become a menace to him, so he sent envoys to incite Helian Duo of Yunzhong to attack from the rear; he then conspired with Zhenzhou to take up arms, arguing that Yizhou and Dingzhou were the last holdings of Yan and Zhao, and that once Yunzhong seized the territory they would set the boundaries aright and partition the land. At that time Keju sent General Li Quanzhong to attack Yizhou. Among them was a deputy commander named Liu Rengong, a man rich in wiles; when a month of assault failed to bring the city down, they tunneled beneath the walls to break in. After the city fell, the soldiers occupying Yizhou grew somewhat overconfident. Wang Chucun led three thousand light infantrymen, disguised them with sheepskins, and hid outside the walls by night, while also placing horsemen along a hidden route to keep watch. The Yan troops spotted them, mistook them for a herd of sheep, and charged eagerly toward them. Wang Chucun struck while they were in disarray, crushing them in a single blow, and soon retook the city. Quanzhong fled homeward, afraid Keju would hold him accountable; he rallied his surviving forces and turned back to assault Youzhou. Keju, brought to the brink of ruin, gathered his kinsmen, climbed a tower, and immolated himself. Li Quanzhong was a native of Fanyang. In the Guangming period he served as Assistant Administrator of Dizhou. Reeds sprouted in his bedchamber, standing a foot tall in three sections, and the sight filled him with unease. He said to Vice-Prefect Zhang Jian, "Reeds have sprung up in my room — could this be an ill omen?" Zhang Jian replied, "Reed belongs to the rush family; where there is moisture it flourishes. In your house this is the sign of a fief sealed with thatch — surely it is Heaven's will!" That it bore three joints foretells that three generations will pass the seal and axe of command." My lord, strive to win glory and achievement, and do not forget what I have said." When his term of office ended, Quanzhong returned to his home district and entered the service of Military Governor Li Keju as a guard officer. Keju's armies were then at their peak; intending to carve up Yizhou and Dingzhou with the forces of Zhenzhou, he dispatched Quanzhong to attack them, but the Dingzhou army crushed him on the banks of the Yishui. Terrified, Quanzhong gathered his surviving troops and launched a surprise assault on Youzhou. Keju perished. The three armies proclaimed Quanzhong acting military governor, and the court thereupon invested him with the seal and axe of command; it was spring.
19
全忠卒，子匡威自襲父位，稱留後。 匡威素稱豪爽，屬遇亂離，繕甲燕薊，有吞四海之志。 赫連鐸據雲中，屢引匡威與河東爭雲、代，並兵積年。 景福初，鎮州王鎔誘河東將李存孝。 克用怒，加兵討之。 時鎔童幼，求援於燕； 匡威親率軍應之。 二年春，河東復出師井陘，再乞師，匡威來援。
After Quanzhong's death, his son Kuangwei took his father's post by force and declared himself acting military governor. Kuangwei had long been famed for open-handed boldness; caught in an age of upheaval, he drilled troops throughout Yan and Ji and nursed the ambition to rule all beneath Heaven. Helian Duo held Yunzhong and repeatedly pulled Kuangwei into wars with Hedong over Yunzhou and Daizhou, and their armies fought in alliance for years on end. Early in the Jingfu reign, Wang Rong of Zhenzhou lured away Li Cunxiao, a general of Hedong. Enraged, Keyong mobilized additional forces to punish him. Wang Rong was still a boy at the time, and he appealed to Yan for help; Kuangwei marched out in person to answer the call. In the spring of the second year Hedong again marched through Jingxing Pass; Wang Rong again appealed for troops, and Kuangwei came to his aid.
20
匡威弟匡籌，妻張氏有國色。 師將發，家人會別，匡威酒酣，留張氏報之。 匡籌私懷忿怒，匡威軍至博野，匡籌乃據城自為節度。 匡威部下聞之，亡歸者半。 匡威退無歸路，將入覲京師。 時匡威留於深州，遣判官李抱貞奉章以聞。 屬京師大亂之後，聞匡威來朝，市人震恐，咸曰「金頭王來謀社稷」，士庶有亡竄山谷者。 匡威其實不行，欲圖鎮州，示無留意。 鎔以匡威再來援己，致其失師，遣使迎歸府第，父事之。 匡威為鎔城郛繕甲，指陳方略，視鎔如子。 每陰謀驟施，以悅人心。 鎮之三軍，素忠於王氏，惡其所為。 會鎔過匡威第慰忌辰，匡威縞衣裹甲，伏兵劫鎔入牙城。 鎔兵逆戰，燔東偏門，軍士呼噪登屋，矢下如雨。 鎔僕墨君和亂中扶鎔登屋免難，而斬匡威以徇。
Kuangwei's younger brother Kuangchou had a wife, Lady Zhang, whose beauty was famed throughout the realm. As the army was about to march, the family gathered to say farewell; Kuangwei, drunk, kept Lady Zhang behind to keep his brother company. Kuangchou secretly seethed with rage; when Kuangwei's army reached Boye, Kuangchou seized the city and installed himself as military commissioner. When Kuangwei's men heard the news, half of them deserted and went home. Kuangwei, retreating with nowhere to go, prepared to enter the capital and pay homage. Kuangwei was then staying at Shenzhou and sent his aide Li Baozhen to the capital with a memorial reporting his situation. This came just after the capital had been thrown into chaos; when word spread that Kuangwei was coming to court, the townspeople panicked, crying that "the Gold-Headed King is coming to seize the throne," and many officials and commoners fled into the hills. Kuangwei never actually went; he meant to take Zhenzhou and gave no sign of leaving. Because Kuangwei had marched to his aid twice and thereby lost his army, Rong sent envoys to welcome him to his mansion and treated him like a father. Kuangwei repaired Rong's walls and armor, laid out strategy for him, and looked on him as a son. He constantly hatched schemes and acted suddenly to win hearts. The three armies of Zhen had long been loyal to the Wang house and detested his conduct. When Rong called at Kuangwei's mansion to observe the anniversary of a parent's death, Kuangwei wore mourning white over his armor and set ambush troops to seize Rong and drag him into the headquarters. Rong's troops fought back; they set fire to the eastern gate, soldiers shouted and scrambled onto the roofs, and arrows fell like rain. Amid the chaos Rong's servant Mo Junhe helped him onto a roof to safety, then beheaded Kuangwei and displayed his head.
21
是歲，匡籌出師攻鎮之樂壽、武強以報恥。 匡威部曲劉仁恭歸於河東。 冬，河東聽仁恭之謀，出師進討。 二月，敗燕軍於居庸，匡籌挈其族遁去，將赴京師。 至景城，為滄州節度使盧彥威所殺，掠其輜車、妓妾。 匡籌妻張氏產於路，不能進，劉仁恭獲之，獻於李克用，後立為夫人，嬖寵專房。 李氏父子三葉，十年而亡。 【贊】史臣曰：大都偶國，亂之本也。 故古先哲王建國，公侯之封，不過千乘，所以強幹弱枝，防其悖慢。 彼幽州者，列九圍之一，地方千里而遙，其民剛強，厥田沃壤。 遠則慕田光、荊卿之義，近則染祿山、思明之風。 二百餘年，自相崇樹，雖朝廷有時命帥，而土人多務逐君。 習苦忘非，尾大不掉，非一朝一夕之故也。 若李載義、張仲武、張允伸因利乘便，獲領旌旗，以仁守之，恭順朝旨，亦足多也。 如硃克融、楊志誠、史元忠、張公素、李可舉、李全忠，以不仁得之，靡更曩志。 或尋為篡奪，或僅傳子孫，咸非令終，蓋其宜也。
That year Kuangchou marched out to attack Lesou and Wuqiang in Zhen to avenge the insult. Liu Rengong, once of Kuangwei's command, went over to Hedong. That winter Hedong took Rengong's advice and marched out to chastise Yan. In the second month they routed the Yan army at Juyong; Kuangchou fled with his clan, intending to reach the capital. At Jingcheng he was killed by Lu Yanwei, military governor of Cangzhou, who plundered his baggage train and concubines. Lady Zhang, Kuangchou's wife, gave birth on the road and could go no farther; Liu Rengong seized her and presented her to Li Keyong, who later made her his principal consort and favored her above all others. The Li father and son held power for three generations and were gone within ten years. [Commentary] The historiographer writes: When great cities rival the throne, disorder takes root. That is why the sage-kings of old, in founding states, capped the fiefs of lords and marquises at a thousand chariots—strengthening the trunk and weakening the branches to forestall rebellion and insolence. Youzhou is one of the nine frontier circuits, a thousand li across and far from the capital; its people are hard and its soil rich. In distant times they looked up to the loyalty of Tian Guang and Jing Ke; in recent memory they were stained by the example of An Lushan and Shi Siming. For more than two hundred years they raised up their own leaders in turn; though the court sometimes named a commander, the locals mostly worked to drive him out. Hardened by hardship, heedless of right and wrong, their tail grown too heavy to command—this did not happen overnight. Men such as Li Zaiyi, Zhang Zhongwu, and Zhang Yunshen seized favorable moments to win the seals of command, then ruled with restraint and bowed to the court's will—that too deserves praise. But Zhu Kerong, Yang Zhicheng, Shi Yuanzhong, Zhang Gongsu, Li Keju, and Li Quanzhong won power without benevolence and never abandoned their old ambitions. Some were soon overthrown, some held on only long enough to pass power to a son—none died well, as was only fitting.
22
贊曰：蠍石之野，氣勁人豪。 二百餘載，自相尊高。 載義、仲武，亦多忠勞。 餘因篡得，不仁何逃？
The encomium runs: On the scorpion-stone frontier the air is harsh and the men are fierce. For more than two hundred years they made themselves masters. Zaiyi and Zhongwu, too, gave much loyal service. The rest won power by usurpation—how could cruelty escape its reckoning?
