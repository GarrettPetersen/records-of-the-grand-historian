1
徐浩徐浩，字季海，越州人。 父嶠，官至洛州刺史。 浩少舉明經，工草隸，以文學為張說所器重，調授魯山主簿。 說薦為麗正殿校理，三遷右拾遺，仍為校理。 幽州節度使張守珪奏在幕府，改監察御史。 丁父憂，服除，授京兆司錄，以母憂去職。 數年，調授河南司錄，歷河陽令，以善政稱。 拜太子司議郎，遷金部員外郎，歷憲部郎中。 安祿山反，出為襄陽太守、本郡防禦使，賜以金紫之服。 肅宗即位，召拜中書舍人，時天下事殷，詔令多出于浩。 浩屬詞贍給，又工楷隸，肅宗悅其能，加兼尚書左丞。 玄宗傳位誥冊，皆浩為之，參兩宮文翰，寵遇罕與為比。 除國子祭酒，坐事貶廬州長史。 代宗征拜中書舍人、集賢殿學士，尋遷工部侍郎、嶺南節度觀察使、兼御史大夫，又為吏部侍郎、集賢殿學士。 坐以妾弟冒選，托侍郎薛邕註授京尉，為御史大夫李棲筠所彈，坐貶明州別駕。
Xu Hao, whose courtesy name was Jihai, came from Yuezhou. His father Xu Jiao had served as prefect of Luozhou. As a young man Xu Hao passed the Classics examination and was skilled in cursive and clerical calligraphy. Zhang Yue valued his literary gifts and had him appointed registrar of Lushan. Zhang Yue recommended him as a collator in the Hall for the Rectification of Literature. After three promotions he became Right Reminder, yet continued to serve as collator. Zhang Shougui, military commissioner of Youzhou, had him serve on his staff, after which he was appointed investigating censor. After his father's death he observed mourning, and when it ended he was made recorder of the capital district. He later resigned to mourn his mother. Some years later he was transferred to recorder of Henan and then served as magistrate of Heyang, where his good administration won praise. He was appointed adviser to the crown prince, then promoted to vice director in the Department of Revenue and later served as a director in the Department of Justice. When An Lushan rose in rebellion, he was sent out as prefect of Xiangyang and defense commissioner of the commandery, and was granted the gold-and-purple insignia of high rank. When Emperor Suzong came to the throne, Xu Hao was summoned and made drafting secretary. With the empire in crisis, many of the court's edicts came from his hand. Xu Hao wrote with abundant fluency and was also accomplished in regular and clerical calligraphy. Emperor Suzong admired his talents and additionally appointed him Left Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs. He drafted all the abdication documents when Emperor Xuanzong transferred the throne, handled literary affairs for both the retired and reigning sovereigns, and enjoyed favor seldom equaled at court. He was appointed director of the Directorate of Education, but after an offense was demoted to chief administrator of Luzhou. Emperor Daizong summoned him back as drafting secretary and academician of the Hall of Assembled Worthies. He was soon promoted to vice minister of works, then made military commissioner and observation commissioner of Lingnan with the concurrent title of censor-in-chief, and later served again as vice minister of personnel and academician of the Hall of Assembled Worthies. He was impeached by Censor-in-Chief Li Qiyun after arranging, through Vice Minister Xue Yong, a fraudulent appointment for his younger half-brother as a capital district officer, and was demoted to assistant prefect of Mingzhou.
2
德宗即位，征拜彭王傅。 建中三年，以疾卒，年八十，贈太子少師。 初，浩以文雅稱； 及授廣州，典選部，多積貨財，又嬖其妾侯莫陳氏，頗幹政事，為時論所貶。 趙涓趙涓，冀州人也。 幼有文學。 天寶初，舉進士，補郾城尉，累授監察御史、右司員外郎。 河南副元帥王縉奏充判官，授檢校兵部郎中、兼侍御史，遷給事中、太常少卿，出為衢州刺史。
When Emperor Dezong came to the throne, Xu Hao was summoned and appointed tutor to the Prince of Peng. In the third year of the Jianzhong era he died of illness at the age of eighty and was posthumously honored as Junior Tutor to the Heir Apparent. At first Xu Hao was known for his refined literary gifts; but after he was sent to Guangzhou and placed in charge of the selection bureau, he amassed great wealth, favored his concubine of the Houmo Chen clan, and meddled heavily in affairs of state, drawing widespread criticism. Zhao Juan came from Jizhou. He showed literary talent from an early age. Early in the Tianbao era he passed the jinshi examination and was appointed assistant magistrate of Yancheng, then rose to investigating censor and vice director of the Right Secretariat. Wang Jin, deputy commander-in-chief of Henan, had him serve as staff judge. He was appointed acting director in the Ministry of War with the concurrent post of attendant censor, then promoted to supervising secretary and vice director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices before being sent out as prefect of Quzhou.
3
永泰初，涓為監察御史。 時禁中失火，燒屋室數十間，火發處與東宮稍近，代宗深疑之，涓為巡使，俾令即訊。 涓周歷需囿，按據跡狀，乃上直中官遺火所致也，推鞫明審，頗盡事情。 既奏，代宗稱賞焉。 德宗時在東宮，常感涓之究理詳細，及刺衢州，年考既深，又與觀察使韓滉不相得，滉奏免涓官，德宗見其名，謂宰臣曰：「豈非永泰初御史趙涓乎？ 」對曰：「然。 」即拜尚書左丞。 無何，知吏部選，扈從梁州。 興元元年卒，贈戶部尚書。 子博宣子博宣，登進士第，文章俊拔，性率多酒。 陳許節度使曲環辟為從事，賓筵之間多所忽略，環不能容。 朝廷方討淮、蔡，環誣奏博宣受吳少誠賂為反間，又妄說國家休咎，扇惑軍情。 時博宣權知舞陽縣事，詔令環決杖四十，流於康州，人皆以為枉。
Early in the Yongtai era Zhao Juan served as an investigating censor. When a fire broke out in the inner palace and burned several dozen rooms, the blaze began uncomfortably close to the Eastern Palace. Emperor Daizong was deeply suspicious and appointed Zhao Juan touring commissioner with orders to investigate immediately. Zhao Juan searched the palace grounds, followed the evidence, and determined that a eunuch on palace duty had left a fire unattended. His inquiry was thorough and clear, and largely brought the matter to light. When he reported his findings, Emperor Daizong praised him warmly. Dezong, then crown prince, had long admired Zhao Juan's meticulous investigation. Later, as prefect of Quzhou, Juan had deep seniority but clashed with observation commissioner Han Huang, who memorialized for his removal. When Dezong saw the name on the memorial, he asked his chief ministers, "Is this not Zhao Juan, the censor from early Yongtai?" They answered, "It is." Zhao Juan was immediately appointed Left Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs. Before long he was placed in charge of personnel selection and accompanied the court to Liangzhou. He died in the first year of the Xingyuan era and was posthumously honored as Minister of Revenue. His son Zhao Bozhuan passed the jinshi examination. His writing was outstanding, and by nature he was forthright and fond of drink. Qu Huan, military commissioner of Chenxu, recruited him as a staff officer, but Bozhuan often showed disrespect at banquets, which Qu Huan could not abide. While the court was campaigning against Huai and Cai, Qu Huan falsely accused Bozhuan of taking bribes from Wu Shaocheng to serve as a spy, and of recklessly prophesying the state's fortunes and stirring up the troops. Bozhuan was then acting magistrate of Wuyang. An edict authorized Qu Huan to have him beaten forty strokes and exiled to Kangzhou, and most considered the punishment unjust.
4
先是，侍御史盧南史坐事貶信州員外司馬，至郡，準例得廳吏一人，每月請紙筆錢，前後五年，計錢一千貫。 南史以官閑冗，放吏歸，納其紙筆錢六十余千。 刺史姚驥劾奏南史，以為贓，又劾南史買鉛燒黃丹。 德宗遣監察御史鄭楚相、刑部員外郎裴澥、大理評事陳正儀充三司使，同往按鞫。 將行，並召於延英，謂之曰：「卿等必須詳審，無令漏罪銜冤。 」三人將退，裴澥獨留，奏曰：「臣按姚驥奏狀，稱南史取廳吏紙筆錢計贓六十余貫，雖於公法有違，量事且非巨蠹。 」上曰：「此事亦未為甚，未知燒鉛何如？ 」澥曰：「燒鉛為丹，格令不禁。 準天寶十三載敕，鉛、銅、錫不許私家買賣貨易，蓋防私鑄錢，本亦不言燒鉛為丹。 南史違敕買鉛，不得無罪。 伏以陛下自登寶位，及天寶、大歷以來，未曾降三司使至江南； 今忽錄此小事，令三司使往，非唯損耗州縣，亦恐遠處聞之，各懷憂懼。 臣聞開元中張九齡為五嶺按察使，有錄事參軍告齡非法，朝廷止令大理評事往按。 大歷中，鄂嶽觀察使吳仲孺與轉運使判官劉長卿紛競，仲孺奏長卿犯贓二十萬貫，時止差監察御史苗伾就推。 今姚驥所奏事狀無多，臣堪任此行，即請獨往，恐不須三司並行為使。 」德宗忻然曰：「卿言是矣。 」乃復召楚相、正儀與澥俱坐，謂之曰：「朕懵於理道，處事未精，適見裴澥所奏，深協事宜，亦不用三人總去，但行首一人行可也，卿等使宣付宰臣改敕。 」德宗不務大體，以察為明，皆此類也。 而博宣、南史坐誣枉擯逐，賴裴澥悟主，南史不至深罪，後得召還。 劉太真劉太真，宣州人。 涉學，善屬文，少師事詞人蕭穎士。 天寶末，舉進士。 大歷中，為淮南節度使陳少遊掌書記，征拜起居郎。 累歷臺閣，自中書舍人轉工部、刑部二侍郎。 性怯懦詭隨。 及轉禮部侍郎，掌貢舉，宰執姻族，方鎮子弟，先收擢之。 又常敘少遊勛績，擬之桓、文，大招物論。 貞元五年，貶信州刺史，到州尋卒。
Earlier, attendant censor Lu Nanshi had been demoted for an offense to supernumerary militia commander of Xinzhou. On reaching his post he was entitled by regulation to one office clerk, who for five years had collected paper-and-brush allowances totaling one thousand strings of cash. Finding his post largely idle, Lu Nanshi released the clerk and turned in more than sixty thousand cash of the collected allowances. Prefect Yao Ji impeached Lu Nanshi for corruption and also accused him of buying lead to smelt yellow cinnabar. Emperor Dezong dispatched investigating censor Zheng Chuxiang, vice director of the Ministry of Justice Pei Xie, and Court of Judicial Review reviewer Chen Zhengyi as a three-commissioner team to investigate the case. Before they departed, all three were summoned to the Yanying Hall and told, "You must investigate carefully and see that no crime goes unpunished and no innocent man bears blame." As the three were about to withdraw, Pei Xie alone remained and said, "According to Prefect Yao Ji's report, Lu Nanshi is accused of taking the clerk's paper-and-brush money—more than sixty strings—as graft. Though this violates the law, the offense is hardly a major corruption case." The emperor said, "That matter is not very serious either. What about the charge of smelting lead?" Pei Xie said, "Smelting lead into cinnabar is not forbidden by statute." The edict of the thirteenth year of Tianbao forbids private trade in lead, copper, and tin to prevent illicit coining; it says nothing about smelting lead into cinnabar. Lu Nanshi did violate the edict by buying lead and cannot be held entirely blameless. I submit that since Your Majesty took the throne—and indeed since the Tianbao and Dali eras—a three-commissioner envoy has never been sent to the Jiangnan region; to dispatch a three-commissioner team for so minor a matter will not only burden the local administrations but may alarm distant regions when they hear of it. In the Kaiyuan era, when a registrar accused Zhang Jiuling, then inspection commissioner of the Five Ridges, of illegal conduct, the court sent only a Court of Judicial Review reviewer to investigate. In the Dali era, when observation commissioner Wu Zhongru of E-Yue quarreled with transport commissioner staff judge Liu Changqing and accused him of two hundred thousand strings in graft, the court sent only investigating censor Miao Pi to examine the case. Prefect Yao Ji's charges are slight, and I am fit for this mission. I ask to go alone; there is no need for all three commissioners to travel together. Emperor Dezong said with pleasure, "You are quite right." He then summoned Zheng Chuxiang, Chen Zhengyi, and Pei Xie and said, "I am still unskilled in governance. Pei Xie's memorial fits the situation well. All three of you need not go—only the lead commissioner will suffice. Announce this and have the chief ministers revise the edict." Emperor Dezong neglected the larger pattern of governance and mistook minute scrutiny for wisdom; this was typical of his rule. Bozhuan and Nanshi were wrongly driven out on false charges. Thanks to Pei Xie's persuasion of the emperor, Nanshi escaped severe punishment and was later recalled. Liu Taizhen came from Xuanzhou. He was broadly learned and skilled at composition; in his youth he studied under the poet Xiao Yingshi. At the end of the Tianbao era he passed the jinshi examination. In the Dali era he served as secretary to Chen Shaoyou, military commissioner of Huainan, and was summoned to the post of attendant of the heir apparent. He rose through the central offices, moving from drafting secretary to vice minister of works and then vice minister of justice. By nature he was timid, evasive, and given to shifting with circumstances. When he became vice minister of rites and took charge of the civil service examinations, he gave priority to the kin of chief ministers and the sons of regional commanders. He also often praised Chen Shaoyou's achievements and compared him to Duke Huan and Duke Wen of Qi, drawing widespread criticism. In the fifth year of Zhenyuan he was demoted to prefect of Xinzhou and died soon after arriving.
5
太真尤長於詩句，每出一篇，人皆諷誦。 德宗文思俊拔，每有禦制，即命朝臣畢和。 貞元四年九月，賜宴曲江亭，帝為詩，序曰：
Liu Taizhen was especially gifted in verse; whenever he published a new poem, people recited it throughout the capital. Emperor Dezong wrote with outstanding literary talent; whenever he composed an imperial poem, he ordered the entire court to write matching verses. In the ninth month of the fourth year of Zhenyuan the emperor granted a banquet at Qujiang Pavilion and composed a poem whose preface read:
6
朕在位僅將十載，實賴忠賢左右，克致小康。 是以擇三令節，錫茲宴賞，俾大夫、卿士得同歡洽也。 夫共其戚者同其休，有其初者貴其終，咨爾群僚，頒朕不暇，樂而能節，職思其憂，鹹若時則，庶乎理矣。 因重陽之會，聊示所懷。 早衣對庭燎，躬化勤意誠。 時此萬樞暇，適與佳節並。 曲池絜寒流，芳菊舒金英。 乾坤爽氣澄，臺殿秋光清。 朝野慶年豐，高會多歡聲。 永懷無荒誡，良士同斯情。
I have been on the throne for nearly ten years, and it is through the loyal worthies at my side that modest prosperity has been achieved. Therefore I have chosen one of the three great festivals and granted this banquet, so that ministers and officials may share in the same joy. Those who share sorrow should share joy; those who begin a task should value its completion. I address you, my officials: though I grant rewards without pause, rejoice yet show restraint, and in office keep your duties in mind. If all observe the season's proprieties, good order may yet be attained. On the occasion of the Double Ninth Festival, I briefly express what is in my heart. Rising early to robe myself before the court torches, I labor in person to transform the realm with sincere diligence. Now, amid the myriad affairs of state, a moment of leisure coincides with this fine festival. The winding pool holds clear cold currents; fragrant chrysanthemums unfold their golden blooms. Heaven and earth are clear with crisp air; terraces and halls shine with autumn light. Court and countryside rejoice in a bountiful year; at this lofty gathering many sounds of joy arise. Forever keeping in mind the warning against excess, good men share this feeling.
7
因詔曰：「卿等重陽會宴，朕想歡洽，欣慰良多，情發於中，因制詩序。 今賜卿等一本，可中書門下簡定文詞士三五十人應制，同用『清』字，明日內於延英門進來。 」宰臣李泌等雖奉詔簡擇，難於取舍，由是百僚皆和。 上自考其詩，以太真及李紓等四人為上等，鮑防、于邵等四人為次等，張濛、殷亮等二十三人為下等； 而李晟、馬燧、李泌三宰相之詩，不加考第。
An edict followed: "At your Double Ninth gathering I think of the joy we shared and am greatly comforted. Feeling arose within me, and therefore I composed this poem and preface." I now grant each of you a copy. The Secretariat may select thirty or fifty literary men to compose matching pieces, all using the rhyme word 'clear,' and submit them at the Yanying Gate by tomorrow. Chief ministers such as Li Bi, though ordered to make a selection, found it hard to choose among the candidates, and so all the officials composed matching pieces. The emperor himself graded the poems, placing Liu Taizhen, Li Shu, and two others in the top grade; Bao Fang, Yu Shao, and two others in the second; and Zhang Meng, Yin Liang, and twenty-one others in the lowest. The poems of the three chief ministers Li Sheng, Ma Sui, and Li Bi were not graded.
8
初，硃泚、懷光之亂，關輔薦饑，貞元三年以後，仍歲豐稔，人始復生人之樂。 德宗詔曰：「比者卿士內外，朝夕公務，今方隅無事，蒸民小康，其正月晦日、三月三日、九月九日三節日，宜任文武百僚擇勝地追賞。 每節宰相、常參官共賜錢五百貫文、翰林學士一百貫文，左右神威、神策等十軍各賜五百貫。 金吾英武、威遠及諸衛將軍共賜二百貫，客省奏事共賜一百貫，委度支每節前五日支付，永為常制。 」李紓李紓，字仲舒，禮部侍郎希言之子。 少有文學。 天寶末，拜秘書省校書郎。 大歷初，吏部侍郎李季卿薦為左補闕，累遷司封員外郎、知制誥，改中書舍人。 尋自虢州刺史征拜禮部侍郎。 德宗居奉天，擇為同州刺史，尋棄州詣梁州行在，拜兵部侍郎。 反正，兼知選事。 李懷光誅，河東節度及諸軍會河中，詔往宣勞節度，使還，敷奏合旨，拜禮部侍郎。
After the disorders of Zhu Ci and Li Huaiguang, the Guanzhong region had suffered repeated famine. From the third year of Zhenyuan onward, harvests were abundant year after year, and people began again to know the ordinary joys of life. Emperor Dezong issued an edict: "Lately officials within and without the capital have been occupied day and night with public business. Now the realm is at peace and the people enjoy modest prosperity. On the last day of the first month, the third day of the third month, and the ninth day of the ninth month—these three festivals—civil and military officials may choose fine places for outings and rewards." Each festival the chief ministers and regular attendees at court were granted five hundred strings of cash together, Hanlin academicians one hundred strings, and each of the ten armies—including the Left and Right Divine Valor and Divine Strategy—five hundred strings. The generals of the Gold Crow, Valiant Martial, Far-reaching Prestige, and the various guard units were granted two hundred strings together; those who presented memorials at the Guest Office one hundred strings. The Department of Public Works was to pay these sums five days before each festival, as a permanent regulation. Li Shu, whose courtesy name was Zhongshu, was the son of Vice Minister of Rites Li Xiyan. He showed literary talent from an early age. At the end of the Tianbao era he was appointed collator of the Secretariat. Early in the Dali era, Vice Minister of Personnel Li Jiqing recommended him as Left Remonstrator. He rose to vice director of the Department of Honors, took charge of drafting edicts, and was then appointed drafting secretary. He was soon summoned from his post as prefect of Guo Prefecture and appointed vice minister of rites. When Emperor Dezong was at Fengtian, Li Shu was appointed prefect of Tong Prefecture. He soon abandoned his post and went to the mobile court at Liangzhou, where he was appointed vice minister of war. After the restoration he was additionally placed in charge of personnel selection. After Li Huaiguang was executed, the military commissioner of Hedong and the various armies assembled at Hezhong. Li Shu was sent to announce imperial favor to the troops. On his return his memorials pleased the emperor, and he was appointed vice minister of rites.

Li Shu was open-minded and skilled in wit, fond of mentoring younger talents, lavish in his personal style, with splendid carriages and horses, and was known for free-spirited refinement. Though a high official, he still wandered freely and joined banquets as before, never putting on airs. He once argued that sacrifices to the Martial Completion King should not be treated like those at the Temple of Literary Propagation, memorializing, "According to the edict of the nineteenth year of Kaiyuan, a temple to Duke Tai of Qi was established with Zhang Liang as associate; the director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and his deputies served as the three offering officers." According to the Kaiyuan Rites, the prayer text reads, 'The emperor sends a certain official to announce to Duke Tai of Qi and the Marquis of Liu of Han.'" In the first year of Shangyuan an edict posthumously ennobled Duke Tai as Martial Completion King, and the sacrificial rites were made identical to those of the Literary Propagation King. The authorities therefore assigned the Grand Marshal as offering officer and had him sign the prayer board. I submit that Duke Tai was Grand Tutor of Zhou and Zhang Liang was Junior Tutor of Han. Our dynasty has placed them in the sacrificial canon and already accorded them the highest honor; To bend ritual before the Son of Heaven and pay the same reverence to mere ministers is, in principle, excessive; the spirits would scarcely dare receive such offerings. I submit that Confucius, styled Wencheng, handed down teachings and stands as the master of a hundred generations. The Five Constants and the Three Bonds would lack clarity without his instruction; no state or household could stand without his institutions. That is why Mencius said that since mankind began, there has been only one such man. For this reason he was accorded the status of the uncrowned king, honored as the First Sage, granted the full palace orchestra, and assigned the Grand Commandant as offering officer — a fitting exaltation of the teacher and the Way, in keeping with the norms of governance. The Grand Duke's literary legacy is confined to the Six Strategies, and his achievements belong to a single age. How can he be ranked with such supreme virtue or granted the same extraordinary rites? The prayer text should not be submitted for the emperor's signature. 'I dare announce' should read 'I respectfully offer sacrifice to,' and 'announce to' should read 'perform sacrifice to Marquis Liu.' Offering officers should follow the former practice, with the Grand Master of Ceremonies and his subordinates serving in that capacity. The emperor ordered all officials to submit their opinions. Culture and military achievement are the twin pillars of kingship; sacrifices, rites, and the teaching of reverence are the clear norms of state governance. He passed the jinshi examination and served as aide to Shi Siming, remaining under Siming and his son Chaoyi and regularly handling military affairs. When Chaoyi was defeated, Yue surrendered to the imperial forces; Guo Ziyi admired his ability and retained him on his staff. He rose through appointments as magistrate of Chang'an, junior director of the Secretariat, vice minister of Personnel, and crown prince tutor, and was recognized for his administrative ability. In the rebel camp he seized dozens of sons and daughters of eminent families as servants and plundered treasures beyond count. He surrendered only when utterly defeated, and the court spared his life. He was allowed back into court rank without shame, yet he aggressively sought wealth, lavishly furnished his residence, cultivated connections with powerful favorites to advance himself, showed no remorse or fear, and even looked pleased with himself — how could such a man endure! Yu Shao, styled Xiangmen, was descended from a family of Dai but was now registered in Wannian, Jingzhao. His great-grandfather Yun served as Minister of Revenue. Yu Shao passed the jinshi examination late in the Tianbao era with outstanding examination essays and was appointed a collation officer at the Chongwen Institute. He served in several provincial staffs before entering the capital as court diarist and rising twice to director in the Ministry of Revenue. Though he had not yet completed twenty years of personnel evaluations, he was judged fully qualified. Shortly thereafter he was appointed prefect of Daozhou but was reassigned to Bazhou before departing. Famine gripped the region, and several thousand tribal forces gathered in the hills and marshes, besieging the prefecture and raiding the populace. Shao rallied the prefectural troops to resist them. After twelve days he sent envoys to negotiate. The insurgents demanded that Shao appear in person to accept their surrender. Dressed in scholarly robes, he went outside the city walls, where the rebels lined up and prostrated themselves. The siege was lifted. Military commissioner Li Baoyu reported the achievement, and Shao was exceptionally promoted to Zizhou, though illness prevented him from taking up the post; he was then appointed director in the Ministry of War. Cui Ning, military commissioner of Xichuan, asked that he remain as deputy commissioner for logistics. Shortly thereafter he joined Censor-in-chief Yuan Gao and Secretariat Recipient Jiang Zhen in investigating the edict-case against Left Vice Director Xue Yong. Shao argued that Yong's offense predated the amnesty and memorialized for his release. This displeased the emperor, and Shao was demoted to chief administrator of Guizhou. Early in the Zhenyuan era he was appointed tutor to the Prince of Yuan and later crown prince attendant. He was on poor terms with Chancellor Lu Zhi. When he requested leave on grounds of illness, he was demoted to vice prefect of Quzhou, then transferred to vice prefect of Jiangzhou, and died at eighty-one.

At that time Liu Zongyuan and eight others were banished for their association with Shuwen. Only Wen was exempted because of his diplomatic mission.

溫天才俊拔，文彩贍逸，為時流柳宗元、劉禹錫所稱。 然性多險詐，好奇近利，與竇群、羊士諤趣尚相狎。 群為韋夏卿所薦，自處士不數年至御史中丞，李吉甫尤奇待之。 三年，吉甫為中官所惡，將出鎮揚州，溫欲乘其有間傾之。 溫自司封員外郎轉刑部郎中，竇群請為知雜。 吉甫以疾在第，召醫人陳登診視，夜宿於安邑裏第。 溫伺知之，詰旦，令吏捕登鞫問之，又奏劾吉甫交通術士。 憲宗異之，召登面訊，其事皆虛，乃貶群為湖南觀察使，羊士諤資州刺史，溫均州刺史。 朝議以所責太輕，群再貶黔南，溫貶道州刺史。 五年，轉衡州，秩滿歸京，不得意，發疾卒。 溫文體富艷，有丘明、班固之風，所著《淩煙閣功臣銘》、《張始興畫贊》、《移博士書》，頗為文士所賞，有文集十卷。 子恭、儉恭、儉皆至侍御史，讓至太子右庶子，皆有美才。 自後吉甫再入中書，長慶以後，李德裕黨盛，呂氏諸子無至達官者。 鄭雲逵鄭雲逵，滎陽人。 大歷初，舉進士。 性果誕敢言。 客遊兩河，以畫幹於硃泚，泚悅，乃表為節度掌書記、檢校祠部員外郎，仍以弟滔女妻之。 泚將入覲，先令雲逵入奏； 及泚至京，以事怒雲逵，奏貶莫州參軍。 滔代泚後，請為判官。 滔助田悅為逆，雲逵渝之不從，遂棄妻子馳歸長安，帝嘉其來，留於客省，超拜諫議大夫。 奉天之難，雲逵奔赴行在，李晟以為行軍司馬，戎略多以咨之。 歷秘書少監、給事中，尋拜大理卿，遷刑部、兵部二侍郎、遷御史中丞，充順宗山陵橋道置頓使。
Wen possessed brilliant talent and elegant literary style, praised by contemporaries such as Liu Zongyuan and Liu Yuxi. Yet he was treacherous by nature, drawn to novelty and profit, and was on intimate terms with Dou Qun and Yang Shie. Dou Qun had been recommended by Wei Xiaqing; in only a few years he rose from unaffiliated scholar to Vice Censor-in-Chief, and Li Jiji treated him with exceptional favor. In the third year of Yuanhe, Li Jiji incurred the hatred of palace eunuchs and was about to take up command at Yangzhou; Lü Wen meant to seize the chance to bring him down. Lü Wen moved from Vice Director in the Bureau of Appointments to Director in the Ministry of Justice, and Dou Qun had him appointed to handle miscellaneous censorate affairs. Li Jiji lay ill at home and summoned the physician Chen Deng to examine him; Deng stayed the night at Jiji's mansion in Anyi Lane. Lü Wen learned of this through surveillance. At dawn he had his clerks seize Chen Deng and interrogate him under torture, and he also memorialized accusing Li Jiji of consorting with occult practitioners. Emperor Xianzong found the affair suspicious and summoned Chen Deng for a personal inquiry; everything proved false. Dou Qun was therefore demoted to Hunan observation commissioner, Yang Shi'e to prefect of Zizhou, and Lü Wen to prefect of Junzhou. Court opinion held that the punishments were too lenient; Dou Qun was demoted again to Qiannan, and Lü Wen to prefect of Daozhou. In the fifth year he was transferred to Hengzhou. When his term expired he returned to the capital, found no satisfaction there, fell ill, and died. Lü Wen's prose was sumptuous and brilliant, in the manner of Qiu Ming and Ban Gu. His "Inscription on the Meritorious Ministers of the Lingyan Pavilion," "Encomium on Zhang Shixing's Painting," and "Letter on Transferring the Erudites" won high praise among literary men, and he left collected works in ten scrolls. His sons Gong and Jian both rose to attendant censor, and Rang to Right Vice Director of the Crown Prince; all were men of notable ability. Later Li Jiji again entered the Secretariat, but after the Changqing period, when Li Deyu's faction was ascendant, none of the Lü clan's sons attained high office. Zheng Yunkui was a native of Xingyang. At the opening of the Dali era he passed the jinshi examination. By nature he was decisive, bold, and outspoken. While traveling in the two He regions he impressed Zhu Ci with his painting. Ci was delighted, memorialized him as secretary on the military commission staff and acting Vice Director in the Bureau of Sacrifices, and gave him Zhu Tao's daughter in marriage. When Zhu Ci was about to proceed to the capital for audience, he first sent Yunkui ahead to present his report. After Zhu Ci reached the capital he took offense at Yunkui over some affair and memorialized his demotion to army adjutant of Mozhou. After Zhu Tao succeeded Zhu Ci, he asked to have Yunkui as his secretarial aide. When Zhu Tao aided Tian Yue in rebellion, Yunkui urged him to desist and, when he would not, abandoned wife and children and rode posthaste to Chang'an. The emperor commended his return, lodged him in the Guest Bureau, and promoted him directly to Remonstrating Grand Master. During the crisis at Fengtian, Yunkui rushed to the emperor's mobile court. Li Sheng appointed him Marching Army Marshal and often consulted him on strategy. He served in turn as Vice Director of the Palace Library and Supervising Secretary, was soon made Chief Judge of the Court of Judicial Review, was promoted to Vice Minister of Justice and Vice Minister of War, then to Vice Censor-in-Chief, and was appointed commissioner for bridge routes and staging posts at Emperor Shunzong's mausoleum.
14
雲逵初為硃泚判官，常忤同幕蔡庭玉； 庭玉白泚，黜為莫州錄事參軍。 滔復奏為判官，因深構庭玉於滔； 滔為泚留後事，有請於泚，庭玉又輒隳之。 又有判官硃體微，亦蒙泚親信，與庭玉常從容言於泚曰：「滔非長者，不可付以兵權。 」滔竊知之。 後滔南討有功，雲逵數激怒之，滔乃抗表論庭玉等離間骨肉； 及滔叛，帝乃召泚以表示之，故歸罪於庭玉等以悅滔，滔亦終叛。 三年，雲逵奏：其弟前太仆丞方逵，「受性兇悖，不知君親，眾惡備身，訓教莫及，結聚兇黨，江中劫人。 臣亡父先臣昈杖至一百，終不能斃。 張延賞任揚州日，亦曾犯延賞法，決殺復蘇。 至於常言，皆呼臣亡父先臣名，親戚所知，無可教語。 昨聞於邠、寧、慶等州幹謁節度及州縣乞丐，今見在武功縣南，西戎俯近，恐有異謀； 若不冒死奏聞，必恐覆臣家族。 」詔令京兆府錮身遞送黔州，付李模於僻遠州驅使，勿許東西。
Yunkui had first served as Zhu Ci's secretarial aide and had often clashed with his colleague on the staff, Cai Tingyu. Cai Tingyu reported him to Zhu Ci, and Yunkui was demoted to recorder of Mozhou. Zhu Tao again memorialized him as secretarial aide, and Yunkui thereupon worked hard to turn Tao against Tingyu. While Zhu Tao acted as regent during Zhu Ci's absence, whenever he sought Ci's approval on some matter, Cai Tingyu would again and again obstruct it. Another secretarial aide, Zhu Tiyi, also enjoyed Zhu Ci's intimate confidence; he and Tingyu would speak to Ci at leisure, saying, "Zhu Tao is no man of stature and must not be entrusted with military authority." Zhu Tao learned of this in secret. Later, after Zhu Tao's successful southern campaigns, Yunkui repeatedly goaded him; Tao then submitted a defiant memorial accusing Tingyu and the others of driving a wedge between kinsmen. When Zhu Tao rebelled, the emperor summoned Zhu Ci and showed him the memorial; blame was therefore placed on Tingyu and the others to placate Tao, yet Tao rebelled in the end nonetheless. In the third year Yunkui memorialized concerning his younger brother, the former Vice Director of the Imperial Stud Fang Kui: "By nature he is savage and rebellious, heedless of sovereign and parent; universal hatred is fixed upon him, and no teaching can reform him. He gathers violent bands and robs travelers on the Yangzi." My late father, the former subject Hao, beat him with the staff as many as a hundred times, yet could not bring him to his death. When Zhang Yanzao governed Yangzhou, Fang Kui likewise once violated his law; though sentenced to execution, he revived afterward. In his daily speech he constantly calls my late father by his personal name; our kinsmen know it—there is no further correcting him. I have just learned that in Bin, Ning, Qing, and other prefectures he importunes commissioners and local officials like a mendicant; he is now south of Wugong County, where the Western Rong lie close at hand, and I fear he may be plotting some irregular design. Unless I risk my life to report this to the throne, I must surely fear the ruin of our whole clan. An edict ordered the Capital District to imprison Fang Kui and escort him under guard to Qianzhou, to hand him over to Li Mo for forced service in a remote prefecture, and not to allow him to travel east or west.
15
雲逵元和元年拜右金吾衛大將軍，歲中改京兆尹。 五年五月卒。 李益李益，肅宗朝宰相揆之族子。 登進士第，長為歌詩。 貞元末，與宗人李賀齊名。 每作一篇，為教坊樂人以賂求取。 唱為供奉歌詞。 其《征人歌》、《早行篇》，好事者畫為屏障； 「回樂峰前沙似雪，受降城外月如霜」之句，天下以為歌詞。 然少有癡病，而多猜忌，防閑妻妾，過為苛酷，而有散灰扃戶之譚聞於時，故時謂妒癡為「李益疾」； 以是久之不調，而流輩皆居顯位。 益不得意，北遊河朔，幽州劉濟辟為從事，常與濟詩而有「不上望京樓」之句。
In the first year of Yuanhe, Yunkui was appointed General of the Right Gold Crow Guard; within the year he was transferred to Intendant of the Capital District. In the fifth month of the fifth year he died. Li Yi was a kinsman of the Suzong-era chancellor Li Huaizai. He passed the jinshi examination and for years excelled in lyric poetry. At the close of the Zhenyuan era he and his kinsman Li He were equally renowned. Whenever he finished a new poem, musicians of the Music Bureau would bribe their way to obtaining it. They performed it as lyrics for the imperial entertainments. His "Song of the Campaigner" and "On Setting Out Early" were painted on screens by admirers. The lines "Before Huile Peak the sand is like snow; outside Shouxiang Fortress the moon is like frost" were taken throughout the realm as song lyrics. Yet from youth he suffered an obsessive disorder and was much given to suspicion; in guarding his wives and concubines he went beyond severity into cruelty, and the story of scattering ashes and bolting doors was widely known—so contemporaries called jealous obsession "Li Yi's disease." For this reason he long went without regular appointment, while men of his own generation all held conspicuous posts. Discontented, Li Yi traveled north to the Hebei region; Liu Ji of Youzhou engaged him on his staff, and in poems to Ji he included the line "not ascending the Wangjing Tower."
16
憲宗雅聞其名，自河北召還，用為秘書少監、集賢殿學士。 自負才地，多所淩忽，為眾不容，諫官舉其幽州詩句，降居散秩。 俄復用為秘書監，遷太子賓客、集賢學士判院事，轉右散騎常侍。 太和初，以禮部尚書致仕，卒。 李賀李賀，字長吉，宗室鄭王之後。 父名晉肅，以是不應進士，韓愈為之作《諱辨》，賀竟不就試。 手筆敏捷，尤長於歌篇。 其文思體勢，如崇巖峭壁，萬仞崛起，當時文士從而效之，無能仿佛者。 其樂府詞數十篇，至於雲韶樂工，無不諷誦。 補太常寺協律郎，卒，時年二十四。 贊史臣曰：文學之士，代不乏才。 永泰、貞元之間，如徐浩、趙涓諸公，可謂一時之秀也。 然太真以畏懦聞，邵說以僭侈失，于公異、呂渭、李益皆有微累，故知全其德者罕矣。
Emperor Xianzong had long admired his reputation and summoned him back from Hebei, appointing him Vice Director of the Palace Library and academician of the Hall of Assembled Worthies. Confident in his talent and standing, he often treated others with contempt; colleagues could not tolerate him, and remonstrating officials cited his Youzhou verses, so he was reduced to a nominal post. Before long he was again made Director of the Palace Library, then promoted to Guest of the Heir Apparent and academician of the Hall of Assembled Worthies with charge of the academy, and finally transferred to Right Regular Attendant. At the opening of the Taihe era he retired as Minister of Rites and died. Li He, whose courtesy name was Changji, was a descendant of the imperial house in the line of Prince Zheng of the Zong clan. His father's name was Jinsu, which barred him from the jinshi examination; Han Yu wrote the "Treatise on the Taboo" on his behalf, yet Li He never sat for the examination. His brush was swift, and he was especially accomplished in lyric verse. The cast and force of his literary imagination were like sheer cliffs rising ten thousand feet; contemporaries tried to imitate him, yet none could even approach him. His yuefu lyrics ran to several dozen pieces; even the musicians of the Cloud Harmony Office knew them by heart. He was appointed Harmonizing Officer of the Grand Sacrifices Office and died at the age of twenty-four. The chronicler comments: Literary men have never been wanting from age to age. Between the Yongtai and Zhenyuan eras, men such as Xu Hao and Zhao Juan may be called the outstanding talents of a single generation. Yet Liu Taizhen was known for timidity, Shao Shuo for ruin through arrogant extravagance, and Yu Gongyi, Lü Wei, and Li Yi each carried minor blemishes—so one sees how rare it is to keep one's virtue intact.
17
贊曰：名以才顯，才兼德尊。 徐、趙、劉、李，厥聲遠聞。 邵、于、呂、鄭，其名久存。 半乏全德，愧於後人。
The encomium reads: A name shines through talent, yet talent should be joined to honored virtue. Xu, Zhao, Liu, and Li—their renown carried far. Shuo, Yu, Lü, and Zheng—their names long endure. Half lacking in full virtue—they stand ashamed before posterity.
