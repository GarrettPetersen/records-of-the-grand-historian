=李揆=李揆字端卿，隴西成紀人，而家於鄭州，代為冠族。 秦府學士、給事中玄道玄孫，秘書監、贈吏部尚書成裕之子。 少聰敏好學，善屬文。 開元末，舉進士，補陳留尉，獻書闕下，詔中書試文章，擢拜右拾遺。 改右補闕、起居郎，知宗子表疏。 遷司勛員外郎、考功郎中，並知制誥。 扈從劍南，拜中書舍人。
Li Kui, whose courtesy name was Duanqing, came from Chengyi in Longxi but made his home in Zhengzhou, where his family had ranked among the foremost clans for generations. He was descended from Xuan Daoxuan, who had served as an academician in the Prince of Qin's household and as an attendant censor, and was the son of Cheng Yu, director of the secretariat and posthumously minister of personnel. As a youth he was quick-witted, devoted to learning, and accomplished at writing. Near the close of the Kaiyuan reign he took the jinshi degree, was appointed district captain of Chenliu, and submitted a work at court; the emperor ordered the secretariat to examine his writing and then promoted him to right remonstrance. He was reassigned as right supplementation censor and attendant of the heir apparent, with responsibility for memorials from members of the imperial clan. He rose to vice director in the bureau of merit and reviewing director in the bureau of evaluation, holding both posts while drafting imperial edicts. When the court withdrew to Jiannan, he accompanied the emperor and was appointed drafting secretary of the secretariat.
2
乾元初，兼禮部侍郎。 揆嘗以主司取士，多不考實，徒峻其堤防，索其書策，殊未知藝不至者，文史之囿亦不能摛詞，深昧求賢之意也。 其試進士文章，請於庭中設《五經》、諸史及《切韻》本於床，而引貢士謂之曰：「大國選士，但務得者，經籍在此，請恣尋檢。 」由是數月之間，美聲上聞，未及畢事，遷中書侍郎、平章事、集賢殿崇文館大學士、修國史。
Early in the Qianyuan era he also served as vice minister of rites. Kui once observed that as chief examiner one often failed to test real ability, contenting oneself with tightening barriers and demanding written essays, never grasping that candidates without genuine talent could not even compose within the fields of letters and history—thus betraying a profound ignorance of what it means to seek worthy men. For the jinshi literary examination he had copies of the Five Classics, the standard histories, and the Qieyun laid out on couches in the courtyard, then addressed the candidates: "When a great state chooses its scholars, it cares only that they succeed—the texts are here; search them as freely as you please." Within a few months his reform won wide praise that reached the throne, and before the examination had even concluded he was promoted to vice director of the secretariat, associate director, grand academician of the Jixian and Chongwen halls, and compiler of the national history.
3
揆美風儀，善奏對，每有敷陳，皆符獻替。 肅宗賞嘆之，嘗謂揆曰：「卿門地、人物、文章，皆當代所推。 」故進人稱為三絕。 其為舍人也，宗室請加張皇后「翊聖」之號，肅宗召揆問之，對曰； 「臣觀往古后妃，終則有謚。 生加尊號，未之前聞。 景龍失政，韋氏專恣，加號翊聖，今若加皇后之號，與韋氏同。 陛下明聖，動遵典禮，豈可蹤景龍故事哉！ 」肅宗驚曰：「凡才幾誤我家事。 」遂止。 時代宗自廣平王改封成王，張皇后有子數歲，陰有奪宗之議。 揆因對見，肅宗從容曰：「成王嫡長有功，今當命嗣，卿意何如？ 」揆拜賀曰：「陛下言及於此，社稷之福，天下幸甚，臣不勝大慶。 」肅宗喜曰：「朕計決矣。 」自此頗承恩遇，遂蒙大用。
Kui possessed an elegant presence and spoke well in audience; every proposal he offered struck to the heart of what should be removed or advanced. Emperor Suzong admired him and once told Kui, "Your lineage, your person, and your writing are all what men of this age hold in highest regard." Hence contemporaries spoke of him as possessing three supreme gifts. While serving as drafting secretary, members of the imperial clan asked that the title "Assisting Sagely" be added to Empress Zhang's name. Suzong summoned Kui for his view, and he answered: "Your servant observes that in antiquity empresses and consorts received posthumous titles only after death." Bestowing honorific titles upon a living empress had no precedent. In the Jinglong years the court lost its way and the Wei clan seized unchecked power, adding the title Assisting Sagely; if Your Majesty now adds such a title to the empress, you will be following the Wei precedent. Your Majesty is enlightened and sagely and always upholds the ritual canon—how could you tread the path of the Jinglong precedent! Suzong exclaimed in alarm, "Mediocre counsel nearly ruined my household's affairs." He dropped the proposal. At that time the future Emperor Daizong had been renamed from Prince of Guangping to Prince Cheng, while Empress Zhang had a son still in his infancy and secretly entertained designs to supplant the heir. On one occasion when Kui was received in audience, Suzong spoke calmly: "Prince Cheng is the legitimate eldest son and has rendered service; the time has come to name him heir. What is your view?" Kui bowed in congratulation and said, "When Your Majesty speaks to this point, it is the fortune of the altars of state and a blessing for all under heaven; your servant cannot contain his joy." Suzong said with satisfaction, "My mind is made up." From that time he enjoyed growing imperial favor and soon rose to great authority.
4
時京師多盜賊，有通衢殺人置溝中者，李輔國方恣橫，上請選羽林騎士五百人以備巡檢。 揆上疏曰：「昔西漢以南北軍相攝，故周勃因南軍入北軍，遂安劉氏。 皇朝置南北衙，文武區分，以相伺察。 今以羽林代金吾警夜，忽有非常之變，將何以制之？ 」遂制罷羽林之請。
Banditry was rife in the capital; some murdered passersby on the main thoroughfares and cast the bodies into ditches. Li Fuguo, then acting with unchecked arrogance, petitioned to select five hundred imperial guard cavalrymen for patrol duty. Kui submitted a memorial: "In the Western Han the southern and northern armies were made to restrain each other, which is why Zhou Bo was able to enter the northern army from the southern and thereby secure the house of Liu." Our dynasty established southern and northern barracks, separating civil and military forces so that each might keep watch upon the other. If the imperial guard is now substituted for the golden guard in night patrols, and some extraordinary crisis suddenly arises, by what means will it be controlled? By decree the request for the imperial guard was rejected.
5
揆在相位，決事獻替，雖甚博辨，性銳於名利，深為物議所非。 又其兄皆自有時名，滯於冗官，竟不引進。 同列呂諲，地望雖懸，政事在揆之右，罷相，自賓客為荊南節度，聲問甚美。 懼其重入，遂密令直省至諲管內抅求諲過失。 諲密疏自陳，乃貶揆萊州長史同正員，其制旨曰：「扇湖南之八州，沮江陵之節制。 」揆既黜官，數日，其兄皆改授為司門員外郎。 後累年，揆量移歙州刺史。 初，揆秉政，侍中苗晉卿累薦元載為重官。 揆自恃門望，以載地寒，意甚輕易，不納，而謂晉卿曰：「龍章鳳姿之士不見用，麞頭鼠目之子乃求官。 」載銜恨頗深。 及載登相位，因揆當徙職，遂奏為試秘書監，江淮養疾。 既無祿俸，家復貧乏，孀孤百口，丐食取給。 萍寄諸州，凡十五六年，其牧守稍薄，則又移居，故其遷徙者，蓋十余州焉。 元載以罪誅，除揆睦州刺史，入拜國子祭酒、禮部尚書，為盧杞所惡。 德宗在山南，令充入蕃會盟使，加左僕射。 行至鳳州，以疾卒，興元元年四月也，年七十四。 贈司空，喪事官給。
As chief minister Kui decided affairs with proposals to remove what was harmful and advance what was good; though highly eloquent in debate, he was keenly driven by profit and reputation and drew deep public censure. Moreover his elder brothers had each earned a reputation in their own day, yet languished in minor posts while he never advanced them. His colleague Lü Yin, though far beneath him in family standing, had ranked above Kui in political affairs; after leaving the chief ministership he went from ministerial guest to military commissioner of Jingnan, where his reputation flourished. Fearing Yin's return to power, he secretly ordered palace secretaries to enter Yin's jurisdiction and hunt for his faults. Yin submitted a secret memorial in his own defense, and Kui was demoted to senior recorder of Laizhou with nominal concurrent rank; the decree read, "He incited the eight prefectures south of the lake and obstructed Jiangling's military control." Within days of Kui's dismissal, all his brothers were reassigned as vice directors in the gate office. Years later Kui was transferred in stages to prefect of Shezhou. While Kui held power, palace attendant Miao Jinqing had repeatedly recommended Yuan Zai for high office. Kui, trusting in his own exalted lineage, looked down on Zai because of his humble origins and refused to accept the recommendation, telling Jinqing, "Men marked by dragon grace and phoenix bearing go unused, while deer-headed, rat-eyed fellows come seeking office." Zai nursed a deep grudge against him. When Zai rose to the chief ministership and Kui was due for reassignment, he memorialized that Kui be made provisional director of the secretariat to recuperate from illness in the Jianghuai region. With no salary and his household again impoverished, more than a hundred widows and orphans in his care survived by begging for their food. He drifted from prefecture to prefecture for some fifteen or sixteen years; whenever a local governor treated him coldly he moved on again, passing through more than ten prefectures in all. After Yuan Zai was executed for his crimes, Kui was appointed prefect of Muzhou, then entered court as grand master of the national university and minister of rites, but incurred the hatred of Lu Qi. While Dezong was in the southern mountains, he appointed Kui envoy to the alliance conference with the Tibetans and additionally made him left vice director. He reached Fengzhou and there died of illness in the fourth month of the first year of Xingyuan, at the age of seventy-four. He was posthumously enfeoffed as minister of works, and his funeral expenses were supplied by the state.

=李涵=李涵，高平王道立曾孫。 父少康，宋州刺史。 涵簡素恭慎，有名宗室，累授贊善大夫、兼侍御史。 朔方節度郭子儀奏為關內鹽池判官。 肅宗北幸平涼，未有所適。 涵與朔方留後杜鴻漸，草箋具朔方兵馬招集之勢，軍資倉儲庫物之數，鹹推涵宗枝之英，純厚忠信，乃令涵奉箋至平涼謁見。 涵敷奏明辯，動合事機，肅宗大悅，除右司員外郎，累至司封郎中、宗正少卿。
Li Han was the great-grandson of Li Daoli, Prince of Gaoping. His father Shao Kang had served as prefect of Songzhou. Han was plain and unassuming, respectful and cautious, and enjoyed a fine reputation among the imperial clan; he was repeatedly appointed good companion of the heir apparent and concurrent attending censor. Guo Ziyi, military commissioner of Shuofang, memorialized him as salt-pond controller within the passes. When Emperor Suzong traveled north to Pingliang, the court had nowhere suitable to settle. Han and Du Hongjian, acting director of Shuofang, drafted a memorial detailing the strength of Shuofang's assembled forces and horses and the quantities of military supplies in its storehouses; all praised Han as outstanding among the imperial clansmen, pure, steadfast, and loyal, and he was ordered to present the memorial at Pingliang for audience. Han presented his memorial with clarity and force, every point fitting the moment's needs; Suzong was greatly pleased and appointed him vice director in the right secretariat, and he rose in stages to reviewing director in the bureau of awards and junior director of the court for the imperial clan.
7
寶應元年，初平河朔，代宗以涵忠謹洽聞，遷左庶子、兼御史中丞、河北宣慰使。 會丁母憂，起復本官而行，每州縣郵驛，公事之外，未嘗啟口，疏飯飲水，席地而息。 使還，請罷官終喪制，代宗以其毀瘠，許之。 服闋，除給事中，遷尚書左丞。 以幽州之亂，充河朔宣慰使。 大歷六年正月，為蘇州刺史、兼御史大夫，充浙江西道都團練觀察等使。 十一年，來朝，拜御史大夫。 京畿觀察使李棲筠歿，代之。 德宗即位，以涵和易，無剸割之才，除太子少傅，充山陵副使。 涵判官殿中侍御史呂渭上言：「涵父名少康，今官名犯諱，恐乖禮典。 」宰相崔祐甫奏曰：「若朝廷事有乖舛，群臣悉能如此，實太平之道。 」除渭司門員外郎。 尋有人言：「涵昔為宗正少卿，此時無言，今為少傅，妄有奏議。 」詔曰：「呂渭僭陳章奏，為其本使薄訴官名。 朕以宋有司城之嫌，晉有詞曹之諱，嘆其忠於所事，亦謂確以上聞。 乃加殊恩，俾膺厚賞。 近聞所陳「少」字，往歲已任少卿，昔是今非，罔我何甚！ 豈得謬當朝典，更廁周行，宜佐遐籓，用誡薄俗。 可歙州司馬同正。 」由是改涵為檢校工部尚書、兼光祿卿，仍充山陵副使。 無幾，以右僕射致仕。 興元元年九月卒，追贈太子太保。
In the first year of Baoying, when Hebei was first pacified, Emperor Daizong, knowing Han's reputation for loyalty and prudence, appointed him left companion of the heir apparent, concurrent vice censor-in-chief, and Hebei comfort commissioner. When his mother died he was recalled from mourning to his former post and set out on his mission; at every courier station along the route, beyond the business of his office, he never opened his mouth, ate plain food and drank water, and slept on the bare ground. On his return he asked to resign his post and complete the mourning rites; Daizong, seeing how grief had wasted him, granted the request. When mourning ended he was appointed director of the secretariat and promoted to left vice director in the ministry of state. Because of the turmoil in Youzhou he was appointed Hebei comfort commissioner. In the first month of the sixth year of Dali he was made prefect of Suzhou and concurrent vice censor-in-chief, with commission as commander of unified training and observation for the Zhexi West circuit. In the eleventh year he came to court and was appointed vice censor-in-chief. When Li Qijun, observation commissioner of the capital region, died, Han replaced him. When Dezong took the throne, judging Han too mild and lacking the talent for stern enforcement, he appointed him junior tutor of the heir apparent and assistant commissioner for the imperial mausoleum. Han's judicial officer, palace attendant Lü Wei, memorialized: "Han's father's name is Shao Kang; the present office title violates taboo and may conflict with ritual propriety." Chief minister Cui Youfu memorialized: "If whenever court affairs go astray all ministers can act as this one has, that would indeed be the way to great peace." Wei was appointed vice director in the gate office. Soon someone observed: "When Han was junior director of the court for the imperial clan, Wei said nothing; now that Han is junior tutor, Wei has presumptuously submitted this memorial." An edict declared: "Lü Wei presumptuously submitted a memorial, making a petty complaint about his superior's office title." We recalled that in Song there was the taboo on Si Cheng and in Jin the taboo on Cishi, and sighed at his loyalty to his charge, taking his report as sincere. We therefore granted him special favor and a generous reward. We have now learned that the character shao in his complaint applied to an office Han had already held years before as junior director—what was true then is false now. How greatly he has deceived us! How could he rightly hold a court office and stand among the ranks at court? He should serve in a distant frontier post as a warning against shallow conduct. He was made senior militia officer of Shezhou with nominal concurrent rank. Han was thereupon reassigned as acting minister of works and concurrent director of the directorate of imperial clan sacrifices, while retaining his post as assistant mausoleum commissioner. Before long he retired on reaching the rank of right vice director. He died in the ninth month of the first year of Xingyuan and was posthumously enfeoffed as junior tutor of the heir apparent.

=陳少遊=陳少遊，博州人也。 祖儼，安西副都護。 父慶，右武衛兵曹參軍，以少遊累贈工部尚書。 少遊幼聰辯，初習《莊》、《列》、《老子》，為崇玄館學生，眾推引講經。 時同列有私習經義者，期升坐日相問難。 及會，少遊攝齊升坐，音韻清辯，觀者屬目。 所引文句，悉兼他義，諸生不能對，甚為大學士陳希烈所嘆賞，又以同宗，遇之甚厚。 既擢第，補渝州南平令，理甚有聲。 至德中，河東節度王思禮奏為參謀，累授大理司直、監察殿中侍御史、節度判官。 寶應元年，入為金部員外郎。 尋授侍御史、迥紇糧料使，改檢校職方員外郎。 充使檢校郎官，自少遊始也。 明年，仆固懷恩奏為河北副元帥判官、兵部郎中、兼侍御史。 遷晉州刺史，改同州刺史，未視事，又歷晉、鄭二州刺史。 少遊為理，長於權變，時推幹濟，然厚斂財貨，交結權幸，以是頻獲遷擢。 無幾，澤潞節度使李抱玉表為副使、御史中丞、陳鄭二州留後。
Chen Shaoyou was a native of Bozhou. His grandfather Chen Yan had served as vice protector-general of Anxi. His father Qing had been army captain of the right martial guard and, through Shaoyou's honors, was repeatedly ennobled posthumously as minister of works. Shaoyou was clever and quick-tongued in youth; he first studied the Zhuangzi, Liezi, and Laozi, became a student at the Chongxuan Hall, and his fellows chose him to lecture on the classics. Some of his fellow students had been studying the classics privately and set a day when they would ascend the lecture seat to question and challenge one another. When the day came, Shaoyou straightened his robes and ascended the seat; his voice was clear and his argument sharp, and all eyes in the hall fixed upon him. Every passage he cited carried additional layers of meaning; the students could not answer him, and Grand Academician Chen Xilie greatly admired his performance; being of the same clan, Xilie thereafter treated him with exceptional generosity. After passing the civil service examination he was appointed magistrate of Nanping district in Yuzhou, where his governance won wide praise. During the Zhide period Wang Silun, military commissioner of Hedong, memorialized him as staff planner; he was repeatedly appointed director in the court of grand justice, palace attending censor, and judicial officer on the commissioner's staff. In the first year of Baoying he entered the capital as vice director in the bureau of metal. Soon he was appointed attending censor and grain commissioner for the Uighurs, then reassigned as acting vice director in the bureau of offices. He was the first to serve as envoy inspecting and verifying officials—a precedent that began with Shaoyou. The following year Pugu Huaien memorialized him as judicial officer to the deputy commander-in-chief of Hebei, director in the bureau of war, and concurrent attending censor. He was promoted to prefect of Jinzhou, then reassigned to prefect of Tongzhou before he had even taken up the post, and subsequently served again as prefect of Jin and Zheng. In governance Shaoyou excelled at expedient adaptation; contemporaries praised his practical ability, yet he levied heavy taxes and cultivated connections with the powerful, and for this reason won frequent promotions. Before long Li Baoyu, military commissioner of Ze-Lu, memorialized him as deputy commissioner, vice censor-in-chief, and acting governor of Chen and Zheng.
9
永泰二年，抱玉又奏為隴右行軍司馬，拜檢校左庶子，依前兼中丞。 其年，除桂州刺史、桂管觀察使。 少遊以嶺僥遐遠，欲規求近郡。 時中官董秀掌樞密用事，少遊乃宿於其裏，候其下直，際晚謁之，從容曰：「七郎家中人數幾何？ 每月所費復幾何？ 」秀曰：「久忝近職，家累甚重，又屬時物騰貴，一月過千餘貫。 」少遊曰：「據此之費，俸錢不足支數日，其餘常須數求外人，方可取濟。 倘有輸誠供億者，但留心庇覆之，固易為力耳。 少遊雖不才，請以一身獨供七郎之費，每歲請獻錢五萬貫。 今見有大半，請即受納，餘到官續送。 免貴人勞慮，不亦可乎？ 」秀既逾於始望，欣愜頗甚，因與之厚相結。 少遊言訖，泣曰：「南方炎瘴，深愴違辭，但恐不生還再睹顏色矣。 」秀遽曰：「中丞美才，不當遠官，請從容旬日，冀竭蹇分。 」時少遊又已納賄於元載子仲武矣。 秀、載內外引薦，數日，拜宣州刺史、宣歙池都團練觀察使。
In the second year of Yongtai Baoyu again memorialized him as field-army major of Longyou; he was appointed acting left companion of the heir apparent while retaining his concurrent post as vice censor-in-chief. That same year he was appointed prefect of Guizhou and observation commissioner of Guiguan. Shaoyou, finding the Lingnan region remote and arduous, schemed to obtain a post nearer the capital. At that time the eunuch Dong Xiu controlled secret affairs and wielded great power; Shaoyou lodged in his lane, waited until he came off duty, and at dusk called on him, saying calmly, "Seventh Brother, how many people are there in your household?" And how much do you spend each month?" Xiu replied, "I have long held a post close to power, and my family burdens are very heavy; moreover prices have risen sharply—each month costs more than a thousand strings of cash." Shaoyou said, "At that rate your salary would not last even a few days; for the rest you must constantly seek help from outsiders before you can make ends meet." If someone were willing to supply you faithfully, you need only take care to protect him—it would be easy enough to arrange. Though I am without talent, I ask to bear all of Seventh Brother's expenses myself, offering fifty thousand strings of cash each year. I have more than half of it here now—please accept it at once; the rest I will send when I reach my post. Would it not spare Your Excellency worry and trouble? Xiu had received far more than he had hoped and was deeply gratified, and from that time formed a close bond with him. When he had finished, Shaoyou wept and said, "The south is feverish and malarial; I grieve deeply at this parting, and fear I shall not live to return and see you again." Xiu said hastily, "Your talent is too fine for a distant post; please wait ten days at your ease while I do what little I can." By then Shaoyou had also paid bribes to Yuan Zai's son Zhongwu. With recommendations from Xiu within and Zai without, within days he was appointed prefect of Xuanzhou and commander of unified training and observation for Xuan, She, and Chi.
10
大歷五年，改越州刺史、兼御史大夫、浙東觀察使。 八年遷揚州大都督府長史、淮南節度觀察使。 仍加銀青光祿大夫，封潁川縣開國子。 所在悉心綏輯，而多以任數為政，好行小惠，胥吏得職，人亦獲安。 及朝廷多事。 奏請本道兩稅錢千增二百。 因詔諸道悉如淮南，鹽每一斗更加一百文。 少遊十餘年間，三總大籓，皆天下殷厚處也。 以故征求貿易，且無虛日，斂積財寶，累巨億萬，多賂遺權貴，視文雅清流之士，蔑如也。 初結元載，每年饋金帛約十萬貫，又多納賂於用事中官駱奉先、劉清潭、吳承倩等，由是美聲達於中禁。 後見元載在相位年深，以過犯漸見疑忌，少遊亦稍疏之。 無何，載子伯和貶官揚州，少遊外與之交結，而陰使人伺其過失，密以上聞。 代宗以為忠，待之益厚。
In the fifth year of Dali he was reassigned as prefect of Yuezhou, concurrent vice censor-in-chief, and Zhedong observation commissioner. In the eighth year he was promoted to senior recorder of the Yangzhou metropolitan prefecture and military commissioner and observation commissioner of Huainan. He was further made silver-green glory grand master of the palace and enfeoffed as founding viscount of Yingchuan county. Wherever he served he devoted himself to soothing and ordering the people, yet often governed through expedient stratagems and small favors; clerks performed their duties and the populace found a measure of peace. When the court faced many pressing affairs, he memorialized to raise the circuit's two-tax levy by two hundred cash per thousand. By imperial decree all circuits were ordered to follow Huainan's example, and the salt tax was raised by one hundred cash per dou. Over more than ten years Shaoyou thrice held great frontier commands, each in among the wealthiest regions of the empire. He levied taxes and pursued trade without a day's rest, amassed wealth in the hundreds of millions, lavished bribes on the powerful, and held refined scholars of pure reputation in contempt. He first cultivated Yuan Zai, sending him gold and silk worth roughly one hundred thousand strings each year, and also paid heavy bribes to influential eunuchs such as Luo Fengxian, Liu Qingtan, and Wu Chengqian, so that his reputation reached the inner palace. Later, seeing Yuan Zai's long tenure as chief minister and his growing suspicion over misdeeds, Shaoyou also grew somewhat distant from him. Before long Zai's son Bohe was demoted to a post in Yangzhou; Shaoyou cultivated his friendship outwardly while secretly sending men to watch for his faults and report them to the throne. Daizong took this as loyalty and treated him all the more generously.
11
上即位，累加檢校禮部、兵部尚書。 建中三年，李納反叛，少遊以師收徐、海等州，尋棄之，退軍盱眙。 又加檢校左僕射，賜實封三百戶。 其年，就加同平章事。 關播嘗為少遊賓僚，盧杞早年與之同在仆固懷恩使府，故驟加其官秩。
When Dezong took the throne, Shaoyou was repeatedly promoted to acting minister of rites and minister of war. In the third year of Jianzhong Li Na rebelled; Shaoyou led troops to recover Xu, Hai, and other prefectures, then soon abandoned them and withdrew his army to Xuyi. He was further made acting left vice director and granted three hundred households of substantive enfeoffment. That same year he was immediately made associate director of the secretariat. Guan Bo had once served on Shaoyou's staff, and Lu Qi had early been with him in Pugu Huaien's mission office—hence his ranks were suddenly raised.
12
四年十月，駕幸奉天，度支汴東兩稅使包佶在揚州，尚未知也。 佶判官崔沅遽報少遊，佶時所總賦稅錢帛約八百萬貫在焉，少遊意以為賊據京師，未即收復，遂脅取其財物。 先使判官崔就佶強索其納給文歷，並請供二百萬貫錢物以助軍費，佶答曰：「所用財帛，須承敕命。 」未與之。 勃然曰：「中丞若得，為劉長卿； 不爾，為崔眾矣。 」長卿嘗任租庸使，為吳仲孺所困，崔眾供軍吝財，為光弼所殺，故言及之，佶大懼，不敢固護，財帛將轉輸入京師者，悉為少遊奪之。 佶自謁，少遊止焉，長揖而遣，既懼禍，奔往白沙。 少遊又遣判官房孺復召之，佶愈懼，托以巡檢，因急棹過江，妻子伏案牘中。 至上元，復為韓滉所拘留。 佶先有兵三千，守禦財貨，令高越、元甫將焉，少遊盡奪之。 隨佶渡江者，又為韓滉所留，佶但領胥吏往江、鄂等州。 佶於彈丸中置表，以少遊脅取財帛事。 會少遊使繼至，上問曰：「少遊取包佶財帛，有之乎？ 」對曰：「臣發揚州後，非所知也。 」上曰：「少遊國之守臣，或防他盜，供費軍旅，收亦何傷。 」時方隅阻絕，國命未振，遠近聞之大驚，鹹以聖情達於變通，明見萬里。 少遊後聞之，乃安。
In the tenth month of the fourth year the emperor traveled to Fengtian; Bao Ji, commissioner for the Bian and Dong two-tax levies, was in Yangzhou and had not yet learned of it. Ji's judicial officer Cui Yuan hastily reported to Shaoyou; the tax revenues and goods under Ji's control amounted to roughly eight million strings; Shaoyou, believing rebels held the capital and recovery was not imminent, coerced him and seized his wealth. He first sent his judicial officer Cui to forcefully demand Ji's payment documents and records, and also requested two million strings in money and goods for military expenses; Ji replied, "Funds may be used only by imperial order." Ji refused. Cui said angrily, "If you obtain them, Commissioner, you will fare like Liu Changqing; if not, you will fare like Cui Zhong." Changqing had once served as rent and corvée commissioner and was ruined by Wu Zhongru; Cui Zhong had been stingy in supplying the army and was killed by Li Guangbi—hence the reference. Ji was greatly afraid and dared not resist; all wealth and goods destined for the capital were seized by Shaoyou. Ji came to call on him in person; Shaoyou detained him, bowed formally, and sent him away; Ji, fearing disaster, fled to Baisha. Shaoyou again sent his judicial officer Fang Rufu to summon him; Ji grew still more afraid, pleaded an inspection tour as pretext, and urgently rowed across the river, his wife and children concealed among the document cases. On reaching Shangyuan he was again detained by Han Huang. Ji had earlier had three thousand troops guarding the wealth, commanded by Gao Yue and Yuan Fu; Shaoyou seized them all. Those who crossed the river with Ji were again detained by Han Huang; Ji led only his clerks on to Jiang, E, and other prefectures. Ji concealed a memorial in a pellet bow, reporting Shaoyou's coercion for wealth. When Shaoyou's envoy arrived in succession, the emperor asked, "Did Shaoyou seize Bao Ji's wealth—was there such a thing?" The envoy replied, "After your servant left Yangzhou, I had no knowledge of it." The emperor said, "Shaoyou is a guardian minister of the realm; he may have been guarding against other bandits or supplying the army—what harm in seizing it?" At that time the frontiers were cut off and the national mandate unsteady; those far and near who heard this were greatly astonished, all saying the sage heart grasped expedient change and saw clearly across ten thousand li. When Shaoyou later heard of this he was reassured.
13
及李希烈陷汴州，聲言欲襲江淮。 少遊懼，乃使參謀溫述由壽州送款於希烈曰：「濠、壽、舒、廬，尋令罷壘，韜戈卷甲，佇候指揮。 」少遊又遣巡官趙詵於鄆州結李納。 其年，希烈僭號，遣其將楊豐賫偽赦書赴揚州，至壽州，為刺史張建封候騎所得，建封對中使二人及少遊判官許子瑞廷責豐而斬之。 希烈聞之大怒，即署其大將杜少誠為偽僕射、淮南節度，令先平壽州，後取廣陵。 建封於霍丘堅柵，嚴加守禁，少誠竟不能進。 後包佶入朝，具奏少遊奪財賦事狀，少遊大懼，乃上表，以所取包佶財貨，皆是供軍急用，今請據數卻納。 既而州府殘破，無以上填，乃與腹心孔目官等設法重稅管內百姓以供之。 無何，劉洽收汴州，得希烈偽起居註「某月日陳少遊上表歸順。 」少遊聞之，慚惶發疾，數日而卒，年六十一，贈太尉，賻布帛，葬祭如常儀。
When Li Xilie captured Bianzhou he declared his intention to strike the Jianghuai region. Fearing attack, Shaoyou sent his staff officer Wen Shu from Shouzhou to offer submission to Xilie, saying, "Hao, Shou, Shu, and Lu—I shall soon dismantle fortifications, sheath our weapons, and roll up our armor, awaiting your command." Shaoyou also sent touring officer Zhao Shen to Yanzhou to establish ties with Li Na. That year Xilie declared himself emperor and sent his general Yang Feng with a forged amnesty to Yangzhou; at Shouzhou he was seized by Prefect Zhang Jianfeng's scouting riders; Jianfeng rebuked Feng in the courtyard before the two palace envoys and Shaoyou's judicial officer Xu Zirui and beheaded him. Xilie heard this and was greatly enraged; he immediately appointed his great general Du Shaoqing as false vice director and Huainan military commissioner, ordering him first to pacify Shouzhou and then take Guangling. Jianfeng built strong palisades at Huoqiu and enforced strict guard; Shaoqing ultimately could not advance. Later when Bao Ji entered court he fully memorialized the circumstances of Shaoyou's seizure; Shaoyou was greatly afraid and submitted a memorial stating that the wealth taken from Bao Ji had all been for urgent military needs and that he now asked to return it in full. But the prefecture and circuit were soon ravaged and unable to make repayment; he therefore devised heavy taxes on the people within his jurisdiction with trusted clerks to supply the sum. Before long Liu Qia recovered Bianzhou and obtained Xilie's forged court diary entry: "On such-and-such a day Chen Shaoyou submitted a memorial of submission." When Shaoyou heard of this he was overcome with shame and alarm, fell ill, and died within days at the age of sixty-one; he was posthumously enfeoffed as grand preceptor, with funeral gifts of cloth and silk and burial rites performed according to ordinary ceremony.
14
=盧鸑=盧鸑，幽州范陽人也，貞觀中工部侍郎義恭玄孫也。 父子騫，潁王府諮議參軍，以鸑贈秘書少監。 鸑少以門廕入仕，在職以幹局稱。 累授閬州錄事參軍、監察殿中御史、侍御史、金州刺史。 宰相楊炎遇之頗厚，召入左司郎中、京兆少尹，遷大尹。 鸑無術學，善事權要，為政苛躁。 盧杞甚惡之，諷有司彈奏，坐貶撫州司馬同正，改饒州刺史，遷福州刺史、福建觀察使。 貞元二年七月，以疾終。
Lu E was a native of Fanyang in Youzhou and the great-great-grandson of Yigong, vice minister of works in the Zhenguan reign. His father Ziqian had been advisory staff officer in the Prince of Ying's household and, through E's honors, was posthumously made junior director of the secretariat. E entered office in youth through yin privilege and was known in his posts for practical competence. He was repeatedly appointed records officer of Langzhou, palace attending censor, attending censor, and prefect of Jinzhou. Chief minister Yang Yan treated him generously, summoned him as reviewing director in the left secretariat and junior vice magistrate of Jingzhao, then promoted him to senior magistrate. E had no scholarly training; he was skilled at cultivating the powerful and governed with harshness and impatience. Lu Qi greatly hated him and prompted the relevant offices to impeach him; he was demoted to senior militia officer of Fuzhou with nominal concurrent rank, reassigned as prefect of Raozhou, then transferred to prefect of Fuzhou and Fujian observation commissioner. In the seventh month of the second year of Zhenyuan he died of illness.
15
=裴谞=裴谞，字士明，河南洛陽人。 父寬，禮部尚書，有重名於開元、天寶間。 谞少舉明經，補河南府參軍，通達簡率，不好苛細。 積官至京兆倉曹，丁父喪，居東都。 是時，安祿山盜陷二京，東都收復，遷太子司議郎。 無幾，虢王巨奏署侍御史、襄鄧營田判官，丁母憂。 東都復為史思明所陷，谞藏匿山谷。 思明嘗為谞父將校，懷舊恩，又素慕谞名，欲必得之，因令捕騎數十跡逐得谞。 思明見之，甚喜，呼為郎君，不名，偽授御史中丞，主擊斷。 時思明殘殺宗室，谞陰緩之，全活者數百人。 又嘗疏賊短長以聞，事泄，思明大怒詬罵，僅而免死。 賊平，除太子中允，遷考功郎中，數召見言事。
Pei Xu, whose courtesy name was Shiming, was a native of Luoyang in Henan. His father Kuan had been minister of rites and enjoyed a weighty reputation during the Kaiyuan and Tianbao reigns. Xu in youth passed the mingjing examination, was appointed staff officer of Henan prefecture, and was open and plain in manner, disliking petty exactness. He rose through offices to granary officer of Jingzhao, then encountered mourning for his father and resided in the eastern capital. At that time An Lushan plundered and captured the two capitals; when the eastern capital was recovered he was transferred to reviewer of the heir apparent. Before long the Prince of Guo Ju memorialized him as attending censor and Xiang-Deng camp-field judicial officer; he then encountered mourning for his mother. The eastern capital was again captured by Shi Siming; Xu hid in mountain valleys. Siming had once served as a commander under Xu's father and cherished the old bond; he had also long admired Xu's reputation and was determined to have him, ordering several dozen pursuing riders to track him down and capture him. When Siming saw him he was greatly pleased, addressed him as Young Master without using his personal name, and falsely appointed him vice censor-in-chief in charge of judgments. At that time Siming was slaughtering members of the imperial clan; Xu secretly slowed the proceedings, and several hundred lives were spared. He also once memorialized the rebels' strengths and weaknesses to the court; when the matter leaked out Siming cursed him in great rage, and he barely escaped death. When the rebels were pacified he was appointed companion of the heir apparent, transferred to reviewing director in the bureau of evaluation, and repeatedly summoned to speak on state affairs.
16
代宗居陜，谞步懷考功及南曹二印赴行在，上見而謂之曰：「疾風知勁草，果信矣。 」將以為御史中丞，為無載所排，為河東道租庸鹽鐵等使。 時關輔大旱，谞入計，代宗召見便殿，問谞：「榷酤之利，一歲出入幾何？ 」谞久之不對。 上復問之，對曰：「臣有所思。 」上曰：「何思？ 」對曰：「臣自河東來，其間所歷三百里，見農人愁嘆，谷菽未種。 誠謂陛下軫念，先問人之疾苦，而乃責臣以利。 孟子曰：理國者，仁義而已，何以利為？ 由是未敢即對也。 」上前坐曰：「微公言，吾不聞此。 」拜左司郎中。 上時訪以事，執政者忌之，出為虔州刺史，歷饒、廬、亳三州刺史。 入為右金吾將軍。
When Daizong was at Shaanxi, Xu walked to the mobile court carrying the seals of the bureau of evaluation and the southern office; on seeing him the emperor said, "In a fierce wind one knows the tough grass—how truly you have proved it." He was to be made vice censor-in-chief but was blocked by Yuan Zai and appointed commissioner for rent, corvée, and salt of the Hedong circuit instead. At that time the Guanzhong region suffered severe drought; Xu entered to report accounts; Daizong summoned him to the side hall and asked, "How much do monopoly wine profits bring in and out in a year?" Xu was silent for a long time and did not answer. The emperor asked again; he replied, "Your servant has been thinking." The emperor said, "Thinking of what?" He replied, "Your servant came from Hedong; along the route for three hundred li I saw farmers grieving and sighing, with grain and beans not yet planted." I truly thought Your Majesty would first ask after the people's suffering, yet you charge me instead with profit. Mencius said that one who orders the state needs only benevolence and righteousness—why speak of profit? Therefore I did not dare answer at once." The emperor leaned forward and said, "But for your words I would never have heard this." He was appointed reviewing director in the left secretariat. The emperor frequently consulted him on affairs; those in power resented him and he was sent out as prefect of Qianzhou, then served as prefect of Rao, Lu, and Bo. He entered court as right general of the golden guards.
17
建中初，上以刑名理天下，百吏震悚。 時十月禁屠殺，以甫近山陵，禁益嚴。 尚父、汾陽王郭子儀隸人殺羊以入，門者覺之，谞列奏狀，上以為不畏強禦，累遣宣諭。 或謂谞曰：「郭公有社稷功，豈不為蓋之？ 」谞笑曰：「非爾所解。 且郭公威權太盛，上新即位，必謂黨附者眾。 今發其細過，以明不弄權耳。 吾上以盡事君之道，下以安大臣，不亦可乎？ 」時於朝堂別置三司以決庶獄，辯爭者輒擊登聞鼓，谞上疏曰：「夫諫豉謗木之設，所以達幽枉，延直言。 今輕猾之人，援桴鳴鼓，始動天聽，竟因纖微。 若然者，安用吏理乎！ 」上然之，悉歸有司。 谞以法吏舞文，多挾宿怨，因獻《獄官箴》以諷。 無何，坐所善僧抵法，貶閬州司馬。 征為右庶子，改千牛上將軍。 會吐蕃入寇，尋拜吏部侍郎、兼御史大夫，為吐蕃使，不行。 無幾，轉太子賓客、兵部侍郎、河南尹、東都副留守。
At the beginning of Jianzhong the emperor governed the realm through penal law and all officials trembled in fear. At that time slaughter was forbidden in the tenth month; because the imperial tombs were near, the prohibition was especially strict. The bondservant of the Honored Father, Prince of Fenyang Guo Ziyi, killed a sheep to bring in; the gatekeeper detected it; Xu memorialized the facts in full; the emperor took this as proof he did not fear the powerful and repeatedly sent imperial commendation. Someone said to Xu, "The Duke has rendered merit for the altars of state—should you not cover for him?" Xu smiled and said, "That is not what you understand." Moreover the Duke's authority and power are too great; the new emperor will surely think those who attach themselves to him are numerous. Now by exposing his small fault I make clear that he does not manipulate power. Above I thereby exhaust the way of serving the ruler; below I thereby secure a great minister—is that not acceptable?" At the time a separate Three Offices was set up in the court hall to decide common cases, and disputants would strike the Petition-to-Heaven drum; Xu memorialized, "The admonition drum and blame wood were established to reach hidden wrongs and extend forthright speech." Now frivolous, cunning men take up the drumstick and beat the drum, first moving the emperor's attention, yet in the end it is over trifles. If that is permitted, of what use are officials in governance!" The emperor approved the proposal and returned all such cases to the regular offices. Because legal officers twisted the law and often pursued old grudges, Xu presented his Admonition for Prison Officials as an indirect remonstrance. Before long, because a monk he favored ran afoul of the law, he was demoted to militia officer of Langzhou. He was recalled as right companion of the heir apparent, then reassigned as senior general of the thousand-oxen guard. When the Tibetans invaded, he was soon appointed vice minister of personnel and concurrent vice censor-in-chief as envoy to Tibet, but did not take up the mission. Before long he was transferred to guest of the heir apparent, vice minister of war, intendant of Henan, and deputy guardian of the eastern capital.
18
谞自河南凡五代為官，入視事，未嘗當正處，不鞫認於贓罪，以寬厚和易為理。 貞元九年十一月，以疾終，年七十五，贈禮部尚書。
For five generations Xu's family had served as officials in Henan; when he took up his duties he never sat in the central seat of judgment, did not press confessions in corruption cases, and governed with generous mildness. In the eleventh month of the ninth year of Zhenyuan he died of illness at the age of seventy-five and was posthumously enfeoffed as minister of rites.
19
=【贊】=史臣曰：李揆發言沃心，幸遇明主； 蔽賢固位，終非令人。 少遊逐勢利隨時，盧惎事權要巧宦，察言觀行，皆無可稱。 涵節行著聞，谞和易為理，庶幾近仁也。
The historiographer writes: Li Kui spoke words that enriched the sovereign's heart and was fortunate to meet an enlightened ruler; yet by blocking the worthy to hold his position he proved in the end no admirable man. Shaoyou chased profit and power as the times shifted; Lu E served the powerful as a crafty courtier—judging their words and conduct, none merits praise. Han was renowned for integrity and conduct, and Xu governed with mildness and ease—they perhaps approached true benevolence.
20
贊曰李、陳、盧鸑，言行非真。 涵、谞和易，庶乎近仁。
The encomium reads: Li, Chen, and Lu E—their words and conduct were not genuine. Han and Xu were mild and easy in manner—perhaps they approached true benevolence.
