太宗十四子：文德皇后生高宗大帝、恆山王承乾、濮王泰，楊妃生吳王恪、蜀王愔，陰妃生庶人祐，燕妃生越王貞、江王囂，韋妃生紀王慎，楊妃生趙王福，楊氏生曹王明，王氏生蔣王惲，後宮生楚王寬、代王簡。
Emperor Taizong had fourteen sons. Empress Wende bore Gaozong, Chengqian Prince of Hengshan, and Tai Prince of Pu. Consort Yang bore Ke Prince of Wu and Yin Prince of Shu. Consort Yin bore the commoner You. Consort Yan bore Zhen Prince of Yue and Xiao Prince of Jiang. Consort Wei bore Shen Prince of Ji. Another Consort Yang bore Fu Prince of Zhao. Lady Yang bore Ming Prince of Cao. Lady Wang bore Yun Prince of Jiang. The rear palace bore Kuan Prince of Chu and Jian Prince of Dai.
5
恆山王承乾，太宗長子也，生於承乾殿，因以名焉。 武德三年，封恆山王。 七年，徙封中山。 太宗即位，為皇太子。 時年八歲，性聰敏，太宗甚愛之。 太宗居諒暗，庶政皆令聽斷，頗識大體。 自此太宗每行幸，常令居守監國。 及長，好聲色，慢游無度，然懼太宗知之，不敢見其跡。 每臨朝視事，必言忠孝之道，退朝後，便與群小褻狎。 宮臣或欲進諫者，承乾必先揣其情，便危坐斂容，引咎自責。 樞機辨給，智足飾非，群臣拜答不暇，故在位者初皆以為明而莫之察也。 承乾先患足，行甚艱難，而魏王泰有當時美譽，太宗漸愛重之。 承乾恐有廢立，甚忌之。 泰亦負其材能，潛懷奪嫡之計。 於是各樹朋黨，遂成釁隙。 有太常樂人年十餘歲，美姿容，善歌舞，承乾特加寵幸，號曰稱心。 太宗知而大怒，收稱心殺之，坐稱心死者又數人。 承乾意泰告訐其事，怨心逾甚。 痛悼稱心不已，於宮中構室，立其形像，列偶人車馬於前，令宮人朝暮奠祭。 承乾數至其處，徘徊流涕。 仍於宮中起冢而葬之，並贈官樹碑，以申哀悼。 承乾自此託疾不朝參者輒逾數月。 常命戶奴數十百人專習伎樂，學胡人椎髻，翦綵為舞衣，尋橦跳劍，晝夜不絕，鼓角之聲，日聞於外。
Chengqian, Prince of Hengshan, was Taizong's eldest son. He was born in Chengqian Hall and named for it. In Wude 3 (620) he was created Prince of Hengshan. In Wude 7 (624) his fief was changed to Zhongshan. When Taizong ascended the throne, Chengqian was made crown prince. He was eight years old, quick-witted, and Taizong doted on him. During Taizong's mourning seclusion he was entrusted with routine government and showed a sound grasp of affairs. After that, whenever Taizong traveled he left Chengqian in the capital as regent. As he grew older he took to pleasure and idle wandering, but fearing his father would find out he kept it hidden. In court he preached loyalty and filial piety; once dismissed he caroused with his hangers-on. When palace tutors tried to remonstrate, he would read their mood, sit stiffly composed, and confess fault before they could speak. Sharp in debate and skilled at masking misconduct, he outtalked his ministers, who at first took him for a capable heir and saw through him only later. Chengqian had long been lame and walked with difficulty, while Prince of Wei Tai won contemporary praise; Taizong's affection gradually shifted toward Tai. Chengqian feared deposition and grew deeply jealous of Tai. Tai, confident in his gifts, secretly plotted to supplant his brother. Each gathered partisans, and a bitter rift opened between them. A court musician in his teens, handsome and gifted in song and dance, won Chengqian's special favor; Chengqian called him Chengxin. When Taizong learned of the affair he was furious, seized Chengxin and had him killed, and several others died in the scandal. Chengqian believed Tai had informed on him, and his hatred deepened. Unable to stop mourning Chengxin, he built a shrine in the palace with the youth's image, set out effigies, horses, and carriages, and had palace women sacrifice to it morning and night. Chengqian visited the shrine repeatedly, pacing and weeping. He even raised a tomb inside the palace, buried Chengxin there, granted him a posthumous office, and set up a stele in mourning. After that Chengqian often pleaded illness and skipped court for months on end. He kept dozens of household slaves drilling music and dance day and night—Hu-style topknots, silk dance robes, pole-climbing and sword dances—until drums and horns could be heard outside the palace daily.
6
時左庶子於志寧、右庶子孔穎達受詔輔導，志寧撰《諫苑》二十捲諷之，穎達又多所規奏。 太宗並嘉之，二人各賜帛百匹、黃金十斤。 以勵承乾之意，仍遷志寧為詹事。 未幾，志寧以母憂去職，承乾侈縱日甚。 太宗復起志寧為詹事，志寧與左庶子張玄素數上書切諫，承乾並不納。 又嘗召壯士左衛副率封師進及刺客張師政、紇干承基，深禮賜之，令殺魏王泰，不克而止。 尋與漢王元昌、兵部尚書侯君集、左屯衛中郎將李安儼、洋州刺史趙節、駙馬都尉杜荷等謀反，將縱兵入西宮。 貞觀十七年，齊王祐反於齊州。 承乾謂紇干承基曰：「我西畔宮牆，去大內正可二十步來耳，此間大親近，豈可並齊王乎？」 會承基亦外連齊王，繫獄當死，遂告其事。 太宗召承乾，幽之別室。 命司徒長孫無忌、司空房玄齡、特進蕭瑀、兵部尚書李勣、大理卿孫伏伽、中書侍郎岑文本、御史大夫馬周、諫議大夫褚遂良等參鞫之，事皆明驗。 廢承乾為庶人，徙黔州； 元昌賜令自盡，侯君集等咸伏誅。 其宮僚左庶子張玄素、右庶子趙弘智、令狐德棻、中書舍人蕭鈞，並以材選用，承乾既敗，太宗引大義以讓之，咸坐免。 十九年，承乾卒於徙所，太宗為之廢朝，葬以國公之禮。 二子象、厥。 象官至懷州別駕，厥至鄂州別駕。 象子適之，別有傳。
Left Mentor Yu Zhining and Right Mentor Kong Yingda were ordered to tutor him. Zhining wrote twenty juan of the Admonitory Garden; Yingda submitted many memorials of counsel. Taizong praised them both and rewarded each with a hundred bolts of silk and ten catties of gold. To encourage Chengqian, he promoted Zhining to Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent. Soon Zhining left on mourning leave for his mother, and Chengqian's excesses worsened daily. Taizong recalled Zhining as Grand Mentor. Zhining and Left Mentor Zhang Xuansu sent sharp remonstrances again and again, but Chengqian ignored them. He also summoned the strongman Feng Shijin, deputy commandant of the Left Guard, and assassins Zhang Shizheng and Qagan Chengji, honored them lavishly, and ordered them to kill Prince of Wei Tai. The attempt failed and the plot was abandoned. Soon he conspired with Prince of Han Yuanchang, Minister of War Hou Junji, Central Commandant of the Left Garrison Li Anyan, Yangzhou prefect Zhao Jie, and imperial son-in-law Du He to rebel and march troops into the Western Palace. In Zhenguan 17 (643) Prince of Qi You rebelled in Qizhou. Chengqian told Qagan Chengji, "My palace wall on the west is barely twenty paces from the Inner Palace — we are that close. How can we be lumped together with Prince of Qi?" Chengji was also secretly linked to Prince of Qi's rebellion, was jailed and condemned to death, and then revealed the plot. Taizong summoned Chengqian and held him in isolation. He ordered Zhangsun Wuji, Fang Xuanling, Xiao Yu, Li Jing, Sun Fuyan, Cen Wenben, Ma Zhou, Chu Suiliang, and others to investigate. Every charge was proved. Chengqian was deposed to commoner status and banished to Qianzhou; Yuanchang was ordered to commit suicide, and Hou Junji and the rest were executed. Palace mentors Zhang Xuansu and Zhao Hongzhi, Linghu Defen, and Secretariat drafter Xiao Jun had been chosen for talent; after Chengqian's fall Taizong rebuked them on principle and all were removed. In Zhenguan 19 (645) Chengqian died in exile. Taizong canceled court and buried him with ducal honors. He left two sons, Xiang and Jue. Xiang became vice-prefect of Huaizhou; Jue became vice-prefect of Ezhou. Xiang's son Shizhi has a separate biography.
7
楚王寬，太宗第二子也。 出繼叔父楚哀王智云。 早薨。 貞觀初追封，無後，國除。
Kuan, Prince of Chu, was Taizong's second son. He was adopted to succeed his uncle Zhiyun, Prince Ai of Chu. He died young. Early in Zhenguan he was posthumously enfeoffed, but left no heir and the fief lapsed.
8
吳王恪，太宗第三子也。 武德三年，封蜀王，授益州大都督，以年幼不之官。 十年，又徙封吳王。 十二年，累授安州都督。 及將赴職，太宗書誡之曰：「吾以君臨兆庶，表正萬邦。 汝地居茂親，寄惟籓屏，勉思橋梓之道，善侔間、平之德。 以義制事，以禮制心，三風十愆，不可不慎。 如此則克固盤石，永保維城。 外為君臣之忠，內有父子之孝，宜自勵志，以勖日新。 汝方違膝下，淒戀何已，欲遺汝珍玩，恐益驕奢。 故誡此一言，以為庭訓。」 高宗即位，拜司空、梁州都督。 恪母，隋煬帝女也。 恪又有文武才，太宗常稱其類己。 既名望素高，甚為物情所向。 長孫無忌既輔立高宗，深所忌嫉。 永徽中，會房遺愛謀反，遂因事誅恪，以絕眾望，海內冤之。 有子四人：仁、瑋、琨、璄，並流於嶺表。
Ke, Prince of Wu, was Taizong's third son. In Wude 3 (620) he was created Prince of Shu and named Grand Governor of Yizhou, but was too young to serve. In Zhenguan 10 (636) his fief was changed to Wu. In Zhenguan 12 (638) he was appointed governor of Anzhou. Before he left for his post, Taizong wrote to admonish him: "As sovereign I rule the myriad people and set the standard for all states. You stand as a honored kinsman and bulwark of the realm. Strive to honor the bond between father and sons and match the virtue of the Lords of Shen and Bi. Let righteousness govern your conduct and ritual govern your heart. Do not neglect the three winds and ten faults. Do so and you will secure the foundation stone and forever uphold the realm's wall. Outwardly show a minister's loyalty; inwardly keep a son's filial piety. Steel your will and renew yourself daily. You are leaving my side, and your grief at parting is boundless. I would have sent rare treasures, but feared they would only feed pride and extravagance. So I offer this single admonition as your instruction from the courtyard." When Gaozong ascended the throne, Ke was made Minister of Works and governor of Liangzhou. Ke's mother was a daughter of Emperor Yang of Sui. Ke had both civil and military gifts; Taizong often said he resembled himself. His reputation was already high and public sentiment favored him. Zhangsun Wuji, who had helped place Gaozong on the throne, deeply envied Ke. During Yonghui, when Fang Yi'ai plotted rebellion, Ke was executed on a pretext to extinguish popular hopes. The realm regarded it as a grave injustice. He had four sons—Ren, Wei, Kun, and Jing—all banished to the far south.
9
尋追封恪為鬱林王，並為立廟。 又封仁為鬱林縣侯。 永昌元年，授襄州刺史。 不知州事，後改名千里。 天授後，歷唐、廬、許、衛、蒲五州刺史。 時皇室諸王有德望者，必見誅戮，惟千里褊躁無才，復數進獻符瑞事，故則天朝竟免禍。 長安三年，充嶺南安撫討擊使，歷遷右金吾將軍。 中興初，進封成王，拜左金吾大將軍，兼領益州大都督，又追贈其父為司空。 三年，又領廣州大都督、五府經略安撫大使。 節愍太子誅武三思，千里與其子天水王禧率左右數十人斫右延明門，將殺三思黨與宗楚客、紀處訥等。 及太子兵敗，千里與禧等坐誅，仍籍沒其家，改姓蝮氏。 睿宗即位，詔曰：「故左金吾衛大將軍成王千里，保國安人，克成忠義，願除凶丑，翻陷誅夷。 永言淪沒，良深痛悼。 宜復舊班，用加新寵，可還舊官。」 又令復姓。
Ke was soon posthumously created Prince of Yulin and temples were established for the family. Ren was also created Marquis of Yulin county. In Yongchang 1 (689) he was appointed prefect of Xiangzhou. He did not actually govern the prefecture and later changed his name to Li Qianli. After the Tianshou era he served successively as prefect of Tang, Lu, Xu, Wei, and Pu. In those days talented imperial princes were routinely killed. Li Qianli alone escaped—narrow, impetuous, and untalented, he kept submitting reports of auspicious omens and so survived Empress Wu's reign. In Chang'an 3 (703) he served as pacification commissioner of Lingnan and was promoted to general of the Right Golden Guard. At the Restoration he was advanced to Prince of Cheng, made grand general of the Left Golden Guard and concurrent grand governor of Yizhou, and his father was posthumously made Minister of Works. In the third year he also became grand governor of Guangzhou and commissioner for pacification of the five prefectures. When Crown Prince Jingmin killed Wu Sansi, Li Qianli and his son Xi, Prince of Tianshui, led dozens of men to hack at the Right Yanming Gate, intending to kill Sansi's allies Zong Chuke and Ji Chuna. When the crown prince's forces were defeated, Li Qianli, Xi, and their followers were executed, their property confiscated, and their clan name changed to Fu. When Ruizong took the throne, an edict said, "The late Grand General of the Left Golden Guard, Prince of Cheng Li Qianli, guarded the state and its people, upheld loyalty and righteousness, sought to remove villains, yet was executed instead. His fall is beyond words; grief is profound. Let his former rank be restored and new honors granted; let him resume his former office." The clan was also ordered to resume its original surname.
10
瑋早卒。 中興初，追封朗陵王。 子𥙆，本名褕，出繼蜀王愔。 景龍四年，加銀青光祿大夫、秘書少監。 開元十三年，改封廣漢郡王、太僕卿同正員，薨。
Wei died young. At the Restoration he was posthumously created Prince of Langling. His son Yi, originally named Yu, was adopted to succeed Yin, Prince of Shu. In Jinglong 4 (710) he was made Silver-Gleam Grand Master of the Palace and vice director of the Secretariat. In Kaiyuan 13 (725) he was transferred to Prince of Guanghan commandery and regular associate grand master of the Imperial Stud, and died.
11
琨，則天朝歷淄、衛、宋、鄭、梁、幽六州刺史，有能名。 聖歷中，嶺南獠反，敕琨為招慰使，安輯荒徼，甚得其宜。 長安二年卒官，贈司衛卿。 神龍初，贈張掖郡王。 開元十七年，以子禕貴，贈工部尚書，追封吳王。
Under Empress Wu, Kun served successively as prefect of Zi, Wei, Song, Zheng, Liang, and You and won a reputation for competence. During Shengli the southern tribes rebelled. Kun was sent as pacification commissioner and handled the remote frontier admirably. In Chang'an 2 (702) he died in office and was posthumously made Minister of the Court for the Imperial Clan. At the Divine Dragon Restoration he was posthumously created Prince of Zhangye commandery. In Kaiyuan 17 (729), because his son Yi had risen in honor, he was posthumously made Minister of Works and Prince of Wu.

璄，中興初封歸政郡王，歷宗正卿，坐千里事貶南州司馬，卒。
Jing was created Prince of Guizheng commandery at the Restoration, served as director of the Imperial Clan, was demoted to vice-prefect of Nanzhou over Li Qianli's affair, and died.
13
琨子禕。 禕少有志尚，事母甚謹，撫弟祗等以友愛稱。 景龍四年，為太子僕，兼徐州別駕，加銀青光祿大夫。 少繼江王囂後，封為嗣江王。 景雲元年，復為德、蔡、衢等州刺史。 開元後，累轉蜀、濮等州刺史。 政號清嚴，人吏畏而服之。 漸見委任，入為光祿卿，遷將作大匠。 丁母憂去官，起復授瀛州刺史。 又上表固請終制，許之。 十二年，改封信安郡王。 十五年，服除，拜左金吾衛大將軍、朔方節度副大使、知節度事，兼攝御史大夫。 尋遷禮部尚書，仍充朔方軍節度使。 先是，石堡城為吐蕃所據，侵擾河右。 敕禕與河西、隴右議取之。 禕到軍，總率士伍，剋期攻之。 或曰：「此城據險，又為吐蕃所惜，今總軍深入，賊必併力拒守。 事若不捷，退則狼狽，不如按軍持重，以觀形勢。」 禕曰：「人臣之節，豈憚艱險？ 必期眾寡不敵，吾則以死繼之。 苟利國家，此身何惜？」 於是督率諸將，倍道兼進，併力攻之，遂拔石堡城，斬獲首級，並獲糧儲器械，其數甚眾。 仍分兵據守，以遏賊路。 上聞之大悅，始改石堡城為振武軍，自是河、隴諸軍游弈拓地千餘里。 十九年，契丹衙官可突干殺其王邵固，率部落降於突厥。 玄宗遣忠王為河北道行軍元帥以討奚及契丹兩蕃，以禕為副。 王既不行，禕率戶部侍郎裴耀卿等諸副將分道統兵出於范陽之北，大破兩蕃之眾，擒其酋長，餘黨竄入山谷。 軍還，禕以功加開府儀同三司，兼關內支度、營田等使，兼採訪處置使，仍與二子官。 禕既有勳績，執政頗害其功，故其賞不厚，甚為當時所嘆。 二十二年，遷兵部尚書，入為朔方節度大使。 久之，坐事出為衢州刺史。 俄歷滑、懷二州刺史。 天寶初，拜太子少師，以年老仍聽致仕。 二年，遷太子太師，製出，病薨，年八十餘。 上聞而痛惜者久之。 禕居家嚴毅，善訓諸子，皆有令命。 三子：峘、嶧、峴，皆至達官，別有傳。
Kun's son was Yi. From youth Yi was resolute, served his mother devotedly, and was known for brotherly care toward Zhi and his other younger brothers. In Jinglong 4 (710) he was master of the crown prince's household and concurrent vice-prefect of Xuzhou, with the rank of Silver-Gleam Grand Master of the Palace. In youth he succeeded Xiao, Prince of Jiang, and was created heir prince of Jiang. In Jingyun 1 (710) he again served as prefect of De, Cai, Qu, and other prefectures. After Kaiyuan he served repeatedly as prefect of Shu, Pu, and other prefectures. His rule was stern and clear; officials and commoners feared him and submitted. As trust in him grew he entered the capital as Minister of the Imperial Household and was promoted to Director of Palace Construction. He left office to mourn his mother, was recalled, and was appointed prefect of Yingzhou. He memorialized again to complete full mourning, and the request was granted. In Kaiyuan 12 (724) he was transferred to Prince of Xin'an commandery. In Kaiyuan 15 (727), when mourning ended, he was made grand general of the Left Golden Guard, deputy military governor of Shuofang with full authority, and acting censor-in-chief. Soon he was made Minister of Rites while continuing as military governor of the Shuofang army. Shibao Fortress on the frontier had been held by Tibet and was harassing the lands west of the Yellow River. Yi was ordered to consult with the Hexi and Longyou commands on capturing it. When Yi reached the army he marshaled the troops and fixed a date for the assault. Someone said, "The fortress sits on perilous ground and Tibet prizes it. If we drive the whole army deep in, the enemy will mass to hold it. If we fail, retreat will be a shambles. Better to hold the army steady and watch how things develop." Yi said, "What subject's integrity shrinks from hardship and peril? If numbers must tell against us, I will follow with my life. If it profits the state, what is one life worth?" He then drove the generals forward by forced marches, attacked with combined strength, and took Shibao Fortress, capturing large numbers of heads, grain, and weapons. He garrisoned the fortress and blocked the enemy's routes. The emperor was greatly pleased and renamed Shibao Fortress Zhenwu Army. From then on the He-Long commands patrolled and expanded territory by more than a thousand li. In Kaiyuan 19 (731) the Khitan yabgu Kedughan killed King Shaogu and led his tribes to surrender to the Turks. Emperor Xuanzong sent the Loyal Prince as commander-in-chief of the Hebei field army against the Xi and Khitan, with Yi as deputy. The prince did not take the field. Yi led Vice Minister of Revenue Pei Yaoqing and other deputies in separate columns north of Fanyang, routed both tribes, captured their chieftains, and drove the rest into the mountains. On return Yi was rewarded with the rank Opening the Government with Equipage Equal to the Three Excellencies, concurrent posts over internal support and military farms, and a commission for investigation and disposition; his two sons also received offices. Those in power resented his merit, so his reward was thin—a fact much lamented at the time. In Kaiyuan 22 (734) he was made Minister of War and grand military governor of Shuofang. After some time he was dismissed for an offense and sent out as prefect of Quzhou. He soon served successively as prefect of Hua and Huai. Prince of Shu hunts without limit, harasses the people, and punishes guiltless magistrates and army controllers. Flattery pleases him; crossing his will angers him. Looking through history's princes, those who kept ritual norms blessed their descendants; those who overstepped statutes were executed without delay. Yin has been impeached by the law officers; I am truly shamed." The emperor also summoned Yang Daozheng, comforted him, appointed him commandant of Kuangdao prefecture, and gave fifty bolts of silk. At the Divine Dragon Restoration Ke's grandson Wei, Prince of Langling, and his son Yu were made to succeed as heir prince of Shu.

From youth Zhen was skilled in mounted archery, versed in literature and history, and capable in administration. Wherever he served he sometimes heeded slander; upright officials were demoted and his pages bullied the people—men respected his talent yet despised his conduct. When Empress Wu held court she added Grand Tutor of the Crown Prince and made him prefect of Cai. After Empress Wu assumed power, Zhen joined Prince of Han Yuanjia, Prince of Lu Lingqian, Prince of Huo Yuanji, Yuanjia's son Shen Duke of Huangguo, Lingqian's son Ai Prince of Fanyang, Yuanji's son Xu Prince of Jiangdu, and Zhen's eldest son Chong, Langye prince and Bo prefect, in a secret plan to restore the Tang house. Act early and report back quickly." That year Empress Wu completed the Bright Hall and was about to perform the great suburban sacrifice, summoning the imperial clansmen. Yuanjia sent word among them: "At the great sacrifice the empress will surely accuse the princes in secret and slaughter them—no imperial descendant will be left." Shen forged an imperial letter to Chong: "I am held in secret imprisonment; you princes must rescue me." Chong in Bo also forged an imperial letter: "The empress wishes to overturn the Li altars and shift the mandate to the Wu clan." He ordered chief administrator Xiao Decong and others to recruit troops and summon the five princes of Han, Lu, Huo, Yue, and Ji to raise forces and march on the divine capital. Chong had conspired with the princes, but when he rose first none responded—only Zhen, bound by father and son, raised troops alone. He sent troops to capture Shangcai county; hearing Chong was defeated he sought chains and prepared to bind himself and rush to confess. His appointed new magistrate of Shangcai, Fu Yanqing, raised more than two thousand stalwarts, and Zhen found the will to resist. He proclaimed to his host, "Prince of Langye has already broken Wei and several prefectures, gathered two hundred thousand men, and will arrive within the day. Exert yourselves." He summoned seven thousand county troops and divided them into five camps. Zhen took the central camp and appointed his close associate Pei Shoude, vice-magistrate of Ruyang, grand general and commander of the inner camp; Zhao Chengmei as left central commandant commanding the left camp; Lu Hongdao as right central commandant commanding the right camp; An Mohe as commandant and commander of the rear army; Wang Xiaozhi as right general and commander of the vanguard. He also made Cai chief administrator Wei Qingli Silver-Gleam Grand Master of the Palace and acting mansion secretary. In all he appointed more than five hundred officials of the ninth rank and above. He ordered Daoist priests and Buddhist monks to recite scriptures for success; household slaves and warriors wore talismans to ward off weapons. His appointed officials were all compelled followers without fighting will; only Pei Shoude was truly with him. Shoude was fierce, brave, and skilled in mounted archery. When Zhen rose he gave him his daughter, Princess of Liangxiang, in marriage and trusted him as henchman.

When Zhen and the others were defeated, Gui and the princess were also executed.

沖，貞長子也。 好文學，善騎射、歷密、濟、博三州刺史，皆有能名。 初，沖自博州募得五千餘人，欲渡河攻濟州，先取武水縣。 縣令郭務悌赴魏州請援，魏州莘縣令馬玄素領兵千七百人邀之於路，恐力不敵，先入武水城，閉門拒守。 沖乃令積草車上，放火燒南門，擬乘火突入。 火之未起，南風甚急，及火已燃，遽回為北風，未至城門，燒草已甚，衝軍由是沮氣。 有堂邑丞董玄寂為沖統帥兵仗，及衝擊武水，玄寂曰：「琅邪王與國家交戰，此乃反也。」 沖聞之，斬玄寂以徇。 兵眾懼而散入草澤，不可禁止，惟有家僮左右不過數十而已。 乃卻走入博州城，為守門者所殺。 則天命左金吾將軍丘神勣為清平道行軍大總管以討沖，兵未至，沖已死，傳首東都，梟於闕下。 衝起兵凡七日而敗。 沖三弟：倩，封常山公，歷常州別駕，坐與父兄連謀伏誅。 溫，以告其朋黨得實，減死流嶺南，尋卒。 神龍初，侍中敬暉等以沖父子翼戴皇家，義存社稷，請復其官爵，武三思令昭容上官氏代中宗手詔不許。 開元四年，詔追復爵土，令備禮改葬。 太常奏謚議曰：「故越王貞，往者願匡宗社，夙懷誅呂之謀； 乃心王國，用擊非劉之議。 以茲獲戾，上悼聖心。 謹按謚法『死不忘君曰敬』，請謚曰敬。」 從之。 五年，下詔曰：「九族以親，克敦其教； 百代必祀，允竟厥德。 故蔡州刺史、越王貞，執心不回，臨事能斷。 粵自籓國，勤於王家。 弘道之後，寶圖將缺，懷劉章之輔漢，追鄭武之翊周。 遂能奮不顧身，率先唱義。 雖英謀未克，而忠節居多。 嗣絕國除，年逾二紀，奠享淪廢，甚為憫焉。 永言興繼，式備典冊。 其封貞侄孫故許王男左監門衛將軍、夔國公琳為嗣越王，以奉其祀。 仍官為立碑。」 琳尋卒，國除。
Chong was Zhen's eldest son. He loved literature, was skilled in mounted archery, and served successively as prefect of Mi, Ji, and Bo, all with reputations for ability. At first Chong recruited more than five thousand men at Bo and intended to cross the river to attack Jizhou, first taking Wushui county. County magistrate Guo Wuti went to Weizhou for aid. Shen county magistrate Ma Xuansu in Weizhou led seventeen hundred troops to intercept, fearing insufficient strength, entered Wushui first, and closed the gates. Chong piled hay on carts, set fire to the south gate, and intended to burst in amid the flames. Before the fire rose a strong south wind blew; when it burned the wind suddenly turned north. Before reaching the gate the hay was consumed, and Chong's army lost heart. Tangyi assistant magistrate Dong Xuanji commanded Chong's weapons. When Chong attacked Wushui, Xuanji said, "Prince of Langye is fighting the state—this is rebellion." Chong beheaded Xuanji and displayed the head. The soldiers feared and scattered into marshes; only several tens of household slaves and attendants remained. He turned back into Bo city and was killed by the gate guards. Empress Wu ordered Left Golden Guard general Qiu Shenji as commander-in-chief of the Qingping circuit to attack Chong. Before the troops arrived Chong was dead; his head was sent to the Eastern Capital and displayed below the gate. Chong's rising lasted only seven days before defeat. Chong had three younger brothers. Qian, Duke of Hengshan and vice-prefect of Changzhou, was executed for conspiring with his father and brothers. Wen, because he reported his faction with verified facts, had death reduced to banishment to Lingnan and soon died. At the Divine Dragon Restoration attendant-in-ordinary Jing Hui and others, because Chong father and son had supported the imperial house, requested restoration of their offices and titles; Wu San Si had Lady Shangguan of Zhaorong substitute for Zhongzong's personal edict refusing. In Kaiyuan 4 (716) an edict posthumously restored title and fief and ordered full rites for reburial. The Director of Ceremonies submitted the posthumous discussion: "The late Prince of Yue Zhen once wished to restore the imperial altars and long harbored the plan to execute the Lü clan; his heart was set on the royal house and he struck at the non-Liu. For this he incurred guilt and the sage heart mourned. According to the posthumous norms, 'dying without forgetting the sovereign is called Jing'—I request the posthumous title Jing." The request was accepted. In the fifth year an edict said, "The nine clans through kinship fulfill their teaching; a hundred generations must sacrifice—fully completing their virtue. Therefore the former Cai prefect, Prince of Yue Zhen, held his will unbending and could decide in affairs. From his territorial state he diligently served the royal house. After the broad way the precious chart was about to fail; he cherished Liu Zhang's aid to Han and pursued Zheng Wu's support of Zhou. Thereupon he roused himself without regard for body and took the lead in proclaiming righteousness. Though heroic planning did not succeed, loyal integrity predominated. The succession was cut off and the state abolished for more than twenty years; sacrifices fell into ruin—greatly pitiable. Words of raising and continuing—prepare the canonical records. Enfeoff Zhen's nephew's son, the former Prince of Xu's son, Left Gate Guard general Duke of Kui Lin, as Heir Prince of Yue to attend his sacrifices. Still order an office to erect a stele." Lin soon died and the fief lapsed.
26
紀王慎，太宗第十子也。 貞觀五年，封申王。 七年，授秦州都督。 十年，改封紀王，賜實封八百戶。 十七年，遷襄州刺史，以善政聞，璽書勞勉，百姓為之立碑。 二十三年，加實封滿千戶。 永徽元年，拜左衛大將軍。 二年，授荊州都督，累除刑州刺史。 文明元年，加授太子太師，轉貝州刺史。 慎少好學，長於文史，皇族中與越王貞齊名，時人號為紀、越。 初，貞將起事，慎不肯同謀； 及貞敗，慎亦下獄。 臨刑放免，改姓虺氏，仍載以檻車，配流嶺表，道至蒲州而卒。 慎長子和州刺史東平王續最知名，早卒。 次子沂州刺史義陽王琮、楚國公睿、遂州別駕襄郡公秀、廣化郡公獻、建平郡公欽等五人，垂拱中並遇害，家屬徙嶺南。 中興初，追復官爵，令以禮改葬。 封慎少子鐵誠為嗣紀王，後改名澄。 景雲元年，加銀青光祿大夫。 開元初，歷德、瀛、冀三州刺史、左驍衛將軍，薨。 子行同嗣，天寶中為右贊善大夫，同正員。
Shen, Prince of Ji, was Taizong's tenth son. In Zhenguan 5 (631) he was created Prince of Shen. In Zhenguan 7 (633) he was appointed governor of Qinzhou. In Zhenguan 10 (636) he was changed to Prince of Ji with a substantive fief of eight hundred households. In Zhenguan 17 (643) he was transferred to prefect of Xiangzhou, was known for good government, received imperial commendation, and the people erected a stele for him. In Zhenguan 23 (649) his substantive fief was increased to a full thousand households. In Yonghui 1 (650) he was appointed grand general of the Left Guard. In Yonghui 2 (651) he was appointed governor of Jingzhou and later served repeatedly as prefect of Xing. In Wenming 1 (684) he was additionally appointed Grand Tutor of the Crown Prince and transferred to prefect of Bei. From youth Shen loved learning and excelled in literature and history; in the imperial clan he was famed equally with Prince of Yue Zhen—men called them Ji and Yue. When Zhen was about to rise Shen would not join the plot; when Zhen was defeated Shen was also imprisoned. At execution he was released, his surname changed to Hui, loaded in a caged cart and banished to Lingnan; he died on the road at Puzhou. Shen's eldest son Xu, He prefect and Prince of Dongping, was most renowned and died young. Second son Cong, Yi prefect and Prince of Yiyang; third son Rui, Duke of Chu; fourth son Xiu, Duke of Xiang; fifth son Xian, Duke of Guanghua; sixth son Qin, Duke of Jianping—all five were killed in the Chuigong era and their families moved to Lingnan. At the Restoration offices and titles were posthumously restored and reburial with rites was ordered. Shen's youngest son Tiechen was created Heir Prince of Ji and later changed his name to Cheng. In Jingyun 1 (710) he was made Silver-Gleam Grand Master of the Palace. Early in Kaiyuan he served successively as prefect of De, Ying, and Ji and general of the Left Valiant Cavalry, and died. His son Xingtong succeeded and in the Tianbao era was right mentor, regular associate.
27
江王囂，太宗第十一子也。 貞觀五年受封，六年薨，謚曰殤。
Xiao, Prince of Jiang, was Taizong's eleventh son. In Zhenguan 5 (631) he was enfeoffed; in Zhenguan 6 (632) he died, with posthumous title Shang.
28
代王簡，太宗第十二子也。 貞觀五年受封，其年薨，無後，國除。
Jian, Prince of Dai, was Taizong's twelfth son. In Zhenguan 5 (631) he was enfeoffed; that year he died without heir and the fief lapsed.
29
趙王福，太宗第十三子也。 貞觀十三年受封，出後隱太子建成。 十八年，授秦州都督，賜實封八百戶。 二十三年，加右衛大將軍，累授梁州都督。 咸亨元年薨，贈司空、并州都督，陪葬昭陵。 中興初，封蔣王惲孫思順為嗣趙王。
Fu, Prince of Zhao, was Taizong's thirteenth son. In Zhenguan 13 (639) he was enfeoffed and adopted to succeed the Hidden Crown Prince Jiancheng. In Zhenguan 18 (644) he was appointed governor of Qinzhou with a substantive fief of eight hundred households. In Zhenguan 23 (649) he was added as grand general of the Right Guard and repeatedly appointed governor of Liangzhou. He died in Xianheng 1 (670) and was posthumously made Minister of Works and governor of Bingzhou, buried at Zhaoling. At the Restoration Yun Prince of Jiang's grandson Sishun was created Heir Prince of Zhao.
30
曹王明，太宗第十四子。 貞觀二十一年受封。 二十三年，賜實封八百戶，尋加滿千戶。 顯慶中，授梁州都督，後歷虢、蔡、蘇三州刺史。 詔令繼巢剌王元吉後。 永崇中，坐與庶人賢通謀，降封零陵王，徙於黔州。 都督謝祐希旨，逼脅令自殺，帝深悼之，黔府官僚咸坐免職。 景雲元年，明喪柩歸於京師，陪葬昭陵。 有二子，南州別駕零陵王俊、黎國公傑，垂拱中並遇害。 中興初，封傑子胤為嗣曹王。 胤叔父備自南州還，又封備為嗣曹王、衛尉少卿、同正員，胤遂停封。 後備招慰忠州叛獠，沒於賊，又封胤為王、銀青光祿大夫、右武衛將軍。 卒，子戢嗣，左衛率府中郎將。 卒，子皋嗣。 皋自有傳。
Ming, Prince of Cao, was Taizong's fourteenth son. In Zhenguan 21 (647) he was enfeoffed. In Zhenguan 23 (649) he received a substantive fief of eight hundred households, soon increased to a full thousand. In the Xianqing era he was appointed governor of Liangzhou and later served successively as prefect of Guo, Cai, and Su. An edict ordered him to succeed Yuanji, Prince Cila of Chao. In Yongchong, for plotting with the commoner Xian, he was demoted to Prince of Lingling and moved to Qianzhou. Governor Xie You, toadying to intent, compelled him to kill himself. The emperor deeply mourned him and Qian prefecture officials were all dismissed. In Jingyun 1 (710) Ming's coffin returned to the capital and he was buried at Zhaoling. He had two sons: Jun, vice-prefect of Nan and Prince of Lingling; Jie, Duke of Li—both were killed in the Chuigong era. At the Restoration Jie's son Yin was created Heir Prince of Cao. Yin's uncle Bei returned from Nan and was also created Heir Prince of Cao and vice minister of the Court of the Imperial Stud, regular associate; Yin's enfeoffment ceased. Later Bei pacified rebellious Liao in Zhongzhou and perished at bandit hands; Yin was again created prince, Silver-Gleam Grand Master of the Palace, and general of the Right Martial Guard. He died; his son Zhan succeeded as central commandant of the Left Guard. He died; his son Gao succeeded. Gao has a separate biography.
31
史臣曰：太宗諸子，吳王恪、濮王泰最賢。 皆以才高辯悟，為長孫無忌忌嫉，離間父子，遽為豺狼，而無忌破家，非陰禍之報歟？ 武後斫喪王室，潛移龜鼎，越王貞父子痛憤，義不圖全。 毀室之悲，《鴟鴞》之詩，傷矣！ 比齊祐之妄作，豈同年而語哉！
The historian says: Among Taizong's sons, Ke Prince of Wu and Tai Prince of Pu were the most worthy. For their talent and brilliance Zhangsun Wuji envied them, sowed division between father and sons, and made wolves of them—yet Wuji destroyed his own house. Is this not retribution for hidden malice? Empress Wu hacked at the royal house and secretly shifted the mandate; Zhen Prince of Yue and his son grieved and raged, choosing righteousness over self-preservation. The sorrow of a house destroyed—the poem of The Owl—how wounding! Compared with Prince of Qi You's reckless revolt, how can they be spoken of in the same breath?
32
贊曰：子弟作籓，磐石維城。 驕侈取敗，身無令名。 沖、譔憤發，視死如生。 承乾、齊祐，愚弟庸兄。
In praise: Imperial sons made territorial princes—the foundation stone, the wall of the realm. Arrogance and extravagance bring defeat; the body wins no fine name. Chong and Shen roused in anger, viewing death as life. Chengqian and Prince of Qi You—a foolish younger brother, a mediocre elder brother.
