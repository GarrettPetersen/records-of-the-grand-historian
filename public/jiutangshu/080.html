=褚遂良=
Chu Suiliang
2
褚遂良，散騎常侍亮之子也。 太業末，隨父在隴右，薛舉僭號，署為通事舍人。 舉敗歸國，授秦州都督府鎧曹參軍。 貞觀十年，自秘書郎遷起居郎。 遂良博涉文史，尤工隸書，父友歐陽詢甚重之。 太宗嘗謂侍中魏徵曰：「虞世南死後，無人可以論書。 」徵曰：「褚遂良下筆遒勁，甚得王逸少體。 」太宗即日召令侍書。 太宗嘗出御府金帛購求王羲之書跡，天下爭齎古書詣闕以獻，當時莫能辯其真偽，遂良備論所出，一無舛誤。 十五年，詔有事太山，先幸洛陽，有星孛於太微，犯郎位。 遂良言於太宗曰：「陛下撥亂反正，功超前烈，將告成東嶽，天下幸甚。 而行至洛陽，彗星輒見，此或有所未允合者也。 且漢武優柔數年，始行岱禮，臣愚伏願詳擇。 」太宗深然之，下詔罷封禪之事。 其年，遷諫議大夫，兼知起居事。 太宗嘗問：「卿知起居，記錄何事，大抵人君得觀之否？ 」遂良對曰：「今之起居，古左右史，書人君言事，且記善惡，以為鑑誡，庶幾人主不為非法。 不聞帝王躬自觀史。 」太宗曰：「朕有不善，卿必記之耶？ 」遂良曰：「守道不如守官，臣職當載筆，君舉必記。 」黃門侍郎劉洎曰：「設令遂良不記，天下亦記之矣。 」太宗以為然。 時魏王為太宗所愛，禮秩如嫡。 其年，太宗問侍臣曰：「當今國家何事最急？ 」中書侍郎岑文本曰：「《傳》稱『導之以德，齊之以禮』，由斯而言。 禮義為急。 」遂良進曰：「當今四方仰德，誰敢為非？ 但太子、諸王，須有定分，陛下宜為萬代法以遺子孫。 」太宗曰：「此言是也。 朕年將五十，已覺衰怠。 既以長子守器東宮，弟及庶子數將五十，心常憂慮，頗在此耳。 但自古嫡庶無良佐，何嘗不傾敗國家？ 公等為朕搜訪賢德，以傅儲宮，爰及諸王，咸求正士。 且事人歲久，即分義情深，非意窺窬，多由此作。 」於是限王府官僚不得過四考。 十七年，太宗問遂良曰：「舜造漆器，禹雕其俎，當時諫舜、禹者十餘人。 食器之間，苦諫何也？ 」遂良對曰：「雕琢害農事，纂組傷女工。 首創奢淫，危亡之漸。 漆器不已，必金為之； 金器不已，必玉為之。 所以諍臣必諫其漸，及其滿盈，無所復諫。 」太宗以為然，因曰：「夫為人君，不憂萬姓而事奢淫，危亡之機可反掌而待也。 」時皇子年幼者多任都督、刺史，遂良上疏曰：「昔兩漢以郡國理人，除郡以外，分立諸子。 割土分疆，雜用周制。 皇唐州縣，祖依秦法。 皇子幼年，或授刺史，陛下豈不以王之骨肉，鎮扞四方？ 此之造制，道高前烈。 如臣愚見，有小未盡。 何者？ 刺史郡帥，民仰以安。 得一善人，部內蘇息； 遇一不善，合州勞弊。 是以人君愛恤百姓，常為擇賢。 或稱河潤九里，京師蒙福； 或人興歌詠，生為立祠。 漢宣帝云：『與我共理者，惟良二千石。 』如臣愚見，陛下兒子內年齒尚幼、未堪臨人者，且留京師，教以經學。 一則畏天之威，不敢犯禁； 二則觀見朝儀，自然成立。 因此積習，自知為人。 審堪臨州，然後遣出。 臣謹按漢明、章、和三帝，能友愛於弟，自茲已降，取為準的。 封立諸王，雖各有國土，年尚幼小者，召留京師，訓以禮法，垂以恩惠。 訖三帝世，諸王數十百人，唯二王稍惡，自余餐和染教，皆為善人。 則前事已驗，惟陛下詳察。 」太宗深納之。 其年，太子承乾以罪廢，魏王泰入侍，太宗面許立為太子。 因謂侍臣曰：「昨青雀自投我懷云：『臣今日始得與陛下為子，更生之日也。 臣唯有一子，臣百年之後，當為陛下殺之，傳國晉王。 』父子之道，故當天性，我見其如此，甚憐之。 」遂良進曰：「陛下失言。 伏願審思，無令錯誤也。 安有陛下百年之後，魏王執權為天下之主，而能殺其愛子，傳國於晉王者乎？ 陛下昔立承乾為太子，而復寵愛魏王，禮數或有逾於承乾者，良由嫡庶不分，所以至此。 殷鑑不遠，足為龜鏡。 陛下今日既立魏王，伏願陛下別安置晉王，始得安全耳。 」太宗涕泗交下曰：「我不能。 」即日召長孫無忌、房玄齡、李勣與遂良等定策，立晉王為皇太子。 時頻有飛雉集於宮殿之內，太宗問群臣曰：「是何祥也？ 」對曰：「昔秦文公時，有童子化為雉，雌者鳴於陳倉，雄者鳴於南陽。 童子曰：得雄者王，得雌者霸。 文公遂以為寶雞。 後漢光武得雄，遂起南陽而有四海。 陛下舊封秦王，故雄雉見於秦地，此所以彰表明德也。 」太宗悅曰：「立身之道，不可無學，遂良博識，深可重也。 」尋授太子賓客。
Chu Suiliang was the son of Chu Liang, who had served as Palace Attendant at Large. In the closing years of the Daye reign, he accompanied his father to Longyou. When Xue Ju declared himself emperor, he appointed Chu as Master of Ceremonies. After Xue Ju was defeated, Chu returned to the Tang court and was appointed Records Officer for Armor in the Qinzhou Area Command headquarters. In the tenth year of Zhenguan, he was promoted from Secretary to Attendant who records the emperor's movements. Suiliang was widely read in literature and history and especially accomplished in clerical script; Ouyang Xun, a friend of his father, held him in high regard. Taizong once said to Palace Attendant Wei Zheng: "After Yu Shinan died, there has been no one with whom I can discuss calligraphy. " Wei replied: "Chu Suiliang's brushwork is forceful and vigorous and captures the manner of Wang Yishao [Wang Xizhi] very well. " That same day Taizong summoned him and ordered him to attend the court for calligraphy. Taizong once offered gold and silk from the imperial storehouses to buy examples of Wang Xizhi's calligraphy. People everywhere rushed to present old manuscripts to the court, and at the time no one could tell which were genuine. Suiliang discussed the provenance of each piece in detail and never made a mistake. In the fifteenth year, an edict ordered ceremonies at Mount Tai. The emperor first visited Luoyang, and a comet appeared in the Taiwei constellation, encroaching on the Chamberlain's seat. Suiliang said to Taizong: "Your Majesty has quelled chaos and restored order, and your achievements surpass those of earlier sage rulers. You are about to complete the rites at the eastern peak, to the great fortune of the realm. Yet upon reaching Luoyang a comet has appeared. This may mean that something is still not in accord with Heaven's will. Moreover, Emperor Wu of Han deliberated for several years before he performed the rites at Mount Tai. I humbly beg Your Majesty to consider the matter with care. " Taizong was deeply persuaded and issued an edict canceling the feng and shan ceremonies. That year he was promoted to Censor and concurrently oversaw the imperial diary. Taizong once asked: "You oversee the imperial diary. What does it record? As a rule, may a ruler read it? " Suiliang replied: "Today's diary attendants are the ancient left and right scribes. They record the ruler's words and deeds and note good and evil alike, so that they may serve as a mirror and warning and the sovereign may be kept from unlawful conduct. I have never heard of an emperor reading the history himself. " Taizong said: "If I do something wrong, will you record it? " Suiliang said: "Upholding the Way is not as binding as upholding one's office. My duty is to hold the brush, and whatever the ruler does must be written down. " Yellow Gate Attendant Liu Ji said: "Even if Suiliang did not write it down, the world would still record it. " Taizong agreed that this was so. At that time the Prince of Wei was a favorite of Taizong, and the ceremonial honors accorded him were like those of the heir apparent. That year Taizong asked his ministers: "What is the most pressing matter for the state today? " Central Secretariat Attendant Cen Wende said: "The Analects says, 'Guide them with virtue and align them with ritual.' From that point of view, ritual and righteousness are what matter most. " Suiliang stepped forward and said: "Today the four quarters look up to Your Majesty's virtue. Who would dare act wrongly? But the crown prince and the other princes must each have a fixed place. Your Majesty should set a precedent for ten thousand generations to pass on to your descendants. " Taizong said: "You are right. I am nearly fifty and already feel my strength failing. I have already installed my eldest son Shouqi as heir in the Eastern Palace, but my younger sons and sons by concubines number nearly fifty. I worry about this constantly, and that is largely what I mean. Yet since antiquity, when legitimate and secondary heirs lack good advisers, has the state not been overturned? Search out men of talent and virtue to tutor the heir apparent, and for all the princes as well find upright scholars. Moreover, when men serve someone for many years, bonds of affection and obligation grow deep, and many illicit ambitions arise from that. " Thereupon he limited officials in princely households to no more than four terms of service. In the seventeenth year Taizong asked Suiliang: "Shun had lacquer vessels made, and Yu carved his serving stands. More than ten men remonstrated with Shun and Yu at the time. Why such strenuous remonstrance over mere eating utensils? " Suiliang replied: "Carving and polishing harm farming, and brocade work harms women's productive labor. To be the first to indulge in luxury is the first step toward ruin. Once lacquer ware is not enough, vessels must be made of gold; and once gold is not enough, they must be made of jade. That is why remonstrating ministers must speak at the first sign of excess, for once luxury is full-blown, nothing more can be said. " Taizong agreed and added: "For a ruler to neglect the people and pursue luxury is to invite ruin in the blink of an eye. " At that time many young princes were appointed as area commanders and prefects. Suiliang submitted a memorial saying: "In the two Han dynasties the realm was governed through commanderies and kingdoms, and apart from the commanderies the emperor's sons were granted separate domains. They carved out territory and divided the realm, mixing in elements of the Zhou system. Our Tang prefectures and counties follow the Qin model. When princes are still young they are sometimes made prefects. Does Your Majesty not intend your own flesh and blood to guard the four quarters? In establishing this system Your Majesty has surpassed the achievements of earlier sage rulers. In my humble view, one small point has not yet been fully realized. What is it? Prefects and commandery chiefs are whom the people rely on for their security. With one good man in office the district revives; with one bad man the whole prefecture suffers hardship. That is why a ruler who cares for the people must always choose the worthy to govern them. Some say that when a river nourishes the land for nine li, the capital shares in the blessing; or the people break into song and build shrines to a living official. Emperor Xuan of Han said, 'Those who govern with me are only good prefects. ' In my humble view, among Your Majesty's sons those who are still young and not yet fit to govern should remain in the capital for now and be instructed in the classics. First, they will fear Heaven's majesty and not dare break the law; second, they will observe court ceremony and mature naturally. Through long habit they will learn how to conduct themselves as men. Only when they are truly fit to govern a prefecture should they be sent out. I respectfully note that Emperors Ming, Zhang, and He of the Han were able to show brotherly love toward their younger brothers. From their example downward I take my standard. They enfeoffed their sons as kings, and although each had his own domain, those who were still young were summoned to remain in the capital, instructed in ritual and law, and showered with favor. Through the reigns of those three emperors there were scores of princes, and only two turned out badly. All the rest, nurtured in harmony and steeped in instruction, became good men. The precedent has already been proved. I beg Your Majesty to consider it carefully. " Taizong accepted the advice wholeheartedly. That year Crown Prince Chengqian was deposed for his crimes. The Prince of Wei, Li Tai, attended the emperor in person, and Taizong openly promised to make him crown prince. He then told his ministers: "Yesterday the Green Sparrow [Li Tai] threw himself into my arms and said, 'Today I have at last truly become Your Majesty's son. It is like being born again. I have only one son. After my hundred years I will kill him for Your Majesty and pass the realm to the Prince of Jin. ' The bond between father and son ought to be natural. When I saw him speak this way I was deeply moved. " Suiliang stepped forward and said: "Your Majesty has spoken unwisely. I beg Your Majesty to think this through carefully and not let a mistake stand. How can it be that after Your Majesty's hundred years the Prince of Wei, holding power as ruler of the realm, would kill his own beloved son and pass the state to the Prince of Jin? Your Majesty once made Chengqian crown prince, yet you again favored the Prince of Wei, and at times the honors shown him exceeded those of Chengqian. It was because the lines between heir and younger sons were not kept clear that matters came to this pass. The lesson of the recent past is close at hand and should serve as a clear mirror. Now that Your Majesty has raised up the Prince of Wei, I beg you to make separate arrangements for the Prince of Jin. Only then will he be safe. " Taizong wept openly and said, "I cannot. " That same day he summoned Zhangsun Wuji, Fang Xuanling, Li Ji, Suiliang, and others to settle the succession and installed the Prince of Jin as crown prince. At that time wild pheasants kept flying into the palace halls. Taizong asked his ministers: "What omen does this signify? " They replied: "In the time of Duke Wen of Qin a boy was transformed into a pheasant. The female called at Chencang and the male at Nanyang. The boy said, 'He who captures the male will become king; he who captures the female will become hegemon.' Duke Wen thereupon took Chencang as his treasure. Later Emperor Guangwu of Han captured the male bird, rose in Nanyang, and won the realm. Your Majesty was once enfeoffed as Prince of Qin, so the male pheasant appears in Qin territory. This is a sign that your bright virtue is being made manifest. " Taizong was pleased and said: "The way to stand in the world cannot do without learning. Suiliang is deeply learned and truly worthy of respect. " Soon afterward he was appointed Companion to the Crown Prince.

時太宗欲親征高麗，顧謂侍臣曰：「高麗莫離支賊殺其王，虐用其人。 夫出師吊伐，當乘機便，今因其弒虐，誅之甚易。 」遂良對曰：「陛下兵機神算，人莫能知。 昔隋末亂離，手平寇亂。 及北狄侵邊，西蕃失禮，陛下欲命將擊之，群臣莫不苦諫，陛下獨斷進討，卒並誅夷。 海內之人，徼外之國，畏威懾伏，為此舉也。 今陛下將興師遼東，臣意熒惑。 何者？ 陛下神武，不比前代人君。 兵既渡遼，指期克捷，萬一差跌，無以威示遠方，若再發忿兵，則安危難測。 」太宗深然之。 兵部尚書李勣曰：「近者延陀犯邊，陛下必欲追擊，此時陛下取魏徵之言，遂失機會。 若如聖策，延陀無一人生還，可五十年間疆場無事。 」帝曰：「誠如卿言，由魏徵誤計耳。 朕不欲以一計不當而尤之，後有良算，安肯矢謀。 」由是從勣之言，經畫渡遼之師。 遂良以太宗銳意三韓，懼其遺悔，翌日上疏諫曰：
At that time Taizong intended to campaign against Goguryeo in person. He turned to his ministers and said: "Molizhi of Goguryeo murdered his king and cruelly oppressed his people. When one sends an army to console the oppressed and punish the guilty, one must seize the right moment. Now, with this murder and tyranny, it will be very easy to destroy him. " Suiliang replied: "Your Majesty's military stratagems and divine calculations are beyond anyone's ken. At the end of the Sui, when the realm was torn apart, you personally quelled the rebellions. When the northern barbarians raided the frontier and the western tribes showed disrespect, Your Majesty wished to send generals against them. Every minister remonstrated strenuously, yet Your Majesty decided alone to advance, and in the end they were all destroyed. The people within the seas and the states beyond the frontiers feared your majesty and submitted. It was because of actions such as these. Now that Your Majesty intends to raise an army against Liaodong, I am deeply troubled. Why? Your Majesty is divinely martial and not to be compared with rulers of former ages. Once the army crosses the Liao River, victory is expected on schedule. But if by any chance there is a setback, Your Majesty will have no way to display your majesty to distant lands. If you must send angry troops again, the outcome for the state cannot be foreseen. " Taizong strongly agreed with him. Minister of War Li Ji said: "When the Yantuo recently raided our borders, Your Majesty surely wanted to pursue them. But at that moment You listened to Wei Zheng and let the opportunity slip away. Had Your Majesty followed your own wise plan, not a single Yantuo raider would have survived, and the frontiers would have been quiet for fifty years. " The emperor said: "You speak truly—it was Wei Zheng's faulty counsel that caused this. I do not wish to reproach him for a single poor plan. When he has sound advice in future, why would he refuse to speak? " Thereupon he followed Li Ji's advice and set about planning the Liaodong campaign. 太宗不納。 十八年，拜黃門侍郎，參綜朝政。 高麗莫離支遣使貢白金，遂良言於太宗曰：「莫離支虐弒其主，九夷所不容，陛下以之興兵，將事吊伐，為遼山之人報主辱之恥。 古者，討弒君之賊，不受其賂。 昔宋督遺魯君以郜鼎，桓公受之於太廟，臧哀伯諫曰：『君人者昭德塞違，今滅德立違，而置其賂器於太廟，百官象之，其又何誅焉？ 武王克商，遷九鼎於洛邑，義士猶或非之，而況將昭違亂之賂器，置諸太廟，其若之何？ 』夫《春秋》之書，百王取法，若受不臣之筐篚，納弒逆之朝貢，不以為愆，何所致伐？ 臣謂莫離支所獻，自不得受。 」太宗納焉，以其使屬吏。
Taizong did not heed it. In the eighteenth year of Zhenguan, he was appointed Vice Director of the Yellow Gate and took part in governing the court. Molizhi of Goguryeo sent envoys bearing tribute of white gold. Suiliang said to Taizong: "Molizhi cruelly murdered his sovereign—something no barbarian people can tolerate. Your Majesty is raising an army to console the oppressed and punish the guilty, to avenge for the people of the eastern mountains the humiliation their lord suffered. In antiquity, those who punished regicides did not accept bribes from them. Long ago the Supervisor of Song presented the Duke of Lu with the Bao tripod, and Duke Huan accepted it in the Grand Temple. Zang Aibo remonstrated, saying: 'A ruler should make virtue manifest and shut out wrongdoing. Now you extinguish virtue and establish wrongdoing, yet place his bribe in the Grand Temple—the officials will take their cue from this—how then can any wrongdoing be punished?' When King Wu conquered Shang and moved the Nine Tripods to Luoyang, men of principle still criticized the act—how much more objectionable is it to display a bribe associated with rebellion and place it in the Grand Temple? What is one to do? 'The Spring and Autumn Annals is the standard for kings through the ages. If we accept gifts from a disloyal subject and tribute from a regicide, and do not regard it as wrong—what grounds have we for attacking him?' I submit that what Molizhi offers must not be accepted on any account. " Taizong accepted the advice and turned Molizhi's envoys over to the officials.

9
遂良前後諫奏及陳便宜書數十上，多見採納，其年，加銀青光祿大夫。 二十一年，以本官檢校大理卿，尋丁父憂解。 明年，起復舊職，俄拜中書令。
Suiliang submitted dozens of memorials of remonstrance and proposals for practical reforms, many of which were adopted. That year he was additionally promoted to Silver Blue Light Bulwark Grand Master. In the twenty-first year he was appointed provisional Grand Minister of Justice while retaining his current title. Soon after he resigned to observe mourning for his father's death. The following year he was recalled from mourning to his former post, and soon afterward was appointed Grand Secretary.
10
二十三年，太宗寢疾，召遂良及長孫無忌入臥內，謂之曰：「卿等忠烈，簡在朕心。 昔漢武寄霍光，劉備托葛亮，朕之後事，一以委卿。 太子仁孝，卿之所悉，必須盡誠輔佐，永保宗社。 」又顧謂太子曰：「無忌、遂良在，國家之事，汝無憂矣。 」仍命遂良草詔。 高宗即位，賜爵河南縣公。 永徽元年，進封郡公。 尋坐事出為同州刺史。 三年，征拜吏部尚書、同中書門下三品，監修國史，加光祿大夫。 其月，又兼太子賓客。 四年，代張行成為尚書右僕射，依舊知政事。
In the twenty-third year, Taizong fell gravely ill. He summoned Suiliang and Zhangsun Wuji to his bedside and said: "You are loyal and steadfast ministers, and I hold you close in my heart. Emperor Wu of Han entrusted Huo Guang, and Liu Bei entrusted Zhuge Liang. I entrust all my remaining affairs entirely to you. The crown prince is benevolent and filial, as you well know. You must assist him with your utmost loyalty and preserve the dynasty forever. " He also turned to the crown prince and said: "With Wuji and Suiliang here, you need not worry about the affairs of state. " He also ordered Suiliang to draft the imperial edict. When Emperor Gaozong ascended the throne, Suiliang was granted the title Marquis of Henan Commandery. In the first year of Yonghui, he was promoted to Duke of a commandery. Soon afterward, implicated in an offense, he was sent out to serve as Prefect of Tongzhou. In the third year he was recalled and appointed Minister of Personnel with the concurrent third rank at the Secretariat and Chancellery, put in charge of compiling the national history, and additionally granted the title Grand Master for Splendid Happiness. That same month he was additionally made Guest of the Crown Prince. In the fourth year he replaced Zhang Xingcheng as Right Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs and continued to participate in governing as before.
11
六年，高宗將廢皇后王氏，立昭儀武氏為皇后，召太尉長孫無忌、司空李勣、尚書左僕射於志寧及遂良以籌其事。 將入，遂良謂無忌等曰：「上意欲廢中宮，必議其事，遂良今欲陳諫，眾意如何？ 」無忌曰：「明公必須極言，無忌請繼焉。 」及入，高宗難於發言，再三顧謂無忌曰：「莫大之罪，絕嗣為甚。 皇后無胤息，昭儀有子，今欲立為皇后，公等以為何如？ 」遂良曰：「皇后出自名家，先朝所娶，伏事先帝，無愆婦德。 先帝不豫，執陛下手以語臣曰：『我好兒好婦，今將付卿。 』陛下親承德音，言猶在耳。 皇后自此未聞有愆，恐不可廢。 臣今不敢曲從，上違先帝之命，特願再三思審。 愚臣上忤聖顏，罪合萬死，但願不負先朝厚恩，何顧性命？ 」遂良致笏於殿陛，曰：「還陛下此笏。 」仍解巾叩頭流血。 帝大怒，令引出。 長孫無忌曰：「遂良受先朝顧命，有罪不加刑。 」翌日，帝謂李勣曰：「冊立武昭儀之事，遂良固執不從。 遂良既是受顧命大臣，事若不可，當且止也。 」勣對曰：「此乃陛下家事，不合問外人。 」帝乃立昭儀為皇后，左遷遂良潭州都督。 顯慶二年，轉桂州都督。 未幾，又貶為愛州刺史。 明年，卒官，年六十三。
In the sixth year, Gaozong intended to depose Empress Wang and install Lady Wu of the Zhao Yi rank as empress. He summoned Grand Preceptor Zhangsun Wuji, Minister of Works Li Ji, Left Vice Director Yu Zhining, and Suiliang to discuss the matter. Before entering, Suiliang said to Wuji and the others: "The emperor means to depose the empress and will surely raise the matter. I intend to remonstrate—what do you all think? " Wuji said: "You must speak out forcefully, and I will add my voice after you. " When they entered, Gaozong found it hard to broach the subject. Again and again he turned to Wuji and said: "Of great offenses, the worst is to be left without an heir. The empress has borne no children, but Lady Wu has a son. I now wish to make her empress—what do you think? " Suiliang said: "The empress comes from a distinguished house and was chosen in marriage by the previous reign. She served the late emperor faithfully and has committed no fault as a consort. When the late emperor was gravely ill, he took Your Majesty's hand and told me: 'My good son and good daughter-in-law—I now entrust them to your care.' ' Your Majesty received this charge directly from him, and his words still ring in our ears. Since then we have heard of no fault on the empress's part—I fear she cannot be deposed. I dare not simply go along with this and violate the late emperor's command. I earnestly beg Your Majesty to reconsider again and again. I, a foolish minister, offend Your Majesty and deserve death a thousand times over. But I wish only not to betray the deep kindness the previous reign showed me—what care I for my own life? " Suiliang placed his court tablet on the palace steps and said: "I return this tablet to Your Majesty. " He then removed his official cap and knocked his head on the ground until blood flowed. The emperor flew into a rage and ordered him removed from the chamber. Zhangsun Wuji said: "Suiliang was entrusted with the late emperor's final charge—though he has offended, do not punish him. " The next day the emperor said to Li Ji: "On the matter of establishing Lady Wu as empress, Suiliang stubbornly refused to go along. Suiliang is a minister entrusted with the late emperor's final charge. If the matter is not feasible, it should be set aside for now. " Li Ji replied: "This is Your Majesty's private family affair—it is not appropriate to consult outsiders on it. " The emperor then installed Lady Wu as empress and demoted Suiliang to Area Commander of Tanzhou. In the second year of Xianqing he was transferred to Area Commander of Guizhou. Before long he was demoted again to Prefect of Ai Prefecture. The following year he died in office at the age of sixty-three.

遂良卒後二歲餘，許敬宗、李義府奏言長孫無忌所構逆謀，並遂良搧動，乃追削官爵，子孫配流愛州。 弘道元年二月，高宗遺詔放還本郡。 神龍元年，則天遺制復遂良及韓瑗爵位。
A little more than two years after Suiliang's death, Xu Jingzong and Li Yifu charged that Zhangsun Wuji had plotted rebellion and that Suiliang had instigated it. Suiliang's titles were posthumously revoked and his descendants were banished to Ai Prefecture. In the second month of the first year of Hongdao, Gaozong's deathbed edict allowed them to return to their native district. In the first year of Shenlong, Empress Wu's deathbed edict restored the titles of Suiliang and Han Yuan.

=韓瑗=
Han Yuan
14

Han Yuan was a native of Sanyuan in Yong Prefecture. His grandfather Han Shao served in the Sui as Vice Minister of the Imperial Stud. His father Han Zhongliang served at the beginning of the Wude reign as Vice Minister of Justice and was ordered, together with Lang Chuzhi and others, to supervise the codification of laws and regulations. Zhongliang said to Emperor Gaozu: "The laws of the Zhou ran to three thousand articles, but since the Qin code the number has been reduced to about five hundred. If we closely follow the Zhou system, the law will only become more complex and burdensome. Moreover, if officials are truly impartial they will naturally obey the law; if they indulge their private interests, what regard will they have for the penal code? I beg that we uphold leniency and simplicity, in keeping with the hope for a fresh start. " Emperor Gaozu agreed. Thereupon they adopted the Kaihuang Code and put it into effect, and at the time it was considered practical. During the Zhenguan era he rose to Minister of Punishments, Senior Administrator of the Qinzhou Area Command, and Duke of Yingchuan. Han Yuan possessed moral integrity from youth. He was broadly learned and had a talent for administration. During the Zhenguan era he rose to Vice Minister of War and inherited his father's title as Duke of Yingchuan. In the third year of Yonghui he was appointed Vice Director of the Yellow Gate. In the fourth year he and Vice Director of the Secretariat Lai Ji were both appointed to the third rank at the Secretariat and Chancellery and put in charge of compiling the national history. In the fifth year he was additionally granted the title Silver Blue Light Bulwark Grand Master. In the sixth year he was promoted to Palace Attendant, and that same year additionally served as Guest of the Crown Prince. At that time Gaozong intended to depose Empress Wang. Han Yuan remonstrated through tears, saying: "The empress was chosen in marriage for Your Majesty by the late emperor when you were still crown prince. She has committed no fault. If you proceed with deposition, what scholar throughout the realm would not be alarmed? Moreover, repeated depositions and installations are no recipe for long-term stability. I beg Your Majesty, for the sake of the dynasty's welfare, not to dismiss my foolish words without consideration. " The emperor did not heed him. The next day Han Yuan remonstrated again, weeping with grief he could not contain. The emperor flew into a rage and ordered him removed at once. Soon Left Vice Director Chu Suiliang, for having defied the imperial will, was demoted to Area Commander of Tanzhou. Han Yuan then submitted a memorial in his defense, saying:

古之聖王，立諫鼓，設謗木，冀欲聞逆耳之言，甘苦口之議，發揚大化，裨益洪猷，垂令譽於將來，播休聲於不朽者也。 伏見詔書以褚遂良為潭州都督，臣夙夜思之，用增感激。 臣識慚知遠，業謝通經，載撫愚情，誠為未可。 遂良運偶昇平，道昭前烈，束髮從宦，方淹累稔。 趨侍陛下，俄歷歲年，不聞涓滴之愆，常睹勤勞之效。 竭忠誠於早歲，罄直道於茲年。 體國忘家，捐身徇物，風霜其操，鐵石其心。 誠可重於皇明，詎專方於曩昔？ 且先帝納之於帷幄，寄之以心膂，德逾水石，義冠舟車，公家之利，言無不可。 及纏悲四海，遏密八音，竭忠國家，親承顧托，一德無二，千古懍然。 此不待臣言，陛下備知之矣。 臣嘗有此心，未敢聞奏。 且萬姓失業，旰食忘勞； 一物不安，納隍軫慮，在於微細，寧得過差。 況社稷之舊臣，陛下之賢佐，無聞罪狀，斥去朝廷，內外氓黎，咸嗟舉措。 觀其近日言事，披誠懇切，詎肯後陛下之德，異於堯、舜； 懼陛下之過，塵於史冊。 而乃深遭厚謗，重負丑言，可以痛志士之心，損陛下之明也。 臣聞晉武弘裕，不貽劉毅之誅； 漢祖深仁，無恚周昌之直。 而遂良被遷，已經寒暑，違忤陛下，其罰塞焉。 伏願纟面鑑無辜，稍寬非罪，俯矜微款，以順人情。
The sage kings of antiquity set up drums for remonstrance and boards for criticism, hoping to hear unwelcome truths and bitter counsel that would extend their great transformation, strengthen their broad designs, win lasting praise for generations to come, and spread a noble reputation through the ages. I have seen the edict appointing Chu Suiliang Area Commander of Tanzhou, and the more I reflect on it day and night, the more deeply I am moved. My insight is far less than that of those with foresight, and my learning falls short of mastery of the classics. Yet as I consider the matter in my heart, I feel I cannot remain silent. Suiliang came of age in a time of peace and upholds the principles of the great ministers of old. He entered government service from youth and has served for many years. He has attended Your Majesty for many years now, and not the slightest fault has ever been reported, while his diligent service has been evident throughout. He gave his utmost loyalty in his early years and has held fast to integrity until this day. He puts the state before his family and gives himself wholly to public service; his integrity is firm as iron and stone. Surely he deserves to be valued by this enlightened reign—is his worth to be recognized only in retrospect? Moreover, the late emperor welcomed him into his inner counsels and entrusted him as one would his own heart and sinews. His virtue was steadfast as water and stone, his loyalty surpassed all others, and in matters benefiting the state none of his counsel was ever rejected. When grief engulfed the realm and music was stilled at the late emperor's death, he gave his utmost loyalty to the state and personally received the deathbed charge—his single-minded devotion was beyond compare and inspires awe through the ages. Your Majesty knows all this better than I need to say. I have long felt this way but have not dared to bring it before the throne. Moreover, the common people are suffering hardship, and Your Majesty rises early and sleeps late in your devotion to governance; When the slightest thing goes wrong you take it to heart as if the realm were in peril—in such careful attention to detail, how could there be any mistake? How much more so when a longtime minister of the dynasty, one of Your Majesty's ablest advisors, is dismissed from court without any known offense—people everywhere, within the palace and without, lament this decision. Judging by his recent memorials, he spoke with the utmost sincerity and candor—surely he wished only to ensure that Your Majesty's virtue would not fall short of that of Yao and Shun; He feared only that Your Majesty's errors might be recorded for posterity. Yet he has been deeply slandered and burdened with shameful accusations. This wounds the hearts of loyal men and dims Your Majesty's discernment. I have heard that Emperor Wu of Jin was magnanimous and did not punish Liu Yi; and the founder of Han was deeply humane and bore no resentment toward Zhou Chang's blunt honesty. Suiliang has been exiled for more than a year. In opposing Your Majesty he has already suffered punishment enough. I humbly beg Your Majesty to look with compassion on an innocent man, ease his undeserved punishment, and show a little mercy in accord with what the people expect.

When the memorial was submitted, the emperor said to Han Yuan: "I know Suiliang's feelings as well. But he was defiant and insulted his sovereign. For that he is being punished. How can I be at fault? Why do you speak so strongly! " Han Yuan replied: "Suiliang may truly be called a loyal minister of the state. I fear that flatterers, like flies spotting a speck on white jade, will slander and destroy a faithful man. In the past, when Weizi left, the Yin state perished; when Zhang Hua lived, the laws were not thrown into chaos. When a state is about to fall, good men are the first to decline. Now Your Majesty possesses all within the four seas and the realm is at peace. Will you suddenly drive out an old minister and not look into the matter carefully! I beg Your Majesty to avoid repeating past mistakes, recall your former minister, and show by your example how a ruler should heed loyal counsel. Then the people will be greatly blessed. " The emperor would not be persuaded. Because his advice went unheeded, Han Yuan submitted a memorial in grief and anger asking to retire to his home estate, but the emperor refused. In the second year of Xianqing, Xu Jingzong and Li Yifu, acting on the empress's wishes, falsely accused Han Yuan and Chu Suiliang of plotting rebellion. They claimed that because Guizhou was a strategic military region, Suiliang had been made prefect of Guizhou to serve as outside support for the plot. Suiliang was further demoted to prefect of Ai Prefecture, and Han Yuan was transferred to prefect of Zhen Prefecture. In the fourth year he died in office at the age of fifty-four. The following year Zhangsun Wuji died. Jingzong and others then accused Han Yuan of conspiring with Wuji and sent agents to kill him. When the envoys arrived Han Yuan was already dead. They opened his coffin to verify the corpse, then returned. His property was confiscated and his grandsons were exiled to the far south. In the first year of Shenlong, Empress Wu's final testament ordered that his offices and titles be restored.

=來濟=
Lai Ji

來濟，揚州江都人，隋左翊衛大將軍榮國公護子也。 宇文化及之難，闔門遇害。 濟幼逢家難，流離艱險，而篤志好學，有文詞，善談論，尤曉時務。 舉進士，貞觀中累轉通事舍人。 太子承乾之敗，太宗謂侍臣曰：「欲何以處承乾？ 」群臣莫敢對，濟進曰：「陛下上不失作慈父，下得盡天年，即為善矣。 」帝納其言。 俄除考功員外郎。 十八年，初置太子司議郎，妙選人望，遂以濟為之，仍兼崇賢館直學士。 尋遷中書舍人，與令狐德棻等撰《晉書》。 永徽二年，拜中書侍郎，兼弘文館學士，監修國史。 四年，同中書門下三品。 五年，加銀青光祿大夫，以修國史功封南陽縣男，賜物七百段。 六年，遷中書令、檢校吏部尚書。 時高宗欲立昭儀武氏為宸妃，濟密表諫曰：「宸妃古無此號，事將不可。 」武皇后既立，濟等懼不自安； 後乃抗表稱濟忠公，請加賞慰，而心實惡之。 顯慶元年，兼太子賓客，進爵為侯，中書令如故。 二年，又兼太子詹事。 尋而許敬宗等奏濟與褚遂良朋黨構扇，左授台州刺史。 五年，徙庭州刺史。 龍朔二年，突厥入寇，濟總兵拒之，謂其眾曰：「吾嘗掛刑網，蒙赦性命，當以身塞責，特報國恩。 」遂不釋甲冑赴賊，沒於陣。 時年五十三，贈楚州刺史，給靈輿遞還鄉。 有文集三十捲，行於代。 兄亙濟兄亙，有學行，與濟齊名。 上元中，官至黃門侍郎、同中書門下三品。
Lai Ji was from Jiangdu in Yangzhou, the son of Hu, Duke of Rong, who had served the Sui as General of the Left Wing Guard. During the catastrophe wrought by Yuwen Huaji, his entire family was killed. Ji suffered family disaster in his youth and wandered through hardship, yet he devoted himself to study. He was accomplished in letters, skilled in debate, and especially well versed in affairs of state. He passed the jinshi examination and during Zhenguan rose through several posts to Master of Ceremonies. After Crown Prince Chengqian's downfall, Taizong asked his ministers: "How should Chengqian be dealt with? " No minister dared answer. Ji stepped forward and said: "Your Majesty will not fail as a loving father, and the prince may live out his natural span. That is enough. " The emperor accepted his advice. Soon afterward he was appointed Vice Director in the Ministry of Personnel. In the eighteenth year the office of Master of Discussion for the crown prince was first created. Men of outstanding reputation were sought, and Ji was appointed, while also serving as Academician in the Palace of Exalted Virtue. He was soon promoted to Secretariat Drafter and joined Linghu Defen and others in compiling the Book of Jin. In the second year of Yonghui he was appointed Central Secretariat Attendant, concurrently served as Academician of the Hongwen Hall, and supervised compilation of the national history. In the fourth year he was made Third Grade at the Central Secretariat and Chancellery. In the fifth year he was promoted to Silver Blue Light Bulwark Grand Master. For his work on the national history he was enfeoffed as Baron of Nanyang and granted seven hundred rolls of goods. In the sixth year he was made Grand Secretary and Acting Minister of Personnel. At that time Gaozong wished to create the Brilliant Consort Lady Wu as Chen Consort. Ji submitted a secret memorial of remonstrance: "There was never such a title as Chen Consort in antiquity. The matter cannot be done. " After Empress Wu was established, Ji and his allies lived in fear; yet she later submitted a memorial praising Ji as loyal and upright and asking that he be rewarded, while in her heart she truly hated him. In the first year of Xianqing he was additionally appointed Companion to the Crown Prince and advanced to Marquis, while retaining his post as Grand Secretary. In the second year he was additionally made Steward of the Heir Apparent's Household. Soon Xu Jingzong and others accused Ji of forming a faction with Chu Suiliang and stirring up trouble. He was transferred to prefect of Taizhou. In the fifth year he was transferred to prefect of Ting Prefecture. In the second year of Longshuo the Turks invaded. Ji took command of the army to resist them and told his troops: "I once faced execution and was spared. I should repay the state's grace with my life. " He did not remove his armor and charged the enemy, dying in battle. He was fifty-three years old. He was posthumously appointed prefect of Chu Prefecture, and a funeral carriage was provided to return his body home. He left a collected works in thirty scrolls, which circulated in his day. His elder brother Heng was a man of learning and integrity and was as renowned as Ji. During the Shangyuan reign he rose to Yellow Gate Attendant and Third Grade at the Central Secretariat and Chancellery.

=上官儀=
Shangguan Yi
20
上官儀，本陝州陝人也。 父弘，隋江都宮副監，因家於江都。 大業末，弘為將軍陳稜所殺，儀時幼，藏匿獲免。 因私度為沙門，游情釋典，尤精《三論》，兼涉獵經史，善屬文。 貞觀初，楊仁恭為都督，深禮待之。 舉進士。 太宗聞其名，召授弘文館直學士。 累遷秘書郎。 時太宗雅好屬文，每遣儀視草，又多令繼和，凡有宴集，儀嘗預焉。 俄又預撰《晉書》成，轉起居郎，加級賜帛。 高宗嗣位，遷秘書少監。 龍朔二年，加銀青光祿大夫、西台侍郎、同東西台三品，兼弘文館學士如故。 本以詞彩自達，工於五言詩，好以綺錯婉媚為本。 儀既貴顯，故當時多有效其體者，時人謂為上官體。 儀頗恃才任勢，故為當代所嫉。 麟德元年，宦者王伏勝與梁王忠抵罪，許敬宗乃構儀與忠通謀，遂下獄而死，家口籍沒。 子庭芝，歷位周王府屬。 與儀俱被殺。 庭芝有女，中宗時為昭容，每侍帝草制誥，以故追贈儀為中書令、秦州都督、楚國公； 庭芝黃門侍郎、岐州刺史、天水郡公，仍令以禮改葬。
Shangguan Yi was originally from Shan in Shan Prefecture. His father Hong had served the Sui as Deputy Supervisor of the Jiangdu Palace and settled the family in Jiangdu. At the end of the Daye reign Hong was killed by the general Chen Suo. Yi was still a child and survived by hiding. He then secretly became a monk, devoted himself to Buddhist scriptures, and became especially expert in the Three Treatises. He also read widely in the classics and histories and was skilled at writing. Early in Zhenguan, when Yang Rengong was area commander, he treated Yi with great respect. He passed the jinshi examination. Taizong heard of him and summoned him to serve as Academician in the Hongwen Hall. He rose through several posts to Secretariat Gentleman. Taizong loved literature and often had Yi review his drafts and compose matching pieces. Yi was a regular presence at court banquets. He soon took part in completing the Book of Jin, was transferred to Attendant of the Imperial Diary, and received an additional rank and a gift of silk. When Gaozong succeeded to the throne, Yi was promoted to Vice Director of the Secretariat. In the second year of Longshuo he was made Silver Blue Light Bulwark Grand Master, Western Terrace Attendant, and Third Grade at the Eastern and Western Terraces, while retaining his post as Academician of the Hongwen Hall. He had risen through the brilliance of his writing and was skilled in five-character verse, favoring an ornate and graceful style. Once Yi had risen to high rank, many poets of the day imitated his manner, which came to be known as the Shangguan style. Yi relied heavily on his talent and power, and for that reason was resented by his contemporaries. In the first year of Linde the eunuch Wang Fusheng and Prince Zhong of Liang were found guilty of crimes. Xu Jingzong then framed Yi as having conspired with Zhong. Yi was imprisoned and died, and his household was confiscated. His son Tingzhi had served as an official in the household of the Prince of Zhou. He was executed together with Yi. Tingzhi had a daughter who under Zhongzong served as Brilliant Lady and often helped the emperor draft edicts. For this reason Yi was posthumously appointed Grand Secretary, Area Commander of Qinzhou, and Duke of Chu; and Tingzhi was posthumously made Yellow Gate Attendant, prefect of Qi Prefecture, and Duke of Tianshui. They were reburied with full ceremony.

=史臣曰=
The Historiographer's Comment
22
史臣曰：褚河南上書言事，亹癖有經世遠略。 魏徵、王珪之後，骨鯁風彩，落落負王佐器者，殆難其人。 名臣事業，河南有焉。 昔齊人饋樂而仲尼去，戎王溺妓而由余奔，婦人之言，聖哲懼罹其禍，況二佞據衡軸之地，為正人之魑魅乎！ 古之志士仁人，一言相期，死不之悔，況於君臣之間，受託孤之寄，而以利害禍福，忘平生之言哉！ 而韓、來諸公，可謂守死善道，求福不回者焉。
The historiographer says: Chu Suiliang of Henan submitted memorials on affairs of state with tireless integrity and far-reaching vision for ordering the realm. After Wei Zheng and Wang Gui, among men of stern integrity and commanding presence who openly bore the capacity of a chief minister, his equal was hard to find. Among the achievements of famous ministers, Henan had his place. In antiquity Confucius left Qi when music was offered to him, and Youyu fled when the Rong king drowned himself in entertainers. Even sages feared the harm that could come from a woman's words. How much worse when two flatterers held the pivot of power and became demons haunting upright men! Men of resolve in antiquity, once they pledged themselves with a single word, did not regret it even unto death. How much more between ruler and minister, when one has received the charge of a dying sovereign, to forget one's lifelong pledge for the sake of profit, loss, or personal safety! Han Yuan, Lai Ji, and their fellows may truly be called men who upheld the Way unto death and would not turn aside even to seek their own fortune.

=贊=
Eulogy
24
贊曰：褚公之言，和樂愔愔，鐘石在虡，動成雅音。 二猘雙吠，三賢一心。 人皆觀望，我不浮沉。
The eulogy says: Lord Chu's words were harmonious and serene, like bells and chimes on their stand—when struck they formed music of the highest refinement. Two mad dogs barked together; three worthy men stood as one. While others watched and waited, they would not drift with the tide.
