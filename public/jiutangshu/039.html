1
《易》曰：「觀乎天文以察時變。」 是故古之哲王，法垂象以施化，考庶徵以致理，以授人時，以考物紀，修其德以順其度，改其過以慎其災，去危而就安，轉禍而為福者也。 夫其五緯七紀之名數，中官外官之位次，淩歷犯守之所主，飛流彗孛之所應，前史載之備矣。
The Changes says: "Observe the patterns of heaven to discern the changes of the seasons." Therefore the sage kings of antiquity took the celestial signs as their model to extend their transforming influence, examined the many portents to achieve good order, granted the seasons to the people, verified the records of things, cultivated their virtue to accord with the celestial measures, corrected their faults to guard against calamities—leaving danger for safety and turning misfortune into blessing. As for the names and numbers of the five planets and seven regulators, the positions of the inner and outer official stars, what transits, invasions, and stations govern, and what meteors, comets, and broom-stars portend—earlier histories record them fully.
2
武德年中，薛頤、庾儉等相次為太史令，雖各善於占候，而無所發明。 貞觀初，將仕郎直太史李淳風始上言靈臺候儀是後魏遺範，法制疏略，難為占步。 太宗因令淳風改造渾儀，鑄銅為之，至七年造成。 淳風因撰《法象志》七卷，以論前代渾儀得失之差，語在《淳風傳》。 其所造渾儀，太宗令置於凝暉閣以用測候，既在宮中，尋而失其所在。 玄宗，太史頻奏日蝕不效，詔沙門一行改造新曆。 一行奏云：「今欲創曆立元，須知黃道進退，請太史令測候星度。」 有司云：「承前唯依赤道推步，官無黃道遊儀，無由測候。」 時率府兵曹梁令瓚待制於麗正書院，因造遊儀木樣，甚為精密。 一行乃上言曰：「黃道游儀，古有其術而無其器。 以黃道隨天運動，難用常儀格之，故昔人潛思皆不能得。 今梁令瓚創造此圖，日道月交，莫不自然契合，既于推步尤要，望就書院更以銅鐵為之，庶得考驗星度，無有差舛。」 從之，至十三年造成。 又上疏曰：
During the Wude era, Xue Yi, Yu Jian, and others served in succession as Director of the Astronomy Bureau; though each was skilled at divination and observation, none made any innovations. At the beginning of the Zhenguan era, Li Chunfeng, Attendant Gentleman on probation attached to the Astronomy Bureau, first submitted that the Lingtai observatory instruments were a Northern Wei legacy—crude in regulation and difficult to use for astronomical computation. Emperor Taizong therefore ordered Chunfeng to redesign the armillary sphere, casting it in bronze; it was completed in the seventh year. Chunfeng thereupon compiled the Record of Celestial Forms in seven scrolls, discussing the gains, losses, and discrepancies of armillary spheres in earlier ages; the account is in his biography. The armillary sphere he made was ordered placed in the Ninghui Pavilion for measurement and observation; being within the palace, its whereabouts were soon lost. Under Emperor Xuanzong, the Astronomy Bureau repeatedly reported that solar eclipses did not match predictions, and an edict ordered the monk Yixing to revise the calendar anew. Yixing memorialized, saying: "We now wish to establish a new calendar and set the epoch, and must know the ecliptic's advance and retreat. I request the Director of the Astronomy Bureau measure and observe the star degrees." The responsible officials said: "Following precedent we rely only on the equator for computation. The office has no ecliptic traveling instrument, and there is no means to measure and observe." At that time Liang Lingzan, military staff officer of the Shuai Prefecture, was on special assignment at the Lizheng Academy; he therefore made a wooden model of the traveling instrument, which was extremely precise. Yixing then submitted, saying: "The ecliptic traveling instrument—antiquity had the method but not the device. Because the ecliptic moves with the turning of heaven, it is difficult to fix with a constant instrument; therefore men of old who pondered deeply could not obtain it. Now Liang Lingzan has created this diagram—the solar path and lunar crossing naturally accord without discrepancy; it is especially essential for computation. I hope it may be recast in bronze and iron at the academy, so that star degrees may be verified without error." The request was approved; it was completed in the thirteenth year. He also submitted a memorial, saying:
3
自周室衰微，疇人喪職，其制度遺象，莫有傳者。 漢興，丞相張蒼首創律曆之學。 至武帝詔司馬遷等更造漢曆，乃定東西、立晷儀、下漏刻，以追二十八宿相距星度，與古不同。 故唐都分天部，洛下閎運算轉曆，今赤道曆星度，則其遺法也。 後漢永元中，左中郎將賈逵奏言：「臣前上傅安等用黃道度日月，弦望多近。 史官壹以赤道度之，不與天合，至差一日以上。 願請太史官日月宿簿及星度課，與待詔星官考校。」 奏可。 「問典星待詔姚崇等十二人，皆曰：『星圖有規法，日月實從黃道，官無其器，不知施行。』， 大司農丞耿壽昌奏，以圓儀度日月行，考驗天運。 日月行赤道，至牽牛、東井，日行一度，月行十五度； 至婁、角，日行一度，月行十三度，此前代所共知也。」 是歲永元四載也。 明年，始詔太史造黃道銅儀。 冬至，日在斗十九度四分度之一，與赤道定差二度。 史官以校日月弦望，雖密近，而不為望日。 儀，黃道與度運轉，難候，是以少終其事。 其後劉洪因黃道渾儀，以考月行出入遲速。 而後代理曆者不遵其法，更從赤道命文，以驗賈逵所言，差謬益甚，此理曆者之大惑也。
From the decline of the Zhou house, the calendar officers lost their offices; their institutions and preserved instruments had no transmitters. When Han arose, Chancellor Zhang Cang first established the study of pitch-pipes and calendrics. At Emperor Wu's edict, Sima Qian and others further made the Han calendar, fixing east and west, setting up the gnomon instrument and lowering the clepsydra graduations, to pursue the star degrees between the twenty-eight lodges—different from antiquity. Therefore Tang Du divided the celestial departments; Luoxia Hong computed and turned the calendar—the present equatorial lodge star degrees are his transmitted method. In the Yongyuan era of Later Han, Left Palace Attendant Jia Kui memorialized, saying: "I previously submitted that Fu An and others, using the ecliptic to measure the Sun and Moon, found new and full moons mostly close. The astronomy officers uniformly measured by the equator; it did not accord with heaven, differing by more than a full day. I wish to request the astronomy office's ledgers of the Sun, Moon, and lodges and the star-degree assessments, to be examined and corrected with the star-official attendants awaiting edict." The memorial was approved. "When the canonical-star attendants awaiting edict Yao Chong and twelve others were questioned, all said: 'The star charts have regulated methods; the Sun and Moon truly follow the ecliptic. The office has no instrument, and does not know how to implement it.' Grand Agriculture Assistant Geng Shouchang memorialized, using a circular instrument to measure the Sun and Moon's motion, verifying the heavenly cycle. The Sun and Moon travel the equator; at Oxherd and Eastern Well, the Sun travels one degree per day and the Moon fifteen degrees; at Bond and Horn, the Sun travels one degree per day and the Moon thirteen degrees—this earlier generations jointly knew." This was the fourth year of Yongyuan. The next year, an edict first ordered the Astronomy Bureau to make a bronze ecliptic instrument. At the winter solstice, the Sun was at nineteen and one-quarter degrees of Dipper, with a fixed difference of two degrees from the equator. The astronomy officers used it to verify the Sun and Moon at new and full moon; though closely near, it was not at the day of full moon. The instrument—the ecliptic turning with the degree motion—was difficult to observe; therefore they scarcely completed the task. Afterward Liu Hong, by the ecliptic armillary sphere, examined the Moon's motion in entering and leaving and its slowness and speed. But later calendar-makers did not follow his method, again assigning lodges from the equator to verify what Jia Kui said—the errors grew ever greater; this is the great confusion of those who regulate calendrics.
4
今靈臺鐵儀，後魏明元時都匠解蘭所造，規制樸略，度刻不均，赤道不動，乃如膠柱，不置黃道，進退無准。 此據赤道月行以驗入曆遲速，多者或至十七度，少者僅出十度，不足以上稽天象，敬授人時。 近秘閣郎中李淳風著《法象志》，備載黃道渾儀法，以玉衡旋規，別帶日道，傍列二百四十九交，以攜月游，用法頗雜，其術竟寢。
The present Lingtai iron instrument was made by the master craftsman Xie Lan in the Mingyuan era of Northern Wei; its regulated form was crude and summary, its degree graduations uneven, the equator unmoving—like a glued pillar—without setting the ecliptic, advance and retreat without standard. This, relying on the equator to verify the Moon's entering the calendar for slowness and speed, in some cases reached seventeen degrees, in others barely exceeded ten degrees—insufficient to examine heavenly signs above and reverently grant the seasons to the people. Recently Secretariat Gentleman Li Chunfeng authored the Record of Celestial Forms, fully recording the method of the ecliptic armillary sphere—using the jade transversal revolving ring, separately carrying the solar path, with two hundred forty-nine crossings arrayed beside to carry the Moon's travel; the method was rather complex, and the technique in the end was abandoned.
5
臣伏承恩旨，更造遊儀，使黃道運行，以追列舍之變，因二分之中以立黃道，交於軫、奎之間，二至陟降各二十四度。 黃道之內，又施白道月環，用究陰陽朓朒之數，動合天運，簡而易從，足以製器垂象，永傳不朽。
Your subject, receiving the gracious directive, has further made the traveling instrument, causing the ecliptic to move, to pursue the changes of the lodges; at the two equinoxes the ecliptic is established, crossing between Chariot and Strider; at the two solstices ascent and descent are each twenty-four degrees. Within the ecliptic, a white-path lunar ring is also set, to investigate the numbers of yin and yang, waxing and waning; moving in accord with the heavenly cycle, simple and easy to follow—sufficient to make instruments and suspend signs, transmitted forever without perishing.
6
於是玄宗親為製銘，置之於靈臺以考星度。 其二十八宿及中外官與古經不同者，凡數十條。 又詔一行與梁令瓚及諸術士更造渾天儀，鑄銅為圓天之象，上具列宿赤道及周天度數。 注水激輪，令其自轉，一日一夜，天轉一周。 又別置二輪絡在天外，綴以日月，令得運行。 每天西轉一幣，日東行一度，月行十三度十九分度之七，凡二十九轉有餘而日月會，三百六十五轉而日行匝。 仍置木櫃以為地平，令儀半在地下，晦明朔望，遲速有准。 又立二木人于地平之上，前置鐘鼓以候辰刻，每一刻自然擊鼓，每辰則自然撞鐘。 皆于櫃中各施輪軸，鉤鍵交錯，關鎖相持。 既與天道合同，當時共稱其妙。 鑄成，命之曰「水運渾天俯視圖」，置於武成殿前以示百僚。 無幾而銅鐵漸澀，不能自轉，遂收置於集賢院，不復行用。
Thereupon Emperor Xuanzong personally composed an inscription and placed it at the Lingtai to examine star degrees. The twenty-eight lodges and inner and outer official stars that differ from the ancient canon number several tens of entries. An edict also ordered Yixing, Liang Lingzan, and various technicians to further make an armillary celestial globe, casting bronze as the image of the round heaven, above equipped with the lodges, equator, and degrees of the full circuit. Water was poured to drive the wheel, causing it to turn of itself; in one day and one night, heaven turns one circuit. Two wheels were separately set linked outside heaven, carrying the Sun and Moon, causing them to move. Each day heaven turns west one coin-width; the Sun travels east one degree, the Moon thirteen and seven-nineteenths degrees; in twenty-nine-odd turns the Sun and Moon meet; in three hundred sixty-five turns the Sun completes a circuit. A wooden cabinet was still set as the horizon, causing half the instrument to be below ground; dark moon, bright moon, new moon, and full moon, slowness and speed had standards. Two wooden figures were also set on the horizon, with bell and drum placed before to mark the double-hours and graduations; each graduation naturally struck the drum, each double-hour naturally struck the bell. All within the cabinet wheels and axles were set, hooks and keys interlaced, bolts and locks mutually holding. Being in accord with the heavenly Way, at the time all together praised its marvel. When casting was complete, it was named the "Water-Driven Armillary Celestial Globe Viewed from Above" and placed before the Wucheng Hall to show the hundred officials. Before long the bronze and iron gradually stiffened and could not turn of itself; it was then collected and stored in the Jixian Academy, no longer used.
7
今錄遊儀制度及所測星度異同，分遣使諸州所測日晷長短，李淳風、僧一行所定十二次分野，武德已來交蝕及五星祥變，著於篇。
Now recorded are the regulations of the traveling instrument and the star degrees measured, their similarities and differences; separately dispatched envoys to the various prefectures to measure the lengths of solar shadows; the twelve divisions and field-allotments fixed by Li Chunfeng and the monk Yixing; eclipses and the five planets' auspicious changes since Wude—set forth in this treatise.
8
旋樞雙環：外一丈四尺六寸一分，豎八分，厚三分，直徑四尺五寸九分，即古所謂旋儀也。 南北斜兩極，上下循規各三十四度，兩面各畫周天度數。 一面加釘，並用銀飾，使東西運轉如渾天遊儀。 中旋樞軸至兩極首內，孔徑大兩度半，長與旋環徑齊，並用古尺四分為度。
Rotating-pivot double ring: outer one zhang four chi six cun one fen, vertical eight fen, thick three fen, diameter four chi five cun nine fen—the ancient so-called rotating instrument. North and south oblique poles, above and below following the ring each thirty-four degrees; on both faces the degrees of heaven's full circuit are painted. On one face nails are added, all silver ornamented, causing east-west rotation like the armillary traveling instrument. The central rotating-pivot axle to within the two pole heads, bore diameter two and a half degrees, length equal to the rotating ring's diameter—all using the ancient chi with four fen as one degree.
9
玉衡望筒：長四尺五寸八分，廣一寸二分，厚一寸，孔徑六分，古用玉飾之。 玉衡，衡施於軸中，旋運持正，用窺七曜及列星之闊狹，外方內圓，孔徑一度半，周日輪也。
Jade-transversal sighting tube: length four chi five cun eight fen, width one cun two fen, thickness one cun, bore diameter six fen; antiquity used jade to ornament it. The jade transversal, the transversal set in the axle's center, rotating and holding true, used to sight the seven luminaries and the lodges' breadth and narrowness—outer square inner round, bore diameter one and a half degrees, the circuit of the daily wheel.
10
陽經雙環：外一丈七尺三寸，內一丈四尺六寸四分，廣四寸，厚四分，直徑五尺四寸四分，置於子午。 左右用八柱相固，兩面畫周天度數，一面加釘，並銀飾之。 半出地上，半入地下，雙間挾樞軸及玉衡望筒，旋環於中也。
Yang meridian double ring: outer one zhang seven chi three cun, inner one zhang four chi six cun four fen, width four cun, thickness four fen, diameter five chi four cun four fen, set at south and north noon. Left and right eight pillars mutually fixed; on both faces the degrees of heaven's full circuit are painted; on one face nails are added, all silver ornamented. Half emerges above ground, half enters below ground; the double interval clasps the pivot axle and jade-transversal sighting tube, the rotating ring in the center.
11
陰緯單環：外內廣厚周徑，皆准陽經，與陽經相銜各半，內外俱齊。 面平上為天，以下為地，橫周陽環，謂之陰渾也。 面上為兩界，內外為周天百刻。 平上御製銘序及書，並金為字。
Yin latitude single ring: outer and inner width, thickness, and full circuit diameter all follow the yang meridian; with the yang meridian each links half, inner and outer both level. The face above level is heaven; below is earth; horizontally circling the yang ring—this is called the yin armillary. On the face above are two boundaries; inner and outer are the hundred graduations of heaven's full circuit. On the level above are the imperially composed inscription preface and text, all characters in gold.
12
天頂單環：外一丈七尺三寸，豎廣八分，厚三分，直徑五尺四寸四分。 當中國人頂之上，東西當卯酉之中，稍南，使見日出入，令與陽經、陰緯相固，如殼之裹黃。 南去赤道三十六度，去黃道十二度，去北極五十五度，去南北平各九十一度強。
Celestial apex single ring: outer one zhang seven chi three cun, vertical width eight fen, thickness three fen, diameter five chi four cun four fen. At the apex above the Central Land's people, east-west at the center of mao and you, slightly south, causing the Sun's emergence and setting to be seen; causing it to be fixed with the yang meridian and yin latitude, like a shell wrapping the yellow. South from the equator thirty-six degrees, from the ecliptic twelve degrees, from the north pole fifty-five degrees, from north and south level each ninety-one degrees strong.
13
赤道單環：外一丈四尺五寸九分，橫八分，厚三分，直徑四尺九寸。 赤道者，當天之中，二十八宿之列位也。 其本，後魏解蘭所造也。 因著雙規，不能運動。 臣今所造者，上列周天星度，使轉運隨天，仍度穿一穴，隨穴退交，不有差謬。 即知古者秋分，日在角五度，今在軫十三度； 冬至，日在牽牛初，今在斗十度。 擬隨差卻退，故置穴也。 傍在卯酉之南，上去天頂三十六度而橫置之。
Equator single ring: outer one zhang four chi five cun nine fen, horizontal eight fen, thickness three fen, diameter four chi nine cun. The equator is at heaven's center—the arrayed positions of the twenty-eight lodges. Its original was made by Xie Lan of Northern Wei. Because double rings were attached, it could not move. What your subject has now made lists the star degrees of heaven's full circuit above, causing rotation to follow heaven; still a hole is bored through the degree, following the hole retreating at crossing, without error. Thus one knows that in antiquity at the autumn equinox the Sun was at five degrees of Horn; now it is at thirteen degrees of Chariot; at the winter solstice the Sun was at the beginning of Oxherd; now it is at ten degrees of Dipper. Intending to follow the difference retreating backward, therefore the hole is set. Beside it south of mao and you, above going to the celestial apex thirty-six degrees and set horizontally.
14
黃道單環：外一丈五尺四寸一分，橫八分，厚四分，直徑四尺八寸四分。 日之所行，故名黃道。 古人知有其事，竟無其器，遂使太陽陟降，積歲有差。 月及五星，亦隨日度出入，規制不知准的，斟量為率，疏闊尤多。 臣今創置此環，置於赤道環內，仍開合使隨轉運，出入四十八度，而極畫兩方，東西列周天度數，南北列百刻，使見日知時，不有差謬。 上列三百六十策，與用卦相准，度穿一穴，與赤道相交。
Ecliptic single ring: outer one zhang five chi four cun one fen, horizontal eight fen, thickness four fen, diameter four chi eight cun four fen. Where the Sun travels—therefore named the ecliptic. Men of old knew the matter existed but ultimately had no instrument; thus the Sun's ascent and descent accumulated years of discrepancy. The Moon and five planets also follow the Sun's degrees in entering and leaving; regulated form did not know the mark, measuring and setting the rate—crude and broad, especially many. Your subject now newly sets this ring, placed within the equator ring, still opening and closing causing it to follow rotation, entering and leaving forty-eight degrees; at the poles both sides are painted, east-west listing heaven's full-circuit degrees, south-north listing the hundred graduations, causing the Sun to be seen and time known, without error. Above are listed three hundred sixty tally-rods, matching the used hexagrams; a hole is bored through the degree, intersecting with the equator.
15
白道月環：外一丈五尺一寸五分，橫度八分，厚三分，直徑四尺七寸六分。 月行有迂曲遲疾，與日行緩急相反。 古無其器，今創置於黃道環內，使就黃道為交合，出入六十度，以測每夜行度。 上畫周天度數，穿一穴，擬移交會，並用銅鐵為之。
White-path lunar ring: outer one zhang five chi one cun five fen, horizontal width eight fen, thickness three fen, diameter four chi seven cun six fen. The Moon's motion has winding curves, slowness and speed, opposite to the Sun's motion in ease and urgency. Antiquity had no instrument; now newly set within the ecliptic ring, causing it to take the ecliptic as crossing and union, entering and leaving sixty degrees, to measure each night's traveled degrees. Above are painted heaven's full-circuit degrees; a hole is bored, intending to follow the shifting crossing and union—all cast in bronze and iron.
16
李淳風《法象志》說有此日月兩環，在旋儀環上。 既用玉衡，不得遂於玉衡內別安一尺望筒。 運用既難，其器已澀。
Li Chunfeng's Record of Celestial Forms says these Sun and Moon two rings are on the rotating-instrument ring. Since the jade transversal is used, one cannot then separately install within the jade transversal another one-chi sighting tube. Application being difficult, the instrument already stiffened.
17
游儀四柱，龍各高四尺七寸。 水槽、山各高一尺七寸五分。 槽長六尺九寸，高廣各四寸。 水池深一寸，廣一寸五分。 龍者能興雲雨，故以飾柱。 柱在四維，龍下有山雲，俱在水準槽上，並銅為之。
Traveling instrument four pillars—the dragons each four chi seven cun high. Water trough and mountain each one chi seven cun five fen high. Trough length six chi nine cun, height and width each four cun. Water pool depth one cun, width one cun five fen. Dragons can raise cloud and rain—therefore used to ornament the pillars. The pillars at the four corners; below the dragons are mountain and cloud—all on the water-level trough, all cast in bronze.
18
游儀初成，太史所測二十八宿等與《經》同異狀：
When the traveling instrument was first completed, the Astronomy Bureau's measurements of the twenty-eight lodges and others, compared with the Canon, same and different in condition:
19
角二星，十二度； 赤道黃道度與古同。 舊《經》去極九十一度，今則九十三度半。 《星經》云：「角去極九十一度，距星正當赤道，其黃道在赤道南，不經角中。」 今測角在赤道南二度半，黃道復經角中，即與天象符合。
Horn two stars, twelve degrees; equatorial and ecliptic degrees same as antiquity. The old Canon's departure from the pole ninety-one degrees; now ninety-three and a half degrees. The Star Canon says: "Horn's departure from the pole ninety-one degrees; the distance star is directly at the equator; its ecliptic is south of the equator, not passing through Horn's center." Men of old therefore relied on gnomon-triangle technique, saying it had verification in near matters. 夫橫既有之，縱亦宜然。 假令設兩表，南北相距十里，其崇皆數十里，若置火炬于南表之端，而植八尺之木於其下，則當無影。 試從南表之下，仰望北表之端，必將積微分之差，漸與南表參合。 表首參合，則置炬於其上，亦當無影矣。 又置火炬於北表之端，而植八尺之木於其下，則當無影。 試從北表之下，仰望南表之端，又將積微分之差，漸與北表參合。 表首參合，則置炬於其上，亦當無影矣。 復於二表之間，相距各五里，更植八尺之木，仰而望之，則表首環屈而相會。 若置火炬於兩表之端，皆當無影。 夫數十里之高與十里之廣，然則斜射之影與仰望不殊。 今欲求其影差以推遠近高下，猶尚不可知也； 而況稽周天積裏之數於不測之中，又可必乎！ 假令學者因二十里之高以立句股之術，尚不知其所以然，況八尺之木乎！ 原人所以步圭景之意，將欲節宣和氣，輔相物宜，而不在於辰次之周徑； 其所以重歷數之意，將欲敬授人時，欽若乾象，而不在於渾、蓋之是非。 若乃述無稽之談於視聽之所不及，則君子闕疑而不質，仲尼慎言而不論也。 而或者各守所傳之器以述天體，謂渾元可任數而測，大象可運算而窺，終以六家之說，迭為矛盾。 今誠以為蓋天，則南方之度漸狹； 以為渾天，則北方之極浸高。 此二者，又渾、蓋之家未能有以通其說也。 由是而觀，則王仲任、葛稚川之徒，區區于異同之辨，何益人倫之化哉！

Tracing why men step the altar-shadow's intent—they wished to moderate and harmonize qi, assist and complement things' suitability, not in the full circuit's diameter; why they valued calendric numbers—they wished reverently to grant seasons to the people, reverently accord with the qian image, not in whether armillary or canopy is right or wrong. If narrating unverified talk beyond sight and hearing's reach—then the noble man leaves doubt and does not examine; Confucius is careful in speech and does not discuss. But some each guard the transmitted instrument to narrate the celestial body, saying the armillary origin can bear numbers and measure, the great image can be computed and peeped—ultimately the six schools' sayings iteratively contradict.
