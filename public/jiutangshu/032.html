Treatise 12 — Calendars, Part 1.
2
太古聖人，體二氣之權輿，賾三才之物象，乃創紀以窮其數，畫卦以通其變。 而紀有大衍之法，卦有推策之文，由是曆法生焉。 殷人用九疇、五紀之書，《周禮》載馮相、保章之職，所以辨三辰之躔次，察九野之吉凶。 歷代疇人，迭相傳授，蓋推步之成法，協用之舊章。 暨秦氏焚書，遺文殘缺，漢興作者，師法多門。 雖同徵鐘律之文，共演蓍龜之說，而建元或異，積蔀相懸，旁取證於《春秋》，強乩疑於《系》、《象》，靡不揚眉抵掌，謂甘、石未稱日官； 運策播精，言裨、梓不知天道。 及至清台眎祲，黃道考祥，言縮則盈，少中多否，否則矯云差算，中則自負知時。 章、亥不生，憑何質證？
In remote antiquity the sages took as their starting point the birth of yin and yang, plumbed the images of Heaven, Earth, and Man, devised chronology to exhaust every reckoning of number, and drew the hexagrams to compass every transformation. The chronicles held the method of Great Expansion; the hexagrams preserved the texts of casting and counting the stalks. From this, calendrical astronomy took form. The Yin drew on the Books of the Nine Categories and the Five Chronologies. The Rites of Zhou list the duties of the director of prognostication and the registrar of astronomical signs, whose task was to set the march of the sun, moon, and stars and to read fortune and misfortune across the nine realms. Generation after generation, experts in the calendar handed their craft down—the settled methods of astronomical reckoning and the time-honored rules they applied together. After the Qin burned the classics, what survived was broken and incomplete. When the Han arose, every scholar who worked on a calendar followed a different school. They invoked the same writings on pitch-pipes and rehearsed the same lore of milfoil and tortoise divination, yet their epoch beginnings disagreed and their accumulated intercalations failed to match. They hunted corroboration in the Spring and Autumn Annals and twisted the Wings and Images to their purpose. Every one of them leaned forward in debate, insisting that Gan De and Shi Shen had never truly merited the title Masters of the Sun; rattling their rods and spinning abstruse reckonings, they claimed that Bi and Zi knew nothing of Heaven's courses. When observation came to the Clear Terrace and auspice to scrutiny along the ecliptic, they spoke of deficiency when abundance followed. More often wrong than right, on failure they blamed faulty arithmetic; on a lucky hit they preened as masters of the seasons. If the cycle never produces the stems zhang or hai, what proof can you offer?

高齊天保中，六月日當蝕朔，文宣先期問候官蝕何時，張孟賓言蝕申，鄭元偉、董峻言蝕辰，宋景業言蝕巳。 是日蝕于申酉之間，言皆不中時。 景業造《天保曆》則疏密可知矣。 昔鄧平、洛下閎造漢《太初曆》，非之者十七家。 後劉洪、蔡伯喈、何承天、祖沖之，皆數術之精粹者，至於宣考曆書之際，猶為橫議所排。 斯道寂寥，知音蓋寡。 所以張胄玄佩印而沸騰，劉孝孫輿棺而慟哭，俾諸後學，益用為疑。 以臣折衷，無如舊法。
During the Tianbao reign of Northern Qi, a new-moon solar eclipse was expected in the sixth month. Emperor Wenxuan asked the court astronomers beforehand when it would come. Zhang Mengbin predicted the hour shen; Zheng Yuanwei and Dong Jun predicted chen; Song Jingye predicted si. On that day the eclipse fell between the hours shen and you, and not one of them had the time right. From this one can see how loose or tight a calendar Song Jingye's Tianbao system really was. Long ago Deng Ping and Luoxia Hong devised the Han Taichu Calendar, and seventeen rival schools rejected it. Later Liu Hong, Cai Yong, He Chengtian, and Zu Chongzhi were all paragons of mathematical astronomy, yet when the throne reviewed their calendar treatises, reckless critics still tore them apart. The art has grown lonely, and those who truly understand it are scarce. That is why Zhang Zhouxuan took office amid a storm of controversy and Liu Xiaosun marched with a coffin in protest, leaving later students all the more uncertain. In my judgment, nothing serves as well as the established methods.

高祖受隋禪，傅仁均首陳七事，言戊寅歲時正得上元之首，宜定新曆，以符禪代，由是造《戊寅曆》。 祖孝孫、李淳風立理駁之，仁均條答甚詳，故法行於貞觀之世。 高宗時，太史奏舊曆加時浸差，宜有改定，乃詔李淳風造《麟德曆》。 初，隋末劉焯造《皇極曆》，其道不行，淳風約之為法，時稱精密。 天后時，瞿曇羅造《光宅曆》。 中宗時，南宮說造《景龍曆》，皆舊法之所棄者，復取用之。 徒云革易，寧造深微，尋亦不行。 開元中，僧一行精諸家曆法，言《麟德曆》行用既久，晷緯漸差。 宰相張說言之，玄宗召見，令造新曆。 遂與星官梁令瓚先造《黃道遊儀圖》，考校七曜行度，准《周易》大衍之數，別成一法，行用垂五十年。 肅宗時，韓潁造《至德曆》。 代宗時，郭獻之造《五紀曆》。 德宗時，徐承嗣造《正元曆》。 憲宗時，徐昂造《觀象曆》。 其法今存，而元紀蔀章之數，或異前經； 而察斂啟閉之期，何殊舊法。 至論征驗，罕及研精。 綿代流行，示存經法耳。
When Emperor Gaozu took the throne from the Sui, Fu Renjun was first to submit seven proposals, arguing that the year wuyin fell exactly at the head of the Superior Origin and that a new calendar should be established to mark the dynastic transition. The Wuyin Calendar was therefore compiled. Zu Xiaosun and Li Chunfeng advanced reasoned objections, which Renjun answered in meticulous detail. The system therefore remained in force throughout the Zhenguan reign. Under Emperor Gaozong the court astronomer reported that the old calendar's hour corrections were slowly drifting out of alignment and ought to be revised. The throne ordered Li Chunfeng to compile the Linde Calendar. Earlier, at the end of the Sui, Liu Chuo had devised the Huangji Calendar, but his method had never been adopted. Chunfeng distilled it into a workable system that contemporaries hailed as precise. During the reign of Empress Wu, Gautama Siddhartha compiled the Guangzhai Calendar. Under Emperor Zhongzong, Nangong Shuo compiled the Jinglong Calendar. Both were systems the older tradition had already set aside, yet they were revived. They talked only of reform, as though inventing something profound, yet none of these calendars lasted in practice. In the Kaiyuan era the monk Yixing mastered the calendrical methods of every school and reported that the Linde Calendar had been in use so long that sundial shadows and planetary positions were gradually slipping out of alignment. Chief Minister Zhang Yue relayed this to the throne. Emperor Xuanzong summoned Yixing and ordered him to compile a new calendar. Together with the court astronomer Liang Lingzan he first produced the Chart of the Yellow-Road Armillary Sphere and recalculated the courses of the seven luminaries. Following the Great Expansion numbers of the Book of Changes, he devised a new system that remained in use for nearly fifty years. Under Emperor Suzong, Han Ying compiled the Zhide Calendar. Under Emperor Daizong, Guo Xianzhi compiled the Wuji Calendar. Under Emperor Dezong, Xu Chengsi compiled the Zhengyuan Calendar. Under Emperor Xianzong, Xu Ang compiled the Guanxiang Calendar. Their methods survive to this day, yet the figures for origin cycles, obscuration periods, and rule constants sometimes diverge from earlier canonical texts; yet in fixing the seasons of gathering in, opening out, and shutting tight, how do they differ from the old methods? When it comes to empirical verification, few of these revisions achieve real precision. Passed down through the ages, they merely show that the canonical methods are still remembered.

前史取傅仁均、李淳風、南宮說、一行四家歷經，為《曆志》四卷。 近代精數者，皆以淳風、一行之法，曆千古而無差，後人更之，要立異耳，無逾其精密也。 《景龍曆》不經行用，世以為非，今略而不載。 但取《戊寅》、《麟德》、《大衍》三曆法，以備此志，示於疇官爾。
The earlier history drew on the calendar treatises of Fu Renjun, Li Chunfeng, Nangong Shuo, and Yixing from four authors and compiled them into four juan of the Calendar Treatise. Modern experts in calculation all agree that the methods of Chunfeng and Yixing have held true across the ages without measurable error. Later revisions chiefly seek novelty and cannot surpass their precision. The Jinglong Calendar was never adopted in practice and was widely regarded as unsound; it is therefore omitted here. Only the methods of the Wuyin, Linde, and Dayan calendars are included in this treatise, set before the calendrical officers for their use.

行五星法
Method for calculating the motion of the five planets.
19
各置星定見之前夜半日所在宿度算及分，各以定見去朔日算及一分加之。 小分滿法十四分，從行分一。 行分滿法六百七十六分，從度一。 又以星初見去日度數，晨減夕加之。 命度以次，即星初見所在度及分。 自此已後，皆棄此小分也。
For each planet, take the sun's lodge, degree, and parts at midnight before the true appearance, then add the count of days and one part from true appearance back to new moon. When minor parts reach fourteen, carry one into the motion parts. When motion parts reach 676, carry one into the degrees. Then apply the planet's first-appearance distance from the sun in degrees: subtract for morning appearances and add for evening appearances. Count through the degrees in sequence to obtain the lodge degree and parts of the planet's first appearance. From this point onward, discard these minor parts.

求次日術
Procedure for finding the next day.
21
各加一日所行度及分。 其火、金之行而有小分者，各以日率為母。 小分滿其母，去從行分一。 行分滿法，去從度一。 其行有益疾遲者，副置一日行分。 各以其分疾益遲損，乃加之。 留者因前，退則減之，伏不注度。 順行出斗去其分，行入斗先加分。 訖，皆以二十六副行分為度分。
Add the degrees and parts traveled in one day to each. For Mars and Venus, whose motions include minor parts, use the daily rate as the denominator. When minor parts fill their denominator, remove one full denominator and carry one into the motion parts. When motion parts fill the divisor, remove one full divisor and carry one into the degrees. When motion accelerates or decelerates, set aside the one-day motion parts. Apply the increases and decreases for faster and slower motion, then add them in. During a station, continue from the previous value; during retrograde motion, subtract; during occultation, do not record degrees. In direct motion leaving the Dipper asterism, remove its parts; when entering the Dipper, add the parts first. When finished, convert all motion parts using twenty-six as the auxiliary divisor for degree parts.

置有蝕之望定小餘。 若入曆一日，即減二百八十。 入十五日，即加之。 若入十四日，即加五百五十。 入二十八日，即減之。 自入諸日，值盈皆加二百八十，值縮皆減之，為定餘。 乃以十二乘之，以時法六千五百三除之，所得為半辰之數。 命以子半起算外，即所在辰。 初命子半以一算，自後皆以二算為一辰。 不盡為時餘。 若時餘在辰半之前者，乃倍之。 如法無所得，為辰初。 又以三因之，如法得一，名為強。 若得強，若得二強，即名少弱。 若倍之，如法得一，為少。 凡四分一為少，二為半，三為太。 不盡者，又三之，如法得一，名為強。 若得二強者，即名為半弱。 若時餘在辰半之後者亦倍之。 如法無所得，為正在辰半。 以三因之，如法得二，名為強，即名半強。 若得二強，即名太弱。 若倍之，如法得一，為態。 不盡者，又三之，如法得一，為強，即名太強； 若得者，又二強者，為辰末。 亦可前辰名之。 月在沖上蝕，日出後入前各一時半外，不注蝕。
Set aside the fixed minor remainder for the eclipsed full moon. On sequence day one, subtract 280. On day fifteen, add instead. On day fourteen, add 550. On day twenty-eight, subtract instead. On all other days entered, add 280 when in surplus and subtract 280 when in deficit; this gives the fixed remainder. Multiply by twelve and divide by the time factor, 6,503; the quotient is the number of half-double-hours. Count from midnight of zi as one tally beyond zero; the result is the double-hour of occurrence. Count midnight of zi as one tally at the start; thereafter, each double-hour counts as two tallies. The undivided remainder is the time remainder. If the time remainder falls before the half-double-hour, double it. Divide by the factor with no quotient; this marks the beginning of the double-hour. Multiply by three and divide by the factor to obtain one; this is called "strong." One or two "strong" yields the name "less-weak." Double it and divide by the factor to obtain one; this is "less." In general: one quarter is "less," two quarters is "half," and three quarters is "great." For what remains, multiply by three again and divide by the factor to obtain one; this is called "strong." Two "strong" yields the name "half-weak." If the time remainder falls after the half-double-hour, double it as well. Divide by the factor with no quotient; this places the time exactly at the half-double-hour. Multiply by three and divide by the factor to obtain two; this is "strong," named "half-strong." Two "strong" yields the name "great-weak." Double it and divide by the factor to obtain one; this is "great." For what remains, multiply by three again and divide by the factor to obtain one "strong"; this is named "great-strong." If one unit is obtained, or if two "strong," this is the end of the double-hour. It may also be named from the preceding double-hour. When the moon eclipses at opposition, if the eclipse falls more than one and a half double-hours after sunrise or before sunset, do not record it.

推日蝕加時術
Method for determining the hour of a solar eclipse.
51
置有蝕之朔定小餘。 若入曆一日，即減三百。 入十五日，即加之。 若入十四日，即加五百五十。 入二十八日，即減之以為定。 自後不入四時加減之限。 春三月，內道，去交四時已上，入曆，值盈加二百八十，值縮反減之。 夏三月，內道，值盈加二百八十，值縮反減之。 秋三月，內道，去交十一時已下，值盈加二百八十，值縮不加； 十一時已上，值盈加五百五十，值縮不加一百八十。 冬三月，內道，去交五時已下，值盈加二百八十，縮不加。 皆為定餘。 乃以十二乘之，以時法除之，所得半辰之數，命以子半起算外，即所在辰。 命辰如前法。 不盡為時餘，別置為副。 若入仲辰半前，即以副減法，餘為差率。 若在半後，即退其半辰，還以法加餘，即以副為差率。 若入季辰半前，即以法加副，而為差率。 若在半後，即其半辰，還以法加餘，乃倍法以加副，而為差率。 若入孟辰半前，即三因其法，而以副減之，餘為差率。 若半後，即退其半辰，還以法加餘，又以法加副，乃三因其法而以副減之，為差率。 又置去交時數，三已下加三，六已下加二，九已下加一，九已上依數，十二以上從十二，以乘差率。 若在季辰半後，孟辰半前，去交六時以上者，皆從其六，以乘差率。 六時已下，自依數，不須加。 如十四得一，為時差。 子至卯半，午至酉半，以時餘加之； 卯至午半，酉至子半，以減時餘。 加之若滿時法者，乃去之，加於辰，即進之於前也。 減之若不足者，減半辰，加時法，乃減之，即退之於後也。 餘為定時餘。 乃如月蝕法，子午卯酉為仲，辰戌醜未為季，寅申已亥為孟。 日出前後各一時半外，不注日蝕。
Set aside the fixed minor remainder for the eclipsed new moon. On sequence day one, subtract 300. On day fifteen, add instead. On day fourteen, add 550. On day twenty-eight, subtract instead to obtain the fixed value. After this, cases outside the four-seasons add-and-subtract limits stop here. On the inner path in spring, if the node distance is four double-hours or more and the sequence is entered, add 280 when in surplus and subtract when in deficit. On the inner path in summer, add 280 when in surplus and subtract when in deficit. On the inner path in autumn, if the node distance is eleven double-hours or below, add 280 when in surplus and make no addition when in deficit. If the node distance is above eleven double-hours, add 550 when in surplus and do not add 180 when in deficit. On the inner path in winter, if the node distance is five double-hours or below, add 280 when in surplus and make no addition when in deficit. In each case, the result is the fixed remainder. Multiply by twelve and divide by the time factor to obtain the number of half-double-hours; count from midnight of zi as one tally beyond zero to find the double-hour of occurrence. Determine the double-hour by the method above. The undivided remainder is the time remainder; set it aside separately. If the time falls before the half-period of an intermediate double-hour, subtract the aside from the factor; the remainder is the difference-rate. If after the half-period, retreat half a double-hour, add the remainder to the factor, and use the aside as the difference-rate. If the time falls before the half-period of a last double-hour, add the aside to the factor to obtain the difference-rate. If after the half-period, retreat half a double-hour, add the remainder to the factor, double the factor and add the aside—that is the difference-rate. If the time falls before the half-period of a first double-hour, triple the factor and subtract the aside; the remainder is the difference-rate. If after the half-period, retreat half a double-hour, add the remainder to the factor, add the aside to the factor, then triple the factor and subtract the aside—that is the difference-rate. Also set the node-distance in double-hours: add 3 if 3 or below, add 2 if 6 or below, add 1 if 9 or below, use the number as is if above 9, and cap at 12 if 12 or above; then multiply the difference-rate. If the time falls in the last double-hour after the half-period or the first double-hour before it, and the node distance is six double-hours or more, use 6 in every case to multiply the difference-rate. At six double-hours or below, use the number itself without adding. Divide by fourteen to obtain one; this is the time-difference. From zi through the half-period of mao, and from wu through the half-period of you, add to the time remainder; from mao through the half-period of wu, and from you through the half-period of zi, subtract from the time remainder. If addition overflows the time factor, cast it out and carry to the double-hour, advancing to the preceding one. If subtraction underflows, retreat half a double-hour, add the time factor, then subtract—moving back to the following one. The remainder is the fixed time remainder. Then, as in the lunar-eclipse method: zi, wu, mao, and you are intermediate double-hours; chen, xu, chou, and wei are last; yin, shen, si, and hai are first. If the eclipse falls more than one and a half double-hours before or after sunrise, do not record the solar eclipse.

推內道日不蝕術
Method for determining when a solar eclipse does not occur on the inner path.
53
夏五月朔，加時在南方三辰，先交十三時外，六月朔，後交十三時外者，不蝕。 啟蟄畢清明，先交十三時外，值縮，加時在未巳西者，亦不蝕。 入處暑，畢寒露，後交十三時，值盈，加時在己巳東者，亦不蝕。
For a fifth-month new moon in summer, if the hour falls in the southern three double-hours and the prior junction lies thirteen double-hours or more away, or for a sixth-month new moon with the later junction thirteen double-hours or more away, there is no eclipse. From Awakening of Insects through Clear and Bright, if the prior junction is thirteen double-hours or more away, the moon is in deficit phase, and the hour falls in the western half of wei and si, there is also no eclipse. From End of Heat through Cold Dew, if the later junction is thirteen double-hours away, the moon is in surplus phase, and the hour falls in the eastern half of si and ji, there is also no eclipse.
54
推外道日蝕術
Method for determining solar eclipse on the outer path.
55
不問交之先後，但去交一時內者，皆蝕也。 若先交二時內者，值盈二時外者，亦蝕。 若後交二時內，值縮二時外者，亦蝕。 其夏去交二時在南方三辰者，亦蝕。 若去分至十二時內，去交六時內者，亦蝕。 若去交春分三日內，後交二時內者，亦蝕。 秋分三日內，先交二時內者，亦蝕。 諸去交三時內，星伏如前者，亦蝕。
Regardless of whether the junction comes before or after, any case within one double-hour of the junction is an eclipse. If within two double-hours of the prior junction and the surplus phase extends beyond two double-hours, there is also an eclipse. If within two double-hours of the later junction and the deficit phase extends beyond two double-hours, there is also an eclipse. In summer, if the moon lies two double-hours from the node and falls among the three chen sectors in the south, an eclipse will also occur. If the moon is within twelve double-hours of an equinox or solstice and within six double-hours of the node, an eclipse will also occur. If the moon is within three days of the node at the spring equinox and within two double-hours after the crossing, an eclipse will also occur. If the moon is within three days of the node at the autumn equinox and within two double-hours before the crossing, an eclipse will also occur. Whenever the moon is within three double-hours of the node and the stars are occulted as described above, an eclipse will also occur.

推月蝕分術
Procedure for computing the magnitude of a lunar eclipse.
57
置去交分。 其在冬，先後交皆去不蝕分二時之數。 若在於春，先交去半時，後交去二時。 夏即依定。 若在於秋，先交去二時，後交去半時。 若不足去者，蝕既，乃以三萬六千一百八十三為法除之，所得為不蝕分。 不盡者，半法已上為半強，已下為半弱，而以減十五，餘為蝕之大分。
Take the parts measuring distance from the node. In winter, subtract from both the preceding and following nodes the non-eclipse allowance for two double-hours. In spring, subtract half a double-hour before the node and two double-hours after it. In summer, apply the fixed standard without adjustment. In autumn, subtract two double-hours before the node and half a double-hour after it. If the subtraction cannot be completed, the eclipse is total; otherwise divide by 36,183, and the quotient gives the non-eclipse parts. For any remainder, count half the divisor or more as half-strong and anything less as half-weak; subtract that from fifteen, and what remains is the eclipse magnitude in great parts.

推月蝕所起術
Procedure for determining the point at which a lunar eclipse begins.
59
若在外道，初起東北，蝕甚西北。 若在內道，初起東南，蝕甚西南。 十三分已上，正東起。 推皆據正南而言。
On the outer path, the eclipse first appears in the northeast and reaches its greatest extent in the northwest. On the inner path, the eclipse first appears in the southeast and reaches its greatest extent in the southwest. When the magnitude is thirteen parts or greater, the eclipse begins due east. All directions in these calculations are given with due south as the reference point.

推日蝕分術
Procedure for computing the magnitude of a solar eclipse.
61
置去交分。 若入冬至已後，畢于立春，皆均減十二萬八百，餘為不蝕分。 不足減者，反以交分減之，餘為不蝕分。 亦減望差為定法。 其後交值縮者，直以望差為定法，不須減之。 自入啟蟄，初日減二十二萬八百分，後日減一千八百一十分，置初日所減之分，計後日減之數以減之，餘以減交分。 畢於芒種。 自入夏至，日減二千四百分，畢于白露。 自入秋分，畢于大雪，皆均減二十二萬八百分。 但不足減者，皆如前，反以交分減之，訖，皆為不蝕。 若入冬至，畢於小寒，不蝕分依定。 若入大寒，畢于立夏，後去交五時外，皆去不蝕分一時。 時差值減者，先交減之，後交加之。 不足減者，蝕既。 時差值加者，先交加之，後交減之。 不足減者，蝕既。 乃為定分，以十五乘之，以定法除之，所得為不蝕分。 不盡者，半法已上為半強，已下為半弱，而以減十五，餘為蝕之大分也。
Take the parts measuring distance from the node. From the winter solstice through the spring commencement, subtract 120,800 uniformly; the remainder is the non-eclipse parts. If the subtraction cannot be completed, subtract instead from the node parts; the remainder is the non-eclipse parts. Also subtract the full-moon difference to obtain the fixed divisor. When the later node falls in a contracting interval, use the full-moon difference directly as the fixed divisor without further subtraction. From Awakening of Insects onward, subtract 228,000 parts on the first day and 1,810 parts for each day thereafter; take the first day's subtraction, apply the cumulative daily reductions, and subtract the result from the node parts. Continue this procedure through Grain in Ear. From the summer solstice through White Dew, subtract 2,400 parts each day. From the autumn equinox through Great Snow, subtract 228,000 parts uniformly. Whenever a subtraction cannot be completed, reverse the operation and subtract from the node parts as before; when the process is finished, the result is the non-eclipse parts. From the winter solstice through Lesser Cold, apply the non-eclipse parts according to the fixed standard. From Greater Cold through the Beginning of Summer, when the moon is more than five double-hours past the node, subtract one double-hour from the non-eclipse parts in each case. When the time-difference value requires subtraction, subtract it from the preceding node first and add it to the following node. If the subtraction cannot be completed, the eclipse is total. When the time-difference value requires addition, add it to the preceding node first and subtract it from the following node. If the subtraction cannot be completed, the eclipse is total. Then multiply the fixed parts by fifteen and divide by the fixed divisor; the quotient gives the non-eclipse parts. For any remainder, count half the divisor or more as half-strong and anything less as half-weak; subtract that from fifteen, and what remains is the eclipse magnitude in great parts.

On the outer path, the eclipse first appears in the southwest and reaches its greatest extent in the southeast. On the inner path, the eclipse first appears in the northwest and reaches its greatest extent in the northeast. When the magnitude is thirteen degrees or greater, the eclipse begins due west. These directions are likewise given with due south as the reference point.

求日出入所在術
Procedure for determining the times of sunrise and sunset.
65
以所入氣辰刻及分，與後氣辰刻及分相減。 餘乘入氣日算，以十五除之。 所得以加減所入氣為定日出人。 從冬至至夏至，日出減之，日入加之。 從夏至至冬至，日出加之，日入減之。 入餘為定刻及分。
Subtract the chen, ke, and parts of the current qi from those of the next qi. Multiply the remainder by the number of days elapsed within the qi and divide by fifteen. Add or subtract the result to or from the entered qi to obtain the corrected times of sunrise and sunset. From the winter solstice to the summer solstice, subtract the correction for sunrise and add it for sunset. From the summer solstice to the winter solstice, add the correction for sunrise and subtract it for sunset. The result gives the corrected ke and fractional parts.

求虧初復滿術
Procedure for determining first contact and full restoration.
80
置蝕分，用刻率副之，以乘所入曆損益率，四千五十七而一。 值盈反其損益，值縮依其損諡，副為蝕定用刻數，乃六乘之，十而一，以減蝕加時辰刻，為虧初。 丈四乘餘之用刻數，十而一，以加蝕加時辰刻，為復滿。
Take the eclipse parts, set the ke rate alongside them, multiply by the increase-decrease rate of the entered calendar, and divide by 4,057. If the value is in an expanding interval, reverse its increase-decrease; if contracting, apply it directly; set this aside as the fixed ke count for the eclipse, multiply by six and take one-tenth, then subtract from the chen ke of the eclipse hour to obtain first contact. Multiply the remaining ke count by fourteen, take one-tenth, and add to the chen ke of the eclipse hour to obtain full restoration.

求所蝕夜初甚末更籌刻術
Procedure for determining the watch-ke of the eclipse's beginning, maximum, and end during the night.
82
因其日日所入辰殘刻及分，依次加辰刻及分，至蝕初辰刻及分，減二刻十二分，從其更用刻及分除之，不滿更，即初蝕更籌。 依所求得至甚刻加之，命即甚。 依求得甚後刻數加之，命即末更籌刻及分。 日出前復滿，日入後初虧，皆不注蝕。
Starting from the residual chen ke and parts entered on that day, add chen ke and parts in sequence until reaching the chen ke and parts of first contact; subtract two ke and twelve parts, divide by the ke usage per watch, and if the quotient does not fill a complete watch, that gives the watch-ke of the eclipse's beginning. Add the amount obtained up to the ke of maximum eclipse; assign the watch, and that is the moment of greatest eclipse. Add the ke count obtained after maximum eclipse; assign the watch, and that gives the watch-ke and parts at the eclipse's end. If full restoration occurs before sunrise or first contact occurs after sunset, do not enter the eclipse in the calendar.
