志第十二曆一
Treatise XII: The Calendar, Part One
2
太古聖人，體二氣之權輿，賾三才之物象，乃創紀以窮其數，畫卦以通其變。 而紀有大衍之法，卦有推策之文，由是曆法生焉。 殷人用九疇、五紀之書，《周禮》載馮相、保章之職，所以辨三辰之躔次，察九野之吉凶。 歷代疇人，迭相傳授，蓋推步之成法，協用之舊章。 暨秦氏焚書，遺文殘缺，漢興作者，師法多門。 雖同徵鐘律之文，共演蓍龜之說，而建元或異，積蔀相懸，旁取證於《春秋》，強乩疑於《系》、《象》，靡不揚眉抵掌，謂甘、石未稱日官； 運策播精，言裨、梓不知天道。 及至清台眎祲，黃道考祥，言縮則盈，少中多否，否則矯云差算，中則自負知時。 章、亥不生，憑何質證？
In deepest antiquity, the sage embodied the dawn of yin and yang and disclosed the forms of Heaven, Earth, and Man; he established eras to fully reckon number and drew hexagrams to track transformation. The era-records held the Great Expansion method, and the hexagrams held the texts for divining with stalks; from these emerged calendrical science. The Yin dynasty relied on the books of the Nine Categories and Five Registers, and the Rites of Zhou set down the posts of the royal astronomers Feng Xiang and Bao Zhang, whose duty was to chart the motions of the sun, moon, and stars and to read good and ill fortune across the nine domains. Down the generations, calendrical experts handed the art from master to pupil; what they preserved were the settled rules of astronomical calculation and the long-standing procedures used together in practice. After the Qin burned the classics, what survived was fragmentary; when the Han rose, every new author followed a different school. They all cited the same writings on pitch pipes and musical measures and rehearsed the same lore of yarrow and tortoise divination, yet their era beginnings differed and their intercalation cycles clashed; they hunted corroboration in the Spring and Autumn Annals and twisted the Attached and Image commentaries into oracular proofs, and every one of them, brows lifted and palms slapping, declared that Gan De and Shi Shen were no true officers of the sun; Shaking the counting rods and scattering their refined calculations, they claimed that Pi and Zi understood nothing of Heaven's Way. Yet when they mounted the observatory to read celestial signs and tested fortune along the ecliptic, what they called contraction turned out to be expansion; they were wrong far more often than right, and when wrong they blamed faulty arithmetic, while when right they congratulated themselves on mastering the seasons. If the cyclical stems Zhang and Hai never come to be, what evidence can possibly verify the claim?

高齊天保中，六月日當蝕朔，文宣先期問候官蝕何時，張孟賓言蝕申，鄭元偉、董峻言蝕辰，宋景業言蝕巳。 是日蝕于申酉之間，言皆不中時。 景業造《天保曆》則疏密可知矣。 昔鄧平、洛下閎造漢《太初曆》，非之者十七家。 後劉洪、蔡伯喈、何承天、祖沖之，皆數術之精粹者，至於宣考曆書之際，猶為橫議所排。 斯道寂寥，知音蓋寡。 所以張胄玄佩印而沸騰，劉孝孫輿棺而慟哭，俾諸後學，益用為疑。 以臣折衷，無如舊法。
During the Tianbao reign of Northern Qi, a solar eclipse at new moon was expected in the sixth month. Emperor Wenxuan asked the court astronomer in advance when it would come: Zhang Mengbin said at shen, Zheng Yuanwei and Dong Jun said at chen, and Song Jingye said at si. On that day the eclipse fell between the shen and you hours, and not one prediction matched the moment. Once Jingye compiled the Tianbao Calendar, its looseness and tightness became plain for all to see. Long ago Deng Ping and Luoxia Hong devised the Han Taichu Calendar, and seventeen rival schools rejected it. Later Liu Hong, Cai Yong, He Chengtian, and Zu Chongzhi were all masters of mathematical astronomy, yet even when their calendars were formally reviewed, hostile controversy still pushed them aside. The discipline stands in lonely neglect, and those who truly understand it are scarce. That is why Zhang Zaixuan, seal on his belt, raged in public protest, and Liu Xiaosun rode in a coffin-cart wailing in grief—leaving later students more uncertain than ever. In my judgment, nothing serves as well as the established method.
4
高祖受隋禪，傅仁均首陳七事，言戊寅歲時正得上元之首，宜定新曆，以符禪代，由是造《戊寅曆》。 祖孝孫、李淳風立理駁之，仁均條答甚詳，故法行於貞觀之世。 高宗時，太史奏舊曆加時浸差，宜有改定，乃詔李淳風造《麟德曆》。 初，隋末劉焯造《皇極曆》，其道不行，淳風約之為法，時稱精密。 天后時，瞿曇羅造《光宅曆》。 中宗時，南宮說造《景龍曆》，皆舊法之所棄者，復取用之。 徒云革易，寧造深微，尋亦不行。 開元中，僧一行精諸家曆法，言《麟德曆》行用既久，晷緯漸差。 宰相張說言之，玄宗召見，令造新曆。 遂與星官梁令瓚先造《黃道遊儀圖》，考校七曜行度，准《周易》大衍之數，別成一法，行用垂五十年。 肅宗時，韓潁造《至德曆》。 代宗時，郭獻之造《五紀曆》。 德宗時，徐承嗣造《正元曆》。 憲宗時，徐昂造《觀象曆》。 其法今存，而元紀蔀章之數，或異前經； 而察斂啟閉之期，何殊舊法。 至論征驗，罕及研精。 綿代流行，示存經法耳。
When Emperor Gaozu accepted the Sui abdication, Fu Renjun was the first to present seven proposals, arguing that the wuyin year aligned precisely with the head of the upper origin and that a new calendar should be instituted to mark the transfer of the mandate; thus the Wuyin Calendar was created. Zu Xiaosun and Li Chunfeng advanced theoretical objections, but Renjun rebutted each point at length, and his system remained in force throughout the Zhenguan reign. Under Emperor Gaozong, the Grand Astrologer reported that the old calendar's time corrections were slowly drifting and ought to be revised, and the court ordered Li Chunfeng to compile the Linde Calendar. Earlier, at the end of the Sui, Liu Zhuo had devised the Huangji Calendar, but his system had never been adopted; Li Chunfeng distilled it into a workable method that his contemporaries hailed as precise. Under Empress Wu, the monk Gautama Siddhartha compiled the Guangzhe Calendar. Under Emperor Zhongzong, Nangong Shuo compiled the Jinglong Calendar; both were systems the established method had already rejected, yet the court took them up again. They promised reform but produced nothing truly profound, and in the end neither system was kept in use. During the Kaiyuan reign, the monk Yixing mastered every school's calendrical methods and reported that the Linde Calendar, long in service, was slowly drifting out of alignment with the gnomon and the stars. Chancellor Zhang Yue relayed the report, and Emperor Xuanzong summoned Yixing and ordered him to devise a new calendar. Together with the court astronomer Liang Lingzan he first built the Armillary Chart of the Yellow Path, recalibrated the motions of the seven luminaries, and, following the Great Expansion numbers of the Book of Changes, fashioned a new system that remained in use for nearly fifty years. Under Emperor Suzong, Han Ying compiled the Zhide Calendar. Under Emperor Daizong, Guo Xianzhi compiled the Wuji Calendar. Under Emperor Dezong, Xu Chengsi compiled the Zhenyuan Calendar. Under Emperor Xianzong, Xu Ang compiled the Guanxiang Calendar. Their methods are still extant, though the figures for era-cycles, obscuration periods, and intercalation rules sometimes diverge from earlier authorities; yet in fixing the seasons of solstice, equinox, and the four qi nodes, they differed little from the old method. When it came to empirical testing, they rarely pursued true refinement. Handed down age after age, they merely attest that the canonical method was kept alive.

前史取傅仁均、李淳風、南宮說、一行四家歷經，為《曆志》四卷。 近代精數者，皆以淳風、一行之法，曆千古而無差，後人更之，要立異耳，無逾其精密也。 《景龍曆》不經行用，世以為非，今略而不載。 但取《戊寅》、《麟德》、《大衍》三曆法，以備此志，示於疇官爾。
Earlier histories drew on the calendrical treatises of Fu Renjun, Li Chunfeng, Nangong Shuo, and Yixing and compiled four fascicles for the Calendar Treatise. Modern experts in calculation agree that the methods of Li Chunfeng and Yixing have stood without error for ages; later reformers mainly sought novelty and never surpassed their precision. The Jinglong Calendar was never adopted in practice and was widely judged unsound; it is therefore omitted here. This treatise includes only the methods of the Wuyin, Linde, and Dayan calendars, set down here for the use of court astronomers.

行五星法
Rules for the Motion of the Five Planets
19
各置星定見之前夜半日所在宿度算及分，各以定見去朔日算及一分加之。 小分滿法十四分，從行分一。 行分滿法六百七十六分，從度一。 又以星初見去日度數，晨減夕加之。 命度以次，即星初見所在度及分。 自此已後，皆棄此小分也。
For each planet, take the sun's lodge-degree position at midnight before the true appearance, then add the interval in days and parts from true appearance back to new moon. When minor parts fill fourteen, carry one to the motion parts. When motion parts fill 676, carry one degree. Then apply the planet's first-appearance solar distance: subtract for morning appearances, add for evening appearances. Count through the lodges in order to obtain the planet's first-appearance degree and parts. From this point onward, discard these minor parts.

求次日術
Procedure for Finding the Next Day
21
各加一日所行度及分。 其火、金之行而有小分者，各以日率為母。 小分滿其母，去從行分一。 行分滿法，去從度一。 其行有益疾遲者，副置一日行分。 各以其分疾益遲損，乃加之。 留者因前，退則減之，伏不注度。 順行出斗去其分，行入斗先加分。 訖，皆以二十六副行分為度分。
Add each planet's daily motion in degrees and parts. For Mars and Venus, which carry minor parts in their motion, use the daily rate as the denominator. When minor parts fill their denominator, remove a full unit and carry one to the motion parts. When motion parts fill the divisor, remove a full unit and carry one degree. For phases of accelerated or slowed motion, set aside the one-day motion parts. Apply the acceleration or retardation parts accordingly, then add them in. During station, hold the previous position; during retrogradation, subtract; during occultation, record no degrees. In direct motion leaving the Dipper asterism, subtract its correction parts; entering the Dipper, add the parts first. When this is done, convert all values using twenty-six auxiliary motion parts per degree.

置有蝕之望定小餘。 若入曆一日，即減二百八十。 入十五日，即加之。 若入十四日，即加五百五十。 入二十八日，即減之。 自入諸日，值盈皆加二百八十，值縮皆減之，為定餘。 乃以十二乘之，以時法六千五百三除之，所得為半辰之數。 命以子半起算外，即所在辰。 初命子半以一算，自後皆以二算為一辰。 不盡為時餘。 若時餘在辰半之前者，乃倍之。 如法無所得，為辰初。 又以三因之，如法得一，名為強。 若得強，若得二強，即名少弱。 若倍之，如法得一，為少。 凡四分一為少，二為半，三為太。 不盡者，又三之，如法得一，名為強。 若得二強者，即名為半弱。 若時餘在辰半之後者亦倍之。 如法無所得，為正在辰半。 以三因之，如法得二，名為強，即名半強。 若得二強，即名太弱。 若倍之，如法得一，為態。 不盡者，又三之，如法得一，為強，即名太強； 若得者，又二強者，為辰末。 亦可前辰名之。 月在沖上蝕，日出後入前各一時半外，不注蝕。
Set aside the fixed minor remainder for the eclipsed full moon. On ephemeris day 1, subtract 280. On day 15, add instead. On day 14, add 550. On day 28, subtract instead. On all other days, add 280 when in excess and subtract when in deficit; this is the fixed remainder. Multiply by 12 and divide by the time divisor, 6,503; the quotient is the number of half-branches. Count from midnight of zi; the branch reached is the one where the eclipse falls. Count midnight of zi as one unit at the start; thereafter each double-hour branch counts as two units. The remainder is the time remainder. If the time remainder falls before the half of the branch, double it. If division by the divisor yields zero, the time is the start of the branch. Multiply by three; if one full divisor is obtained, name it strong. If one or two strong units are obtained, name the time minor-weak. If doubling yields one full divisor, the quarter-mark is minor. In general, one quarter is minor, two quarters is half, and three quarters is great. If a remainder remains, multiply by three again; if one full divisor is obtained, name it strong. If two strong units are obtained, name the time half-weak. If the time remainder falls after the half of the branch, double it as well. If division yields zero, the time is exactly at the half of the branch. Multiply by three; if two full divisors are obtained, name it strong—that is, half-strong. If two strong units are obtained, name the time great-weak. If doubling yields one full divisor, the quarter-mark is great. If a remainder remains, multiply by three again; if one full divisor is obtained, name it strong—that is, great-strong; If two strong units are obtained, the time is the end of the branch. It may also be named from the preceding branch. When the moon eclipses at opposition, if the eclipse falls more than one and a half double-hours after sunrise or before sunset, do not record it.

推日蝕加時術
Procedure for Calculating the Hour of Solar Eclipse
51
置有蝕之朔定小餘。 若入曆一日，即減三百。 入十五日，即加之。 若入十四日，即加五百五十。 入二十八日，即減之以為定。 自後不入四時加減之限。 春三月，內道，去交四時已上，入曆，值盈加二百八十，值縮反減之。 夏三月，內道，值盈加二百八十，值縮反減之。 秋三月，內道，去交十一時已下，值盈加二百八十，值縮不加； 十一時已上，值盈加五百五十，值縮不加一百八十。 冬三月，內道，去交五時已下，值盈加二百八十，縮不加。 皆為定餘。 乃以十二乘之，以時法除之，所得半辰之數，命以子半起算外，即所在辰。 命辰如前法。 不盡為時餘，別置為副。 若入仲辰半前，即以副減法，餘為差率。 若在半後，即退其半辰，還以法加餘，即以副為差率。 若入季辰半前，即以法加副，而為差率。 若在半後，即其半辰，還以法加餘，乃倍法以加副，而為差率。 若入孟辰半前，即三因其法，而以副減之，餘為差率。 若半後，即退其半辰，還以法加餘，又以法加副，乃三因其法而以副減之，為差率。 又置去交時數，三已下加三，六已下加二，九已下加一，九已上依數，十二以上從十二，以乘差率。 若在季辰半後，孟辰半前，去交六時以上者，皆從其六，以乘差率。 六時已下，自依數，不須加。 如十四得一，為時差。 子至卯半，午至酉半，以時餘加之； 卯至午半，酉至子半，以減時餘。 加之若滿時法者，乃去之，加於辰，即進之於前也。 減之若不足者，減半辰，加時法，乃減之，即退之於後也。 餘為定時餘。 乃如月蝕法，子午卯酉為仲，辰戌醜未為季，寅申已亥為孟。 日出前後各一時半外，不注日蝕。
Set aside the fixed minor remainder for the eclipsed new moon. On ephemeris day 1, subtract 300. On day 15, add instead. On day 14, add 550. On day 28, subtract instead to obtain the fixed value. After this, do not apply the four-seasons add-subtract limits. In spring, on the inner path, if the node distance is 4 double-hours or more and the ephemeris is entered, add 280 when in excess and subtract when in deficit. In summer, on the inner path, add 280 when in excess and subtract when in deficit. In autumn, on the inner path, if the node distance is 11 double-hours or below, add 280 when in excess and add nothing when in deficit; If the node distance is above 11 double-hours, add 550 when in excess and do not add 180 when in deficit. In winter, on the inner path, if the node distance is 5 double-hours or below, add 280 when in excess and add nothing when in deficit. In all cases this is the fixed remainder. Multiply by 12 and divide by the time divisor; count the half-branches from midnight of zi to find the branch where the eclipse falls. Determine the branch by the method above. The remainder is the time remainder; set it aside as the auxiliary value. If the time falls before the half of an intermediate branch, subtract the auxiliary from the divisor; the remainder is the difference-rate. If after the half-period, retreat half a branch, add the remainder back to the divisor, and use the auxiliary as the difference-rate. If the time falls before the half of a terminal branch, add the auxiliary to the divisor to obtain the difference-rate. If after the half-period, take the half-branch, add the remainder back to the divisor, double the divisor and add the auxiliary—that is the difference-rate. If the time falls before the half of an initial branch, triple the divisor and subtract the auxiliary; the remainder is the difference-rate. If after the half-period, retreat half a branch, add the remainder to the divisor, add the divisor to the auxiliary, then triple the divisor and subtract the auxiliary to obtain the difference-rate. Also set the node distance in double-hours: if 3 or below add 3; if 6 or below add 2; if 9 or below add 1; if above 9 use the number as is; if 12 or above cap at 12; then multiply by the difference-rate. If the time falls after the half of a terminal branch and before the half of an initial branch, and the node distance is 6 double-hours or more, use 6 in all cases to multiply the difference-rate. At 6 double-hours or below, use the number as is without adding. Divide by 14 to obtain the time-difference. From zi through the half of mao, and from wu through the half of you, add the time-difference to the time remainder; From mao through the half of wu, and from you through the half of zi, subtract the time-difference from the time remainder. If addition fills the time divisor, discard the divisor and advance one branch forward. If subtraction underflows, retreat half a branch, add the time divisor, then subtract—moving one branch backward. The remainder is the fixed time remainder. Then, as in the lunar eclipse method: zi, wu, mao, and you are intermediate branches; chen, xu, chou, and wei are terminal branches; yin, shen, si, and hai are initial branches. If the eclipse falls more than one and a half double-hours before or after sunrise, do not record the solar eclipse.

推內道日不蝕術
Procedure for Determining No Solar Eclipse on the Inner Path
53
夏五月朔，加時在南方三辰，先交十三時外，六月朔，後交十三時外者，不蝕。 啟蟄畢清明，先交十三時外，值縮，加時在未巳西者，亦不蝕。 入處暑，畢寒露，後交十三時，值盈，加時在己巳東者，亦不蝕。
On a fifth-month new moon in summer with eclipse time in the three southern branches and prior node beyond 13 double-hours, or on a sixth-month new moon with subsequent node beyond 13 double-hours, there is no eclipse. From Awakening of Insects through Clear Brightness, with prior node beyond 13 double-hours, moon in deficit, and eclipse time in wei, si, or the west, there is also no eclipse. From End of Heat through Cold Dew, with subsequent node at 13 double-hours, moon in excess, and eclipse time in si or the east, there is also no eclipse.
54
推外道日蝕術
Procedure for Determining Solar Eclipse on the Outer Path
55
不問交之先後，但去交一時內者，皆蝕也。 若先交二時內者，值盈二時外者，亦蝕。 若後交二時內，值縮二時外者，亦蝕。 其夏去交二時在南方三辰者，亦蝕。 若去分至十二時內，去交六時內者，亦蝕。 若去交春分三日內，後交二時內者，亦蝕。 秋分三日內，先交二時內者，亦蝕。 諸去交三時內，星伏如前者，亦蝕。
Regardless of whether the node comes before or after, if the distance from the node is within 1 double-hour, there is an eclipse. If the prior node is within 2 double-hours and the moon is in excess beyond the second double-hour, there is also an eclipse. If the subsequent node is within 2 double-hours and the moon is in deficit beyond the second double-hour, there is also an eclipse. In summer, if the node stands two double-hours from conjunction within the three southern double-hours, an eclipse also occurs. If the moment falls within twelve double-hours of an equinox or solstice and within six double-hours of conjunction, an eclipse also occurs. If within three days of the spring equinox from conjunction and within two double-hours after conjunction, an eclipse also occurs. Within three days of the autumn equinox and within two double-hours before conjunction, an eclipse also occurs. Whenever conjunction falls within three double-hours and the planets are occulted as described above, an eclipse also occurs.

推月蝕分術
Procedure for Calculating the Magnitude of Lunar Eclipse
57
置去交分。 其在冬，先後交皆去不蝕分二時之數。 若在於春，先交去半時，後交去二時。 夏即依定。 若在於秋，先交去二時，後交去半時。 若不足去者，蝕既，乃以三萬六千一百八十三為法除之，所得為不蝕分。 不盡者，半法已上為半強，已下為半弱，而以減十五，餘為蝕之大分。
Enter the parts from conjunction. In winter, subtract the two-double-hour non-eclipse amount for both before and after conjunction. In spring, subtract half a double-hour before conjunction and two double-hours after conjunction. In summer, use the fixed values. In autumn, subtract two double-hours before conjunction and half a double-hour after conjunction. If the subtraction cannot be completed, the eclipse is total; otherwise divide by 36,183 to obtain the non-eclipse parts. For the remainder, if it is half the divisor or more, count it as half-strong; if less, half-weak; subtract from fifteen, and the remainder is the eclipse magnitude in great parts.

推月蝕所起術
Procedure for Determining the Direction of Lunar Eclipse
59
若在外道，初起東北，蝕甚西北。 若在內道，初起東南，蝕甚西南。 十三分已上，正東起。 推皆據正南而言。
On the outer path, the shadow first appears in the northeast and reaches maximum in the northwest. On the inner path, the shadow first appears in the southeast and reaches maximum in the southwest. If the magnitude is thirteen parts or more, it begins due east. All directions are reckoned from due south.

推日蝕分術
Procedure for Calculating the Magnitude of Solar Eclipse
61
置去交分。 若入冬至已後，畢于立春，皆均減十二萬八百，餘為不蝕分。 不足減者，反以交分減之，餘為不蝕分。 亦減望差為定法。 其後交值縮者，直以望差為定法，不須減之。 自入啟蟄，初日減二十二萬八百分，後日減一千八百一十分，置初日所減之分，計後日減之數以減之，餘以減交分。 畢於芒種。 自入夏至，日減二千四百分，畢于白露。 自入秋分，畢于大雪，皆均減二十二萬八百分。 但不足減者，皆如前，反以交分減之，訖，皆為不蝕。 若入冬至，畢於小寒，不蝕分依定。 若入大寒，畢于立夏，後去交五時外，皆去不蝕分一時。 時差值減者，先交減之，後交加之。 不足減者，蝕既。 時差值加者，先交加之，後交減之。 不足減者，蝕既。 乃為定分，以十五乘之，以定法除之，所得為不蝕分。 不盡者，半法已上為半強，已下為半弱，而以減十五，餘為蝕之大分也。
Enter the parts from conjunction. From after the Winter Solstice through Start of Spring, reduce evenly by 120,800; the remainder is the non-eclipse parts. If the subtraction cannot be completed, subtract in reverse from the conjunction parts; the remainder is the non-eclipse parts. Also subtract the full-moon difference to obtain the fixed divisor. If after conjunction the ephemeris shows deficit, use the full-moon difference directly as the fixed divisor without further subtraction. From Awakening of Insects, subtract 228,000 parts on the first day and 1,810 parts on each following day; record the first day's subtraction, apply the cumulative daily reductions, and subtract the remainder from the conjunction parts. Continue until Grain in Ear. From the Summer Solstice, subtract 2,400 parts per day until White Dew. From the Autumn Equinox through Major Snow, reduce evenly by 228,000 parts. If subtraction cannot be completed, reverse the procedure as before and subtract from the conjunction parts; when finished, all yield non-eclipse parts. From the Winter Solstice through Minor Cold, use the fixed non-eclipse parts. From Major Cold through Start of Summer, if after conjunction is more than five double-hours away, subtract one double-hour from the non-eclipse parts. If the time-difference is subtractive, subtract it before conjunction and add it after conjunction. If the subtraction cannot be completed, the eclipse is total. If the time-difference is additive, add it before conjunction and subtract it after conjunction. If the subtraction cannot be completed, the eclipse is total. Take the fixed parts, multiply by fifteen, divide by the fixed divisor, and obtain the non-eclipse parts. For the remainder, if it is half the divisor or more, count it as half-strong; if less, half-weak; subtract from fifteen, and the remainder is the eclipse magnitude in great parts.

若在外道，初起西南，蝕甚東南。 若在內道，初起西北，蝕甚東北。 十三度已上，正西起。 亦據正南而言之。
On the outer path, the eclipse first appears in the southwest and reaches maximum in the southeast. On the inner path, the eclipse first appears in the northwest and reaches maximum in the northeast. If the magnitude is thirteen degrees or more, it begins due west. These directions too are reckoned from due south.

Procedure for Finding Sunrise and Sunset Times
65
以所入氣辰刻及分，與後氣辰刻及分相減。 餘乘入氣日算，以十五除之。 所得以加減所入氣為定日出人。 從冬至至夏至，日出減之，日入加之。 從夏至至冬至，日出加之，日入減之。 入餘為定刻及分。
Subtract the entered qi period's double-hour marks and parts from those of the following qi period. Multiply the remainder by the day count within the qi period and divide by fifteen. Add or subtract the result from the entered qi values to obtain the fixed sunrise and sunset times. From the Winter Solstice to the Summer Solstice, subtract for sunrise and add for sunset. From the Summer Solstice to the Winter Solstice, add for sunrise and subtract for sunset. The resulting remainder gives the fixed marks and parts.

Procedure for Calculating the Fixed Marks of Solar and Lunar Eclipse Hours

求虧初復滿術
Procedure for Finding First Contact, Totality, and Restoration
80
置蝕分，用刻率副之，以乘所入曆損益率，四千五十七而一。 值盈反其損益，值縮依其損諡，副為蝕定用刻數，乃六乘之，十而一，以減蝕加時辰刻，為虧初。 丈四乘餘之用刻數，十而一，以加蝕加時辰刻，為復滿。
Enter the eclipse fraction, set aside the mark-rate, multiply by the ephemeris increase-decrease rate for the day entered, and divide by 4,057. If the ephemeris shows excess, reverse the increase-decrease; if deficit, follow it; set this aside as the fixed mark-count for the eclipse, multiply by six, divide by ten, subtract from the eclipse hour mark, and obtain first contact. Multiply the remaining mark-count by four, divide by ten, add to the eclipse hour mark, and obtain restoration and fullness.

求所蝕夜初甚末更籌刻術
Procedure for Finding the Night-Watch Tallies at Eclipse Beginning, Maximum, and End
82
因其日日所入辰殘刻及分，依次加辰刻及分，至蝕初辰刻及分，減二刻十二分，從其更用刻及分除之，不滿更，即初蝕更籌。 依所求得至甚刻加之，命即甚。 依求得甚後刻數加之，命即末更籌刻及分。 日出前復滿，日入後初虧，皆不注蝕。
From that day's entered double-hour remainder marks and parts, add marks and parts in sequence until first contact; subtract two marks and twelve parts, divide by the watch-period marks in use—if it does not fill a full watch, that is the first-eclipse watch tally. Add the marks obtained for maximum eclipse and count to find the moment of greatest eclipse. Add the marks obtained after maximum eclipse and count to find the final watch tally in marks and parts. If totality ends before sunrise or first contact occurs after sunset, do not record an eclipse.
