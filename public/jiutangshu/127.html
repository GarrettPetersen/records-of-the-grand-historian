1
劉晏，字士安，曹州南華人。 年七歲，舉神童，授秘書省正字。 累授夏縣令，有能名。 歷殿中侍御史，遷度支郎中、杭隴華三州刺史，尋遷河南尹。 時史朝義盜據東都，寄理長水。 入為京兆尹，頃之，加戶部侍郎、兼御史中丞，判度支，委府事於司錄張群、杜亞，綜大體，議論號為稱職。 無何，為酷吏敬羽所構，貶通州刺史。 復入為京兆尹、戶部侍郎，判度支。 時顏真卿以文學正直出為利州刺史，晏舉真卿自代為戶部，乃加國子祭酒。 寶應二年，遷吏部尚書、平章事，領度支鹽鐵轉運租庸使。 坐與中官程元振交通，元振得罪，晏罷相，為太子賓客。 尋授御史大夫，領東都、河南、江淮、山南等道轉運租庸鹽鐵使如故。 時新承兵戈之後，中外艱食，京師米價鬥至一千，官廚無兼時之積，禁軍乏食，畿縣百姓乃挼穗以供之。 晏受命後，以轉運為己任，凡所經歷，必究利病之由。 至江淮，以書遺元載曰：
Liu Yan, whose style name was Shi'an, came from Nanhua in Cao Prefecture. At the age of seven he was presented as a child prodigy and given the post of Secretariat Rectifier. After successive appointments he served as magistrate of Xia County and earned a name for competence. He served as a palace attendant censor, then was promoted to director of the revenue bureau and prefect of Hang, Long, and Hua; not long afterward he became metropolitan governor of Henan. Shi Chaoyi had by then seized the Eastern Capital by force, so Yan governed in exile from Changshui. He was recalled to serve as metropolitan governor of Jingzhao, and shortly thereafter was also made vice minister of revenue and concurrent vice censor-in-chief with responsibility for revenue. He left routine office work to the registrars Zhang Qun and Du Ya while he himself handled broad policy, and public opinion held that his judgment suited the office. Before long the harsh official Jing Yu framed him, and he was demoted to prefect of Tong. He was brought back in as metropolitan governor of Jingzhao and vice minister of revenue, again overseeing revenue affairs. Yan Zhenqing, noted for scholarship and integrity, had been sent out as prefect of Li; Yan recommended him to take over the revenue portfolio and was himself given the additional title of rector of the Directorate of Education. In Baoying year 2 he was made minister of civil appointments and grand councilor, and took charge of revenue, salt and iron, transport, and corvée collection. He was punished for dealings with the eunuch Cheng Yuanzhen; when Yuanzhen fell, Yan lost his councilorship and was made mentor to the heir apparent. He was soon made censor-in-chief and continued to head transport, corvée, and salt-and-iron affairs for the Eastern Capital, Henan, Jiang-Huai, Shannan, and related circuits. The empire had only just come through war; famine gripped court and countryside alike. In the capital a dou of rice cost a thousand cash, the palace kitchens had no reserve worth two meals, the palace armies went hungry, and people in the capital districts rubbed grain from the ears by hand to feed them. Once commissioned, Yan made transport his personal mission; everywhere he went he traced the roots of what helped or hurt the system. On reaching the Jiang-Huai region he wrote to Yuan Zai:
2
浮於淮、泗，達於汴，入於河，西循底柱、硤石、少華，楚帆越客，直抵建章、長樂，此安社稷之奇策也。 晏賓於東朝，猶有官謗，相公終始故舊，不信流言，賈誼復召宣室，弘羊重興功利，敢不悉力以答所知。 驅馬陜郊，見三門渠津遺跡。 到河陰、鞏、洛，見宇文愷置梁公堰，分黃河水入通濟渠； 大夫李傑新堤故事，飾像河廟，凜然如生。 涉滎郊、浚澤，遙瞻淮甸，步步探討，知昔人用心，則潭、衡、桂陽必多積谷，關輔汲汲，只緣兵糧。 漕引瀟、湘、洞庭，萬里幾日，淪波卦席，西指長安。 三秦之人，待此而飽； 六軍之眾，待此而強。 天子無側席之憂，都人見泛舟之役； 四方旅拒者可以破膽，三河流離者於茲請命。 相公匡戴明主，為富人侯，此今之切務，不可失也。 使仆湔洗瑕穢，率罄愚懦，當憑經義，請護河堤，冥勤在官，不辭水死。
To float grain on the Huai and Si, reach the Bian Canal, enter the Yellow River, and follow west past Dizhu, Xia Gorge, and Lesser Hua so that Chu sails and Yue traders bear it straight to the imperial palaces—this is the bold strategy that would secure the realm. Though I still face official slander as a guest at the Eastern Court, you have been my friend from the beginning and will not heed rumor—like Jia Yi recalled to the Xuan Chamber or Sang Hongyang reviving state profit, I dare not hold back my full strength in repaying your trust. Riding through the Shaan region I saw the remains of the Sanmen canal works. At Heyin, Gong, and Luoyang I saw Yuwen Kai's Liang Duke Weir diverting Yellow River water into the Tongji Canal; Grandee Li Jie's work on the new dike, and the adorned figures in the River Temple standing stern as though alive. Passing through the Xing outskirts and dredged marshlands and looking out toward the Huai, I examined each stretch and saw how the ancients had labored—Tan, Heng, and Guiyang must hold great grain reserves, while the capital region strains solely for lack of army rations. To channel the Xiao, Xiang, and Dongting westward—ten thousand li in a matter of days, waves spread like mats, bearing grain straight to Chang'an. The people of the Three Qin would be fed by it; the Six Armies would be strengthened by it. The Son of Heaven would no longer sit uneasy on his throne, and the capital would see grain boats on the waterways; rebels in distant regions would lose heart, and refugees of the Three Rivers would here beg for relief. You who support a brilliant sovereign and may become a lord of wealth—this is the urgent task of our age and must not be let slip. Let me wash away my faults and offer all my feeble wit; guided by principle I ask to guard the river dikes, labor in office without complaint, and face drowning if need be.
3
然運之利病，各有四五焉。 晏自尹京入為計相，共五年矣。 京師三輔百姓，唯苦稅畝傷多，若使江、湖米來每年三二十萬，即頓減徭賦，歌舞皇澤，其利一也。 東都殘毀，百無一存。 若米運流通，則饑人皆附，村落邑厘，從此滋多。 受命之日引海陵之倉以食鞏、洛，是計之得者，其利二也。 諸將有在邊者，諸戎有侵敗王略者，或聞三江、五湖，貢輸紅粒，雲帆桂楫，輸納帝鄉，軍誌曰：「先聲後實，可以震耀夷夏。 」其利三也。 自古帝王之盛，皆云書同文，車同軌，日月所照，莫不率俾。 今舟車既通，商賈往來，百貨雜集，航海梯山，聖神輝光，漸近貞觀、永徽之盛，其利四也。
Yet transport has its gains and its troubles, four or five of each. From my years governing Jingzhao to serving as finance chancellor, it has been five years in all. The people of the capital districts suffer chiefly from crushing land taxes; if two or three hundred thousand shi of grain came yearly from the rivers and lakes, levies would drop at once and they would rejoice in the emperor's bounty—the first benefit. The Eastern Capital lies in ruins; scarcely one building in a hundred still stands. If grain transport were restored, the hungry would gather there and villages and market towns would spring up again. From the day I took office I could draw on Hailing's granaries to feed Gong and Luoyang—a sound plan and the second benefit. For generals on the frontier and for barbarians who encroach on the realm, to hear that red grain from the Three Rivers and Five Lakes sails on cloud-white banners and cassia-wood oars to the imperial capital would fulfill the maxim that reputation precedes force and can overawe both Chinese and foreigners alike. That would be the third benefit. Since antiquity the glory of sage rulers has been told as one script and one gauge for wheels, with all beneath the sun and moon submitting in turn. With waterways and roads linked again, merchants would travel, goods would gather from sea and mountain, and the sage radiance of the throne would draw near the prosperity of the Zhenguan and Yonghui eras—the fourth benefit.
4
所可疑者，函、陜雕殘，東周尤甚。 過宜陽、熊耳，至武牢、成臯，五百里中，編戶千余而已。 居無尺椽，人無煙爨，蕭條淒慘，獸遊鬼哭。 牛必羸角，輿必說輹，棧車輓漕，亦不易求。 今於無人之境，興此勞人之運，固難就矣，其病一也。 河、汴有初，不修則毀澱，故每年正月發近縣丁男，塞長茭，決沮淤，清明桃花已後，遠水自然安流，陽侯、宓妃，不復太息。 頃因寇難，總不掏拓，澤滅水，岸石崩，役夫需於沙，津吏旋於濘，千里洄上，罔水舟行，其病二也。 東垣、底柱，澠池、二陵，北河運處五六百里，戍卒久絕，縣吏空拳。 奪攘奸宄，窟穴囊橐。 夾河為藪，豺狼狺狺，舟行所經，寇亦能往，其病三也。 東自淮陰，西臨蒲阪，亙三千里，屯戍相望。 中軍皆鼎司元侯，賤卒儀同青紫，每雲食半菽，又雲無挾纊，輓漕所至，船到便留，即非單車使折簡書所能制矣，其病四也。 惟小子畢其慮奔走之，惟中書詳其利病裁成之。
What gives pause is that the Hangu and Shaan region lies in ruins, Eastern Zhou worst of all. From Yiyang and Xiong'er to Wulao and Chenggao, five hundred li hold barely a thousand registered households. Homes lack even a foot of timber, hearths show no smoke, and desolation is so deep that beasts wander the roads and ghosts seem to wail. Oxen are wasted to the horn, cart hubs are stripped away, and even plank carts for haulage are hard to find. To launch this labor-heavy transport through uninhabited country is bound to fail—that is the first difficulty. The Yellow River and Bian Canal must be dredged yearly or silt destroys them; each first month laborers from nearby counties block rushes and clear mud, and after Clear Brightness the distant currents run calm again—the river spirits need sigh no more. Lately, because of rebellion, dredging has stopped altogether; marshes have swallowed the channel, banks have collapsed, laborers stand idle on sandbars, ferry officials spin in mud, and for a thousand li upstream there is no depth for boats—the second difficulty. From Eastern Yuan and Dizhu through Mianchi and the Two Mounds, five or six hundred li of the northern transport route have no garrison troops left and county officials have no force at hand. Robbers and traitors seize goods and hole up with their plunder. Marshes line the riverbanks, wolves snarl along the route, and wherever boats can go bandits can go too—the third difficulty. From Huaiyin in the east to Pufan in the west, three thousand li of garrison posts stand face to face. The central armies are filled with chief ministers and great lords, while common soldiers dress like officials of high rank; they claim half a meal of beans and no quilt for warmth, yet wherever transport boats arrive they are held up on the spot—not something a lone commissioner with a letter can restrain—the fourth difficulty. Only I will exhaust my wits in carrying it out; only the Secretariat can weigh its gains and losses and bring it to completion.
5
晏累年已來，事缺名毀，聖慈含育，特賜生全。 月余家居，遽即臨遣，恩榮感切，思殞百身。 見一水不通，願荷鍤而先往； 見一粒不運，願負米而先趨。 焦心苦形，期報明主，丹誠未克，漕引多虞，屏營中流，掩泣獻狀。
For years my record has been flawed and my name tarnished, yet imperial mercy spared my life. After little more than a month at home I was suddenly sent out on mission; the grace overwhelmed me and I would have given a hundred lives in return. Where a channel was blocked I wished to take up the spade and go first; where a single grain failed to move I wished to carry rice on my back and run ahead. I wore myself thin with worry, hoping to repay my sovereign; though my loyalty was not yet fulfilled and transport still faced many doubts, I paced the river in distress and, weeping, submitted this report.
6
自此每歲運米數十萬石以濟關中。
From then on several hundred thousand shi of rice were shipped each year to supply Guanzhong.
7
又至德初，為國用不足，令第五琦於諸道榷鹽以助軍用，及晏代其任，法益精密，官無遺利。 初，歲入錢六十萬貫，季年所入逾十倍，而人無厭苦。 大歷末，通計一歲征賦所入總一千二百萬貫，而鹽利且過半。 累遷吏部尚書。 大歷四年六月，與右僕射裴遵慶同赴本曹視事，敕尚食增置儲供，許內侍魚朝恩及宰臣已下常朝官鹹詣省送上。 八年，知三銓選事。 十二年三月，誅宰臣元載，晏奉詔訊鞫。 晏以載居任樹黨，布於天下，不敢專斷，請他官共事。 敕御史大夫李涵、右散騎常侍蕭昕、兵部侍郎袁傪、禮部侍郎常袞、諫議大夫杜亞同推，載皆款伏。 初，晏承旨，門下侍郎、同平章事王縉亦處極法，晏謂涵等曰：重刑再覆，國之常典，況誅大臣，得不覆奏？ 又法有首從，二人同刑，亦宜重取進止。 」涵等從命。 及晏等覆奏，代宗乃減縉罪從輕。 縉之生，晏平反之力也。
Early in the Zhide era, with state funds short, Di Wuqi was ordered to impose salt monopolies across the circuits to support the armies; when Yan took over, the system grew tighter still and the government left no revenue on the table. At first annual receipts were six hundred thousand strings of cash; in later years they rose more than tenfold, yet the people did not complain. By the end of the Dali era total annual tax receipts reached twelve million strings of cash, with salt profits alone accounting for more than half. He rose through successive promotions to minister of civil appointments. In the sixth month of Dali year 4 he and right vice director Pei Zunqing went together to oversee their ministry; the emperor ordered extra provisions from the imperial kitchen and allowed the eunuch Yu Chao'en and all regular court officials down to the grand councilors to visit the ministry with congratulatory gifts. In year 8 he took charge of selection affairs in all three appointment bureaus. In the third month of year 12 the grand councilor Yuan Zai was put to death, and Yan was ordered to conduct the interrogation. Because Zai had built a factional network across the empire while in office, Yan did not dare rule alone and asked other officials to join the inquiry. The emperor ordered Censor-in-Chief Li Han, Right Regular Attendant Xiao Xin, Vice Minister of War Yuan Kan, Vice Minister of Rites Chang Gao, and Remonstrance Grandee Du Ya to join the inquiry; Zai confessed to everything. When Yan first received the order, Wang Jin, vice director of the secretariat and associate grand councilor, was also slated for the death penalty. Yan told Han and the others: 'A second review of a heavy sentence is standard state practice; when a great minister is to be executed, must we not submit a follow-up memorial?' The law also distinguishes ringleader from follower; when two men face the same penalty, we should again seek the throne's final decision. Han and the others complied. When Yan and the others submitted their follow-up memorial, Daizong lightened Jin's sentence. Jin owed his life to Yan's intervention.
8
十三年十二月，為尚書左僕射。 時宰臣常袞專政，以晏久掌銓衡，時議平允，兼司儲蓄，職舉功深，慮公望日崇，上心有屬。 竊忌之，乃奏晏朝廷舊德，宜為百吏師長，外示崇重，內實去其權。 及奏上，以晏使務方理，代其任者難其人，使務、知三銓並如故。 李靈曜之亂也，河南節帥所據，多不奉法令，征賦亦隨之； 州縣雖益減，晏以羨余相補，人不加賦，所入仍舊，議者稱其能。 自諸道巡院距京師，重價募疾足，置遞相望，四方物價之上下，雖極遠不四五日知，故食貨之重輕，盡權在掌握，朝廷獲美利而天下無甚貴甚賤之憂，得其術矣。 凡所任使，多收後進有幹能者。 其所總領，務乎急促，趨利者化之，遂以成風。 當時權勢，或以親戚為托，晏亦應之，俸給之多少，命官之遲速，必如其誌，然未嘗得親職事。 其所領要務，必一時之選，故晏沒後二十余年，韓洄、元琇、裴腆、包佶、盧征、李衡繼掌財賦，皆晏故吏。 其部吏居數千里之外，奉教令如在目前，雖寢興宴語，而無欺紿，四方動靜，莫不先知，事有可賀者，必先上章奏。 江淮茶、橘，晏與本道觀察使各歲貢之，皆欲其先至。 有土之官，或封山斷道，禁前發者，晏厚以財力致之，常先他司，由是甚不為籓鎮所便。
In the twelfth month of year 13 he was made left vice director of the Department of State Affairs. Grand councilor Chang Gao then dominated the government. Yan had long controlled appointments and was regarded as even-handed, while also managing state stores with deep achievement; Chang feared Yan's public standing was rising daily and that the emperor's favor might turn toward him. Jealous in private, he memorialized that Yan, a veteran of the court, should become mentor to the officials—honoring him in name while stripping his real authority. When the memorial reached the throne, Yan's transport duties were still in good order and no suitable replacement could be found, so his transport portfolio and control of the three selection bureaus remained unchanged. During Li Lingyao's rebellion the Henan military commissioners for the most part ignored imperial law, and tax collection suffered accordingly; though prefectures and counties reported further shortfalls, Yan used surpluses elsewhere to cover them so the people faced no new levies and revenue held steady—observers praised his skill. From his circuit inspection offices to the capital he hired swift couriers at high pay and set relay posts along the routes, so price movements even in the farthest regions were known within four or five days. He thus controlled the weight of goods and money, brought handsome profit to the court, and kept the realm from extreme inflation or collapse—he had mastered the art. In appointments he favored promising younger men of proven ability. The offices he led prized speed above all, and those who sought profit followed his example until haste became the norm. Powerful men often asked him to place their relatives; Yan obliged on salary and the timing of appointments, yet never gave them substantive posts. For key posts he chose the best men of the day, so more than twenty years after his death Han Hui, Yuan Xiu, Pei Tian, Bao Ji, Lu Zheng, and Li Heng in turn ran state finance—all former subordinates of his. Subordinates thousands of li away obeyed his orders as if he stood before them; whether waking or sleeping, at feast or in talk, none deceived him. He learned of events across the realm before anyone else, and good news was reported to him by memorial before all others. Tea and oranges from the Jiang-Huai were presented yearly as tribute by Yan and each circuit observation commissioner, each striving to arrive first. Local officials sometimes blocked roads to keep tribute from leaving early; Yan spent heavily to secure priority and usually beat other offices—making him deeply unpopular with the regional commanders.
9
晏理家以儉約稱，而重交敦舊，頗以財貨遺天下名土，故人多稱之。 善訓諸子，鹹有學藝。 任事十余年，權勢之重，鄰於宰相，要官重職，頗出其門。 既有材力，視事敏速，乘機無滯，然多任數，挾權貴，固恩澤，有口者必利啖之。 當大歷時，事貴因循，軍國之用，皆仰於晏，未嘗檢轄。
Yan ran his household frugally, yet cherished old friendships and often gave money to renowned scholars across the land, which won him wide praise. He trained his sons well, and all gained learning and skill. After more than ten years in power his authority rivaled that of a chancellor, and many key offices passed through his followers. Talented and decisive, he handled business with speed and seized every opening—yet he relied heavily on maneuver, cultivated the powerful, secured imperial favor, and bought off anyone who might speak against him. In the Dali era government prized routine; military and civil spending all depended on Yan and went largely unchecked.
10
德宗嗣位，言事者稱轉運可罷多矣。 初，楊炎為吏部侍郎，晏為尚書，各恃權使氣，兩不相得。 炎坐元載貶，晏快之，昌言於朝。 及炎入相，追怒前事，且以晏與元載隙憾，時人言載之得罪，晏有力焉。 炎將為載復仇，又時人風言代宗寵獨孤妃而又愛其子韓王迥，晏密啟請立獨孤為皇后。 炎因對易攵流涕奏言：「賴祖宗福祐，先皇與陛下不為賊臣所間。 不然，劉晏、黎幹之輩，搖動社稷，兇謀果矣。 今幹以伏罪，晏猶領權，臣為宰相，不能正持此事，罪當萬死。 」崔祐甫奏言：「此事曖昧，陛下以廓然大赦，不當究尋虛語。 」硃泚、崔寧又從傍與祐甫救解之，寧言頗切，炎大怒，故斥寧令出鎮鄜坊以摧挫之。 遂罷晏轉運等使，尋貶為忠州刺史。 炎欲誣構其罪，知庾準與晏素有隙，舉為荊南節度，以伺晏動靜。 準乃奏晏與硃泚書祈救解，言多怨望，炎又證成其事，上以為然。 是月庚午，晏已受誅，使回奏報，誣晏以忠州謀叛，下詔暴言其罪，時年六十六，天下冤之。 家屬徙嶺表，連累者數十人。 貞元五年，上悟，方錄晏子執經，授太常博士； 少子宗經，秘書郎。 執經上請削官贈父，特追贈鄭州刺史。
When Dezong came to the throne, many memorialists argued that the transport system should be abolished. Earlier, when Yang Yan was vice minister of civil appointments and Yan was minister, each abused his authority and the two could not abide each other. When Yang Yan was demoted because of Yuan Zai, Yan rejoiced and spoke openly against him at court. When Yang Yan became chancellor he nursed that grudge and noted Yan's feud with Yuan Zai; contemporaries said Yan had helped bring Zai down. Yang Yan meant to avenge Zai, and rumor held that Daizong favored Consort Dugu and her son Prince Han of Huai; Yan had secretly urged making Dugu empress. Yang Yan then addressed the throne in tears: 'By the blessings of our ancestors, the late emperor and Your Majesty were not torn apart by traitorous ministers.' Otherwise men like Liu Yan and Li Gan would have shaken the throne, and their wicked plot would have succeeded. Gan has now paid for his crime, yet Yan still holds power; as chancellor I have failed to correct this and deserve death ten thousand times over.' Cui Youfu replied: 'This matter is unclear; Your Majesty has proclaimed a great amnesty and should not pursue idle rumor.' Zhu Ci and Cui Ning sided with Youfu in mediation; Ning spoke sharply, and Yang Yan in fury sent him out to govern Zuo-Fang as punishment. He died in year 9, aged seventy-two, and was posthumously made left vice director with the posthumous title Respectful.

李巽，字令叔，趙郡人。 少苦心為學，以明經調補華州參軍，拔萃登科，授鄠縣尉。 周歷臺省，由左司郎中出為常州刺史。 逾年，召為給事中，出為湖南觀察使，銳於為理。 五年，改江西觀察使，加檢校散騎常侍、兼御史大夫。 巽持下以法，吏不敢欺，而動必察之。 順宗即位，入為兵部侍郎。 司徒杜佑判度支鹽鐵轉運使，以巽幹治，奏為副使。 佑辭重位，巽遂專領度支鹽鐵使。 榷筦之法，號為難重，唯大歷中僕射劉晏雅得其術，賦入豐羨。 巽掌使一年，征課所入，類晏之多歲，明年過之，又一年加一百八十萬貫。 舊制，每歲運江淮米五十萬斛抵河陰，久不盈其數，唯巽三年登焉。 遷兵部尚書，明年改吏部尚書，使任如故。
Li Xun, whose style name was Lingshu, came from Zhao Commandery. He studied hard in youth, passed the Mingjing examination and became a military aide in Hua Prefecture, then by special selection gained office and was made defender of E County. He served throughout the central ministries and left as director of the left bureau to become prefect of Chang. A year later he was recalled as supervising censor and sent out as Hunan observation commissioner, governing with sharp rigor. In year 5 he became Jiangxi observation commissioner and was additionally made acting regular attendant and concurrent censor-in-chief. Xun governed subordinates by law; clerks dared not deceive him, and he scrutinized every action. When Shunzong came to the throne he entered the capital as vice minister of war. Situ Du You headed revenue, salt and iron, and transport; he recommended Xun as deputy commissioner for his administrative skill. When You stepped down, Xun took sole charge of revenue and salt and iron. Monopoly administration was considered the hardest fiscal task; only Liu Yan in the Dali era had mastered it and filled the treasury. In his first year as commissioner revenue matched Yan's best years; the next year surpassed it; in the third year it rose by 1.8 million strings of cash. By regulation five hundred thousand hu of Jiang-Huai grain were shipped yearly to Heyin; for years the quota was missed—only Xun met it within three years. He was made minister of war, then minister of civil appointments the next year, retaining his commissioner duties.
20
巽精於吏職，蓋性使然也。 雖在私家，亦置案牘簿書，勾檢如公署焉。 人吏有過，絲毫無所貸，雖在千里外，其恐栗如在巽前。 初，程異附王叔文貶竄，巽知其吏才明辯，奏而用之，憲宗不違其請。 異勾檢簿籍，又精於巽，故課最加衍，亦異之助焉。 巽為吏部尚書，臥疾，郎官相率省問，巽初不言其病，與之考校程課，商略功利，至其夕而卒。 然性強很狡惡，忌刻頗甚，乘德宗之怒，謀殺竇參，物論冤之。 初，參為宰相，不悅於巽，自左司郎中出為常州刺史，仍促其行。 不數月，參貶郴州司馬。 久之，巽自給事中為湖南觀察使，郴即屬郡也。 宣武軍節度使劉士寧以擅襲父任，物議不可，朝廷不得已而授之。 及參之貶，士寧嘗以絹數千匹賂參，巽在湖南具奏其事，言參與籓鎮交通，德宗怒，遂賜參死，議者冤之。 巽廉察江西，徇喜怒之情，而無罪被戮者多矣。 元和四年四月卒，時年七十一，贈尚書左僕射。
Xun excelled at administrative work by nature. Even at home he kept documents and ledgers and audited them as in a government office. He forgave no fault in subordinates; clerks a thousand li away trembled as if before him. Cheng Yi, banished for ties to Wang Shuwen, was known to Xun for sharp administrative talent; Xun memorialized to employ him and Xianzong agreed. Yi audited the books with even greater skill than Xun, so revenue targets rose year by year—with Yi's help. As minister of civil appointments Xun fell ill; when bureau officials called he did not mention his illness but reviewed schedules and discussed revenue with them—and died that evening. Yet he was stubborn, crafty, and deeply jealous; exploiting Dezong's anger he engineered Dou Can's death, which public opinion deemed unjust. When Can was chancellor he disliked Xun and sent him from the left bureau directorship to Chang prefecture, urging a quick departure. Within months Can was demoted to military adjutant of Chen. Long afterward Xun became Hunan observation commissioner from supervising censor; Chen was under his jurisdiction. Xuanwu commissioner Liu Shining seized his father's post on his own authority, which public opinion rejected; the court granted it only reluctantly. When Can was demoted, Shining had once bribed him with thousands of bolts of silk. Xun in Hunan reported that Can had dealings with regional commanders; Dezong in anger ordered Can's death, which many regarded as unjust. As Jiangxi inspector Xun indulged personal moods, and many innocent men were executed. He died in the fourth month of Yuanhe year 4, aged seventy-one, and was posthumously made left vice director of the Department of State Affairs.
21
史臣曰：歷代操利柄為國計者，莫不損下益上，危人自安，變法以弄權，斂怨以構禍，皆有之矣。 如劉晏通擁滯，任才能，富其國而不勞於民，儉於家而利於眾。 或問曰：鄭子產吏不能欺，宓子賤吏不忍欺，西門豹吏不敢欺。 三子者，古之賢人也，吏皆懷其欺而不能、不忍、不敢也。 晏之吏，遠近自不欺者何也？ 答曰：蓋任其才而得其人也。 晏歿，故吏二十余年繼掌財賦，不其是哉！ 《史記貨殖》云：「平糶齊物，關市不乏，治國之道也。 」晏治天下，無甚貴甚賤之物，泛言治國者，其可及乎！ 舉真卿才，忠也，減王縉罪，正也，忠正之道，復出於人，嗚呼！ 本秀於林，風必摧之，常袞見忌於前，楊炎致冤於後，可為長嘆息矣！ 時譏有口者以利啖之，茍不塞讒口，何以持重權？ 即無以展其才，濟其國矣。 是其術也，又何譏焉。 第五琦促辦應卒，民不加賦，而國豐饒，亦庶幾矣。 然鑄錢變法，物貴身危，其何陋哉！ 凡利國者，農商之外，不可為也。 宏、滂爭權樹黨，皆非令人。 紹之謹密幹事，巽之皦察精辨，亦足可稱。
The historiographer writes: Throughout history those who held fiscal power for state planning have harmed the people to enrich the throne, endangered others for their own safety, changed laws to manipulate power, and gathered resentment unto disaster. Liu Yan understood hoarding and release, employed talent wisely, enriched the state without burdening the people, and was frugal at home while benefiting the public. Someone asked: 'Zichan of Zheng—clerks could not deceive him; Master Mi Jian—clerks could not bear to deceive him; Ximen Bao—clerks dared not deceive him.' These three were sages of antiquity; clerks might think to deceive them yet could not, could not bear to, or dared not. Why were Yan's clerks, near and far, not deceiving him of their own accord? The answer: he entrusted work to capable men and chose the right people. After Yan's death his former clerks ran state finance for more than twenty years—is that not proof? The Treatise on Commodities in the Records says: 'Equalizing grain sales and leveling prices, keeping markets supplied—that is how to govern a state.' Yan governed the realm so that nothing became extremely dear or cheap—can those who speak generally of statecraft match him! Recommending Zhenqing showed loyalty; lightening Wang Jin's sentence showed rectitude—the way of loyalty and justice lived again in a man—alas! A tree that stands out in the forest is broken by the wind; Chang Gao envied him before, Yang Yan wronged him after—how deeply one must sigh! The age mocked him for buying off critics; yet without silencing slander, how could he hold great power? Without that he could neither display his talent nor save the state. That was his method—why mock it? Di Wuqi met emergencies swiftly without raising taxes, and the state grew rich—he nearly succeeded. Yet his coin reforms made goods dear and endangered his own life—how crude a policy! State profit must come only from agriculture and commerce, not elsewhere. Hong and Pang fought for power and built factions—neither was admirable. Shao's discreet competence and Xun's sharp scrutiny are also praiseworthy.
22
贊曰：豐財忠良，晏道為長。 琦、宏、滂、巽，鹹以利彰。
Encomium: Enriching the treasury through loyal service—Yan's way stood foremost. Qi, Hong, Pang, and Xun—all won fame through fiscal skill.
