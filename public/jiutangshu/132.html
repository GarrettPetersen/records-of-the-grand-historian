1
○李抱玉李抱真王虔休盧從史李芃李澄族弟元素李抱玉李抱玉，武德功臣安興貴之裔。 代居河西，善養名馬，為時所稱。 群從兄弟，或徙居京華，習文儒，與士人通婚者，稍染士風。 抱玉少長西州，好騎射，常從軍幕，沉毅有謀，小心忠謹。
○ Li Baoyu, Li Baozhen, Wang Qianxiu, Lu Congshi, Li Peng, Li Cheng, and his clansman Yuan Su. Li Baoyu traced his line to An Xinggui, one of the founding heroes of the Wude era. The family had lived in Hexi for generations, renowned for breeding fine horses. Some of his kinsmen moved to the capital, took up literary studies, and married into gentry families, slowly acquiring the manners of scholar-officials. Baoyu had grown up in Xizhou, loved horsemanship and archery, and spent much of his youth in army camps—steady, shrewd, and scrupulously loyal.
2
乾元初，太尉李光弼引為偏裨，屢建勳績，由是知名。 二年，自特進、右羽林軍大將軍、知軍事，遷鴻臚卿員外置同正員，持節鄭州諸軍事兼鄭州刺史、攝御史中丞、鄭陳潁亳四州節度。 時史思明陷洛陽，光弼守河陽，賊兵鋒方盛，光弼謂抱玉曰：「將軍能為我守南城二日乎？ 」抱玉曰：「過期若何？ 」光弼曰：「過期而救不至，任棄城也。 」賊帥周摯領安太清、徐黃玉等先次南城，將陷之，抱玉乃紿之曰：「吾糧盡，明日當降。 」賊眾大喜，斂軍以俟之。 抱玉因得繕完設備，明日，堅壁請戰。 賊怒欺紿，急攻之。 抱玉出奇兵，表裡夾攻，殺傷甚眾，摯軍退。 光弼自將於中潬城，摯舍南城攻中潬，不勝，乃整軍將攻北城。 光弼以兵出戰，大敗之。 固河陽，復懷州，皆功居第一，遷澤州刺史、兼御史中丞。 代宗即位，擢為澤潞節度使、潞州大都督府長史、兼御史大夫，加領陳、鄭二州，遷兵部尚書。 抱玉上言：「臣貫屬涼州，本姓安氏，以祿山構禍，恥與同姓，去至德二年五月，蒙恩賜姓李氏，今請割貫屬京兆府長安縣。 」許之，因是舉宗並賜國姓。
Early in the Qianyuan era, Grand Marshal Li Guangbi took him on as a staff officer; repeated battlefield successes soon made his name known. In the second year he rose from Special Grand Tutor and Grand General of the Right Imperial Guard, with command of military affairs, to Vice Director of the Palace Receptions Service (acting full rank), and was commissioned to oversee Zhengzhou’s armies while serving as prefect, acting Vice Censor-in-chief, and military commissioner over Zheng, Chen, Ying, and Bo. Shi Siming had seized Luoyang while Guangbi held Heyang; rebel forces were at their peak. Guangbi asked Baoyu, "Can you hold the south wall for two days? " Baoyu asked, "And if the two days pass? " Guangbi replied, "If relief has not come by then, you may abandon the city. " Rebel commander Zhou Zhi, with An Taiqing, Xu Huangyu, and others, reached the south city first and was poised to storm it. Baoyu deceived them: "Our provisions are gone—we will surrender tomorrow. " The rebels were delighted and held their troops back to wait. Baoyu used the respite to repair the walls and stock supplies; the next day he shut the gates and challenged them to fight. Enraged at the trick, the rebels assaulted the walls at once. Baoyu sent out ambush troops to strike from within and without; casualties were heavy, and Zhi’s force retreated. Guangbi commanded from Zhongtan; Zhi abandoned the south city to attack Zhongtan, failed, then regrouped to strike the north wall. Guangbi marched out and inflicted a crushing defeat. He ranked first in merit for holding Heyang and retaking Huaizhou, and was made prefect of Zezhou with the concurrent title of Vice Censor-in-chief. When Daizong succeeded, Baoyu was appointed military commissioner of Ze and Lu, chief administrator of Lu prefecture, and Censor-in-chief, with added authority over Chen and Zheng, and later became Minister of War. Baoyu memorialized: "I was long registered in Liangzhou and bore the surname An. After An Lushan’s rebellion I was ashamed to share that name; in the fifth month of Zhide 2 the throne granted me the surname Li. I now ask to be registered in Chang’an county, Jingzhao. " The request was approved, and his entire clan received the imperial surname as well.
3
廣德元年冬，吐蕃寇京師，乘輿幸陝，諸軍潰卒及村閭亡命相聚為盜，京城南面子午等五穀群盜頗害居人，朝廷遣薛景仙領兵為五穀使招討，連月不捷，乃詔抱玉兼鳳翔節度使討之。 抱玉探知賊帥行止之處，先分屯諸谷，乃設奇潛使輕銳數百南自洋州入攻之。 賊帥高玉方與諸偷會，遽為銳卒數十人掩擒之，因大搜獲偷黨，悉斬之，餘黨不討自潰，旬日內五穀平。 以功遷司空，余並如故。
That winter of Guangde 1 Tibetans raided the capital and the emperor withdrew to Shan. Routed soldiers and village outlaws formed bandit bands; south of Chang’an, in the Ziwu and other Five Valleys, gangs preyed on civilians. Xue Jingxian led a pacification force for months without success, so Baoyu was ordered to take Fengxiang and crush them. After learning where the chiefs were operating, he posted troops in the valleys, then sent several hundred picked men south from Yangzhou in a surprise strike. Chief Gao Yu was meeting his fellows when a few dozen raiders seized him; a sweep captured the rest and beheaded them. The remnant bands melted away, and within ten days the Five Valleys were quiet. For this he was promoted to Minister of Works; his other posts remained unchanged.
4
時吐蕃每歲犯境，上以岐陽國之西門，寄在抱玉，恩寵無比，遷同中書門下平章事，又兼山南西道節度使、河西隴右山南西道副元帥、判梁州事，連統三道節制，兼領鳳翔、潞、梁三大府，秩處三公。 抱玉以任位崇重，抗疏懇讓司空及山南西道節度、判梁州事，乞退授兵部尚書。 上嘉其謙讓，許之。 抱雲凡鎮鳳翔十餘年，雖無破虜之功，而禁暴安人，頗為當時所稱。 大曆十二年卒，上甚悼之，輟朝三日，贈太保。 李抱真李抱真，抱玉從父弟也。 抱玉為澤潞節度使，甚器抱真，任以軍事，累授汾州別駕。 當是時，僕固懷恩反於汾州，抱真陷焉，乃脫身歸京師。 代宗以懷恩倚回紇，所將朔方兵又勁，憂甚，召見抱真問狀，因奏曰：「郭子儀領朔方之眾，人多思之。 懷恩欺其眾，曰『子儀為朝恩所殺』，詐而用之。 今復子儀之位，可不戰而克。 」其後懷恩子瑒為其下所殺，懷恩奔遁，多如抱真策，因是遷殿中少監。 居頃之，為陳鄭、澤潞節度留後，抱真因中謝言曰：「臣雖無可取，當今百姓勞逸，系在牧守，願得一郡以自試。 」上許之，改授澤州刺史，兼為澤潞節度副使。 居二年，轉懷州刺史，復為懷澤潞觀察使留後，凡八年。 抱玉卒，抱真仍領留後。 抱真密揣山東當有變，上黨且當兵沖，是時乘戰余之地，土瘠賦重，人益困，無以養軍士。 籍戶丁男，三選其一，有材力者免其租徭，給弓矢，令之曰：「農之隙，則分曹角射； 歲終，吾當會試。 」及期，按簿而征之，都試以示賞罰，覆命之如初。 比三年，則皆善射，抱真曰：「軍可用矣。 」於是舉部內鄉兵，得成卒二萬，前既不廩費，府庫益實，乃繕甲兵，為戰具，遂雄視山東。 是時，天下稱昭義軍步兵冠諸軍。 無幾，復代李承昭為昭義軍及磁邢節度觀察留後，加散騎常侍。
Tibetans raided the frontier every year; the emperor treated Qiyang as the realm’s western gate and placed it in Baoyu’s hands with unmatched favor. He became a co-director of the Secretariat, deputy grand marshal over Hexi, Longyou, and the Huainan west circuit, and administrator of Liangzhou—commanding three circuits and three major prefectures at a rank equal to the Three Excellencies. Finding his posts too weighty, Baoyu petitioned to relinquish Minister of Works, the Huainan west commission, and Liangzhou administration, asking only to remain Minister of War. The emperor commended his modesty and agreed. Baoyu governed Fengxiang for more than ten years. He won no great victories over foreign foes, but his suppression of lawlessness and care for the people won wide praise. He died in Dali 12. The emperor mourned deeply, suspended court for three days, and posthumously named him Grand Tutor. Li Baozhen was Baoyu’s paternal cousin. While Baoyu held Ze and Lu, he relied heavily on Baozhen for military affairs and repeatedly named him acting prefect of Fenzhou. When Pugu Huai’en rebelled at Fenzhou, Baozhen was caught up in the revolt but broke free and reached the capital. Daizong, alarmed that Huai’en had Uyghur backing and commanded the formidable Shuofang army, summoned Baozhen and asked what he knew. Baozhen replied, "Guo Ziyi still commands the Shuofang troops in the soldiers’ hearts. Huai’en deceives them, claiming ‘Ziyi was murdered by court intrigue,’ and leads them by that lie. Restore Ziyi to command and the rebellion will collapse without a fight. " Huai’en’s son Chang was later killed by his own men; Huai’en fled—much as Baozhen had predicted—and Baozhen was promoted to Junior Supervisor of the Palace. Soon he became acting commissioner of Chen, Zheng, Ze, and Lu. When he thanked the throne, Baozhen said, "I have little to offer, but the people’s welfare rests on their governors; grant me one prefecture to prove myself. " The emperor agreed and named him prefect of Zezhou and deputy commissioner of Ze and Lu. Two years later he became prefect of Huaizhou and again acting commissioner of the Huai–Ze–Lu circuit—for eight years in all. When Baoyu died, Baozhen remained as acting commissioner. Baozhen sensed trouble brewing in the east: Shangdang would become a battleground. The region was war-ravaged, the soil poor and taxes heavy; the people were exhausted and could not sustain an army. He registered every third able-bodied man, exempted the strong from taxes and corvée, issued bows, and ordered: "In slack farming seasons, drill by squads in archery; at year’s end I will hold a muster and examination. " When the season came he called them up by register, held a general review with rewards and penalties, and renewed the order for the next year. Within three years they were all crack archers. Baozhen said, "The army is ready. " He then mobilized local militia into twenty thousand regular troops. Having spent nothing from the granaries, his treasury grew rich; he outfitted armor and weapons and came to dominate the east. At the time the Zhaoyi infantry were reckoned the finest in the empire. Soon he succeeded Li Chengzhao as acting commissioner of the Zhaoyi Army and the Ci–Xing circuit, with the added title of Regular Attendant-in-Ordinary.
5
德宗即位，拜檢校工部尚書，兼潞州長史、昭義軍節度支度營田、澤潞磁邢觀察使。 建中二年，田悅以魏博反，乃悉兵圍邢州及臨洺益急，詔河東節度使馬燧及神策兵救之。 抱真與燧敗悅兵於雙岡，斬悅將楊朝光，又擊破悅於臨洺，遂解臨洺及邢州之圍，以功加檢校兵部尚書。 復與燧大破悅於洹水，悅以數百騎走歸魏州。 復與燧圍魏州，又敗悅於城下，以功加檢校右僕射。 時悅窘蹙，硃滔、王武俊皆反，聯兵救悅，抱真與燧等退次魏縣。 上幸奉天，中使告問至，諸將皆仰天慟哭。 李懷光席捲奔命，馬燧、李芃各引兵歸鎮。 硃泚既汙宮闕，時李希烈陷大梁，李納亦反鄆州。 無何，上幸梁州，李懷光又竊據河中。 抱真獨於擾攘傾潰之中，以山東三州外抗群賊，內輯軍士，群賊深憚之。
When Dezong succeeded, Baozhen was named Acting Minister of Works, chief administrator of Lu, Zhaoyi military commissioner with control of supplies and garrison lands, and observation commissioner over Ze, Lu, Ci, and Xing. In Jianzhong 2 Tian Yue rebelled in Weibo and pressed the siege of Xingzhou and Linming. Ma Sui of Hedong and the Shence guards were ordered to relieve them. Baozhen and Ma Sui routed Tian Yue at Shuanggang, killed his general Yang Chaoguang, defeated him again at Linming, and lifted both sieges; Baozhen was promoted to Acting Minister of War. With Sui he again crushed Tian Yue on the Huan River; Yue fled to Weizhou with a few hundred horsemen. They besieged Weizhou and defeated Yue again beneath the walls; Baozhen was made Acting Right Vice Director. Tian Yue was cornered, but Zhu Tao and Wang Wujun rebelled and marched to his aid; Baozhen and Ma Sui fell back to Weixian. The emperor fled to Fengtian; when palace envoys brought word, the generals wept aloud to heaven. Li Huai’guang rushed off in haste; Ma Sui and Li Peng each withdrew to their own circuits. Zhu Ci had seized the capital; Li Xilie held Daliang; Li Na rebelled in Yanzhou. Soon the emperor withdrew to Liangzhou, and Li Huai’guang seized Hezhong. Alone amid chaos, Baozhen held three eastern prefectures, fought the rebel hosts without, and kept his army in order within; the rebels feared him deeply.
6
興元初，遷檢校左僕射、平章事。 時硃滔悉幽薊軍，借兵回紇，擁眾五萬，南向以應泚，攻圍貝州。 初，群賊附於希烈，希烈僭偽，有臣屬群賊意，群心稍離。 上自奉天下罪己之詔，悉赦群賊，抱真乃遣門客賈林以大義說武俊，合從擊硃滔，武俊許之。 時兩軍尚相疑，抱真乃以數騎徑入武俊營。 其將去也，賓客皆止之，抱真遣軍司馬盧玄卿勒軍部分曰：「僕今日此舉，系天下安危。 僕死不還，領軍事以聽朝命，亦唯子； 奮勵士馬，東向雪僕之恥，亦唯子。 」言訖而去。 武俊設備甚嚴，抱真曰：「硃泚、希烈僭竊大位，硃滔攻圍貝州，此輩皆欲陵駕吾屬。 足下既不能自振數賊之上，舍九葉天子而北面臣反虜乎？ 乃者聖上奉天下罪己之詔，可謂禹、湯之主也。 」因言及播越，持武俊哭，涕泗交下，武俊亦哭，感動左右。 因退臥武俊帳中，酣寢久之。 武俊感其不疑，待之益恭，指心仰天曰：「此身已許公死敵矣。 」遂與結為兄弟而別，約明日合戰，遂擊破硃滔於經城，以功加檢校司空，實封五百戶。 貞元初，朝於京師，居頃之，還鎮。
Early in the Xingyuan era he became Acting Left Vice Director and a co-director of the Secretariat. Zhu Tao gathered the You–Ji armies, borrowed Uyghur auxiliaries, and marched south with fifty thousand men to support Zhu Ci, besieging Beizhou. The rebel leaders had rallied to Li Xilie, but when he declared himself emperor and treated them as vassals, their loyalty began to fray. From Fengtian the emperor issued his self-reproach edict and pardoned the rebels. Baozhen sent his client Jia Lin to persuade Wang Wujun by righteous argument to join against Zhu Tao, and Wujun agreed. The two armies still distrusted each other, so Baozhen rode into Wujun’s camp with only a few escorts. His staff tried to stop him. Baozhen sent his army vice director Lu Xuanqing to address the troops: "What I do today concerns the fate of the empire. If I do not return, take command and obey the court—that duty is yours alone; and rouse the army to march east and avenge my disgrace—that too rests on you alone. " With that he rode off. Wujun had taken strict precautions. Baozhen said, "Zhu Ci and Li Xilie have seized imperial titles; Zhu Tao besieges Beizhou—all of them mean to dominate us. If you cannot stand above these rebels, will you abandon the Son of Heaven of nine generations and bow north to traitors? The Son of Heaven has just issued his self-reproach edict to the realm—a ruler in the mold of Yu and Tang. " He spoke of the emperor’s exile, seized Wujun, and wept until both men were in tears, moving everyone present. He then retired to Wujun’s tent and slept deeply for a long time. Moved by such trust, Wujun treated him with still greater respect, pointed to his heart, and swore to heaven, "I have pledged my life to fight your enemies. " They swore brotherhood and parted, agreeing to fight together the next day. They defeated Zhu Tao at Jingcheng; Baozhen was made Acting Minister of Works with a fief of five hundred households. Early in the Zhenyuan era he visited the capital, then soon returned to his post.
7
抱真沉斷多智計，嘗欲招致天下賢俊，聞人之善，必令持貨幣數千里邀致之； 至與語無可採者，漸退之。 時天下無事，乃大起台榭，穿池沼以自娛。 晚節又好方士，以冀長生。 有孫季長者，為抱真練金丹，紿抱真曰：「服之當升仙」遂署為賓僚。 數謂參佐曰：「此丹秦皇、漢武皆不能得，唯我遇之，他年朝上清，不復偶公輩矣。 」復夢駕鶴衝天，寤而刻木鶴、衣道士衣以習乘之。 凡服丹二萬丸，腹堅不食，將死，不知人者數日矣。 道士牛洞玄以豬肪谷漆下之，殆盡。 病少間，季長復曰：「垂上仙，何自棄也！ 」益服三千丸，頃之卒。 初，抱真久疾，好禨祥，或令厭勝，為巫祝所惑，請降官爵以禳除之。 是年，凡七上章讓司空，復為檢校左僕射。 貞元十年卒，時年六十二，廢朝三日，贈太保，賻以布帛米粟有差。
Baozhen was shrewd and resolute. He sought to gather talented men from across the empire; whenever he heard of someone’s merit, he sent agents with gifts thousands of li to fetch them; but if conversation proved unrewarding, he quietly let them go. When the realm was at peace he built pavilions and dug ponds for his amusement. In his later years he turned to Daoist adepts, hoping for immortality. A man named Sun Jichang prepared elixir pills for him and promised ascension if he took them; Baozhen made him a personal adviser. He often told his staff, "Even the First Emperor and Han Wudi never obtained this elixir—only I have. One day I shall ascend to the heavens and leave you behind. " He dreamed of riding a crane to heaven, then carved a wooden crane, donned Daoist robes, and practiced mounting it. He swallowed twenty thousand pills until his belly hardened and he stopped eating; for days before death he recognized no one. The Daoist Niu Dongxuan purged him with pig fat and grain lacquer until he was nearly dead. When he briefly recovered, Jichang said, "You are on the verge of immortality—why throw that away! " Baozhen took three thousand more pills and soon died. Earlier, during his long illness, he sought omens and exorcisms, was misled by shamans, and petitioned to reduce his rank to avert calamity. That year he seven times petitioned to resign as Minister of Works and was restored as Acting Left Vice Director. He died in Zhenyuan 10 at sixty-two. Court was suspended for three days; he was posthumously named Grand Tutor, and graded gifts of cloth, silk, rice, and grain were sent.

Cheng went to Zhengzhou; the court named Qing Acting Crown Prince Guest and Vice Censor-in-chief, changing his name to Kening.

貞元元年三月，就加澄檢校左僕射、義成軍鄭滑許等州節度使。 二年卒，年五十四，廢朝一日，贈司空，賵布帛粟有差，仍令左散騎常侍歸崇敬充弔祭使，所緣喪葬，並勒官給。 澄實以八月癸未終，克寧秘之，以九月庚寅，欲自起視事。 其行軍司馬馬鉉不許，克寧陰遣殺之，乃墨絰而出，加卒於城門，將為不順。 劉洽出師屯於境上以制之，且使告諭切至，由是克寧不敢妄發，然道路絕商旅者凡十四五日。 及賈躭代澄，克寧護喪將歸，乃悉索府中財貨，以夜出城，軍人從而剽奪，及明殆盡。 澄柩至京師，又賜克寧莊一所、錢千貫、粟麥二千石。 澄初封隴西郡公，進武威郡王，每上疏連稱二封，頗為時人所哂。 李元素李元素，字大朴，蒲山公密之孫。 任侍御史，時杜亞為東都留守，惡大將令狐運，會盜發洛城之北，運適與其部下畋於北郊，亞意其為盜，遂執訊之，逮系者四十餘人。 監察御史楊寧按其事，亞以為不直，密表陳之，寧遂得罪。 亞將逞其宿怒，且以得賊為功，上表指明運為盜之狀，上信而不疑。 宰臣以獄大宜審，奏請覆之，命元素就決，亞迎路以獄成告。 元素驗之五日，盡釋其囚以還。 亞大驚，且怒，親追送，馬上責之，元素不答。 亞遂上疏，又誣元素。 元素還奏，言未畢，上怒曰：「出俟命。 」元素曰：「臣未盡詞。 」上又曰：「且去」。 元素復奏曰：「一出不得復見陛下，乞容盡詞。 」上意稍緩，元素盡言運冤狀明白，上乃寤曰：「非卿，孰能辨之！ 」後數月，竟得其真賊，元素由是為時器重，遷給事中。 時美官缺，必指元素。 遷尚書右丞。 數月，鄭滑節度盧群卒，遂命元素兼御史大夫，鎮鄭滑，就加檢校工部尚書，在鎮稱理。
In the third month of Zhenyuan 1 Cheng was named Acting Left Vice Director and military commissioner of Yicheng and the Zheng, Hua, and Xu circuits. He died in the second year at fifty-four. Court halted for a day; he was posthumously named Minister of Works, with graded gifts of cloth, silk, and grain. Gui Jingchong was sent to mourn him, and all funeral costs were paid by the state. Cheng had actually died on the guimwei day of the eighth month; Kening kept it secret and on the gengyin day of the ninth month tried to take command himself. Campaign vice director Ma Xuan refused; Kening had him murdered secretly, donned mourning, posted extra guards at the gates, and prepared to rebel. Liu Qia camped on the border to restrain him and sent stern warnings; Kening dared not move, but travel was cut off for fifteen days. When Jia Dan succeeded Cheng, Kening escorted the coffin home, stripped the treasury by night, and soldiers looted until dawn left little behind. When Cheng’s coffin reached the capital, Kening was still granted an estate, a thousand strings of cash, and two thousand shi of grain. Cheng was first made Duke of Longxi, then Prince of Wuwei, and in memorials he cited both titles—a habit people mocked. Li Yuan Su, courtesy name Dapu, was a grandson of Li Mi, Duke of Pushan. As an attendant censor, he served under Du Ya, regent of the eastern capital, who hated general Linghu Yun. When bandits struck north of Luoyang, Yun was hunting nearby; Du Ya arrested him and more than forty others. Investigating censor Yang Ning reviewed the case; Du Ya thought him biased, secretly denounced him, and Yang Ning was punished. Du Ya vented his old grudge and claimed credit for catching the bandits, memorializing that Yun was the culprit; the emperor believed him. The chancellors urged a review; Yuan Su was sent to decide. Du Ya met him on the road and said the case was already closed. Yuan Su investigated for five days and released every prisoner. Du Ya was shocked and furious, rode after him reproaching him from horseback; Yuan Su did not answer. Du Ya memorialized again, slandering Yuan Su. Yuan Su reported back; before he finished, the emperor snapped, "Leave and await my orders. " Yuan Su said, "I have not finished, Your Majesty. " The emperor said again, "Go now." Yuan Su pleaded, "If I leave now I may never see Your Majesty again—let me finish. " The emperor relented; Yuan Su laid out Yun’s innocence clearly, and the emperor exclaimed, "Without you, who could have seen this! " Months later the real culprits were caught, and Yuan Su won wide esteem and promotion to supervising secretary. Whenever a prestigious post opened, Yuan Su was the expected choice. He became Right Assistant Director of the Ministry of State. When Lu Qun of Zheng-Hua died, Yuan Su was named Censor Grandee and commissioner, with Acting Minister of Works, and governed well.
18
元和初，征拜御史大夫。 自貞元中位缺，久難其人，至是元素以名望召拜，中外聳聽。 及居位，一無修舉，但規求作相。 久之，浸不得志，見客必曰：「無以某官散相疏也。 」見屬官必先拜，脂韋在列，大失人情。 李錡為亂江南，遂授元素浙西道節度觀察處置等使。 數月受代，入拜國子祭酒，尋遷太常卿，轉戶部尚書、判度支。
Early in Yuanhe he was summoned to the post of Censor Grandee. The post had been vacant since Zhenyuan; Yuan Su’s appointment by reputation drew attention throughout the court and realm. In office he accomplished nothing and only schemed to become chancellor. As time passed he grew frustrated and told every visitor, "Do not shun me because I am only a chancellor in name. " He bowed first to his own subordinates and kept sycophants about him—alienating everyone. When Li Qi rebelled in the south, Yuan Su was made military commissioner of the Zhexi circuit. After a few months he was replaced, became Director of the Imperial University, then Grand Music Director, then Minister of Revenue with charge of the Finance Commission.
19
元素少孤，奉長姊友敬加於人，及其姊歿，沉悲遘疾，上疏懇辭，從之。 數月，以出妻免官。 初，元素再娶妻王氏，石泉公方慶之孫，性柔弱，元素為郎官時娶之，甚禮重，及貴，溺情僕妾，遂薄之。 且又無子，而前妻之子已長，無良，元素寢疾昏惑，聽譖遂出之，給與非厚。 妻族上訴，乃詔曰：「李元素病中上表，懇切披陳，雲『妻王氏，禮義殊乖，願與離絕』。 初謂素有醜行，不能顯言，以其大官之家，所以令自處置。 訪聞不曾告報妻族，亦無明過可書，蓋是中情不和，遂至於此。 脅以王命，當日遣歸，給送之間，又至單薄。 不唯王氏受辱，實亦朝情悉驚。 如此理家，合當懲責。 宜停官，仍令與王氏錢物，通所奏數滿五千貫。 」元和五年卒，贈陝州大都督。 【贊】史臣曰：李抱玉、李抱真，以武勇之材，兼忠義之行，有唐之良將也。 且如農隙教潞人之射，數騎入武俊之營，非有奇謀，孰能如是。 惜乎服食求仙，為藥所誤。 王虔休不黨僭命，有足可嘉； 盧從史動多懷奸，自貽伊戚。 芃則老也知足，澄則過而改圖。 元素為御史時，執德不回； 居大夫日，其心甚短。 因緣七出，益露醜聲，善少惡多，又何足算。
Orphaned young, he revered his elder sister; when she died he fell ill with grief, petitioned to resign, and was allowed to withdraw. Months later he was dismissed for divorcing his wife. He had remarried Lady Wang, granddaughter of Fang Qing, while still a junior official and treated her well; once powerful he favored concubines and neglected her. She bore no children; his grown son by a former wife was worthless. Ill and confused, he heeded slander, divorced her, and gave her a meager settlement. Her family appealed to the throne, which declared: "Li Yuan Su, while ill, petitioned that his wife Wang had violated ritual and he wished to divorce her. We first assumed she had long misbehaved and he could not say so openly; as she came from a great official family, he was allowed to handle it privately. Inquiry showed he never informed her family and no clear fault was found—only mutual estrangement brought this about. Under imperial pressure she was sent home the same day with a paltry settlement. Lady Wang was shamed, and court opinion was shocked. Such household conduct deserved punishment. He is dismissed from office and ordered to pay Lady Wang five thousand strings in all, as petitioned. " He died in Yuanhe 5 and was posthumously named Grand Governor of Shanzhou. [Appraisal] The historiographer writes: Li Baoyu and Li Baozhen combined martial courage with loyal service—they were among the Tang’s finest generals. Training Lu archers in farming seasons, or riding into Wang Wujun’s camp with a handful of men—without exceptional daring, who could do such things? Alas that Baozhen’s quest for immortality through elixirs destroyed him. Wang Qianxiu refused to side with usurpers—praiseworthy; Lu Congshi’s treachery brought ruin on himself. Peng in old age knew when to be content; Cheng erred, then changed course. As a censor Yuan Su held firm to principle; as Censor Grandee his ambitions proved petty. His divorce on the seven grounds exposed his shame; virtues were few and faults many—little remains to his credit.
20
贊曰：抱玉、抱真，我朝良將。 虔休之心，亦多可尚。 史懷奸謀，芃將祿讓。 澄迷卻行，素貪一響。 吾誰與欺，豈如忠諒。
Praise: Baoyu and Baozhen—fine generals of our dynasty. Qianxiu’s loyalty also deserves esteem. Congshi plotted treason; Peng would yield his post and pay. Cheng wavered, then turned back—ever greedy for renown. Whom would we deceive—better loyal candor every time.
