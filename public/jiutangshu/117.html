1
苗晉卿裴冕裴遵慶子向向子寅寅子樞
Miao Jingqing; Pei Mian; Pei Zunqing; Pei Xiang, son of Zunqing; Pei Yin, son of Xiang; and Pei Shu, son of Yin.
2
苗晉卿，上黨壺關人。 世以儒素稱。 祖夔，高道不仕，追贈禮部尚書。 父殆庶，官至絳州龍門縣丞，早卒，以晉卿贈太子少保。
Miao Jingqing came from Huguan in Shangdang. His family had long been esteemed for unadorned Confucian scholarship. His grandfather Kui lived in reclusion as a man of high principle and was posthumously appointed Minister of Rites. His father Daisu served as assistant magistrate of Longmen in Jiang Prefecture and died young; Jingqing's honors later brought him a posthumous appointment as Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent.
3
晉卿幼好學，善屬文，進士擢第。 初授懷州修武縣尉，歷奉先縣尉，坐累貶徐州司戶參軍。 秩滿隨調，判入高等，授萬年縣尉。 遷侍御史，歷度支、兵、吏部三員外郎。 開元二十三年，遷吏部郎中。 二十四年，與吏部郎中孫逖並拜中書舍人。 二十七年，以本官權知吏部選事。 晉卿性謙柔，選人有訴訟索好官者，雖至數千言，或聲色甚厲者，晉卿必含容之，略無慍色。 二十九年，拜吏部侍郎。 前後典選五年，政既寬弛，胥吏多因緣為奸，賄賂大行。 時天下承平，每年赴選常萬余人。 李林甫為尚書，專任廟堂，銓事唯委晉卿及同列侍郎宋遙主之。 選人既多，每年兼命他官有識者同考定書判，務求其實。 天寶二年春，御史中丞張倚男奭參選，晉卿與遙以倚初承恩，欲悅附之，考選人判等凡六十四人，分甲乙丙科，奭在其首。 眾知奭不讀書，論議紛然。 有蘇孝慍者，嘗為范陽薊令，事安祿山，具其事告之。 祿山恩寵特異，謁見不常，因而奏之。 玄宗大集登科人，禦花萼樓親試，登第者十無一二； 而奭手持試紙，竟日不下一字，時謂之「曳白」。 上怒，晉卿貶為安康郡太守，遙為武當郡太守，張倚為淮陽太守。 敕曰：「門庭之間，不能訓子； 選調之際，仍以托人。 」時士子皆以為戲笑。
From boyhood Jingqing loved study, wrote well, and earned his jinshi degree. He began as county lieutenant of Xiuwu in Huai Prefecture, later served at Fengxian, and was demoted to registrar in Xu Prefecture after being implicated in a case. At the end of his term he entered the regular reassignment cycle, ranked in the top tier, and received an appointment as county lieutenant of Wannian. He rose to attending censor and then held vice-director posts in the revenue, war, and personnel bureaus in turn. In Kaiyuan 23 he was made a director in the Ministry of Personnel. The next year he and Sun Su of the personnel directorate were both appointed drafting attendants at the Secretariat. In Kaiyuan 27 he was placed in provisional charge of civil-service selections while keeping his existing rank. Jingqing was gentle by nature. Candidates who came to argue their cases and demand favorable appointments might rant for thousands of words or raise their voices in fury, yet he always received them patiently and never showed irritation. In Kaiyuan 29 he was appointed vice minister of personnel. He oversaw selections for five years in all. His rule was lenient, clerks seized every chance to cheat, and bribery ran rampant. The empire was at peace, and each year well over ten thousand candidates presented themselves for selection. Li Linfu held the ministry portfolio and dominated the court, leaving selection affairs entirely to Jingqing and his colleague Song Yao. With so many candidates, each year additional knowledgeable officials were enlisted to grade the written examinations, aiming at honest results. In the spring of Tianbao 2, Zhang Yi's son Yi entered the civil-service examinations. Jingqing and Yao, eager to court the newly favored censor-in-chief, ranked sixty-four candidates in three tiers and placed Yi at the top. Everyone knew Yi was unlettered, and public outcry spread. A man named Su Xiaoyin, formerly magistrate of Ji in Fanyang and now in An Lushan's service, reported the whole affair to him in detail. Lushan enjoyed extraordinary favor and was rarely granted audience, so he brought the matter up when he could. Emperor Xuanzong summoned every newly passed candidate to the Hua'e Tower for a personal examination; fewer than one in ten of those already listed as graduates could pass; Yi held his examination paper all day without writing a word, and people nicknamed him 'the blank scroll.' The emperor was furious. Jingqing was demoted to prefect of Ankang, Yao to prefect of Wudang, and Zhang Yi to prefect of Huaiyang. An edict declared: 'You could not discipline your son within your own household; yet at selection time you still used your office to pull strings for others.' Scholars everywhere treated the edict as a public joke.
4
天寶三載閏二月，轉魏郡太守，充河北采訪處置使，居職三年，政化洽聞。 會入計，因上表請歸鄉里。 既至壺關，望縣門而步。 小吏進曰：「太守位高德重，不宜自輕。 」晉卿曰：「《禮》：『下公門，式路馬。 』況父母之邦，所宜尊敬。 汝何言哉！ 」大會鄉黨，歡飲累日而去。 又俸錢三萬為鄉學本，以教授子弟。 尋改河東太守、河東采訪使，入為尚書、東京留守，征為憲部尚書。 屬祿山叛逆，楊國忠以晉卿有時望，將抑之，乃奏云：「宜以大臣鎮東道。 」遂出為陜州刺史、陜虢兩州防禦使。 及入對，固辭老病，由是忤旨，改憲部尚書致仕。 及朝廷失守，衣冠流離道路，多為逆黨所脅，自陳希烈、張均已下數十人盡赴洛陽，晉卿潛遁山谷，南投金州。 會肅宗至鳳翔，手詔追晉卿赴行在，即日拜為左相，軍國大務悉以咨之。 既收兩京，以功封韓國公，食實封五百戶，改為侍中。 後以賊寇漸除，屢乞骸骨，優詔許之，罷知政事，為太子太傅。 明年，帝思舊臣，復拜為侍中。
In the intercalary second month of Tianbao 3 he became prefect of Wei and Hebei investigation commissioner. After three years his administration was renowned for its harmony. On coming to court for the annual review he memorialized asking to return home. When he reached Huguan he walked on foot toward the county gate. A clerk stepped forward and said, 'Your rank and standing are too high for such self-abasement.' Jingqing replied, 'The Rites says that when one leaves a nobleman's gate one should salute the horses on the road.' 'How much more should one show respect in the land of one's parents.' What sort of talk is that! He feasted with his kinsmen and neighbors for days before taking his leave. He also endowed the local school with thirty thousand cash from his salary to educate the young men of the district. He was soon made prefect and investigation commissioner of Hedong, then recalled to serve as a minister and intendant of the Eastern Capital before being appointed minister of justice. When Lushan rose in rebellion, Yang Guozhong, wishing to curb Jingqing's popular prestige, memorialized that a senior minister should be sent to secure the eastern circuit. Jingqing was accordingly sent out as prefect of Shaan and defense commissioner for Shaan and Guo. At audience he firmly pleaded age and illness, offended the throne, and was retired from his post as minister of justice. After the capital fell, court officials scattered along the roads, many forced into rebel service. Dozens from Chen Xilie and Zhang Jun down went to Luoyang, while Jingqing hid in the hills and fled south to Jin Prefecture. When Suzong reached Fengxiang he summoned Jingqing by autograph letter. That same day Jingqing was made left chancellor, and all major civil and military affairs were referred to him. After the two capitals were recovered he was enfeoffed as Duke of Han with five hundred taxable households and appointed palace attendant. As the rebels were gradually suppressed he repeatedly asked to retire. The throne graciously agreed, relieved him of administrative duties, and made him grand tutor of the heir apparent. The following year the emperor recalled his veteran ministers and again appointed him palace attendant.
5
晉卿寬厚廉謹，為政舉大綱，不問小過，所到有惠化。 魏人思之，為立碑頌德。 及秉鈞衡，小心畏慎，未嘗忤人意。 性聰敏，達練事體，百司文簿，經目必曉，而修身守位，以智自全，議者比漢之胡廣。
Jingqing was magnanimous, upright, and prudent. In office he kept to the broad principles, overlooked petty faults, and left benevolent rule wherever he served. The people of Wei cherished his memory and raised a stele in praise of his virtue. Once he held the helm of state he was cautious and deferential and never gave offense. Clever and experienced, he grasped the substance of government at a glance; no document from any bureau escaped his understanding. Yet he cultivated himself and kept his post by prudence, and critics compared him to Hu Guang of the Han.
6
玄宗崩，肅宗詔晉卿攝冢宰。 上表固辭曰：「臣聞古者殷高宗在諒闇之中，百官聽於冢宰，更無事跡，但存文字。 且一時之事，禮不相沿。 今殘寇猶虞，日殷萬務，皆緣兵馬屯守討襲，善算良謀，立勝擒敵。 陛下若行古之道，居喪不言，蒼生何依，百事皆廢。 伏讀國家起居註，亦於禮部檢見舊敕，恭惟太宗、高宗、、大行皇帝在位之日，皆有國哀，視事不輟，以為君臨天下，難徇常情。 今遺詔有處分，皇帝宜三日而聽政。 陛下遵太宗故事，則無冢宰； 遵大行皇帝遺詔，便合聽朝。 萬姓颙颙，不勝大願。 伏惟陛下知理國之重，順人心之切，以義斷恩，從宜無改。 今朝臣一命已上，皆言臣心昏貌朽，加以疾病，事有急速，斷在須臾，凡聖不同，豈合受詔。 陛下發哀已五日矣，願準遺詔聽政，則四夷萬國，無任悲幸。 」肅宗時疾彌留，覽表殞絕，乃許。
When Xuanzong died, Suzong ordered Jingqing to serve as regent during the mourning. He memorialized a firm refusal: 'I have read that in antiquity, when King Gaozong of Yin mourned in seclusion, the hundred officials took orders from the regent; beyond that we have only written records, not living precedent.' That was a single historical moment; ritual does not simply carry forward unchanged.' Remnant rebels still threaten us, and a thousand urgent matters press in daily—all depending on armies in camp, on garrison and pursuit, on sound strategy to win at once and take the foe.' If Your Majesty followed the ancient way and held court silence in mourning, what would the people lean on? Every affair of state would stall.' I have read the court diaries and old edicts preserved in the Ministry of Rites. Respectfully considered, Taizong, Gaozong, their successors, and the late emperor all continued to transact state business during periods of national mourning, judging that one who rules the realm cannot indulge ordinary private feeling.' The late emperor's testament directs that the new emperor should assume government after three days.' If Your Majesty follows Taizong's precedent, no regent is needed; if Your Majesty follows the late emperor's testament, you should take court immediately.' The people look up in eager hope beyond what words can express.' May Your Majesty see how heavy is the burden of rule, how urgent the people's need, cut private grief by public duty, and hold to what expedience requires.' Every minister of the first rank and above says my mind is dim, my body worn out, and my illnesses many; when urgent affairs must be decided in a moment, how could a common man accept such a charge as a sage might?' Your Majesty has already mourned five days. If you would follow the testament and take up government, the four quarters and all nations would be overwhelmed with grief and joy alike.' Suzong was then gravely ill. Reading the memorial he nearly fainted, then consented.
7
數日，肅宗晏駕，代宗踐祚，又詔晉卿攝冢宰。 晉卿上表懇辭曰：「臣以昔者天子居喪之時，百官聽於冢宰者，蓋君幼小，禦極事殷，情理當然。 沿革不一，今古異同，而周武、漢文，合於通變，垂範作則，可舉而行。 又士或墨缞，時遇金革，豈非銜恤，謂義在斷恩。 且百善之至，無加於孝也，其有容瘁心絕，指景悼生，此匹夫守節之常情，殊王者嗣續之大計。 昨二十日，陛下於大行皇帝柩前即位，是承先帝遺顧之言，亦前代不易之典。 則知所略不為害，所存是適權，防威滅端，所利者大。 陛下因心純至，天地明察。 伏以報劬勞之恩，申罔極之思，終身之痛，豈計朝夕！ 但以一日之內，萬務在中，須達宸聰，始成國政。 百僚萬姓及僧道耆壽等，相顧聚言，以臣老且無能，愚豈測聖，況久無居攝，臣不敢奉詔。 特乞陛下遵遺命，三日而政。 臣博聽眾情，不勝懇願，伏望割痛抑哀，則天下悲幸。 」上號泣從之。 時晉卿年已衰暮，又患兩足，上特許肩輿至中書，入閣不趨，累日一視事。 歷三朝，皆以謹密見稱。
Within days Suzong died and Daizong succeeded. Again Jingqing was ordered to serve as mourning regent. Jingqing memorialized again in earnest refusal: 'In former times, when a young emperor mourned and officials took orders from a regent, the ruler was a child and the throne's burdens pressing—such an arrangement was only natural.' Customs change with the ages, yet King Wu of Zhou and Emperor Wen of Han adapted wisely and left models we may still follow.' Scholars too may wear mourning garb yet take up arms when the state is threatened—grief held in the heart while duty cuts private ties.' Of all virtues none ranks above filial piety; yet to waste away in grief and mourn one's own life is the way of a private man keeping faith, not the great design of a king who must continue the line.' Only twenty days ago Your Majesty took the throne before the late emperor's bier, fulfilling his final charge and following an immemorial precedent.' What was set aside did no harm; what was kept served urgent need—forestalling peril at its root brings the greater good.' Your Majesty's heart is utterly sincere—Heaven and Earth themselves perceive it.' To repay a parent's labor, to express boundless grief—a lifetime's mourning cannot be measured by days and nights alone!' Yet within a single day ten thousand affairs press upon the throne; only when they reach Your Majesty's ear can government proceed.' Officials, common people, monks, Daoists, and elders all say among themselves that I am old and incompetent, too dull to serve a sage emperor, and that regency has long been obsolete—I dare not accept the charge.' I beg Your Majesty only to follow the testament and assume government after three days.' Having heard the people's voice everywhere, I cannot contain my plea: may Your Majesty cut short private grief for public duty, and the realm will be overwhelmed with sorrow and relief alike.' The emperor wept aloud and assented. Jingqing was by then elderly and lame in both legs. The emperor allowed him a sedan chair to the Secretariat, excused him from hurrying in the halls, and let him conduct business only every few days. Across three reigns he was praised for discretion and reliability.
8
裴冕，河東人也，為河東冠族。 天寶初，以門廕再遷渭南縣尉，以吏道聞。 御史中丞王鉷充京畿采訪使，表為判官。 遷監察御史，歷殿中侍御史。 冕雖無學術，守職通明，果於臨事，鉷甚委之。 及鉷得罪伏法，時宰臣李林甫方竊權柄，人鹹懼之，鉷賓佐數百，不敢窺鉷門。 冕獨收鉷屍，親自護喪，瘞於近郊，冕自是知名。 河西節度使哥舒翰表為行軍司馬，累遷員外郎中。
Pei Mian came from Hedong and belonged to its foremost clan. Early in the Tianbao era he rose through hereditary privilege to county lieutenant of Weinan and won a reputation for administrative skill. When Censor-in-Chief Wang Hong became investigation commissioner for the capital region he recommended Mian as his aide. He rose to investigating censor and then served as palace attending censor. Though without scholarly training, Mian was lucid in office and resolute in action, and Hong relied on him heavily. When Hong was condemned and executed, Li Linfu was seizing power and everyone feared him. None of Hong's hundreds of retainers dared even approach his gate. Mian alone recovered Hong's body, conducted the funeral himself, and buried him in the suburbs. From that day his name was known. Geshu Han, commissioner of Hexi, recommended him as campaign marshal, and he rose to vice director.
9
玄宗幸蜀，至益昌郡，遙詔太子充天下兵馬元帥，以冕為御史中丞兼左庶子，為之副。 是時，冕為河西行軍司馬，授御史中丞，詔赴朝廷。 遇太子於平涼，具陳事勢，勸之朔方，亟入靈武。 冕與杜鴻漸、崔漪等勸進曰：「主上厭勤大位，南幸蜀川，宗社神器，須有所歸，天意人事，不可固違。 若逡巡退讓，失億兆心，則大事去矣！ 臣等猶知之，況賢智乎！ 」太子曰：「南平寇逆，奉迎鑾輿，退居儲貳，侍膳左右，豈不樂哉！ 公等何言之過也？ 」冕與杜鴻漸又進曰：「殿下藉累聖之資，有天下之表。 元貞萬國，二十余年，殷憂啟聖，正在今日。 所從殿下六軍將士，皆關輔百姓，日夜思歸。 大軍一散，不可復集，不如因而撫之以從眾，臣等敢以死請。 」凡勸進五上，乃依。 肅宗即位，以定策功，遷中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事，倚以為政。
When Xuanzong fled to Shu and reached Yichang, he issued a distant edict making the crown prince commander of all armies under heaven, with Mian as censor-in-chief and left guardian of the heir as his deputy. Mian was then campaign marshal in Hexi. Appointed censor-in-chief, he was ordered to court. He met the crown prince at Pingliang, explained the situation fully, urged him toward Shuofang, and pressed him to enter Lingwu at once. Mian, Du Hongjian, Cui Yi, and others urged him to take the throne: 'The emperor has wearied of rule and gone south to Shu. The altars of state must have a lord. Heaven's will and men's hearts cannot be defied.' If you hang back in false modesty and lose the people's hearts, the cause is lost!' Even we understand this—how much more men of talent and wisdom!' The crown prince replied: 'To crush the rebels in the south, welcome back the imperial carriage, and then serve at my father's side as heir—what could be happier?' Why do you speak so extravagantly?' Mian and Du Hongjian pressed again: 'Your Highness inherits the virtue of many sage emperors and bears the countenance of one destined to rule the realm.' For more than twenty years the dynasty has held the realm in rectitude. Deep sorrow now summons a sage ruler—today is the day.' The soldiers who follow you are common men of the capital region who long day and night to return home.' Once the army scatters it cannot be reassembled. Better win them now by following their will. We beg this even at the cost of our lives.' They pressed him five times before he finally agreed. When Suzong ascended the throne, Mian was rewarded for helping fix the succession with a vice directorship at the Secretariat and a seat on the privy council, and the emperor relied on him in government.
10
冕性忠勤，悉心奉公，稍得人心。 然不識大體，以聚人曰財，乃下令賣官鬻爵，度尼僧道士，以儲積為務。 人不願者，科令就之，其價益賤，事轉為弊。 肅宗移幸鳳翔，罷冕知政事，遷右僕射。 兩京平，以功封冀國公，食實封五百戶。 尋加御史大夫、成都尹，充劍南西川節度使。 又入為右僕射。 永泰元年，與裴遵慶等並集賢待制。 代宗求舊，拜冕兼御史大夫，充護山陵使。 冕以幸臣李輔國權盛，將附之，乃表輔國親昵術士中書舍人劉烜充山陵使判官。 烜坐法，冕坐貶施州刺史。 數月，移澧州刺史，復征為左僕射。 元載秉政。 載為新平縣尉，王鉷辟在巡內，冕常引之，載頗德冕。 會宰臣杜鴻漸卒，載遂舉冕代之。 冕時已衰瘵，載以其順己，引為同列。 受命之際，蹈舞絕倒，載趨而扶起，代為謝詞。 冕兼掌兵權留守之任，俸錢每月二千余貫。 性本侈靡，好尚車服及營珍饌，名馬在櫪，直數百金者常十數。 每會賓友，滋味品數，坐客有昧於名者。 自創巾子，其狀新奇，市肆因而效之，呼為「僕射樣」。 初代鴻漸，小吏以俸錢文簿白之，冕顧子弟，喜見於色，其嗜利若此。 拜職未盈月，卒，大歷四年十二月也。 上悼之，輟朝三日，贈太尉，賻制五百匹、粟五百石。
Mian was loyal and diligent, devoted himself wholly to public service, and gradually won people's trust. But he lacked a statesman's sense of proportion. Believing that amassing people meant amassing wealth, he ordered offices and titles sold, ordained nuns, monks, and Daoists, and made hoarding his chief aim. Those who resisted were forced by regulation to comply. Prices kept falling, and the policy became a public scandal. When Suzong moved to Fengxiang, Mian was removed from the privy council and made right vice director. After the two capitals were recovered he was enfeoffed as Duke of Ji with five hundred taxable households. He was soon made censor-in-chief and prefect of Chengdu, and appointed military commissioner of western Sichuan. He later returned to court as right vice director. In Yongtai 1 he and Pei Zunqing and others were made attendant draftsmen at the Hall of Assembled Worthies. Daizong sought veteran ministers and appointed Mian censor-in-chief and commissioner for guarding the imperial tombs. Seeing that the favorite Li Fuguo was all-powerful, Mian sought to court him and recommended Fuguo's confidant Liu Xuan, a drafting attendant skilled in occult arts, as aide to the tomb commission. Liu Xuan was punished by law, and Mian was demoted to prefect of Shi. Months later he was moved to Li Prefecture, then recalled as left vice director. Yuan Zai now dominated the government. Zai had once been county lieutenant of Xinping. When Wang Hong recruited him for his staff, Mian often promoted him, and Zai felt deep gratitude. When chief minister Du Hongjian died, Zai nominated Mian to replace him. Mian was already frail with chronic illness, but Zai valued his compliance and brought him onto the council. When he received the appointment he danced himself into a faint. Zai rushed to lift him and spoke the thanks on his behalf. Mian also held military authority and the capital intendant's post, with a monthly salary of more than two thousand strings of cash. By nature he was extravagant, fond of fine carriages and robes and of rare delicacies. A dozen famous horses worth hundreds of gold each stood in his stable. At his banquets the dishes were so numerous and exotic that guests often could not name what they were eating. He invented a novel style of headcloth that shops copied and called 'the Vice Director's style.' When he first replaced Hongjian, a clerk presented his salary accounts. Mian glanced at his sons and nephews with open delight—such was his love of gain. He died less than a month after taking office, in the twelfth month of Dali 4. The emperor mourned him, suspended court for three days, posthumously appointed him Grand Commandant, and granted five hundred bolts of silk and five hundred piculs of grain for the funeral.
11
裴遵慶，絳州聞喜人也。 代襲冠冕，為河東著族。 遵慶志氣深厚，機鑒敏達，自幼強學，博涉載籍，謹身晦跡，不幹當世之務。 以門廕累授潞府司法參軍，時年已老，未為人所知。 隨調吏部，授大理寺丞，剖斷刑獄，舉正綱條，理行始著。 遷司門員外、吏部員外郎，專判南曹。 天寶中，海內無事，九流輻輳會府，每歲吏部選人，動盈萬數。 遵慶敏識強記，精核文簿，詳而不滯，時稱吏事第一，由是大知名。
Pei Zunqing came from Wenxi in Jiang Prefecture. His family had held high rank for generations and was one of Hedong's great clans. Zunqing was deep-spirited and keen-witted. From youth he studied hard and read widely, kept a low profile, and avoided meddling in public affairs. Through hereditary privilege he rose to legal administrator in Lu Prefecture, but he was already elderly and still little known. Entering the personnel ministry for reassignment, he was made assistant director of the Court of Judicial Review. His handling of criminal cases and enforcement of statutes first won notice. He became vice director of the gate office and of the personnel ministry, specializing in the Southern Bureau. During the Tianbao era the empire was at peace and candidates of every sort thronged the capital. Each year's personnel selections regularly drew more than ten thousand men. Zunqing was quick-witted with a formidable memory, scrutinized documents with precision, and was thorough without being slow. He was hailed as the finest administrator of his day and became widely famous.
12
天寶末，楊國忠當國，出不附己者例為外官，遵慶亦出為郡守。 肅宗即位，征拜給事中、尚書右丞、吏部侍郎。 恭儉克己，遲重謹密，頗有時望。 上元中，蕭華輔政，素知遵慶，每奏見，累稱之，遷黃門侍郎、同中書門下平章事。 廣德初，仆固懷恩阻兵汾上，指中官為詞，上以遵慶忠純，特遣往汾州宣慰懷恩。 遵慶既見懷恩，具陳朝旨，懷恩引過聽命，將隨遵慶朝謁，為副將範誌誠以邪說惑之，懷恩遂以懼死為詞。 會蕃寇陷京師，乘輿幸陜，遵慶自汾州奔赴行在。 及乘輿還京，以遵慶為太子少傅。 永泰元年，與裴冕等並於集賢院待制，罷知政事。 尋改吏部尚書、右僕射，復知選事。 時選人天興縣尉陳琯於銓庭言詞不遜，淩突無禮，代宗詔付遵慶於省門鞭三十，貶為吉州員外司戶參軍。 遵慶敦守儒行，老而彌謹。 嘗為風狂族侄撾登聞鼓告以不順，上知遵度，不省，其見信如此。 大歷十年十月薨於位，年九十余。
At the end of Tianbao, Yang Guozhong dominated the court and routinely posted opponents to provincial office. Zunqing too was sent out as a prefect. When Suzong ascended the throne, Zunqing was summoned as drafting attendant, right assistant in the secretariat, and vice minister of personnel. Respectful, frugal, and self-controlled, deliberate and discreet, he enjoyed wide esteem. During the Shangyuan era, chief minister Xiao Hua, who had long known Zunqing, praised him repeatedly at audience. Zunqing was made vice director of the palace gate and a privy councilor. Early in Guangde, Pugu Huai'en held troops on the Fen River and blamed the eunuchs. The emperor, trusting Zunqing's loyalty, sent him to Fen Prefecture to reassure Huai'en. When Zunqing met Huai'en he explained the court's intent. Huai'en admitted fault and agreed to obey, and was about to accompany him to court when his deputy Fan Zhicheng swayed him with sinister counsel. Huai'en then pleaded that he feared execution. When Tibetan forces took the capital and the emperor fled to Shaan, Zunqing hurried from Fen Prefecture to the mobile court. When the emperor returned to the capital, Zunqing was appointed junior tutor of the heir apparent. In Yongtai 1 he and Pei Mian and others were made draftsmen at the Hall of Assembled Worthies and left the privy council. He was soon made minister of personnel and right vice director and again oversaw civil-service selections. When a candidate named Chen Guan, county lieutenant of Tianxing, spoke insolently at the selection courtyard, Daizong ordered Zunqing to have him flogged thirty strokes at the ministry gate and demoted him to acting registrar in Ji Prefecture. Zunqing steadfastly upheld Confucian conduct and grew more cautious with age. Once a deranged kinsman beat the petition drum and accused him of disloyalty. The emperor knew Zunqing's character and paid no heed—such was the trust he enjoyed. He died in office in the tenth month of Dali 10, aged more than ninety.
13
遵慶初登省郎，嘗著《王政記》，述今古禮體，識者覽之，知有公輔之量。
Early in his career as a capital bureau officer he wrote Records of Royal Government on ancient and modern ritual. Readers who understood such matters saw in it the makings of a chief minister.
14
子向，字傃仁，少以門廕歷官至太子司議郎。 建中初，李紓為同州刺史，奏向為從事。 硃泚反，李懷光又叛河中，使其將趙貴先築壘於同州，紓來奔奉天，向領州務。 貴先因脅縣尉林寶役徒板築，不及期，將斬之，吏人百姓奔竄。 向即詣貴先軍壘，以逆順之理責之，貴先感悟，遂來降，故同州不陷。 向由是知名。 累為京兆府戶曹，轉櫟陽、渭南縣令，奏課皆第一，朝廷亟聞其理行，擢為戶部員外郎。
His son Xiang, courtesy name Surén, rose through hereditary privilege to master of protocol for the heir apparent. Early in the Jianzhong era, when Li Shu was prefect of Tong, he recommended Xiang as his staff officer. When Zhu Ci rebelled and Li Huai'guang rose in Hezhong, Huai'guang's general Zhao Guixian built fortifications at Tong. Shu fled to Fengtian, and Xiang took charge of the prefecture. Guixian forced the county lieutenant Lin Bao to press laborers into construction work. When the deadline was missed he was about to execute Lin, and officials and commoners fled in terror. Xiang went at once to Guixian's camp and rebuked him with the logic of loyalty and rebellion. Guixian was moved, surrendered, and Tong Prefecture was saved. From that day Xiang was widely known. He served as revenue clerk in Jingzhao, then as magistrate of Liyang and Weinan, earning top ratings each year. The court soon heard of his record and promoted him to vice director in the revenue ministry.
15
德宗季年，天下方鎮副亻卒多自選於朝，防一日有變，遂就而授之節制。 向已選為太原少尹，德宗召見喻旨，尋用為行軍司馬、兼御史中丞，改汾州刺史，轉鄭州。 又復為太原少尹，兼河東節度副使。 改晉州刺史，充本州防禦使，遷虢州刺史。 入為京兆少尹，拜同州刺史，充本州防禦使。 入為大理寺卿，出遷陜虢都防禦、觀察使。 三歲，拜左散騎常侍，自常侍復為大理。
Late in Dezong's reign, many deputy commissioners of the regional commands were chosen at court in advance, so that if trouble arose they could at once be given full command. Xiang had been chosen as junior intendant of Taiyuan. Dezong summoned him, explained his plans, and soon appointed him campaign marshal and censor-in-chief, then prefect of Fen and later of Zheng. He again served as junior intendant of Taiyuan and deputy military commissioner of Hedong. He became prefect and defense commissioner of Jin, then prefect of Guo. He returned to court as junior intendant of Jingzhao, then was appointed prefect and defense commissioner of Tong. He became director of the Court of Judicial Review, then was sent out as defense and surveillance commissioner for Shaan and Guo. After three years he was made left attendant of the cavalry guard, then returned to head the Court of Judicial Review.
16
向本以名相子，以學行自飭，謹守其門風。 歷官仁智推愛，利及於人。 至是，以年過致政，朝廷優異，乃以吏部尚書致仕於新昌裏第。 內外支屬百余人，向所得俸祿，必同其費，及領外任，亦挈而隨之。 有孤煢疾苦不能自恤，向尤周給，至今稱其孝睦焉。 大和四年九月卒，年八十。 贈太子少保。
As the son of a famous chief minister, Xiang disciplined himself through learning and conduct and carefully preserved his family's reputation. In every office he extended benevolence and practical kindness so that others benefited. By then he retired on account of age. The court treated him with exceptional favor, and he retired as minister of personnel to his home in Xinchang Lane. More than a hundred kinsmen depended on him. He always shared his salary with them, and when he took provincial posts he brought them along. He was especially generous to orphans and the destitute who could not care for themselves, and people still praise his filial kindness. He died in the ninth month of Dahe 4, aged eighty. He was posthumously appointed junior guardian of the heir apparent.
17
子寅，登進士第，累官至御史大夫卒。
His son Yin passed the jinshi examination, rose to censor-in-chief, and died in that office.
18
子樞，字紀聖，咸通十二年登進士第。 宰相杜審權出鎮河中，辟為從事，得秘書省校書郎，再遷藍田尉。 直弘文館。 大學士王鐸深知之，鐸罷相失職，樞亦久之不調。 從僖宗幸蜀，中丞李煥奏為殿中侍御史，遷起居郎。 中和初，王鐸復見用，以舊恩徙為鄭滑掌書記、檢校司封郎中，賜金紫，入朝歷兵、吏二員外郎。 龍紀初，擢拜給事中，改京兆尹。 宰相孔緯尤深獎遇。 大順中，緯以用兵無功貶官，樞坐累為右庶子，尋出為歙州刺史。 乾寧初，入為右散騎常侍，從昭宗幸華州，為汴州宣諭使。
His son Shu, courtesy name Jisheng, passed the jinshi examination in Xiantong 12. When chief minister Du Shenquan went out to command Hezhong he recruited Shu as staff officer. Shu became a collator in the Palace Library, then county lieutenant of Lantian. He served on duty at the Hall of Splendid Culture. Grand Academician Wang Duo knew him well. When Duo lost the chancellorship, Shu too went long without a new appointment. He followed Xizong to Shu. Vice Censor Li Huan recommended him as palace attending censor, and he was made attendant for drafting the imperial diary. Early in the Zhonghe era Wang Duo returned to power. Out of old friendship Shu was made secretary at Zheng-Hua and acting director of enfeoffments, granted gold and purple insignia, and later served as vice director in the war and personnel ministries. At the beginning of Longji he was promoted to drafting attendant, then made intendant of Jingzhao. Chief minister Kong Wei especially favored him. During Dashun, when Wei was demoted for military failure, Shu was implicated and made right guardian of the heir apparent, then sent out as prefect of She. Early in Qianning he returned as right attendant of the cavalry guard, followed Zhaozong to Hua, and was made proclaiming commissioner to Bian.
19
初，樞自歙州罷郡歸朝，路經大梁，時硃全忠兵威已振，樞以兄事之，全忠由是重之。 及樞傳詔，全忠皆稟朝旨，獻奉相繼，昭宗甚悅，遷兵部侍郎。 時崔胤專政，亦倚全忠，二人因是相結，改樞吏部侍郎。 未幾，換戶部侍郎、同平章事。 其年冬，昭宗幸華州，崔胤貶官，樞亦為工部尚書。 天子自岐下還宮，以樞檢校右僕射、同平章事，出為廣南節度使。 制出，硃全忠保薦之，言樞有經世才，不可棄之嶺表，尋復拜門下侍郎，監修國史，累兼吏部尚書，判度支。 崔胤誅，以全忠素厚，相位如故。 從昭宗遷洛陽，駐蹕陜州，進右僕射、弘文館大學士、太清宮使，充諸道鹽鐵轉運使。
When Shu left She and returned to court he passed through Daliang. Zhu Quanzhong's power was already formidable, and Shu treated him like an elder brother. Quanzhong came to esteem him. When Shu delivered the imperial edict, Quanzhong obeyed the court's orders and sent tribute in succession. Zhaozong was delighted and promoted Shu to vice minister of war. Cui Yin then dominated the government and also relied on Quanzhong. The two allied, and Shu was made vice minister of personnel. Soon he was transferred to vice minister of the household and appointed a privy councilor. That winter Zhaozong went to Hua. Cui Yin was demoted, and Shu was made minister of works. When the emperor returned from Qi to the capital, Shu was made acting right vice director and privy councilor, then sent out as military commissioner of Guangnan. When the appointment was issued, Zhu Quanzhong intervened, saying Shu had talent to govern the age and should not be sent to the far south. Shu was soon recalled as vice director of the gate, supervisor of the national history, minister of personnel, and controller of the revenue bureau. After Cui Yin was executed, Shu kept his chancellorship because of his long ties to Quanzhong. He followed Zhaozong to Luoyang and halted with him at Shaan. He was promoted to right vice director, grand academician of the Hall of Splendid Culture, commissioner of the Great Pure Palace, and transport commissioner for salt and iron.
20
哀帝初嗣位，柳璨用事，全忠嘗奏用牙將張廷範為太常卿，諸相議，樞曰：「廷範勛臣，幸有方鎮節鉞之命，何藉樂卿？ 恐非元帥梁王之旨。 」乃持之不下。 俄而全忠聞樞言，謂賓佐曰：「吾常以裴十四器識真純，不入浮薄之伍，觀此議論，本態露矣。 」切齒含怒。 柳璨聞全忠言，尋希旨罷樞相位，和陵祔享，拜尚書左僕射。 五月，責授朝散大夫、登州刺史，尋再貶瀧州司戶。 六月十一日，行及滑州，全忠遣人殺之於白馬驛，投屍於河，時年六十五。
When Emperor Ai first succeeded, Liu Can held power. Quanzhong proposed his guard officer Zhang Tingfan as director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. The councilors debated the matter, and Shu said, 'Tingfan is a meritorious officer. If he receives a regional commander's appointment, what need has he of the director of music?' I fear this is not what Prince of Liang, the commander, intends.' He therefore withheld the appointment. Soon Quanzhong heard what Shu had said and told his staff, 'I always thought Pei the Fourteenth was sincere and above shallow company. This remark shows his true colors.' He gnashed his teeth in fury. Liu Can heard Quanzhong and soon removed Shu from the chancellorship to please him. At the joint offering at He Tomb, Shu was made left vice director. In the fifth month he was demoted to grand master of palace leisure and prefect of Deng, then soon further demoted to registrar in Long Prefecture. On the eleventh day of the sixth month, as he reached Hua Prefecture, Quanzhong had him killed at Baima Post and thrown into the river. He was sixty-five.
21
史臣曰：晉卿謹身蒞事，足為純臣，避寇全忠，固彰大節。 然博達精審，豈不知寬猛之道哉！ 奉林甫之旨，順胥吏之意，悅附張倚，欺罔時君。 生為重臣，諂林甫之勢也； 歿改美謚，引元載之恩焉。 或言晉卿不為巧宦者，誠不信也。 冕力贊中興，名居大位，奉公抱義，可以致身； 賣官度僧，是何為政？ 及其老也，貪冒尤深。 遵慶學術貞明，為國忠所出； 恭儉謹密，遇蕭華素知。 位重行純，老而彌篤，彼二公固有慚德。 向克荷堂構，不墜門風。 樞因盜而振，盜憎而亡，宜哉！ 君子守道遠刑，蓋慮此也。
The historian writes: Jingqing conducted himself with restraint and served faithfully enough to rank as a pure minister. In fleeing the rebels he preserved his integrity and showed true greatness of character. Yet he was learned and discerning—how could he not have known the balance of leniency and severity! He followed Li Linfu's wishes, indulged the clerks, courted Zhang Yi, and deceived his sovereign. In life he rose to high office by flattering Li Linfu's power; in death he received a flattering posthumous title through Yuan Zai's favor. Anyone who claims Jingqing was no crafty courtier simply cannot be believed. Mian helped restore the dynasty and rose to the highest rank. Devoted to public duty and righteous principle, he might have died for the state; yet he sold offices and ordained monks—what kind of rule was that? In old age his greed grew worse still. Zunqing's scholarship was upright and lucid, yet Guozhong posted him to the provinces; respectful, frugal, and discreet, until Xiao Hua, who knew him well, brought him to power. High in rank and pure in conduct, he grew more steadfast with age—the other two men had reason to feel ashamed beside him. Xiang upheld the family estate and never let its reputation fall. Shu rose through the rebel, was hated by the rebel, and died—no more than he deserved! A gentleman keeps to the Way and shuns disgrace—doubtless he had this in mind.
22
贊曰：奧矣晉卿，貪哉裴冕。 遵慶父子，及之者鮮。
The encomium reads: Jingqing was subtle; Pei Mian was greedy. Zunqing and his line—few could equal them.
