1
苗晉卿裴冕裴遵慶子向向子寅寅子樞
Miao Jingqing, Pei Mian, and Pei Zunqing; [their sons:] Zi Xiang, Zi Yin, and Zi Shu.
2
苗晉卿，上黨壺關人。 世以儒素稱。 祖夔，高道不仕，追贈禮部尚書。 父殆庶，官至絳州龍門縣丞，早卒，以晉卿贈太子少保。
Miao Jingqing was a native of Huguan in Shangdang prefecture. His clan had long been esteemed for scholarly integrity and unadorned virtue. His grandfather Kui, a man of exalted moral stature who never entered government service, was posthumously granted the title Minister of Rites. His father Daishu had risen to the post of assistant magistrate of Longmen county in Jiang prefecture and died young; on Jingqing's account he was posthumously honored as Vice Guardian of the Heir Apparent.
3
晉卿幼好學，善屬文，進士擢第。 初授懷州修武縣尉，歷奉先縣尉，坐累貶徐州司戶參軍。 秩滿隨調，判入高等，授萬年縣尉。 遷侍御史，歷度支、兵、吏部三員外郎。 開元二十三年，遷吏部郎中。 二十四年，與吏部郎中孫逖並拜中書舍人。 二十七年，以本官權知吏部選事。 晉卿性謙柔，選人有訴訟索好官者，雖至數千言，或聲色甚厲者，晉卿必含容之，略無慍色。 二十九年，拜吏部侍郎。 前後典選五年，政既寬弛，胥吏多因緣為奸，賄賂大行。 時天下承平，每年赴選常萬余人。 李林甫為尚書，專任廟堂，銓事唯委晉卿及同列侍郎宋遙主之。 選人既多，每年兼命他官有識者同考定書判，務求其實。 天寶二年春，御史中丞張倚男奭參選，晉卿與遙以倚初承恩，欲悅附之，考選人判等凡六十四人，分甲乙丙科，奭在其首。 眾知奭不讀書，論議紛然。 有蘇孝慍者，嘗為范陽薊令，事安祿山，具其事告之。 祿山恩寵特異，謁見不常，因而奏之。 玄宗大集登科人，禦花萼樓親試，登第者十無一二； 而奭手持試紙，竟日不下一字，時謂之「曳白」。 上怒，晉卿貶為安康郡太守，遙為武當郡太守，張倚為淮陽太守。 敕曰：「門庭之間，不能訓子； 選調之際，仍以托人。 」時士子皆以為戲笑。
From boyhood Jingqing was devoted to study and excelled at composition; he passed the jinshi civil examination. He was first appointed magistrate of Xiuwu in Huai prefecture, later served as magistrate of Fengxian, and was demoted to registrar of Xu prefecture after being implicated in a colleague's offense. When his term expired he entered the regular reassignment cycle, earned a top evaluation, and was appointed magistrate of Wannian county. He was promoted to attending censor and served in turn as vice director in the ministries of Revenue, War, and Personnel. In Kaiyuan 23 (735) he was transferred to director in the Ministry of Personnel. The following year he and Sun Ti of the Ministry of Personnel were jointly appointed drafting attendants of the Secretariat. In Kaiyuan 27 (739) he was temporarily assigned to oversee civil-service selection while retaining his existing post. Jingqing was by nature modest and forbearing. Candidates who came to plead or sue for better appointments might harangue him at great length or raise their voices in anger, yet he always received them patiently and never showed irritation. In Kaiyuan 29 (741) he was appointed vice minister of personnel. Over five years in charge of selection his administration was notably lenient; clerks took advantage of the slack discipline to practice fraud, and bribery became widespread. The empire was at peace, and each year more than ten thousand candidates presented themselves for selection. Li Linfu held the post of minister and dominated the court; he left selection affairs entirely to Jingqing and his colleague Song Yao. Because the candidate pool was so large, each year other capable officials were also assigned to grade the written examinations, so that the results might reflect genuine merit. In the spring of Tianbao 2 (743), Zhang Yi's son Yi entered the selection process. Jingqing and Yao, eager to curry favor with the newly favored Zhang Yi, ranked sixty-four candidates in three grades—and placed Yi at the top. Everyone knew Yi was unlettered, and public outcry followed. A man named Su Xiaoyin, formerly magistrate of Ji in Fanyang and now in An Lushan's service, reported the whole affair to him in detail. Lushan enjoyed extraordinary imperial favor and was not often granted audience; he therefore submitted a memorial on the matter. Emperor Xuanzong summoned a large gathering of degree-holders to the Hua'e Tower and personally tested them; fewer than one in ten of those who had passed the examinations could meet the standard; Yi, holding his examination paper, could not write a single character all day long—hence the nickname 'dragging the blank.' The emperor was enraged. Jingqing was demoted to administrator of Ankang commandery, Yao to administrator of Wudang commandery, and Zhang Yi to administrator of Huaiyang. The edict read: 'Unable to discipline your son within your own household; yet at the time of selection you still relied on pulling strings for others.' Scholars everywhere treated the edict as a laughingstock.
4
天寶三載閏二月，轉魏郡太守，充河北采訪處置使，居職三年，政化洽聞。 會入計，因上表請歸鄉里。 既至壺關，望縣門而步。 小吏進曰：「太守位高德重，不宜自輕。 」晉卿曰：「《禮》：『下公門，式路馬。 』況父母之邦，所宜尊敬。 汝何言哉！ 」大會鄉黨，歡飲累日而去。 又俸錢三萬為鄉學本，以教授子弟。 尋改河東太守、河東采訪使，入為尚書、東京留守，征為憲部尚書。 屬祿山叛逆，楊國忠以晉卿有時望，將抑之，乃奏云：「宜以大臣鎮東道。 」遂出為陜州刺史、陜虢兩州防禦使。 及入對，固辭老病，由是忤旨，改憲部尚書致仕。 及朝廷失守，衣冠流離道路，多為逆黨所脅，自陳希烈、張均已下數十人盡赴洛陽，晉卿潛遁山谷，南投金州。 會肅宗至鳳翔，手詔追晉卿赴行在，即日拜為左相，軍國大務悉以咨之。 既收兩京，以功封韓國公，食實封五百戶，改為侍中。 後以賊寇漸除，屢乞骸骨，優詔許之，罷知政事，為太子太傅。 明年，帝思舊臣，復拜為侍中。
In the intercalary second month of Tianbao 3 (744) he was transferred to administrator of Wei commandery and appointed Hebei investigation commissioner. After three years in office his benevolent rule was renowned throughout the region. When he traveled to the capital for the annual accounting, he submitted a memorial asking permission to return home. On reaching Huguan he dismounted and walked on foot as he approached the county gate. A junior clerk stepped forward and said, 'Your Excellency's rank is lofty and your virtue weighty—you should not humble yourself in this way.' Jingqing replied, 'The Book of Rites says: "When one descends from the duke's gate, one bows toward the horses on the road."' 'How much more should one show respect in the land of one's parents!' What sort of talk is that! He then held a great feast for the local community, celebrated for several days, and departed. He also contributed thirty thousand cash from his salary as an endowment for the local school, to educate the young men of the district. He was soon transferred to administrator and investigation commissioner of Hedong, then recalled to serve as minister and regent of the Eastern Capital, and finally summoned to the post of minister of justice. When Lushan rebelled, Yang Guozhong, wishing to sideline the widely respected Jingqing, memorialized that 'a senior minister should be posted to guard the eastern circuit.' Jingqing was accordingly sent out as prefect of Shan and defense commissioner for Shan and Guo prefectures. When he appeared at court he firmly pleaded old age and illness; this displeased the emperor, and he was transferred to minister of justice and granted retirement. When the capital fell, officials fled in disarray along the roads, many forced into the rebels' service. Dozens of senior figures, from Chen Xilie and Zhang Jun on down, made their way to Luoyang; Jingqing slipped into the mountains and fled south to Jin prefecture. When Emperor Suzong reached Fengxiang he issued a personal edict summoning Jingqing to the mobile court and that same day appointed him left chancellor; all major military and civil affairs were referred to him. After the two capitals were recovered he was enfeoffed as Duke of Han with a substantive fief of five hundred households and appointed palace attendant. As the rebellion was gradually suppressed he repeatedly asked to retire; the emperor graciously consented, relieved him of administrative duties, and appointed him grand tutor of the heir apparent. The following year, missing his veteran minister, the emperor reappointed him palace attendant.
5
晉卿寬厚廉謹，為政舉大綱，不問小過，所到有惠化。 魏人思之，為立碑頌德。 及秉鈞衡，小心畏慎，未嘗忤人意。 性聰敏，達練事體，百司文簿，經目必曉，而修身守位，以智自全，議者比漢之胡廣。
Jingqing was magnanimous, honest, and prudent. In office he grasped the broad principles of governance and overlooked minor faults; wherever he served he left a record of benevolent rule. The people of Wei cherished his memory and erected a stele in praise of his virtue. When he held the highest offices he was cautious and deferential and never gave offense. He was quick-witted and thoroughly versed in affairs of state; a glance at any document from the bureaucracy was enough for him to grasp it. He cultivated himself and held his post with such tact that he preserved himself through difficult times; contemporaries compared him to Hu Guang of the Han.
6
玄宗崩，肅宗詔晉卿攝冢宰。 上表固辭曰：「臣聞古者殷高宗在諒闇之中，百官聽於冢宰，更無事跡，但存文字。 且一時之事，禮不相沿。 今殘寇猶虞，日殷萬務，皆緣兵馬屯守討襲，善算良謀，立勝擒敵。 陛下若行古之道，居喪不言，蒼生何依，百事皆廢。 伏讀國家起居註，亦於禮部檢見舊敕，恭惟太宗、高宗、、大行皇帝在位之日，皆有國哀，視事不輟，以為君臨天下，難徇常情。 今遺詔有處分，皇帝宜三日而聽政。 陛下遵太宗故事，則無冢宰； 遵大行皇帝遺詔，便合聽朝。 萬姓颙颙，不勝大願。 伏惟陛下知理國之重，順人心之切，以義斷恩，從宜無改。 今朝臣一命已上，皆言臣心昏貌朽，加以疾病，事有急速，斷在須臾，凡聖不同，豈合受詔。 陛下發哀已五日矣，願準遺詔聽政，則四夷萬國，無任悲幸。 」肅宗時疾彌留，覽表殞絕，乃許。
When Emperor Xuanzong died, Emperor Suzong ordered Jingqing to serve as regent during the mourning period. He submitted a memorial firmly declining: 'I have read that in antiquity, when King Gaozong of Yin was in mourning seclusion, the hundred officials took their orders from the regent—but beyond that tradition we have only written records, not living precedent.' Moreover, that was a practice of a particular age; rites are not handed down unchanged from one era to the next.' Today remnant rebels still threaten us, and a multitude of urgent affairs presses upon us daily—all depending on troops garrisoned and deployed for pursuit, on sound strategy and timely counsel to win swift victories and capture the enemy.' If Your Majesty were to follow the ancient way and remain in mourning without conducting affairs, what would the people rely upon? Every matter of state would come to a halt.' I have respectfully read the court diaries and examined old edicts in the Ministry of Rites. Emperors Taizong, Gaozong, Xuanzong, and the late emperor, while on the throne, all continued to conduct state business even during periods of national mourning, holding that one who rules the realm cannot be bound by ordinary private sentiment.' The late emperor's testamentary edict expressly provides that the new emperor should assume governance after three days.' If Your Majesty follows Emperor Taizong's precedent, no regent is needed; if you follow the late emperor's testamentary edict, you should take the throne and hold court at once.' The myriad people look to you with eager hope—that is their deepest wish.' I humbly pray that Your Majesty, knowing how weighty the task of governing is and how urgently the people long for stability, will set private grief aside for the sake of public duty and act as circumstances require.' Every minister from the ninth rank upward says that my mind is clouded, my body worn out, and my illnesses many; urgent affairs must be decided in an instant. How could an ordinary man such as I be fit to receive such a charge?' Your Majesty has already mourned for five days. I pray that you will follow the testamentary edict and assume governance; then the four quarters and all nations will know both grief and boundless relief.' Emperor Suzong, then gravely ill, read the memorial and was overcome with grief, but he granted the request.'
7
數日，肅宗晏駕，代宗踐祚，又詔晉卿攝冢宰。 晉卿上表懇辭曰：「臣以昔者天子居喪之時，百官聽於冢宰者，蓋君幼小，禦極事殷，情理當然。 沿革不一，今古異同，而周武、漢文，合於通變，垂範作則，可舉而行。 又士或墨缞，時遇金革，豈非銜恤，謂義在斷恩。 且百善之至，無加於孝也，其有容瘁心絕，指景悼生，此匹夫守節之常情，殊王者嗣續之大計。 昨二十日，陛下於大行皇帝柩前即位，是承先帝遺顧之言，亦前代不易之典。 則知所略不為害，所存是適權，防威滅端，所利者大。 陛下因心純至，天地明察。 伏以報劬勞之恩，申罔極之思，終身之痛，豈計朝夕！ 但以一日之內，萬務在中，須達宸聰，始成國政。 百僚萬姓及僧道耆壽等，相顧聚言，以臣老且無能，愚豈測聖，況久無居攝，臣不敢奉詔。 特乞陛下遵遺命，三日而政。 臣博聽眾情，不勝懇願，伏望割痛抑哀，則天下悲幸。 」上號泣從之。 時晉卿年已衰暮，又患兩足，上特許肩輿至中書，入閣不趨，累日一視事。 歷三朝，皆以謹密見稱。
A few days later Emperor Suzong died and Emperor Daizong ascended the throne; he again ordered Jingqing to serve as regent during the mourning period. Jingqing submitted another earnest memorial of refusal: 'In former times, when the Son of Heaven was in mourning and the hundred officials took orders from a regent, it was because the ruler was a child and the burdens of rule were pressing—that was only natural.' Customs change from age to age, yet King Wu of Zhou and Emperor Wen of Han adapted rites to circumstance and left models we may follow today.' Moreover, a gentleman in deep mourning who meets a time of war must set private grief aside when public duty demands it—that is what it means to sever private ties for the sake of righteousness.' Of all virtues none ranks above filial piety; yet the anguish of a private man who mourns until he wastes away is one thing, while the succession of the imperial line is a matter of an altogether different order.' On the twentieth day Your Majesty took the throne before the late emperor's bier, fulfilling his final charge and following an unchanging precedent of former dynasties.' Thus what is set aside does no harm, and what is preserved is timely adaptation; to forestall danger at its root brings the greatest benefit to the realm.' Your Majesty's sincerity is pure and complete; Heaven and Earth themselves bear witness.' To repay a parent's labor and express boundless grief is a lifelong sorrow—not a matter of a few days!' Yet within a single day a myriad affairs demand the emperor's attention; only when your sacred ear is engaged can governance proceed.' Officials, common people, monks, Daoist elders, and the aged all say among themselves that I am old and incapable, and that no one has held the regency in many years. I dare not accept this charge.' I earnestly beg Your Majesty to follow the testamentary command and assume governance after three days.' Having listened widely to the people's wishes, I cannot express my earnest plea strongly enough: I bow and pray that Your Majesty will master your grief; then the whole realm will know both sorrow and relief.' The emperor wept aloud and acceded to his request.' By then Jingqing was elderly and afflicted in both legs. The emperor specially allowed him to be carried by sedan chair to the Secretariat, to enter the gate without hurrying, and to conduct business only every few days. Through three reigns he was esteemed for his prudence and discretion.
8
裴冕，河東人也，為河東冠族。 天寶初，以門廕再遷渭南縣尉，以吏道聞。 御史中丞王鉷充京畿采訪使，表為判官。 遷監察御史，歷殿中侍御史。 冕雖無學術，守職通明，果於臨事，鉷甚委之。 及鉷得罪伏法，時宰臣李林甫方竊權柄，人鹹懼之，鉷賓佐數百，不敢窺鉷門。 冕獨收鉷屍，親自護喪，瘞於近郊，冕自是知名。 河西節度使哥舒翰表為行軍司馬，累遷員外郎中。
Pei Mian was a native of Hedong and belonged to one of the region's foremost families. In the early Tianbao years he entered service through hereditary privilege and was twice promoted to magistrate of Weinan county, where he gained a reputation for administrative competence. When Censor-in-Chief Wang Hong served as metropolitan investigation commissioner, he recommended Mian as his administrative aide. He was promoted to investigating censor and later served as attending censor in the palace. Though without scholarly training, Mian was clear-minded in office and resolute in action; Wang Hong relied on him heavily. When Wang Hong was condemned and executed, Chief Minister Li Linfu was at the height of his power and everyone feared him. Hundreds of Hong's retainers dared not even approach his gate. Mian alone recovered Hong's body, personally conducted the funeral, and buried him in a nearby suburb. From that act he won wide renown. Geshu Han, military commissioner of Hexi, recommended him as campaigning marshal, and he rose to vice director.
9
玄宗幸蜀，至益昌郡，遙詔太子充天下兵馬元帥，以冕為御史中丞兼左庶子，為之副。 是時，冕為河西行軍司馬，授御史中丞，詔赴朝廷。 遇太子於平涼，具陳事勢，勸之朔方，亟入靈武。 冕與杜鴻漸、崔漪等勸進曰：「主上厭勤大位，南幸蜀川，宗社神器，須有所歸，天意人事，不可固違。 若逡巡退讓，失億兆心，則大事去矣！ 臣等猶知之，況賢智乎！ 」太子曰：「南平寇逆，奉迎鑾輿，退居儲貳，侍膳左右，豈不樂哉！ 公等何言之過也？ 」冕與杜鴻漸又進曰：「殿下藉累聖之資，有天下之表。 元貞萬國，二十余年，殷憂啟聖，正在今日。 所從殿下六軍將士，皆關輔百姓，日夜思歸。 大軍一散，不可復集，不如因而撫之以從眾，臣等敢以死請。 」凡勸進五上，乃依。 肅宗即位，以定策功，遷中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事，倚以為政。
When Emperor Xuanzong fled to Shu and reached Yichang commandery, he issued a distant edict appointing the crown prince commander-in-chief of all forces under heaven, with Mian as censor-in-chief and left vice director of the crown prince's household to serve as his deputy. At the time Mian was campaigning marshal in Hexi; he was appointed censor-in-chief and ordered to proceed to court. He met the crown prince at Pingliang, laid out the situation in full, and urged him to go to Shuofang and enter Lingwu without delay. Mian, together with Du Hongjian, Cui Yi, and others, urged the prince to ascend the throne: 'The sovereign has grown weary of rule and gone south to Shu. The altars of state and the imperial regalia must find a home. Heaven's intent and the people's will cannot be defied.' If you hesitate and hold back, you will lose the hearts of the millions—and the cause will be lost!' Even we understand this—how much more should a man of your wisdom!' The crown prince replied, 'To pacify the rebels in the south, welcome the imperial carriage home, and then withdraw to serve as heir at my father's side—would that not be the happier course?' Why do you speak so extravagantly?' Mian and Du Hongjian pressed further: 'Your Highness inherits the accumulated virtue of successive sage emperors and bears the countenance of one destined to rule all under heaven.' For more than twenty years you have upheld the dynasty's integrity amid ten thousand states. Deep trial now summons forth sage rule—today is that day.' The six-army troops who follow you are all common folk from the Guanzhong region; day and night they long to return home.' Once a great army disperses it cannot be reassembled. Better to win them over now and follow the will of the multitude—we beg this of you at the risk of our lives.' They pressed their plea for enthronement five times before he at last consented. When Emperor Suzong took the throne, Mian was promoted to vice director of the Secretariat and appointed concurrent grand councilor for his role in the succession plan; the emperor relied on him to govern.
10
冕性忠勤，悉心奉公，稍得人心。 然不識大體，以聚人曰財，乃下令賣官鬻爵，度尼僧道士，以儲積為務。 人不願者，科令就之，其價益賤，事轉為弊。 肅宗移幸鳳翔，罷冕知政事，遷右僕射。 兩京平，以功封冀國公，食實封五百戶。 尋加御史大夫、成都尹，充劍南西川節度使。 又入為右僕射。 永泰元年，與裴遵慶等並集賢待制。 代宗求舊，拜冕兼御史大夫，充護山陵使。 冕以幸臣李輔國權盛，將附之，乃表輔國親昵術士中書舍人劉烜充山陵使判官。 烜坐法，冕坐貶施州刺史。 數月，移澧州刺史，復征為左僕射。 元載秉政。 載為新平縣尉，王鉷辟在巡內，冕常引之，載頗德冕。 會宰臣杜鴻漸卒，載遂舉冕代之。 冕時已衰瘵，載以其順己，引為同列。 受命之際，蹈舞絕倒，載趨而扶起，代為謝詞。 冕兼掌兵權留守之任，俸錢每月二千余貫。 性本侈靡，好尚車服及營珍饌，名馬在櫪，直數百金者常十數。 每會賓友，滋味品數，坐客有昧於名者。 自創巾子，其狀新奇，市肆因而效之，呼為「僕射樣」。 初代鴻漸，小吏以俸錢文簿白之，冕顧子弟，喜見於色，其嗜利若此。 拜職未盈月，卒，大歷四年十二月也。 上悼之，輟朝三日，贈太尉，賻制五百匹、粟五百石。
Mian was loyal and diligent, devoted himself wholly to public service, and gradually won popular regard. Yet he lacked a grasp of larger policy. Believing that amassing people meant amassing wealth, he ordered the sale of offices and ranks, the ordination of Buddhist and Daoist clergy, and made hoarding revenue his chief aim. Those who resisted were compelled by regulation to comply anyway. Prices kept falling, and the practice became a growing abuse. When Emperor Suzong moved to Fengxiang, Mian was removed from active governance and appointed right vice director of the Department of State Affairs. After the two capitals were recovered he was enfeoffed as Duke of Ji with a substantive fief of five hundred households. He was soon made censor-in-chief and prefect of Chengdu, and appointed military commissioner of Jiannan West Circuit. He was later recalled to serve again as right vice director. In Yongtai 1 (765) he and Pei Zunqing and others were appointed attendant draftsmen at the Hall of Assembled Worthies. Emperor Daizong, seeking veteran ministers, appointed Mian concurrent censor-in-chief and commissioner for guarding the imperial tombs. Because the powerful favorite Li Fuguo was at the height of his influence, Mian sought to win his favor and memorialized that Fuguo's close associate, the technician and drafting attendant Liu Xuan, should serve as aide to the tomb commissioner. When Liu Xuan was convicted by law, Mian was implicated and demoted to prefect of Shi prefecture. A few months later he was transferred to prefect of Li prefecture, then recalled to serve as left vice director. Yuan Zai held power at court. Yuan Zai had once served as magistrate of Xinping county; Wang Hong recruited him into his inspection staff, and Mian had often recommended him. Zai felt deep gratitude toward Mian. When Chief Minister Du Hongjian died, Zai recommended Mian to replace him. Mian was by then enfeebled by chronic illness, but Zai, knowing he would not oppose him, brought him onto the council as a fellow minister. When he received the appointment he performed the ritual dance and collapsed in a faint. Zai rushed forward to help him up and delivered the words of thanks on his behalf. Mian also held military authority and the post of regent, with a monthly salary of more than two thousand strings of cash. By nature he was extravagant, fond of fine carriages and robes and of preparing rare delicacies. A dozen or more famous horses worth hundreds of gold pieces each stood in his stables. Whenever he entertained guests, the dishes were so numerous and exotic that seated guests could not name them all. He devised a new style of official headcloth so distinctive that shops copied it and called it the 'Vice Director style.' When he first replaced Du Hongjian, a clerk presented the salary ledger. Mian turned to his sons and nephews with visible delight at the figures—such was his greed. He had held the post less than a full month when he died, in the twelfth month of Dali 4 (769). The emperor mourned his death, suspended court for three days, posthumously appointed him grand commandant, and granted five hundred bolts of silk and five hundred piculs of grain for the funeral.
11
裴遵慶，絳州聞喜人也。 代襲冠冕，為河東著族。 遵慶志氣深厚，機鑒敏達，自幼強學，博涉載籍，謹身晦跡，不幹當世之務。 以門廕累授潞府司法參軍，時年已老，未為人所知。 隨調吏部，授大理寺丞，剖斷刑獄，舉正綱條，理行始著。 遷司門員外、吏部員外郎，專判南曹。 天寶中，海內無事，九流輻輳會府，每歲吏部選人，動盈萬數。 遵慶敏識強記，精核文簿，詳而不滯，時稱吏事第一，由是大知名。
Pei Zunqing was a native of Wenxi in Jiang prefecture. His family had held high office for generations and ranked among the leading clans of Hedong. Zunqing was deep-spirited and keenly perceptive. From youth he studied diligently and read widely, yet he kept a low profile and did not thrust himself into public affairs. Through hereditary privilege he was appointed judicial administrator in the Lu prefecture office. He was already advanced in years and still little known. After entering the regular reassignment process at the Ministry of Personnel he was appointed assistant director of the Court of Judicial Review, where his handling of criminal cases and enforcement of regulations first brought his administrative talent to notice. He was promoted to vice director in the Ministry of Justice and the Ministry of Personnel, with special responsibility for the Southern Bureau of personnel selection. During the Tianbao era the empire was at peace and men of every calling thronged the capital. Each year the Ministry of Personnel processed more than ten thousand candidates for selection. Zunqing was quick-witted with an exceptional memory, and his scrutiny of documents was thorough yet never bogged down. He was acclaimed as the foremost administrator of his day and won wide renown.
12
天寶末，楊國忠當國，出不附己者例為外官，遵慶亦出為郡守。 肅宗即位，征拜給事中、尚書右丞、吏部侍郎。 恭儉克己，遲重謹密，頗有時望。 上元中，蕭華輔政，素知遵慶，每奏見，累稱之，遷黃門侍郎、同中書門下平章事。 廣德初，仆固懷恩阻兵汾上，指中官為詞，上以遵慶忠純，特遣往汾州宣慰懷恩。 遵慶既見懷恩，具陳朝旨，懷恩引過聽命，將隨遵慶朝謁，為副將範誌誠以邪說惑之，懷恩遂以懼死為詞。 會蕃寇陷京師，乘輿幸陜，遵慶自汾州奔赴行在。 及乘輿還京，以遵慶為太子少傅。 永泰元年，與裴冕等並於集賢院待制，罷知政事。 尋改吏部尚書、右僕射，復知選事。 時選人天興縣尉陳琯於銓庭言詞不遜，淩突無禮，代宗詔付遵慶於省門鞭三十，貶為吉州員外司戶參軍。 遵慶敦守儒行，老而彌謹。 嘗為風狂族侄撾登聞鼓告以不順，上知遵度，不省，其見信如此。 大歷十年十月薨於位，年九十余。
Near the end of the Tianbao era Yang Guozhong dominated the government and routinely posted opponents to provincial posts. Zunqing too was sent out as a commandery administrator. When Emperor Suzong took the throne, Zunqing was summoned and appointed drafting attendant, right assistant director of the Department of State Affairs, and vice minister of personnel. He was respectful, frugal, and self-restrained, grave and discreet in manner, and enjoyed considerable public esteem. During the Shangyuan era Xiao Hua held power and had long known Zunqing's worth. Whenever he appeared before the emperor he spoke highly of him, and Zunqing was promoted to vice director of the Palace Gate and appointed concurrent grand councilor. In early Guangde (763) Pugu Huai'en held his army on the Fen River and blamed the court eunuchs for his grievances. The emperor, trusting Zunqing's loyalty, specially dispatched him to Fen prefecture to reassure Huai'en on the court's behalf. When Zunqing met Huai'en he laid out the court's position in full. Huai'en admitted his errors and agreed to obey, and was about to accompany Zunqing to court—but his deputy Fan Zhicheng swayed him with sinister counsel, and Huai'en then pleaded that he feared for his life. When Tibetan forces seized the capital and the emperor fled to Shan, Zunqing hurried from Fen prefecture to join the mobile court. When the emperor returned to the capital, Zunqing was appointed grand tutor of the heir apparent. In Yongtai 1 (765) he and Pei Mian and others were appointed attendant draftsmen at the Hall of Assembled Worthies and removed from active governance. He was soon made minister of personnel and right vice director, and again put in charge of civil-service selection. When a candidate named Chen Guan, magistrate of Tianxing county, spoke insolently and behaved outrageously at the selection courtyard, Emperor Daizong ordered Zunqing to have him flogged thirty times at the ministry gate and demoted him to vice registrar of Ji prefecture. Zunqing steadfastly upheld Confucian conduct and grew ever more careful in his old age. Once a deranged kinsman beat the Denunciation Drum at the palace gate to accuse him of disloyalty. The emperor, knowing Zunqing's character, took no notice—such was the trust he enjoyed. In the tenth month of Dali 10 (775) he died in office at more than ninety years of age.
13
遵慶初登省郎，嘗著《王政記》，述今古禮體，識者覽之，知有公輔之量。
Early in his career, when he first entered the provincial bureaus, he wrote the Record of Royal Government, treating ritual institutions past and present. Readers who saw it recognized in him the makings of a chief minister.
14
子向，字傃仁，少以門廕歷官至太子司議郎。 建中初，李紓為同州刺史，奏向為從事。 硃泚反，李懷光又叛河中，使其將趙貴先築壘於同州，紓來奔奉天，向領州務。 貴先因脅縣尉林寶役徒板築，不及期，將斬之，吏人百姓奔竄。 向即詣貴先軍壘，以逆順之理責之，貴先感悟，遂來降，故同州不陷。 向由是知名。 累為京兆府戶曹，轉櫟陽、渭南縣令，奏課皆第一，朝廷亟聞其理行，擢為戶部員外郎。
His son Xiang, courtesy name Suren, entered service through hereditary privilege and rose to director of court discussion for the heir apparent. In early Jianzhong (780) Li Shu became prefect of Tong prefecture and recommended Xiang as his administrative aide. When Zhu Ci rebelled, Li Huai'guang also rose in Hezhong and sent his general Zhao Guixian to build fortifications at Tong prefecture. Li Shu fled to Fengtian, and Xiang took charge of the prefecture. Guixian forced the county magistrate Lin Bao to press conscript laborers into building ramparts. When the work fell behind schedule he was about to execute Lin, and officials and common people fled in panic. Xiang went at once to Guixian's camp and rebuked him on the principles of loyalty and rebellion. Guixian was moved to repentance and surrendered, and so Tong prefecture was spared. From this exploit Xiang won wide renown. He served repeatedly in the metropolitan prefecture's revenue office and as magistrate of Liyang and Weinan counties, earning top marks in every evaluation. The court soon took note of his administrative record and promoted him to vice director in the Ministry of Revenue.
15
德宗季年，天下方鎮副亻卒多自選於朝，防一日有變，遂就而授之節制。 向已選為太原少尹，德宗召見喻旨，尋用為行軍司馬、兼御史中丞，改汾州刺史，轉鄭州。 又復為太原少尹，兼河東節度副使。 改晉州刺史，充本州防禦使，遷虢州刺史。 入為京兆少尹，拜同州刺史，充本州防禦使。 入為大理寺卿，出遷陜虢都防禦、觀察使。 三歲，拜左散騎常侍，自常侍復為大理。
In the late years of Emperor Dezong's reign, deputy commanders of the regional military governors were often chosen at court in advance, so that if trouble arose the court could immediately appoint them to full command. Xiang had been selected as vice governor of Taiyuan. Emperor Dezong summoned him, explained the court's intent, and soon appointed him campaigning marshal and concurrent censor-in-chief, then prefect of Fen and later of Zheng prefecture. He was again appointed vice governor of Taiyuan and deputy military commissioner of Hedong. He was transferred to prefect of Jin and defense commissioner of that prefecture, then to prefect of Guo. He entered the capital as vice governor of the metropolitan prefecture, was appointed prefect of Tong, and served as defense commissioner of that prefecture. He was appointed director of the Court of Judicial Review, then sent out as metropolitan defense and observation commissioner for Shan and Guo. After three years he was appointed left regular attendant, then returned from that post to serve again as director of the Court of Judicial Review.
16
向本以名相子，以學行自飭，謹守其門風。 歷官仁智推愛，利及於人。 至是，以年過致政，朝廷優異，乃以吏部尚書致仕於新昌裏第。 內外支屬百余人，向所得俸祿，必同其費，及領外任，亦挈而隨之。 有孤煢疾苦不能自恤，向尤周給，至今稱其孝睦焉。 大和四年九月卒，年八十。 贈太子少保。
As the son of a renowned chief minister, Xiang disciplined himself through learning and conduct and carefully upheld his family's reputation. In every post he governed with benevolence and wisdom, bringing benefit to those he served. When he reached advanced age he retired from office. The court honored him with exceptional favor, granting him retirement as minister of personnel at his home in Xinchang Lane. He had more than a hundred kinsmen within and outside his immediate family. Whatever salary he received he shared with them, and when he took provincial appointments he brought them along. He gave special care to orphans, the destitute, and the sick who could not fend for themselves. To this day he is remembered for his filial devotion and kindness to kin. He died in the ninth month of Dahe 4 (830) at the age of eighty. He was posthumously honored as vice guardian of the heir apparent.
17
子寅，登進士第，累官至御史大夫卒。
His son Yin passed the jinshi examination, rose to censor-in-chief, and died in that post.
18
子樞，字紀聖，咸通十二年登進士第。 宰相杜審權出鎮河中，辟為從事，得秘書省校書郎，再遷藍田尉。 直弘文館。 大學士王鐸深知之，鐸罷相失職，樞亦久之不調。 從僖宗幸蜀，中丞李煥奏為殿中侍御史，遷起居郎。 中和初，王鐸復見用，以舊恩徙為鄭滑掌書記、檢校司封郎中，賜金紫，入朝歷兵、吏二員外郎。 龍紀初，擢拜給事中，改京兆尹。 宰相孔緯尤深獎遇。 大順中，緯以用兵無功貶官，樞坐累為右庶子，尋出為歙州刺史。 乾寧初，入為右散騎常侍，從昭宗幸華州，為汴州宣諭使。
His son Shu, courtesy name Jisheng, passed the jinshi examination in Xiantong 12 (871). When Chief Minister Du Shenquan went out to command Hezhong he recruited Shu as his aide. Shu was appointed collator in the Secretariat and later promoted to magistrate of Lantian. He served on duty at the Hall of Illustrious Culture. Grand Academician Wang Duo thought highly of him, but when Duo lost the chancellorship Shu too went for a long time without reassignment. He accompanied Emperor Xizong to Shu. Vice censor Li Huan recommended him as attending censor in the palace, and he was promoted to diarist. In early Zhonghe (881) Wang Duo returned to power and, out of old friendship, appointed Shu secretary of the Zheng-Hua circuit and acting director in the Ministry of Honors, with the gold-and-purple distinction. Shu entered court and served in turn as vice director in the ministries of War and Personnel. In early Longji (889) he was promoted to drafting attendant and then appointed metropolitan governor. Chief Minister Kong Wei especially favored and promoted him. In Dashun (890) Kong Wei was demoted for military failure, and Shu was implicated and made right vice director of the heir apparent's household, then sent out as prefect of She. In early Qianning (894) he was recalled as right regular attendant, accompanied Emperor Zhaozong to Hua prefecture, and was appointed commissioner to proclaim and console at Bian.
19
初，樞自歙州罷郡歸朝，路經大梁，時硃全忠兵威已振，樞以兄事之，全忠由是重之。 及樞傳詔，全忠皆稟朝旨，獻奉相繼，昭宗甚悅，遷兵部侍郎。 時崔胤專政，亦倚全忠，二人因是相結，改樞吏部侍郎。 未幾，換戶部侍郎、同平章事。 其年冬，昭宗幸華州，崔胤貶官，樞亦為工部尚書。 天子自岐下還宮，以樞檢校右僕射、同平章事，出為廣南節度使。 制出，硃全忠保薦之，言樞有經世才，不可棄之嶺表，尋復拜門下侍郎，監修國史，累兼吏部尚書，判度支。 崔胤誅，以全忠素厚，相位如故。 從昭宗遷洛陽，駐蹕陜州，進右僕射、弘文館大學士、太清宮使，充諸道鹽鐵轉運使。
Earlier, when Shu left his post at She and returned to court, his route passed through Daliang. Zhu Quanzhong's military power was already formidable, and Shu treated him as an elder brother—whereupon Quanzhong came to esteem him. When Shu delivered the imperial edict, Quanzhong followed the court's instructions and sent tribute in succession. Emperor Zhaozong was greatly pleased and promoted Shu to vice minister of war. Cui Yin then dominated the government and also relied on Quanzhong; the two formed an alliance and had Shu transferred to vice minister of personnel. Before long he was made vice minister of revenue and appointed concurrent grand councilor. That winter Emperor Zhaozong went to Hua prefecture. Cui Yin was demoted, and Shu was made minister of works. When the emperor returned to the capital from Qi, Shu was appointed acting right vice director and concurrent grand councilor, then sent out as military commissioner of Guangnan. When the appointment was issued Zhu Quanzhong intervened on his behalf, declaring that Shu had talent to govern the age and should not be banished to the far south. Shu was soon reappointed vice director of the Chancellery, put in charge of compiling the national history, and concurrently made minister of personnel with responsibility for the Ministry of Revenue. When Cui Yin was executed, Shu kept his chancellorship unchanged because of his long-standing ties to Quanzhong. He accompanied Emperor Zhaozong to Luoyang and halted at Shan prefecture, where he was promoted to right vice director, grand academician of the Hall of Illustrious Culture, and commissioner of the Great Pure Palace, and appointed transport commissioner for salt and iron across all circuits.
20
哀帝初嗣位，柳璨用事，全忠嘗奏用牙將張廷範為太常卿，諸相議，樞曰：「廷範勛臣，幸有方鎮節鉞之命，何藉樂卿？ 恐非元帥梁王之旨。 」乃持之不下。 俄而全忠聞樞言，謂賓佐曰：「吾常以裴十四器識真純，不入浮薄之伍，觀此議論，本態露矣。 」切齒含怒。 柳璨聞全忠言，尋希旨罷樞相位，和陵祔享，拜尚書左僕射。 五月，責授朝散大夫、登州刺史，尋再貶瀧州司戶。 六月十一日，行及滑州，全忠遣人殺之於白馬驛，投屍於河，時年六十五。
When Emperor Aidi first succeeded to the throne, Liu Can held power. Quanzhong once memorialized to appoint his military officer Zhang Tingfan director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. When the councilors discussed it, Shu said, 'Tingfan is a meritorious officer who may yet receive a regional military commission—why should he need the post of director of sacrifices?' I fear this is not what the commander, the Prince of Liang, intends.' He therefore withheld the appointment and would not issue it. Soon Quanzhong heard of Shu's remarks and told his staff, 'I always thought Pei the Fourteenth was genuinely upright and above the company of shallow men. This opinion shows his true colors.' He ground his teeth in fury. Liu Can heard Quanzhong's words and soon removed Shu from the chancellorship to please him. At the joint enshrinement ceremony at Heling, Shu was appointed left vice director of the Department of State Affairs. In the fifth month he was demoted to grand master of palace deliberation and prefect of Deng, then demoted again to registrar of Long prefecture. On the eleventh day of the sixth month, as he traveled through Hua prefecture, Quanzhong sent men to kill him at Baima Post and throw his body into the river. He was sixty-five years old.
21
史臣曰：晉卿謹身蒞事，足為純臣，避寇全忠，固彰大節。 然博達精審，豈不知寬猛之道哉！ 奉林甫之旨，順胥吏之意，悅附張倚，欺罔時君。 生為重臣，諂林甫之勢也； 歿改美謚，引元載之恩焉。 或言晉卿不為巧宦者，誠不信也。 冕力贊中興，名居大位，奉公抱義，可以致身； 賣官度僧，是何為政？ 及其老也，貪冒尤深。 遵慶學術貞明，為國忠所出； 恭儉謹密，遇蕭華素知。 位重行純，老而彌篤，彼二公固有慚德。 向克荷堂構，不墜門風。 樞因盜而振，盜憎而亡，宜哉！ 君子守道遠刑，蓋慮此也。
The historiographer comments: Jingqing conducted himself with scrupulous care in office and was truly a loyal minister. In fleeing the rebels he preserved his integrity—a mark of great moral stature. Yet he was learned, perceptive, and meticulous—how could he not have understood the balance between leniency and severity! He followed Li Linfu's wishes, indulged the clerks, curried favor with Zhang Yi, and deceived his sovereign. In life he rose to high office by flattering Li Linfu's power; in death he received a flattering posthumous title through Yuan Zai's favor. Those who claim Jingqing was no crafty courtier simply cannot be believed. Mian vigorously supported the restoration and rose to the highest offices; in serving the public and upholding righteousness he might have achieved true greatness; yet what sort of governance is it to sell offices and ordain monks for profit? In his old age his greed knew no bounds. Zunqing was learned and upright, yet Yang Guozhong posted him to the provinces; respectful, frugal, and discreet—until Xiao Hua, who had long known his worth, brought him to power. He held high office with pure conduct and grew ever more earnest in old age. The other two men might well have felt ashamed beside him. Xiang upheld the family estate and did not let its reputation decline. Shu rose through the rebel's favor; the rebel came to hate him and had him killed—how fitting! The gentleman who keeps to the Way and shuns disgrace does so with cases like this in mind.
22
贊曰：奧矣晉卿，貪哉裴冕。 遵慶父子，及之者鮮。
The encomium reads: Jingqing was subtle; Pei Mian was greedy. Few could match Zunqing and his sons.
