1
韓愈，字退之，昌黎人。 父仲卿，無名位。 愈生三歲而孤，養於從父兄。 愈自以孤子，幼刻苦學儒，不俟獎勵。 大歷、貞元之間，文字多尚古學，效楊雄、董仲舒之述作，而獨孤及、梁肅最稱淵奧，儒林推重。 愈從其徒遊，銳意鉆仰，欲自振於一代。 洎舉進士，投文於公卿間，故相鄭餘慶頗為之延譽，由是知名於時。 尋登進士第。
Han Yu, whose courtesy name was Tuizhi, was a native of Changli. His father Zhongqing held no fame or office. Yu was orphaned at the age of three and was raised by an elder cousin. As an orphan, Yu from childhood applied himself strenuously to Confucian learning, needing no encouragement. Between the Dali and Zhenyuan reigns, writers largely favored antiquarian learning and imitated the works of Yang Xiong and Dong Zhongshu. Dugu Ji and Liang Su were most praised for depth, and the scholarly world held them in high regard. Yu studied under their followers, drilling himself relentlessly in the hope of distinguishing himself in his generation. When he sat for the jinshi examination, he circulated his writings among high officials. The former chancellor Zheng Yuqing spoke highly of him, and he thereby became known in his day. He soon passed the jinshi examination.
2
宰相董晉出鎮大梁，辟為巡官。 府除，徐州張建封又請為其賓佐。 愈發言真率，無所畏避，操行堅正，拙於世務。 調授四門博士，轉監察御史。 德宗晚年，政出多門，宰相不專機務。 宮市之弊，諫官論之不聽。 愈嘗上章數千言極論之，不聽，怒貶為連州山陽令，量移江陵府掾曹。
When Chancellor Dong Jin took up command at Daliang, he recruited Yu as a touring officer. When that appointment ended, Zhang Jianfeng of Xuzhou also invited him to serve as his aide. Yu spoke with blunt sincerity and without fear or evasion. His conduct was firm and upright, but he was awkward in worldly affairs. He was appointed Four Gates Erudite and later promoted to Supervising Censor. In the late years of Emperor Dezong, government issued from many hands, and the chancellor did not monopolize state affairs. As for the abuses of the palace market, remonstrating officials spoke against them but were not heeded. Yu once submitted a memorial of several thousand characters arguing the matter at length. It went unheeded, and in anger he was demoted to magistrate of Shanyang in Lianzhou, then transferred in rank to a staff officer of Jiangling Prefecture.
3
元和初，召為國子博士，遷都官員外郎。 時華州刺史閻濟美以公事停華陰令柳澗縣務，俾攝掾曹。 居數月，濟美罷郡，出居公館，澗遂諷百姓遮道索前年軍頓役直。 後刺史趙昌按得澗罪以聞，貶房州司馬。 愈因使過華，知其事，以為刺史相黨，上疏理澗，留中不下。 詔監察御史李宗奭按驗，得澗贓狀，再貶澗封溪尉。 以愈妄論，復為國子博士。 愈自以才高，累被擯黜，作《進學解》以自喻曰：
At the beginning of the Yuanhe reign, he was summoned to serve as Erudite of the Directorate of Education and was later promoted to Vice Director of the Ministry of Revenue. At that time Yan Jimei, governor of Hua Prefecture, suspended the magistrate of Huayin, Liu Jian, from county duties on official grounds and had him serve as acting staff officer. After several months Yan Jimei left the prefecture and took lodging at the official residence. Jian then incited the people to block the road and demand payment for the previous year's military supply labor. Later Prefect Zhao Chang investigated, found Jian guilty, and reported the matter. Jian was demoted to prefectural adjutant of Fangzhou. While on a mission passing through Hua, Yu learned of the affair and believed the prefect and magistrate were in collusion. He submitted a memorial defending Jian, but it was retained in the palace and never issued. An edict ordered Supervising Censor Li Zongshi to investigate. Jian's corrupt conduct was established, and he was demoted again to assistant magistrate of Fengxi. Because Yu had argued rashly, he was returned to his post as Erudite of the Directorate of Education. Considering himself highly talented yet repeatedly rejected and demoted, Yu composed An Explication for Advancing in Learning to explain himself, saying:
4
言未既，有笑於列者曰：「先生欺予哉！ 弟子事先生，於茲有年矣。 先生口不絕吟於六藝之文，手不停披於百家之編。 記事者必提其要，纂言者必鉤其玄。 貪多務得，細大不捐。 燒膏油以繼晷，常矻矻以窮年。 先生之業，可謂勤矣。 牴排異端，攘斥佛、老； 補苴罅漏，張皇幽眇； 尋墜緒之茫茫，獨旁搜而遠紹； 障百川而東之，回狂瀾於既倒。 先生之於儒，可謂有勞矣。 沈浸醲郁，含英咀華，作為文章，其書滿家。 上規姚、姒，渾渾無涯； 《周誥》、《殷盤》，佶屈聱牙； 《春秋》謹嚴，《左氏》浮誇； 《易》奇而法，《詩》正而葩； 下迨《莊》、《騷》，太史所錄，子雲、相如，同工異曲。 先生之於文，可謂閎其中而肆其外矣。 少始知學，勇於敢為； 長通於方，左右具宜。 先生之於為人，可謂成矣。 然而公不見信於人，私不見助於友； 跋前躓後，動輒得咎。 暫為御史，遂竄南夷； 三為博士，冗不見治。 命與仇謀，取敗幾時。 冬暖而兒號寒，年豐而妻啼饑。 頭童齒豁，竟死何裨？ 不知慮此，而反教人為！」
Before he had finished speaking, someone in the assembly laughed and said, "Sir, you deceive me! Your disciple has served you, sir, for years now. Sir, your mouth never ceases reciting the texts of the Six Arts, and your hand never stops unfolding the compilations of the hundred schools. In recording affairs you always seize the essentials; in compiling words you always hook their profundities. Greedy for abundance and striving to obtain it, you let nothing large or small go. You burn lamp oil to extend the sundial's shadow, constantly toiling thus through the year. Sir, in your labors one may say you have been diligent. You repel heterodox teachings and drive off Buddhism and Daoism; you patch leaks and gaps and expand what is hidden and subtle; you seek fallen threads in vast obscurity, alone searching widely and carrying them far back; you block the hundred streams and turn them east, turning back the raging flood already fallen. Sir, in Confucian learning one may say you have labored. Immersed in rich fragrance, savoring blossoms and chewing splendor, you compose writings until your books fill the house. Above you take Yao and Si as models, vast and without limit; the Announcements of Zhou and Pan-geng of Yin are gnarled, twisted, and hard in the mouth; the Spring and Autumn Annals are strict and careful; the Zuo Tradition is inflated and extravagant; the Changes are marvelous yet patterned; the Odes are upright yet ornate; down to Zhuangzi and the Songs of Chu, what the Grand Historian recorded, Yang Yun and Sima Xiangru—same craft, different tunes. Sir, in literature one may say you are broad within and free without. In youth you first knew learning and were bold in daring action; grown, you mastered the ways, fitting all around. Sir, in conduct one may say you have succeeded. Yet publicly you are not trusted by people, and privately you are not aided by friends; stumbling forward and tripping back, at every move you meet blame. briefly made censor, then banished to the southern barbarians; three times made erudite, superfluous and unseen in governance. Fate conspires with enmity; defeat was only a matter of time. Winter is warm yet your children cry with cold; the harvest is abundant yet your wife weeps with hunger. Head bald and teeth fallen out—what good if you die at last? Not knowing to consider this, yet instead teaching people how to live!"
5
先生曰：「吁，子來前！ 夫大木為杗，細木為桷，欂櫨侏儒，椳闑扂楔，各得其宜，施以成室者，匠氏之工也。 玉劄丹砂，赤箭青芝，朱溲馬勃，敗鼓之皮，俱收並蓄，待用無遺者，醫師之良也。 登明選公，雜進巧拙，紆餘為妍，卓犖為傑，校短量長，唯器是適者，宰相之方也。 昔者，孟軻好辯，孔道以明，轍環天下，卒老於行。 茍卿守正，大論是弘，逃讒於楚，廢死蘭陵。 是二儒者，吐辭為經，舉足為法，絕類離倫，優入聖域，其遇於世何如也？ 今先生學雖勤，不由其統； 言雖多，不要其中； 文雖奇，不濟於用； 行雖修，不顯於眾。 猶且月費俸錢，歲靡廩粟，子不知耕，婦不知織，乘馬從徒，安坐而食，踵常塗之促促，窺陳編以盜竊。 然而聖主不加誅，宰臣不見斥，此非其幸哉！ 動而得謗，名亦隨之。 投閑置散，乃分之宜。 若夫商財賄之有無，計班資之崇庳，忘己量之所稱，指前人之瑕疵，是所謂詰匠氏之不以杙為楹，而訾醫師以昌陽引年，欲進其豨苓也。」
The master said, "Ah, come forward, my son! Great timbers become ridgepoles, small timbers become rafters; bracket arms, dwarf pillars, door sockets, door pins, and wedges—each gets its proper place, and applied thus a room is completed: that is the carpenter's craft. Jade thorn, cinnabar, red arrow, blue lingzhi, red soak, horse dung fungus, and the skin of a broken drum—all are gathered and stored, kept without omission for use: that is the good physician. Ascending to clarity and selecting the worthy, mixing the skillful and the clumsy, turning what is ample into beauty and what is outstanding into excellence, comparing short and measuring long, fitting only the vessel—that is the chancellor's way. In the past Mencius loved debate; the way of Confucius was thereby clarified. His carriage tracks circled the realm, yet in the end he aged on the road. Xunzi upheld the orthodox and greatly expounded the great discourse; fleeing slander in Chu, he died in disuse at Lanling. These two Confucians—when they spoke, their words became classics; when they stepped, they became models; surpassing their kind and leaving their peers, excelling into the sage's domain—how were they treated by the world? Now, sir, though your learning is diligent, it does not follow its tradition; though your words are many, they do not hit the center; though your writing is marvelous, it does not serve use; though your conduct is cultivated, it does not shine among the multitude. Yet month by month you spend salary money, year by year you exhaust granary grain; your son does not know plowing, your wife does not know weaving; riding horses with followers, you sit at ease and eat; treading the common path in haste, you peep at old compilations to steal. Yet the sage ruler does not punish you, and the chief minister does not dismiss you—is this not your good fortune! Move and you meet slander; fame follows as well. Cast into idleness and scattered posts—that is the proper allotment of your lot. As for bargaining over the presence or absence of wealth and bribes, calculating the height or lowness of rank and seniority, forgetting what your measure can bear, and pointing out flaws in predecessors—that is what is called reproaching the carpenter for not making a peg into a pillar, and blaming the physician for using changyang to extend years, wishing to advance one's poria cocos."
6
執政覽其文而憐之，以其有史才，改比部郎中、史館修撰。 逾歲，轉考功郎中、知制誥，拜中書舍人。
Those in power read his essay and pitied him. Because he had talent for history, he was appointed Director of the Ministry of Revenue and Compiler in the Historiography Institute. After a year he was transferred to Director of the Ministry of Personnel, charged with drafting edicts, and appointed Attendant in the Secretariat.
7
俄有不悅愈者，摭其舊事，言愈前左降為江陵掾曹，荊南節度使裴均館之頗厚，均子鍔凡鄙，近者鍔還省父，愈為序餞鍔，仍呼其字。 此論喧於朝列，坐是改太子右庶子。
Soon there were those displeased with Yu who dredged up his old affairs, saying that when Yu had earlier been demoted to staff officer of Jiangling, Jingnan military commissioner Pei Jun had entertained him quite generously. Jun's son E was altogether vulgar. Recently, when E returned to the capital to visit his father, Yu composed a preface to send him off and still called him by his personal name. This discussion clamored through the court ranks, and for this he was changed to Right Vice Director of the Crown Prince's Household.
8
鳳翔法門寺有護國真身塔，塔內有釋迦文佛指骨一節，其書本傳法，三十年一開，開則歲豐人泰。 十四年正月，上令中使杜英奇押宮人三十人，持香花赴臨臯驛迎佛骨。 自光順門入大內，留禁中三日，乃送諸寺。 王公士庶，奔走舍施，唯恐在後。 百姓有廢業破產、燒頂灼臂而求供養者。 愈素不喜佛，上疏諫曰：
At Famen Temple in Fengxiang stood the True Body Pagoda protecting the state. Within the pagoda was one joint of the finger bone of Shakyamuni Buddha. Its scripture transmitted the dharma; every thirty years it was opened once, and when opened the year was abundant and the people at peace. In the first month of the fourteenth year, the emperor ordered palace envoy Du Yingqi to escort thirty palace women bearing incense and flowers to welcome the Buddha bone at Lingao Post. From Guangshun Gate they entered the inner palace. It remained in the forbidden precincts three days, then was sent to the temples. Kings, dukes, scholars, and commoners rushed to give alms, fearing to come last. Among the people were those who abandoned their occupations, ruined their property, burned the crowns of their heads, and scorched their arms in order to make offerings. Yu had always disliked Buddhism and submitted a remonstrance memorial, saying:
9
漢明帝時始有佛法，明帝在位，才十八年耳。 其後亂亡相繼，運祚不長。 宋、齊、梁、陳、元魏已下，事佛漸謹，年代尤促。 唯梁武帝在位四十八年，前後三度舍身施佛，宗廟之祭，不用牲牢，晝日一食，止於菜果。 其後竟為侯景所逼，餓死臺城，國亦尋滅。 事佛求福，乃更得禍。 由此觀之，佛不足信，亦可知矣。
In the time of Emperor Ming of Han, Buddhism first appeared. Ming was on the throne only eighteen years. Afterward disorder and ruin followed in succession, and fortunes did not last long. From Song, Qi, Liang, Chen, and Northern Wei downward, reverence for Buddhism grew ever stricter, yet their dynasties were especially brief. Only Emperor Wu of Liang was on the throne forty-eight years. Thrice before and after he renounced the body and gave to Buddha. In ancestral temple sacrifices he used no livestock; by day he ate one meal, stopping at vegetables and fruit. Afterward he was finally driven by Hou Jing, starved to death at Taicheng, and the state soon perished. Seeking fortune through serving Buddha, one instead obtained calamity. Viewed thus, Buddha is not to be trusted; this too may be known.
10
高祖始受隋禪，則議除之。 當時群臣識見不遠，不能深究先王之道、古今之宜，推闡聖明，以救斯弊，其事遂止。 臣嘗恨焉！ 伏惟皇帝陛下，神聖英武，數千百年以來未有倫比。 即位之初，即不許度人為僧尼、道士，又不許別立寺觀。 臣當時以為高祖之誌，必行於陛下之手。 今縱未能即行，豈可恣之轉令盛也！
When Gaozu first received the abdication from Sui, he then discussed abolishing Buddhism. At that time the ministers' insight did not reach far. They could not deeply examine the way of former kings and what was fitting between ancient and present, extend and clarify sage illumination, and rescue this abuse. The matter then stopped. Your servant has resented this! Respectfully considering Your Majesty the Emperor—sacred, heroic, and martial—there has been no equal in several thousand years. At the beginning of your accession you did not permit people to be ordained as monks and nuns or Daoist priests, and you did not permit separate founding of temples and abbeys. Your servant at that time thought Gaozu's intent would surely be carried out by Your Majesty's hand. Now, though you cannot immediately carry it out, how can you indulge it and let it grow ever more flourishing!
11
今聞陛下令群僧迎佛骨於鳳翔，御樓以觀，舁入大內，令諸寺遞迎供養。 臣雖至愚，必知陛下不惑於佛，作此崇奉以祈福祥也。 直以年豐人樂，徇人之心，為京都士庶設詭異之觀、戲玩之具耳。 安有聖明若此而肯信此等事哉！ 然百姓愚冥，易惑難曉，茍見陛下如此，將謂真心信佛。 皆云天子大聖，猶一心敬信； 百姓微賤，於佛豈合惜身命。 所以灼頂燔指，百十為群，解衣散錢，自朝至暮。 轉相仿效，唯恐後時，老幼奔波，棄其生業。 若不即加禁遏，更歷諸寺，必有斷臂臠身以為供養者。 傷風敗俗，傳笑四方，非細事也。
Now I hear that Your Majesty has ordered the monks as a group to welcome the Buddha bone from Fengxiang, to view it from the imperial tower, carry it into the inner palace, and order the temples in turn to welcome and make offerings. Though your servant is extremely foolish, he must know that Your Majesty is not deluded by Buddha and does this reverent service to seek blessing and auspice. It is simply because the year is abundant and the people joyful, indulging people's hearts, setting up strange spectacles and playful devices for the scholars and commoners of the capital. How could there be sage illumination such as this yet willing to believe such affairs! Yet the common people are foolish and dark, easily deluded and hard to enlighten. If they see Your Majesty thus, they will say you truly believe in Buddha. All say the Son of Heaven is greatly sage, yet still single-mindedly reveres and believes; the people are slight and lowly—how should they not spare body and life for Buddha. For this they burn the crown and scorch the fingers, in groups of tens and hundreds, strip off clothes and scatter money, from morning to evening. Turning to imitate one another, fearing to come late, old and young rush about and abandon their livelihoods. If it is not immediately forbidden and stopped, and if it passes through the temples further, there will surely be those who sever arms and slice flesh to make offerings. It wounds custom and ruins morals, making laughter in the four directions—not a small matter.
12
佛本夷狄之人，與中國言語不通，衣服殊制。 口不道先王之法言，身不服先王之法行，不知君臣之義、父子之情。 假如其身尚在，奉其國命，來朝京師，陛下容而接之，不過宣政一見，禮賓一設，賜衣一襲，衛而出之於境，不令惑於眾也。 況其身死已久，枯朽之骨，兇穢之餘，豈宜以入宮禁！ 孔子曰：「敬鬼神而遠之。」 古之諸侯，行吊於國，尚令巫祝先以桃茢，祓除不祥，然後進吊。 今無故取朽穢之物，親臨觀之，巫祝不先，桃茢不用，群臣不言其非，御史不舉其失，臣實恥之。 乞以此骨付之水火，永絕根本，斷天下之疑，絕後代之惑。 使天下之人，知大聖人之所作為，出於尋常萬萬也，豈不盛哉！ 豈不快哉！ 佛如有靈，能作禍祟，凡有殃咎，宜加臣身。 上天鑒臨，臣不怨悔。
Buddha was originally a man of barbarian lands. With China his speech did not communicate and his dress was of a different system. His mouth did not speak the law-words of former kings; his body did not wear the law-conduct of former kings; he did not know the righteousness between ruler and minister or the affection between father and son. Suppose his body were still alive and he bore his state's command and came to court at the capital: Your Majesty would receive and meet him, but no more than one audience at Xuanzheng, one reception by the Office of Ritual Guests, one gift of a suit of clothes, and guards escorting him out of the border—not permitting him to delude the multitude. How much the less, when his body has long been dead, this withered rotten bone, the remainder of foul filth—should it enter the palace forbidden precinct! Confucius said, "Revere the spirits and ghosts, yet keep them at a distance." Ancient feudal lords, when conducting condolence within the state, still ordered shamans and invokers first with peach wands and reed brushes to exorcise inauspiciousness, and then advanced condolence. Now, without reason taking a rotten foul thing and personally viewing it—shamans and invokers not first, peach wands and reed brushes not used, ministers not speaking of its wrong, censors not presenting its fault—your servant is truly shamed. I beg that this bone be handed to fire and water, forever severing the root, cutting off the realm's doubts and ending later generations' delusion. Let the realm's people know that the greatly sage one's actions go beyond the ordinary by ten thousand ten thousands—would this not be magnificent! Would this not be joyful! If Buddha has spirit and can work calamity and harm, whatever misfortune and blame there is, let it be added to your servant's person. Heaven looks down and witnesses; your servant will not resent or regret.
13
疏奏，憲宗怒甚。 間一日，出疏以示宰臣，將加極法。 裴度、崔群奏曰：「韓愈上忤尊聽，誠宜得罪，然而非內懷忠懇，不避黜責，豈能至此？ 伏乞稍賜寬容，以來諫者。」 上曰：「愈言我奉佛太過，我猶為容之。 至謂東漢奉佛之後，帝王鹹致夭促，何言之乖刺也？ 愈為人臣，敢爾狂妄，固不可赦！」 於是人情驚惋，乃至國戚諸貴，亦以罪愈太重，因事言之，乃貶為潮州刺史。
When the memorial was submitted, Xianzong was extremely angry. After one day he produced the memorial to show the chief ministers and was about to impose the extreme penalty. Pei Du and Cui Qun submitted, "Han Yu above offended Your Majesty's hearing and truly ought to be punished; yet if he did not harbor loyal earnestness within and avoid demotion and blame, how could he reach this point? I humbly beg slight granted lenience, to draw remonstrators." The emperor said, "Yu says I serve Buddhism too much; I still bear with him. Yet to say that after Eastern Han served Buddhism, emperors and kings all met early death—how piercing and contrary the words! Yu as a minister of men dares thus to be arrogant and reckless—he truly cannot be pardoned!" Thereupon popular feeling was startled and grieved. Even imperial kin and various nobles, also thinking Yu's punishment too heavy, spoke on the matter. He was then demoted to prefect of Chaozhou.

愈至潮陽，上表曰：

Only he intensely loves learning and literature and has never for one day briefly ceased—truly the contemporaries pushed and approved him. Your servant in the literature of his time also had none surpassing others. As for discussing and setting forth Your Majesty's merit and virtue, it matches the Odes and Documents inside and out. Composing songs and odes, presenting them at the suburban altars, recording the feng and shan of Mount Tai, engraving the white jade tablet; spreading the great blessing facing Heaven, raising and displaying unprecedented traces of achievement; compiled in the strategies of the Odes and Documents without shame, set between Heaven and Earth without fault. Even if the ancients were reborn, your servant would not willingly yield much. Respectfully considering that Great Tang received the mandate and possessed the realm, within the four seas none failed to be subject north, south, east, and west; each region ten thousand li. Four sage rulers passed in order down to Your Majesty; personally you hear and decide; where spears and halberds were waved, none failed to follow and submit. In the fifteenth year he was summoned as Libationer of the Directorate of Education, then transferred to Vice Minister of War. It happened that Zhenzhou killed Tian Hongzheng and set up Wang Tingcou. The order made Yu go to Zhenzhou to proclaim and instruct. When Yu had arrived, he gathered army and people and instructed them on rebellion and submission. His words were earnest to the utmost; Tingcou feared and honored him. He was changed to Vice Minister of the Civil Service. He was transferred to metropolitan governor of Jingzhao, concurrently Grand Censor. Because he did not attend the censorate assembly, he was impeached by Vice Censor-in-Chief Li Shen. Yu did not submit; he said that by edict standard he still did not attend the censorate assembly. Shen and Yu were both by nature narrow and eccentric. Transfer memorials went back and forth, clamoring without stop. Shen was then sent out as Zhexi observation commissioner, and Yu also was removed as governor and made Vice Minister of War. Yu's nature was broad and penetrating. In befriending people, glory and decline did not change. In youth he was friendly with Meng Jiao of Luoyang and Zhang Ji of Dongjun. The two men's fame and position were not yet risen. Yu did not avoid cold and heat, praising and recommending them among ministers and grandees. Ji in the end completed the degree and was honored in salary office. Later, though passing to high honor, whenever he retired from public duty's gaps, they together talked and feasted, discussed literature and composed poetry, as in former days. Yet viewing various powerful gates and heroic gentlemen like servants and slaves, he stared without regard. Yet he was quite able to entice and encourage later comers. Those he lodged were six or seven out of ten. Though morning cooking was not supplied, he was pleased and did not mind. Broadly he took raising the name of teaching and broadly encouraging benevolence and righteousness as his affairs. In all he married off nearly ten orphaned daughters inside and outside the family and among friends.

He always thought that from Wei and Jin onward, those who wrote literature mostly clung to parallel couplets, while the directives of classics and edicts and the spirit and style of Sima Qian and Yang Xiong no longer revived. Therefore what Yu did in literature strove to reverse recent forms; expressing intent and establishing words, he himself became one family's new language. Later-learning scholars took him as teacher and model. Writers of the time were very many; none could surpass him; therefore the age called it "Han wen." Yet at times relying on talent he acted willfully; there were also points that overturned Confucius and Mencius. For example, southerners falsely took Liu Zongyuan as the god of Luo Pool, and Yu composed a stele inscription to substantiate it; Li He's father's name was Jin; he ought not take the jinshi examination, yet Yu composed On the Taboo for He, making him take the jinshi; again he composed Biography of Brush Mao, mocking play not near human feeling: these are the greatest errors in his literature. Muzong and Wenzong once ordered historiographers to add and revise. At that time Yu's sons-in-law Li Han and Jiang Xi were in prominent position; various lords found it difficult. Yet Wei Chuhou finally separately compiled the Veritable Record of Emperor Shunzong in three scrolls. He had a collected works in forty scrolls; Li Han composed the preface for it.

Tang Qu sat for the jinshi examination for a long time without passing. He could compose songs and poetry; his intent was much moved and aroused. Seeing others' writings with matters that wounded and sighed, after reading he always wept, tears and mucus not stopping. Whenever he discussed with people, after they parted he uttered one cry. His voice and words were mournful and cutting; those who heard were all bleak and wept. He once traveled as a guest to Taiyuan. It happened the military commander held an army feast, and Qu was able to join the meeting. When wine was deep and he spoke of affairs, he cried with raised voice. The whole seat was not joyful; for this the meeting was dismissed. Therefore the age called Tang Qu good at weeping. Left Reminder Bai Juyi sent him a poem, saying, "Jia Yi wept at current affairs, Ruan Ji wept at crossroads. Tang Sheng now also weeps; a different age, the same grief. Who is Tang Sheng? Fifty, cold and also hungry. Not grieving mouth without food, not grieving body without clothes. What he grieves is loyalty and righteousness; when grief is extreme then he weeps it. Li Ao, whose courtesy name was Xizhi, was a descendant of Prince Wuzhao of Liang. His father Chujin was judicial aide of Beizhou. Ao in youth was diligent in Confucian learning, broadly elegant and loving antiquity. In writing he prized spirit and substance. He passed the jinshi degree and was appointed proofreader. He was promoted three times to Registrar of the Jingzhao metropolitan prefecture. At the beginning of Yuanhe he was transferred to Erudite of the Directorate of Education and Compiler in the Historiography Institute.

In the fourteenth year Court of Imperial Sacrifices Aide Wang Jing submitted a memorial requesting removal of morning and new-moon offerings at the ancestral temple. An edict ordered the hundred officials to discuss. Discussants cited the Kaiyuan Rites: the ancestral temple each year had yu, ci, zheng, chang, and la—five offerings in all. At the end of Tianbao Emperor Xuanzong ordered the Imperial Kitchen each month on new and full moon to prepare regular food, ordering the palace women's office to present food at the ancestral temple. Afterward it became constant. From this on new and full moon he did not hold court audience, compared to great sacrifices. Ao submitted a discussion, saying:

Respectfully considering ancestral temple offerings—baskets, beans, livestock, and victims—the common rites of the Three Dynasties, this is the meaning of valuing sincerity. Offerings at imperial tombs changed to use regular food; Qin and Han expedient institutions—this is the way of food flavors. Now presenting food on new and full moon at the ancestral temple—is this not using regular daily flavors and valuing many kinds? Moreover it is not the meaning in the Rites of "supreme reverence does not feast on flavor but values qi and odor." The Tradition says: Qu Dao loved water chestnuts. Having illness, he summoned his clan elders and entrusted them, saying, "When you sacrifice to me you must use water chestnuts." At the sacrifice they presented water chestnuts. His son disobeyed the command, removed water chestnuts and used sheep, presenting baskets, beans, dried meat, and pickled meats. Gentlemen approved this. Speaking of serving ancestors and forefathers, one ought to take ritual as weighty, not presenting what they loved while alive—this clearly means it is not eating flavor. Then presenting regular food at the ancestral temple—is it not comparable to water chestnuts? Moreover it is not what the sage kings of the Three Dynasties practiced. Moreover sacrificial vessels do not display meat and beans; sacrificial officers are not ordered by the Three Dukes; those executing affairs are only the palace women's director and the Director of the Imperial Clan. Calling it presenting food—how can it be taken as sacrifice? Moreover at seasonal offerings in the ancestral temple, officials act by proxy. The prayer text says, "Filial great-grandson the emperor, your servant so-and-so, respectfully sends Grand Marshal so-and-so, daring to announce to Gaozu the Divine Martial Emperor and ancestral consort Empress Taimu of the Dou clan. The season is early spring; forever cherishing boundless longing. Respectfully with one great martial victim, soft fur and hard bristles, bright grain and fragrant millet, fine vegetables and fine offerings, sacrificial ale, respectfully performing the seasonal offering to express pursuing remembrance." This is the prayer wording. Seven days before the offering at dawn, the Grand Marshal swore the hundred officials at the Ministry of Civil Service, saying, "On such month such day the seasonal offering at the ancestral temple—each display his duty. Not supplying his affair—the state has constant punishment." In all, accompanying officials fasted in dispersal four days, reached fasting three days, and then could perform sacrifice. Ancestral temple ritual—not daring to discuss rashly; though there were those who knew, who dared speak? Therefore for sixty-plus years it was practiced without abolition. Now the sage court, since bows and arrows are sheathed and ritual and music are great, therefore sent down the hundred officials for detailed discussion. We your servants think the Zhenguan and Kaiyuan Rites both lack text on ancestral temple food presentation. Cutting feeling by ritual, abolishing it is possible. As for tomb food presentation, adopting the Guoyu and Liji words on daily and monthly sacrifice, following Qin and Han institutions, repairing and preserving to broaden filial way is possible. Like this, then classic meaning may be relied on and old precedents not lost. Great ritual being clear, forever ending divergent discussion, can continue the two emperors and three kings and become law for ten thousand generations. Compared with violating ritual and surpassing antiquity, valuing following precedent and fearing reform—it is like Heaven and Earth far apart.

Shuwen wished greatly to employ him. It happened he was in position not long. Shuwen failed, and with the same cohort seven men all were demoted. Zongyuan was made Shaozhou prefect. On the road, he was again demoted to Yongzhou adjutant. Having suffered banishment and expulsion, treading barbarian miasma through rugged blockage and calamity, he harbored the Sao poets' gloomy grief. Writing feeling and narrating affairs, whenever he moved he must use literature. He composed more than ten pieces of Sao literature. Viewers were bleak for them.
46
柳州土俗，以男女質錢，過期則沒入錢主，宗元革其鄉法。 其已沒者，仍出私錢贖之，歸其父母。 江嶺間為進士者，不遠數千里皆隨宗元師法； 凡經其門，必為名士。 著述之盛，名動於時，時號柳州云。 有文集四十卷。
Liuzhou local custom used men and women as pledge for money. When expired they were confiscated to the money master. Zongyuan abolished that village law. Those already confiscated, he still produced private money to redeem them and return them to their parents. Among the Jiang and Ling ranges those becoming jinshi scholars, not far several thousand li all followed Zongyuan's teacher method. In all passing his gate, they must become famous scholars. Writings' flourishing, fame moving in the time, the age called him Liuzhou. He had collected works in forty scrolls.
47
韋辭，字踐之。 祖召卿，洛陽丞。 父翃，官至侍御史。 辭少以兩經擢第，判入等，為秘書省校書郎。 貞元末，東都留守韋夏卿辟為從事。 後累佐使府，皆以參畫稱職， 自藍田令入拜侍御史，以事累出為朗州刺史，再貶江州司馬。
Han Ci, whose courtesy name was Jianzhi. His grandfather Zhaoging was Luoyang assistant magistrate. His father Hong, office reached Attending Censor. Ci in youth with the two classics passed selection, judgment entered rank, and became Secretariat proofreader. At the end of Zhenyuan eastern capital resident guardian Wei Xiaqing recruited him as staff member. Later repeatedly assisting envoy staffs, all by planning and drawing were called fulfilling duty. From Lantian magistrate he entered appointment as Attending Censor. Because of affairs he was repeatedly sent out as Langzhou prefect and again demoted to Jiangzhou adjutant.
48
長慶初，韋處厚、路隨以公望居顯要，素知辭有文學理行，亟稱薦之。 擢為戶部員外，轉刑部郎中，充京西北和糴使。 尋為戶部郎中、兼御史中丞，充鹽鐵副使，轉吏部郎中。 文宗即位，韋處厚執政，且以澄汰浮華、登用藝實為事，乃以辭與李翺同拜中書舍人。
At the beginning of Changqing Wei Chuhou and Lu Sui with public expectation occupied prominent importance. They always knew Ci had literary learning and principle in conduct and urgently praised and recommended him. He was promoted to Vice Director of the Ministry of Revenue, transferred to Director of the Ministry of Justice, filling northwest capital He grain commissioner. Soon Director of the Ministry of Revenue, concurrently Vice Censor-in-Chief, filling salt and iron vice commissioner, he was transferred to Director of the Ministry of Personnel. When Wenzong ascended, Wei Chuhou held government and with clarifying and washing flashy floatation and elevating artistic substance as affairs. Therefore Ci and Li Ao together were appointed Attendants in the Secretariat.
49
辭素無清藻，文筆不過中才，然處事端實，遊官無黨。 與李翺特相善，俱擅文學高名。 疏達自用，不事檢操。 處厚以激時用，頗不厭公論； 辭亦倦於潤色，苦求外任。 乃出為潭州刺史、御史中丞、湖南觀察使。 在鎮二年，吏民稱治。 卒，時年五十八，贈右散騎常侍。
Ci originally had no clear ornament. His literature brush did not pass middle talent. Yet handling affairs upright and solid, wandering office without faction. With Li Ao especially mutually good, both excelled in literary learning and high fame. Loose and penetrating self-using, not serving restraint in conduct. Chuhou with stirring the time's employment, quite not satisfied public discussion. Ci also wearied of polishing and bitterly sought outer appointment. He was then sent out as Tanzhou prefect, Vice Censor-in-Chief, and Hunan observation commissioner. At command two years, officials and people called it well governed. He died, aged fifty-eight. He was posthumously made Right Regular Mounted Attendant.
50
史臣曰：貞元、太和之間，以文學聳動搢紳之伍者，宗元、禹錫而已。 其巧麗淵博，屬辭比事，誠一代之宏才。 如俾之詠歌帝載，黼藻王言，足以平揖古賢，氣吞時輩。 而蹈道不謹，昵比小人，自致流離，前隳素業。 故君子群而不黨，戒懼慎獨，正為此也。 韓、李二文公，於陵遲之末，遑遑仁義； 有志於持世範，欲以人文化成，而道未果也。 至若抑楊、墨，排釋、老，雖於道未弘，亦端士之用心也。
The historian says: Between Zhenyuan and Taihe, those who with literature stirred the gentry ranks were only Zongyuan and Yuxi. Their clever ornament and profound breadth, arranging words and comparing affairs, were truly one generation's great talent. Like employing them to sing and praise the emperor's records and embroider and adorn royal words, they would be sufficient to level bow to ancient worthies and spirit-swallow contemporaries. Yet treading the Way not carefully, drawing near and comparing small men, they self-caused wandering exile, before destroying plain achievement. Therefore the gentleman groups yet does not faction, vigilance and fear in careful solitude—rightly for this. The two literary lords Han and Li, at the end of decline, urgently pursued benevolence and righteousness. Having intent in upholding the age's model, wishing with human culture to transform and complete, yet the Way was not realized. As for suppressing Yang and Mo and repelling Buddhism and Daoism—though in the Way not broadly spread, also upright gentlemen's intent.
51
贊曰：天地經綸，無出斯文。 愈、翺揮翰，語切典墳。 犧雞斷尾，害馬敗群。 僻塗自噬，劉、柳諸君。
The eulogy says: Heaven and Earth are woven through order—nothing lies outside this culture. Han Yu and Li Ao wield the brush; their words cut straight to the classics and tombs. The sacrificial cock cuts off its tail; the harmful horse ruins the herd. On crooked paths they devour themselves—the two Lius and their company.
