李乂李乂，本名尚真，趙州房子人也。 少與兄尚一、尚貞俱以文章見稱，舉進士。 景龍中，累遷中書舍人。 時中宗遣使江南分道贖生，以所在官物充直。 乂上疏曰：「江南水鄉，采捕為業，魚鱉之利，黎元所資，土地使然，有自來矣。 伏以聖慈含育，恩周動植，布天下之大德，及鱗介之微品。 雖雲雨之私，有霑於末類； 而生成之惠，未洽於平人。 何則？ 江湖之饒，生育無限； 府庫之用，支供易殫。 費之若少，則所濟何成； 用之倘多，則常支有闕。 在於拯物，豈若憂人。 且鬻生之徒，唯利斯視，錢刀日至，網罟年滋，施之一朝，營之百倍，未若回救贖之錢物，減困貧之徭賦，活國愛人，其福勝彼。 」
Li Yi, whose original name was Shangzhen, came from Fangzi in Zhao Prefecture. In his youth he and his elder brothers Shangyi and Shangzhen were all renowned for their writing, and each passed the jinshi examination. During the Jinglong period he rose through several posts to Secretariat Drafter. At that time Emperor Zhongzong dispatched envoys to the south by separate routes to buy back captive animals, paying with government goods found in each locality. Yi submitted a memorial, saying: "The Jiangnan region is a country of rivers and lakes where people live by gathering and fishing. Fish and turtles are what the common folk depend on; the land has made it so, and this has long been the case. I humbly reflect that Your Majesty's compassionate nurture embraces all living things, extending grace to every creature that moves or grows, and spreading great virtue across the realm even to the lowliest fish and shellfish. Though the private blessing of rain may reach even the humblest creatures, the grace that sustains life has not yet reached the common people. Why is this? The rivers and lakes teem with life beyond counting; yet the treasury's resources are soon spent in paying for them. If the expense is small, little can actually be saved; if it is large, the ordinary budget will fall short. When the aim is to rescue creatures, how does that compare with relieving the distress of people? Moreover, those who traffic in living creatures care only for profit. Money changes hands daily, and nets and traps multiply year by year. What is spent in a single day returns a hundredfold to their trade. Far better to redirect the funds used for ransom, lighten the corvée and taxes borne by the poor, and revive the state while caring for the people—the blessing would far exceed that. "
2
乂知制誥凡數載。 景雲元年，遷吏部侍郎，與宋璟、盧從願同時典選，銓敘平允，甚為當時所稱。 尋轉黃門侍郎。 時睿宗令造金仙、玉真二觀，乂頻上疏諫，帝每優容之。 開元初，特令乂與中書侍郎蘇颋纂集起居註，錄其嘉謨昌言可體國經遠者，別編奏之。 乂在門下，多所駁正。 開元初，姚崇為紫微令，薦乂為紫微侍郎，外托薦賢，其實引在己下，去其糾駁之權也。 俄拜刑部尚書。 乂方雅有學識，朝廷稱其有宰相之望，會病卒。 兄尚一，清源尉，早卒； 尚貞，官至博州刺史。 兄弟同為一集，號曰《李氏花萼集》，總二十卷。 薛登薛登，本名謙光，常州義興人也。 父士通，大業中為鷹揚郎將。 江都之亂，士通與鄉人聞人嗣安等同據本郡，以禦寇賊。 武德二年，遣使歸國，高祖嘉之，降璽書勞勉，拜東武州刺史。 俄而輔公祏於江都構逆，遣其將西門君儀等寇常州，士通率兵拒戰，大破之，君儀等僅以身免。 及公祏平，累功封臨汾侯。 貞觀初，歷遷泉州刺史，卒。
Yi drafted edicts and ordinances for several years. In the first year of the Jingyun era he was promoted to Vice Minister of Personnel. He served concurrently with Song Jing and Lu Congyuan as overseer of appointments, and his evaluations were so even-handed that contemporaries spoke highly of him. He was soon transferred to Vice Minister of the Yellow Gate. When Emperor Ruizong ordered the construction of the Jinxian and Yuzhen monasteries, Yi repeatedly submitted memorials in protest, and each time the emperor received him with gracious forbearance. Early in the Kaiyuan reign he was specially ordered, together with Vice Minister of the Secretariat Su Ting, to compile the court diaries and to select from them excellent counsels and far-sighted words fit to guide the state, which they then submitted in a separate compilation. While serving at the Gate Department, Yi rejected and corrected many drafts. Early in Kaiyuan, Yao Chong was Director of the Purple Forbidden Office and recommended Yi as Vice Director. Outwardly he claimed to be promoting talent; in truth he placed Yi beneath himself and stripped him of his power to criticize and reject drafts. Before long he was appointed Minister of Justice. Yi was refined, learned, and accomplished, and the court regarded him as a man with the makings of a chief minister, but he died of illness. His elder brother Shangyi, magistrate of Qingyuan, died young; Shangzhen rose to serve as prefect of Bo. The brothers jointly compiled a collection entitled Collected Writings of the Li Family at the Flower Calyx, twenty scrolls in all. Xue Deng, whose original name was Qianguang, came from Yixing in Chang Prefecture. His father Shitong served during the Daye reign as General of Soaring Hawks. During the upheaval at Jiangdu, Shitong joined his fellow townsman Wenren Sian and others in holding their home commandery against bandits and rebels. In the second year of Wude he sent envoys to submit to the new dynasty. Emperor Gaozu commended him, issued an imperial letter of encouragement, and appointed him prefect of Dongwu. Before long Fu Gongshi rebelled at Jiangdu and sent his generals Ximen Junyi and others to attack Chang Prefecture. Shitong led troops against them, won a great victory, and Junyi and his men barely escaped with their lives. After Gongshi was suppressed, Shitong's accumulated merit earned him the title Marquis of Linfen. Early in the Zhenguan reign he rose through several posts to prefect of Quan, where he died.

謙光博涉文史，每與人談論前代故事，必廣引證驗，有如目擊。 少與徐堅、劉子玄齊名友善。 文明中，解褐閬中主簿。 天授中，為左補闕，時選舉頗濫，謙光上疏曰：
Qianguang was widely versed in literature and history. Whenever he discussed events of earlier ages, he cited extensive evidence with the vividness of an eyewitness. In his youth he was ranked alongside Xu Jian and Liu Zixuan and was close friends with both. During the Wenming reign he entered office as registrar of Langzhong. During the Tianshou reign he served as Left Remonstrator. Appointments at the time were notoriously lax, and Qianguang submitted a memorial, saying:

尋轉水部員外郎，累遷給事中、檢校常州刺史。 屬宣州狂寇硃大目作亂，百姓奔走，謙光嚴備安輯，闔境肅然。 轉刑部侍郎，加銀青光祿大夫，再遷尚書左丞。 景雲中，擢拜御史大夫。 時僧惠範恃太平公主權勢，逼奪百姓店肆，州縣不能理。 謙光將加彈奏，或請寢之，謙光曰：「憲臺理冤滯，何所回避，朝彈暮黜，亦可矣。 」遂與殿中慕容玽奏彈之，反為太平公主所構，出為岐州刺史。 惠範既誅，遷太子賓客，轉刑部尚書，加金紫光祿大夫、昭文館學士。 開元初，為東都留守，又轉太子賓客。 以與太子同名，表請行字，特敕賜名登。 尋以孽子悅千牛為憲司所劾，放歸田裏。 朝廷以其家貧，又特給致仕祿。 七年卒，年七十三，贈晉州刺史。 撰《四時記》二十卷。 韋湊韋湊，京兆萬年人。 曾祖瓚，隋尚書右丞。 祖叔諧，蒲州刺史。 父玄，桂州都督府長史。 湊，永淳二年，解褐授婺州參軍，累轉揚府法曹參軍。 州人前仁壽令孟神爽豪縱，數犯法，交通貴戚，前後官吏莫敢繩按，湊白長史張潛，請因事除之。 會神爽坐事推問，湊無所假借，神爽妄稱有密旨，究問引虛，遂杖殺之，遠近稱伏。 湊，景龍中歷遷將作少匠、司農少卿。 嘗以公事忤宗楚客，出為貝州刺史。
He was soon transferred to Vice Director of the Ministry of Works for Water Control, and eventually rose to Supervising Censor and acting prefect of Chang. When the rebel Zhu Damu of Xuan Prefecture rose in revolt and the people fled in panic, Qianguang made strict preparations to restore order, and the whole jurisdiction became calm. He was transferred to Vice Minister of Justice, granted the additional title Grand Master of Splendid Brightness with Silver Seal, and later promoted to Left Assistant Director of the Department of State Affairs. During the Jingyun period he was promoted to Censor-in-Chief. At that time the monk Huifan, relying on Princess Taiping's power, seized shops from common people by force, and local officials could do nothing about it. Qianguang was about to impeach him, and some urged him to let the matter drop. Qianguang said, "The Censorate exists to redress injustice—what is there to avoid? To be impeached in the morning and dismissed by evening would be reward enough." " He then joined Palace Censor Murong Ting in impeaching Huifan, but was in turn framed by Princess Taiping and sent out as prefect of Qi. After Huifan was executed, he was made Mentor of the Heir Apparent, then Minister of Justice, with the additional titles Grand Master of Splendid Brightness with Gold Seal and Scholar of the Zhaowen Hall. Early in Kaiyuan he served as defender of the eastern capital, then again as Mentor of the Heir Apparent. Because he shared the heir apparent's personal name, he petitioned to use his style name instead, and an edict specially granted him the name Deng. Before long his illegitimate son Yue, a member of the Thousand-Ox Guard, was impeached by the censorate, and Deng was dismissed and sent home. Because his family was poor, the court also specially granted him a retirement stipend. He died in the seventh year at the age of seventy-three and was posthumously appointed prefect of Jin. He compiled Records of the Four Seasons in twenty scrolls. Wei Cou came from Wannian in Jingzhao. His great-grandfather Zan served the Sui as Right Assistant Director of the Department of State Affairs. His grandfather Shuxie served as prefect of Pu. His father Xuan was chief administrator of the Guizhou area command. Cou entered office in the second year of Yongchun as military aide of Wu Prefecture and eventually rose to legal aide of Yang Prefecture. A former magistrate of Renshou named Meng Shenshuang in the prefecture was arrogant and lawless, repeatedly broke the law, and cultivated connections with powerful families. Successive officials dared not prosecute him. Cou reported to chief administrator Zhang Qian and asked to remove him when opportunity offered. When Shenshuang was implicated in a case and interrogated, Cou showed him no leniency. Shenshuang falsely claimed a secret edict protected him, but investigation proved the claim false, and he was beaten to death. People near and far applauded the result. During the Jinglong period Cou rose through several posts to Vice Director of the Directorate for Palace Buildings and Vice Minister of the Directorate of Agriculture. Once, on official business, he offended Zong Chuke and was sent out as prefect of Bei.

睿宗即位，拜鴻臚少卿，加銀青光祿大夫。 景雲二年，轉太府少卿，又兼通事舍人。 時改葬節湣太子，優詔加謚； 又雪李多祚等罪，還其官爵，仍議更加贈官。 湊上書曰：
When Emperor Ruizong took the throne, Cou was appointed Vice Director of the Court for Diplomatic Reception and granted the additional title Grand Master of Splendid Brightness with Silver Seal. In the second year of Jingyun he was transferred to Vice Director of the Court of the Imperial Treasury and also served concurrently as imperial protocol officer. At that time the Jiemin Crown Prince was being reburied, and a gracious edict added a posthumous title; the crimes of Li Duozuo and others were also cleared, their offices and titles restored, and officials still debated granting them further posthumous honors. Cou submitted a memorial, saying:
6
書奏，睿宗引湊謂曰：「誠如卿言。 事已如此，如何改動？ 」湊曰：「太子實行悖逆，不可褒美，請稱其行，改謚以一字。 多祚等以兵犯君，非曰無罪，只可雲放，不可稱雪。 」帝然其言。 當時執政以制令已行，難於改易，唯多祚等停贈官而已。
When the memorial was submitted, Emperor Ruizong summoned Cou and said, "What you say is true. But matters have already gone this far—how can they be changed?" " Cou said, "The crown prince truly acted in rebellion and must not be praised. Describe his conduct and change the posthumous title by a single character. Duozuo and others took up arms against their ruler—they were not without guilt. Say only that they were released; do not call it clearing their crimes." " The emperor approved his words. Those then in power held that the ordinance had already been issued and was hard to change; only the additional posthumous offices for Duozuo and his associates were halted.
7
明年春，起金仙、玉真兩觀，用工巨億。 湊進諫曰：「陛下去夏，以妨農停兩觀作，今正農月，翻欲興功。 雖知用公主錢，不出庫物，但土木作起，高價雇人，三輔農人，趨目前之利，舍農受雇，棄本逐末。 臣聞一夫不耕，天下有受其饑者，臣竊恐不可。 」帝不應。 湊又奏曰：「日陽和布氣，萬物生育，土木之間，昆蟲無數。 此時興造，傷殺甚多，臣亦恐非仁聖本旨。 」睿宗方納其言，令在外詳議。 中書令崔湜、侍中岑羲謂湊曰：「公敢言此，大是難事。 」湊曰：「叨食厚祿，死且不辭，況在明時，必知不死。 」尋出為陜州刺史，無幾，轉汝州刺史。 開元二年夏，敕靖陵建碑，征料夫匠。 湊以自古園陵無建碑之禮，又時正旱儉，不可興功，飛表極諫，工役乃止。 尋遷岐州剌史。
The following spring work began on the Jinxian and Yuzhen monasteries at a cost of hundreds of millions. Cou remonstrated, saying, "Your Majesty halted work on the two monasteries last summer because it interfered with farming. Now, in the very heart of the farming season, you wish to resume construction. Although the princess's funds are used and nothing is drawn from the treasury, once earthworks and timber construction begin, laborers are hired at high wages. Farmers of the capital region, chasing immediate profit, abandon their fields for hire and forsake the essential for the trivial. I have heard that if one man does not plow, someone under Heaven goes hungry because of it. I fear this cannot be permitted." " The emperor did not reply. Cou submitted again, saying, "The sun now spreads harmonious warmth and the ten thousand things are born and grow. Between earthworks and timber there are countless insects. To build at this season kills and injures a great many of them. I also fear this is not the intent of a benevolent sage-ruler." " Only then did Emperor Ruizong accept his words and order the matter discussed in detail outside court. Chief Minister of the Secretariat Cui Shi and Palace Attendant Cen Yi said to Cou, "Sir, for you to speak thus was a very bold thing." " Cou said, "I enjoy an undeserved generous stipend and would not refuse death. How much more in an enlightened age, where one may be sure not to die." " Before long he was sent out as prefect of Shan, and soon afterward transferred to prefect of Ru. In the summer of the second year of Kaiyuan an edict ordered a stele erected at Jing Mausoleum and laborers and craftsmen requisitioned. Cou argued that imperial mausoleums had never had the ritual of erecting steles from antiquity, and that drought and scarcity made construction impossible at the time. He sent an urgent memorial of the strongest protest, and the labor was halted. He was soon transferred to prefect of Qi.

湊前後上書論時政得失，多見采納。 再遷河南尹，累封彭城郡公。 以公事左授杭州刺史，轉汾州刺史。 十年，拜太原尹兼節度支度營田大使。 其年卒官，年六十五。 贈幽州都督，謚曰文。 子見素，自有傳。 湊從子虛心。 湊從子虛心虛心父維，少習儒業，博涉文史，舉進士。 自大理丞累至戶部郎中，善於剖判，時員外郎宋之問工於詩，時人以為戶部有二妙。 終於左庶子。 虛心舉孝廉，為官嚴整，累至大理丞、侍御史。 神龍年，推按大獄，時僕射竇懷貞、侍中劉幽求意欲寬假，虛心堅執法令，有不可奪之志。 景龍中，西域羌胡背叛，時並擒獲，有敕盡欲誅之。 虛心論奏，但罪元首，其所全者千餘人。 虛心有孝行，及丁父憂，哀毀過禮，鬚鬢盡白，朝廷深所嗟尚。 後遷御史中丞、左右丞、兵部侍郎、荊揚潞長史兼采訪使，所在官吏振肅，威令甚舉，中外以為標準。 歷戶部尚書、東京留守，卒，年六十七。 虛心季弟虛舟季弟虛舟，亦以舉孝廉，自御史累至戶部、司勛、左司郎中，歷荊州長史，洪、魏州刺史兼采訪使，多著能政。 入為刑部侍郎，終大理卿。 家有禮則，父子兄弟更踐郎署，時稱「郎官家」。 韓思復韓思復，京兆長安人也。 祖倫，貞觀中為左衛率，賜爵長山縣男。 思復少襲祖爵。 初為汴州司戶參軍，為政寬恕，不行杖罰。 在任丁憂，家貧，鬻薪終喪制。 時姚崇為夏官侍郎，知政事，深嘉嘆之，擢授司禮博士。
Cou submitted memorial after memorial on the strengths and failings of current policy, and many of his recommendations were adopted. He was again promoted to Intendant of Henan and was enfeoffed as Duke of Pengcheng Commandery. On account of an official matter he was demoted to prefect of Hang, then transferred to prefect of Fen. In the tenth year he was appointed intendant of Taiyuan and concurrently commissioner for military affairs, finance, and agricultural colonies. That year he died in office at the age of sixty-five. He was posthumously appointed area commander of You and given the posthumous title Wen. His son Jiansu has his own biography. Cou had a nephew named Xuxin. Cou's nephew Xuxin: Xuxin's father Wei studied Confucian learning in his youth, was widely versed in literature and history, and passed the jinshi examination. He rose from assistant director of the Court of Judicial Review to director in the Ministry of Revenue and was skilled in legal analysis. At the time Vice Director Song Zhiwen excelled in poetry, and contemporaries said the Ministry of Revenue had two outstanding talents. He ended his career as Left Assistant to the Heir Apparent. Xuxin was recommended as Filial and Incorrupt, served with stern rectitude, and rose to assistant director of the Court of Judicial Review and attending censor. During the Shenlong reign he investigated a major prison case. Vice Director Dou HuaiZhen and Palace Attendant Liu Youqiu wished to show leniency, but Xuxin firmly upheld the law with an unshakable will. During the Jinglong period the Qiang and Hu of the western regions rebelled. When they were captured, an edict ordered that all of them be executed. Xuxin submitted a memorial arguing that only the ringleaders should be punished, thereby sparing more than a thousand people. Xuxin was known for filial devotion. When his father died, his grief and emaciation exceeded what ritual required; his beard and temples turned completely white, and the court deeply admired him for it. He later served as vice censor-in-chief, left and right assistant director, vice minister of war, and chief administrator of Jing, Yang, and Lu with concurrent investigating commissioner. Wherever he served, officials were stirred to rectitude and his authority was vigorously enforced; court and countryside alike took him as a standard. He served as Minister of Revenue and defender of the eastern capital, then died at the age of sixty-seven. Xuxin's younger brother Xuzhou was also recommended as Filial and Incorrupt. He rose from censor to director in the ministries of Revenue, Personnel Evaluation, and the Left Department, served as chief administrator of Jing and as prefect of Hong and Wei with concurrent investigating commissioner, and earned a reputation for capable administration. He entered court as Vice Minister of Justice and ended his career as director of the Court of Judicial Review. The family observed ritual propriety; father, sons, and brothers in turn held posts in the secretariat bureaus, and contemporaries called them "the Bureau-Official Family." Han Sifu came from Chang'an in Jingzhao. His grandfather Lun served in the Zhenguan era as Commander of the Left Guard and was granted the title Baron of Changshan County. Sifu inherited his grandfather's title in his youth. He first served as revenue aide of Bian Prefecture, governing with leniency and never imposing corporal punishment. While in office he entered mourning for a parent. His family was poor, and he sold firewood to complete the mourning period. Yao Chong was then vice minister of the Ministry of War and handling government affairs. He deeply admired Sifu's conduct and promoted him to Erudite of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices.
11
景龍中，累遷給事中。 時左散騎常侍嚴善思坐譙王重福事下制獄，有司言：「善思昔嘗任汝州刺史，素與重福交遊，召至京師，竟不言其謀逆，唯奏雲『東都有兵氣』。 據狀正當匿反，請從絞刑。 」思復駁奏曰：「議獄緩死，列聖明規； 刑疑從輕，有國常典。 嚴善思往在先朝，屬韋氏擅內，恃寵宮掖，謀危宗社。 善思此時遂能先覺，因詣相府有所發明，進論聖躬必登宸極。 雖交遊重福，蓋謀陷韋氏。 及其謁見，猶不奏聞，將此行藏，即從極法。 且敕追善思，書至便發，向懷逆節，寧即奔命？ 一面疏網，誠合順生； 三驅取禽，來而可宥。 惟刑是恤，事合昭詳。 請付刑部集群官議定奏裁，以符慎獄。 」是時議者多雲善思合從原宥，有司仍執前議請誅之。 思復又駁曰：「臣聞刑人於市，爵人於朝，必僉謀攸同，始行之無惑。 謹按諸司所議，嚴善思十才一入，抵罪惟輕。 夫帝閽九重，塗遠千里。 故借天下之耳以聽，聽無不聰； 借天下之目以視，視無不接。 今群言上聞，采擇宜審，若棄多就少，臣實懼焉。 輿誦一乖，下情不達，雖欲從眾，其可及乎！ 凡百京司，逢時之泰，列官分職，有賢有親。 親則列籓諸王，陛下愛子； 賢則胙茅開國，陛下名臣。 見無禮於君，寧肯雷同不異？ 今措詞多出，法令從輕。 」上納其奏，竟免善思死，配流靜州。 思復尋轉中書舍人，數上疏陳得失，多見納用。
During the Jinglong period he rose to Supervising Censor. At that time Left Regular Attendant Yan Shansi was implicated in the affair of Prince Qiao Chongfu and sent to the imperial prison. The responsible offices argued: "Shansi had once served as prefect of Ru and was on familiar terms with Chongfu. When summoned to the capital he never reported Chongfu's plot to rebel, but only submitted that 'there is a martial aura in the eastern capital.'" According to the facts he properly concealed rebellion and should be sentenced to strangulation. " Sifu submitted a rebuttal memorial, saying, "Deliberating on prison cases and mitigating death sentences is the enlightened rule of successive sage emperors; when punishment is doubtful, follow the lighter penalty—this is the standing statute of the state. Yan Shansi served in the previous reign when the Wei clan usurped power within the palace, relying on favor in the inner quarters and plotting to endanger the dynasty. At that time Shansi perceived the danger in advance, went to the chief minister's residence and made certain disclosures, and argued that Your Majesty must ascend the throne. Although he associated with Chongfu, this was in effect a plot to entrap the Wei clan. When he had an audience he still did not report the plot. To punish this conduct with the extreme penalty— Moreover, when an edict summoned Shansi, he set out as soon as the letter arrived. If he had harbored rebellious intent, would he have hastened to obey? To loosen the net on one side truly accords with sparing life; in the three-sided hunt, game that comes forward may be pardoned. To be concerned only with punishment is to make the matter clear and thorough. I request that the case be referred to the Ministry of Justice for deliberation by assembled officials and a memorialized decision, in accord with cautious handling of prison cases." " At that time many in the deliberations favored pardoning Shansi, but the responsible offices still held to the earlier opinion and requested execution. Sifu submitted another rebuttal, saying, "I have heard that punishing a man in the marketplace and ennobling a man at court require unanimous counsel before they are carried out without confusion. I respectfully examine what the various offices have deliberated: Yan Shansi is guilty in only one part out of ten; the offense charged admits only the lighter penalty. The emperor's gate has nine layers; the road to it is a thousand li distant. Therefore one borrows the ears of all under Heaven to listen, and nothing escapes hearing; one borrows the eyes of all under Heaven to see, and nothing escapes sight. Now many opinions have been reported upward; selection should be careful. If the many are discarded in favor of the few, I truly fear the consequences. If public report diverges in one point, the feelings below do not reach upward. Even if one wishes to follow the multitude, how can that be achieved! All the capital offices, in this time of great peace, array officials and divide duties—there are the worthy and there are the kin. The kin are the enfeoffed princes of the imperial house, Your Majesty's beloved sons; the worthy are those granted fiefs and founding states, Your Majesty's famed ministers. Seeing lack of propriety toward the ruler, would they willingly echo one another without dissent? Now the wording of many opinions has been produced, and the law should follow the lighter penalty." " The emperor accepted his memorial and in the end spared Shansi from death, banishing him to Jing Prefecture. Sifu was soon transferred to Secretariat Drafter and repeatedly submitted memorials on policy; many of his recommendations were adopted.
12
開元初，為諫議大夫。 時山東蝗蟲大起，姚崇為中書令，奏遣使分往河南、河北諸道殺蝗蟲而埋之。 思復以為蝗蟲是天災，當修德以禳之，恐非人力所能翦滅。 上疏曰：「臣聞河南、河北蝗蟲，頃日更益繁熾，經歷之處，苗稼都損。 今漸翾飛河西，遊食至洛，使命來往，不敢昌言，山東數州，甚為惶懼。 且天災流行，埋瘞難盡。 望陛下悔過責躬，發使宣慰，損不急之務，召至公之人，上下同心，君臣一德，持此誠實，以答休咎。 前後驅蝗使等，伏望總停。 《書》云：『皇天無親，惟德是輔； 人心無親，惟惠是懷。 』不可不收攬人心也。 」上深然之，出思復疏以付崇。 崇乃請遣思復往山東檢蝗蟲所損之處，及還，具以實奏。 崇又請令監察御史劉沼重加詳覆，沼希崇旨意，遂箠撻百姓，回改舊狀以奏之。 由是河南數州，竟不得免。 思復遂為崇所擠，出為德州刺史，轉絳州刺史。 入為黃門侍郎，加銀青光祿大夫，代裴漼為御史大夫。 思復性恬淡，好玄言，安仁體道，非紀綱之任。 無幾，轉太子賓客。 十三年卒，年七十餘。 思復子朝宗子朝宗，天寶初為京兆尹。 思復曾孫佽曾孫佽，字相之，少有文學，性尚簡淡。 舉進士，累辟籓方。 自襄州從事征拜殿中侍御史，遷刑部員外。 求為澧州刺史。 歲滿受代，宰相牛僧孺鎮鄂渚，辟為從事，征拜刑部郎中，轉京兆少尹，遷給事中。 出為桂州觀察使。 桂管二十餘郡，州掾而下至邑長三百員，由吏部而補者什一，他皆廉吏量其才而補之。 佽既至桂，吏以常所為官者數百人引謁，一吏執籍而前曰：「具員請補其闕。 」佽戒曰：「在任有政者，不奪所理； 有過者，必繩以法。 缺者當俟稽諸故籍，取其可者，然後補之。 」會春衣使內官至，求賄於郵吏，三豪家因厚其資以求邑宰，佽悉諾之。 使去，坐以撓法，各笞其背。 自是豪猾斂跡，皆得清廉吏以蘇活其人。 未幾，詔置五管都監，計所費盡一境地征，不足飽其意，佽特用儉約處之，遂為定制，君子以為難。 開成二年，卒於官，贈工部侍郎。 張廷珪張廷珪，河南濟源人，其先自常州徙焉。 廷珪少以文學知名，性慷慨，有誌尚。 弱冠應制舉。 長安中，累遷監察御史。 則天稅天下僧尼出錢，欲於白司馬阪營建大像。 廷珪上疏諫曰：
Early in the Kaiyuan reign he served as Remonstrance Grand Master. At that time locusts swarmed across Shandong. Yao Chong was chief minister of the Secretariat and memorialized sending envoys to the circuits of Henan and Hebei to kill locusts and bury them. Sifu held that locusts are a heavenly calamity and that one should cultivate virtue to expel them; he doubted human effort could exterminate them. He submitted a memorial, saying, "I have heard that locusts in Henan and Hebei have grown still more numerous in recent days; wherever they have passed, crops have been destroyed. Now they are gradually flying west of the river, roaming as far as Luoyang. Envoys come and go but dare not speak plainly; several prefectures of Shandong are greatly fearful. Moreover, when heavenly calamity spreads, burial cannot eliminate them all. I hope Your Majesty will repent and examine yourself, send envoys to proclaim consolation, reduce non-urgent affairs, summon the most impartial men, unite ruler and minister in one mind, hold to this sincerity, and respond to fortune and misfortune. The former and later locust-driving commissioners—I humbly hope they may all be halted. The Documents says, 'High Heaven has no favorites; it assists only virtue; the hearts of the people have no favorites; they embrace only kindness.' " One cannot fail to win the hearts of the people." " The emperor deeply approved this and gave Sifu's memorial to Chong. Chong then requested that Sifu be sent to Shandong to inspect locust damage; when he returned he reported everything according to fact. Chong also requested that Investigating Censor Liu Zhao conduct a further detailed review. Zhao hoped to please Chong's intent, beat the common people with rods, altered the former reports, and memorialized accordingly. Because of this several prefectures of Henan in the end could not escape disaster. Sifu was then forced out by Chong and sent out as prefect of De, then transferred to prefect of Jiang. He entered court as Vice Minister of the Yellow Gate, was granted the additional title Grand Master of Splendid Brightness with Silver Seal, and replaced Pei Huan as Censor-in-Chief. Sifu was by nature tranquil and indifferent, fond of arcane discourse, at ease in benevolence and embodying the Way—not fit for the duties of law and discipline. Before long he was transferred to Mentor of the Heir Apparent. He died in the thirteenth year at the age of over seventy. Sifu's son Chaozong served as intendant of Jingzhao at the beginning of the Tianbao reign. Sifu's great-grandson Ci, style name Xiangzhi, showed literary talent in his youth and valued simplicity and detachment. He passed the jinshi examination and was repeatedly summoned to regional administrations. From aide in Xiang Prefecture he was summoned and appointed palace attending censor, then transferred to vice director of the Ministry of Justice. He requested appointment as prefect of Li. When his term ended he was replaced. Chief Minister Niu Sengru was stationed at Ezhu and summoned him as aide; he was then summoned as director of the Ministry of Justice, transferred to vice intendant of Jingzhao, and promoted to Supervising Censor. He was sent out as observation commissioner of Gui Prefecture. Gui administration had more than twenty commanderies; from prefectural aides down to district magistrates there were three hundred posts. Only one in ten was filled through the Ministry of Personnel; the rest were filled by upright officials measuring talent. When Ci arrived at Gui, clerks led several hundred men who were customarily made officials to an audience. One clerk came forward holding a register and said, "The posts are filled; please fill the vacancies." " Ci admonished them, saying, "Those in office who have achieved results shall not be removed from what they govern; those who have faults must be bound by law. Vacancies should await checking against former registers, taking those who are acceptable, and only then filling them." " When the Spring-Garments Commissioner arrived from the palace and sought bribes from postal clerks, three powerful families increased their funds to seek district magistracies, and Ci assented to all of them. When the commissioner left, he charged them with obstructing the law and had each flogged on the back. From then on the powerful and cunning restrained themselves; upright officials were appointed and the people were revived. Before long an edict established a general supervisor of the Five Circuits. The calculated expenses exhausted the tax levies of an entire territory and were not enough to satisfy their desires. Ci handled the matter with special frugality, and it then became a fixed regulation; gentlemen considered this a difficult achievement. In the second year of Kaicheng he died in office and was posthumously appointed vice minister of the Ministry of Works. Zhang Tinggui came from Jiyuan in Henan; his ancestors had moved there from Chang Prefecture. Tinggui was renowned for literary accomplishment in his youth; he was generous by nature and had lofty aims. At the capping age he responded to the special examination. During the Chang'an era he rose to Investigating Censor. Empress Zetian taxed monks and nuns throughout the realm to contribute money, wishing to build a great image at the White Horse Slope. Tinggui submitted a memorial in protest, saying:
13
則天從其言，即停所作，仍於長生殿召見，深賞慰之。 景龍末，為中書舍人，再轉洪州都督，仍為江南西道按察使。
Empress Zetian followed his advice, immediately halted the construction, and summoned him to audience in the Hall of Long Life, deeply praising and comforting him. At the end of the Jinglong era he served as Secretariat Drafter, then as area commander of Hong and investigating commissioner of the Jiangnan West Circuit.
14
開元初，入為禮部侍郎。 時久旱，關中饑儉，下制求直諫昌言、弘益政理者。 廷珪上疏曰：
Early in the Kaiyuan reign he entered court as Vice Minister of Rites. At that time a long drought had brought famine to Guanzhong. An edict was issued seeking direct remonstrance and counsel that would enlarge and benefit government. Tinggui submitted a memorial, saying:
15
再遷黃門侍郎。 時監察御史蔣挺以監決杖刑稍輕，敕朝堂杖之，廷珪奏曰：「御史憲司，清望耳目之官，有犯當殺即殺，當流即流，不可決杖。 士可殺，不可辱也。 」時制命已行，然議者以廷珪之言為是。 俄坐泄禁中語，出為沔州刺史，又歷蘇、宋、魏三州刺史。 入為少府監，加金紫光祿大夫，封范陽男。 四遷太子詹事，以老疾致仕。 二十二年卒，年七十余，贈工部尚書，謚曰貞穆。 廷珪素與陳州刺史李邕親善，屢上表薦之，邕所撰碑碣之文，必請廷珪八分書之。 廷珪既善楷隸，甚為時人所重。 王求禮王求禮，許州長社人。 則天朝為左拾遺，遷監察御史。 性忠謇敢言，每上封彈事，無所畏避。 時契丹李盡忠反叛，其將孫萬榮寇陷河北數州，河內王武懿宗擁兵討之，畏懦不敢進。 既而賊大掠而去，懿宗條奏滄、瀛百姓為賊詿誤者數百家，請誅之。 求禮執而劾之曰：「此詿誤之人，比無良吏教習，城池又不完固，為賊驅逼，茍徇圖全，豈素有背叛之心哉！ 懿宗擁強兵數十萬，聞賊將至，走保城邑，罪當誅戮。 今乃移禍於詿誤之人，豈是為臣之道？ 請斬懿宗以謝河北百姓。 」懿宗大懼，則天竟降制赦之。
He was again promoted to Vice Minister of the Yellow Gate. At that time Investigating Censor Jiang Ting, because the beating punishment at supervised execution was considered too light, was ordered by edict to be beaten in the court hall. Tinggui submitted, "Censors are the law office of clear repute and the eyes and ears of the ruler. If guilty they should be killed if death is fitting, exiled if exile is fitting—they cannot be sentenced to beating. A gentleman may be killed but cannot be humiliated." " At the time the ordinance had already been carried out, yet those who discussed the matter held Tinggui's words to be correct. Before long, on account of leaking palace secrets, he was sent out as prefect of Mian and then served in succession as prefect of Su, Song, and Wei. He entered court as Director of the Court of the Imperial Treasury, was granted the additional title Grand Master of Splendid Brightness with Gold Seal, and was enfeoffed as Baron of Fanyang. He was transferred four times to Mentor of the Heir Apparent, then retired on account of age and illness. He died in the twenty-second year at the age of over seventy, was posthumously appointed Minister of Works, and given the posthumous title Zhenmu. Tinggui had long been close to Li Yong, prefect of Chen, and repeatedly recommended him in memorials. For every stele text Yong composed, he asked Tinggui to write it in clerical script. Tinggui excelled in regular and clerical script and was greatly esteemed in his day. Wang Qiuli came from Changshe in Xu Prefecture. During Empress Zetian's reign he served as Left Reminder and was promoted to Investigating Censor. He was loyal, forthright, and fearless in speech; whenever he submitted sealed impeachments he held nothing back. When the Khitan Li Jinzhong rebelled, his general Sun Wanrong overran several prefectures in Hebei. Wang Wuyizong of Henei led troops against them but, fearful and timid, dared not advance. Before long the rebels plundered heavily and withdrew. Wuyizong submitted an itemized memorial listing several hundred households in Cang and Ying as having been misled by the rebels and requested their execution. Qiuli seized on this and impeached him, saying, "These misled people lacked good officials to guide them, and their defenses were weak. Driven by the rebels, they merely sought to save themselves—how could they have harbored rebellion from the start!" Wuyizong commanded a strong army of several hundred thousand men. When he heard the rebels were coming he fled to defend walled towns—his offense deserves execution. Now he shifts the blame onto people who were misled—is this the way of a minister? I request that Wuyizong be beheaded to answer to the people of Hebei. " Wuyizong was greatly afraid, and Empress Zetian in the end issued an ordinance pardoning him.
16
契丹陷幽州，饋輓不給，左相豆盧欽望請輟京官兩月俸料以助軍，求禮謂欽望曰：「公祿厚俸優，輟之可也。 國家富有四海，足以儲軍國之用，何藉貧官薄俸。 公此舉豈宰相法邪？ 」欽望作色拒之，乃奏曰：「秦、漢皆有稅算以贍軍，求禮不識大體，妄有訟辭。 」求禮對曰：「秦皇、漢武稅天下，虛中以事邊，奈何使聖朝則效？ 不知欽望此言是大體耶！ 」事遂不行。
When the Khitan captured You Prefecture and supply lines failed, Left Chancellor Dou Luqinwang proposed suspending two months of salary for capital officials to aid the army. Qiuli said to Qinwang, "Your stipend is generous and your salary ample—suspending yours would be acceptable. The state is rich and possesses all within the four seas, enough to supply army and state—why rely on the meager salaries of poor officials? Is this action of yours worthy of a chief minister?" " Qinwang flushed with anger and rejected him, then memorialized, "Qin and Han both levied taxes to support the army. Qiuli does not understand the larger principle and rashly offers disputatious words." " Qiuli replied, "The First Emperor of Qin and Emperor Wu of Han taxed all under Heaven and emptied the center to serve the frontier—how can you have our sagely dynasty imitate them? Do you not know whether Qinwang's words represent the larger principle!" " The proposal was not carried out.
17
時三月雪，鳳閣侍郎蘇味道等以為瑞，草表將賀，求禮止之曰：「宰相調燮陰陽，而致雪降暮春，災也，安得為瑞？ 如三月雪為瑞雪，則臘月雷亦瑞雷也。 」舉朝嗤笑，以為口實。 求禮竟以剛正，名位不達而卒。 辛替否辛替否，京兆人也。 景龍年為左拾遺。 時中宗置公主府官屬，安樂公主府所補尤多猥濫。 又駙馬武崇訓死後，棄舊宅別造一宅，侈麗過甚。 時又盛興佛寺，百姓勞弊，帑藏為之空竭。 替否上疏諫曰：
When snow fell in the third month, Vice Minister of the Phoenix Pavilion Su Weidao and others took it as an auspicious sign and drafted a congratulatory memorial. Qiuli stopped them, saying, "Chief ministers harmonize yin and yang, yet snow falls in late spring—this is a calamity; how can it be an auspicious sign? If snow in the third month is auspicious snow, then thunder in the twelfth month is also auspicious thunder." " The whole court laughed in scorn and made it a byword. Qiuli died without attaining fame or high rank, renowned to the end for his firm uprightness. Xin Tifou came from Jingzhao. During the Jinglong period he served as Left Reminder. When Emperor Zhongzong established staffs for princesses' establishments, appointments to Princess Anle's household were especially numerous and indiscriminate. After her consort Wu Chongxun died, she abandoned the old residence and built another, extravagantly lavish beyond measure. Buddhist temples were also being built on a vast scale; the common people were worn out with labor, and the treasury was emptied. Tifou submitted a memorial in protest, saying:
18
疏奏不納。 歲餘，安樂公主被誅。
The memorial was submitted but not accepted. A little over a year later Princess Anle was executed.
19
睿宗即位，又為金仙、玉真公主廣營二觀。 先是，中宗時斜封受官人一切停任，凡數百千人，又有敕放令卻上。 替否時為左補闕，又上疏陳時政曰：
When Emperor Ruizong took the throne, the Jinxian and Yuzhen princesses again undertook the broad construction of the two monasteries. Earlier, in Emperor Zhongzong's time, all holders of offices through irregular sealed appointments had been stopped from appointment—several hundred or a thousand people in all—and there was also an edict releasing them and ordering them to come forward again. Tifou was then Left Remonstrator and again submitted a memorial on current policy, saying:
20
疏奏，睿宗嘉其公直。 稍遷為右臺殿中侍御史。 開元中，累轉潁王府長史。 天寶初卒，年八十余。 史臣曰史臣曰：夫好聞其善，惡聞其過，君人者之常情也； 寧諂媚以取容，不逆耳以招禍，臣人者之常情也。 能反此者，不亦善乎！ 李、薛等六君，吐忠讜之言，補朝廷之失，有犯無隱，不愧古人，有唐之良臣也。 贊贊曰：臣之事君，有邪有正。 君之使臣，從諫則聖。 李、薛輸忠，救人之命。 韋、韓讜言，醫國之病。 辛、王章疏，犯顏竦聽。 張子法言，實裨時政。
When the memorial was submitted, Emperor Ruizong praised his public-minded integrity. He was gradually promoted to palace attending censor of the Right Office. During the Kaiyuan era he rose to chief administrator of the Prince of Ying's establishment. He died at the beginning of the Tianbao reign at the age of over eighty. The historiographer says: To love hearing of one's virtues and hate hearing of one's faults is the constant disposition of rulers; to prefer flattery for favor rather than unwelcome words that invite disaster is the constant disposition of ministers. Those who can reverse this—are they not admirable! Li, Xue, and the other five spoke loyal and forthright words, repaired the court's failings, spoke without concealment, and did not shame the ancients—they were worthy ministers of Tang. The encomium says: In how a minister serves his ruler, there is the crooked and there is the upright. In how a ruler employs his ministers, to heed remonstrance is to be sagely. Li and Xue offered loyalty and saved men's lives. Wei and Han spoke forthrightly and treated the state's ills. Xin and Wang submitted memorials that offended the throne yet made the ruler listen with rapt attention. Master Zhang's words on law truly benefited current policy.
