1
姚令言，河中人也。 少應募，起於卒伍，隸涇原節度馬璘。 以戰功累授金吾大將軍同正，為衙前兵馬使，改試太常卿、兼御史中丞。 建中元年，孟暤為涇原節度留後，自以文吏進身，不樂軍旅，頻表薦令言謹肅，堪任將帥。 暤尋歸朝廷，遂拜令言為四鎮北庭行營涇原節度使、涇州刺史、兼御史大夫。
Yao Lingyan was from Hezhong. He enlisted while young, rose from the ranks, and served under Ma Lin, military commissioner of Jingyuan. Battlefield honors brought him repeated promotions to acting Jinwu Grand General and yamen-front military commissioner, then to provisional Grand Master of Ceremonies with concurrent appointment as Vice Censor-in-Chief. In Jianzhong 1 (780), Meng Hui was acting military commissioner of Jingyuan. A civil official by background, he disliked military command and repeatedly recommended Lingyan as sober, reliable, and fit to lead troops. Meng Hui soon returned to the capital, and Lingyan was appointed Jingyuan military commissioner of the Four Garrisons Northern Court campaign command, prefect of Jing, and concurrent Censor-in-Chief.
2
建中四年，李希烈叛，寇陷汝州，詔哥舒曜率師攻之，營於襄城。 希烈兵數萬圍襄城，勢甚危急。 十月，詔令言率本鎮兵五萬赴援。 涇師離鎮，多攜子弟而來，望至京師以獲厚賞，及師上路，一無所賜。 時詔京兆尹王翃犒軍士，唯糲食菜啖而已，軍士覆而不顧，皆憤怒，揚言曰：「吾輩棄父母妻子，將死於難，而食不得飽，安能以草命捍白刃耶！ 國家瓊林、大盈，寶貨堆積，不取此以自活，何往耶？ 」行次浐水，乃返戈，大呼鼓噪而還。 令言曰：「比約東都有厚賞，兒郎勿草草，此非求活之良圖也。 」眾不聽，以戈環令言請退，令言急奏之。 上恐，令內庫出繒彩二十車馳賜之，軍聲浩浩，令言不能戢。 街市居人狼狽走竄，亂兵呼曰：「勿走，不稅汝間架矣！ 」德宗令普王與學士姜公輔往撫勞之，才出內門，賊已斬關，陣於丹鳳樓下。 是日，德宗倉卒出幸，賊縱入府庫輦運，極力而止。
In Jianzhong 4 (783), Li Xilie rebelled and seized Ru Prefecture. The court ordered Ge Shuya to attack him and encamped at Xiangcheng. Li Xilie mustered tens of thousands of men to besiege Xiangcheng, and the crisis was acute. In the tenth month the court ordered Lingyan to march fifty thousand men of his own command to the rescue. The Jing army left its post with many men bringing wives and children, expecting rich rewards once they reached the capital—but after they set out, they received nothing. The court had ordered Wang Hong, metropolitan prefect of Jingzhao, to reward the troops, but they were fed only coarse grain and vegetables. The men overturned the food and refused it, raging aloud: "We left parents, wives, and children behind to die in battle, and we cannot even eat our fill—why should we throw away our lives against cold steel?" "The imperial treasuries at Qionlin and Daying are heaped with treasure—if we will not take that to live on, where else can we turn?" When they reached the Chan River they reversed their weapons, shouting and beating drums as they marched back. Lingyan said, "We were promised rich rewards in the eastern capital—men, do not act rashly; this is no good way to save yourselves." The troops would not listen. They ringed Lingyan with spears and pressed him to step aside, and he sent an urgent report to the throne. Alarmed, the emperor ordered twenty cartloads of silk rushed from the inner treasury as gifts, but the army's uproar was overwhelming and Lingyan could not quiet them. Shopkeepers and townspeople fled in panic while the mutineers shouted, "Do not run—we will not collect your housing tax!" Emperor Dezong sent Prince Pu and academician Jiang Gongfu to reassure the troops, but they had barely left the inner gate when the rebels had forced the gates and formed ranks below Danfeng Tower. That day Dezong fled the capital in haste. The rebels poured into the treasuries and hauled away goods until they were stopped by force.
3
時太尉硃泚罷鎮居晉昌裏第，是夜，叛卒謀曰：「硃太尉久囚於宅，若迎為主，大事濟矣。 」泚嘗節制涇州，眾知其失權，廢居怏怏，又幸泚寬和，乃請令言率騎迎泚於晉昌裏。 泚初遲疑，以食飼之，徐觀眾意，既而諸校齊至，乃自第張炬火入居含元殿。 既僭號，乃以令言為侍中，與源休同知賊政事。 既以身先逆亂，頗盡心於賊，害宗室，圍奉天，皆令言為首帥也。 群兇宴樂，既醉，令言與源休論功，令言自此蕭何，源休曰：「帷幄之謀，成秦之業，無出予之右者。 吾比蕭何無讓，子當曹參可矣。 」時朝士在賊廷者，聞之皆笑，謂源休為火迫酂侯。 硃泚敗，令言與張廷芝尚有眾萬人，從泚將入吐蕃。 至涇州，欲投田希鑒，希鑒偽致禮誘之，與泚俱斬首來獻。
Grand Mentor Zhu Ci had been stripped of his command and was living at his mansion in Jinchang Lane. That night the mutineers plotted: "Grand Mentor Zhu has been shut up in his house too long—if we make him our leader, the cause will succeed." Zhu Ci had once governed Jing Prefecture. The men knew he had lost office and brooded in retirement; they also trusted his mild temper, and asked Lingyan to lead cavalry to bring Zhu Ci from Jinchang Lane. Zhu Ci hesitated at first. They fed him while he watched the mood of the troops; when the officers had all gathered, he left his house with torches blazing and entered Hanyuan Hall. After he declared himself emperor, he made Lingyan Grand Counselor and had him administer rebel affairs jointly with Yuan Xiu. Because he had personally led the revolt, he threw himself into the rebel cause. The slaughter of imperial clansmen and the siege of Fengtian were both commanded by Lingyan. At a rebel feast, once drunk Lingyan and Yuan Xiu argued over their merits. Lingyan declared himself Xiao He henceforth. Yuan Xiu said, "In counsel from the tent and in founding the Qin enterprise, no one ranks above me." "I yield nothing to Xiao He—you can be Cao Can." Court officials trapped in the rebel court heard this and laughed, dubbing Yuan Xiu "the Marquis of Zan pressed by fire"—a pun on Xiao He's title that also suggested a marquis scorched by flame. When Zhu Ci was defeated, Lingyan and Zhang Tingzhi still commanded ten thousand men and followed Zhu Ci, intending to flee into Tibet. At Jing Prefecture they tried to surrender to Tian Xijian. Tian Xijian pretended courtesy to lure them in, then beheaded them along with Zhu Ci and sent their heads to the court.
4
張光晟，京兆盩厔人，起於行間。 天寶末，哥舒翰兵敗潼關，大將王思禮所乘馬中流矢而斃，光晟時在騎卒之中，因下，以馬授思禮。 思禮問其姓名，不告而退，思禮陰記其形貌，常使人密求之。 無何，思禮為河東節度使，其偏將辛雲京為代州刺史，屢為將校譖毀，思禮怒焉。 雲京惶懼，不知所出。 光晟時隸雲京麾下，因間進曰：「光晟素有德於王司空，比不言諸，恥以舊恩受賞。 今使君憂迫，光晟請奉命一見司空，則使君之難可解。 」雲京然其計，即令之太原。 乃謁思禮，未及言舊，思禮識之，遽曰：「爾豈非吾故人乎？ 何相見之晚也！ 」光晟遂陳潼關之事，思禮大喜，因執其手感泣曰：「吾有今日，子之力也。 求子頗久，竟此相遇，何慰如之？ 」命同榻而坐，結為兄弟。 光晟遂述雲京之屈，思禮曰：「雲京比涉謗言，過亦不細，今為故人，特舍之矣。 」即日擢光晟為兵馬使，賚田宅、縑帛甚厚，累奏特進，試太常少卿，委以心腹。 及雲京為河東節度使，又奏光晟為代州刺史。
Zhang Guangcheng was from Zhouzhi in metropolitan Jingzhao and rose from the ranks on campaign. Late in the Tianbao era Geshu Han's army was routed at Tong Pass. The great general Wang Silun's horse was struck by an arrow in midstream and fell. Guangcheng, then among the cavalry, dismounted and gave his own mount to Silun. Silun asked his name, but he refused to say and slipped away. Silun memorized his face and often sent men to search for him in secret. Before long Silun became military commissioner of Hedong. His subordinate Xin Yunjing was prefect of Dai and was repeatedly slandered by other officers until Silun grew furious. Yunjing was terrified and did not know what to do. Guangcheng was then serving under Yunjing and spoke up privately: "I once did Wang the Minister a service. I have never spoken of it, for I would be ashamed to claim reward for an old favor. Now that you are in distress, let me go on your orders and see the Minister once. Your trouble can then be resolved." Yunjing agreed to the plan and sent him at once to Taiyuan. He called on Silun. Before he could mention the past, Silun recognized him and cried, "Are you not my old friend? Why did we meet so late!" Guangcheng told the story of Tong Pass. Silun was overjoyed, seized his hand, and wept: "I owe my life today to you. I have searched for you a long time. To meet you at last—what greater comfort could there be?" He had them sit on the same couch and sworn as brothers. Guangcheng then explained Yunjing's plight. Silun said, "Yunjing has indeed been tangled in slander, and his fault is not small—but for your sake I will let it go." That same day he promoted Guangcheng to military commissioner, gave him fields, houses, and lavish gifts of silk, repeatedly recommended him for special advancement, appointed him provisional Vice Minister of Ceremonies, and treated him as a trusted confidant. When Yunjing became military commissioner of Hedong, he again recommended Guangcheng as prefect of Dai.
5
大歷末，遷單於都護、兼御史中丞、振武軍使。 代宗密謂之曰：「北蕃縱橫日久，當思所禦之計。 」光晟既受命，至鎮，威令甚行。 建中元年，回紇突董梅錄領眾並雜種胡等自京師還國，輿載金帛，相屬於道。 光晟訝其裝橐頗多，潛令驛吏以長錐刺之，則皆輦歸所誘致京師婦人也。 遂給突董及所領徒悉令赴宴，酒酣，光晟伏甲盡拘而殺之，死者千余人，唯留二胡歸國復命。 遂部其婦人，給糧還京，收其金帛，賞賚軍士。 後回紇遣使來訴，上不欲甚阻蕃情，征拜右金吾將軍。 回紇猶怨懟不已，又降為睦王傅，尋改太仆卿，負才怏怏不得誌。
Late in the Dali era he was appointed protector-general of Chanyu, with concurrent posts as Vice Censor-in-Chief and commissioner of the Zhenwu army. Emperor Daizong told him privately, "The northern tribes have run rampant for years. You must find a way to hold them in check." Once Guangcheng took up the post, his authority was firmly enforced. In Jianzhong 1 (780), the Uyghur leader Tudun Meilu led his men and various other Hu groups home from the capital, their carts piled with gold and silk in an endless line along the road. Guangcheng was struck by how heavy their baggage was and secretly had relay clerks pierce the packs with long awls. Inside were women they had lured from the capital and were hauling home in carts. He invited Tudun and all his followers to a banquet. When they were drunk, Guangcheng's hidden troops seized and killed them—more than a thousand dead—sparing only two Hu to carry the report back to their country. He sent the women back to the capital with provisions, seized their gold and silk, and used the spoils to reward his soldiers. When the Uyghurs sent envoys to protest, the court did not wish to estrange them further and recalled Guangcheng to serve as Right Jinwu General. The Uyghurs remained bitter, and he was demoted again to tutor of the Prince of Mu, then made Grand Master of the Stud. Talented but thwarted, he brooded over his frustrated ambitions.
6
賊泚僭逆，署光晟偽節度使兼宰相。 及泚眾頻敗，遂擇精兵五千配光晟，營於九曲，去東渭橋凡十餘里。 光晟潛使於李晟，有歸順之意。 晟進兵入苑，光晟勸賊泚宜速西奔，光晟以數千人送泚出城，因率眾回降於晟。 晟以其誠款，又愛其材，欲奏用之，俾令歸私第，表請特減其罪。 每大宴會，皆令就坐，華州節度使駱元光詬之曰：「吾不能與反虜同席！ 」拂衣還營。 晟不得已，拘之私第，後有詔言其狀跡不可原，乃斬之。
When Zhu Ci rebelled and seized power, he appointed Guangcheng rebel military commissioner and chancellor. After Zhu Ci's army suffered repeated defeats, he gave Guangcheng five thousand picked troops and encamped them at Jiuqu, a little over ten li from East Wei Bridge. Guangcheng secretly contacted Li Sheng, signaling his wish to defect. As Sheng advanced into the imperial park, Guangcheng urged Zhu Ci to flee west immediately. He escorted Zhu Ci out of the city with several thousand men, then turned his troops about and surrendered to Sheng. Sheng valued his sincere surrender and admired his ability. He wanted to recommend him for office, sent him to his private residence, and petitioned for a special reduction of his punishment. At every grand feast Sheng had him take a seat, but Luo Yuanguang, military commissioner of Hua, reviled him: "I will not sit at table with a rebel!" He swept his robes aside and returned to camp. Sheng had no choice but to confine him at his residence. Later an edict declared his crimes beyond pardon, and he was executed.
7
源休，相州臨漳人，京兆尹光輿之子也。 休以幹局，累授監察御史、殿中侍御史、青苗使判官，遷虞部員外郎，。 出潭州刺史，入為主客郎中，遷給事中、御史中丞、左庶子。 其妻即吏部侍郎王翊女也。 因小忿而離，妻族上訴，下御史臺驗理，休遲留不答款狀，除名，配流溱州。 久之，移岳州。
Yuan Xiu was from Linzhang in Xiang Prefecture and the son of Guangyu, metropolitan prefect of Jingzhao. For his administrative ability Xiu rose through the ranks as investigating censor, palace censor, and aide to the Green Sprouts commissioner, then was promoted to Vice Director of the Parks and Forests Office. He served as prefect of Tan, then returned to the capital as director of the Hosts and Guests Bureau and was promoted to supervising secretary, Vice Censor-in-Chief, and Left Vice Heir Apparent. His wife was the daughter of Wang Yi, Vice Minister of Personnel. After a petty quarrel they separated. His wife's kin appealed to the throne, and the Censorate was ordered to investigate. Xiu stalled and refused to answer the formal interrogatory. He was expelled from office and exiled to Qin Prefecture. After a long interval he was transferred to Yue Prefecture.
8
建中初，楊炎執政，以京兆尹嚴郢威名稍著，心欲傾之。 郢，即王翊甥婿也。 休與王氏離絕之時，炎風聞休、郢有隙，遂擢休自流人為京兆少尹，俾令伺郢過失。 休既職久，與郢親善，炎怒之，奏令以本官兼御史中丞，奉使回紇。 休至振武，軍使張光晟已殺回紇突董等，上初欲遂絕其使，令休還，待命於太原。 久之方遣，仍令休歸其突董、翳密施大小梅錄等四屍。 突董者，即武義可汗之叔父也。 屍既至，可汗令宰臣已下具彩服車馬來迎。 其宰相頡於思迦坐大帳，立休等於帳外雪中，詰殺突董等故。 休曰：「突董等自與張光晟忿鬥而死，非天子命也。 」又問：「使者背唐國，負罪當死，不能自戮耶？ 不然，何假手於我殺之也？ 」凡將殺者數矣，言頗悖慢，乃引去，供餼甚薄，留之五十余日，乃得還。 可汗使謂休曰：「我國人皆欲殺汝，唯我不然。 汝國已殺突董等，吾又殺汝，猶以血洗血，汙益甚爾。 吾今以水洗血，不亦善乎！ 所欠吾馬直絹一百八十萬疋，當速歸之。 」遣散支將軍康赤心等隨休來朝，休竟不得見其可汗。 尋遣赤心等歸，與之帛十萬疋、金銀十萬兩，償其馬直。 休履危而還，宰相盧杞又恐復命之日以口辯結恩，將至太原，遽奏為光祿卿。 休以其還使賞薄，居常怨望。
Early in Jianzhong, with Yang Yan in power, Yan Ying as metropolitan prefect of Jingzhao was gaining a reputation, and Yang Yan resolved to destroy him. Ying was Wang Yi's nephew by marriage. When Xiu and the Wang family had fallen out, Yang Yan heard that Xiu and Ying were at odds. He recalled Xiu from exile to be metropolitan vice-prefect of Jingzhao and ordered him to spy on Ying's missteps. After Xiu had been in office for some time he grew friendly with Ying. Yang Yan was furious and had him sent to the Uyghurs as envoy while retaining his post with concurrent appointment as Vice Censor-in-Chief. When Xiu reached Zhenwu, army commissioner Zhang Guangcheng had already killed Tudun and the other Uyghur leaders. The emperor first considered cutting off relations entirely and ordered Xiu back to await instructions at Taiyuan. Only after a long delay was he sent on his way, still charged with returning the four corpses: Tudun, Yimishi, and the great and small Meilu. Tudun was the uncle of the Wuyi Qaghan. When the bodies arrived, the qaghan ordered his ministers and officials to meet them in colored robes with carriages and horses. Chancellor Xieyusijia sat in the great tent and had Xiu and his party stand in the snow outside, demanding to know why Tudun and the others had been killed. Xiu said, "Tudun and the others died in a private quarrel with Zhang Guangcheng. It was not by imperial order." They asked again, "You have betrayed Tang and deserve death. Will you not kill yourselves?" If not, why make us do your killing for you?" Several times they were nearly executed. Their replies were insolent. They were led away, given scant provisions, and held for more than fifty days before they were allowed to return. The qaghan's envoy told Xiu, "Everyone in my country wants you dead—only I do not." "Your court has already killed Tudun and the others. If I kill you as well, that is only washing blood with blood—the stain grows worse." "I would rather wash blood away with water—is that not better?" "The one million eight hundred thousand bolts of silk still owed for our horses must be paid promptly." He sent General Kang Chixin of the Sanzhi branch and others to accompany Xiu to court. Xiu never obtained an audience with the qaghan. Soon Chixin and his party were sent home with one hundred thousand bolts of silk and one hundred thousand taels of gold and silver to settle the horse debt. Xiu returned after a perilous mission. Chancellor Lu Qi feared that on reporting back Xiu would win favor through his eloquence, and when Xiu was nearing Taiyuan Lu Qi hastily had him appointed Grand Master for Splendid Happiness. Because his reward for the embassy was meager, Xiu nursed a constant grievance.
9
會涇原兵叛，立硃泚為主。 初但稱太尉，朝官謁泚者，悉勸奏迎鑾駕，既不合泚意而退。 及休至，遂屏人移時，言多悖逆，盛陳成敗，稱述符命，勸令僭號。 泚悅其言，以休為宰相，判度支。 休遂為謀主，至於兵食軍資，遷除補擬，內外咨謀，一稟休畫。 故時人云：「源休之逆，甚於硃泚。 」朝廷大臣之奔竄不獲者，多為休所誘致，以至戮辱，職休而為，蓋非一焉。 又勸泚鋤翦宗室，以絕人望，命萬年縣賊曹尉楊偡專其斷決，諸王子孫遇害不可勝數。 泚敗走，休隨至寧州。 泚死，休走鳳翔，為其部曲所殺，傳首來獻。 休三子並斬於東市，籍沒其家。
When the Jingyuan army mutinied, they made Zhu Ci their leader. At first Zhu Ci was addressed only as Grand Mentor. Court officials who visited him all urged welcoming the emperor back to the capital, and when that did not suit Zhu Ci they withdrew. When Xiu arrived he dismissed attendants and spoke at length in private. His words were treasonous: he laid out the balance of victory and defeat, cited omens and destiny, and urged Zhu Ci to declare himself emperor. Zhu Ci was pleased and made Xiu chancellor with charge of the treasury. Xiu became the chief strategist. Troops, provisions, military supplies, appointments and replacements—every decision inside and outside the court followed his plans alone. People said at the time, "Yuan Xiu's treason was worse than Zhu Ci's." Many court officials who tried to flee but were caught were lured back by Xiu and then slaughtered or humiliated. Such deeds attributable to Xiu were not few. He also urged Zhu Ci to exterminate the imperial clan to destroy any hope of restoration, and put Yang Yan of the Wanian county bandit-suppression office in charge of the killings. The princes and their descendants who perished were beyond count. When Zhu Ci was defeated and fled, Xiu followed him to Ning Prefecture. After Zhu Ci died, Xiu fled to Fengxiang, where Zhu Ci's personal troops killed him and sent his head to the court. All three of Xiu's sons were executed at the eastern market, and his family's property was confiscated.
10
喬琳，太原人。 少孤貧誌學，以文詞稱。 天寶初，舉進士，補成武尉，累授興平尉。 朔方節度郭子儀辟為掌書記，尋拜監察御史。 琳倜儻疏誕，好談諧，侮謔僚列，頗無禮檢。 同院御史畢耀初與琳嘲誚往復，因成釁隙，遂以公事互相告訴，坐貶巴州員外司戶。 遂起為南郭令，改殿中侍御史，充山南節度張獻誠行軍司馬。 使罷，為劍南東川節度鮮于叔明判官。 改檢校駕部郎中、果綿遂三州刺史、兼御史中丞。 入為大理少卿、國子祭酒。 出為懷州刺史。 琳素與張涉友善，上在春宮，涉嘗為侍讀。 及嗣位，多以政事詢訪於涉，盛稱琳識度材略，堪備大用，因拜御史大夫、平章事。 琳本粗材，又年高有耳疾，上每顧問，對答失次，論奏不合時。 幸居相位凡八十余日，除工部尚書，罷知政事，尋加迎皇太后副使。
Qiao Lin was from Taiyuan. Orphaned and poor as a youth, he devoted himself to learning and won renown for his literary skill. Early in Tianbao he passed the jinshi examination, served as defender of Chengwu, and rose through successive appointments to defender of Xingping. Guo Ziyi, military commissioner of Shuofang, took him on as chief secretary, and he soon became an investigating censor. Lin was unconventional and unrestrained, fond of banter, and often mocked his colleagues with little regard for decorum. Bi Yao, a fellow censor in his bureau, traded barbs with Lin until a feud developed. They then lodged official charges against each other, and Lin was demoted to acting registrar of Ba Prefecture. He was recalled as magistrate of Nanguo, made palace censor, and served as campaign adjutant to Zhang Xiancheng, military commissioner of Shannan. When that assignment ended, he became aide to Xianyu Shuming, military commissioner of eastern Sichuan. He was appointed acting director of the Imperial Transport Bureau, prefect of Guo, Mian, and Su, and concurrent Vice Censor-in-Chief. He returned to the capital as vice minister of the Court of Judicial Review and libationer of the Imperial University. He was appointed prefect of Huai. Lin had long been close to Zhang She, who had served as study companion when the emperor was crown prince. After he took the throne, he often consulted She on affairs of state. She praised Lin's judgment and ability as fit for high office, and Lin was appointed Censor-in-Chief and Grand Councilor. Lin was a rough talent at best, and in old age suffered from deafness. Whenever the emperor questioned him, his answers were confused and his policy proposals out of step with the times. He held the chancellorship for only a little over eighty days before being made Minister of Works and removed from government. He was soon named deputy envoy to welcome the empress dowager.
11
硃泚之亂，扈從至奉天，轉吏部尚書，遷太子少師。 再幸梁、洋，從至盩厔，托以馬乏遲留，上以琳舊老，心敬重之，慰諭頗至，以禦馬一匹給焉。 又懇辭以老疾不堪山阻登頓，上悵然，賜之所執策曰：「勉為良圖，與卿決矣。 」後數日，乃削發為僧，止仙遊寺。 賊泚聞之，遂令數十騎追至京城，俾為偽吏部尚書。 令源休被公服，饋肉食，琳雖辭讓，而僧言求施。 琳掌賊中吏部，選人前請曰：「所註某官不穩便。 」琳謂之曰：「足下謂此選竟穩便乎？ 」及官軍收京師，當處極刑，時琳已七十余，李晟憫其衰老，表請減死。 上以其累經重任，頓虧臣節，自受逆命，頗聞譏諧悖慢之言，背義負恩，固不可舍，命斬之。 臨刑嘆曰：「喬琳以七月七日生，亦以此日死，豈非命歟！」
During Zhu Ci's rebellion he accompanied the emperor to Fengtian, became Minister of Personnel, and was promoted to Junior Tutor of the Heir Apparent. On the second flight to Liang and Yang he followed as far as Zhouzhi, then pleaded exhaustion of his horses and stayed behind. The emperor, who still respected the old man, comforted him warmly and gave him an imperial horse. He begged again, saying age and illness left him unable to endure mountain passes. The emperor sighed and gave him the whip in his hand, saying, "Take care of yourself. This is our parting." A few days later he shaved his head, became a monk, and took up residence at Xianyou Temple. When Zhu Ci heard of it, he sent several dozen horsemen to bring Lin back to the capital and forced him to serve as rebel Minister of Personnel. Yuan Xiu was told to dress Lin in official robes and offer him meat. Lin declined, but in his monk's guise asked for alms instead. Lin ran the rebel Ministry of Personnel. A candidate came to him and said, "The post you assigned me is not secure." Lin replied, "Do you think any appointment in this regime is secure?" When the imperial army retook the capital, Lin faced execution. He was over seventy, and Li Sheng, pitying his age, petitioned to spare his life. The emperor held that Lin, having held high office repeatedly, had violated his duty, accepted rebel commands of his own accord, and was known for mocking, insolent speech. Having betrayed his obligations, he could not be spared, and the emperor ordered his execution. At the block he sighed, "Qiao Lin was born on the seventh day of the seventh month and dies on it as well—is this not destiny?"
12
張涉者，蒲州人，家世儒者。 涉依國學為諸生講說，稍遷國子博士，亦能為文，嘗請有司日試萬言，時呼張萬言。 德宗在春宮，受經於涉。 及即位之夕，召涉入宮，訪以庶政，大小之事皆咨之。 翌日，詔居翰林，恩禮甚厚，親重莫比。 自博士遷散騎常侍。 上方屬意宰輔，唯賢是擇，故求人於不次之地。 涉舉懷州刺史喬琳為相，上授之不疑，天下聞之者皆愕然。 數月，琳以不稱職罷，上由是疏涉。 俄受前湖南都團練使辛京杲贓事發，詔曰：「尊師之道，禮有所加； 議故之法，恩有所掩。 張涉賄賂交通，頗駭時聽，常所親重，良深嘆惜。 宜放歸田裏。」
Zhang She was from Pu Prefecture, from a long line of Confucian scholars. He lectured students at the Directorate of Education, rose to erudite of the Imperial University, and was a capable writer. He once asked the authorities to test him at ten thousand characters a day, earning the nickname Zhang Ten Thousand Words. When Dezong was crown prince, he studied the classics under She. On the night he took the throne he summoned She to the palace and consulted him on every matter of government, large and small. The next day he was ordered into the Hanlin Academy with lavish favor. No one was closer to the emperor. He rose from erudite to attendant cavalier. The emperor was intent on finding worthy chancellors and searched for talent outside the usual channels. She recommended Qiao Lin, prefect of Huai, for chancellor. The emperor appointed him without hesitation, and the whole empire was astonished. Within months Lin was dismissed as unfit for office, and the emperor grew distant from She. Soon the bribery case of the former Hunan military training commissioner Xin Jinggao came to light. An edict declared, "In honoring one's teacher, ritual grants special consideration; and in weighing past service, mercy may cover faults." Zhang She had taken bribes and maintained improper connections, shocking public opinion. The emperor deeply regretted having favored a man he had once held so close. He is to be released and sent home to his fields."
13
蔣鎮，常州義興人，尚書左丞洌之子也。 與兄練並以文學進。 天寶末舉賢良，累授左拾遺、司封員外郎，轉諫議大夫。 時戶部侍郎、判度支韓滉上言：「河中鹽池生瑞鹽，實土德之上瑞。 」上以秋霖稍多，水潦為患，不宜生瑞，命鎮馳驛檢行之。 鎮奏與滉同，仍上表賀，請宣付史館，並請置神祠，錫其嘉號寶應靈慶池。 地霖潦彌月，壞居人廬舍非一，鹽池為潦水所入，其味多苦。 韓滉慮鹽戶減稅，詐奏雨不壞池，池生瑞鹽，鎮庇之飾詐，識者醜之。 轉給事中、工部侍郎，以簡儉稱於時。
Jiang Zhen was from Yixing in Chang Prefecture and the son of Jiang Lie, Left Vice Director of the Ministry of Civil Office. He and his elder brother Lian both rose through literary accomplishment. Late in Tianbao he was recommended as worthy and good, served as Left Reminder and Vice Director of the Seals and Investitures Bureau, and became a remonstrance official. At the time Han Huang, Vice Minister of Revenue with charge of the treasury, memorialized, "The salt ponds of Hezhong have produced auspicious salt—a supreme omen of the virtue of Earth." The emperor, noting heavy autumn rains and widespread flooding, thought it unfitting that omens should appear and ordered Zhen to inspect the site by urgent relay. Zhen reported as Huang had, submitted a congratulatory memorial, asked that the event be recorded in the Historiography Office, and requested a spirit shrine with the honorific name Precious Response Spirit Celebration Pond. Flooding had lasted a full month, destroying many homes. Floodwater had entered the salt ponds, and the salt tasted mostly bitter. Han Huang, fearing reduced salt tax revenue, falsely reported that rain had not harmed the ponds and that auspicious salt had appeared. Zhen covered for him and dressed up the fraud. Men of judgment despised them both. He was made supervising secretary and Vice Minister of Works and was known at court for simplicity and frugality.
14
其妹婿源溥，即休之弟也，以姻媾之故，與休交好。 涇師之叛，鎮潛竄，夜至鄠縣西，馬躓墮溝澗中，傷足不能進。 時史練已與源休相率受賊偽官。 鎮仆人有逃歸投練，雲鎮病足在鄠。 練與源休聞之大喜，遂言於賊泚此。 泚素慕鎮清名，即令騎二百求之鄠縣西。 明日，擁鎮而至，署為偽宰相。 既知不免，每憂沮，常懷刃將自裁，多為兄練所救而罷。 數日後，復謀竄匿，竟以性懦畏怯，計終不果。 然源休與泚頻議，欲逼脅潛藏衣冠，大加殺戮，鎮輒力爭救，獲全者甚眾。 至是，與兄練等並授偽職，斬於東市西北街。
His sister's husband was Yuan Pu, younger brother of Yuan Xiu. Through this marriage tie Zhen was friendly with Xiu. When the Jing army rebelled, Zhen fled in secret. By night he reached west of E County, where his horse stumbled into a ravine and injured his foot so that he could go no farther. By then Shi Lian had already followed Yuan Xiu in accepting rebel appointments. One of Zhen's servants escaped and reported to Lian that Zhen lay injured at E. Lian and Yuan Xiu were delighted and told the rebel Zhu Ci. Zhu Ci had long admired Zhen's reputation and sent two hundred horsemen to find him west of E County. The next day they brought Zhen in and appointed him rebel chancellor. Knowing he could not escape, he was constantly despondent and often carried a blade to kill himself, but his brother Lian usually stopped him. Days later he plotted to flee again, but his timid nature kept him from carrying the plan through. Yuan Xiu and Zhu Ci often discussed hunting down hidden officials and killing them in large numbers. Zhen repeatedly argued to spare them, and many lives were saved. In the end he, his brother Lian, and others who had accepted rebel offices were executed at the northwest corner of the eastern market.
15
初鎮父洌，叔渙，當祿山、思明之亂，並授偽職，然以家風修整，為士大夫所稱。 鎮兄弟亦以教義禮法為己任，而貪祿愛死，節隳身戮，為天下笑。
Zhen's father Lie and uncle Huan had both accepted false offices during the rebellions of An Lushan and Shi Siming, yet their household was so well ordered that scholar-officials still praised them. The Jiang brothers too professed devotion to righteousness and ritual, yet clung to office and feared death. Their integrity was ruined, their lives forfeited, and they became a laughingstock throughout the realm.
16
洪經綸，建中初為黜陟使。 至東都，訪聞魏州田悅食糧兵凡七萬人，經綸素昧時機，先以符停其兵四萬人，令歸農畝。 田悅偽順命，即依符罷之； 而大集所罷兵士，激怒之曰：「爾等在軍旅，各有父母妻子，既為黜陟使所罷，如何得衣食？ 」遂大哭。 悅乃盡出家財衣服厚給之，各令還其部伍，自此人堅叛心，由是罷職。 及硃泚反，偽授太常少卿。
Hong Jinglun served early in Jianzhong as promotion-and-demotion commissioner. At the eastern capital he learned that Tian Yue of Weizhou maintained seventy thousand grain-paid troops. Jinglun, who had no sense of the political moment, first ordered forty thousand men disbanded and sent back to farming. Tian Yue pretended to obey and dismissed them as ordered; then gathered the dismissed troops and inflamed them: "You serve in the army with parents, wives, and children at home. Now the promotion commissioner has dismissed you—how will you feed and clothe yourselves?" They burst into loud weeping. Yue gave them lavish gifts from his family wealth and sent each man back to his unit. From that day their loyalty to rebellion was fixed, and Jinglun was dismissed from office. When Zhu Ci rebelled, he was given the false title of Vice Minister of Ceremonies.
17
彭偃，少負俊才，銳於進取，為當塗者所抑，形於言色。 大歷末，為都官員外郎。 時劍南東川觀察使李叔明上言，以「佛、道二教，無益於時，請粗加澄汰。 其東川寺觀，請定為二等：上寺留僧二十一人； 上觀留道士十四人，降殺以七，皆精選有道行者，余悉令返初。 蘭若、道場無名者皆廢。 」德宗曰：「叔明此奏，可為天下通制，不唯劍南一道。 」下尚書集議。 偃獻議曰：
Peng Yan was gifted in youth, ambitious, and openly resentful of the powerful men who blocked his rise. Late in the Dali era he served as Vice Director of the Capital Offices Bureau. At the time Li Shuming, observation commissioner of eastern Sichuan, memorialized that Buddhism and Daoism were of no benefit to the age and should be roughly reduced in scale. For monasteries and abbeys in eastern Sichuan he proposed two grades: upper monasteries would keep twenty-one monks; upper abbeys fourteen Daoist priests, with reductions by sevens at lower grades. Only proven practitioners were to remain; all others were to return to lay life. Unregistered chapels and halls were to be abolished entirely. Dezong said, "Shuming's proposal can serve as a general regulation for the whole empire, not only for Jiannan." The matter was referred to the Ministry of Civil Office for collective deliberation. Yan submitted a memorial that read:
18
王者之政，變人心為上，因人心次之，不變不因，循常守固者為下。 故非有獨見之明，不能行非常之事。 今陛下以惟新之政，為萬代法，若不革舊風，令歸正道者，非也。 當今道士，有名無實，時俗鮮重，亂政猶輕。 唯有僧尼，頗為穢雜。 自西方之教，被於中國，去聖日遠，空門不行五濁，比丘但行粗法。 爰自後漢，至於陳、隋，僧之廢滅，其亦數乎！ 或至坑殺，殆無遺余。 前代帝王，豈惡僧道之善如此之深耶？ 蓋其亂人亦已甚矣。 且佛之立教，清凈無為，若以色見，即是邪法，開示悟入，唯有一門，所以三乘之人，比之外道。 況今出家者皆是無識下劣之流，縱其戒行高潔，在於王者，已無用矣，況是茍避征徭，於殺盜淫，無所不犯者乎！ 今叔明之心甚善，然臣恐其奸吏詆欺，而去者未必非，留者不必是，無益於國，不能息奸。 既不變人心，亦不因人心，強制力持，難致遠耳。
In the government of a true king, transforming the people's hearts ranks highest, following their hearts ranks next, doing neither ranks lower, and clinging blindly to convention ranks lowest. Without singular vision, one cannot carry out extraordinary measures. Your Majesty's renewing government is meant to set law for ten thousand generations. If old abuses are not reformed and brought back to the correct Way, the effort will fail. Today's Daoist priests are nominal rather than real. The age scarcely esteems them, and their harm to government is still slight. Only monks and nuns are truly numerous and corrupt. Since the Western teaching spread in China, the age has grown distant from the sage. The clergy no longer uphold the pure precepts, and monks practice only the crudest observances. From Later Han through Chen and Sui, the suppression and extinction of the clergy—has this not happened again and again? At times they were slaughtered by the pitful until scarcely any survived. Did emperors of former ages hate the goodness of monks and Daoists so deeply? Rather, their harm to the people had already gone very far. The Buddha's teaching was pure, tranquil, and free of striving. To cling to appearances is already heterodox. Enlightenment admits only one gate; hence even followers of the three vehicles are classed with outer ways. Today's clergy are ignorant and base. Even if some kept lofty precepts, they would be useless to the throne—and how much more those who flee taxes and corvée and violate every prohibition against killing, theft, and lust! Shuming's intent is admirable, but I fear corrupt officials will twist the policy. Those expelled may not be the worst offenders, and those retained may not be the best. The measure will not benefit the state or end abuse. Because it neither transforms hearts nor follows them, enforcing it by brute power will not last.
19
臣聞天生烝人，必將有職，遊行浮食，王制所禁。 故有才者受爵祿，不肖者出租征，此古之常道也。 今天下僧道，不耕而食，不織而衣，廣作危言險語，以惑愚者。 一僧衣食，歲計約三萬有余，五丁所出，不能致此。 舉一僧以計天下，其費可知。 陛下日旰憂勤，將去人害，此而不救，奚其為政？ 臣伏請僧道未滿五十者，每年輸絹四疋； 尼及女道士未滿五十者，每年輸絹二疋； 其雜色役與百姓同。 有才智者令入仕，請還俗為平人者聽。 但令就役輸課，為僧何傷。 臣竊料其所出，不下今之租賦三分之一，然則陛下之國富矣，蒼生之害除矣。 其年過五十者，請皆免之。 夫子曰：「五十而知天命。 」列子曰：「不班白，不知道。 」人年五十，嗜欲巳衰，縱不出家，心已近道，況戒律檢其情性哉！ 臣以為此令既行，僧道規避還俗者固已太半。 其年老精修者，必盡為人師，則道、釋二教益重明矣。
I have heard that Heaven gives birth to the multitude and assigns each a calling. Wandering idlers who live without labor are forbidden by royal law. The talented receive rank and salary; the unworthy pay rent and tax. This is the constant Way of antiquity. Today's monks and Daoists eat without farming and dress without weaving. They spread alarming words to delude the foolish. A single monk's food and clothing cost more than thirty thousand cash a year—more than five farming households can produce. Extrapolate from one monk to the whole empire, and the cost is plain. Your Majesty toils until sundown to remove harm from the people. If you do not remedy this, what is government for? I respectfully propose that monks and Daoists under fifty pay four bolts of silk each year; nuns and female Daoist priests under fifty pay two bolts of silk each year; and their miscellaneous corvée duties would be the same as those of commoners. Men of talent would be urged to enter office, and those who wished to return to lay life would be permitted to do so. If they perform corvée and pay taxes, what harm is there in their remaining monks? I estimate their payments would amount to at least one-third of present rent and tax. The treasury would grow rich and the people's burden would be lifted. Those over fifty, I ask, should all be exempted. Confucius said, "At fifty one knows Heaven's mandate." Liezi said, "Without gray hair, one does not know the Way." At fifty one's appetites have already waned. Even without leaving home, the heart is near the Way—how much more when precepts restrain the passions! Once this order is enforced, more than half the clergy will surely return to lay life to avoid it. Those who are old and truly devoted will become teachers, and the Daoist and Buddhist traditions will shine all the brighter.
20
議者是之，上頗善其言。 大臣以二教行之已久，列聖奉之，不宜頓擾，宜去其太甚，其議不行。
The deliberators approved his proposal, and the emperor was quite pleased with it. Senior ministers argued that the two faiths had long been practiced and honored by successive emperors, and should not be suddenly disturbed—only excesses should be trimmed. The proposal was not adopted.
21
偃以才地當掌文誥，以躁求為時論所抑，郁郁不得誌。 涇師之亂，從駕不及，匿於田家，為賊所得。 硃泚素知之，得偃甚喜，偽署中書舍人，僭號辭令，皆偃為之。 賊敗，與偽中丞崔宣、賊將杜如江、吳希光等十三人，李晟收之，俱斬於安國寺前。
By talent Yan ought to have held charge of literary edicts, but his impatient striving earned him public disdain, and he brooded over his frustrated ambitions. During the Jing army rebellion he failed to keep up with the imperial flight, hid on a farm, and was captured by the rebels. Zhu Ci knew him well and was delighted to capture him. He made Yan rebel Secretariat Drafter, and Yan composed all the rebel proclamations and edicts. When the rebels fell, Li Sheng seized Yan together with the false Vice Censor Cui Xuan, rebel generals Du Rujiang and Wu Xiguang, and thirteen others. All were executed before Anguo Temple.
22
史臣曰：肇分陰陽，爰有生死，修短二事，賢愚一途。 故君子遇夷險之機，不易其節； 小人昧逆順之道，而陷於刑。 鴻毛泰山，斯為至論。 令言遠總師徒，首為叛逆； 光晟初當委任，危輸款誠； 源休雖曰士流，甚於元惡； 喬琳巧辭真主，俯就偽官； 蔣鎮貪祿隳節，皆曰小人。 經綸之徒，不足言爾。
The historiographer writes: When yin and yang were first divided, life and death arose. Long life and short life are two fates, but the worthy and the foolish walk the same road. Therefore the gentleman, whether in ease or peril, does not alter his integrity; the petty man, blind to the way of loyalty and rebellion, falls under the law. Light as a swan's down, heavy as Mount Tai—such is the supreme truth. Lingyan commanded troops from afar and led the rebellion; Guangcheng, once entrusted with command, offered sincere submission in peril; Yuan Xiu, though a scholar-official by birth, was worse than the chief villain; Qiao Lin with clever words abandoned the true sovereign and bowed to rebel office; Jiang Zhen clung to salary and ruined his integrity—all are called petty men. Men like Jinglun are scarcely worth mention.
23
贊曰：時爭逆順，命擊死生。 君子守節，小人正刑。
The encomium reads: In that age men disputed loyalty and rebellion, and fate struck at life and death. The gentleman keeps his integrity; the petty man meets the law's correction.
