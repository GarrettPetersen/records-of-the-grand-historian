○苗晉卿裴冕裴遵慶子向向子寅寅子樞=苗晉卿=苗晉卿，上黨壺關人。 世以儒素稱。 祖夔，高道不仕，追贈禮部尚書。 父殆庶，官至絳州龍門縣丞，早卒，以晉卿贈太子少保。
[Biographies:] Miao Jinqing; Pei Mian; Pei Zunqing (and descendants Xiang, Yin, and Shu). — Miao Jinqing was a native of Huguan in Shangdang. For generations the family was known for scholarly refinement and unadorned virtue. His grandfather Kui, a man of lofty principle who never entered official service, was posthumously granted the title Minister of Rites. His father Dai Shu had risen to assistant magistrate of Longmen in Jiang Prefecture but died young; on account of Jinqing he was later posthumously honored as Junior Tutor of the Heir Apparent.
2
晉卿幼好學，善屬文，進士擢第。 初授懷州修武縣尉，歷奉先縣尉，坐累貶徐州司戶參軍。 秩滿隨調，判入高等，授萬年縣尉。 遷侍御史，歷度支、兵、吏部三員外郎。 開元二十三年，遷吏部郎中。 二十四年，與吏部郎中孫逖並拜中書舍人。 二十七年，以本官權知吏部選事。 晉卿性謙柔，選人有訴訟索好官者，雖至數千言，或聲色甚厲者，晉卿必含容之，略無慍色。 二十九年，拜吏部侍郎。 前後典選五年，政既寬弛，胥吏多因緣為奸，賄賂大行。 時天下承平，每年赴選常萬余人。 李林甫為尚書，專任廟堂，銓事唯委晉卿及同列侍郎宋遙主之。 選人既多，每年兼命他官有識者同考定書判，務求其實。 天寶二年春，御史中丞張倚男奭參選，晉卿與遙以倚初承恩，欲悅附之，考選人判等凡六十四人，分甲乙丙科，奭在其首。 眾知奭不讀書，論議紛然。 有蘇孝慍者，嘗為范陽薊令，事安祿山，具其事告之。 祿山恩寵特異，謁見不常，因而奏之。 玄宗大集登科人，禦花萼樓親試，登第者十無一二； 而奭手持試紙，竟日不下一字，時謂之「曳白」。 上怒，晉卿貶為安康郡太守，遙為武當郡太守，張倚為淮陽太守。 敕曰：「門庭之間，不能訓子； 選調之際，仍以托人。 」時士子皆以為戲笑。
From boyhood Jinqing loved study and wrote well; he eventually passed the jinshi civil examination. He was first appointed magistrate of Xiuwu in Huai Prefecture, later served at Fengxian, and was then demoted to registrar in Xuzhou after being implicated in a case. When his term expired he entered the regular reassignment cycle; his evaluation placed him in the top tier, and he received appointment as magistrate of Wannian. He rose to Attending Censor and in turn held vice-director posts in the ministries of Revenue, War, and Personnel. In Kaiyuan year 23 he was made a director in the Ministry of Personnel. The following year he and Sun Ti of the Ministry of Personnel were jointly appointed drafting attendants at the Secretariat. In year 27 he was placed in provisional charge of civil-service selection while retaining his existing rank. Jinqing was by nature modest and forbearing. Candidates who came to argue their cases and demand better appointments might harangue him for thousands of words or raise their voices in fury, yet he always received them patiently and never showed irritation. In year 29 he was appointed vice minister of personnel. Over five years in charge of selection his administration grew lax; clerks seized every opening for corruption, and bribery became rampant. The empire was at peace, and each year more than ten thousand candidates presented themselves for examination. Li Linfu held the ministry post and dominated the court; he left selection affairs entirely to Jinqing and his colleague Song Yao. With so many candidates, each year additional officials of judgment were assigned to grade the examination essays together, in an effort to keep the process honest. In the spring of Tianbao 2, Censor-in-Chief Zhang Yi's son Shi sat for the examinations. Jinqing and Yao, wishing to curry favor with the newly favored Zhang Yi, ranked sixty-four candidates in three tiers—and placed Shi at the top. Everyone knew Shi was illiterate, and public outcry followed. A man named Su Xiaoyin, formerly magistrate of Ji in Fanyang and now in An Lushan's service, reported the affair in full. Lushan enjoyed extraordinary favor and was rarely granted audience at court, so the matter reached the emperor by that route. Emperor Xuanzong summoned all recent degree-holders to the Hua'e Tower for a personal examination; scarcely one candidate in ten could truly pass; Shi, holding his examination paper, could not write a single character all day—the scandal was dubbed 'dragging a blank sheet.' The emperor was furious. Jinqing was demoted to prefect of Ankang, Yao to prefect of Wudang, and Zhang Yi to prefect of Huaiyang. An edict declared: 'You cannot discipline your own son within your own household; yet at selection time you still pull strings for others.' ' Scholars everywhere treated the edict as a public joke.
3
天寶三載閏二月，轉魏郡太守，充河北采訪處置使，居職三年，政化洽聞。 會入計，因上表請歸鄉里。 既至壺關，望縣門而步。 小吏進曰：「太守位高德重，不宜自輕。 」晉卿曰：「《禮》：『下公門，式路馬。 』況父母之邦，所宜尊敬。 汝何言哉！ 」大會鄉黨，歡飲累日而去。 又俸錢三萬為鄉學本，以教授子弟。 尋改河東太守、河東采訪使，入為尚書、東京留守，征為憲部尚書。 屬祿山叛逆，楊國忠以晉卿有時望，將抑之，乃奏云：「宜以大臣鎮東道。 」遂出為陜州刺史、陜虢兩州防禦使。 及入對，固辭老病，由是忤旨，改憲部尚書致仕。 及朝廷失守，衣冠流離道路，多為逆黨所脅，自陳希烈、張均已下數十人盡赴洛陽，晉卿潛遁山谷，南投金州。 會肅宗至鳳翔，手詔追晉卿赴行在，即日拜為左相，軍國大務悉以咨之。 既收兩京，以功封韓國公，食實封五百戶，改為侍中。 後以賊寇漸除，屢乞骸骨，優詔許之，罷知政事，為太子太傅。 明年，帝思舊臣，復拜為侍中。
In the intercalary second month of Tianbao 3 he became prefect of Wei and Hebei investigation commissioner; after three years in post his benevolent rule was renowned throughout the region. When he traveled to the capital for the annual review he memorialized the throne asking leave to return home. On reaching Huguan he dismounted and walked on foot as he approached the county gate. A clerk stepped forward and said, 'Your post is exalted and your standing great—you should not humble yourself in this way. ' Jinqing replied, 'The Rites of Zhou says that when one passes the duke's gate one must bow to the horses tied along the road. ' How much more should one show respect in the land of one's parents! What sort of talk is that! ' He then feasted the township for days before taking his leave. He also endowed the local school with thirty thousand cash from his salary to educate the village youth. He was soon transferred to Hedong as prefect and investigation commissioner, then recalled to serve as a ministry director and regent of the Eastern Capital before being summoned as minister of justice. When Lushan rose in rebellion, Yang Guozhong—fearing Jinqing's public prestige and wishing to sideline him—memorialized that a senior minister should be posted to guard the eastern circuits. ' Jinqing was accordingly sent out as prefect of Shan and defense commissioner for Shan and Guo. At his audience he firmly pleaded age and illness; this displeased the emperor, and he was reassigned as retired minister of justice. When the capital fell, officials fled along the roads, many forced into rebel service. Dozens from Chen Xilie and Zhang Jun down surrendered to Luoyang; Jinqing slipped into the hills and made his way south to Jin Prefecture. When Suzong reached Fengxiang he issued a personal edict recalling Jinqing to the mobile court and that same day named him left chancellor, consulting him on every major military and civil matter. After the two capitals were retaken he was enfeoffed as Duke of Han with a substantive fief of five hundred households and appointed palace attendant. As the rebels were gradually suppressed he repeatedly asked to retire; the emperor graciously agreed, relieved him of administrative duties, and made him grand tutor of the heir apparent. The following year the emperor, remembering his old minister, again appointed him palace attendant.
4
晉卿寬厚廉謹，為政舉大綱，不問小過，所到有惠化。 魏人思之，為立碑頌德。 及秉鈞衡，小心畏慎，未嘗忤人意。 性聰敏，達練事體，百司文簿，經目必曉，而修身守位，以智自全，議者比漢之胡廣。
Jinqing was magnanimous, upright, and cautious in office. He governed by broad principles, overlooked petty faults, and left benevolent traces wherever he served. The people of Wei cherished his memory and raised a stele in praise of his virtue. Once he held the scales of state power he remained circumspect and never gave offense. Quick-witted and thoroughly versed in affairs, he could grasp any document from the hundred offices at a glance; yet he cultivated himself and guarded his post, preserving himself through tact—commentators compared him to Hu Guang of the Han.
5
玄宗崩，肅宗詔晉卿攝冢宰。 上表固辭曰：「臣聞古者殷高宗在諒闇之中，百官聽於冢宰，更無事跡，但存文字。 且一時之事，禮不相沿。 今殘寇猶虞，日殷萬務，皆緣兵馬屯守討襲，善算良謀，立勝擒敵。 陛下若行古之道，居喪不言，蒼生何依，百事皆廢。 伏讀國家起居註，亦於禮部檢見舊敕，恭惟太宗、高宗、、大行皇帝在位之日，皆有國哀，視事不輟，以為君臨天下，難徇常情。 今遺詔有處分，皇帝宜三日而聽政。 陛下遵太宗故事，則無冢宰； 遵大行皇帝遺詔，便合聽朝。 萬姓颙颙，不勝大願。 伏惟陛下知理國之重，順人心之切，以義斷恩，從宜無改。 今朝臣一命已上，皆言臣心昏貌朽，加以疾病，事有急速，斷在須臾，凡聖不同，豈合受詔。 陛下發哀已五日矣，願準遺詔聽政，則四夷萬國，無任悲幸。 」肅宗時疾彌留，覽表殞絕，乃許。
When Xuanzong died, Suzong ordered Jinqing to serve as acting regent. He memorialized a firm refusal: 'I have read that in antiquity, when King Gaozong of Yin mourned in seclusion, the hundred officials took orders from the regent—but we have only textual records of that precedent, not living practice. Moreover that was a unique moment in history; ritual precedent does not carry forward unchanged. Today remnant rebels still threaten the realm and affairs press upon us daily—all depend on armies in the field, sound strategy, and swift victory over the enemy. If Your Majesty followed the ancient way and observed mourning in silence, what would become of the people? Every affair of state would halt. I have studied the court diaries and old edicts in the Ministry of Rites and find that Emperors Taizong and Gaozong, and the late emperor, all continued to conduct state business during periods of national mourning—ruling the realm cannot bow to ordinary private feeling. The late emperor's testament directs that the new emperor should assume governance after three days. If Your Majesty follows Taizong's precedent, no regent is needed; if Your Majesty follows the late emperor's testament, you should take the throne at once. The people look to you with eager hope beyond measure. I trust Your Majesty understands the weight of governing the realm, the urgency of the people's need, and will set duty above private grief and act as circumstances require. Every minister of the first rank and above says my mind is clouded, my body worn out, and my illnesses many; affairs demand instant decisions—how could a mere mortal accept such a mandate? Your Majesty has mourned five days already; I beg you to follow the testament and resume governance—the four quarters and all nations would be overwhelmed with grief and joy. ' Suzong was then gravely ill; reading the memorial he nearly fainted, then consented.
6
數日，肅宗晏駕，代宗踐祚，又詔晉卿攝冢宰。 晉卿上表懇辭曰：「臣以昔者天子居喪之時，百官聽於冢宰者，蓋君幼小，禦極事殷，情理當然。 沿革不一，今古異同，而周武、漢文，合於通變，垂範作則，可舉而行。 又士或墨缞，時遇金革，豈非銜恤，謂義在斷恩。 且百善之至，無加於孝也，其有容瘁心絕，指景悼生，此匹夫守節之常情，殊王者嗣續之大計。 昨二十日，陛下於大行皇帝柩前即位，是承先帝遺顧之言，亦前代不易之典。 則知所略不為害，所存是適權，防威滅端，所利者大。 陛下因心純至，天地明察。 伏以報劬勞之恩，申罔極之思，終身之痛，豈計朝夕！ 但以一日之內，萬務在中，須達宸聰，始成國政。 百僚萬姓及僧道耆壽等，相顧聚言，以臣老且無能，愚豈測聖，況久無居攝，臣不敢奉詔。 特乞陛下遵遺命，三日而政。 臣博聽眾情，不勝懇願，伏望割痛抑哀，則天下悲幸。 」上號泣從之。 時晉卿年已衰暮，又患兩足，上特許肩輿至中書，入閣不趨，累日一視事。 歷三朝，皆以謹密見稱。
Days later Suzong died and Daizong succeeded; again Jinqing was ordered to serve as acting regent. Jinqing memorialized again in earnest refusal: 'In former times, when an emperor mourned in seclusion the hundred officials took orders from a regent because the ruler was a child and affairs of state were pressing—that was only reasonable. Precedent varies with the age, yet King Wu of Zhou and Emperor Wen of Han adapted ritual to circumstance and left models we may follow. Scholars sometimes wore plain mourning garb yet took up arms when the state was threatened—grief held in the heart, but duty required setting private feeling aside. Filial piety is the highest of virtues, yet wasting away in grief and mourning one's own life is the conduct of a private man—not the great design of a king who must continue the dynastic line. Twenty days ago Your Majesty took the throne before the late emperor's bier—fulfilling his dying charge and following an unchanging precedent of former dynasties. What is set aside does no harm; what is preserved serves timely expedience—guarding against peril at its root brings the greatest benefit. Your Majesty's filial heart is pure and sincere—Heaven and Earth witness it. To repay parental toil and express boundless grief is a lifelong sorrow—not a matter of days alone! Yet within a single day ten thousand affairs demand the emperor's ear—only then can government proceed. Officials, commoners, monks, and elders alike declare that I am old and incompetent—how could such a fool measure a sage emperor? Regency has long been obsolete; I dare not accept the mandate. I beg Your Majesty especially to follow the testament and assume governance after three days. Having heard the people's voice everywhere, I cannot contain my earnest plea: restrain your grief, and the realm will be overwhelmed with sorrow and joy alike. ' The emperor wept aloud and assented. Jinqing was by then elderly and afflicted in both legs; the emperor specially allowed him a sedan chair to the Secretariat, excused him from hurrying into court, and let him conduct business only every few days. Across three reigns he was praised for discretion and reliability.
7
廣德初，吐蕃寇長安。 晉卿時病臥於私第，蕃聞之，輿入逼脅，晉卿閉口不言，賊不敢害。 及上自陜至，冊為太保，罷知政事，又詔以太保致仕。 永泰元年四月薨。 輟朝三日，令京兆少尹一員護喪事，緣葬諸物並官給，賻絹布五百段、米粟五百石。 太常議謚曰「懿獻」。 初，晉卿東都留守，引用大理評事元載為推官。 至是載為中書侍郎、平章事，懷舊恩，諷有司改謚曰文貞。 大歷七年，令配享肅宗廟庭。
Early in the Guangde era Tibetans raided Chang'an. Jinqing lay ill at home; the Tibetans heard of him, came in a litter to coerce him, but he would not speak—and the raiders did not dare harm him. When the emperor returned from Shan, Jinqing was enfeoffed grand guardian, relieved of administrative duties, and ordered to retire in that rank. He died in the fourth month of Yongtai 1. Court mourning lasted three days; a vice director of the capital oversaw the funeral; all burial goods were supplied by the state; and five hundred bolts of silk and five hundred piculs of grain were granted as condolence gifts. The Directorate of Ceremonies proposed the posthumous name Yi Xian ('Benevolent and Offering'). Earlier, while regent of the Eastern Capital, Jinqing had appointed Yuan Zai, an evaluator in the Court of Judicial Review, as his investigating officer. By then Yuan Zai was vice director of the Secretariat and chief councillor; remembering old kindness, he prompted the authorities to change the posthumous name to Wen Zhen ('Cultured and Upright'). In Dali year 7 he was granted a place in the sacrifices at Suzong's temple.
8
=裴冕=裴冕，河東人也，為河東冠族。 天寶初，以門廕再遷渭南縣尉，以吏道聞。 御史中丞王鉷充京畿采訪使，表為判官。 遷監察御史，歷殿中侍御史。 冕雖無學術，守職通明，果於臨事，鉷甚委之。 及鉷得罪伏法，時宰臣李林甫方竊權柄，人鹹懼之，鉷賓佐數百，不敢窺鉷門。 冕獨收鉷屍，親自護喪，瘞於近郊，冕自是知名。 河西節度使哥舒翰表為行軍司馬，累遷員外郎中。
— Pei Mian — Pei Mian was a native of Hedong and belonged to the region's foremost clan. Early in the Tianbao era he rose through hereditary privilege to magistrate of Weinan and won a reputation for administrative skill. When Censor-in-Chief Wang Hong served as capital-region investigation commissioner he recommended Mian as his administrative aide. He was promoted to investigating censor and then served as palace attending censor. Though without scholarly training, Mian was lucid in office and resolute in action; Wang Hong relied on him heavily. When Wang Hong was executed, Chief Councillor Li Linfu held power and all feared him; Hong's hundreds of staff did not dare even approach his gate. Mian alone recovered Hong's body, personally conducted the funeral, and buried him in the suburbs—after which Mian became widely known. Hexi commissioner Geshu Han recommended him as campaigning marshal; he rose in turn to vice director.
9
玄宗幸蜀，至益昌郡，遙詔太子充天下兵馬元帥，以冕為御史中丞兼左庶子，為之副。 是時，冕為河西行軍司馬，授御史中丞，詔赴朝廷。 遇太子於平涼，具陳事勢，勸之朔方，亟入靈武。 冕與杜鴻漸、崔漪等勸進曰：「主上厭勤大位，南幸蜀川，宗社神器，須有所歸，天意人事，不可固違。 若逡巡退讓，失億兆心，則大事去矣！ 臣等猶知之，況賢智乎！ 」太子曰：「南平寇逆，奉迎鑾輿，退居儲貳，侍膳左右，豈不樂哉！ 公等何言之過也？ 」冕與杜鴻漸又進曰：「殿下藉累聖之資，有天下之表。 元貞萬國，二十余年，殷憂啟聖，正在今日。 所從殿下六軍將士，皆關輔百姓，日夜思歸。 大軍一散，不可復集，不如因而撫之以從眾，臣等敢以死請。 」凡勸進五上，乃依。 肅宗即位，以定策功，遷中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事，倚以為政。
When Xuanzong fled to Shu and reached Yichang, he issued a distant edict naming the crown prince commander of all forces under heaven, with Mian as censor-in-chief and left guardian of the heir as his deputy. Mian was then campaigning marshal in Hexi; appointed censor-in-chief, he was ordered to proceed to court. He met the crown prince at Pingliang, laid out the strategic situation in full, urged him toward Shuofang, and pressed him to enter Lingwu at once. Mian, Du Hongjian, Cui Yi, and others urged the prince to take the throne: 'The emperor has grown weary of rule and gone south to Shu; the altars of state and the imperial regalia must have a home—Heaven's will and human necessity cannot be defied. If you hesitate and step aside, you will lose the hearts of the people—and the great cause will be lost! Even we understand this—how much more must the wise among men! ' The crown prince replied, 'To pacify the south, defeat the rebels, welcome back the imperial carriage, and then serve at my father's side as heir apparent—what could be happier than that! Your words go too far, gentlemen. ' Mian and Du Hongjian pressed again: 'Your Highness inherits the accumulated virtue of successive sage emperors and bears the countenance of one who should rule the realm. For more than twenty years the dynasty has held the realm in sincere devotion; out of deep crisis a sage ruler must emerge—and that moment is today. The soldiers of the six armies who follow you are all men of the capital region who long day and night to return home. Once the army disperses it cannot be reassembled; accept the throne now to hold the men together—we beg this at the risk of our lives. ' After five rounds of urging, the prince at last agreed. When Suzong took the throne, Mian was promoted to vice director of the Secretariat and chief councillor for his role in the succession, and the emperor relied on him to govern.

冕性忠勤，悉心奉公，稍得人心。 然不識大體，以聚人曰財，乃下令賣官鬻爵，度尼僧道士，以儲積為務。 人不願者，科令就之，其價益賤，事轉為弊。 肅宗移幸鳳翔，罷冕知政事，遷右僕射。 兩京平，以功封冀國公，食實封五百戶。 尋加御史大夫、成都尹，充劍南西川節度使。 又入為右僕射。 永泰元年，與裴遵慶等並集賢待制。 代宗求舊，拜冕兼御史大夫，充護山陵使。 冕以幸臣李輔國權盛，將附之，乃表輔國親昵術士中書舍人劉烜充山陵使判官。 烜坐法，冕坐貶施州刺史。 數月，移澧州刺史，復征為左僕射。 元載秉政。 載為新平縣尉，王鉷辟在巡內，冕常引之，載頗德冕。 會宰臣杜鴻漸卒，載遂舉冕代之。 冕時已衰瘵，載以其順己，引為同列。 受命之際，蹈舞絕倒，載趨而扶起，代為謝詞。 冕兼掌兵權留守之任，俸錢每月二千余貫。 性本侈靡，好尚車服及營珍饌，名馬在櫪，直數百金者常十數。 每會賓友，滋味品數，坐客有昧於名者。 自創巾子，其狀新奇，市肆因而效之，呼為「僕射樣」。 初代鴻漸，小吏以俸錢文簿白之，冕顧子弟，喜見於色，其嗜利若此。 拜職未盈月，卒，大歷四年十二月也。 上悼之，輟朝三日，贈太尉，賻制五百匹、粟五百石。
Mian was loyal and diligent, devoted himself wholly to public service, and gradually won popular regard. Yet he lacked a grasp of larger policy; believing that amassing people meant amassing wealth, he ordered the sale of offices and ranks, ordained nuns, monks, and Daoist priests, and made hoarding his chief concern. Those who resisted were compelled by statute to comply; prices fell ever lower, and the policy became a public scandal. When Suzong moved the court to Fengxiang, Mian was relieved of administrative duties and made right vice director. After the two capitals were recovered he was enfeoffed as Duke of Ji with a substantive fief of five hundred households. He was soon also made censor-in-chief, prefect of Chengdu, and military commissioner of Jiannan West. He later returned to the capital as right vice director. In Yongtai 1 he and Pei Zunqing and others were appointed academicians awaiting orders at the Hall of Assembled Worthies. Daizong, seeking veteran ministers, appointed Mian concurrent censor-in-chief and commissioner for guarding the imperial tombs. Wishing to curry favor with the powerful favorite Li Fuguo, Mian recommended Fuguo's intimate, the drafting attendant and occultist Liu Xuan, as administrative aide on the tomb commission. When Xuan was convicted by law, Mian was implicated and demoted to prefect of Shi. Months later he was transferred to prefect of Li and again summoned as left vice director. Yuan Zai held the reins of government. Zai had once been magistrate of Xinping; Wang Hong recruited him on his inspection tours, and Mian had often advanced him—Zai remained deeply grateful. When Chief Councillor Du Hongjian died, Zai recommended Mian to replace him. Mian was by then gravely ill; Zai, knowing he would not oppose him, brought him onto the council as a fellow minister. At his investiture Mian stamped, danced, and collapsed; Zai rushed forward to lift him and delivered the thanks on his behalf. Mian also held military authority and the regency; his monthly salary exceeded two thousand strings of cash. By nature he was extravagant, fond of fine carriages and robes and elaborate feasts; a dozen famous horses worth hundreds of gold each stood in his stables. At every banquet for guests the dishes were so numerous and exotic that some guests could not even name what they were eating. He invented a new style of headcloth so striking that shops across the city copied it and called it the 'vice director's style.' When he first replaced Hongjian, a clerk presented the salary ledger; Mian glanced at his sons and nephews with visible delight—such was his love of gain. He had held office less than a month when he died, in the twelfth month of Dali 4. The emperor mourned him with three days of court suspension, posthumously granted him grand marshal, and sent five hundred bolts of silk and five hundred piculs of grain.
11
=裴遵慶=裴遵慶，絳州聞喜人也。 代襲冠冕，為河東著族。 遵慶志氣深厚，機鑒敏達，自幼強學，博涉載籍，謹身晦跡，不幹當世之務。 以門廕累授潞府司法參軍，時年已老，未為人所知。 隨調吏部，授大理寺丞，剖斷刑獄，舉正綱條，理行始著。 遷司門員外、吏部員外郎，專判南曹。 天寶中，海內無事，九流輻輳會府，每歲吏部選人，動盈萬數。 遵慶敏識強記，精核文簿，詳而不滯，時稱吏事第一，由是大知名。
— Pei Zunqing — Pei Zunqing was a native of Wenxi in Jiang Prefecture. For generations the family held high rank and were a leading clan of Hedong. Zunqing was deep-spirited and keen-witted; from youth he studied hard and read widely, kept a low profile, and avoided entanglement in contemporary politics. Through hereditary privilege he rose to judicial administrator in Lu Prefecture, but he was already elderly and still unknown to the wider world. On routine reassignment at the Ministry of Personnel he was made assistant director of the Court of Judicial Review, where his handling of criminal cases and enforcement of statutes first brought his talent to notice. He was promoted to vice director of the Gate Office and the Ministry of Personnel, with sole charge of the Southern Bureau. During the Tianbao era the empire was at peace and candidates of every stripe thronged the capital; each year's civil-service examinations drew well over ten thousand applicants. Zunqing was quick-witted with a prodigious memory, mastered documents with meticulous care yet without delay, and was acclaimed the foremost administrator of his day—winning wide renown.
12
天寶末，楊國忠當國，出不附己者例為外官，遵慶亦出為郡守。 肅宗即位，征拜給事中、尚書右丞、吏部侍郎。 恭儉克己，遲重謹密，頗有時望。 上元中，蕭華輔政，素知遵慶，每奏見，累稱之，遷黃門侍郎、同中書門下平章事。 廣德初，仆固懷恩阻兵汾上，指中官為詞，上以遵慶忠純，特遣往汾州宣慰懷恩。 遵慶既見懷恩，具陳朝旨，懷恩引過聽命，將隨遵慶朝謁，為副將範誌誠以邪說惑之，懷恩遂以懼死為詞。 會蕃寇陷京師，乘輿幸陜，遵慶自汾州奔赴行在。 及乘輿還京，以遵慶為太子少傅。 永泰元年，與裴冕等並於集賢院待制，罷知政事。 尋改吏部尚書、右僕射，復知選事。 時選人天興縣尉陳琯於銓庭言詞不遜，淩突無禮，代宗詔付遵慶於省門鞭三十，貶為吉州員外司戶參軍。 遵慶敦守儒行，老而彌謹。 嘗為風狂族侄撾登聞鼓告以不順，上知遵度，不省，其見信如此。 大歷十年十月薨於位，年九十余。
Late in Tianbao, Yang Guozhong dominated the court and routinely posted opponents to provincial office; Zunqing too was sent out as a prefect. When Suzong took the throne, Zunqing was summoned as drafting attendant, right assistant director of the secretariat, and vice minister of personnel. Respectful, frugal, and self-restrained, deliberate and discreet, he enjoyed considerable standing at court. During the Shangyuan era, Chief Councillor Xiao Hua, who had long known Zunqing's worth, repeatedly praised him at audience and had him promoted to vice director of the palace gate and chief councillor. Early in Guangde, Pugu Huai'en held troops on the Fen River and blamed the eunuchs; the emperor, trusting Zunqing's loyalty, specially dispatched him to Fen Prefecture to reassure Huai'en. When Zunqing met Huai'en he laid out the court's intent in full; Huai'en acknowledged his errors and agreed to accompany Zunqing to court, but his deputy Fan Zhicheng swayed him with sinister counsel, and Huai'en pleaded that he feared execution. When Tibetan raiders took the capital and the emperor fled to Shan, Zunqing rushed from Fen Prefecture to join the mobile court. When the emperor returned to the capital, Zunqing was appointed junior tutor of the heir apparent. In Yongtai 1 he and Pei Mian and others were made academicians at the Hall of Assembled Worthies and relieved of administrative duties. He was soon made minister of personnel and right vice director and again put in charge of civil-service selection. When the candidate Chen Guan, magistrate of Tianxing, spoke insolently at the selection courtyard, Daizong ordered Zunqing to have him flogged thirty strokes at the ministry gate and demoted to acting registrar in Ji Prefecture. Zunqing steadfastly upheld Confucian conduct and grew only more cautious with age. Once a deranged clansman beat the petition drum at the Gate of Imperial Audience and accused him of disloyalty; the emperor, knowing Zunqing's character, paid no heed—such was the trust he enjoyed. He died in office in the tenth month of Dali 10, aged more than ninety.
13
遵慶初登省郎，嘗著《王政記》，述今古禮體，識者覽之，知有公輔之量。 遵慶子向子向，字傃仁，少以門廕歷官至太子司議郎。 建中初，李紓為同州刺史，奏向為從事。 硃泚反，李懷光又叛河中，使其將趙貴先築壘於同州，紓來奔奉天，向領州務。 貴先因脅縣尉林寶役徒板築，不及期，將斬之，吏人百姓奔竄。 向即詣貴先軍壘，以逆順之理責之，貴先感悟，遂來降，故同州不陷。 向由是知名。 累為京兆府戶曹，轉櫟陽、渭南縣令，奏課皆第一，朝廷亟聞其理行，擢為戶部員外郎。
Early in his career as a provincial bureau officer he wrote Record of Royal Government, surveying ritual institutions past and present; readers recognized in it the makings of a chief minister. Zunqing's son Xiang, courtesy name Surén, rose through hereditary privilege to petition reviewer of the heir apparent while still young. Early in the Jianzhong era, when Li Shu was prefect of Tong, he recommended Xiang as his staff officer. When Zhu Ci rebelled and Li Huai'guang rose in Hezhong, sending his general Zhao Guixian to fortify Tong, Shu fled to Fengtian and Xiang took charge of the prefecture. Guixian forced Assistant Magistrate Lin Bao to press laborers into construction work; when the deadline was missed he was about to execute Bao, and officials and commoners fled in terror. Xiang went at once to Guixian's camp and rebuked him on the principles of loyalty and rebellion; Guixian was moved to repentance and surrendered, and Tong Prefecture was thus spared. From this Xiang won wide renown. He served as revenue clerk in the capital prefecture and as magistrate of Liyang and Weinan, earning top ratings in every assessment; the court soon heard of his governance and promoted him to vice director in the Ministry of Revenue.
14
德宗季年，天下方鎮副倅多自選於朝，防一日有變，遂就而授之節制。 向已選為太原少尹，德宗召見喻旨，尋用為行軍司馬、兼御史中丞，改汾州刺史，轉鄭州。 又復為太原少尹，兼河東節度副使。 改晉州刺史，充本州防禦使，遷虢州刺史。 入為京兆少尹，拜同州刺史，充本州防禦使。 入為大理寺卿，出遷陜虢都防禦、觀察使。 三歲，拜左散騎常侍，自常侍復為大理。
Late in Dezong's reign, deputy commissioners for the military provinces were largely chosen from the central government so that, if crisis arose, trusted men would already be in place to receive command. Xiang had been selected as junior governor of Taiyuan; Dezong summoned him to explain his intentions, then appointed him campaigning marshal and concurrent censor-in-chief, later prefect of Fen and then of Zheng. He again served as junior governor of Taiyuan and deputy military commissioner of Hedong. He was made prefect of Jin and defense commissioner of that prefecture, then transferred to prefect of Guo. He returned to the capital as junior governor of the metropolitan prefecture, then was appointed prefect of Tong and its defense commissioner. He was recalled as director of the Court of Judicial Review, then sent out as defense and surveillance commissioner for Shan and Guo. After three years he was made left regular attendant of the cavalry guard, then returned to the directorship of the Court of Judicial Review.

向本以名相子，以學行自飭，謹守其門風。 歷官仁智推愛，利及於人。 至是，以年過致政，朝廷優異，乃以吏部尚書致仕於新昌裏第。 內外支屬百余人，向所得俸祿，必同其費，及領外任，亦挈而隨之。 有孤煢疾苦不能自恤，向尤周給，至今稱其孝睦焉。 大和四年九月卒，年八十。 贈太子少保。 向子寅子寅，登進士第，累官至御史大夫卒。 寅子樞子樞，字紀聖，咸通十二年登進士第。 宰相杜審權出鎮河中，辟為從事，得秘書省校書郎，再遷藍田尉。 直弘文館。 大學士王鐸深知之，鐸罷相失職，樞亦久之不調。 從僖宗幸蜀，中丞李煥奏為殿中侍御史，遷起居郎。 中和初，王鐸復見用，以舊恩徙為鄭滑掌書記、檢校司封郎中，賜金紫，入朝歷兵、吏二員外郎。 龍紀初，擢拜給事中，改京兆尹。 宰相孔緯尤深獎遇。 大順中，緯以用兵無功貶官，樞坐累為右庶子，尋出為歙州刺史。 乾寧初，入為右散騎常侍，從昭宗幸華州，為汴州宣諭使。
As the son of a famous chief minister, Xiang disciplined himself through learning and conduct and carefully upheld his family's reputation. In every post he extended benevolence and wisdom, bringing benefit to those around him. By then, advanced in years, he retired; the court honored him with exceptional favor and allowed him to retire as minister of personnel at his home in Xinchang Lane. More than a hundred kinsmen of the inner and outer branches depended on him; he always shared his salary with them, and when he took provincial posts he brought them along. Orphans, the destitute, and the sick who could not care for themselves he especially supported; to this day he is praised for filial devotion and family harmony. He died in the ninth month of Dahe 4, aged eighty. He was posthumously honored as junior tutor of the heir apparent. Xiang's son Yin passed the jinshi examination and rose through the ranks to censor-in-chief before his death. Yin's son Shu, courtesy name Jisheng, passed the jinshi examination in Xiantong 12. When Chief Councillor Du Shenquan took command of Hezhong he recruited Shu as staff officer; Shu became collator of the Secretariat and later magistrate of Lantian. He served as a direct attendant at the Hall of Expansive Culture. Grand Academician Wang Duo thought highly of him; when Duo lost the chancellorship, Shu too went long without reassignment. He accompanied Xizong to Shu; Vice Censor Li Huan recommended him as palace attending censor, and he was promoted to attendant for drafting the imperial diary. Early in the Zhonghe era Wang Duo returned to power and, remembering old ties, appointed Shu secretary at Zheng-Hua and acting director in the Office for Enfeoffment, with the gold-and-purple insignia; recalled to court he served as vice director in the ministries of War and Personnel. At the start of the Longji era he was promoted to drafting attendant and then made governor of the metropolitan prefecture. Chief Councillor Kong Wei especially favored and promoted him. During Dazhun, when Wei was demoted for military failure, Shu was implicated and made right guardian of the heir apparent, then sent out as prefect of She. Early in Qianning he returned as right regular attendant of the cavalry guard, accompanied Zhaozong to Hua Prefecture, and served as imperial envoy to Bian.

初，樞自歙州罷郡歸朝，路經大梁，時硃全忠兵威已振，樞以兄事之，全忠由是重之。 及樞傳詔，全忠皆稟朝旨，獻奉相繼，昭宗甚悅，遷兵部侍郎。 時崔胤專政，亦倚全忠，二人因是相結，改樞吏部侍郎。 未幾，換戶部侍郎、同平章事。 其年冬，昭宗幸華州，崔胤貶官，樞亦為工部尚書。 天子自岐下還宮，以樞檢校右僕射、同平章事，出為廣南節度使。 制出，硃全忠保薦之，言樞有經世才，不可棄之嶺表，尋復拜門下侍郎，監修國史，累兼吏部尚書，判度支。 崔胤誅，以全忠素厚，相位如故。 從昭宗遷洛陽，駐蹕陜州，進右僕射、弘文館大學士、太清宮使，充諸道鹽鐵轉運使。
When Shu left the prefecture of She and returned to court, his route passed through Daliang; Zhu Quanzhong's military power was already formidable, and Shu treated him as an elder brother—whereupon Quanzhong came to esteem him. When Shu delivered the imperial edict, Quanzhong complied with the court's wishes and sent tribute in succession; Zhaozong was greatly pleased and promoted Shu to vice minister of war. Cui Yin then dominated the government and also relied on Quanzhong; the two allied, and Shu was made vice minister of personnel. Soon he was transferred to vice minister of the household and appointed chief councillor. That winter, when Zhaozong went to Hua and Cui Yin was demoted, Shu was made minister of works. When the emperor returned from Qi to the capital, Shu was made acting right vice director and chief councillor, then sent out as military commissioner of Guangnan. When the appointment was issued, Zhu Quanzhong intervened, declaring that Shu had talent to govern the age and must not be sent to the far south; Shu was soon recalled as vice director of the gate department, supervisor of the national history, and concurrently minister of personnel with charge of the treasury. When Cui Yin was executed, Shu retained his chancellorship through his long-standing ties to Quanzhong. He accompanied Zhaozong to Luoyang and, when the court halted at Shan, was promoted to right vice director, grand academician of the Hall of Expansive Culture, commissioner of the Great Pure Palace, and salt-and-iron transport commissioner for all circuits.
17
哀帝初嗣位，柳璨用事，全忠嘗奏用牙將張廷範為太常卿，諸相議，樞曰：「廷範勛臣，幸有方鎮節鉞之命，何藉樂卿？ 恐非元帥梁王之旨。 」乃持之不下。 俄而全忠聞樞言，謂賓佐曰：「吾常以裴十四器識真純，不入浮薄之伍，觀此議論，本態露矣。 」切齒含怒。 柳璨聞全忠言，尋希旨罷樞相位，和陵祔享，拜尚書左僕射。 五月，責授朝散大夫、登州刺史，尋再貶瀧州司戶。 六月十一日，行及滑州，全忠遣人殺之於白馬驛，投屍於河，時年六十五。
When Emperor Aidi first succeeded, Liu Can held power; Quanzhong memorialized to appoint his military officer Zhang Tingfan director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. The chief ministers debated; Shu said, 'Tingfan is a meritorious officer who might well receive a frontier military commission—why does he need the directorship of the Court of Sacrifices? I fear this is not the intent of the commander-in-chief, the Prince of Liang.' ' He held firm and refused to issue the appointment. Soon Quanzhong heard what Shu had said and told his staff, 'I always thought Pei the Fourteenth was genuinely upright and above the shallow crowd—but this argument shows his true colors. ' He gnashed his teeth in fury. Liu Can, hearing Quanzhong's words, soon pleased his intent by removing Shu from the chancellorship; Shu was made left vice director of the secretariat for the joint tomb offering. In the fifth month he was demoted to gentleman for dispersing at court and prefect of Deng; soon he was demoted again to registrar in Long Prefecture. On the eleventh day of the sixth month, when he reached Hua Prefecture, Quanzhong had him killed at Baima Post Station and his body thrown into the river; he was sixty-five.

=【贊】=史臣曰：晉卿謹身蒞事，足為純臣，避寇全忠，固彰大節。 然博達精審，豈不知寬猛之道哉！ 奉林甫之旨，順胥吏之意，悅附張倚，欺罔時君。 生為重臣，諂林甫之勢也； 歿改美謚，引元載之恩焉。 或言晉卿不為巧宦者，誠不信也。 冕力贊中興，名居大位，奉公抱義，可以致身； 賣官度僧，是何為政？ 及其老也，貪冒尤深。 遵慶學術貞明，為國忠所出； 恭儉謹密，遇蕭華素知。 位重行純，老而彌篤，彼二公固有慚德。 向克荷堂構，不墜門風。 樞因盜而振，盜憎而亡，宜哉！ 君子守道遠刑，蓋慮此也。
[Eulogy] The historiographer writes: Jinqing conducted himself with care in office and was a true loyal minister; in fleeing the rebels he preserved his integrity and showed great moral courage. Yet he was learned and discerning—how could he not have known the balance of leniency and severity! He followed Li Linfu's wishes, indulged the clerks, curried favor with Zhang Yi, and deceived the emperor. In life he was a great minister who flattered Li Linfu's power; in death his posthumous name was improved through Yuan Zai's favor. Those who claim Jinqing was no crafty courtier simply cannot be believed. Mian vigorously supported the restoration and rose to high office; devoted to public duty and righteousness, he might have been remembered with honor— yet he sold offices and ordained monks—what manner of governance was that? In old age his greed grew worse still. Zunqing was learned and upright, though Yang Guozhong posted him to the provinces; respectful, frugal, and discreet—he won recognition from Xiao Hua, who had long known his worth. He held high rank with pure conduct and grew only more steadfast with age—the other two men had reason to feel shame beside him. Xiang upheld the family legacy and did not let its reputation fall. Shu rose through the rebel, and when the rebel turned against him he perished—as was fitting! The gentleman keeps to the Way and stays clear of punishment—perhaps he had this in mind.
