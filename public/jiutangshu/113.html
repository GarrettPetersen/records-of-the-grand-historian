○苗晉卿裴冕裴遵慶子向向子寅寅子樞=苗晉卿=苗晉卿，上黨壺關人。 世以儒素稱。 祖夔，高道不仕，追贈禮部尚書。 父殆庶，官至絳州龍門縣丞，早卒，以晉卿贈太子少保。
○ Biographies: Miao Jinqing; Pei Mian; Pei Zunqing; his son Zi Xiang; Zi Xiang's son Yin; Yin's son Shu. — Miao Jinqing was a native of Huguan in Shangdang commandery. His clan was renowned for scholarly rectitude. His grandfather Kui, a man of lofty principles who never entered government service, was posthumously honored as Minister of Rites. His father Dai Shu rose to assistant magistrate of Longmen County in Jiang Prefecture and died young; on account of Jinqing's eminence he was posthumously granted the title Junior Mentor to the Heir Apparent.
2
晉卿幼好學，善屬文，進士擢第。 初授懷州修武縣尉，歷奉先縣尉，坐累貶徐州司戶參軍。 秩滿隨調，判入高等，授萬年縣尉。 遷侍御史，歷度支、兵、吏部三員外郎。 開元二十三年，遷吏部郎中。 二十四年，與吏部郎中孫逖並拜中書舍人。 二十七年，以本官權知吏部選事。 晉卿性謙柔，選人有訴訟索好官者，雖至數千言，或聲色甚厲者，晉卿必含容之，略無慍色。 二十九年，拜吏部侍郎。 前後典選五年，政既寬弛，胥吏多因緣為奸，賄賂大行。 時天下承平，每年赴選常萬余人。 李林甫為尚書，專任廟堂，銓事唯委晉卿及同列侍郎宋遙主之。 選人既多，每年兼命他官有識者同考定書判，務求其實。 天寶二年春，御史中丞張倚男奭參選，晉卿與遙以倚初承恩，欲悅附之，考選人判等凡六十四人，分甲乙丙科，奭在其首。 眾知奭不讀書，論議紛然。 有蘇孝慍者，嘗為范陽薊令，事安祿山，具其事告之。 祿山恩寵特異，謁見不常，因而奏之。 玄宗大集登科人，禦花萼樓親試，登第者十無一二； 而奭手持試紙，竟日不下一字，時謂之「曳白」。 上怒，晉卿貶為安康郡太守，遙為武當郡太守，張倚為淮陽太守。 敕曰：「門庭之間，不能訓子； 選調之際，仍以托人。 」時士子皆以為戲笑。
As a youth Jinqing devoted himself to study, excelled at composition, and qualified as a jinshi. He was first appointed sheriff of Xiuwu in Huai Prefecture, later served as sheriff of Fengxian, then was demoted to registrar of Xuzhou on account of implicated misconduct. Upon completing his term he entered the routine transfer process, received top marks on his evaluation, and was appointed sheriff of Wannian. He rose to attendant censor and served in succession as vice director in the ministries of revenue, war, and personnel. In Kaiyuan 23 (735) he was promoted to director within the Ministry of Personnel. The following year he and Sun Ti were both appointed drafting attendants of the Secretariat. In Kaiyuan 27 he was assigned, while retaining his rank, to oversee civil-service examinations and appointments. By nature Jinqing was mild and forbearing. Candidates who came to plead their cases or demand favorable posts might harangue him for thousands of words or raise their voices in fury, yet he always received them with equanimity and never showed irritation. In Kaiyuan 29 he was appointed vice minister of personnel. Over five years in charge of the examinations, his administration grew lax; clerks exploited the slackness for corruption, and bribery became widespread. The empire was at peace, and more than ten thousand candidates presented themselves for examination each year. Li Linfu, as minister of personnel, dominated the court and left the examinations entirely to Jinqing and his fellow vice minister Song Yao. Because the candidate pool was so large, each year additional experienced officials were brought in to grade the written examinations as rigorously as possible. In the spring of Tianbao 2 (743), Zhang Yi's son Shi entered the examinations. Hoping to curry favor with the newly favored Zhang Yi, Jinqing and Yao ranked sixty-four candidates in three tiers—and placed Shi at the top. Everyone knew Shi was illiterate, and outrage spread through the capital. A former magistrate of Ji in Fanyang named Su Xiaoyin, who was now attached to An Lushan, reported the affair to him in full. An Lushan held extraordinary favor at court and had irregular access to the emperor, through which channel he brought the matter before the throne. Xuanzong summoned the newly passed candidates to the Hua'e Pavilion for a personal examination; fewer than one in ten succeeded. Shi held his examination paper and could not write a single character all day long—they called it "dragging a blank sheet." The emperor's wrath fell on them all: Jinqing was banished to prefect of Ankang, Yao to prefect of Wudang, and Zhang Yi to prefect of Huaiyang. The edict read: "He could not discipline his son under his own roof; at examination time he still used influence on others' behalf." The literati treated the whole affair as a public laughingstock.
3
天寶三載閏二月，轉魏郡太守，充河北采訪處置使，居職三年，政化洽聞。 會入計，因上表請歸鄉里。 既至壺關，望縣門而步。 小吏進曰：「太守位高德重，不宜自輕。 」晉卿曰：「《禮》：『下公門，式路馬。 』況父母之邦，所宜尊敬。 汝何言哉！ 」大會鄉黨，歡飲累日而去。 又俸錢三萬為鄉學本，以教授子弟。 尋改河東太守、河東采訪使，入為尚書、東京留守，征為憲部尚書。 屬祿山叛逆，楊國忠以晉卿有時望，將抑之，乃奏云：「宜以大臣鎮東道。 」遂出為陜州刺史、陜虢兩州防禦使。 及入對，固辭老病，由是忤旨，改憲部尚書致仕。 及朝廷失守，衣冠流離道路，多為逆黨所脅，自陳希烈、張均已下數十人盡赴洛陽，晉卿潛遁山谷，南投金州。 會肅宗至鳳翔，手詔追晉卿赴行在，即日拜為左相，軍國大務悉以咨之。 既收兩京，以功封韓國公，食實封五百戶，改為侍中。 後以賊寇漸除，屢乞骸骨，優詔許之，罷知政事，為太子太傅。 明年，帝思舊臣，復拜為侍中。
In the intercalary second month of Tianbao 3 (744) he became prefect of Wei and Hebei investigation commissioner; after three years his enlightened rule was renowned throughout the region. While in the capital for his annual report to the court, he petitioned to visit his hometown. On reaching Huguan he dismounted and walked on foot as he approached the county gate. A clerk came forward and said, "Your Excellency holds high rank and great prestige—you should not humble yourself." Jinqing replied, "The Book of Rites says: 'When leaving the duke's gate, one bows toward the horses on the road.' How much more should one show respect in the land of one's parents?" What sort of talk is that!" He held a great feast for his kinsmen and neighbors, reveling for days before departing. He also endowed the local school with thirty thousand cash from his salary to educate the village youth. He was soon transferred to prefect of Hedong and Hedong investigation commissioner, then recalled to serve as minister and intendant of the Eastern Capital before being appointed minister of justice. When An Lushan rebelled, Yang Guozhong, wishing to sideline the popular Jinqing, memorialized that a senior minister should be posted to guard the eastern circuit. Jinqing was accordingly sent out as prefect of Shaan and defense commissioner for Shaan and Guo. At his audience he firmly pleaded age and infirmity, thereby offending the emperor, who reassigned him as retired minister of justice. When the capital fell, court officials fled along the roads, many forced into rebel service; from Chen Xilie and Zhang Jun on down, dozens went over to Luoyang, while Jinqing hid in the mountains and made his way south to Jin Prefecture. When Suzong reached Fengxiang he issued a personal summons for Jinqing to join the court; that same day Jinqing was appointed left chancellor, and all major military and civil affairs were referred to him. After the two capitals were recovered he was enfeoffed as Duke of Han with a substantive fief of five hundred households and appointed palace attendant. As the rebels were gradually suppressed he repeatedly asked to retire; the emperor graciously consented, relieved him of administrative duties, and appointed him grand tutor to the heir apparent. The following year the emperor, missing his veteran minister, recalled him as palace attendant.
4
晉卿寬厚廉謹，為政舉大綱，不問小過，所到有惠化。 魏人思之，為立碑頌德。 及秉鈞衡，小心畏慎，未嘗忤人意。 性聰敏，達練事體，百司文簿，經目必曉，而修身守位，以智自全，議者比漢之胡廣。
Jinqing was generous, upright, and prudent in office; he governed by broad principles rather than petty infractions, and wherever he served he left a legacy of benevolent rule. The people of Wei cherished his memory and erected a stele in praise of his virtue. Once he held the highest offices he was cautious and deferential, never giving offense. Clever and experienced, he grasped the workings of government at a glance; yet he cultivated himself and guarded his position with shrewd self-preservation, and critics compared him to Hu Guang of the Han.
5
玄宗崩，肅宗詔晉卿攝冢宰。 上表固辭曰：「臣聞古者殷高宗在諒闇之中，百官聽於冢宰，更無事跡，但存文字。 且一時之事，禮不相沿。 今殘寇猶虞，日殷萬務，皆緣兵馬屯守討襲，善算良謀，立勝擒敵。 陛下若行古之道，居喪不言，蒼生何依，百事皆廢。 伏讀國家起居註，亦於禮部檢見舊敕，恭惟太宗、高宗、、大行皇帝在位之日，皆有國哀，視事不輟，以為君臨天下，難徇常情。 今遺詔有處分，皇帝宜三日而聽政。 陛下遵太宗故事，則無冢宰； 遵大行皇帝遺詔，便合聽朝。 萬姓颙颙，不勝大願。 伏惟陛下知理國之重，順人心之切，以義斷恩，從宜無改。 今朝臣一命已上，皆言臣心昏貌朽，加以疾病，事有急速，斷在須臾，凡聖不同，豈合受詔。 陛下發哀已五日矣，願準遺詔聽政，則四夷萬國，無任悲幸。 」肅宗時疾彌留，覽表殞絕，乃許。
When Xuanzong died, Suzong ordered Jinqing to serve as regent. He submitted a memorial firmly declining: "I have read that in antiquity, when King Gaozong of Yin mourned in seclusion, the hundred officials took orders from the regent—but beyond that record we have only the text, no further details." Moreover, that was a unique circumstance; ritual precedent does not carry forward unchanged." Today remnant rebels still threaten us, and a thousand urgent matters press daily upon the throne—all requiring troops in the field, sound strategy, and swift victory over the enemy. If Your Majesty follows the ancient way and observes mourning in silence, to whom will the people turn? Every affair of state will founder. I have consulted the court diaries and old edicts preserved in the Ministry of Rites: Taizong, Gaozong, and the late emperor all continued to govern during periods of national mourning, holding that one who rules the realm cannot indulge ordinary grief. The late emperor's testament expressly provides that the new emperor should assume governance after three days. If Your Majesty follows Taizong's precedent, no regent is needed; if Your Majesty follows the late emperor's testament, you should take the throne at once. The people look to you with eager hope. I beg Your Majesty to recognize the weight of governing the realm, heed the people's urgent need, set duty above private grief, and do what circumstances require. The ministers of the first rank and above all say my mind is clouded, my body worn out, and my illness severe; urgent affairs cannot wait on a moment's delay. How can an ordinary man accept a mandate meant for a sage? Your Majesty has mourned for five days already; I pray you will follow the testament and assume governance—the four quarters and all nations will rejoice amid their sorrow." Suzong was then gravely ill; reading the memorial he was overcome with grief, then consented.
6
數日，肅宗晏駕，代宗踐祚，又詔晉卿攝冢宰。 晉卿上表懇辭曰：「臣以昔者天子居喪之時，百官聽於冢宰者，蓋君幼小，禦極事殷，情理當然。 沿革不一，今古異同，而周武、漢文，合於通變，垂範作則，可舉而行。 又士或墨缞，時遇金革，豈非銜恤，謂義在斷恩。 且百善之至，無加於孝也，其有容瘁心絕，指景悼生，此匹夫守節之常情，殊王者嗣續之大計。 昨二十日，陛下於大行皇帝柩前即位，是承先帝遺顧之言，亦前代不易之典。 則知所略不為害，所存是適權，防威滅端，所利者大。 陛下因心純至，天地明察。 伏以報劬勞之恩，申罔極之思，終身之痛，豈計朝夕！ 但以一日之內，萬務在中，須達宸聰，始成國政。 百僚萬姓及僧道耆壽等，相顧聚言，以臣老且無能，愚豈測聖，況久無居攝，臣不敢奉詔。 特乞陛下遵遺命，三日而政。 臣博聽眾情，不勝懇願，伏望割痛抑哀，則天下悲幸。 」上號泣從之。 時晉卿年已衰暮，又患兩足，上特許肩輿至中書，入閣不趨，累日一視事。 歷三朝，皆以謹密見稱。
Within days Suzong died and Daizong ascended the throne; he too ordered Jinqing to serve as regent. Jinqing submitted another earnest refusal: "In former times when the Son of Heaven mourned and officials took orders from a regent, it was because the ruler was a child and affairs were pressing—such an arrangement was only natural." Customs differ across ages, yet King Wu of Zhou and Emperor Wen of Han adapted wisely to circumstance and left models we may follow. Moreover, scholars sometimes wear mourning garb even amid war—not because they feel no grief, but because duty requires setting private sorrow aside. Filial piety is the highest of virtues, yet wasting away in grief and mourning one's own life is the conduct of a private man—not the great concern of a ruler who must secure the succession. On the twentieth day Your Majesty took the throne before the late emperor's bier—fulfilling his final charge and following an immemorial precedent. What is set aside does no harm; what is preserved serves the greater good—preventing disaster at its root yields the greatest benefit. Your Majesty's filial heart is pure and sincere—Heaven and Earth witness it. To repay a parent's toil and express boundless grief is a lifelong sorrow—not a matter of days alone! Yet within a single day ten thousand affairs demand the emperor's ear—only then can the state be governed. Officials, common people, monks, and elders alike agree that I am old and incompetent—how could a fool measure a sage? Regency has long been obsolete; I dare not accept this charge. I beg Your Majesty to follow the testament and assume governance after three days. Having heard the people's unanimous plea, I cannot but beg you to set grief aside for the nation's sake—the realm will mourn and rejoice together." The emperor wept and assented. By then Jinqing was elderly and afflicted in both legs; the emperor specially allowed him a sedan chair to the Secretariat, exemption from hurrying in court, and attendance at office only every few days. Through three reigns he was praised for his discretion and prudence.
7
廣德初，吐蕃寇長安。 晉卿時病臥於私第，蕃聞之，輿入逼脅，晉卿閉口不言，賊不敢害。 及上自陜至，冊為太保，罷知政事，又詔以太保致仕。 永泰元年四月薨。 輟朝三日，令京兆少尹一員護喪事，緣葬諸物並官給，賻絹布五百段、米粟五百石。 太常議謚曰「懿獻」。 初，晉卿東都留守，引用大理評事元載為推官。 至是載為中書侍郎、平章事，懷舊恩，諷有司改謚曰文貞。 大歷七年，令配享肅宗廟庭。
At the start of the Guangde era (763), the Tibetans invaded Chang'an. Jinqing lay ill at home; the Tibetans heard of him and came in a litter to coerce him, but he remained silent and they did not dare harm him. When the emperor returned from Shaan, Jinqing was appointed grand mentor, relieved of administrative duties, and granted retirement in that rank. He died in the fourth month of Yongtai 1 (765). Court mourning was observed for three days; a junior intendant of the capital was assigned to oversee the funeral; all burial expenses were provided by the state, with five hundred bolts of silk and five hundred piculs of grain as condolence gifts. The Court of Imperial Sacrifices proposed the posthumous name Yi Xian ("Benevolent and Offering"). Earlier, when Jinqing served as intendant of the Eastern Capital, he appointed Yuan Zai of the Court of Judicial Review as his investigating officer. By then Yuan Zai was vice minister of the Secretariat and chief councilor; remembering old favors, he persuaded the authorities to change the posthumous name to Wen Zhen ("Cultured and Upright"). In Dali 7 (772) he was granted posthumous worship in Suzong's ancestral temple.

=裴冕=裴冕，河東人也，為河東冠族。 天寶初，以門廕再遷渭南縣尉，以吏道聞。 御史中丞王鉷充京畿采訪使，表為判官。 遷監察御史，歷殿中侍御史。 冕雖無學術，守職通明，果於臨事，鉷甚委之。 及鉷得罪伏法，時宰臣李林甫方竊權柄，人鹹懼之，鉷賓佐數百，不敢窺鉷門。 冕獨收鉷屍，親自護喪，瘞於近郊，冕自是知名。 河西節度使哥舒翰表為行軍司馬，累遷員外郎中。
— Pei Mian — Pei Mian was a native of Hedong and belonged to one of the region's leading families. Early in the Tianbao era he entered service by hereditary privilege and rose to sheriff of Weinan, gaining a reputation for administrative competence. When Censor-in-Chief Wang Hong served as metropolitan investigation commissioner, he recommended Mian as his administrative aide. He was promoted to investigating censor and then served as palace attending censor. Though lacking scholarly training, Mian was clear-minded in office and decisive in action; Wang Hong relied on him heavily. When Wang Hong was executed, Li Linfu held power and everyone feared him; none of Hong's hundreds of retainers dared approach his house. Mian alone recovered Hong's body, personally conducted the funeral, and buried him in the suburbs—thereby winning widespread renown. Geshu Han, military commissioner of Hexi, recommended him as campaigning marshal, and he rose to vice director.
9
玄宗幸蜀，至益昌郡，遙詔太子充天下兵馬元帥，以冕為御史中丞兼左庶子，為之副。 是時，冕為河西行軍司馬，授御史中丞，詔赴朝廷。 遇太子於平涼，具陳事勢，勸之朔方，亟入靈武。 冕與杜鴻漸、崔漪等勸進曰：「主上厭勤大位，南幸蜀川，宗社神器，須有所歸，天意人事，不可固違。 若逡巡退讓，失億兆心，則大事去矣！ 臣等猶知之，況賢智乎！ 」太子曰：「南平寇逆，奉迎鑾輿，退居儲貳，侍膳左右，豈不樂哉！ 公等何言之過也？ 」冕與杜鴻漸又進曰：「殿下藉累聖之資，有天下之表。 元貞萬國，二十余年，殷憂啟聖，正在今日。 所從殿下六軍將士，皆關輔百姓，日夜思歸。 大軍一散，不可復集，不如因而撫之以從眾，臣等敢以死請。 」凡勸進五上，乃依。 肅宗即位，以定策功，遷中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事，倚以為政。
When Xuanzong fled to Shu and reached Yichang, he issued a distant edict appointing the crown prince commander of all armies under heaven, with Mian as censor-in-chief and left subordinate to the heir as his deputy. At the time Mian was campaigning marshal in Hexi; he received appointment as censor-in-chief and was ordered to the court. He met the crown prince at Pingliang, laid out the full situation, and urged him to proceed to Shuofang and enter Lingwu without delay. Mian, together with Du Hongjian, Cui Yi, and others, urged the prince to ascend the throne: "The emperor has grown weary of rule and fled south to Shu; the altars of state and the imperial regalia must find a home—Heaven's will and human necessity cannot be defied." If you hesitate and step aside, losing the hearts of the people, the cause will be lost! Even we ministers understand this—how much more so a man of your wisdom! The crown prince replied: "When the south is pacified and rebels crushed, when we escort the emperor home and I resume my place as heir, attending at his side—is that not happiness enough?" Why do you speak so extravagantly? Mian and Du Hongjian pressed further: "Your Highness inherits the accumulated virtue of past emperors and bears the bearing of one meant to rule the realm. The dynasty has stood firm for more than twenty years; it is today's deep crisis that summons forth a true ruler. The officers and soldiers who follow you are common folk from the Guanzhong heartland, longing night and day to return home. Once this army disperses, it cannot be reassembled; better to seize the moment, reassure the troops, and follow the people's will—we beg this even at the price of our lives. After five rounds of urging, he finally assented. When Emperor Suzong took the throne, Mian was promoted to vice minister of the secretariat and associate chancellor for his role in the succession and was entrusted with governing.

冕性忠勤，悉心奉公，稍得人心。 然不識大體，以聚人曰財，乃下令賣官鬻爵，度尼僧道士，以儲積為務。 人不願者，科令就之，其價益賤，事轉為弊。 肅宗移幸鳳翔，罷冕知政事，遷右僕射。 兩京平，以功封冀國公，食實封五百戶。 尋加御史大夫、成都尹，充劍南西川節度使。 又入為右僕射。 永泰元年，與裴遵慶等並集賢待制。 代宗求舊，拜冕兼御史大夫，充護山陵使。 冕以幸臣李輔國權盛，將附之，乃表輔國親昵術士中書舍人劉烜充山陵使判官。 烜坐法，冕坐貶施州刺史。 數月，移澧州刺史，復征為左僕射。 元載秉政。 載為新平縣尉，王鉷辟在巡內，冕常引之，載頗德冕。 會宰臣杜鴻漸卒，載遂舉冕代之。 冕時已衰瘵，載以其順己，引為同列。 受命之際，蹈舞絕倒，載趨而扶起，代為謝詞。 冕兼掌兵權留守之任，俸錢每月二千余貫。 性本侈靡，好尚車服及營珍饌，名馬在櫪，直數百金者常十數。 每會賓友，滋味品數，坐客有昧於名者。 自創巾子，其狀新奇，市肆因而效之，呼為「僕射樣」。 初代鴻漸，小吏以俸錢文簿白之，冕顧子弟，喜見於色，其嗜利若此。 拜職未盈月，卒，大歷四年十二月也。 上悼之，輟朝三日，贈太尉，賻制五百匹、粟五百石。
Mian was loyal and diligent by nature, devoted himself wholeheartedly to public service, and gradually won people's trust. Yet he failed to see the bigger picture; believing that amassing people was wealth, he ordered the sale of offices and titles, ordained nuns, monks, and Daoist priests, and made hoarding his main priority. Those who refused were forced by regulation to comply anyway; prices kept falling, and the practice grew ever more corrupt. When Suzong relocated to Fengxiang, Mian was removed from the chancellorship and made right vice director. After the two capitals were recovered, he was enfeoffed as Duke of Ji with five hundred taxable households for his service. He was soon made grand censor and intendant of Chengdu, serving as military commissioner of Jiannan West. He later returned to court as right vice director. In Yongtai 1 (765), he and Pei Zunqing and others were appointed attendant drafting officials at the Hall of Assembled Worthies. Seeking out old associates, Emperor Daizong appointed Mian concurrently grand censor and commissioner for guarding the imperial tombs. As the favored minister Li Fuguo's power rose, Mian sought to align with him and recommended Liu Xuan, a drafting secretary and occultist close to Fuguo, as adjudicator on the tomb commission. Liu Xuan was punished for breaking the law, and Mian was demoted to prefect of Shi. Several months later he was transferred to prefect of Li, then recalled as left vice director. Yuan Zai held power. When Yuan Zai had been magistrate of Xinping County, Wang Hong recruited him into his circuit staff; Mian had often recommended him, and Zai felt deep gratitude toward Mian. When chief minister Du Hongjian died, Yuan Zai recommended Mian to succeed him. Mian was by then frail and ill; Yuan Zai, finding him compliant, brought him onto the council as a colleague. When he received the appointment, he danced for joy and collapsed; Yuan Zai rushed to help him up and delivered the thanks on his behalf. Mian also held military authority and the duties of capital intendant, with a monthly salary of more than two thousand strings of cash. By nature he was extravagant, fond of fine carriages and dress and of preparing rare delicacies; he kept more than ten famed horses in his stables, each worth several hundred gold pieces. Whenever he entertained guests, the dishes were so numerous and exotic that some seated guests could not even name what they were eating. He designed his own style of headcloth; its shape was so novel that shops copied it, calling it the "Vice Director style." When he first replaced Du Hongjian, a clerk presented him with the salary ledger; Mian glanced at his sons and nephews with visible delight—such was his greed. Less than a month after taking office he died, in the twelfth month of Dali 4 (769). The emperor mourned him, suspended court for three days, posthumously appointed him grand preceptor, and granted five hundred bolts of silk and five hundred piculs of grain as condolence gifts.

=裴遵慶=裴遵慶，絳州聞喜人也。 代襲冠冕，為河東著族。 遵慶志氣深厚，機鑒敏達，自幼強學，博涉載籍，謹身晦跡，不幹當世之務。 以門廕累授潞府司法參軍，時年已老，未為人所知。 隨調吏部，授大理寺丞，剖斷刑獄，舉正綱條，理行始著。 遷司門員外、吏部員外郎，專判南曹。 天寶中，海內無事，九流輻輳會府，每歲吏部選人，動盈萬數。 遵慶敏識強記，精核文簿，詳而不滯，時稱吏事第一，由是大知名。
— Pei Zunqing — Pei Zunqing was a native of Wenxi in Jiang Prefecture. For generations the family had held rank and distinction, and they were a prominent clan of Hedong. Zunqing had a deep and steadfast character and keen, penetrating judgment; from youth he studied hard, read widely in the classics, kept himself disciplined and out of sight, and did not meddle in the affairs of the day. Through hereditary privilege he rose cumulatively to legal administrator in Lu Prefecture; by then he was already elderly and little known. On routine reassignment to the Ministry of Personnel, he was appointed assistant director of the Court of Judicial Review; in adjudicating criminal cases and upholding the law, his administrative ability first became evident. He was promoted to vice director of the Gate Office and vice director in the Ministry of Personnel, with sole charge of the Southern Bureau. During the Tianbao era the realm was at peace; men of every calling thronged the capital, and each year the Ministry of Personnel's candidates regularly numbered more than ten thousand. Zunqing had a sharp mind and prodigious memory; he scrutinized documents with precision, thorough yet never bogged down, and was acclaimed the finest administrator of his day—by which he gained great renown.
12
天寶末，楊國忠當國，出不附己者例為外官，遵慶亦出為郡守。 肅宗即位，征拜給事中、尚書右丞、吏部侍郎。 恭儉克己，遲重謹密，頗有時望。 上元中，蕭華輔政，素知遵慶，每奏見，累稱之，遷黃門侍郎、同中書門下平章事。 廣德初，仆固懷恩阻兵汾上，指中官為詞，上以遵慶忠純，特遣往汾州宣慰懷恩。 遵慶既見懷恩，具陳朝旨，懷恩引過聽命，將隨遵慶朝謁，為副將範誌誠以邪說惑之，懷恩遂以懼死為詞。 會蕃寇陷京師，乘輿幸陜，遵慶自汾州奔赴行在。 及乘輿還京，以遵慶為太子少傅。 永泰元年，與裴冕等並於集賢院待制，罷知政事。 尋改吏部尚書、右僕射，復知選事。 時選人天興縣尉陳琯於銓庭言詞不遜，淩突無禮，代宗詔付遵慶於省門鞭三十，貶為吉州員外司戶參軍。 遵慶敦守儒行，老而彌謹。 嘗為風狂族侄撾登聞鼓告以不順，上知遵度，不省，其見信如此。 大歷十年十月薨於位，年九十余。
Near the end of Tianbao, Yang Guozhong dominated the government and routinely posted those who did not support him to provincial offices; Zunqing was likewise sent out as a prefect. When Emperor Suzong took the throne, Zunqing was summoned and appointed drafting counselor, vice director of the Department of State Affairs, and vice minister of personnel. Respectful and frugal, self-restrained, deliberate and discreet, he enjoyed considerable standing in his day. During the Shangyuan era, Xiao Hua served as chief minister and, having long known Zunqing, praised him repeatedly at imperial audiences; Zunqing was promoted to vice minister of the secretariat and associate chancellor. At the start of Guangde, Pugu Huai'en held his army at Fen and used the eunuchs as a pretext for rebellion; the emperor, trusting Zunqing's loyalty and integrity, specially dispatched him to Fen Prefecture to announce consolation to Huai'en. When Zunqing met Huai'en, he fully conveyed the court's intent; Huai'en admitted his fault and agreed to obey, and was about to follow Zunqing to court, but his deputy Fan Zhicheng swayed him with perverse counsel, and Huai'en then pleaded that he feared for his life. When Tibetan raiders took the capital and the emperor fled to Shan, Zunqing rushed from Fen Prefecture to join the mobile court. When the emperor returned to the capital, Zunqing was appointed junior mentor to the heir apparent. In Yongtai 1 (765), he and Pei Mian and others were appointed attendant drafting officials at the Hall of Assembled Worthies and removed from the chancellorship. He was soon made minister of personnel and right vice director, again in charge of official selection. At the time a candidate, Chen Guan, magistrate of Tianxing County, spoke insolently at the selection courtyard and behaved with gross disrespect; Emperor Daizong ordered Zunqing to have him flogged thirty times at the ministry gate and demoted him to outer assistant registrar for revenue in Ji Prefecture. Zunqing steadfastly upheld Confucian conduct and grew ever more careful in old age. Once a deranged clansman beat the drum at the Hall for Hearing Grievances accusing Zunqing of disloyalty; the emperor, knowing Zunqing well, did not even look into it—such was the trust placed in him. In the tenth month of Dali 10 (775) he died in office at over ninety years of age.
13
遵慶初登省郎，嘗著《王政記》，述今古禮體，識者覽之，知有公輔之量。 遵慶子向子向，字傃仁，少以門廕歷官至太子司議郎。 建中初，李紓為同州刺史，奏向為從事。 硃泚反，李懷光又叛河中，使其將趙貴先築壘於同州，紓來奔奉天，向領州務。 貴先因脅縣尉林寶役徒板築，不及期，將斬之，吏人百姓奔竄。 向即詣貴先軍壘，以逆順之理責之，貴先感悟，遂來降，故同州不陷。 向由是知名。 累為京兆府戶曹，轉櫟陽、渭南縣令，奏課皆第一，朝廷亟聞其理行，擢為戶部員外郎。
When Zunqing first rose to a provincial bureau secretaryship, he wrote the Record of Royal Governance, setting out ancient and modern ritual forms; readers who saw it knew he had the makings of a chief minister. Pei Xiang, styled Suren, was the son of Pei Zunqing; from youth through hereditary privilege he rose cumulatively to secretariat drafter of the heir apparent. At the start of the Jianzhong era, Li Shu was prefect of Tong Prefecture and recommended Xiang as a staff officer. Zhu Ci rebelled, and Li Huai'guang also rebelled in Hezhong, sending his general Zhao Guixian to build fortifications at Tong Prefecture; Li Shu fled to Fengtian, and Xiang took charge of the prefecture. Guixian then forced the county magistrate Lin Bao to impress laborers for wall-building; when the work fell behind schedule he was about to execute Lin, and officials and commoners fled in panic. Xiang went at once to Guixian's camp and rebuked him on the grounds of loyalty and rebellion; Guixian was moved and surrendered, and so Tong Prefecture was spared. From this Xiang gained renown. He served cumulatively as revenue section chief in the capital metropolitan prefecture, then as magistrate of Liyang and Weinan; his performance reports were always ranked first, the court soon heard of his governance, and he was promoted to vice director in the Ministry of Revenue.

德宗季年，天下方鎮副倅多自選於朝，防一日有變，遂就而授之節制。 向已選為太原少尹，德宗召見喻旨，尋用為行軍司馬、兼御史中丞，改汾州刺史，轉鄭州。 又復為太原少尹，兼河東節度副使。 改晉州刺史，充本州防禦使，遷虢州刺史。 入為京兆少尹，拜同州刺史，充本州防禦使。 入為大理寺卿，出遷陜虢都防禦、觀察使。 三歲，拜左散騎常侍，自常侍復為大理。
In the late years of Emperor Dezong's reign, many deputies of military commissioners were selected at court so that, should crisis arise, they could immediately be granted full command. Xiang had already been selected as junior intendant of Taiyuan; Dezong summoned him, explained his intent, and soon appointed him campaigning marshal and concurrently censor-in-chief, then prefect of Fen, and later transferred him to Zheng Prefecture. He again served as junior intendant of Taiyuan, concurrently deputy military commissioner of Hedong. He was made prefect of Jin and defense commissioner of that prefecture, then promoted to prefect of Guo. He entered court as junior intendant of the capital metropolitan prefecture, was appointed prefect of Tong, and served as defense commissioner of that prefecture. He entered court as director of the Court of Judicial Review, then went out as defense and observation commissioner of the Shan-Guo circuit. After three years he was appointed left regular attendant; from that post he again became director of the Court of Judicial Review.

Xiang was the son of a famous chief minister; through learning and conduct he disciplined himself and carefully preserved his family's reputation. In successive offices he extended benevolence and wisdom, bringing benefit to the people. At this point, having reached advanced age, he retired; the court treated him with exceptional honor and granted him retirement as minister of personnel at his estate in Xinchang Lane. He had more than a hundred dependents within and outside the clan; whatever salary Xiang received he always shared with them, and when he took provincial appointments he brought them along. Those who were orphaned, alone, sick, or destitute and could not care for themselves he especially supported; to this day he is praised for his filial devotion and familial kindness. He died in the ninth month of Taihe 4 (830) at the age of eighty. He was posthumously appointed junior mentor to the heir apparent. Xiang's son Pei Yin passed the jinshi examination, rose cumulatively to grand censor, and died in that office. Yin's son Pei Shu, styled Jisheng, passed the jinshi examination in Xiantong 12 (871). When chief minister Du Shenquan went out to command Hezhong, he recruited Shu as a staff officer; Shu obtained the post of collator in the Secretariat and was soon promoted to magistrate of Lantian. He served on duty at the Hongwen Hall. Grand Academician Wang Duo thought highly of him; when Duo lost the chancellorship and fell from power, Shu also went long without a new appointment. Following Emperor Xizong to Shu, Censor-in-Chief Li Huan recommended him as palace censor; he was later promoted to diarist. At the start of Zhonghe, Wang Duo was again employed; out of old favor Shu was transferred to chief clerk of Zheng-Hua and acting director of the Bureau of Enfeoffments, granted gold and purple insignia; returning to court he served successively as vice director in the ministries of war and personnel. At the start of Longji he was promoted to drafting counselor and made intendant of the capital metropolitan prefecture. Chief minister Kong Wei especially favored and promoted him. During Dazhun, Kong Wei was demoted for military failure; Shu was implicated and made right subordinate to the heir apparent, then soon sent out as prefect of She. At the start of Qianning he entered court as right regular attendant; following Emperor Zhaozong to Hua Prefecture, he served as envoy to announce and console at Bian Prefecture.
16
初，樞自歙州罷郡歸朝，路經大梁，時硃全忠兵威已振，樞以兄事之，全忠由是重之。 及樞傳詔，全忠皆稟朝旨，獻奉相繼，昭宗甚悅，遷兵部侍郎。 時崔胤專政，亦倚全忠，二人因是相結，改樞吏部侍郎。 未幾，換戶部侍郎、同平章事。 其年冬，昭宗幸華州，崔胤貶官，樞亦為工部尚書。 天子自岐下還宮，以樞檢校右僕射、同平章事，出為廣南節度使。 制出，硃全忠保薦之，言樞有經世才，不可棄之嶺表，尋復拜門下侍郎，監修國史，累兼吏部尚書，判度支。 崔胤誅，以全忠素厚，相位如故。 從昭宗遷洛陽，駐蹕陜州，進右僕射、弘文館大學士、太清宮使，充諸道鹽鐵轉運使。
Earlier, when Shu left his post at She and returned to court, he passed through Daliang; Zhu Quanzhong's military prestige was already on the rise, and Shu treated him as an elder brother—whereupon Quanzhong came to hold him in high regard. When Shu transmitted imperial edicts, Quanzhong followed the court's intent and sent tribute in succession; Emperor Zhaozong was greatly pleased and promoted Shu to vice minister of war. At the time Cui Yin dominated the government and also relied on Quanzhong; the two allied through this connection, and Shu was made vice minister of personnel. Before long he was made vice minister of revenue and associate chancellor. That winter Emperor Zhaozong fled to Hua Prefecture; Cui Yin was demoted and Shu was made minister of works. When the emperor returned to the palace from Qi, Shu was made acting right vice director and associate chancellor and sent out as military commissioner of Guangnan. When the appointment edict was issued, Zhu Quanzhong vouched for him, saying Shu had the talent to govern the age and should not be abandoned in the far south; he was soon reappointed vice minister of the chancellery, commissioner for revising the national history, cumulatively minister of personnel, and controller of the treasury and expenditure. When Cui Yin was executed, Shu retained his chancellorship because of his long-standing closeness to Quanzhong. Following Emperor Zhaozong's move to Luoyang, when the court halted at Shan Prefecture, Shu was promoted to right vice director, grand academician of the Hongwen Hall, commissioner of the Great Pure Palace, and transport commissioner for salt and iron of all circuits.

哀帝初嗣位，柳璨用事，全忠嘗奏用牙將張廷範為太常卿，諸相議，樞曰：「廷範勛臣，幸有方鎮節鉞之命，何藉樂卿？ 恐非元帥梁王之旨。 」乃持之不下。 俄而全忠聞樞言，謂賓佐曰：「吾常以裴十四器識真純，不入浮薄之伍，觀此議論，本態露矣。 」切齒含怒。 柳璨聞全忠言，尋希旨罷樞相位，和陵祔享，拜尚書左僕射。 五月，責授朝散大夫、登州刺史，尋再貶瀧州司戶。 六月十一日，行及滑州，全忠遣人殺之於白馬驛，投屍於河，時年六十五。
When Emperor Aidi first succeeded to the throne, Liu Can wielded power; Quanzhong once memorialized to appoint his guard officer Zhang Tingfan as director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices; the chancellors deliberated, and Shu said: "Tingfan is a meritorious minister; he may well receive a military commissioner's seal and axe—what need is there for the director of imperial sacrifices? I fear this is not the intent of Marshal, the Prince of Liang." He therefore withheld the appointment and refused to issue the decree. Before long Quanzhong heard what Shu had said and told his staff, "I always thought Pei the Fourteenth was genuinely upright and above the company of shallow men—but this remark shows his true colors." He gnashed his teeth in fury. Liu Can heard Quanzhong's words and soon obliged him by removing Shu from the chancellorship; at the Heling mausoleum enshrinement ceremony Shu was appointed left vice director of the Secretariat. In the fifth month he was demoted to grand master of splendid happiness and prefect of Deng; soon he was demoted again to registrar of Long Prefecture. On the eleventh day of the sixth month, as he reached Hua Prefecture, Quanzhong sent men to kill him at Baima Post Station and throw his body into the river; he was sixty-five years old.

=【贊】=史臣曰：晉卿謹身蒞事，足為純臣，避寇全忠，固彰大節。 然博達精審，豈不知寬猛之道哉！ 奉林甫之旨，順胥吏之意，悅附張倚，欺罔時君。 生為重臣，諂林甫之勢也； 歿改美謚，引元載之恩焉。 或言晉卿不為巧宦者，誠不信也。 冕力贊中興，名居大位，奉公抱義，可以致身； 賣官度僧，是何為政？ 及其老也，貪冒尤深。 遵慶學術貞明，為國忠所出； 恭儉謹密，遇蕭華素知。 位重行純，老而彌篤，彼二公固有慚德。 向克荷堂構，不墜門風。 樞因盜而振，盜憎而亡，宜哉！ 君子守道遠刑，蓋慮此也。
Appraisal: The historian writes: Jinqing conducted himself with care and devotion in office—worthy of a loyal minister; in fleeing the rebels he preserved his integrity and displayed true greatness of character. Yet he was learned and perceptive—how could he not have known the balance of leniency and severity! He followed Li Linfu's wishes, indulged the clerks, curried favor with Zhang Yi, and deceived the emperor. In life he was a great minister who flattered Li Linfu's power; after death his posthumous name was improved through Yuan Zai's favor. Those who claim Jinqing was no crafty courtier simply cannot be believed. Mian vigorously aided the restoration and rose to high office; in serving the state with integrity he might have distinguished himself— yet he sold offices and licensed monks—what kind of governance was that? In his old age his greed grew worse still. Zunqing was learned and upright, yet Yang Guozhong banished him from court; he was respectful, frugal, and discreet, and won recognition from Xiao Hua, who had long known his worth. He held high rank with pure conduct and grew more steadfast with age; beside him the other two men had reason to feel ashamed. Xiang upheld the family legacy and did not let its reputation decline. Shu rose through the rebel's favor and died when the rebel turned against him—as was fitting! The gentleman holds to the Way and keeps punishment at a distance—perhaps he has just such cases in mind.

贊曰：奧矣晉卿，貪哉裴冕。 遵慶父子，及之者鮮。
In sum: Jinqing was profound; Pei Mian was greedy. Few could match Zunqing and his sons.
