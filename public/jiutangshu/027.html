1
封禪之禮，自漢光武之後，曠世不修。 隋開皇十四年，晉王廣率百官抗表，固請封禪。 文帝令牛弘、辛彥之、許善心等創定儀注。 至十五年，行幸兗州，遂於太山之下，為壇設祭，如南郊之禮，竟不升山而還。
After Han Guangwu, the feng and shan rites went unrepaired for generations. In Sui Kaihuang 14, Prince of Jin Yang Guang led officials in a memorial firmly requesting feng and shan. Wendi ordered Niu Hong, Xin Yanzhi, Xu Shansin, and others to draft new ritual regulations. By year 15, touring Yanzhou, he set an altar below Mount Tai and sacrificed as at the southern suburb—but never ascended and returned.
2
貞觀六年，平突厥，年穀屢登，群臣上言請封泰山。 太宗曰：「議者以封禪為大典。 如朕本心，但使天下太平，家給人足，雖闕封禪之禮，亦可比德堯、舜； 若百姓不足，夷狄內侵，縱修封禪之儀，亦何異於桀、紂？ 昔秦始皇自謂德洽天心，自稱皇帝，登封岱宗，奢侈自矜。 漢文帝竟不登封，而躬行儉約，刑措不用。 今皆稱始皇為暴虐之主，漢文為有德之君。 以此而言，無假封禪。 禮雲，'至敬不壇'，掃地而祭，足表至誠，何必遠登高山，封數尺之土也！」 侍中王珪對曰：「陛下發德音，明封禪本末，非愚臣之所及。」 秘書監魏征曰：「隨末大亂，黎民遇陛下，始有生望。 養之則至仁，勞之則未可。 升中之禮，須備千乘萬騎，供帳之費，動役數州。 戶口蕭條，何以能給？」 太宗深嘉征言，而中外章表不已。 上問禮官兩漢封山儀注，因遣中書侍郎杜正倫行太山上七十二帝壇跡。 是年兩河水潦，其事乃寢。 至十一年，群臣復勸封山，始議其禮。 於是國子博士劉伯莊、睦州刺史徐令言等，各上封祀之事，互設疑議，所見不同。 多言新禮中封禪儀注，簡略未周。 太宗敕秘書少監顏思古、諫議大夫硃子奢等，與四方名儒博物之士參議得失。 議者數十家，遞相駁難，紛紜久不決。 於是左僕射房玄齡、特進魏征、中書令楊師道，博采眾議堪行用而與舊禮不同者奏之。
In Zhenguan 6, with the Turks pacified and harvests full, ministers asked to feng Mount Tai. Taizong said, "Debaters call feng and shan a grand ceremony. In my heart, if the realm is peaceful and households prosper, even without feng and shan one may match Yao and Shun; if the people lack and barbarians invade, feng and shan ceremonies would differ little from Jie and Zhou. Qin Shihuang deemed his virtue Heaven's will, called himself emperor, fenged at Dai, and luxuriated in pride. Han Wendi never fenged, yet practiced frugality and left punishments unused. Today the First Emperor is called tyrannical and Wendi virtuous. By this measure, feng and shan are unnecessary. The Rites say, 'supreme reverence needs no altar'—sweeping ground to sacrifice shows utmost sincerity; why climb mountains to seal a few feet of earth?" Attendant Wang Gui replied, "Your Majesty's virtuous words clarify feng and shan's roots and branches—beyond this fool's reach." Secretariat Director Wei Zheng said, "After late-Sui chaos, the people met you and first hoped to live. Nourishing them is utmost benevolence; laboring them is not yet fit. The ascent-and-libation rite needs thousands of chariots and myriad riders; supply costs would mobilize several provinces. Households are desolate—how could it be supplied?" Taizong deeply praised Zheng, yet memorials inside and out did not cease. He asked ritual officials about Han feng ceremonies and sent Du Zhenlun to trace the seventy-two emperors' altars on Mount Tai. That year both rivers flooded, and the matter rested. By year 11 ministers again urged fenging, and rite discussions began. Erudite Liu Bozhuang, Prefect Xu Lingyan, and others submitted feng proposals with conflicting doubts. Many said new-rite feng and shan regulations were brief and incomplete. Taizong ordered Yan Sigu, Zhu Zishe, and renowned scholars from all quarters to debate gains and losses. Dozens of schools refuted one another; confusion long went unresolved. Fang Xuanling, Wei Zheng, and Yang Shidao gathered workable proposals differing from old rites and submitted them.
3
其議昊天上帝壇曰：「將封先祭，義在告神，且備謁敬之儀，方展慶成之禮。 固當於壇下阯，預申齊潔。 贊饗已畢，然後登封。 既表重慎之深，兼示行事有漸。 今請祭于泰山下，設壇以祀上帝，以景皇帝配享。 壇長一十二丈，高一丈二尺。」 又議制玉牒曰：「金玉重寶，質性貞堅，宗祀郊禋，皆充器幣，豈嫌華美，實貴精確。 況乎三神壯觀，萬代鴻名，禮極殷崇，事資藻縟。 玉牒玉檢，式韞靈奇， 傳之無窮，永存不朽。 今請玉牒長一尺三寸，廣厚各五寸。 玉檢厚二寸，長短闊狹一如玉牒。 其印齒請隨璽大小，仍纏以金繩五周。」 又議玉策曰：「封禪之祭，嚴配作主，皆奠玉策，肅奉虔誠。 今玉策四枚，各長一尺三寸，廣一寸五分，厚五分。 每策五簡，俱以金編。 其一奠上帝，一奠太祖座，一奠皇地祇，一奠高祖座。」 又議金匱曰：「登配之策，盛以金匱，歸格藝祖之廟室。 今請長短令容玉策，高廣各六寸。 形制如今之表函。 纏以金繩，封以金泥，印以受命璽。」 又議方石再累曰：「舊藏玉牒，止用石函，亦猶盛書篋笥，所以或呼石篋。 今請方石三枚，以為再累。 其十枚石檢，刻方石四邊而立之。 纏以金繩，封以石泥，印以受命璽。」 又議泰山上圓壇曰：「四出開道，壇場通義，南面入升，於事為允。 今請介丘上圓壇廣五丈，高九尺，用五色土加之。 四面各設一階。 御位在壇南，升自南階，而就上封玉牒。」 又議圓壇上土封曰：「凡言封者，皆是積土之名。 利建分封，亦以班社立號。 謂之封禪，厥義可知。 今請於圓壇之上，安置方石，璽緘既畢，加土築以為封。 高一丈二尺，而廣二丈，以五色土益封，玉牒藏於其內。 祀禪之土，其封制亦同此。」 又議玉璽曰：「謹詳前載方石緘封，玉檢金泥，必資印璽，以為秘固。 今請依令用受命璽以封石檢。 其玉檢既與石檢大小不同，請更造璽一枚，方一寸二分，文同受命璽，以封玉牒。 石檢形制，依漢建武故事。」 又議立碑曰：「勒石紀號，顯揚功業，登封降禪，肆覲之壇，立碑紀之。」 又議設告至壇曰：「既至山下，禮行告至，柴於東方上帝，望秩遍禮群神。 今請其壇方八十一尺，高三尺，陛仍四出。 其禪方壇及餘儀式，請從今禮。 仍請柴祭、望秩，同時行事。」 又議廢石闕及大小距石曰：「距石之設，意取牢固，本資實用，豈雲雕飾。 今既積土厚封，足與天長地久。 其小距環壇，石闕回建，事非經誥，無益禮義，煩而非要，請從減省。」
On the August Heaven altar: "Before feng, sacrifice first—to announce to spirits and prepare audience rites before celebrating completion. Surely purification should precede at the altar foot. When assisting sacrifice ends, then ascend to feng. This shows deep caution and staged procedure. We request sacrifice below Mount Tai, worshipping August Heaven with Emperor Jing as associate. The altar is twelve zhang long and one zhang two chi high." On the jade tablet: "Gold and jade are heavy treasures, firm and true; in ancestral and suburban rites they serve as vessels—splendor is not disdained; precision is valued. For the three spirits' magnificent sight and a name for ten thousand generations, rites reach utmost honor and require full ornament. Jade tablet and jade clasp enfold spiritual wonder, transmitted without end, stored forever without decay. We request the jade tablet one chi three cun long, five cun wide and thick. The jade clasp two cun thick, dimensions matching the tablet. Seal teeth follow seal size; wrap with gold cord five circuits." On jade slips: "In feng and shan, solemn matching makes the chief offering; all set jade slips in reverent devotion. Now four jade slips, each one chi three cun long, one cun five fen wide, five fen thick. Each slip has five tablets, all gold-bound. One for August Heaven, one for the Great Ancestor's seat, one for Imperial Earth Sovereign, one for the High Ancestor's seat." On the gold casket: "Ascent-and-matching slips go in a gold casket returned to the Artistic Ancestor's temple. We request dimensions to hold the slips, six cun high and wide. Form like present memorial cases. Wrap with gold cord, seal with gold mud, impress with the Seal of Receiving the Mandate." On layered square stones: "Formerly the jade tablet was stored in a stone case, like a book case—sometimes called a stone case. We request three square stones as further layers. Ten stone clasps are carved on the square stone's four sides and set upright. Wrap with gold cord, seal with stone mud, impress with the Seal of Receiving the Mandate." On Tai's round altar: "Four roads open from the altar field; ascending from the south is fitting. We request Jie Hill's round altar five zhang wide, nine chi high, with five-colored earth. Each side has one flight of steps. The imperial position is south of the altar; ascend the southern steps to feng with the jade tablet." On earthen sealing atop the round altar: "Feng always means heaping earth. Establishing merit and dividing fiefs also distributes altars and titles. Calling it feng and shan—the meaning is clear. We request placing the square stone atop the round altar; when sealing is done, heap earth as feng. One zhang two chi high, two zhang wide; augment with five-colored earth; the jade tablet is stored within. For shan-sacrifice earth, the feng form is the same." On the jade seal: "Former records show square-stone sealing and gold mud require seals for security. We request the Seal of Receiving the Mandate to seal stone clasps. Since jade and stone clasps differ in size, we request another seal, one cun two fen square, same text as the Mandate seal, for the jade tablet. Stone clasp form follows Han Jianwu precedent." On erecting a stele: "Carve stone to record titles and display achievements at feng, descent, libation, and grand-audience altars." On the Announce-Arrival altar: "At the mountain foot, perform announcing arrival; offer firewood to the Eastern Lord on High and ranked audience to all spirits. We request the altar eighty-one chi square, three chi high, steps on four sides. For the shan square altar and remaining forms, follow present rites. Request firewood offering and ranked audience at the same time." On abolishing stone que and distance-stones: "Distance-stones mean solidity and practical use—not ornament. Thick heaped-earth sealing suffices to endure with heaven. Small distance-stones and encircling stone que are uncanonical, benefit ritual not at all, troublesome and inessential—reduce them."
4
太宗從其議，仍令附之於禮。
Taizong followed their proposal and ordered it attached to the rites.
5
十五年，下詔，將有事于泰山，復令公卿諸儒詳定儀注。 太常卿韋挺、禮部侍郎令狐德棻為封禪使，參考其議。 時論者又執異見，顏師古上書申明前議。 太宗覽其奏，多依師古所陳為定。 車駕至洛陽宮，會有彗星之變，乃下詔罷其事。
In year 15 an edict announced affairs at Mount Tai and ordered dukes, ministers, and Confucians to fix regulations. Court of Sacrifices Director Wei Ting and Rites Vice Minister Linghu Defen served as feng-shan commissioners. Debaters again differed; Yan Shigu submitted a memorial clarifying earlier proposals. Taizong reviewed it and largely fixed matters per Shigu. At Luoyang Palace a comet appeared; an edict halted the affair.
6
高宗即位，公卿數請封禪，則天既立為皇后，又密贊之。 麟德二年二月，車駕發京，東巡狩，詔禮官、博士撰定封禪儀注：
After Gaozong's accession, officials repeatedly requested feng and shan; Empress Wu secretly endorsed it. Qide 2, month 2, the carriage departed on an eastern tour; ritual officials and erudites were ordered to draft feng-shan regulations:
7
有司于乾封元年正月戊辰朔。 先是，有司齋戒。 于前祀七日平旦，太尉誓百官于行從中台，云：「來月一日封祀，二日登封泰山，三日禪社首，各揚其職，不供其事，國有常刑。」 上齋於行宮四日，致齋三日。 近侍之官應從升者，及從事群官、諸方客使，各本司公館清齋一宿。 前祀一日，諸衛令共屬：未後一刻，設黃麾半仗於外壝之外，與樂工人俱清齋一宿。
The relevant offices on Qianfeng 1, month 1, day 1. Earlier the relevant offices had abstained. Seven days before sacrifice, at dawn the Grand Marshal swore in officials at the traveling terrace: "Month 1 day 1: feng sacrifice; day 2: ascend and feng Tai; day 3: shan at Sheshou—each perform your duty or face statutory punishment." The emperor abstained four days at the traveling palace and three days in full abstention. Close attendants, attending officials, and envoys each abstained one night at their offices. One day before sacrifice, guard commandants ordered half the yellow awning guard outside the outer enclosure after wei quarter 1; musicians abstained one night.
8
有司于太岳南四里為圓壇，三成、十二階，如圓丘之制。 壇上飾以青，四面各依方色，並造燎壇及壝三重。 又造玉策三枚，皆以金繩連編玉簡為之。 每簡長一尺二寸，廣一寸二分，厚三分，刻玉填金為字。 又為玉匱一，以藏正座玉策，長一尺三寸。 並玉檢方五寸，當繩處刻為五道，當封璽處刻深二分，方一寸二分。 又為金匱二，以藏配座玉策，制度如玉匱。 又為黃金繩以纏金玉匱，各五周。 為金泥、玉匱、金匱。 為玉璽一枚，方一寸二分，文同受命璽，封玉匱、金匱。 又為石感，以藏玉匱。 用方石再累，各方五尺，厚一尺，刻方石中令容玉匱。 感旁施檢處，皆刻深三寸三分，闊一尺。 當繩處皆刻深三分，闊一寸五分。 為石檢十枚，以檢石感，皆長三尺，闊一尺，厚七寸。 皆刻為印齒三道，深四寸。 當封璽處方五寸，當通繩處闊一寸五分。 皆有小石蓋，制與檢刻處相應，以檢擫封泥。 其檢立於感旁，南方、北方各三，東方、西方各二，去感隅皆七寸。 又為金繩以纏石感，各五周，徑三分。 為石泥以泥石感，其泥，末石和方色土為之。 為距石十二枚，分距感隅，皆再累，各闊二尺，長一丈，斜刻其首，令與感隅相應。
Offices made a round altar four li south of Great Peak, three tiers and twelve steps, like the round mound. The altar top was green; each side followed its color; a firewood altar and triple enclosure were made. Three jade slips were made, jade tablets linked by gold cord. Each tablet one chi two cun long, one cun two fen wide, three fen thick, jade carved and gold-filled. One jade casket stored the chief-seat slip, one chi three cun long. Jade clasps five cun square; five cord channels carved; seal recess two fen deep, one cun two fen square. Two gold caskets stored matching-seat slips, like the jade casket. Gold cord wrapped jade and gold caskets, five circuits each. Gold mud, jade caskets, and gold caskets were made. One jade seal, one cun two fen square, Mandate-seal text, sealed jade and gold caskets. A stone receptacle stored the jade casket. Layered square stones, each five chi square, one chi thick, carved within to hold the jade casket. Clasp sites beside the receptacle were carved three cun three fen deep, one chi wide. Cord sites were carved three fen deep, one cun five fen wide. Ten stone clasps clasped the receptacle, three chi long, one chi wide, seven cun thick. All had three seal teeth carved four cun deep. Seal sites were five cun square; cord sites one cun five fen wide. All had small stone lids matching clasp carvings to press sealing mud. Clasps stood beside the receptacle—three south and north, two east and west, seven cun from corners. Gold cord wrapped the stone receptacle five circuits each, three fen diameter. Stone mud sealed the receptacle—powdered stone mixed with directional-colored earth. Twelve distance-stones at receptacle corners, layered, two chi wide, one zhang long, slanted heads matching corners.
9
泰山之上，設登封之壇，上徑五丈，高九尺，四出陛。 壇上飾以青，四面依方色。 一壝，隨地之宜。 其玉牒、玉匱、石感、石檢、距石，皆如封祀之制。 又為降禪壇於社首山上，方壇八隅，一成八陛，如方丘之制。 壇上飾以黃，四面依方色。 三壝，隨地之宜。 其玉策、玉匱、石感、石檢、距石等，亦同封祀之制。
Atop Mount Tai an ascent-feng altar was set, five zhang diameter, nine chi high, four flights of steps. The altar top was green; sides followed directional colors. One enclosure, according to terrain. Jade tablet, jade casket, stone receptacle, stone clasps, and distance-stones followed feng-sacrifice form. A descent-shan altar on Sheshou Hill—square with eight corners, one tier, eight steps, like the square mound. The altar top was yellow; sides followed directional colors. Three enclosures, according to terrain. Jade slips, jade casket, stone receptacle, stone clasps, and distance-stones matched feng-sacrifice form.
10
至其年十二月，車駕至山下。 及有司進奏儀注，封祀以高祖、太宗同配，禪社首乙太穆皇后、文德皇后同配，皆以公卿充亞獻、終獻之禮。 於是皇后抗表曰：
By month 12 the imperial carriage reached below the mountain. Offices submitted regulations: feng paired Gaozu and Taizong; Sheshou shan paired Empresses Tai Mu and Wende; dukes served as secondary and final offerers. Thereupon the empress submitted a memorial:
11
伏尋登封之禮，遠邁古先，而降禪之儀，竊為未允。 其祭地祇之日，以太后昭配，至於行事，皆以公卿。 以妾愚誠，恐未周備。 何者？ 乾坤定位，剛柔之義已殊； 經義載陳，中外之儀斯別。 瑤壇作配，既合于方祇； 玉豆薦芳，實歸於內職。 況推尊先後，親饗瓊筵，豈有外命宰臣，內參禋祭？ 詳於至理，有紊徽章。 但禮節之源，雖興於昔典； 而升降之制，尚缺於遙圖。 且往代封嶽，雖雲顯號，或因時俗，意在尋仙； 或以情覬名，事深為己。 豈如化被乎四表，推美于神宗； 道冠乎二儀，歸功於先德。 寧可仍遵舊軌，靡創彝章？
Ascending feng far surpasses antiquity; yet descent shan, I venture, is not approved. On Earth Sovereign's day Empress Zhao was matched; in the rite all were dukes and ministers. In my foolish sincerity, I fear it is incomplete. Why? Heaven and Earth are positioned; firm and yielding already differ; the classics state it clearly; inner and outer ceremonies differ. Jade altar as matching already fits square Earth Sovereign; jade vessels offering fragrance truly belong to inner duties. Honoring predecessors at the jade banquet—could outer orders to ministers and inner sacrifice coexist? Examined to principle, regulations are disordered. Though ritual's source arose in ancient canons, ascending and descending systems still lack in distant plans. Former fenging sometimes sought glorious titles, followed custom, sought immortality; some coveted reputation, the affair deeply for oneself. How unlike transforming the four quarters and extending beauty to the divine ancestor; the Way crowns the two principles and returns merit to former virtue. How follow the old track and not create constant regulations?
12
妾謬處椒闈，叨居蘭掖。 但以職惟中饋，道屬於蒸、嘗； 義切奉先，理光於蘋、藻。 罔極之思，載結於因心； 祗肅之懷，實深於明祀。 但妾早乖定省，已闕侍於晨昏； 今屬崇禋，豈敢安於帷帟。 是故馳情夕寢，睠嬴裏而翹魂； 疊慮宵興，仰深郊而聳念。 伏望展禮之日，總率六宮內外命婦，以親奉奠。 冀申如在之敬，式展虔拜之儀。 積此微誠，已淹氣序。 既屬鑾輿將警，奠璧非賒，輒效丹心，庶裨大禮。 冀聖朝垂則，永播於芳規； 螢燭末光，增輝於日月。
I wrongly occupy the pepper chambers and dwell in the orchid side. Yet my duty is inner provision; the Way belongs to steaming and tasting; serving ancestors cuts to duckweed and water-shield offerings. Boundless thought knots in the heart; reverent solemnity runs deep for bright sacrifice. Yet I early lost dawn-and-dusk attendance; now facing great sacrifice, how dare I rest within curtains? Therefore feelings race at evening rest, soul straining toward the inner palace; thoughts layer at night rising, lifted to the deep suburb in remembrance. I humbly hope on rite day to lead six palaces and titled women to offer in person. May it extend 'as if present' reverence and unfold sincere bowing. Gathering this slight sincerity has spanned seasons. The carriage will soon depart; offering jade cannot delay—I venture loyal heart to aid the great rite. May the sagely court set precedent and spread fragrant regulation; firefly light add radiance to sun and moon.
