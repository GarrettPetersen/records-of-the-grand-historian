5
李暠，淮安王神通玄孫，清河王孝節孫也。 暠少孤，事母甚謹。 睿宗時，累轉衛尉少卿。 丁憂去職，在喪柴毀，家人密親未嘗窺其言笑。 開元初，授汝州刺史，為政嚴簡，州境肅然。 與兄昇弟暈，尤相篤睦，昇等每月自東都省暠，往來微行，州人不之覺，其清慎如此。 俄入授太常少卿，三遷黃門侍郎，兼太原尹，仍充太原已北諸軍節度使。 太原舊俗，有僧徒以習禪為業，及死不殮，但以屍送近郊以飼鳥獸。 如是積年，土人號其地為「黃坑」。 側有餓狗千數，食死人肉，因侵害幼弱，遠近患之，前後官吏不能禁止。 暠到官，申明禮憲，期不再犯，發兵捕殺群狗，其風遂革。 久之，轉太常卿，旬日，拜工部尚書、東都留守。
Li Gao was a great-grandson of Prince Huai'an Li Shentong and a grandson of Prince of Qinghe Li Xiaojie. Gao lost his father early and attended his mother with exceptional devotion. Under Emperor Ruizong he rose through successive posts to Vice Minister of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. He left office to observe mourning and during the funeral period emaciated himself through fasting and self-mortification; even close kin never saw him speak or smile. In the early Kaiyuan period he was appointed prefect of Ru Prefecture; his administration was strict yet economical, and the region became notably orderly. He was especially close to his elder brother Sheng and younger brother Yun; each month they would leave the Eastern Capital to visit Gao, travelling so unobtrusively that the people of the prefecture never noticed—such was his probity and restraint. He soon entered court as vice minister of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, was promoted three times to vice minister of the Secretariat, served concurrently as prefect of Taiyuan, and was also military commissioner for all armies north of Taiyuan. Taiyuan had an old custom whereby monks who devoted themselves to Chan practice, when they died, were not buried but their corpses were taken to the outskirts to feed birds and wild animals. Year after year this continued, until locals called the place the "Yellow Pit." Thousands of starving dogs beside it fed on corpses and then attacked children; the trouble spread far and wide, yet successive officials could not put a stop to it. When Gao took office he proclaimed ritual law, fixed a deadline after which the practice must cease, and sent troops to kill the dog packs; the custom was reformed. After a time he became minister of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices; ten days later he was appointed minister of works and defender of the Eastern Capital.
6
開元二十一年正月，制曰：「繼好之義，雖屬邊鄙； 受命以出，必在親賢。 事欲重於當時，禮故崇於殊俗，選眾之舉，無出宗英。 工部尚書李暠，體含柔嘉，識致明允，為公族之領袖，是朝廷之羽儀。 金城公主既在蕃中，漢庭公卿非無專對，有懷於遠，夫豈能忘，宜持節充入吐蕃使，準式發遣。 」以國信物一萬匹、私覿物二千匹，皆雜以五彩遣之。 及還，金城公主上言，請以今年九月一日樹碑於赤嶺，定蕃、漢界。 樹碑之日，詔張守珪、李行袆與吐蕃使莽布支同往觀焉。 既而吐蕃遣其臣隨漢使分往劍南及河西、磧西，歷告邊州曰：「兩國和好，無相侵掠。 」漢使告亦如之。 以暠奉使稱職，轉吏部尚書。 時吏部告身印與曹印文同，行用參雜，難以區分，暠奏請準司勛兵部印文例，加「官告」兩字，至今行之。
In the first month of 733 an edict declared: "The duty of maintaining friendly ties, though it belongs to the frontier; when an envoy is commissioned to depart, he must be a close kinsman or a man of worth. The mission must carry weight in its time, and ritual therefore honors foreign custom; in selecting from the many, none surpasses an outstanding member of the imperial clan. Minister of Works Li Gao combines gentle excellence with clear and sound judgment; he is a leader among the imperial kin and an ornament of the court. Since Princess Jincheng already resides in Tibet, the Han court surely has ministers capable of diplomatic reply; concern for distant lands cannot be set aside. He should go as credentialed envoy to Tibet and be dispatched according to protocol. " He was sent with ten thousand bolts of state gifts and two thousand bolts of private gifts, all woven in the five colors. On his return, Princess Jincheng petitioned that on the first day of the ninth month a boundary stele be erected at Red Ridge to mark the Tibetan and Han frontier. On the day the stele was raised, Zhang Shougui and Li Xingwei were ordered to attend with the Tibetan envoy Mangbuzhi. Tibet then sent officials to accompany Han envoys to Jiannan, Hexi, and the western frontier, announcing at each border prefecture: "The two realms are at peace and shall not encroach on one another." " The Han envoys made the same proclamation. Because Gao discharged his embassy with distinction, he was promoted to minister of personnel. At that time the seals on ministry appointment documents and office seals bore identical inscriptions and were easily confused. Gao memorialized that, following the precedent of the ministries of honors and war, the words "official appointment" be added—a practice that continues today.
7
暠風儀秀整，所歷皆以威重見稱，朝廷稱其有宰相之望。 累封武都縣伯，俄為太子少傅。 病卒，年六十余，贈益州大都督。
Gao's bearing was refined and imposing; wherever he served he was praised for gravitas, and the court regarded him as a man with the makings of a chief minister. He was ennobled in succession as Baron of Wudu and soon appointed junior tutor to the heir apparent. He died of illness in his sixties and was posthumously appointed governor-general of Yizhou.
8
齊物，淮安王神通子、鹽州刺史銳孫也。 齊物無學術，在官嚴整。 開元二十四年後，歷懷、陜二州刺史。 齊物天寶初開砥柱之險，以通流運，於石中得古鐵犁鏵，有「平陸」字，因改河北縣為平陸縣，加齊物銀青光祿大夫，為鴻臚卿、河南尹。 齊物與右相李適之善，適之為林甫所構貶官，齊物坐謫竟陵太守。 入為司農、鴻臚卿。 至德初，拜太子賓客，遷刑部尚書、鳳翔尹、太常卿、京兆尹。 為政發官吏陰事，以察為能，於物少恩，而清廉自飭，人吏莫敢抵犯。 晚年除太子太傅、兼宗正卿。 上元二年五月卒，輟朝一日。 詔曰：「故金紫光祿大夫、太子太傅、兼宗正卿齊物，宗室珪璋，士林楨幹，清廉獨斷，剛毅不群。 歷踐周行，備經中外，威名益振，忠效彌彰。 三尹神州，一登會府，擒奸掩鉤距之術，恤獄正喉舌之官。 遂令調護儲闈，再登師傅，從容賓友，師長官僚。 桑榆之時，壯誌逾勵； 松柏之性，晚歲常堅。 天不慭遺，奄然殂謝，念親感舊，深軫於懷。 宜錫寵章，載光營魄。 可贈太子太師。」
Qi Wu was a son of Prince Huai'an Li Shentong and grandson of Yan Prefecture prefect Li Rui. Qi Wu had little scholarly learning, but in office he was strict and disciplined. After 736 he served in succession as prefect of Huai and Shan prefectures. Early in the Tianbao reign Qi Wu cleared the Dizhu rapids to open river transport; among the rocks he found an ancient iron plowshare inscribed "Pinglu," and renamed Hebei County Pinglu County. He was promoted to silver-gleaming grand master of splendid happiness and appointed director of the court for dependencies and prefect of Henan. Qi Wu was friendly with Right Chancellor Li Shizhi; when Shizhi was framed by Li Linfu and demoted, Qi Wu was implicated and exiled as prefect of Jingling. He was recalled to serve as minister of agriculture and director of the court for dependencies. At the start of the Zhide reign he was appointed household companion to the heir apparent, then rose to minister of justice, prefect of Fengxiang, minister of the court of imperial sacrifices, and prefect of Jingzhao. In office he exposed officials' secret misconduct and prided himself on sharp scrutiny; he was sparing of mercy yet scrupulously self-restrained in integrity, and neither subordinates nor the public dared cross him. In his later years he was appointed grand tutor to the heir apparent and concurrently director of the imperial clan court. He died in the fifth month of 761; the court suspended audiences for one day. An edict read: "The late Gold-Gleaming Grand Master Qi Wu, grand tutor to the heir apparent and concurrent director of the imperial clan court, was a jewel of the imperial house and a pillar of the scholarly world—incorruptible and decisive, resolute beyond the common run." He served through the full range of offices, both at court and in the provinces; his reputation for authority grew and his loyal service shone ever brighter. Thrice he governed the capital region, once he headed the metropolitan prefecture; he exposed wrongdoing and mastered subtle stratagems, showed compassion in prisons, and set right the censorial offices. He was then charged with overseeing the heir's household, twice served as tutor, was a steady companion to the heir, and guided the officials. In his twilight years his resolve grew only stronger; like pine and cypress, firm to the end. Heaven was not kind to leave him; he has suddenly passed away. Remembering kinship and old ties, our hearts are deeply grieved. Let favor and posthumous honors be granted to bring glory to his departed spirit. He is posthumously appointed grand tutor to the heir apparent."
9
子復，字初陽，以父廕累官至江陵府司錄。 精曉吏道，衛伯玉厚遇之，府中之事，多以咨委。 性苛刻，為伯玉所信，奏為江陵縣令，遷少尹，歷饒州、蘇州刺史，皆著政聲。 李希烈背叛，荊南節度張伯儀數出兵，為希烈所敗，朝廷憂之。 以復久在江陵，得軍民心，復方在母喪，起為江陵少尹、兼御史中丞，充節度行軍司馬。 伯儀既受代，以復為容州刺史、兼御史中丞，充本管招討使，加檢校常侍。 先時西原叛亂，前後經略使征討反者，獲其人皆沒為官奴婢，配作坊重役，復乃令訪其親屬，悉歸還之。 在容州三歲，南人安悅。 遷廣州刺史、兼御史大夫、嶺南節度觀察使。 會安南經略使高正平、張應相次卒官，其下參佐偏裨李元度、胡懷義等阻兵，黷亂州縣，奸贓狼藉。 復誘懷義杖殺之，奏元度流於荒裔。 又勸導百姓，令變茅屋為瓦舍。 瓊州久陷於蠻獠中，復累遣使喻之，因奏置瓊州都督府以綏撫之。 復曉於政道，所在稱理，征拜宗正卿，加檢校工部尚書。 未一歲，會華州節度李元諒卒，以復為華州刺史、潼關防禦鎮國軍使，仍檢校戶部尚書，兼御史大夫。
His son Fu, courtesy name Chuyang, rose through his father's yin privilege to registrar of Jiangling prefecture. He was expert in administrative practice; Wei Boyu favored him highly, and most prefectural affairs were referred to him. Harsh by nature, he won Boyu's trust, was recommended as magistrate of Jiangling, promoted to vice prefect, and served as prefect of Rao and Su—winning renown for his governance at each post. When Li Xilie rebelled, Jingnan military commissioner Zhang Boyi repeatedly took the field and was defeated; the court grew anxious. Because Fu had long served at Jiangling and won army and populace alike, he was summoned from mourning for his mother to be vice prefect of Jiangling and concurrent vice censor-in-chief, serving as campaigning marshal under the military commission. When Boyi was relieved, Fu was appointed prefect of Rong, concurrent vice censor-in-chief, and pacification commissioner of the circuit, with the added title of acting regular attendant. Earlier, when the western plains rebelled, frontier commissioners who suppressed the uprising enslaved captives for heavy workshop labor. Fu had their kin traced and returned them all to their families. After three years at Rong, the southern peoples were tranquil and content. He was promoted to prefect of Guang, concurrent censor-in-chief, and military commissioner and observer of Lingnan. When Annan commissioners Gao Zhengping and Zhang Ying died in succession, subordinates Li Yuandu, Hu Huaiyi, and others held troops unlawfully, ravaged the prefectures, and left corruption everywhere. Fu lured Hu Huaiyi and had him beaten to death, then memorialized that Li Yuandu be exiled to the frontier. He also urged the people to replace thatched huts with tiled dwellings. Qiong Prefecture had long fallen among tribal peoples; Fu repeatedly sent envoys to win them over and memorialized to establish an area command at Qiong to pacify the region. Skilled in governance, he was praised for good order wherever he served. He was summoned as director of the imperial clan court with the added title of acting minister of works. Within a year, when Hua military commissioner Li Yuanliang died, Fu was appointed prefect of Hua, defender of Tong Pass and commissioner of the army that stabilizes the state, with the added titles of acting minister of revenue and concurrent censor-in-chief.
10
貞元十年，鄭滑節度使李融卒，軍中潰亂，以復檢校兵部尚書，兼滑州刺史、義成軍節度、鄭滑觀察營田等使、兼御史大夫。 復到任，置營田數百頃，以資軍食，不率於民，眾皆悅之。 十二年，加檢校左僕射。 十三年四月卒官，年五十九。 廢朝三日，贈司空。 賻布帛米粟有差。 復久典方面，積財頗甚，為時所譏。
In 794, Zheng-Hua military commissioner Li Rong died and the army fell into disorder; Fu was appointed acting minister of war, concurrent prefect of Hua, military commissioner of the Yicheng army, observer of Zheng and Hua, commissioner of military farms, and concurrent censor-in-chief. On taking office he established several hundred qing of military farms to supply the army without burdening the populace, and all were pleased. In 796 he was given the added title of acting left vice director of the department of state affairs. In the fourth month of 797 he died in office at fifty-nine. Court audiences were suspended for three days; he was posthumously appointed minister of works. Funerary gifts of cloth, silk, rice, and grain were granted in due measure. Having long governed frontier circuits, he amassed considerable wealth and was criticized by contemporaries.
11
李麟，皇室之疏屬，太宗之從孫也。 父浚，開元初置十道按察使，精選吏才，以浚為潤州刺史、江南東道按察使。 轉虢潞二州刺史，益州大都督府長史、攝御史大夫、劍南節度按察使。 所歷以誠信待物，稱為良吏。 八年卒，贈戶部尚書，謚曰誠。
Li Lin was a distant kinsman of the imperial house, a grandnephew of Emperor Taizong. His father Jun, when the ten-circuit investigation commissioners were established early in Kaiyuan and official talent was carefully chosen, was appointed prefect of Run and investigation commissioner of the Jiangnan East circuit. He served in turn as prefect of Guo and Lu, chief administrator of the Yizhou governor-general's office, acting censor-in-chief, and investigation commissioner of the Jiannan military commission. Wherever he served he treated others with sincerity and trust and was acclaimed as a capable official. He died in 720 and was posthumously appointed minister of revenue with the posthumous name Sincere.
12
麟以父任補職，累授京兆府戶曹。 開元二十二年，舉宗室異能，轉殿中侍御史，歷戶部、考功、吏部三員外郎。 天寶元年，遷郎中，尋改諫議大夫。 五載，充河西、隴右、磧西等道黜陟使，稱旨，遷給事中。 七載，遷兵部侍郎。 同列楊國忠專權，不悅麟同職，宰臣奏麟以本官權知禮部貢舉。 俄而國忠為御史大夫，麟復本官。 十一載，遷銀青光祿大夫、國子祭酒。 十四年七月，以本官出為河東太守、河東道采訪使，為政清簡，民吏稱之。 其年冬，祿山構逆，朝廷以麟儒者，恐非禦侮之用，仍以將軍呂崇賁代還。 復以祭酒歸朝，賜爵渭源縣男。 六月，玄宗幸蜀，麟奔赴行在。 既至成都，拜戶部侍郎，兼左丞。 遷憲部尚書。 至德二年正月，拜同中書門下平章事。 時扈從宰相韋見素、房琯、崔渙已赴鳳翔，俄而崔圓繼去，玄宗以麟宗室子，獨留之，行在百司，麟總攝其事。 其年十一月，從上皇還京，策勛行賞，加金紫光祿大夫、刑部尚書、同中書門下三品，進封褒國公。
Lin entered service through his father's yin privilege and rose to household section chief of Jingzhao prefecture. In 734 he was nominated from the imperial clan for exceptional ability, became palace attendant censor, and served as vice director in the ministries of revenue, honors, and personnel. In 742 he was promoted to director; soon after he was appointed remonstrating grand master. In 746 he served as promotion-and-demotion commissioner for Hexi, Longyou, and the western desert circuits; finding favor, he was promoted to palace attendant. In 748 he was promoted to vice minister of war. His colleague Yang Guozhong monopolized power and disliked sharing rank with Lin; the chief ministers had Lin, in his present office, temporarily oversee the civil examination. Soon Guozhong became censor-in-chief and Lin resumed his original post. In 752 he was promoted to silver-gleaming grand master of splendid happiness and chancellor of the directorate of education. In the seventh month of 755 he left court as prefect of Hedong and investigation commissioner of the Hedong circuit; his administration was pure and economical, and officials and people praised him. That winter when Lushan rebelled, the court regarded Lin as a scholar unsuited to military defense and sent General Lü Chongben to replace him. He returned to court as chancellor of the directorate and was ennobled as Baron of Weiyuan. In the sixth month, when Emperor Xuanzong fled to Shu, Lin hurried to the mobile court. On reaching Chengdu he was appointed vice minister of revenue and concurrent left vice director. He was transferred to minister of justice. In the first month of 757 he was appointed associate grand counselor. The accompanying chancellors Wei Jiansu, Fang Guan, and Cui Huan had already gone to Fengxiang, and Cui Yuan soon followed. Because Lin was of the imperial clan, Xuanzong kept him alone to oversee all affairs of the mobile court. In the eleventh month he accompanied the retired emperor back to the capital; for his merit he was given gold-gleaming grand master, minister of justice, and third rank of the secretariat-chancellery, and enfeoffed as Duke of Bao.
13
李國貞，淮安王神通子、淄川王孝同之曾孫。 父廣業，劍州長史。 國貞本名若幽，性剛正，有吏才，歷安定、扶風錄事參軍，皆稱職。 乾元中累遷長安令，尋拜河南尹。 會史思明逼城，元帥李光弼東保河陽，國貞領官吏寓於陜。 數月，征為京兆尹。 上元初，改成都尹、兼御史大夫，充劍南節度使。 入為殿中監。 二年八月，遷戶部尚書、兼御史大夫，持節充朔方、鎮西、北庭、興平、陳鄭等節度行營兵馬及河中節度都統處置使，鎮於絳，賜名國貞。 既至，又加充管內河中、晉、絳、慈、隰、沁等州觀察處置等使，余並如故。
Li Guozhen was a son of Prince Huai'an Li Shentong and great-grandson of Prince of Zichuan Li Xiaotong. His father Guangye was chief administrator of Jian Prefecture. Guozhen's original name was Ruoyou; upright and capable in administration, he served as recording advisor in Anding and Fufeng and was praised as competent at each post. During the Qianyuan reign he rose to magistrate of Chang'an and soon became prefect of Henan. When Shi Siming besieged the city, commander Li Guangbi withdrew east to defend Heyang, and Guozhen led officials to lodge at Shan. Several months later he was summoned as prefect of Jingzhao. Early in the Shangyuan reign he was appointed prefect of Chengdu, concurrent censor-in-chief, and military commissioner of Jiannan. He was recalled to serve as director of the palace domestic service. In the eighth month of 759 he became minister of revenue and concurrent censor-in-chief, with credentials as overall commander of the Shuofang, western frontier, northern court, Xingping, Chen-Zheng, and related campaigning forces and disposition commissioner for Hedong, stationed at Jiang; he was granted the name Guozhen. On arrival he was also made observer and disposition commissioner for Hedong, Jin, Jiang, Ci, Xi, Qin, and other prefectures under his authority; his other titles remained unchanged.
14
國貞既至絳，屬軍中素無儲積，百姓饑饉，難為聚斂，將士等糧賜多闕。 國貞頻以狀聞，未報。 軍中喧喧怨讀，左右以告國貞，國貞喻之曰：「軍將何苦如是，已為奏聞，終有所給。 」信宿軍亂，攻國貞，夜燒衙城門。 國貞莫知所圖，左右勸國貞棄城遁去，國貞曰：「吾銜命為將，不能靖難，安可棄城乎！ 」左右固勸回避，乃隱於州獄，詐負縲紲。 會國貞麾下為賊所擒，因指所在，遂於獄中執國貞，將害之，國貞曰：「軍中乏糧，已有陳請，人不堪賦，予無負於將士耳。 」眾引退。 突將王元振獨曰：「今日之事，豈須問焉！ 」抽刀害國貞及二男、三大將。
When Guozhen reached Jiang, the army had no reserves, the people were starving, taxes could scarcely be raised, and soldiers' rations and pay were largely unpaid. Guozhen repeatedly memorialized the court but received no response. The troops grew loud with complaint; when his staff reported this, Guozhen told them: "Officers, why be so distressed? I have already memorialized the throne; supplies will come in time." " Within a day and night the army mutinied, attacked him, and burned the government gate that night. Guozhen was at a loss; his attendants urged him to abandon the city and flee. He said: "I hold a general's commission; if I cannot restore order, how can I abandon the city!" " His staff pressed him to hide; he concealed himself in the prefectural jail and feigned imprisonment in chains. When one of his men was captured by the mutineers and revealed his hiding place, they seized him in the jail and were about to kill him. Guozhen said: "The army lacks grain and I have already petitioned the throne. The people cannot bear more levies—I have wronged no soldier." " The mob withdrew. Assault officer Wang Yuanzhen alone cried: "For today's business, what need is there to ask!" " He drew his sword and killed Guozhen, his two sons, and three senior generals.

國貞有風采，清白守法，為政急於操下，時論以辨吏稱之。 追贈揚州大都督。
Guozhen had presence and bearing, kept himself pure and law-abiding, and governed with stern control of subordinates; contemporaries praised him as a capable administrator. He was posthumously appointed governor-general of Yangzhou.
16
子锜，以父廕貞元中累至湖、杭二州刺史。 多以寶貨賂李齊運，由是遷潤州刺史兼鹽鐵使，持積財進奉，以結恩澤，德宗甚寵之。 锜恃恩驕恣，有浙西人布衣崔善貞詣闕上封，論锜罪狀，而德宗械送賜锜，锜遂坑殺善貞，天下切齒。 乃增置兵額，選善弓矢者聚之一營，名曰「挽硬隨身」； 以胡、奚雜類虬須者為一將，名曰「蕃落健兒」。 德宗復於潤州置鎮海軍，以锜為節度使，罷其鹽鐵使務。 锜雖罷其利權，且得節度，反狀未發。
His son Qi, through his father's yin privilege, rose during the Zhenyuan reign to prefect of Hu and Hang prefectures. He lavished treasures on Li Qiyun and was promoted to prefect of Run and concurrent salt and iron commissioner; he amassed wealth for tribute to court favor, and Emperor Dezong favored him greatly. Relying on imperial favor, Qi grew arrogant. A commoner of western Zhe, Cui Shanzhen, submitted a sealed memorial at court denouncing his crimes, but Dezong had him shackled and sent to Qi, who buried him alive; the empire seethed with outrage. He then expanded his forces and gathered skilled archers in one camp called the "Hard-Drawing Personal Guard"; and formed another unit of Hu and Xi men with bristling beards under a commander called the "Frontier Elite Warriors." Dezong then established the Zhenhai army at Run and made Qi its military commissioner, relieving him of his salt and iron post. Though Qi lost his lucrative salt monopoly, he gained a military commission, and his rebellion had not yet broken out.
17
憲宗即位已二年，諸道倔強者入朝，而锜不自安，亦請入朝，乃拜锜左僕射。 锜乃署判官王淡為留後。 既而遷延發期，淡與中使頻喻之，不悅，遂諷將士以給冬衣日殺淡而食之。 監軍使聞亂，遣衙將趙锜慰喻，又臠食之。 復以兵註中使之頸，锜佯驚救解之，囚於別館。 遂稱兵，飾五劍，分授管內鎮將，令殺刺史。 於是常州刺史顏防用客李雲謀，矯制傳檄於蘇、杭、湖、睦等州，遂殺其鎮將李深； 湖州辛秘亦殺其鎮將趙惟忠； 而蘇州刺史李素為鎮將姚誌安所系，釘於船舷，生致於锜，未至而锜敗，得免。
Two years after Xianzong's accession, recalcitrant regional commanders came to court; uneasy, Qi also requested an audience and was appointed left vice director of the department of state affairs. Qi appointed his judicial officer Wang Dan acting commissioner in his absence. He then delayed departure; when Dan and the palace envoy repeatedly urged him, he grew angry and incited the troops, on the day winter clothing was issued, to kill Dan and devour him. The army supervisor sent yamen general Zhao Qi to calm the troops; they butchered and ate him too. They then held blades to the envoy's throat; Qi feigned alarm, "rescued" him, and imprisoned him in a separate lodge. He then took up arms, had five swords ornamented, and gave them to garrison commanders throughout his circuit with orders to kill the prefects. Changzhou prefect Yan Fang, following his retainer Li Yun's plan, forged an edict and circulated a call to Su, Hang, Hu, Mu, and other prefectures, and killed garrison commander Li Shen; at Huzhou, Xin Mi also killed garrison commander Zhao Weizhong; while Suzhou prefect Li Su was seized by garrison commander Yao Zhi'an, nailed to a ship's side, and sent alive toward Qi; before arrival Qi was defeated, and Li Su was spared.

初，锜以宣州富饒，有並吞之意，遣兵馬使張子良、李奉仙、田少卿領兵三千分略宣、池等州。 三將夙有向順誌，而锜甥裴行立亦思向順，其密謀多決於行立，乃回戈趣城，執锜於幕，縋而出之，斬於闕下，年六十七。 其「挽硬」、「蕃落」將士，或投井自縊，紛紛枕藉而死者，不可勝紀。
Earlier, coveting the wealth of Xuancheng, Qi sent military commissioners Zhang Ziliang, Li Fengxian, and Tian Shaoqing with three thousand men to overrun Xuancheng, Chi, and neighboring prefectures. The three commanders had long wished to submit; Qi's nephew Pei Xingli also favored loyalty, and their plot was largely his. They turned back toward the city, seized Qi in his tent, lowered him by rope, and beheaded him before the gate at sixty-seven. Officers and men of his Hard-Drawing and Frontier Elite units threw themselves into wells or hanged themselves; the dead lay piled beyond count.

" Wu replied: "Greater merit would implicate Qi's paternal cousins—the line of Prince Huai'an Li Shentong. Huai'an rendered great service to the state; a rebellious descendant must not drag him down." " They asked further: "Should Qi's full brothers be punished with him?" " Wu said: "Qi's full brothers are sons of Ruoyou, who died in the ruler's service. To punish the brothers would require striking Ruoyou from the registers—which would also be unjust." " The chief ministers agreed; when the execution edict was issued, only the chief villain's household was punished.

Li Huan was a grandson of Prince of Wu Li Ke, the third son of Emperor Taizong. Ke's third son Kun fathered Prince of Xin'an Li Yi, who had three sons: Huan, Yi, and Xian. Huan's conduct was upright and cultivated; during Tianbao he served in the Southern Palace and for more than ten years headed various bureaus in turn. In mourning for his father he observed the rites with proper grief; when mourning ended he was enfeoffed as Duke of Zhao by the precedent for commandery princes' sons. When Yang Guozhong held power, palace officials who would not attach to him were sent to the provinces; Huan left his post as director of the bureau of honors and became prefect of Suiyang. Soon his brother Xian became prefect of Wei commandery; the brothers governed facing prefectures on opposite banks of the Yellow River and were both praised for good governance. In 755 he went to the capital on administrative review. When the Lushan rebellion broke out and Xuanzong fled to Shu, Huan hurried to the mobile court and was appointed vice minister of war and concurrent censor-in-chief. He was soon appointed prefect of Shu commandery and investigation commissioner of the Jiannan military commission. At Chengdu the retired emperor faced a night mutiny led by stalwart Guo Qianren; when summons from the Mysterious Glory Tower failed, Huan and Six Armies commissioner Chen Xuanli quelled it; for this he was given gold-gleaming grand master. Meanwhile Xian was prefect of Fengxiang, supporting Emperor Suzong; both brothers rendered distinguished service. On returning to the capital with the retired emperor, he became minister of revenue; Xian became censor-in-chief and concurrent prefect of Jingzhao and was enfeoffed as Duke of Liang. The brothers were enfeoffed as dukes in the same edict.

Early in the Qianyuan reign he was concurrent censor-in-chief with credentials as overall commander of Huainan, Jiangnan, and Jiangxi and as pacification, observation, and disposition commissioner. In 759, because Song prefect Liu Zhan held troops in Henan and harbored disloyal intent, he was openly made Huainan military commissioner while a secret edict ordered Yangzhou chief administrator Deng Jingshan and Huan to plot against him. Zhan's faction was then powerful; once he received the appointment he immediately crossed the Huai with his army. Jingshan and Huan resisted at Shouchun and were defeated. Huan fled south across the Yangzi and held Danyang; he was demoted to military advisor of Yuan prefecture. In 763 he died of illness in exile; he was posthumously appointed governor-general of Yangzhou, official relay transport was provided, and his coffin was escorted to the capital.

At their height Huan was minister of revenue, Xian minister of personnel and chief counselor, and Yi vice minister of revenue; the brothers shared a mansion in Changxing Lane with three halberds at the gate—sixteen for a duke's household, twelve for first- and third-rank—outshining their contemporaries. Yi ended his career as prefect of Shu.

Xian delighted in virtue and treated scholars with respect; from youth he showed administrative talent. He entered service through family privilege and rose to magistrate of Gaoling, famed for his governance. He was specially promoted to magistrate of Wannian, vice prefect of Henan, and prefect of Wei commandery; then entered as general of the golden chariots, became director of palace construction, and prefect of Jingzhao—winning renown wherever he served. In 754 rain fell for more than sixty days; Chief Minister Yang Guozhong, who disliked his independence, blamed the disaster on the Jingzhao prefect and exiled him as prefect of Changsha. Grain prices in the capital soared; the people sang: "If you want cheap grain, nothing beats bringing back Li Xian." " Such was the esteem his governance commanded. Early in the Zhide reign the court sought outstanding men to quell rebellion; Xian was summoned to the mobile court as prefect of Fufeng and concurrent censor-in-chief. In the twelfth month of 757 an edict read: "Silver-gleaming grand master, acting minister of rites Li Xian has supplied the armies fully and advanced the state's business." He is appointed grand master of splendid happiness, acting censor-in-chief, concurrent prefect of Jingzhao, and Duke of Liang." " In 759 an edict declared: "Li Xian is a pillar of the court and a loyal minister of the imperial house." He is appointed vice director of the secretariat and associate grand counselor." " He was appointed chief minister together with Lü Yin, Li Kui, and Diwu Qi. Xian's standing was somewhat higher; on military and state affairs the other ministers dared not speak, and he decided alone—wherefore Yin and the others resented him.
24

Earlier Li Fuguo served as campaigning marshal and secretly ordered troops to spy on the populace, calling it "inspection affairs." Loyal men were framed one after another; when summons came no agency dared resist. Serious prisoners in the censorate and court of judicial review, trials unfinished, were summoned to the Silver Terrace and released regardless of gravity; none dared refuse. Each day at the Silver Terrace Gate he decided state affairs; whatever required action he called an edict; he carried palace seals in and out at will. Even genuine edicts required Fuguo's countersignature before execution. When Xian became chief minister he kowtowed and denounced Fuguo's monopoly of power; the emperor took heed, praised Xian's integrity, and reforms followed. Fuguo yielded the campaigning marshal post, sought his former office, and inspection affairs ceased; he deeply resented Xian.

Fuguo, formerly commissioner of the Flying Dragon Office, was the man's patron and appealed for him; the throne ordered supervisory censor Sun Jin to investigate. Jin initially upheld the execution as lawful. She appealed again; Vice censor-in-chief Cui Boyang, vice minister of justice Li Ye, and chief justice Quan Xian were ordered to re-examine the case; all three agreed with Jin. Boyang grew angry and summoned Ruoxu with harsh words. Before Boyang could report upward, Ruoxu raced to audience and cried urgency to Suzong, who said: "I know—leave." He was recalled as minister of rites and concurrent director of the imperial clan court. When the emperor went to Shan, Xian took the Shangshan route to the mobile court. After the return to the capital he was appointed vice director of the secretariat and associate grand counselor. By precedent chief ministers did not entertain guests in the Hall of Administration; with affairs pressing everywhere, Yuan Zai and other chief ministers, when eunuchs arrived at the Secretariat bearing edicts, would escort them into the hall and set out couches for them; when Xian became chief minister he ordered those couches removed. Xian, then commissioner of the three offices, objected: "Crimes have leaders and followers, guilt has degrees of severity; to execute all alike would betray Your Majesty's magnanimity and the state's rite of renewal." When the rebels overran the realm, both capitals fell, the emperor fled south, and each man sought to survive—officials and gentry were swept away. Some are Your Majesty's kin, some descendants of meritorious ministers—to punish all with the utmost penalty would betray benevolence and forbearance. Enlightened kings of old punished only the ringleaders; coerced followers went unpunished. The following year he became acting minister of war and concurrent prefect of Qu. In the seventh month of 766 he died of illness at fifty-eight.

李巨，曾祖父虢王鳳，高祖之第十四子也。 鳳孫邕，嗣虢王，巨即邕之第二子也。 剛銳果決，頗涉獵書史，好屬文。 開元中為嗣虢王。 天寶五載，出為西河太守。 皇太子杜良娣之妹婿柳勣陷詔獄，巨母扶余氏，吉溫嫡母之妹也，溫為京兆士曹，推勣之黨，以徐征等往來巨家，資給之，由是坐貶義陽郡司馬。 六載，御史中丞楊慎矜為李林甫、王鉷構陷得罪，其黨史敬忠亦伏法。 以巨與敬忠相識，坐解官，於南賓郡安置。 又起為夷陵郡太守。 及祿山陷東京，玄宗方擇將帥，張垍言巨善騎射，有謀略，玄宗追至京師。 楊國忠素與巨相識，忌之，謂人曰：「如此小兒，豈得令見人主！ 」經月余日不得見。 玄宗使中官召入奏事，玄宗大悅，遂令中官劉奉庭宣敕令宰相與巨語，幾亭午，方出。 國忠頗怠，對奉庭謂巨曰：「比來人多口打賊，公不爾乎？ 」巨曰：「不知若個軍將能與相公手打賊乎？ 」尋授陳留譙郡太守、攝御史大夫、河南節度使。 翌日，巨稱官銜奉謝，玄宗驚曰：「何得令攝？ 」即日詔兼御史大夫。 巨奏曰：「方今艱難，恐為賊所詐，如忽召臣，不知何以取信？ 」玄宗劈木契分授之，遂以臣兼統嶺南節度使何履光、黔中節度使趙國珍、南陽節度使魯炅，先領三節度事。 有詔貶炅為果毅，以潁川太守來瑱兼御史中丞代之。 巨奏曰：「若炅能存孤城，其功足以補過，則何以處之？ 」玄宗曰：「卿隨宜處置之。 」巨至內鄉，趣南陽，賊將畢思琛聞之，解圍走。 巨趣何履光、趙國珍同至南陽，宣敕貶炅，削其章服，令隨軍效力。 至日晚，以恩命令炅復位。
Li Ju's great-grandfather was Prince of Guo Li Feng, the fourteenth son of Emperor Gaozu. Feng's grandson Yong succeeded as Prince of Guo; Ju was Yong's second son. Firm, sharp, and decisive, he read widely in history and delighted in literary composition. During the Kaiyuan reign he was heir to the Prince of Guo. In 746 he was appointed prefect of Xihe. When Crown Prince's consort Du Liangdi's brother-in-law Liu Ji was imprisoned, Ju's mother Lady Fu was a younger sister of Ji Wen's stepmother; Wen, a Jingzhao staff officer, traced Ji's faction through Xu Zheng's visits and support to Ju's household, and Ju was demoted to military advisor of Yiyang. In 747 vice censor-in-chief Yang Shenjin was framed by Li Linfu and Wang Hong; his associate Shi Jingzhong was also executed. Because Ju knew Jingzhong, he was dismissed and settled in Nanbin commandery. He was later restored as prefect of Yiling. When Lushan took the Eastern Capital, Xuanzong was choosing generals; Zhang Yan praised Ju's horsemanship, archery, and strategy, and the emperor summoned him to the capital. Yang Guozhong, who knew Ju well, resented him and said: "Such a stripling—how can he be allowed before the throne!" " For more than a month he was denied audience. Xuanzong had a eunuch summon him to report; greatly pleased, he ordered Liu Fengting to direct the chief ministers to confer with Ju; Ju did not emerge until nearly noon. Guozhong, indifferent, said to Ju before Fengting: "Lately everyone only talks of fighting rebels—are you not the same?" " Ju replied: "Which general, I wonder, could fight rebels hand to hand with the chancellor?" " He was soon appointed prefect of Chenliu and Qiao, acting censor-in-chief, and Henan military commissioner. The next day Ju gave thanks as "acting" censor-in-chief; Xuanzong exclaimed: "Why was he only acting?" " That same day an edict appointed him concurrent censor-in-chief. Ju memorialized: "These are desperate times; rebels may deceive us—if I am suddenly summoned, how will men trust me?" " Xuanzong split wooden tallies and gave him portions; he was charged to oversee Lingnan commissioner He Luguang, Qianzhong commissioner Zhao Guozhen, and Nanyang commissioner Lu Hui, first taking command of all three. An edict demoted Hui to resolute cavalry officer and appointed Yingchuan prefect Lai Tian concurrent vice censor-in-chief in his place. Ju asked: "If Hui can hold the isolated city, his merit may offset his fault—how then should he be treated?" " Xuanzong said: "Dispose of it as you see fit." " Ju reached Neixiang and pressed on to Nanyang; rebel general Bi Sichen heard and lifted the siege. Ju summoned He Luguang and Zhao Guozhen to Nanyang, proclaimed Hui's demotion, stripped his insignia, and ordered him to serve with the army. By evening he graciously restored Hui to office.
29
至德二年，為太子少傅。 十月，收西京，為留守、兼御史大夫。 三年夏四月，加太子少師、兼河南尹，充東京留守，判尚書省事，充東畿采訪等使。 於城市橋梁稅出入車牛等錢以供國用，頗有幹沒，士庶怨讟。 後與妃張氏不睦，張氏即皇后從父妹也。 宗正卿李遵構之，發其所犯贓賄，貶為遂州刺史。 屬劍南東川節度兵馬使、梓州刺史段子璋反，以眾襲節度使李奐於綿州，路經遂州，巨蒼黃修屬郡禮迎之，為子璋所殺。
In 757 he became junior tutor to the heir apparent. In the tenth month, when the Western Capital was recovered, he served as defender and concurrent censor-in-chief. In the fourth month of 758 he was made junior tutor to the heir apparent, concurrent prefect of Henan, defender of the Eastern Capital, with charge over the department of state affairs and investigation commissioner of the eastern metropolitan region. He levied bridge tolls on carts and livestock entering the city to supply state revenue, embezzled heavily, and drew popular resentment. Later he quarreled with his consort Lady Zhang, the empress's cousin. Imperial clan director Li Zun framed him, exposed his bribery, and he was demoted to prefect of Su. When eastern Jiannan military commissioner and Zi prefect Duan Zizhang rebelled and attacked commissioner Li Huan at Mian, passing through Su, Ju hastily had his staff prepare a ceremonial welcome and was killed by Zizhang.

子則之，以宗室歷官，好學，年五十余，每執經詣太學聽受。 嗣曹王臯自荊南來朝，稱薦之，貞元二年，自睦王府長史遷左金吾衛大將軍，以從父甥竇申追遊無閑親累，貶昭州司馬。
His son Zhezhi, a clansman in office who loved learning, still in his fifties went regularly to the Imperial Academy with classics in hand to hear lectures. Heir to Prince of Cao Li Gao, arriving from Jingnan, recommended him; in 786 he rose from chief administrator of the Prince of Mu's mansion to general of the Left Golden Chariots Guard; implicated by his cousin Dou Shen's dissolute companionship, he was demoted to military advisor of Zhao.
31
史臣曰：暠孝友清慎，居官有稱； 齊物貞廉整肅，復節制權謀； 國貞清白守法，皆神通之曾玄，宗室之翹楚。 锜之為逆，不累其親，前人之積德彰矣，當朝之用法明矣。 然暠發人陰私，齊物積財興議，國貞急於操下，皆尺之短也。 麟修整，峘循良，匪躬立事，始終無玷者，皆宗室之英也。 峴之剛正才略，有足可稱。 初為國忠所憎，終沮朝恩之勢。 處群邪之內，堅獨正之心，是不吐也； 活東都之命，是不茹也。 庶幾乎仲山甫之道焉！ 巨以剛銳果決，亦可嘉焉，終以贓賄貪殘，良可痛也。
The historiographer writes: Gao was filial, friendly, pure, and cautious, and won repute in office; Qi Wu was upright, incorruptible, and stern; Fu exercised command with shrewd stratagem; Guozhen was pure and law-abiding—all were descendants of Shentong, outstanding men of the imperial clan. Qi's rebellion did not implicate his kin—the virtue of forebears shone clear, and the court's use of law was manifest. Yet Gao exposed private misconduct, Qi Wu amassed wealth and drew criticism, and Guozhen was harsh with subordinates—these were their flaws. Lin was cultivated, Huan dutiful and capable; those who devoted themselves to office without stain from start to finish were all eminent clansmen. Xian's firm integrity and strategic talent deserve praise. Hated first by Guozhong, he ultimately checked the power of court favoritism. Among the wicked he kept an upright heart alone—he would not yield; he spared the lives of the Eastern Capital—he would not swallow injustice. He approached the way of Zhong Shanfu! Ju's firmness and resolve were admirable; yet his bribery, greed, and cruelty are truly lamentable.
32
贊曰：宗室賢良，枝葉茂盛。 最尤者誰？ 峴獨守正。
Encomium: The imperial clan is worthy; its branches flourish. Who stands highest among them? Xian alone held to the upright path.
