肅宗皇帝十四子：章敬皇后生代宗皇帝，宮人孫氏生越王係，張氏生承天皇帝，王氏生衛王佖，陳婕妤生彭王僅，韋妃生兗王僴，張美人生涇王侹，裴昭儀生襄王僙，段婕妤生杞王倕，崔妃生召王偲，張皇后生恭懿太子佋、定王侗，宮人生鄆王榮、宋王僖。
Emperor Suzong had fourteen sons. Empress Zhangjing bore Emperor Daizong; the palace woman Lady Sun bore Prince of Yue Xi; Lady Zhang bore Emperor Chengtian; Lady Wang bore Prince of Wei Bi; Lady Chen, Jieyu, bore Prince of Peng Jin; Consort Wei bore Prince of Yan Xian; Lady Zhang the Beauty bore Prince of Jing Ting; Lady Pei, Zhaoyi, bore Prince of Xiang Pu; Lady Duan, Jieyu, bore Prince of Qi Chui; Consort Cui bore Prince of Shao Si; Empress Zhang bore Crown Prince Gongyi Shao and Prince of Ding Tong; palace women bore Prince of Yun Rong and Prince of Song Xi.
2
越王係，本名儋，肅宗第二子也。 天寶中，封南陽郡王，授特進。 至德二年十二月，進封趙王。 乾元二年三月，九節度之兵潰於河北，史思明僭號於相州，王師未集，朝廷震駭。 詔以李光弼握兵關東以代子儀。 光弼請以親賢統師，七月，詔曰：
Prince of Yue Xi, whose original name was Dan, was Suzong's second son. During the Tianbao era, he was enfeoffed as Prince of Nanyang commandery and appointed Special Advancement. In the twelfth month of Zhide 2 (757), he was promoted to Prince of Zhao. In the third month of Qianyuan 2 (759), the armies of the nine frontier commands collapsed in Hebei; Shi Siming usurped a title at Xiang prefecture; the imperial armies had not yet assembled; and the court was shaken with alarm. An edict appointed Li Guangbi to hold military command east of the Pass in place of Guo Ziyi. Guangbi requested that a member of the imperial kin command the army; in the seventh month, an edict said:
3
握兵之要，古先為重； 命帥之道，心膂攸憑。 是知靖難夷兇，必資於金革； 總戎授律，實仗於親賢。 蓋將底寧邦家，保息黎獻者矣。 朕以薄德，纘承鴻緒，往屬元兇暴亂，中夏不寧。 上憑宗社之靈，下藉熊羆之力，由是廓清鹹、洛，拯此生人。 頃以河朔殘妖，尚稽天討，蛇豕竊依於城堡，塗炭久被於齊氓，朕為人父母，寧忘閔念。 雖好生息戰，每冀其歸降； 而余孽昧恩，靡聞於悔禍。 所以軒後親征於獯鬻，周文致役於昆夷，古之用兵，蓋非獲已。 趙王係幼稟異操，夙懷韜略，負東平之文學，蘊任城之智勇。 性惟忠孝，持愛敬以立身； 誌尚權謀，有經通之遠智。 知子者父，方有屬於維城； 擇能而授，俾克申於戎律。 且兇徒嘯聚，頗歷歲時，惡既貫盈，理當撲滅。 君親有命，可不敬乎！ 俾展龍豹之韜，永清梟獍之類。 可充天下兵馬元帥，仍令司空、兼侍中、薊國公光弼副知節度行營事。 應緣軍司署置，所司準式。
To hold the army's key—from antiquity this has been weighty; in appointing a commander, one relies on the heart and backbone. Thus one knows that to quell calamity and subdue the violent, one must rely on arms; to command the host and confer the laws of war truly depends on the imperial kin. This is how one will settle the state and household and give the people peace and rest. I, of meager virtue, have succeeded to the great succession; formerly the chief rebel wrought violent disorder and the Central Plain was not at peace. Above I relied on the spirit of the ancestral altars; below I borrowed the strength of bear and tiger; thereby I cleared Xian and Luo and saved these living people. Recently the remnant rebels of Heshuo still await Heaven's punishment; serpent and swine steal refuge in walled cities; smearing and burning have long afflicted the people of Qi; I, as father and mother to the people, could not forget compassionate concern. Though I love life and would cease from war, I ever hoped they would surrender; yet the surviving remnant knew not grace and gave no sign of repenting their calamity. Therefore the Yellow Emperor personally campaigned against the Xunyu, and King Wen of Zhou levied service against the Kunyi—in antiquity the use of arms was surely not by choice. Prince of Zhao Xi from youth received an uncommon character and long harbored strategy; he bore the literary learning of Dongping and contained the wisdom and courage of Rencheng. In nature he was only loyal and filial, holding love and reverence to establish himself; his aim favored stratagem and he possessed far-reaching wisdom that penetrated the classics. He who knows his son is the father—only then is there reliance on the wall of the city; choosing the able and entrusting him, so that he may fulfill the laws of war. Moreover the violent band has gathered and cried out for quite some time; their evil is full to the brim—reason demands they be struck down and extinguished. When lord and parent give command, can one fail to revere it! Let him deploy the stratagems of dragon and leopard and forever clear the owl-and-tiger kind. He may serve as Commander-in-Chief of All Armies and Cavalry under Heaven; Li Guangbi, Minister of Works, concurrently Palace Attendant, Duke of Ji, is further ordered as deputy to manage the frontier command and field headquarters. For all military offices to be established in connection with this, the relevant offices shall follow regulation.
4
九月，史思明陷洛陽，光弼以副元帥董兵守河陽，王不出京師。 十月，下詔車駕親征，諫官論奏乃止； 王請行，不許。 三年四月，改封越王。 寶應元年四月，肅宗寢疾彌留。 皇后張氏與中官李輔國有隙，因皇太子監國，謀誅輔國，使人以肅宗命召太子入宮。 皇后謂太子曰：「賊臣輔國，久典禁軍，四方詔令，皆出其口。 頃矯制命，逼徙聖皇。 今聖體彌留，心懷怏怏，常忌吾與汝。 又聞射生內侍程元振結托黃門，將圖不軌，若不誅之，禍在頃刻。」 太子泣而對曰：「此二人是陛下勛舊內臣，今聖躬不康，重以此事驚撓聖慮，情所難任。 若決行此命，當出外徐圖之。」 後知太子難與共事，乃召係謂之曰：「皇太子仁惠，不足以圖平禍難。」 復以除輔國謀告之，曰：「汝能行此事乎？」 係曰：「能。」 後令內謁者監段恆俊與越王謀，召中官有武勇者二百余人，授甲於長生殿。 是月乙丑，皇后矯詔召太子，程元振伺知之，告輔國。 元振握兵於淩霄門候之，太子既至，以難告。 太子曰：「必無此事。 聖恙危篤，吾豈懼死不赴召乎？」 元振曰：「為社稷計，行則禍及矣。」 遂以兵護太子匿於飛龍廄。 丙寅夜，元振、輔國勒兵於三殿前，收捕越王及同謀內侍硃光輝、段恆俊等百余人。 禁系幽皇后於別殿，侍者十數人隨之。 是日，皇后、越王俱為輔國所害
In the ninth month, Shi Siming took Luoyang; Guangbi, as deputy commander-in-chief, led troops to hold Heyang; the prince did not leave the capital. In the tenth month, an edict was issued for the imperial carriage to campaign in person; remonstrating officials submitted memorials and it was stopped; the prince requested to go; permission was denied. In the fourth month of the third year, his title was changed to Prince of Yue. In the fourth month of Baoying 1 (762), Suzong lay ill near death. Empress Zhang had a rift with the inner official Li Fuguo; because the Crown Prince was regent, she plotted to execute Fuguo and sent men with Suzong's command to summon the Crown Prince into the palace. The Empress said to the Crown Prince: "The traitorous minister Fuguo has long controlled the Forbidden Army; imperial edicts to the four quarters all issue from his mouth. Recently he forged an edict and forced the transfer of the Sacred Emperor. Now the Sacred Body lingers near death, brooding with resentment, and constantly bears ill will toward me and you. I have also heard that the inner attendant of the Archers' Corps, Cheng Yuanzhen, has allied with the Yellow Gate and intends treason; if we do not execute them, disaster will come within moments." The Crown Prince wept and replied: "These two men are Your Majesty's meritorious old inner officials; now the Sacred Person is unwell—to further alarm the Sacred Mind with this matter is more than I can bear in conscience. If this order is carried through, I should withdraw and plan it at leisure." Later, knowing the Crown Prince was difficult to work with, she summoned Xi and said to him: "The Crown Prince is benevolent and mild; he is not enough to plan the clearing of this calamity." She then told him the plan to eliminate Fuguo and said: "Can you carry out this affair?" Xi said: "I can." Later she had the Inner Usher Supervisor Duan Hengjun plot with Prince of Yue, summoned more than two hundred inner officials noted for martial valor, and issued armor in the Hall of Long Life. On the yichou day of that month, the Empress forged an edict summoning the Crown Prince; Cheng Yuanzhen learned of it by surveillance and informed Fuguo. Yuanzhen held troops at Lingxiao Gate awaiting him; when the Crown Prince arrived, he told him of the difficulty. The Crown Prince said: "There must be no such thing. The Sacred affliction is grave; how could I fear death and fail to answer the summons?" Yuanzhen said: "For the sake of the altars of state, if you go, calamity will follow." He then used troops to protect the Crown Prince and hid him in the Flying Dragon Stable. On the night of bingyin, Yuanzhen and Fuguo marshaled troops before the Three Halls, seized Prince of Yue and the conspiring inner attendants Zhu Guanghui, Duan Hengjun, and more than a hundred others. Empress Zhang was confined in a separate hall; a dozen attendants followed her. That day, the Empress and Prince of Yue were both killed by Fuguo.
5
係子：建、逌、逾。 建，建中元年十一月，封武威郡王，授殿中監同正員； 逌封興道郡王，授殿中監同正員； 逾封齊國公，光祿卿同正員。
Xi's sons were Jian, You, and Yu. Jian: in the eleventh month of Jianzhong 1 (780), he was enfeoffed as Prince of Wuwei commandery and appointed Director of the Palace Domestic Service with Honorary Rank; You was enfeoffed as Prince of Xingdao commandery and appointed Director of the Palace Domestic Service with Honorary Rank; Yu was enfeoffed as Duke of Qi and made Grand Master for Glorious Happiness with Honorary Rank.
6
承天皇帝倓，肅宗第三子也。 天寶中，封建寧郡王，授太常卿同正員。 英毅有才略，善射。 祿山之亂，玄宗幸蜀，倓兄弟典親兵扈從。 車駕渡渭，百姓遮道乞留太子，太子諭之曰：「至尊奔播，吾不忍違離左右，俟吾見上奏聞。」 倓於行宮謂太子曰：「逆胡犯順，四海分崩，不因人情，何以興復？ 夫有國家者，大孝莫若存社稷。 今從至尊入蜀，則散關已東，非皇家所有，何以維屬人情？ 殿下宜購募豪傑，暫往河西，收拾戎馬，點集防邊將卒，不下十萬人，光弼、子儀，全軍河朔，謀為興復，計之上也。」 廣平王亦贊成之，於是令李輔國奏聞。 玄宗欣然聽納，乃分從官、士卒以遣之。 時敗卒膽破，兵仗不完，太子既北上，渡渭，一日百戰。 倓自選驍騎數百衛從，每蒼黃顛沛之際，血戰在前。 太子或過時不得食，倓涕泗不自勝，上尤憐之，軍士屬目歸於倓。 至靈武，太子即帝位。 廣平既為元子，欲以倓為天下兵馬元帥。 侍臣曰：「廣平王冢嗣，有君人之量。」 上曰：「廣平地當儲貳，何假更為元帥？」 左右曰：「廣平今未冊立，艱難時人尤屬望於元帥。 況太子從曰撫軍，守曰監國。 今之元帥，撫軍也，廣平為宜。」 遂以廣平為元帥，倓典親軍，李輔國為元帥府司馬。
Emperor Chengtian Tan was Suzong's third son. During the Tianbao era, he was enfeoffed as Prince of Jianning commandery and appointed Minister of Ceremonies with Honorary Rank. He was resolute and had talent and stratagem; he excelled at archery. When the An Lushan rebellion broke out, Xuanzong fled to Shu; Tan and his brothers commanded the imperial guard escorting him. When the imperial carriage crossed the Wei, the people blocked the road begging to keep the Crown Prince; the Crown Prince instructed them: "The Supreme One is fleeing in distress; I cannot bear to leave his side; wait until I have seen him and reported." Tan said to the Crown Prince at the traveling palace: "The rebellious barbarian has violated order; the four seas are shattered; without relying on popular sentiment, how can restoration be achieved? For one who holds a state, there is no greater filial piety than preserving the altars of state. If we now follow the Supreme One into Shu, then east of San Pass will no longer be held by the house—how can we hold the loyalty of the people? Your Highness should recruit heroes, go temporarily to Hexi, gather troops and horses, muster frontier commanders and soldiers—no fewer than a hundred thousand—with Guangbi and Ziyi bringing their full armies from Heshuo to plan restoration: that is the best plan." Prince of Guangling also approved; thereupon Li Fuguo was ordered to report. Xuanzong gladly accepted; troops and attendant officials were divided and sent off. At that time the defeated soldiers were terror-stricken and their weapons incomplete; once the Crown Prince marched north and crossed the Wei, he fought a hundred battles in a day. Tan himself selected several hundred fierce horsemen to guard him; in every moment of sudden peril he fought in the van, bloodied. Sometimes the Crown Prince went without food for hours; Tan wept beyond bearing; the emperor especially pitied him; the soldiers looked to Tan. At Lingwu the Crown Prince assumed the throne. Guangling, as eldest imperial son, wished to make Tan commander-in-chief of all armies and cavalry under Heaven. Attending ministers said: "Prince of Guangling is heir apparent and has the measure of a ruler." The emperor said: "Guangling's place is as heir designate—why borrow another as commander-in-chief?" Those beside him said: "Guangling is not yet formally invested; in hardship the people look especially to the commander-in-chief. Moreover the Crown Prince from the day he is made heir is styled Pacifier of the Army; when he guards the state he is styled Regent. Today's commander-in-chief is the Pacifier of the Army—Guangling is fitting." Thereupon Guangling was made commander-in-chief; Tan commanded the imperial guard; Li Fuguo was made vice marshal of the commander-in-chief's headquarters.
7
時張良娣有寵，倓性忠謇，因侍上屢言良娣頗自恣，輔國連結內外，欲傾動皇嗣。 自是，日為良娣、輔國所構，雲「建寧恨不得兵權，頗畜異志。」 肅宗怒，賜倓死。 既而省悟，悔之。
At that time Lady Liangdi was favored; Tan's nature was loyal and outspoken, and in attending the emperor he repeatedly spoke of Liangdi's self-indulgence; Fuguo linked inside and outside, intending to overturn the heir. From then on, he was daily slandered by Liangdi and Fuguo, who spread word that "Prince of Jianning resented not holding military authority and secretly harbored divergent ambitions." Suzong was angered and ordered Tan to death. Afterward he came to his senses and regretted it.
8
明年冬，廣平王收復兩京，遣判官李泌入朝獻捷。 泌與上有東宮之舊，從容語及建寧事，肅宗改容謂泌曰：「倓於艱難時實得氣力，無故為下人之所間，欲圖害其兄，朕以社稷大計，割愛而為之所也。」 泌對曰：「爾時臣在河西，豈不知其故。 廣平兄弟，天倫篤睦，至今廣平言及建寧，則嗚咽不已。 陛下之言，出於讒口也。」 帝因泣下曰：「事已及此，無如之何！」 泌因奏曰：「臣幼稚時念《黃臺瓜辭》，陛下嘗聞其說乎？ 高宗大帝有八子，睿宗最幼。 天後所生四子，自為行第，故睿宗第四。 長曰孝敬皇帝，為太子監國，而仁明孝悌。 天後方圖臨朝，乃鴆殺孝敬，立雍王賢為太子。 賢每日憂惕，知必不保全，與二弟同侍於父母之側，無由敢言。 乃作《黃臺瓜辭》，令樂工歌之，冀天後聞之省悟，即生哀湣。 辭云：『種瓜黃臺下，瓜熟子離離。 一摘使瓜好，再摘令瓜稀，三摘猶尚可，四摘抱蔓歸。』 而太子賢終為天後所逐，死於黔中。 陛下有今日運祚，已一摘矣，慎無再摘。」 上愕然曰：「公安得有是言！」 時廣平王立大功，亦為張皇后所忌，潛構流言，泌因事諷動之。
The next winter, Prince of Guangling recovered the two capitals and sent judge Li Bi to court to present the victory. Bi had old ties with the emperor from the Eastern Palace; speaking at ease of Prince of Jianning, Suzong changed countenance and said to Bi: "Tan truly gave strength in the time of hardship; for no reason he was slandered by men beneath him, said to be plotting harm to his elder brother; for the great plan of the state, I severed affection and did what had to be done." Bi replied: "At that time your servant was in Hexi—how could I not know the reason? The brothers of Guangling were bound by natural affection; even now when Guangling speaks of Jianning he sobs without cease. Your Majesty's words issue from slanderous mouths." The emperor wept and said: "It has come to this; nothing can be done!" Bi then memorialized: "When your servant was young I recited the Melon-Picking Poem at the Yellow Terrace; has Your Majesty ever heard its story? Emperor Gaozong had eight sons; Ruizong was youngest. The four sons born to Empress Tian arranged their own order among brothers; thus Ruizong was fourth. The eldest was Emperor Xiaojing, who as Crown Prince regent was benevolent, bright, filial, and brotherly. Empress Tian was then plotting to hold court; she poisoned Xiaojing and made Prince of Yong Xian crown prince. Xian daily lived in dread, knowing he could not be preserved; with his two younger brothers he attended their parents' side and dared not speak. He composed the Melon-Picking Poem at the Yellow Terrace and had musicians sing it, hoping Empress Tian would hear and awaken, and pity would arise. The poem runs: 'Melons are planted beneath the Yellow Terrace; when ripe the fruits hang thick. One picking leaves the melons fair; a second thins them; a third is yet tolerable; a fourth leaves only the vine in one's arms.' Yet Crown Prince Xian was finally driven out by Empress Tian and died in Qianzhong. Your Majesty's fortune today has already had one picking—take care there is no second." The emperor started and said: "How can you, sir, speak such words!" At that time Prince of Guangling had achieved great merit and was also envied by Empress Zhang; hidden slander was woven; Bi used the occasion to admonish and move him.
9
及代宗即位，深思建寧之冤，追贈齊王。 大歷三年五月，詔曰：「故齊王倓，承天祚之慶，保鴻名之光。 降誌尊賢，高才好學，藝文博洽，智略宏通。 斷必知來，謀皆先事，識無不達，理至逾精。 乃者寇盜橫流，鑾輿南幸。 先聖以宸扆之戀，將侍君親； 惟王以宗廟之重，誓寧家國。 克協朕誌，載符天時，立辨群議之非，同獻五原之計。 中興之盛，實藉奇功。 景命不融，早從厚穸，天倫之愛，震惕良深。 流涕追封，胙於東海，頃加表飾，未極哀榮。 夫以參舊邦再造之勤，成天下一家之業，而存未峻其等，歿未尊其稱，非所以旌徽烈，明至公也。 朕以眇身，纘膺大寶，不及讓王之禮，莫申太弟之嗣，所懷靡殫，邈想逾切，非常之命，寵錫攸宜。 敬用追謚曰承天皇帝，與興信公主第十四女張氏冥婚，謚曰恭順皇后。 有司準式，擇日冊命，改葬於順陵，仍祔於奉天皇帝廟，同殿異室焉。」
When Daizong took the throne, he deeply pondered Jianning's injustice and posthumously enfeoffed him as Prince of Qi. In the fifth month of Dali 3 (768), an edict said: "The late Prince of Qi Tan received Heaven's felicitous blessing and preserved the great name's radiance. He lowered his will to honor the worthy, was lofty in talent and loved learning; his arts and learning were broad and harmonious, his wisdom and stratagem grand and penetrating. In decision he knew what was coming; in planning he always preceded events; in insight nothing was not reached; in principle he went ever deeper. Then when bandits and robbers flowed across the land, the imperial carriage went south. The former sage, from love of the imperial seat, was about to serve lord and parent; only the prince, from the weight of the ancestral temple, swore to give the state and house peace. He accorded with my will and matched Heaven's time; he promptly distinguished the error of the many opinions and together offered the plan of the Five Plains. The splendor of restoration truly relied on his singular merit. His bright mandate did not fuse; early he followed the deep tomb; love within the natural bond shook and grieved the good deeply. With flowing tears he was posthumously enfeoffed in Donghai; shortly after, honors were added, yet the utmost of mourning glory was not reached. To share in the toil of restoring the old domain and completing the enterprise of one family under Heaven, yet in life not raise his rank and in death not exalt his title—this is not how to display heroic virtue and make utmost fairness clear. I, in my slight person, have succeeded to the great treasure; I did not attain the rites of yielding the throne; I could not declare the succession of the Grand Younger Brother; what I harbor is not exhausted; distant thought grows ever keener; an extraordinary command, favor and bestowal, is fitting. Respectfully use posthumous title Emperor Chengtian; perform posthumous marriage with the fourteenth daughter of Princess Xingxin, Lady Zhang; posthumous title Empress Gongshun. The relevant offices shall follow regulation, choose a day for investiture, reinter at Shun Mausoleum, and still enshrine at the Temple of Emperor Fengtian, same hall, separate chamber."
10
衛王佖，肅宗第四子。 天寶中，封西平郡王，授殿中監同正員。 早薨。 寶應元年五月，追贈衛王。
Prince of Wei Bi was Suzong's fourth son. During the Tianbao era, he was enfeoffed as Prince of Xiping commandery and appointed Director of the Palace Domestic Service with Honorary Rank. He died early. In the fifth month of Baoying 1 (762), he was posthumously enfeoffed as Prince of Wei.

Now equally bestow the favor of thatched altars; grand the ceremony of the sophora court; confer axe and ascend carriage—inherit this court's statute; the solidity of the wall—exert yourselves! Shu may be enfeoffed as Prince of Mu and serve as military commissioner of Lingnan, frontier supply, farming, and the five prefectures' pacification and observation commissioner; Yu may be enfeoffed as Prince of Chen and serve as military commissioner of Weibei, Bin, and Fang; Lian may be enfeoffed as Prince of En; Prince of Han Hui may serve as military commissioner of Bian, Song, and other circuits' pacification and observation commissioner; Gou may be enfeoffed as Prince of Bin; Zao may be enfeoffed as Prince of Xin and serve as military commissioner of Zhaoyi's pacification and observation commissioner; Xian may be enfeoffed as Prince of Shao; Yun may be enfeoffed as Prince of Jia; Yu may be enfeoffed as Prince of Duan; Yu may be enfeoffed as Prince of Xun; Tong may be enfeoffed as Prince of Gong; Kui may be enfeoffed as Prince of Yuan; Yi may be enfeoffed as Prince of Ya: all may also be enfeoffed as Grand Preceptor with Honorary Credentials Equal to the Three Excellencies. At that time, imperial sons old enough to wear shortened robes were all given princely titles and did not leave the palace.
33
是時，皇子勝衣者盡加王爵，不出閣。 德宗朝，述為諸王之長。 時分命中使周行天下，求訪沈太后，詔以睦王為奉迎太后使，以工部尚書喬琳副之。 貞元七年薨。
In Dezong's reign, Shu was eldest among the princes. At that time orders were separately sent for inner envoys to travel the realm seeking Empress Dowager Shen; an edict made Prince of Mu the commissioner to welcome the Empress Dowager, with Qiao Lin, Minister of Works, as deputy. He died in Zhenyuan 7 (791). Prince of Dan Yu was Daizong's fifth son.
34
丹王逾，代宗第五子。 大歷十年，封郴王，領渭北鄜坊節度大使， 改丹王。 薨。
In Dali 10 (775), he was enfeoffed as Prince of Chen and held the post of military commissioner of Weibei, Bin, and Fang. He was changed to Prince of Dan. He died. Prince of En Lian was Daizong's sixth son.
35
恩王連，代宗第六子。 大歷十年封，薨。
Enfeoffed in Dali 10 (775); he died. Prince of Han Hui was Daizong's seventh son.
36
韓王迥，代宗第七子。 以母寵，既生而受封，雖沖幼，恩在鄭王之亞， 封韓王。 薨，時年四十七。
Because his mother was favored, he received enfeoffment at birth; though still an infant, favor ranked next after Prince of Zheng. He was enfeoffed as Prince of Han. He died, aged forty-seven. Prince of Jian Gou was Daizong's eighth son.
37
簡王遘，代宗第八子。 大歷十年，封鄜王，建中四年，改封簡王。 薨。
In Dali 10 (775), he was enfeoffed as Prince of Bin; in Jianzhong 4 (783), his title was changed to Prince of Jian. He died. Prince of Yi Nai was Daizong's ninth son.
38
益王乃，代宗第九子。 大歷四年封。
Enfeoffed in Dali 4 (769). Prince of Sui Xun was Daizong's tenth son.
39
隋王迅，代宗第十子。 大歷十年封，薨。
Enfeoffed in Dali 10 (775); he died. Prince of Jing Xuan, Daizong's eleventh son, died young.
40
荊王選，代宗第十一子，早世。 正月，追封荊王，贈開府儀同三司。
In the first month, he was posthumously enfeoffed as Prince of Jing and posthumously granted Grand Preceptor with Honorary Credentials Equal to the Three Excellencies. Prince of Shu Su was Daizong's twelfth son.
41
蜀王溯，代宗第十二子。 大歷十四年封。 本名遂，建中二年改今名。
Enfeoffed in Dali 14 (779). Original name Sui; in Jianzhong 2 (781), the present name was adopted. Prince of Xin Zao was Daizong's thirteenth son.
42
忻王造，代宗第十三子。 大歷十年封，仍領昭義軍節度觀察大使。 薨。
Enfeoffed in Dali 10 (775); he still held the post of military commissioner of Zhaoyi's pacification and observation. He died. Prince of Shao Xian was Daizong's fourteenth son.
43
韶王暹，代宗第十四子。 大歷十年封，薨。
Enfeoffed in Dali 10 (775); he died. Prince of Jia Yun was Daizong's fifteenth son.
44
嘉王運，代宗第十五子。 大歷十年封，薨。
Enfeoffed in Dali 10 (775); he died. Prince of Duan Yu was Daizong's sixteenth son.
45
端王遇，代宗第十六子。 大歷十年封，薨。
Enfeoffed in Dali 10 (775); he died. Prince of Xun Yu was Daizong's seventeenth son.
46
循王遹，代宗第十七子。 大歷十年封。
Enfeoffed in Dali 10 (775). Prince of Gong Tong was Daizong's eighteenth son.
47
恭王通，代宗第十八子。 大歷十年封。
Enfeoffed in Dali 10 (775). Prince of Yuan Kui was Daizong's nineteenth son.
48
原王逵，代宗第十九子。 大歷十年封。 大和六年薨。
Enfeoffed in Dali 10 (775). He died in Taihe 6 (832). Prince of Ya Yi was Daizong's twentieth son.
49
雅王逸，代宗第二十子。 大歷十年封，薨。
Prince of Ya Yi was Daizong's twentieth son. Enfeoffed in Dali 10 (775); he died.
50
史臣曰：艷妻破國，孽子敗宗。 前代英傑之君，率不免於斯累者，何也？ 良以愛惡不由於義斷，毀譽遽逐於情移。 雖申生孝己之仁，卒不能回君父之愛，悲哉！ 孝宣皇帝當屯剝之運，收忠義之心，忍行愛子之刑，終宥奸閹之罪，大雅君子，為之痛心。 張後卒以兇終，固其宜矣。
The historiographer says: A beautiful wife destroys a state; a wicked son ruins the clan. Former ages' brilliant and heroic rulers mostly could not escape this burden—why? Truly because love and hate did not follow judgment of righteousness; ruin and praise suddenly shifted with emotion. Though Shen Sheng and Xiao Ji had the benevolence of filial sons, in the end they could not turn back a lord-father's love—alas! Emperor Xiaoxuan, when fortune was withdrawn and stripped, gathered hearts of loyalty and righteousness; he endured executing a beloved son, yet finally pardoned the crime of treacherous eunuchs—great refined gentlemen were pained at heart for this. Empress Zhang finally met a violent end—fitting indeed.
51
贊曰：床簀之愛，人情易惑。 以義制情，哲王令德。 李侯悟主，韻諧金石。 褒謚建寧，良堪太息。
The encomium says: Love of bed and mat—human feeling is easily misled. To restrain feeling by righteousness—the wise king's fine virtue. Marquis Li moved his lord; his rhyme harmonized metal and stone. Praising and posthumously titling Jianning—truly worth a deep sigh.
