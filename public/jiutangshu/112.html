杜鴻漸
Du Hongjian
2
韋見素，字會微，京兆萬年人。 父湊，開元中太原尹。 見素學科登第。 景龍中，解褐相王府參軍，歷衛佐、河南府倉曹。 丁父憂，服闋，起為大理寺丞，襲爵彭城郡公。 坐事出為坊州司馬。 入為庫部員外郎，加朝散大夫，歷右司兵部二員外，左司兵部二郎中，遷諫議大夫。 天寶五年，充江西、山南、黔中、嶺南等黜陟使，觀省風俗，彈糾長吏，所至肅然。 使還，拜給事中，駁正繩違，頗振臺閣舊典。 尋檢校尚書工部侍郎，改右丞。 九載，遷吏部侍郎，加銀青光祿大夫。 見素仁恕長者，意不忤物，及典選累年，銓敘平允，人士稱之。 時右相楊國忠用事，左相陳希烈畏其權寵，凡事唯諾，無敢發明，玄宗頗知之，聖情不悅。 天寶十三年秋，霖雨六十餘日，京師廬舍垣墉頹毀殆盡，凡一十九坊汙潦。 天子以宰輔或未稱職，見此咎征，命楊國忠精求端士，時兵部侍郎吉溫方承寵遇，上意用之。 國忠以溫祿山賓佐，懼其威權，奏寢其事。 國忠訪於中書舍人竇華、宋昱等，華、昱言見素方雅，柔而易制。 上亦以經事相王府，有舊恩，可之。 其年八月，拜武部尚書、同中書門下平章事，充集賢院學士，知門下省事，代陳希烈。 見素既為國忠引用，心德之。 時祿山與國忠爭寵，兩相猜嫌，見素亦無所是非，署字而已，遂至凶胡犯順，不措一言。
Wei Jiansu, whose courtesy name was Huiwei, came from Wannian in the Jingzhao metropolitan district. His father Cou had served as mayor of Taiyuan during the Kaiyuan reign. Jiansu earned his jinshi degree in the Classics track. During Jinglong he entered service as an aide in the household of the Prince of Xiang, then held posts as a guard officer and as a grain clerk in Henan prefecture. After his father's death he observed the mourning period, then returned to office as a deputy in the Court of Judicial Review and inherited the title Duke of Pengcheng. He was dismissed from the capital for an offense and appointed military aide at Fang prefecture. He returned to the capital as an outside director in the Storehouse Ministry, received the sinecure title Grand Master of Palace Leisure, served successively as outside director and vice director in the war ministries of both the right and left courts, and was promoted to Remonstrance Grandee. In Tianbao 5 he was appointed touring inspector for promotion and demotion across Jiangxi, Shannan, Qianzhong, Lingnan, and other circuits. He surveyed local customs, impeached long-serving officials, and everywhere he went discipline returned. On his return he was made an Imperial Attendant, where he corrected irregular edicts and helped restore the old standards of the Secretariat and Chancellery. He was soon made acting Vice Minister of Works, then transferred to Right Vice Director of the Secretariat. In year 9 of Tianbao he was promoted to Vice Minister of Personnel and granted the rank Grand Master with Silver and Blue Girdle. Jiansu was by nature a forgiving man of senior bearing who never set himself against others. During his many years in charge of personnel selection his rankings were fair and even, and men of standing spoke well of him. The right chancellor Yang Guozhong held real power at court, and the left chancellor Chen Xilie, fearing Yang's influence, assented to everything and never dared speak his mind plainly. Xuanzong knew this and was displeased. In the autumn of Tianbao 13 rain fell for more than sixty days. In the capital almost every dwelling and compound wall collapsed, and nineteen wards stood under filthy floodwater. The emperor, taking the floods as a sign that his chief ministers might not be fit for their posts, ordered Yang Guozhong to search diligently for upright men. Vice Minister of War Ji Wen was then in favor at court, and the emperor meant to appoint him. Guozhong, knowing that Wen had once served as a staff officer to An Lushan, feared his growing power and memorialized to drop the appointment. Guozhong consulted the Secretariat drafters Dou Hua and Song Yu, who said that Jiansu was upright and cultured, gentle, and easy to manage. The emperor also approved the choice, recalling that Jiansu had long served in the household of the Prince of Xiang and still owed him old kindness. In the eighth month of that year he was appointed Minister of War and made a chief councilor, with concurrent duties as an academician of the Hall of Assembled Worthies and charge of Secretariat business, replacing Chen Xilie. Jiansu, having been promoted by Guozhong's patronage, felt deeply obliged to him. An Lushan and Guozhong were then contending for imperial favor and eyeing each other with suspicion. Jiansu too offered no judgment on either side and merely countersigned documents, until the rebel barbarian rose in defiance and he never spoke a word against it.
3
十五年六月，哥舒翰兵敗桃林，潼關不守。 是月，玄宗蒼黃出幸，莫知所詣。 楊國忠以身領劍南旄鉞，請幸成都。 見素與國忠、御史大夫魏方進遇上於延秋門，便扈從之咸陽。 翌日，次馬嵬驛，軍士不得食，流言不遜。 龍武將軍陳玄禮懼其亂，乃與飛龍馬家李護國謀於皇太子，請誅國忠，以慰士心。 是日，玄禮等禁軍圍行宮，盡誅楊氏。 見素遁走，為亂兵所傷，眾呼曰：「勿傷韋相！ 」識者救之，獲免。 上聞之，令壽王瑁宣慰，賜藥傅瘡。 魏方進為亂兵所殺。 是日，朝士獨見素一人。 是夜宿馬嵬，上命見素子京兆府司錄參軍諤為御史中丞，充置頓使。 淩晨將發，六軍將士曰：「國忠反叛，不可更往蜀川，請之河、隴。 」或言靈武、太原，或云還京，議者不一。 上意在劍南，慮違士心，無所言。 諤曰：「還京須有捍賊之備。 今兵馬數少，恐非萬全，不如且至扶風，徐圖去就。 」上詢於眾，眾以為然，乃令皇太子後殿。
In the sixth month of year 15, Geshu Han's army was defeated at Taolin and Tong Pass fell. That same month Xuanzong fled the capital in panic, with no clear destination in mind. Yang Guozhong, who himself held military command in Jiannan, urged the emperor to take refuge in Chengdu. Jiansu met the emperor at Yanqiu Gate together with Guozhong and Censor-in-Chief Wei Fangjin, then escorted him to Xianyang. The next day they halted at Mawei Station. The troops had no food, and insolent rumors spread through the ranks. Longwu General Chen Xuanli, fearing a mutiny, plotted with Li Huguo of the Flying Dragon Horse Household and asked the crown prince to have Guozhong killed in order to calm the army. That day Xuanli and the imperial guard surrounded the traveling palace and killed the entire Yang clan. Jiansu fled and was wounded by the mutinous troops. The crowd shouted, "Do not harm Chancellor Wei! " Those who knew him pulled him free, and he escaped death. When the emperor heard of this he sent Prince Shou Wang Mao to comfort him and gave him medicine for his wounds. Wei Fangjin was killed by the mutinous soldiers. That day Jiansu was the only court official still in attendance. That night they lodged at Mawei. The emperor appointed Jiansu's son E, who was a recorder in the Jingzhao prefectural office, vice censor-in-chief and commissioner for arranging the journey's supplies. At dawn, just as they were preparing to depart, the officers and men of the Six Armies said, "Guozhong has rebelled. We cannot continue on to Shu. Take us instead to the He and Longyou regions. " Some urged Lingwu or Taiyuan, others a return to the capital, and the advisers could not agree. The emperor still intended to go south to Jiannan but, fearing to defy the army's will, said nothing. E said, "A return to the capital would require adequate defenses against the rebels. Our force is now too small to make that safe. It would be better to go first to Fufeng and decide our next move from there. " The emperor asked the group, and all agreed. He then ordered the crown prince to bring up the rear.
4
上至扶風郡，從駕諸軍各圖去就，頗出醜言。 陳玄禮不能制，上聞之憂懼。 會益州貢春彩十萬疋，乃以其綱使濛陽尉劉景溫為監察御史，其彩悉陳於廷，召六軍將士等入，上謂之曰：「卿等皆國之功臣，勛勞素著，朕之優賞，常亦不輕。 逆胡負恩，事須回避，甚知卿等不得別父母妻子，朕亦不及辭九廟。 」言發涕流。 又曰：「朕今須幸蜀，蜀路險狹，人若多往，恐難祗供。 今有此彩，卿等即宜分取，各自圖去就。 朕自有子弟、中官等相隨，便與卿等訣別。 」眾咸俯伏號泣，曰：「死生從陛下。 」上良久曰：「去住聽卿自便。 」自是醜言方息。 七月，至巴西郡，以見素兼左相、武部尚書。 數日，至蜀郡，加金紫光祿大夫，進封豳國公，與一子五品官。
When the emperor reached Fufeng commandery, the various units in his escort began plotting their own departures, and ugly talk spread through the ranks. Chen Xuanli could not control them. When the emperor heard of it he grew anxious and fearful. Just then Yi prefecture had sent one hundred thousand bolts of spring-colored silk as tribute. The emperor appointed the convoy leader, Mengyang magistrate Liu Jingwen, investigating censor, had all the silk displayed in the courtyard, and summoned the officers and soldiers of the Six Armies. He told them, "You are all proven servants of the state, men whose merit is long established, and my rewards to you have never been small. The rebel has betrayed every obligation, and we must withdraw. I know full well that you cannot leave your parents, wives, and children behind — and I myself have not even had time to bid farewell at the ancestral shrines. " As he spoke, tears streamed down his face. He went on, "I must now go to Shu. The roads there are narrow and perilous, and if too many of you come along I fear there will not be enough to sustain us all. Here is this silk. Take your share now and decide for yourselves where you will go. I will keep my sons, younger kinsmen, palace eunuchs, and a few others with me. Let us part here. " The men all prostrated themselves, weeping, and cried, "In life or in death we follow Your Majesty. " After a long silence the emperor said, "Stay or go as you please. " From then on the ugly talk died away. In the seventh month they reached Baxi commandery, where Jiansu was given concurrent appointment as left chancellor and minister of war. A few days later they reached Shu commandery. Jiansu was granted the rank Grand Master with Golden Purple Girdle, advanced to Duke of Bin, and given a fifth-grade commission for one son.
5
是月，皇太子即位於靈武，道路艱澀，音驛未通。 八月，肅宗使至，始知靈武即位。 尋命見素與宰臣房琯賫傳國寶玉冊奉使靈武，宣傳詔命，便行冊禮。 將行，上皇謂見素等曰：「皇帝自幼仁孝，與諸子有異，朕豈不知。 往十三年，已有傳位之意，屬其歲水旱，左右勸朕且俟豐年。 爾來便屬祿山構逆，方隅震擾，未遂此心。 昨發馬嵬，亦有處分。 今皇帝受命，朕心頓如釋負。 勞卿等遠去，勉輔佐之。 多難興王，自古皆有，卿等乃心王室，以宗社為念，早定中原，吾之望也。 」見素等悲泣不自勝。 仍以見素子諤及中書舍人賈至充冊禮使判官。 時肅宗已回幸順化郡。 九月，見素等至，冊禮畢，從幸彭原郡。 肅宗在東宮，素聞房琯名重，故虛懷以待； 以見素常附國忠，禮遇稍薄。 明年，至鳳翔。 三月，除左僕射，罷知政事，以憲部尚書致仕。 苗晉卿代為左相。
That month the crown prince took the throne at Lingwu, but the roads were difficult and courier routes had not yet been restored. In the eighth month an envoy from Suzong arrived, and they first learned of the enthronement at Lingwu. Soon Jiansu and chief minister Fang Guan were ordered to carry the imperial heirloom seal and jade book to Lingwu, proclaim the abdication edict, and perform the investiture ceremony. As they prepared to depart, the retired emperor told Jiansu and the others, "The emperor has been benevolent and filial since childhood, unlike my other sons. Do you think I did not know that? As early as thirteen years ago I had intended to abdicate, but that year brought flood and drought, and my advisers urged me to wait for a better harvest. Since then An Lushan's rebellion has shaken the realm, and that wish has never been fulfilled. Even when we left Mawei yesterday, I had already begun to arrange it. Now that the emperor has received the mandate, a weight has lifted from my heart. I am sending you far from me. Do your utmost to assist him. Kings who rise through hardship are known in every age. Keep the royal house in your hearts, think of the altars of state, and bring the Central Plains under order as soon as you can. That is my hope. " Jiansu and the others wept until they could bear no more. Jiansu's son E and Secretariat drafter Jia Zhi were also appointed assistant commissioners for the investiture mission. By then Suzong had already moved his court to Shunhua commandery. In the ninth month Jiansu and his party arrived. After the investiture ceremony they accompanied Suzong to Pengyuan commandery. While still crown prince, Suzong had long heard of Fang Guan's great reputation and received him with open respect; but because Jiansu had long been attached to Guozhong, his reception was somewhat cooler. The following year they reached Fengxiang. In the third month he was made left vice director of the Department of State Affairs, relieved of governmental duties, and retired with the title minister of justice. Miao Jinqing replaced him as left chancellor.
6
初，肅宗在鳳翔，喪亂之後，綱紀未立，兵吏三銓，簿籍煨燼，南曹選人，文符悉多偽濫。 上以凶醜未滅，且示招懷，據到註擬，一無檢括。 見素曰：「臣典選歲久，周知此弊。 今寰區未復，員闕不多。 若總無條綱，恐難持久。 」上然之，未暇厘革。 及還京，選人數千，補授無所，喧訴於朝，由是行見素之言。 及房琯以敗軍左降，崔圓、崔渙等皆罷知政事，上皇所命宰臣，無知政事者。 五月，遷見素太子太師。 十一月，肅宗自右輔還京，詔見素入蜀奉迎太上皇。 十二月，上皇至京師，肅宗禦樓大赦。 見素以奉上皇幸蜀功，加開府儀同三司，食實封三百戶。 上元中，以足疾上表請致仕，許之。 寶應元年十二月卒，年七十六，贈司空，謚曰忠貞，喪事官給。 子倜、諤、益、丱。 倜、諤皆位至給事中，益終刑部員外郎，丱終秘書丞。 倜子頌。
Earlier, when Suzong was at Fengxiang after the upheaval, government order had not yet been restored. The three personnel registers of the war and civil ministries had been burned to ash. Candidates at the Southern Bureau presented documents that were for the most part forged. Because the rebel leader had not yet been destroyed, the emperor wished to show outreach and accepted appointments on the basis of whatever documents arrived, without any review at all. Jiansu said, "Your servant has managed personnel selection for many years and knows this abuse well. The realm is not yet restored, and vacancies are still few. If we have no regulations at all, I fear this cannot last. " The emperor agreed but had no time yet to reform the system. When the court returned to the capital, several thousand candidates had nowhere to be placed and raised a clamor at court. On that account Jiansu's advice was put into practice. When Fang Guan was demoted for military defeat, Cui Yuan, Cui Huan, and others were all relieved of governmental duties. Among the chief ministers the retired emperor had appointed, none remained in charge of government. In the fifth month Jiansu was appointed Grand Tutor of the Crown Prince. In the eleventh month Suzong returned to the capital from the right metropolitan region and ordered Jiansu to go into Shu to welcome the retired emperor. In the twelfth month the retired emperor reached the capital, and Suzong proclaimed a great amnesty from the tower. For his service escorting the retired emperor to Shu, Jiansu was granted the title Pillar of State with Triple Honors and an actual fief of three hundred households. During the Shangyuan era he memorialized, citing a foot ailment, to request retirement, and permission was granted. He died in the twelfth month of Baoying 1 at the age of seventy-six. He was posthumously made Minister of Works, given the posthumous title Loyal and Upright, and his funeral expenses were supplied by the state. His sons were Tong, E, Yi, and Guan. Tong and E both rose to the post of Imperial Attendant; Yi ended his career as an outside director in the Ministry of Punishment; Guan ended as assistant director of the Secretariat. Tong's son was Song.
7
益子顗，字周人，生一歲而孤，事姊稱為恭孝。 性嗜學，尤精陰陽、象緯、經略、風俗之書。 善持論，有清譽。 少以門廕補千牛備身，自鄠縣尉判入等，授萬年尉，歷御史、補闕、尚書郎，累遷給事中、尚書左丞、戶部侍郎、中丞、吏部侍郎。 其在諫垣，與李約、李正辭叠申裨諷，頗回大政。 宰相裴垍、李絳、崔群輩多與友善，而後進之有浮名者，亦遊其門，以是稱有時望。 及李逢吉殲朋黨以專政柄，而顗附麗之跡尤密，頗為時人所譏。 然處身儉約，有足多者。 著《易蘊解》，推演潛亢終始之義，甚有奧旨。 寶歷元年七月卒，贈禮部尚書。
Yi's son Yan, whose courtesy name was Zhouren, was orphaned at the age of one. In caring for his elder sister he was known for respectful filial devotion. He loved learning by nature and was especially versed in works on yin and yang, astral omens, statecraft, and local customs. He was skilled in debate and enjoyed a reputation for integrity. In youth he entered service through hereditary privilege as an attendant of the Thousand Oxen Guard. After distinguished service as magistrate of Huo county he was appointed magistrate of Wannian, then served successively as censor, remonstrator, and Secretariat gentleman. He rose through the ranks to Imperial Attendant, left vice director of the Department of State Affairs, vice minister of revenue, censor-in-chief, and vice minister of personnel. While serving in the remonstrance bureau, he and Li Yue and Li Zhengchen repeatedly offered corrective remonstrance and materially influenced major policy. Chancellors Pei Ji, Li Jiang, Cui Qun, and others were largely on friendly terms with him, and ambitious younger men with inflated reputations also sought his patronage. For this he was regarded as a man of standing in his time. When Li Fengji destroyed rival factions to monopolize power, Yan's closeness to him was especially evident, and he was widely ridiculed by contemporaries. Yet in his personal conduct he was frugal and abstemious, which was much to his credit. He wrote Explication of the Stored in the Changes, expounding the meaning of hidden and dominant phases from beginning to end, with considerable depth of insight. He died in the seventh month of Baoli 1 and was posthumously made Minister of Rites.
8
崔圓，清河東武城人也。 後魏左僕射亮之後。 父景晊，官至大理評事。 圓少孤貧，誌尚閎博，好讀兵書，有經濟宇宙之心。 開元中，詔搜訪遺逸，圓以鈐謀射策甲科，授執戟。 自負文藝，獲武職，頗不得意。 蕭炅為京兆尹，薦為會昌丞，累遷司勛員外郎。 宰臣楊國忠遙制劍南節度使，引圓佐理，乃奏授尚書郎，兼蜀郡大都督府左司馬，知節度留後。 天寶末，玄宗幸蜀郡，特遷蜀郡大都督府長史、劍南節度。 圓素懷功名，初聞國難，潛使人探國忠深旨，知有行幸之計，乃增修城池，建置館宇，儲備什器。 及乘輿至，殿宇牙帳咸如宿設，玄宗甚嗟賞之，即日拜中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事、劍南節度，余如故。
Cui Yuan came from Dongwucheng in Qinghe commandery. He was descended from Liang, who had served as left vice director under the Northern Wei. His father Jingzhi rose to the post of judicial reviewer in the Court of Judicial Review. Yuan was orphaned and poor in youth, but his ambitions were broad. He loved reading military texts and dreamed of putting the realm in order. During Kaiyuan the court sought out hidden talent. Yuan passed the military-strategy examination in the top grade and was appointed Halberd Bearer. Confident in his literary gifts but given a military post, he was deeply dissatisfied. When Xiao Jiong was mayor of Jingzhao he recommended Yuan as assistant magistrate of Huichang. Yuan was later promoted to outside director in the Ministry of Merit Records. Chief minister Yang Guozhong controlled the Jiannan military commissioner from afar and brought Yuan in to assist him. He memorialized for Yuan to be appointed Secretariat gentleman, with concurrent duties as left military aide of the Shu metropolitan prefecture and acting military commissioner of the circuit. At the end of Tianbao, when Xuanzong visited Shu commandery, Yuan was specially promoted to chief administrator of the Shu metropolitan prefecture and military commissioner of Jiannan. Yuan had long coveted fame and achievement. When he first heard of the national crisis he secretly sent men to learn Guozhong's deeper intentions, discovered that an imperial progress to Shu was planned, and then enlarged the city walls, built lodges and halls, and stockpiled equipment and supplies. When the imperial carriage arrived, the halls and military encampments looked as though they had been prepared long in advance. Xuanzong was deeply impressed and that same day appointed Yuan vice director of the Secretariat, chief councilor, and military commissioner of Jiannan, with his other duties unchanged.
9
肅宗即位，玄宗命圓同房琯、韋見素並赴肅宗行在所，玄宗親制遺愛碑於蜀以寵之。 從肅宗還京，以功拜中書令，封趙國公，賜實封五百戶。 明年，罷知政事，遷太子少師，留守東都。 會官軍不利於相州，軍回過洛陽，所在剽掠。 圓棄城南奔襄陽，詔削除階封。 尋起為濟王傅。 李光弼用為懷州刺史，除太子詹事，改汾州刺史，皆以理行稱。 拜揚州大都督府長史、淮南節度觀察使，加檢校右僕射、兼御史大夫，轉檢校左僕射知省事。 大曆三年六月薨，年六十四，輟朝三日，贈太子太師，謚曰昭襄。
When Suzong took the throne, Xuanzong ordered Yuan, together with Fang Guan and Wei Jiansu, to proceed to Suzong's traveling court. Xuanzong personally composed a stele of enduring affection in Shu to honor him. When he followed Suzong back to the capital, he was appointed director of the Secretariat for his service, enfeoffed as Duke of Zhao, and granted an actual fief of five hundred households. The following year he was relieved of governmental duties, appointed junior tutor of the crown prince, and left to guard the eastern capital. When the government army suffered defeat at Xiang prefecture, the troops passed back through Luoyang and plundered wherever they went. Yuan abandoned the city and fled south to Xiangyang. An edict stripped him of his graded enfeoffment. He was soon recalled and appointed tutor to the Prince of Ji. Li Guangbi appointed him prefect of Huai prefecture. He was later made grand mentor of the heir apparent and then transferred to prefect of Fen prefecture. In each post he was praised for orderly governance. He was appointed chief administrator of the Yangzhou metropolitan prefecture and military commissioner and observer of Huainan. He was given the additional titles of acting right vice director and concurrent censor-in-chief, then transferred to acting left vice director with charge of Secretariat business. He died in the sixth month of Dali 3 at the age of sixty-four. Court was suspended for three days. He was posthumously made grand tutor of the crown prince and given the posthumous title Illustrious and Assisting.
10
崔渙，祖玄暐，神龍功臣，封博陵郡王。 父璩，文學知名，位至禮部侍郎。 渙少以士行聞，博綜經籍，尤善談論，累遷尚書司門員外郎。 天寶末，楊國忠出不附己者，渙出為劍州刺史。 天寶十五載七月，玄宗幸蜀，渙迎謁於路，抗詞忠懇，皆究理體，玄宗嘉之，以為得渙晚。 宰臣房琯又薦之，即日拜黃門侍郎、同中書門下平章事，扈從成都府。
Cui Huan's grandfather Xuanwei had been a meritorious servant of the Shenlong restoration and was enfeoffed as Duke of Boling. His father Jun was renowned for literary accomplishment and rose to vice minister of rites. Huan was known from youth for exemplary conduct. He mastered the classics broadly and was especially skilled in debate. He rose to outside director in the Gatekeeping Ministry of the Department of State Affairs. At the end of Tianbao, Yang Guozhong removed those who would not attach themselves to him, and Huan was sent out as prefect of Jian prefecture. In the seventh month of Tianbao 15, when Xuanzong traveled to Shu, Huan met him on the road and spoke with frank loyal earnestness on matters of principle. Xuanzong praised him and lamented that he had found Huan too late. Chief minister Fang Guan also recommended him. That same day Huan was appointed vice director of the Yellow Gate and chief councilor, and accompanied the court to Chengdu.
11
肅宗靈武即位。 八月，與左相韋見素、同平章事房琯、崔圓同賫冊赴行在。 時未復京師，舉選路絕，詔渙充江淮宣諭選補使，以收遺逸。 惑於聽受，為下吏所鬻，濫進者非一，以不稱職聞。 乃罷知政事，除左散騎常侍，兼余杭太守、江東采訪防禦使。 旋授正議大夫、太子賓客。 乾元三年正月，轉大理卿。 再遷吏部侍郎、檢校工部尚書、集賢院待詔。 性尚簡淡，不交世務，頗為時望所歸。 遷御史大夫，加稅地青苗錢物使。 時以此錢充給京百官料，渙為屬吏希中，以下估為使料，上估為百官料。 其時為皇城副留守張清發之，詔下有司訊鞫，渙無詞以對，坐是貶道州刺史。 大曆三年十二月壬寅，以疾終。
Suzong took the throne at Lingwu. In the eighth month he went with left chancellor Wei Jiansu, chief councilor Fang Guan, and Cui Yuan to carry the investiture book to the traveling court. The capital had not yet been restored and the examination routes were cut off. Huan was ordered to serve as envoy to proclaim edicts and select candidates in the Jiang and Huai regions, in order to gather overlooked talent. Confused by what he heard, he was manipulated by subordinate officials. Improper promotions were numerous, and he became known as unfit for his post. He was then relieved of governmental duties and appointed left regular attendant of the cavalry, with concurrent posts as grand protector of Yuhang and commissioner for investigation and defense in Jiangdong. He was soon granted the title Grand Master of Corrective Discussion and appointed guest of the heir apparent. In the first month of Qianyuan 3 he was transferred to director of the Court of Judicial Review. He was promoted again to vice minister of personnel, acting minister of works, and awaiting edicts at the Hall of Assembled Worthies. By nature he favored simplicity and detachment and did not involve himself in worldly affairs. He enjoyed considerable standing among his contemporaries. He was promoted to censor-in-chief and given the additional post of commissioner for green-sprout tax revenues on taxed land. At the time this money was used to pay the salaries of capital officials. To please his subordinates, Huan used the low valuation for his own commissioner's supplies and the high valuation for officials' salaries. Vice protector of the imperial city Zhang Qing exposed the matter. An edict ordered the relevant offices to investigate. Huan had no defense and was demoted to prefect of Dao prefecture. On the renyin day of the twelfth month of Dali 3 he died of illness.
12
子縱，初以廕補協律郎，三遷為監察御史。 詔擇令長於臺省，除藍田令，寬明勤幹，德化大行，縣人為之立碑頌德。 轉京兆府司錄，累遷金部員外郎。 以父貶道州刺史，棄官就養。 丁父憂，終制，六遷大理卿、兼御史中丞、汴西水陸運兩稅鹽鐵等使。 田悅連敗，走魏州，嬰城自守，諸道兵圍之，屢乏食，詔縱兼魏州四節度糧料使，軍儲稍給。 德宗幸奉天，四方握兵，未有至者。 縱先知之，潛告李懷光勸令奔命，懷光從之。 縱乃悉斂軍財與懷光俱來，調給具備。 懷光兵士久戰河外，及次河中，將遷延。 縱之貨幣先已渡河，縱謂眾曰：「若濟，悉以分賜。 」眾利之，乃西。 至奉天，加右庶子，充使。 無幾，拜京兆尹、兼御史大夫。 數奏懷光剛愎反覆，宜陰備之。 及行幸梁州，左右或短之曰：「縱素善懷光，今不來矣。 」上曰：「他人不知縱，吾可保其心。 」不數日，縱至，拜御史大夫。 嘗議其大體，不親細事，獄訴儀制，皆付之僚吏。
His son Zong entered service through hereditary privilege as harmonizing regulation officer and was promoted three times to investigating censor. An edict called for selecting magistrates from the central offices. Zong was appointed magistrate of Lantian, where he governed with breadth, clarity, diligence, and ability. Moral transformation spread widely, and the county people erected a stele praising his virtue. He was transferred to recorder of Jingzhao prefecture and later promoted to outside director in the Ministry of Revenue. When his father was demoted to prefect of Dao prefecture, he resigned his office to go and care for him. After his father's death he observed the full mourning period, then rose through six promotions to director of the Court of Judicial Review, concurrent vice censor-in-chief, and commissioner for Bianxi water and land transport, two-tax revenues, salt, iron, and related matters. Tian Yue suffered repeated defeats and fled to Wei prefecture, where he shut himself inside the city walls. Armies from several circuits besieged him but repeatedly ran short of food. Zong was ordered to serve concurrently as grain commissioner for the four military commissioners at Wei, and army supplies were gradually restored. When Dezong went to Fengtian, none of the regional commanders holding armies in the four directions had yet arrived. Zong learned of the situation first and secretly urged Li Huaiguang to hurry to the emperor's aid. Huaiguang complied. Zong then gathered all military funds and came with Huaiguang, bringing fully prepared supplies and distributions. Huaiguang's soldiers had fought for a long time beyond the Yellow River. When they halted at Hezhong they were inclined to delay further. Zong's money and goods had already crossed the river ahead of the army. He told the troops, "If we cross, I will divide it all among you. " The troops were enticed by the promise and marched west. When they reached Fengtian he was given the additional post of right subordinate of the heir apparent and appointed commissioner. Before long he was appointed mayor of Jingzhao and concurrent censor-in-chief. He repeatedly memorialized that Huaiguang was obstinate, willful, and unreliable, and that secret preparations should be made against him. When the emperor traveled to Liang prefecture, those around him sometimes spoke against Zong, saying, "Zong has long been friendly with Huaiguang. He will not come now. " The emperor said, "Others do not know Zong as I do. I can vouch for his loyalty. " Within a few days Zong arrived and was appointed censor-in-chief. He concerned himself only with larger principles and did not attend to minor matters personally. Lawsuits, petitions, and ritual regulations were all entrusted to his subordinate officials.
13
縱孝悌，修飭自立，以父為元載排抑，居退十餘年，左宦外府，訖載得罪，不求聞達。 初，渙有寵妾鄭氏，縱以母事之。 鄭氏性剛戾，待縱不以理，雖為大僚，每加笞詬。 縱率妻子候顏，敬順不懈，時以為難。
Zong was filial and brotherly, cultivated restraint, and stood on his own. Because his father had been suppressed by Yuan Zai, he lived in retirement for more than ten years and served in demoted posts in outer prefectures. He sought no fame or advancement until Zai was punished. Early on Huan had a favored concubine, Lady Zheng, whom Zong treated as his mother. Lady Zheng was harsh and perverse by nature. She treated Zong unreasonably and, though he was a high official, often beat and reviled him. Zong led his wife and children to attend her with respectful compliance and never slackened. Men of the time regarded this as a difficult virtue.
14
杜鴻漸，故相暹之族子。 祖慎行，益州長史。 父鵬舉，官至王友。 鴻漸敏悟好學，舉進士，解褐王府參軍。 天寶末，累遷大理司直，朔方留後、支度副使。
Du Hongjian was a clansman of the former chancellor Du Xian. His grandfather Shenxing served as chief administrator of Yi prefecture. His father Pengju rose to the post of friend of a prince. Hongjian was quick-witted and loved learning. He passed the jinshi examination and entered service as an aide in a prince's household. At the end of Tianbao he rose to judicial rectifier in the Court of Judicial Review, acting military commissioner of Shuofang, and vice commissioner for expenditures.
15
肅宗北幸，至平涼，未知所適。 鴻漸與六城水運使魏少遊、節度判官崔漪、支度判官盧簡金、關內鹽池判官李涵謀曰：「今胡羯亂常，二京陷沒，主上南幸於巴蜀，皇太子理兵於平涼。 然平涼散地，非聚兵之處，必欲制勝，非朔方不可。 若奉殿下，旬日之間，西收河、隴，回紇方強，與國通好，北征勁騎，南集諸城，大兵一舉，可復二京。 雪社稷之恥，上報明主，下安蒼生，亦臣子之用心，國家之大計也。 」鴻漸即日草箋具陳兵馬招集之勢，錄軍資、器械、倉儲、庫物之數，令李涵賫赴平涼，肅宗大悅。 鴻漸知肅宗發平涼，於北界白草頓迎謁，因勞諸使及兵士，進言曰：「朔方天下勁兵，靈州用武之處。 今回紇請和，吐蕃內附，天下郡邑，人皆堅守，以待制命。 其中雖為賊所據，亦望不日收復，殿下整理軍戎，長驅一舉，則逆胡不足滅也。 」肅宗然之。 及至靈武，鴻漸與裴冕等勸即皇帝位，以歸中外之望，五上表，乃從。 鴻漸素習帝王陳布之儀，君臣朝見之禮，遂采摭舊儀，綿蕝其事。 城南設壇壝，先一日具儀註草奏。 肅宗曰：「聖君在遠，寇逆未平，宜罷壇場。 」余可其奏。 肅宗即位，授兵部郎中，知中書舍人事，尋轉武部侍郎。 至德二年，兼御史大夫，為河西節度使、涼州都督。 兩京平，遷荊州大都督府長史、荊南節度使。
When Suzong traveled north he reached Pingliang without knowing where to go next. Hongjian plotted with Wei Shaoyou, commissioner for water transport of the Six Cities, military commissioner judge Cui Yi, expenditure judge Lu Jianjin, and Guannei salt-pond judge Li Han. He said, "The barbarians have thrown the realm into disorder, the Two Capitals have fallen, the sovereign has fled south to Ba and Shu, and the crown prince is organizing troops at Pingliang. But Pingliang is open country, not a place to gather troops. If victory is to be won, there is no alternative but Shuofang. If we escort Your Highness, within ten days we can recover He and Longyou to the west. The Uyghurs are then strong and on friendly terms with the state. With a northern expedition of powerful cavalry and a southern gathering of loyal cities, one great stroke of the army can restore the Two Capitals. To wipe away the shame of the altars of state, repay the enlightened sovereign above, and settle the common people below — that is both the duty of a loyal subject and the great plan of the state. " That same day Hongjian drafted a memorial fully describing the situation for gathering troops and horses, recorded the numbers of military supplies, weapons, storehouses, and treasury goods, and sent Li Han to bear it to Pingliang. Suzong was greatly pleased. When Hongjian learned that Suzong had set out from Pingliang, he met him at Baicao Station on the northern border and consoled the various envoys and soldiers. He urged, "Shuofang has the strongest troops under Heaven. Ling prefecture is the place from which to wield military force. The Uyghurs now seek peace, Tibet has submitted, and throughout the realm commanderies and counties hold firm, awaiting imperial command. Even where some places are held by the rebels, they can surely be recovered before long. If Your Highness puts the army in order and drives forward in one stroke, the rebel barbarian can easily be destroyed. " Suzong agreed. When they reached Lingwu, Hongjian with Pei Mian and others urged him to take the throne immediately in order to answer the hopes of court and realm. After five memorials Suzong consented. Hongjian had long been versed in imperial ceremonial display and the rites of ruler and minister at court audience. He gathered the old rites and arranged the mats for the ceremony. South of the city an altar and enclosure were set up. One day beforehand the ritual notes and draft memorial were prepared. Suzong said, "The sage sovereign is far away and the rebels are not yet pacified. The altar ground should be suspended. " The rest of the memorial was approved. When Suzong took the throne, Hongjian was appointed director in the Ministry of War, given charge of Secretariat drafter business, and soon transferred to vice minister of war. In Zhide 2 he was made concurrent censor-in-chief and appointed military commissioner of Hexi and protector-general of Liang prefecture. When the Two Capitals were pacified he was transferred to chief administrator of the Jingzhou metropolitan prefecture and military commissioner of Jingnan.

襄州大將康楚元、張嘉延盜所管兵，據襄州城叛，刺史王政遁走。 嘉延南襲荊州，鴻漸聞之，棄城而遁。 澧、朗、硤、歸等州聞鴻漸出奔，皆惶駭，潛竄山谷。 歲餘，徵拜尚書右丞、吏部侍郎、太常卿，充禮儀使。 二聖晏駕，鴻漸監護儀制，山陵畢，加光祿大夫，封衛國公。 廣德二年，代宗將享郊廟，拜鴻漸兵部侍郎、同中書門下平章事，尋轉中書侍郎。
The great generals of Xiang prefecture Kang Chuyuan and Zhang Jiayan seized the troops under their command, occupied Xiang prefecture city in rebellion, and the prefect Wang Zheng fled. Jiayan attacked south toward Jing prefecture. When Hongjian heard of it he abandoned the city and fled. The prefectures of Li, Lang, Xia, Gui, and others, hearing that Hongjian had fled, were alarmed and terrified and secretly hid in the mountains. After more than a year he was summoned and appointed right vice director of the Department of State Affairs, vice minister of personnel, director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, and commissioner of ritual ceremonies. When the two deceased emperors passed away, Hongjian supervised the ritual regulations for their funerals. When the mountain tombs were complete he was granted the title Grand Master of Splendid Happiness and enfeoffed as Duke of Wei. In Guangde 2, when Daizong was about to offer sacrifice at the suburban altars, Hongjian was appointed vice minister of war and chief councilor, and soon transferred to vice director of the Secretariat.

永泰元年十月，劍南西川兵馬使崔旰殺節度使郭英乂，據成都，自稱留後。 邛州衙將柏貞節、瀘州衙將楊子琳、劍州衙將李昌巙等興兵討旰，西蜀大亂。 明年二月，命鴻漸以宰相兼充山、劍副元帥、劍南西川節度使，以平蜀亂。 鴻漸心無遠圖，志氣怯懦，又酷好浮圖道，不喜軍戎。 既至成都，懼旰雄武，不復問罪，乃以劍南節制表讓於旰。 時西戎寇邊，關中多事，鴻漸孤軍陷險，兵威不振，代宗不獲已，從之。 仍以旰為劍南西川行軍司馬，柏貞節為邛州刺史，楊子琳為瀘州刺史，各罷兵。 尋請入覲，仍表崔旰為西川兵馬留後。 大曆二年，詔以旰為成都尹、劍南西川節度使，召鴻漸還京。 鴻漸仍率旰同入覲，代宗嘉之。 後知政事，轉門下侍郎，讓山南副元帥。 三年八月，代王縉為東都留守，充河南、淮西、山南東道副元帥，平章事如故。 以疾上表乞骸骨，從之，竟不之任。 四年十一月卒，贈太尉，謚曰文憲。 輟朝三日，賜物五百疋，粟五百石。
In the tenth month of Yongtai 1, Cui Gan, military envoy of Jiannan West Circuit, killed the military commissioner Guo Yingyi, seized Chengdu, and styled himself acting commissioner. The yamen generals of Qiong prefecture Bo Zhenjie, Lu prefecture Yang Zilin, Jian prefecture Li Changchong, and others raised troops to attack Gan. Western Shu fell into great disorder. In the second month of the following year Hongjian was ordered, as chancellor, to serve concurrently as deputy commander of Shan and Jian circuits and military commissioner of Jiannan West Circuit in order to pacify the Shu rebellion. Hongjian had no far-reaching plans, his spirit was timid, he was passionately devoted to Buddhism, and he disliked military affairs. When he reached Chengdu he feared Gan's martial prowess, abandoned any effort to punish him, and submitted a memorial yielding control of Jiannan to Gan. At the time the western barbarians were raiding the borders and Guanzhong was troubled. Hongjian's isolated army was trapped in peril and his military prestige collapsed. Daizong, having no alternative, consented. Gan was confirmed as military campaign aide of Jiannan West Circuit, Bo Zhenjie as prefect of Qiong, Yang Zilin as prefect of Lu, and each disbanded his troops. He soon requested permission to return to court and memorialized to make Cui Gan acting military commissioner of western Shu. In Dali 2 an edict made Gan mayor of Chengdu and military commissioner of Jiannan West Circuit, and summoned Hongjian back to the capital. Hongjian still led Gan to enter court audience together, and Daizong praised him for it. He later resumed governmental duties, was transferred to vice director of the Chancellery, and relinquished the post of deputy commander of Shannan. In the eighth month of year 3 he replaced Wang Jin as protector of the eastern capital, served as deputy commander of Henan, Huaixi, and Shannan East circuits, and remained chief councilor as before. Citing illness he memorialized to request retirement. Permission was granted, but in the end he never took up the post. He died in the eleventh month of year 4. He was posthumously made Grand Commandant and given the posthumous title Cultured and Lawful. Court was suspended for three days. He was granted five hundred bolts of goods and five hundred piculs of grain.

鴻漸晚年樂於退靜，私第在長興裏，館宇華靡，賓僚宴集。 鴻漸悠然賦詩曰：「常願追禪理，安能挹化源。 」朝士多屬和之。 及休致後病，令僧剃頂發，及卒，遺命其子依胡法塔葬，不為封樹，冀類緇流，物議哂之。
In his later years Hongjian delighted in retirement and quiet. His private residence in Changxing ward was splendid and lavish, and he often gathered guests and subordinates for feasts. Hongjian composed a poem at leisure, saying, "I have long wished to pursue the principles of Chan. How could I draw from the source of transformation? " Many court gentlemen composed matching verses in response. After he retired he fell ill and had monks shave his crown. When he died he instructed his sons to bury him according to Buddhist custom in a pagoda, without a mound or trees, hoping to be counted among the clergy. Public opinion ridiculed this.

史臣曰：祿山狂悖已顯，玄宗寵任無疑，見素知國危，陳廟算，直言極諫，而君不從，獨正犯難，而人不咎，出生入死，善始令終者鮮矣。 時論以見素取容於國忠，無言匡大政。 且國忠恃內戚，弄重權，沮林甫奸豪，取其大位，若見素之孤直，豈許取容？ 蓋禍胎已成，政柄久紊，見素入相余年，言不從而難作，雖有周、孔之才，其能匡救者乎？ 諤才辯，顗儉約，雅符積善之慶矣。 圓守文之士，非禦侮之才。 渙才兼行聞，命與時會。 發言上沃主意，遽致顯榮； 當官屢為吏欺，終及竄逐。 所謂可與適道，未可與權。 縱忠於國，能於官，孝於家，三者備矣，孰能繼之？ 鴻漸有衛社之功，非幹城之責，時以任崔旰為非，則不然矣。 且旰南拒貞節，北敗獻誠，宜以懷來，未可力制，終致歸國，豈非臧謀？ 向討之，即為劇賊矣。 然事佛僥福，朋勢取容，非君子之道焉。
The historiographer writes: Lushan's madness and perversity were already plain, and Xuanzong's favor was beyond question. Jiansu knew the state was in peril, set forth the strategy of the altars, and remonstrated with utmost frankness, yet the ruler would not listen. He alone upheld rectitude and braved hardship, yet men did not blame him. Those who pass through birth and death and achieve a good beginning and a fine end are few indeed. Contemporary opinion held that Jiansu sought accommodation with Guozhong and never spoke to rectify major policy. Moreover Guozhong relied on imperial kinship, wielded heavy power, blocked the treacherous hero Li Linfu, and took his great post. If Jiansu were truly isolated and upright, how could he have been allowed to seek accommodation? The seeds of disaster had already been sown and government power had long been in disorder. Jiansu served as chancellor for more than a year, but his words went unheeded and calamity followed. Even with the talents of the Duke of Zhou and Confucius, how could he have rescued the state? E was talented in debate and Yan frugal and abstemious — both aptly reflected the blessing of accumulated virtue. Yuan was a man who upheld literary culture, not one with talent to repel invasion. Huan was renowned for both talent and conduct, and his appointment came at the right moment. His words enriched the emperor's thinking and swiftly won him conspicuous glory; yet in office he was repeatedly deceived by subordinate officials and in the end was driven into exile. This is what is meant by one who may be joined in following the Way but not in wielding power. Zong was loyal to the state, capable in office, and filial in the family. All three virtues were complete in him. Who could succeed him? Hongjian had merit in guarding the altars of state but not the responsibility of defending the walls. Men of the time regarded his appointment of Cui Gan as wrong — but that is not so. Moreover Gan resisted Bo Zhenjie to the south and defeated Xian Cheng to the north. He should be won by conciliation and could not be controlled by force alone. In the end he brought the region back to the state. Was this not sound strategy? Had the court attacked him, he would at once have become a fierce rebel. Yet in serving the Buddha to seek fortune and forming factions to gain accommodation — this was not the way of a gentleman.

The encomium reads: Xuanzong lost virtue, and Lushan unleashed rebellion. Jiansu gave his utmost loyalty, and these lords joined their strength together.
