1
玄宗三十子：元獻楊皇后生肅宗，劉華妃生奉天皇帝琮、靖恭太子琬、儀王璲，趙麗妃生廢太子瑛，錢妃生棣王琰，皇甫德儀生鄂王瑤，劉才人生光王琚，貞順武皇后生夏悼王一、懷哀王敏、壽王瑁、盛王琦，高婕妤生潁王璬、郭順儀生永王璘，柳婕妤生延王玢，鐘美人生濟王環，盧美人生信王瑝，閻才人生義王玭，王美人生陳王珪，陳美人生豐王珙，鄭才人生恆王瑱，武賢儀生涼王璿，汴哀王璥，余七王早夭。
Emperor Xuanzong had thirty sons. Empress Yuanxian of the Yang clan bore Emperor Suzong. Consort Liu Hua bore the future Emperor of Fengtian (Cong), the Jinggong Crown Prince (Wan), and the Prince of Ceremonies (Lin). Consort Zhao Li bore the deposed Crown Prince Ying. Consort Qian bore the Prince of Di (Yan). Huangfu Deyi bore the Prince of E (Yao). Lady Liu bore the Prince of Guang (Ju). Empress Zhenshun of the Wu clan bore the Xiaosi Prince (named Yi), the Huai'ai Prince Min, the Prince of Shou (Mao), and the Prince of Sheng (Qi). Lady Gao bore the Prince of Ying (Jing). Lady Guo Shun bore the Prince of Yong (Lin). Lady Liu bore the Prince of Yan (Bin). Lady Zhong bore the Prince of Ji (Huan). Lady Lu bore the Prince of Xin (Huang). Lady Yan bore the Prince of Yi (Mi). Lady Wang bore the Prince of Chen (Gui). Lady Chen bore the Prince of Feng (Gong). Lady Zheng bore the Prince of Heng (Zhen). Lady Wu Xian bore the Prince of Liang (Xuan) and the Bian'ai Prince (Jin). Seven other princes died in infancy.
2
廢太子瑛，玄宗第二子也，本名嗣謙。 景雲元年九月，封真定郡王。 先天元年八月，進封郢王。 開元三年正月，立為皇太子。 七年正月，加元服。 其年，玄宗又令太子詣國子學行齒胄之禮，仍敕右散騎常侍褚無量升筵講論，學官及文武百官節級加賜。 十三年，改名鴻，納妃薛氏，禮畢，曲赦京城之內，侍講潘肅等並加級改職，中書令蕭嵩親迎，特封徐國公。 二十五年七月，改名瑛。
The deposed Crown Prince Ying was Xuanzong's second son. His birth name was Siqian. In the ninth month of the first year of the Jingyun era (710), he was created Prince of Zhending. In the eighth month of the first year of the Xiantian era (712), he was promoted to Prince of Ying. In the first month of the third year of Kaiyuan (715), he was named crown prince. In the first month of the seventh year (719), he underwent the coming-of-age ceremony. That same year, Xuanzong had the crown prince visit the Imperial University to perform the ceremonial donning of armor according to seniority. He also ordered Chu Wuliang, Right Regular Attendant of the Imperial Cavalry, to take the lectern and discourse on the classics. The academic officers and all civil and military officials received additional gifts scaled by rank. In the thirteenth year (725), his name was changed to Hong. After he took Lady Xue of the Xue clan as his consort and the wedding rites were concluded, a partial amnesty was proclaimed within the capital. Lecturers including Pan Su were promoted and given new posts. Chief Minister Xiao Song went in person to welcome the bride, and the prince was specially created Duke of Xu. In the seventh month of the twenty-fifth year (737), his name was changed to Ying.
3
瑛母趙麗妃，本伎人，有才貌，善歌舞，玄宗在潞州得幸。 及景雲升儲之後，其父元禮、兄常奴擢為京職，開元初皆至大官。 及武惠妃寵幸，麗妃恩乃漸弛。 時鄂王瑤母皇甫德儀、光王琚母劉才人，皆玄宗在臨淄邸以容色見顧，出子朗秀而母加愛焉。 及惠妃承恩，鄂、光之母亦漸疏薄，惠妃之子壽王瑁，鐘愛非諸子所比。 瑛於內第與鄂、光王等自謂母氏失職，嘗有怨望。 惠妃女咸宜公主出降於楊洄，洄希惠妃之旨，規利於己，日求其短，譖於惠妃。 妃泣訴於玄宗，以太子結黨，將害於妾母子，亦指斥於至尊。 玄宗惑其言，震怒，謀於宰相，意將廢黜。 中書張九齡奏曰：「陛下纂嗣鴻業，將三十年，太子已下，常不離深宮，日受聖訓。 今天下之人，皆慶陛下享國日久，子孫蕃育，不聞有過，陛下奈何以一日之間廢棄三子？ 伏惟陛下思之。 且太子國本，難於動搖。 昔晉獻公惑寵嬖之言，太子申生憂死，國乃大亂。 漢武威加六合，受江充巫蠱之事，將禍及太子，遂至城中流血。 晉惠帝有賢子為太子，容賈後之譖，以至喪亡。 隋文帝取寵婦之言，廢太子勇而立晉王廣，遂失天下。 由此而論之，不可不慎。 今太子既長無過，二王又賢，臣待罪左右，敢不詳悉。 」玄宗默然，事且寢。 其年，駕幸西京，以李林甫代張九齡為中書令，希惠妃之旨，托意於中貴人，揚壽王瑁之美，惠妃深德之。 二十五年四月，楊洄又構於惠妃，言瑛兄弟三人與太子妃兄駙馬薛鏽常構異謀。 玄宗遽召宰相籌之，林甫曰：「此蓋陛下家事，臣不合參知。 」玄宗意乃決矣。 使中官宣詔於宮中，並廢為庶人，鏽配流，俄賜死於城東驛。 天下之人不見其過，鹹惜之。 其年，武惠妃數見三庶人為崇，怖而成疾，巫者祈請彌月，不痊而殞。
Ying's mother, Consort Zhao Li, had been a performing artist. Gifted and beautiful, she was skilled in song and dance. Xuanzong took her to his bed while he was still in Luzhou. After he was named heir apparent in the Jingyun era, her father Yuanli and brother Changnu were appointed to posts in the capital. By the early Kaiyuan years they had all risen to high office. Once Consort Wu Huifei won the emperor's favor, Consort Zhao Li's standing gradually waned. At that time, Huangfu Deyi, mother of the Prince of E (Yao), and Lady Liu, mother of the Prince of Guang (Ju), had both caught Xuanzong's eye for their beauty while he was still prince at Linzi. Their sons were handsome and accomplished, and the mothers were all the more cherished for it. When Consort Wu came into favor, the mothers of the Princes of E and Guang were likewise gradually pushed aside. Her son, the Prince of Shou (Mao), was doted upon as none of the other princes were. Within the inner quarters, Ying and the Princes of E and Guang and the like felt that their mothers had fallen from favor, and they sometimes gave voice to their grievances. Huifei's daughter, Princess Xianyi, had married Yang Hui. Eager to curry favor with Huifei and advance his own interests, Hui daily hunted for their failings and whispered accusations against them to the consort. The consort wept before Xuanzong and accused the crown prince of forming factions and plotting harm against her and her son, even casting aspersions on the emperor himself. Taken in by her words, Xuanzong was furious. He took counsel with his chief ministers and resolved to depose the crown prince. Chief Minister Zhang Jiuling submitted: "Your Majesty has inherited the great enterprise and will soon complete thirty years on the throne. From the crown prince on down, your sons have scarcely left the inner palace and have daily received your instruction. All the realm rejoices that you have long held the throne and that your sons and grandsons flourish. No wrongdoing has been reported against them. How can Your Majesty, in a single day, cast aside three sons? I humbly urge Your Majesty to reconsider. Moreover, the crown prince is the foundation of the state and must not be lightly moved. In antiquity, Duke Xian of Jin was swayed by a favored woman's words. Crown Prince Shensheng died in despair, and the state was thrown into chaos. Emperor Wu of Han, whose martial might reached the four seas, was taken in by Jiang Chong's witchcraft scandal. Calamity nearly engulfed the crown prince, and blood ran in the streets of the capital. Emperor Hui of Jin had a worthy son as crown prince, yet he tolerated Empress Jia's slanders until both throne and heir were lost. Emperor Wen of Sui heeded a favored woman's counsel, deposed Crown Prince Yong, and installed Prince Guang of Jin in his place—and in the end lost the empire. Judging from these precedents, the matter cannot be treated lightly. Today the crown prince has come of age without fault, and the two princes are likewise worthy. Your servant, who stands in attendance at Your Majesty's side, dares not withhold the full truth. " Xuanzong said nothing, and for the time being the matter was set aside. That year the court moved to the Western Capital. Li Linfu replaced Zhang Jiuling as chief minister. Eager to please Huifei, he worked through the palace eunuchs to praise the Prince of Shou (Mao), and the consort was deeply obliged to him. In the fourth month of the twenty-fifth year (737), Yang Hui again pressed his case before Huifei, alleging that Ying and his two brothers, together with Xue Xiu—the crown prince's consort's elder brother and an imperial son-in-law—were constantly plotting treason. Xuanzong at once summoned the chief ministers to deliberate. Li Linfu said, "This is surely a matter of Your Majesty's household. It is not for your servant to intervene. " With that, Xuanzong's mind was made up. He sent palace eunuchs to proclaim the edict within the palace. All three princes were reduced to commoners. Xue Xiu was sentenced to exile and soon ordered to take his own life at the relay station east of the city. Throughout the realm, no one could see what crime they had committed, and all mourned their fate. That same year, Consort Wu Huifei repeatedly dreamed that the three deposed princes haunted her as spirits. Stricken with terror, she fell ill. Sorcerers prayed for her for a full month, but she did not recover and died.
4
瑛有六男：儼、伸、倩、俅、備、亻敬。 慶王琮先無子，瑛得罪後，玄宗遣鞫之。 天寶中，儼為新平郡王、光祿卿同正員，伸為平原郡王、宗正卿同正員，俅為嗣慶王。 寶應元年，詔雪瑤、瑛、琚之罪，贈瑛為皇太子，瑤、琚復贈為王。
Ying had six sons: Yan, Shen, Qian, Qiu, Bei, and Jing. The Prince of Qing (Cong) had had no sons. After Ying's disgrace, Xuanzong ordered an inquiry to select an heir for him. During the Tianbao era, Yan was created Prince of Xinping and given the acting rank of Commissioner of the Imperial Household; Shen was created Prince of Pingyuan and given the acting rank of Director of the Imperial Clan; and Qiu was named heir to the Prince of Qing. In the first year of Baoying (762), an edict cleared the crimes of Yao, Ying, and Ju. Ying was posthumously restored as crown prince; Yao and Ju were again granted their princely titles.
5
棣王琰，玄宗第四子也，初名嗣真。 開元二年十二月，封為鄫王。 十二年三月，改封棣王，仍改名洽。 十五年，遙領太原牧、太原已北諸軍節度大使。 二十二年，加太子太傅，余如故。 二十四年，改名琰。 天寶元年六月，遙領兼武威郡都督、河西隴右經略節度大使。
The Prince of Di (Yan) was Xuanzong's fourth son. His birth name was Sizhen. In the twelfth month of the second year of Kaiyuan (714), he was created Prince of Zeng. In the third month of the twelfth year (724), he was re-created Prince of Di and his name was changed to Qia. In the fifteenth year (727), he was given nominal appointment as governor of Taiyuan and military commissioner for all forces north of Taiyuan. In the twenty-second year (734), he was additionally appointed Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent; his other titles were unchanged. In the twenty-fourth year (736), his name was changed to Yan. In the sixth month of the first year of Tianbao (742), he was given nominal concurrent appointment as governor of Wuwei and military commissioner for the Hexi and Longyou frontier.
6
先是，琰妃韋氏有過，琰怒之，不敢奏聞，乃斥於別室。 寵二孺人，孺人又不相協。 至十一載，孺人乃密求巫者，書符置於琰履中以求媚。 琰與監院中官有隙，中官聞其事，密奏於玄宗，雲琰厭魅聖躬。 玄宗使人掩其履而獲之。 玄宗大怒，引琰詰責之。 琰頓首謝曰：「臣之罪合死矣，請一言以就鼎鑊。 然臣與新婦，情義絕者，二年於茲，臣有二孺人，又皆爭長。 臣實不知有符，恐此三人所為也。 惟三哥辯其罪人。 」及推問之，竟孺人也。 玄宗猶疑琰知情，怒未解，太子已下皆為請，命囚於鷹狗坊中，絕朝請，憂懼而死。 琰妃即少師韋滔女，無子，琰死後，妃得還其父。 琰男女繁衍，至五十五人。 天寶中封為王者三人：僎為汝南郡王、秘書監同正員，僑為宜都王、衛尉卿同正員，雋為濟南王、光祿卿同正員。 寶應元年五月，代宗即位，舍琰罪，贈其王位。
Earlier, Yan's consort, Lady Wei of the Wei clan, had given offense. Yan was furious but dared not report it to the throne. He shut her away in a separate apartment. He took two junior consorts to his bed, and the two could not abide each other. By the eleventh year of Tianbao (752), one of the junior consorts secretly engaged a sorcerer to inscribe a charm and slip it into Yan's shoe in hopes of winning his affection. Yan had fallen out with a eunuch who supervised his household. When the eunuch learned of the charm, he secretly reported to Xuanzong that Yan was practicing witchcraft against the emperor's person. Xuanzong sent men to search Yan's shoes and found the charm. Xuanzong was furious. He summoned Yan and interrogated him under accusation. Yan kowtowed and said, "Your servant's crime deserves death. Allow me one word before I face the executioner's cauldron. Yet between your servant and his principal consort, all affection has been dead these two years. I have two junior consorts, and both vie for dominance. I truly knew nothing of the charm. I fear it was the work of these three women. I ask only that the third brother be allowed to identify the culprit. " When the matter was investigated, the culprit proved to be one of the junior consorts. Xuanzong still suspected that Yan had known of the plot, and his anger did not abate. From the crown prince on down, all pleaded for him, but the emperor ordered Yan imprisoned in the Hawk and Hound Ward, barred from court attendance. He died of grief and fear. Yan's consort was the daughter of Junior Preceptor Wei Tao. She bore no sons. After Yan's death, she was permitted to return to her father's house. Yan's sons and daughters multiplied until they numbered fifty-five. During the Tianbao era, three of his sons were created princes: Fu as Prince of Runan with the acting rank of Director of the Secretariat; Qiao as Prince of Yidu with the acting rank of Minister of the Guard; and Jun as Prince of Jinan with the acting rank of Commissioner of the Imperial Household. In the fifth month of the first year of Baoying (762), when Emperor Daizong ascended the throne, Yan's crime was pardoned and his princely title was posthumously restored.
7
鄂王瑤，玄宗第五子也，初名嗣初。 開元二年五月，封為鄂王。 十二年，改名涓，遙領幽州都督、河北道節度大使。 二十一年四月，加太子太保，兼幽州都督，余如故。 二十三年，改名瑤。 二十五年，得罪廢。 寶應元年五月追復。
The Prince of E (Yao) was Xuanzong's fifth son. His birth name was Sichu. In the fifth month of the second year of Kaiyuan (714), he was created Prince of E. In the twelfth year (724), his name was changed to Juan. He was given nominal appointment as governor of Youzhou and military commissioner for the Hebei circuit. In the fourth month of the twenty-first year (733), he was additionally appointed Grand Protector of the Heir Apparent with concurrent governorship of Youzhou; his other titles were unchanged. In the twenty-third year (735), his name was changed to Yao. In the twenty-fifth year (737), he fell from favor and was deposed. In the fifth month of the first year of Baoying (762), his title was posthumously restored.
8
靖恭太子琬，玄宗第六子也，初名嗣玄。 開元二年三月，封為甄王。 十二年三月，改名滉，封為榮王。 十五年，授京兆牧，又遙領隴右節度大使。 二十三年，加開府儀同三司，余如故。 二十五年，改名琬。 天寶元年六月，授單於大都護。 十四年十一月，安祿山反於范陽，其月制以琬為征討元帥，高仙芝為副，令仙芝征河、隴兵募屯於陜郡以禦之。 數日，琬薨。 琬素有雅稱，風格秀整，時士庶冀琬有所成功，忽然殂謝，遠近鹹失望焉。 贈靖恭太子，葬於見子西原。 琬諸子尤繁衍，男女五十八人。 天寶中封為郡王者二：俯為濟陰王、太仆卿同正員，偕為北平王、國子祭酒同正員。
The Jinggong Crown Prince (Wan) was Xuanzong's sixth son. His birth name was Sixuan. In the third month of the second year of Kaiyuan (714), he was created Prince of Zhen. In the third month of the twelfth year (724), his name was changed to Huang and he was re-created Prince of Rong. In the fifteenth year (727), he was appointed governor of the capital district and given nominal command as military commissioner for Longyou. In the twenty-third year (735), he was additionally appointed Grand Master of the Palace with Golden Girdle and Privy Seal of the First Rank; his other titles were unchanged. In the twenty-fifth year (737), his name was changed to Wan. In the sixth month of the first year of Tianbao (742), he was appointed Grand Protector of Chanyu. In the eleventh month of the fourteenth year (755), An Lushan rebelled at Fanyang. That same month an edict named Wan supreme commander for the punitive campaign, with Gao Xianzhi as his deputy, and ordered Xianzhi to levy troops from the He and Long circuits and encamp at Shaan to meet the rebels. Within days, Wan died. Wan had long enjoyed a reputation for refinement; his bearing was elegant and dignified. Scholars and commoners alike had hoped he would distinguish himself. When he died so suddenly, people throughout the realm were deeply disappointed. He was posthumously honored as the Jinggong Crown Prince and buried on the western plain at Jianzi. Wan's offspring were especially numerous: fifty-eight sons and daughters in all. During the Tianbao era, two of his sons were created princes: Fu as Prince of Jiyin with the acting rank of Minister of the Stud; and Xie as Prince of Beiping with the acting rank of Rector of the Imperial University.
9
夏悼王一，玄宗第九子也。 母貞順皇后為惠妃，見寵。 一生而美秀，上鐘愛無比，名之為一。 開元五年，孩孺而薨，玄宗追封謚。 時車駕在東都，葬於城南龍門東岑，欲宮中舉目見之。
The Xiaosi Prince (named Yi) was Xuanzong's ninth son. His mother, later Empress Zhenshun, was then Consort Wu Huifei, who was in high favor. From birth he was beautiful and accomplished. The emperor doted on him as on no other child and gave him the name Yi. In the fifth year of Kaiyuan (717), he died in infancy. Xuanzong posthumously enfeoffed him and granted a posthumous title. The court was then at the Eastern Capital. He was buried on the eastern slope of Longmen, south of the city, so that the palace could look out and see the tomb.
10
儀王璲，玄宗第十二子也，初名濰。 開元十三年五月，封為儀王。 十五年，授河南牧。 二十三年，加開府儀同三司，兼河南牧，其年改名璲。 永泰元年二月薨，廢朝三日，贈太傅。 天寶中有子封王者二人：侁為鐘陵郡王、光祿卿同正員，僆為廣陵王、國子祭酒同正員。
The Prince of Ceremonies (Lin) was Xuanzong's twelfth son. His birth name was Wei. In the fifth month of the thirteenth year of Kaiyuan (725), he was created Prince of Ceremonies. In the fifteenth year (727), he was appointed governor of Henan. In the twenty-third year (735), he was additionally appointed Grand Master of the Palace with Golden Girdle and Privy Seal of the First Rank, with concurrent governorship of Henan. That year his name was changed to Lin. In the second month of the first year of Yongtai (765), he died. Court mourning was observed for three days. He was posthumously honored as Grand Tutor. During the Tianbao era, two of his sons were created princes: Shen as Prince of Zhongling with the acting rank of Commissioner of the Imperial Household; and Mian as Prince of Guangling with the acting rank of Rector of the Imperial University.
11
潁王璬，玄宗第十三子也。 讀書有文詞。 初名澐。 開元十三年，封潁王。 十五年，遙領安東都護、平盧軍節度大使。 二十三年，加開府儀同三司，改名璬。 安祿山反，除蜀郡大都督、劍南節度大使，楊國忠為之副。 玄宗幸蜀，令御史大夫魏方進充置頓使，先移牒至蜀，托以潁王之籓，令設儲供。 玄宗至馬嵬，方進被殺，乃令璬先赴本郡，以蜀郡長史崔圓為副。 璬性儉率，將渡綿州江，登舟見彩緣席為藉者，顧曰：「此可以為寢處，奈何踐之？ 」命撤去。 璬初奉命之籓，卒遽不遑受節，綿州司馬史賁進說曰：「王，帝子也，且為節度大使。 今之籓而不持節，單騎徑進，人何所贍？ 請建大槊，蒙之油囊，為旌節狀，先驅道路，足以威眾。 」璬笑曰：「但為真王，何用假旌節乎？ 」將至成都，崔圓迓之，拜於馬前，璬不止之，圓頗怒。 玄宗至，璬視事兩月，人甚安之。 為圓所奏，罷居內宅。 後令宣慰肅宗於彭原，遂從歸京師。 建中四年薨。 年六十六，輟朝三日。 子伸，天寶中封滎陽郡王，授衛尉卿同正員。
The Prince of Ying (Jing) was Xuanzong's thirteenth son. He was learned and skilled in literary composition. His birth name was Yun. In the thirteenth year of Kaiyuan (725), he was created Prince of Ying. In the fifteenth year (727), he was given nominal appointment as Protector-General of Andong and military commissioner of the Pinglu army. In the twenty-third year (735), he was additionally appointed Grand Master of the Palace with Golden Girdle and Privy Seal of the First Rank, and his name was changed to Jing. When An Lushan rebelled, Jing was appointed Grand Protector of Shujun and military commissioner of Jiannan, with Yang Guozhong as his deputy. When Xuanzong fled to Shu, he appointed Censor-in-Chief Wei Fangjin provisioning commissioner. Fangjin sent word ahead to Shu in the name of the Prince of Ying's fief and ordered provisions laid in. At Mawei, Fangjin was killed. Xuanzong then ordered Jing to proceed at once to his own commandery and appointed Cui Yuan, senior administrator of Shujun, as his deputy. Jing was frugal by nature. As he was about to cross the river at Mianzhou, he boarded a boat and saw brocade-edged mats laid out as bedding. He turned and said, "These could serve as a bed—how can they be trodden underfoot? " He ordered them taken away. When Jing first set out for his fief, the rush was so great that he had no time to receive his commissioner's credentials. The Mianzhou aide Shi Benjin urged him: "Your Highness is the emperor's son and a military commissioner besides. To go to one's fief without credentials, riding ahead alone—what authority will that convey to the people? Let us raise a great spear, drape it with an oiled bag in the shape of banners and credentials, and send it ahead on the road—that alone will be enough to overawe the crowd. " Jing laughed and said, "If one is a true prince, what need is there for counterfeit banners and credentials? " As he neared Chengdu, Cui Yuan came out to meet him and bowed before his horse. Jing did not bid him rise, and Yuan was deeply offended. After Xuanzong arrived, Jing governed for two months, and the people were greatly reassured. Cui Yuan memorialized against him, and he was removed to dwell in the inner palace quarters. He was later sent as envoy of consolation to Suzong at Pengyuan and thereafter returned with him to the capital. He died in the fourth year of the Jianzhong era, 783. He was sixty-six. Court mourning was observed for three days. His son Shen was created Prince of Xingyang during the Tianbao era and given the acting rank of Minister of the Guard.

懷哀王敏，玄宗第十五子也。 幼而豐秀，以母惠妃之寵，玄宗特加顧念。 才晬，開元八年二月薨，追封謚，權窆於景龍觀。 天寶十三載，改葬京城南，以祔其母敬陵也。
The Huai'ai Prince Min was Xuanzong's fifteenth son. As a child he was plump and handsome. Because his mother, Consort Wu Huifei, was in favor, Xuanzong doted on him all the more. He had scarcely reached his first birthday when he died in the second month of the eighth year of Kaiyuan, 720. He was posthumously enfeoffed and given a posthumous title and provisionally interred at the Jinglong Abbey. In the thirteenth year of Tianbao, 754, he was reburied south of the capital so that he might be placed beside his mother in the Jing Mausoleum.

The Prince of Yong, Lin, was Xuanzong's sixteenth son. His mother was Lady Guo Shun, younger sister of Xuji, who served as military commissioner of Jiannan and Minister of Works. Lin lost his mother while still a small child. Suzong took him in and would hold him in his arms at night to sleep. As a youth he was clever and eager to learn, but his features were plain and his eyes did not align properly. In the third month of the thirteenth year of Kaiyuan, 725, he was created Prince of Yong. In the fifth month of the fifteenth year, 727, he was given nominal appointment as Grand Protector of Jingzhou. In the seventh month of the twentieth year, 732, he was additionally appointed Grand Master of the Palace with Golden Girdle and Privy Seal of the First Rank, and his name was changed to Lin.

天寶十四載十一月，安祿山反范陽。 十五載六月，玄宗幸蜀，至漢中郡，下詔以璘為山南東路及嶺南黔中江南西路四道節度采訪等使、江陵郡大都督，余如故。 璘七月至襄陽，九月至江陵，召募士將數萬人，恣情補署，江淮租賦，山積於江陵，破用鉅億。 以薛镠、李臺卿、蔡坰為謀主，因有異志。 肅宗聞之，詔令歸覲於蜀，璘不從命。 十二月，擅領舟師東下，甲仗五千人趨廣陵，以季廣琛、渾惟明、高仙琦為將。 璘生於宮中，不更人事，其子襄城王亻易又勇而有力，馭兵權，為左右眩惑，遂謀狂悖。 璘雖有窺江左之心，而未露其事。 吳郡采訪使李希言乃平牒璘，大署其名，璘遂激怒，牒報曰：「寡人上皇天屬，皇帝友於，地尊侯王，禮絕僚品，簡書來往，應有常儀，今乃平牒抗威，落筆署字，漢儀隳紊，一至於斯！ 」乃使渾惟明取希言，季廣琛趣廣陵攻采訪李成式。 璘進至當塗，希言在丹陽，令元景曜、閻敬之等以兵拒之，身走吳郡，李成式使將李承慶拒之。 先是，肅宗以璘不受命，先使中官啖廷瑤、段喬福招討之。 中官至廣陵，反式括得馬數百匹。 時河北招討判官、司虞郎中李銑在廣陵，瑤等結銑為兄弟，求之將兵。 銑麾下有騎一百八十人，遂率所領屯於楊子，成式使判官評事裴茂以廣陵步卒三千同拒於瓜步洲伊婁埭。 希言將元景曜及成式將李神慶並以其眾迎降於璘，璘又殺丹徒太守閻敬之以徇。 江左大駭。
In the eleventh month of the fourteenth year of Tianbao, 755, An Lushan rebelled at Fanyang. In the sixth month of the fifteenth year, 756, Xuanzong fled to Shu. At Hanzhong he issued an edict appointing Lin commissioner for investigation and military affairs over the four circuits of Shannan East, Lingnan, Qianzhong, and Jiangnan West, and Grand Protector of Jiangling; his other titles were unchanged. Lin reached Xiangyang in the seventh month and Jiangling in the ninth. He recruited tens of thousands of officers and soldiers and filled posts at his pleasure. Tax revenues from the Yangzi and Huai regions piled up at Jiangling like mountains, and he squandered hundreds of millions. He took Xue Liu, Li Taiqing, and Cai Jiong as his chief advisers and thus conceived rebellious designs. When Suzong learned of this, he ordered Lin to return to court at Shu. Lin refused. In the twelfth month he led a fleet eastward on his own authority. Five thousand armed men marched on Guangling under the generals Ji Guangchen, Hun Weiming, and Gao Xianqi. Lin had been born in the palace and knew nothing of the world outside. His son, the Prince of Xiangcheng, Yi, was bold and strong, held military power, and was led astray by his advisers until they plotted open rebellion. Although Lin harbored designs on the lands south of the Yangzi, he had not yet revealed his intent. Li Xiyan, commissioner for investigation of Wujun, sent Lin a dispatch on equal footing and signed his own name large upon it. Lin was furious and replied: "I am kin by Heaven to the Retired Emperor and a brother in affection to the reigning emperor. In rank I stand above feudal lords; in ritual I am set apart from the official grades. Written communications ought to observe proper forms. Yet you send a dispatch on equal footing to flaunt your authority, setting down your brush to sign your name—court ritual is thrown into disorder to such a degree! " He then sent Hun Weiming to seize Xiyan and Ji Guangchen to hurry to Guangling and attack the commissioner for investigation Li Chengshi. Lin advanced to Dangtu. Xiyan was at Danyang and ordered Yuan Jingyao, Yan Jingzhi, and others to resist with troops while he himself fled to Wujun. Li Chengshi sent the general Li Chengqing to oppose him. Earlier, because Lin had refused orders, Suzong had sent the eunuchs Dan Tingyao and Duan Qiaofu to campaign against him. When the eunuchs reached Guangling, Chengshi instead mustered several hundred horses. At that time Li Xian, recruitment commissioner for Hebei and Gentleman of the Palace for Ceremonial, was at Guangling. Tingyao and the others swore brotherhood with him and asked him to lead troops. Xian had one hundred eighty horsemen under his command. He led them to encamp at Yangzi. Chengshi sent his aide Pei Mao with three thousand Guangling foot soldiers to join in resisting at Yilou embankment on Guazhou isle. Xiyan's general Yuan Jingyao and Chengshi's general Li Shenqing both brought their forces to surrender to Lin. Lin also executed Yan Jingzhi, administrator of Dantu, as a warning to others. The entire region south of the Yangzi was thrown into terror.

裴茂至瓜步洲，廣張旗幟，耀於江津。 璘與亻易登陴望之竟日，始有懼色。 季廣琛召諸將割臂而盟，以貳於璘。 是日，渾惟明走於江寧，馮季康、康謙投於廣陵之白沙。 廣琛以步卒六千趨廣陵，璘使騎追之，廣琛曰：「我感王恩，是以不能決戰，逃而歸國。 若逼我，我則不擇地而回戰矣。 」使者返報。 其夕，銑等多燃火，人執兩炬以疑之，隔江望者，兼水中之影，一皆為二矣。 璘軍又以火應之。 璘懼，以官軍悉濟矣，遂以兒女及麾下宵遁。 遲明，不見濟者，遂入城具舟楫，使襄城王驅其眾以奔晉陵。 宵諜曰：「王走矣。 」於是江北之軍齊進，募敢死士趙侃、庫狄岫、趙連城等共二十人，先鋒遊弈於新豐，皆因醉而寐。 璘聞官軍之至，乃使襄城王、高仙琦逆擊之。 驛騎奔告，侃等介馬而出，襄城王已隨而至，銑等奔救，張左右翼擊之，射中襄城王首，亻易軍遂敗。 高仙琦等四騎與璘南奔，至鄱陽郡，司馬陶備閉城拒之。 璘怒，命焚其城。 至余幹，及大庾嶺，將南投嶺外，為江西采訪使皇甫侁下防禦兵所擒，因中矢而薨。 子亻易等為亂兵所害。 肅宗以璘愛弟，隱而不言。
Pei Mao reached Guazhou isle, spread his banners wide, and displayed them glittering along the river crossing. Lin and Yi climbed the ramparts and gazed at them all day before fear first showed on their faces. Ji Guangchen summoned the generals to cut their arms and swear an oath, thereby turning against Lin. That day Hun Weiming fled to Jiangning, while Feng Jikang and Kang Qian surrendered at Baisha in Guangling. Guangchen led six thousand foot soldiers toward Guangling. Lin sent cavalry in pursuit. Guangchen said, "I am moved by the prince's grace and therefore cannot bring myself to fight. I flee only to return to the lawful court. If you press me, I will turn and fight wherever I stand. " The messenger returned with his report. That evening Xian and the others lit many fires, and each man held two torches to create confusion. Those watching from across the river, together with the reflections in the water, saw every flame doubled. Lin's army answered with fires of their own. Lin was afraid, believing the government army had all crossed the river. He fled by night with his children and followers. At dawn, seeing that no one had crossed, he entered the city to prepare boats and ordered the Prince of Xiangcheng to drive his troops toward Jinling. A night scout reported, "The prince has fled. " The army north of the river then advanced together. They recruited twenty dare-to-die men including Zhao Kan, Kudixiu, and Zhao Liancheng. The vanguard ranged at Xinfeng, and all, drunk, fell asleep. When Lin heard the government army had arrived, he sent the Prince of Xiangcheng and Gao Xianqi to meet them in counterattack. Courier riders came galloping with the news. Kan and the others mounted in armor and sallied forth. The Prince of Xiangcheng was already close behind. Xian and the others rushed to the rescue, deployed wings on both flanks, and shot the Prince of Xiangcheng in the head. Yi's army was routed. Gao Xianqi and four horsemen fled south with Lin. At Poyang commandery the aide Tao Bei closed the city against them. Lin was furious and ordered the city burned. At Yugan and the Dayu Range, as he was about to flee south beyond the mountains, he was seized by defensive troops sent by Huangfu Shen, commissioner for investigation of Jiangxi, and died of an arrow wound. His son Yi and others were killed by mutinous soldiers. Because Lin had been a beloved younger brother, Suzong concealed the matter and did not speak of it.

壽王瑁，玄宗第十八子也，初名清。 初，瑁母武惠妃，開元元年見幸，寵傾後宮，頻產夏悼王、懷哀王、上仙公主，皆端麗，繈褓不育。 及瑁之初生，讓帝妃元氏請瑁在於邸中收養，妃自乳之，名為己子。 十余年在寧邸，故封建之事晚於諸王。 宮中常呼為十八郎。 十三年三月，封為壽王，始入宮中。 十五年，遙領益州大都督、劍南節度大使。 二十三年，加開府儀同三司，改名瑁。 二十五年，惠妃薨，葬以後禮。 二十九年，讓帝薨，瑁請制服，以報乳養之恩，玄宗從之。 瑁，天寶中有子封為王者二人：懷為濟陽郡王，偡為廣陽郡王、鴻臚卿同正員。
The Prince of Shou, Mao, was Xuanzong's eighteenth son. His birth name was Qing. Mao's mother, Consort Wu Huifei, had won favor in the first year of Kaiyuan and come to dominate the inner palace. She bore the Xiaosi Prince, the Huai'ai Prince, and Princess Shangxian in succession—all fair and comely—yet none survived infancy. When Mao was born, the Retired Emperor's consort, Lady Yuan of the Yuan clan, asked to raise him in her own residence. She nursed him herself and treated him as her own son. He spent more than ten years in the Ning residence, and therefore his enfeoffment came later than the other princes. Within the palace he was commonly called the Eighteenth Lad. In the third month of the thirteenth year, 725, he was created Prince of Shou and first entered the palace. In the fifteenth year, 727, he was given nominal appointment as Grand Protector of Yizhou and military commissioner of Jiannan. In the twenty-third year, 735, he was additionally appointed Grand Master of the Palace with Golden Girdle and Privy Seal of the First Rank, and his name was changed to Mao. In the twenty-fifth year, 737, Huifei died and was buried with the rites due an empress. In the twenty-ninth year, 741, the Retired Emperor died. Mao asked to wear mourning garments in return for the grace of having been nursed and reared, and Xuanzong consented. During the Tianbao era, two of Mao's sons were created princes: Huai as Prince of Jiyang, and Xu as Prince of Guangyang with the acting rank of Minister of the Court for Diplomatic Reception.

唐法，親王食封八百戶，有至一千戶； 公主三百戶，長公主加三百戶，有至六百戶。 高宗朝以沛、英、豫王、太平公主武後所生，食逾於制。 垂拱中，太平至一千二百戶。 聖歷初，皇嗣封為相王，食封與太平同三千戶。 長安中，壽春王兄弟五人，並賜實封三百戶。 神龍初，相府與太平同至五千戶，衛王三千戶，溫王二千戶，成王七百戶。 壽春王加四百戶，通前七百戶； 嗣雍、衡陽、臨淄、巴陵、中山各加二百戶，通前五百戶。 安樂初封二千戶，長寧一千五百戶，宣城、宜城、宣安各一千戶，相王女為縣主者各三百戶。 衛王尋升儲位，相府增至七千戶，太平至五千戶，安樂三千戶，長寧二千五百戶，宣城已下各二千戶。 相府、太平、長寧、安樂皆以七千為限，雖水旱亦不破損免，以正租庸充數。 唐隆元年，遺制以嗣雍王守禮、壽春王成器封為親王，各賜實封一千戶。 開元之後，朝恩睦親，以寧府最長，封至五千五百戶； 岐、薛愛弟著勛，五千戶； 申府以外家微，至四千戶； 邠府以外枝，至一千八百戶。 皇妹為公主者，食封一千戶，中宗女亦同。 其後，皇子封王者賜封二千戶，皇女為公主者賜封五百戶。 咸宜賜湯沐，以母惠妃封至一千戶，諸皇女為公主者，例加至一千戶。 其封自開元已來，皆約以三千為限。
Under Tang law, imperial princes received enfeoffment income of eight hundred households, though some received as many as one thousand. Princesses received three hundred households; senior princesses received an additional three hundred, for a total of up to six hundred. During Gaozong's reign, because the Princes of Pei, Ying, and Yu and Princess Taiping were born of Empress Wu, their incomes exceeded the statutory limit. During the Chuigong era, Taiping's income reached one thousand two hundred households. At the beginning of the Shenglü era, the heir apparent was created Prince of Xiang with enfeoffment income equal to Taiping's at three thousand households. During the Chang'an era, the five brothers of the Prince of Shouchun were each granted actual enfeoffment of three hundred households. At the beginning of the Shenlong era, the Xiang residence and Taiping alike reached five thousand households; the Prince of Wei three thousand; the Prince of Wen two thousand; and the Prince of Cheng seven hundred. The Prince of Shouchun received an additional four hundred households, bringing his total to seven hundred. Siyong, Hengyang, Linzi, Baling, and Zhongshan each received an additional two hundred households, bringing their totals to five hundred each. Anle at first enfeoffment received two thousand households; Changning one thousand five hundred; Xuancheng, Yicheng, and Xuan'an each one thousand; and daughters of the Prince of Xiang created county mistresses each three hundred. When the Prince of Wei soon became heir apparent, the Xiang residence was increased to seven thousand households; Taiping to five thousand; Anle to three thousand; Changning to two thousand five hundred; and Xuancheng and below each to two thousand. The Xiang residence, Taiping, Changning, and Anle all had seven thousand as their ceiling. Even in years of flood or drought their incomes were not reduced or exempted; regular tax and corvée revenues made up the allotted amount. In the first year of Tanglong, 710, the testamentary decree created Siyong Prince Shouli and Shouchun Prince Chengqi as imperial princes, each granted actual enfeoffment of one thousand households. After Kaiyuan, the court favored kinship and amity. Because the Ning residence was the senior line, its enfeoffment reached five thousand five hundred households. Qi and Xue, beloved younger brothers with recorded merit, received five thousand households each. The Shen residence, because the maternal kin were of modest standing, received up to four thousand households. The Bin residence, as a collateral branch, received up to one thousand eight hundred households. Imperial younger sisters created princesses received enfeoffment income of one thousand households; daughters of Emperor Zhongzong were treated the same. Thereafter, imperial sons created princes were granted enfeoffment of two thousand households; imperial daughters created princesses were granted five hundred. Princess Xianyi was granted bath and maintenance revenues. Because her mother was Huifei, her enfeoffment reached one thousand households. All imperial daughters created princesses were by precedent increased to one thousand households. From Kaiyuan onward, enfeoffments were generally capped at three thousand households.

The Prince of Yan, Bin, was Xuanzong's twentieth son. His birth name was Hui. Bin's mother was the granddaughter of Vice Minister of the Right Liu Fan and came from one of the most eminent families of the age. Xuanzong held her in the highest regard. Bin was likewise benevolent and learned. In the thirteenth year of Kaiyuan, 725, he was created Prince of Yan. In the fifteenth year, 727, he was given nominal appointment as Grand Protector of Anxi and military commissioner of Qixi. At the end of the Tianbao era, his son Zhuo was created Prince of Pengcheng with the acting rank of Director of the Secretariat, and Yan as Prince of Pingyang with the acting rank of Director of the Palace Bureau.

濟王環，玄宗第二十二子也，初名溢。 開元十三年三月，封濟王。 二十三年七月，授開府儀同三司，其月改名環。 天寶末有子封為王者二人，傃為永嘉郡王、衛尉卿同正員，俛為平樂郡王、光祿卿同正員。
The Prince of Ji, Huan, was Xuanzong's twenty-second son. His birth name was Yi. In the third month of the thirteenth year of Kaiyuan, 725, he was created Prince of Ji. In the seventh month of the twenty-third year, 735, he was appointed Grand Master of the Palace with Golden Girdle and Privy Seal of the First Rank, and that same month his name was changed to Huan. At the end of the Tianbao era, two of his sons were created princes: Su as Prince of Yongjia with the acting rank of Minister of the Guard, and Mian as Prince of Pingle with the acting rank of Commissioner of the Imperial Household.
21
信王瑝，玄宗第二十三子也，初名沔。 開元十三年三月，封為信王。 二十三年七月，授開府儀同三司，仍改名瑝。
The Prince of Xin, Huang, was Xuanzong's twenty-third son. His birth name was Mian. In the third month of the thirteenth year of Kaiyuan, 725, he was created Prince of Xin. In the seventh month of the twenty-third year, 735, he was appointed Grand Master of the Palace with Golden Girdle and Privy Seal of the First Rank, and his name was changed to Huang.
22
天寶末有子封為王者二人：亻冬為新安郡王、太常卿同正員，倜為晉陵郡王、光祿卿同正員。
At the end of the Tianbao era, two of his sons were created princes: Dong as Prince of Xin'an with the acting rank of Minister of Ceremonial, and Ti as Prince of Jinling with the acting rank of Commissioner of the Imperial Household.
23
義王玭，玄宗第二十四子也，初名漼。 開元十三年三月，封為義王。 二十三年七月，授開府儀同三司，仍改名玭。 天寶末有子封為王者二人：儀為舞陽郡王、太仆卿同正員，僇為高密郡王、宗正卿同正員。
The Prince of Yi, Mi, was Xuanzong's twenty-fourth son. His birth name was Cui. In the third month of the thirteenth year of Kaiyuan, 725, he was created Prince of Yi. In the seventh month of the twenty-third year, 735, he was appointed Grand Master of the Palace with Golden Girdle and Privy Seal of the First Rank, and his name was changed to Mi. At the end of the Tianbao era, two of his sons were created princes: Yi as Prince of Wuyang with the acting rank of Minister of the Stud, and Lu as Prince of Gaomi with the acting rank of Director of the Imperial Clan.
24
陳王珪，玄宗第二十五子也，初名渙。 開元二十三年七月，封為陳王。 二十四年三月改名珪。 天寶末男女二十一人，封為王者二人：佗為臨淮郡王、太常卿同正員，佼為安陽王、殿中監同正員。
The Prince of Chen, Gui, was Xuanzong's twenty-fifth son. His birth name was Huan. In the seventh month of the twenty-third year of Kaiyuan, 735, he was created Prince of Chen. In the third month of the twenty-fourth year, 736, his name was changed to Gui. At the end of the Tianbao era he had twenty-one sons and daughters. Two sons were created princes: Tuo as Prince of Linhuai with the acting rank of Minister of Ceremonial, and Jiao as Prince of Anyang with the acting rank of Director of the Palace Bureau.
25
豐王珙，玄宗第二十六子也，初名澄。 開元二十三年七月，封為豐王。 二十四年二月改名珙。 天寶十五年六月，玄宗幸蜀，至扶風郡，授珙武威郡都督，仍領河西隴右安西北庭等路節度支度采訪使； 以隴右太守鄧景山為之副，兼武威長史、御史中丞，充都副大使。 珙竟不行。
The Prince of Feng, Gong, was Xuanzong's twenty-sixth son. His birth name was Cheng. In the seventh month of the twenty-third year of Kaiyuan, 735, he was created Prince of Feng. In the second month of the twenty-fourth year, 736, his name was changed to Gong. In the sixth month of the fifteenth year of Tianbao, 756, as Xuanzong fled to Shu, at Fufeng commandery he appointed Gong governor of Wuwei and commissioner for investigation, military affairs, and fiscal administration over Hexi, Longyou, Anbei, Beiting, and other circuits. Deng Jingshan, administrator of Longyou, was made his deputy with concurrent appointment as senior administrator of Wuwei and Censor-in-Chief, serving as overall deputy commissioner. Gong never took up the post.
26
廣德元年十月，吐蕃淩逼上都，上將幸陜州，自苑中而出，騎從半渡浐水。 將軍王懷忠遂閉苑門，橫截五百余騎，擁十宅諸王西投吐蕃。 至城西，適遇元帥郭子儀，懷忠謂子儀曰：「主上東遷，社稷無主，萬國颙颙，何所瞻仰！ 今仆奉諸王等西奔，以副天下之望。 令公身為元帥，廢置在手，何不行冊立之事乎？ 」子儀未及對，珙遂越次而言曰：「令公作何語，何不言也？ 」行軍司馬王延昌責之曰：「主上雖蒙塵於外，聖德欽明，王身為籓翰，何乃發狂悖之詞也？ 延昌當奏聞於上。 」子儀又數讓之，命軍士領之盡赴行在。 潼關謁見，上不之責，珙歸幕次，詞又不順。 群臣恐遂為亂，請除之，遂賜死。 天寶中有子二人為王：佻齊安郡王、宗正卿同正員，伷宜春郡王、鴻臚卿同正員。
In the tenth month of the first year of Guangde, 763, as the Tibetans pressed upon the capital, the emperor prepared to flee to Shaanzhou and came out from within the imperial park. His mounted escort had half crossed the Chan River. General Wang Huaizhong then closed the park gates, intercepted more than five hundred horsemen, and drove the princes of the Ten Residences west to surrender to the Tibetans. At the west of the city they happened upon the commander Guo Ziyi. Huaizhong said to him, "The sovereign has moved east. The altars of state stand without a master. The realm looks up in longing—upon whom can they gaze? I now lead these princes west to answer the hopes of the realm. Your Lordship stands as commander, with deposition and installation in your hands—why not perform the rite of enthronement? " Before Ziyi could reply, Gong stepped forward out of turn and said, "What is Your Lordship saying? Why do you not speak? " Campaign aide Wang Yanchang reproached him: "Although the sovereign suffers hardship abroad, his sagely virtue remains revered and bright. Your Highness stands as a screen for the realm—how can you utter words of madness and rebellion? I shall memorialize and report this to the sovereign. " Ziyi reproached him again and again and ordered soldiers to escort them all to the imperial encampment. At Tong Pass they had audience with the emperor, who did not blame him. Gong returned to his quarters, but his words were again improper. The ministers feared he would then stir rebellion and requested his removal. He was ordered to take his own life. During the Tianbao era he had two sons created princes: Tiao as Prince of Qi'an with the acting rank of Director of the Imperial Clan, and Zhou as Prince of Yichun with the acting rank of Minister of the Court for Diplomatic Reception.
27
恆王瑱，玄宗第二十七子也，初名潓。 開元二十三年七月，封為恆王。 性好道，常服道士衣。 授右衛大將軍，加開府儀同三司。 二十四年二月改名瑱。 天寶十五載，從幸巴蜀，不復衣道士衣矣。
The Prince of Heng, Zhen, was Xuanzong's twenty-seventh son. His birth name was Guan. In the seventh month of the twenty-third year of Kaiyuan, 735, he was created Prince of Heng. He was devoted to the Way and habitually wore Daoist robes. He was appointed General-in-Chief of the Right Guard and additionally given the rank of Grand Master of the Palace with Golden Girdle and Privy Seal of the First Rank. In the second month of the twenty-fourth year, 736, his name was changed to Zhen. In the fifteenth year of Tianbao, 756, he followed the flight to Ba-Shu and no longer wore Daoist robes.
28
涼王璿，玄宗第二十九子也，初名漎。 母武賢儀，則天時高平王重規女也，開元中入宮中，號為「小武妃」。 二十三年七月，封為涼王。 二十四年二月，改名璿。
The Prince of Liang, Xuan, was Xuanzong's twenty-ninth son. His birth name was Long. His mother was Lady Wu Xian, daughter of Prince Chonggui of Gaoping in the time of Empress Wu. She entered the palace during Kaiyuan and was called "Little Consort Wu." In the seventh month of the twenty-third year, 735, he was created Prince of Liang. In the second month of the twenty-fourth year, 736, his name was changed to Xuan.
29
初，貞觀中，高宗為晉王，以文德皇后最少子，後崩後累年，太宗憐之，不令出閣，至立為太子。 高宗朝，睿宗為豫王，雖成長，亦以則天最小子，不令出閣。 及至聖歷初，封為相王，始出閣。 中宗時，以譙王重福失愛，出遷外籓，衛王重俊為太子，入與成王千里等起兵，將誅韋後，故溫王重茂雖年十六七，竟亦居中。 先天之後，皇子幼則居內，東封年，以漸成長，乃於安國寺東附苑城同為大宅，分院居，為十王宅。 令中官押之，於夾城中起居，每日家令進膳。 又引詞學工書之人入教，謂之侍讀。 十王，謂慶、忠、棣、鄂、榮、光、儀、潁、永、延、濟，蓋舉全數。 其後，盛、儀、壽、陳、豐、恆、涼六王又就封，入內宅。 二十五年，鄂、光得罪，忠繼大統，天寶中，慶、棣又歿，唯榮、儀等十四王居院，而府幕列於外坊，時通名起居而已。 外諸孫成長，又於十宅外置百孫院。 每歲幸華清宮，宮側亦有十王院、百孫院。 宮人每院四百，百孫院三四十人。 又於宮中置維城庫，諸王月俸物，約之而給用。 諸孫納妃嫁女，亦就十宅中。 太子不居於東宮，但居於乘輿所幸之別院。 太子亦分院而居，婚嫁則同親王、公主，在於崇仁之禮院。
Earlier, during the Zhenguan era, when Gaozong was Prince of Jin, he was the youngest son of Empress Wende. After the empress died he remained in mourning for many years, and Taizong pitied him and would not let him leave the inner quarters until he was named crown prince. During Gaozong's reign, when Ruizong was Prince of Yu, although he had come of age, as Wu Zetian's youngest son he was not permitted to leave the inner quarters. Only at the beginning of the Shenglü era, when he was created Prince of Xiang, did he first leave the inner quarters. During Zhongzong's reign, because the Prince of Qiao, Chongfu, had lost favor he was sent to an outer fief. The Prince of Wei, Chongjun, as crown prince remained within the palace. When he joined the Prince of Cheng, Qianli, and others in raising troops to execute Empress Wei, the Prince of Wen, Chongmao, though only sixteen or seventeen, likewise remained within. After the Xiantian era, young imperial sons lived within the palace. In the year of the eastern feng sacrifice, as they gradually grew up, a great compound was built east of Anguo Temple against the park wall, with separate courtyards for each prince—the Ten Princes' Residence. Eunuchs were assigned to supervise them. They lived within the walled lane, and each day the household steward brought their meals. Scholars skilled in literary composition and calligraphy were brought in to instruct them; they were called lecturing attendants. The ten princes were Qing, Zhong, Di, E, Rong, Guang, Yi, Ying, Yong, Yan, and Ji—a round figure for the full complement. Thereafter the six princes Sheng, Yi, Shou, Chen, Feng, Heng, and Liang were in turn enfeoffed and moved into the inner residence. In the twenty-fifth year, 737, the Princes of E and Guang fell from favor. Zhong succeeded to the throne. During Tianbao, the Princes of Qing and Di also died. Only Rong, Yi, and fourteen other princes remained in the residence, while their staffs were posted in the outer wards and merely sent their names for attendance reports. As the imperial grandsons in the outer line grew up, the Hundred Grandsons' Court was established outside the Ten Residences. Each year when the court visited Huaqing Palace, there were also a Ten Princes' Court and a Hundred Grandsons' Court beside the palace. Each prince's courtyard had four hundred palace women assigned; the Hundred Grandsons' Court had thirty or forty. Within the palace a Weicheng storehouse was also established. The princes' monthly stipends in goods were rationed and distributed from it. When grandsons took consorts or married off daughters, the ceremonies also took place within the Ten Residences. The crown prince did not dwell in the Eastern Palace but only in whatever separate residence the emperor favored at the time. The crown prince also lived in a separate courtyard. For weddings he followed the same practice as imperial princes and princesses, at the ceremonial court in Chongren.
30
汴哀王璥，玄宗第三十子也，初名滔。 開元二十五年七月，封為汴王。 二十四年二月，改名璥，以其月薨。
The Bian'ai Prince Jin was Xuanzong's thirtieth son. His birth name was Tao. In the seventh month of the twenty-fifth year of Kaiyuan, 737, he was created Prince of Bian. In the second month of the twenty-fourth year, 736, his name was changed to Jin, and he died that same month.
31
史臣曰：前史有云：「母愛者子抱」，太子瑛之廢，有由然矣。 琬為元帥，不幸遽薨，豈天啟亂階，何失眾望之速也！ 永王璘，父在蜀城，兄居靈武，不能立忠孝之節，為社稷之謀，而乃聚兵江上，規為己利，不義不昵，以災其身，《書》所謂「自作孽，不可逭」也。 豐王珙因緣厄運，竊有覬覦，不慎樞機，自貽伊咎，悲矣！
The historiographer remarks: The former histories say, "Where the mother is loved, the son is embraced." The deposing of Crown Prince Ying had its causes indeed. Wan as supreme commander died suddenly and untimely—was Heaven itself opening the way to disorder? How swiftly he lost the hopes of the realm! The Prince of Yong, Lin: his father was in Shu, his elder brother at Lingwu. He could not uphold loyalty and filial piety or serve the altars of state, but instead gathered troops on the Yangzi and schemed for his own gain. Unrighteous and disloyal, he brought disaster upon himself—as the Documents say, "He who brings calamity upon himself cannot escape it." The Prince of Feng, Gong, seizing a moment of calamity, secretly harbored designs on the throne. He did not heed the pivot of power and brought ruin upon himself—lamentable indeed!
32
贊曰：《螽斯》之詠，樂有子孫。 用建籓屏，以崇本根。 讒勝瑛廢，恩移至尊。 盜熾琬卒，情乖萬民。 口禍豐珙，自災永璘。 惜乎二胤，不如仁人。
Encomium: The ode "Zhongsi" rejoices in sons and grandsons. They were made to establish the screen of the feudal domains and exalt the root of the house. Slander prevailed and Ying was cast aside; favor shifted to the throne itself. Rebellion flared and Wan died; the people's hopes were betrayed. Reckless words doomed Feng Gong; Yong Lin brought ruin on himself. Pity these two princes—they were no match for men of virtue.
