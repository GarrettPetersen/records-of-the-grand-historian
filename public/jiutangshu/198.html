2
臣觀前代秉筆論文者多矣。 莫不憲章《謨》、《誥》，祖述《詩》、《騷》； 遠宗毛、鄭之訓論，近鄙班、揚之述作。 謂「采采苤諲」，獨高比興之源； 「湛湛江楓」，長擅詠歌之體。 殊不知世代有文質，風俗有淳醨； 學識有淺深，才性有工拙。 昔仲尼演三代之《易》，刪諸國之《詩》，非求勝於昔賢，要取名於今代。 實以淳樸之時傷質，民俗之語不經，故飾以文言，考之弦誦。 然後致遠不泥，永代作程，即知是古非今，未為通論。 夫執鑒寫形，持衡品物，非伯樂不能分駑驥之狀，非延陵不能別《雅》、《鄭》之音。 若空混吹竽之人，即異聞《韶》之嘆。 近代唯沈隱俟斟酌《二南》，剖陳三變； 攄雲、淵之抑郁，振潘、陸之風徽。 俾律呂和諧，宮商輯洽，不獨子建總建安之霸，客兒擅江左之雄。 爰及我朝，挺生賢俊，文皇帝解戎衣而開學校，飾賁帛而禮儒生； 門羅吐鳳之才，人擅握蛇之價。 靡不發言為論，下筆成文，足以緯俗經邦，豈止雕章縟句。 韻諧金奏，詞炳丹青，故貞觀之風，同乎三代。 高宗、天後，尤重詳延； 天子賦橫汾之詩，臣下繼柏梁之奏； 巍巍濟濟，輝爍古今。 如燕、許之潤色王言，吳、陸之鋪揚鴻業，元稹、劉蕡之對策，王維、杜甫之雕蟲，並非肄業使然，自是天機秀絕。 若隋珠色澤，無假淬磨，孔璣翠羽，自成華彩，置之文苑，實煥緗圖。 其間爵位崇高，別為之傳。 今采孔紹安已下，為《文苑》三篇，凱懷才憔悴之徒，千古見知於作者。
Your subject observes that in former ages many men took up the brush to discuss literature. All took the Documents of Yu and the Announcements as their model and traced their lineage to the Classic of Poetry and the Songs of Chu; in distant antiquity they revered the commentaries of Mao and Zheng, while in recent times they disdained the compositions of Ban and Yang. They held that "Plucking the water-chestnut" alone towers as the source of allegory and evocation; "Deep, deep the river maples" excels in the form of lyric song. They little knew that generations differ in refinement and plainness, and that customs differ in purity and decadence; that learning and knowledge differ in depth and shallowness, and talent and nature in skill and clumsiness. Formerly Confucius elaborated the Changes of the Three Dynasties and culled the poems of the various states—not to surpass the sages of old, but to win a name in the present age. In truth, in an age of plain simplicity he lamented crudity, and the speech of common folk was unpolished; therefore he adorned it with literary language and set it to recitation on the strings. Only then did it reach far without sticking fast and serve as a standard for all time—thus one knows that to exalt antiquity and reject the present is no comprehensive judgment. To hold a mirror and draw a likeness, to hold a balance and appraise things—without Bo Le one cannot distinguish nags from thoroughbreds; without Yanling one cannot tell apart the tones of the Elegant and the Zheng. If one merely mixes in those who blow on the yu without knowing how, one differs from those who heard the Shao and sighed. In recent times only Shen Yue weighed the Two Souths and analyzed the three transformations; he gave voice to the melancholy of Bao and Yuan and revived the fine style of Pan and Lu. He made pitch and mode harmonious and palace and merchant tones accord—not only did Zijian gather the hegemony of the Jian'an era and Keren monopolize the glory of the Jiangzuo. Down to our dynasty worthy and brilliant men arose; the Literary Emperor laid aside armor and opened schools, adorned silks and honored Confucian scholars; his gates gathered talents that could make the phoenix speak, and every man possessed the worth of one who grasped the snake. None failed to speak and become discourse or take up the brush and complete a composition—enough to bind custom and govern the state, not merely to carve chapters and weave sentences. Rhyme harmonized with golden music and words blazed like cinnabar and azure; therefore the style of the Zhenguan era matched the Three Dynasties. Under Gaozong and the Heavenly Empress, court patronage was especially broad and detailed; the Son of Heaven composed the poem on crossing the Fen, and his ministers followed with the Bo Liang ode; towering and abundant, they shine through past and present. Such as Yan and Xu polishing the royal words, Wu and Lu spreading the great enterprise, Yuan Zhan and Liu Zao answering policy questions, Wang Wei and Du Fu carving fine phrases—all were not merely so because of training, but by nature possessed supreme genius. Like the luster of Sui pearls, needing no tempering by grindstone; like the emerald feathers of Kong feathers, forming their own splendor—placed in the grove of letters, they truly brighten the silk scrolls. Among them those of lofty rank and eminence are given separate biographies. Now I gather from Kong Shao'an downward into three chapters of Men of Letters, cherishing those of talent who withered in obscurity, that through the ages they may be known to authors.

孔紹安
Kong Shao'an
4
孔紹安，越州山陰人，陳吏部尚書奐之子。 少與兄紹新，俱以文詞知名。 十三，陳亡入隋，徙居京兆鄠縣。 閉門讀書，誦古文集數十萬言，外兄虞世南嘆異之。 紹新嘗謂世南曰：「本朝淪陷，分從湮滅，但見此弟，竊謂家族不亡矣！」 時有詞人孫萬壽，與紹安篤忘年之好，時人稱為孫、孔。 紹安大業末為監察御史。 時高祖為隋討賊於河東，詔紹安監高祖之軍，深見接遇。 及高祖受禪，紹安自洛陽間行來奔。 高祖見之甚悅，拜內史舍人，賜宅一區、良馬兩匹、錢米絹布等。 時夏侯端亦嘗為御史，監高祖軍，先紹安歸朝，授秘書監。 紹安因侍宴，應詔詠《石榴詩》曰：「只為時來晚，開花不及春。」 時人稱之。 尋詔撰《梁史》，未成而卒。 有文集五卷。
Kong Shao'an was a man of Shanyin in Yue Prefecture, son of Huan, Minister of the Civil Service of Chen. In youth he and his elder brother Shaoxin were both renowned for literary composition. At thirteen, when Chen fell and he entered Sui, he moved to Hu County in Jingzhao. He shut his doors and read books, reciting several hundred thousand words of ancient collected writings; his maternal cousin Yu Shinan marveled at him. Shaoxin once said to Shinan, "Our dynasty has fallen and been scattered and extinguished, but seeing this younger brother, I secretly say the clan is not dead!" At the time the writer Sun Wanshou and Shao'an were deeply bound in friendship across the years; contemporaries called them Sun and Kong. In the late Daye era Shao'an served as Supervising Censor. At the time Gaozu was suppressing bandits at Hedong for Sui; an edict made Shao'an overseer of Gaozu's army, and he was deeply received and treated. When Gaozu accepted the abdication, Shao'an came from Luoyang by secret route to join him. Gaozu was greatly pleased to see him, appointed him Palace Secretary Drafting Clerk, and granted him one residence, two fine horses, and money, rice, silk, and cloth. At the time Xiahou Duan had also once been a censor overseeing Gaozu's army; before Shao'an he returned to court and was appointed Director of the Secretariat. Shao'an then, while attending a banquet, composed on imperial command the poem On the Pomegranate, saying, "Only because the season came late—its blossoms did not keep pace with spring." Contemporaries praised it. Soon an edict ordered him to compile the History of Liang; he died before completing it. He had a collected works in five scrolls.

Son Zhen

子禎，高宗時為蘇州長史。 曹王明為刺史，不循法度，禎每進諫。 明曰：「寡人天子之弟，豈失於為王哉！」 禎曰：「恩寵不可恃，大王不奉行國命，恐今之榮位，非大王所保，獨不見淮南之事乎？」 明不悅。 明左右有侵暴下人者，禎捕而杖殺之。 明後果坐法，遷於黔中，謂人曰：「吾愧不用孔長史言，以及於此！」 禎累遷絳州刺史，封武昌縣子。 卒，謚曰溫。
Zi Zhen, under Gaozong served as Chief Administrator of Suzhou. Prince of Cao Li Ming was prefectural governor and did not follow the law; Zhen often remonstrated. Ming said, "I am the Son of Heaven's younger brother—surely I am not inferior as a prince!" Zhen said, "Favor and grace cannot be relied upon. If the Great King does not carry out the state's commands, I fear today's honored rank is not what the Great King can keep—have you alone not seen the affair of Huainan?" Ming was displeased. Among Ming's attendants were those who tyrannized the people below; Zhen seized and beat them to death. Ming later indeed fell afoul of the law and was moved to Qianzhong; he said to people, "I am ashamed that I did not heed Director Kong's words, and have come to this!" Zhen was repeatedly promoted to Prefect of Jiang Prefecture and enfeoffed as Baron of Wuchang. He died; his posthumous title was Wen.

紹安孫若思。
Shao'an's grandson Ruosi.
11
若思孤，母褚氏親自教訓，遂以學行知名。 年少時，有人賫褚遂良書跡數卷以遺若思，唯受其一卷。 其人曰：「此書當今所重，價比黃金，何不總取？」 若思曰：「若價比金寶，此為多矣！」 更截去半以還之。 明經舉，累遷庫部郎中。 若思常謂人曰：「仕至郎中足矣！」 至是持一石止水，置於座右，以示有止足之意。 尋遷給事中。
Ruosi was orphaned; his mother Lady Chu personally taught and instructed him, and he became renowned for learning and conduct. In his youth someone brought several scrolls of Chu Suiliang's calligraphy as a gift to Ruosi; he accepted only one scroll. The man said, "This writing is prized in the present age, its price equal to gold—why not take them all?" Ruosi said, "If the price equals gold and treasure, this is already too much!" He further cut off half and returned it. Recommended through the Mingjing examination, he was repeatedly promoted to Director in the Ministry of Revenue. Ruosi often said to people, "To reach Director is enough!" At this he placed a stone of still water before his seat to show his intent of knowing when to stop. Soon he was promoted to Supervising Censor.
12
中宗即位，敬暉、桓彥範等知國政，以若思多識故事，所有改革大事及疑議，多訪於若思。 再轉禮部侍郎，出衛州刺史。 先是，諸州別駕，皆以宗室為之，不為刺史致敬，由是多行不法。 若思至州，舉奏別駕李道欽犯狀，請加鞫訊。 乃詔別駕於刺史致禮，自若思始也。 俄以清白稱，加銀青光祿大夫，賜絹百匹。 歷汝州刺史、太子右諭德，封梁郡公。 開元十七年卒，謚曰惠。
When Zhongzong took the throne, Jing Hui, Huan Yanfan, and others managed state affairs; because Ruosi knew many precedents, in all major reforms and doubtful matters they often consulted Ruosi. He was twice transferred to Vice Minister of Rites and sent out as Prefect of Weizhou. Previously, Vice Prefects of the various prefectures were all members of the imperial clan; the prefect did not show them ceremonial respect, and therefore many acted unlawfully. When Ruosi reached the prefecture, he memorialized and reported Vice Prefect Li Daoqin's offenses and requested intensified interrogation. An edict then required Vice Prefects to show ceremonial respect to prefects—beginning with Ruosi. Soon he was famed for integrity and promoted to Grand Master of Silver and Blue Light, and granted one hundred bolts of silk. He served as Prefect of Ru Prefecture and Right Tutor of the Heir Apparent, and was enfeoffed as Duke of Liang. In the seventeenth year of Kaiyuan he died; his posthumous title was Hui.

袁朗
Yuan Lang
14
袁朗，雍州長安人，陳尚書左僕射樞之子。 其先自陳郡仕江左，世為冠族，陳亡徙關中。
Yuan Lang was a man of Chang'an in Yong Prefecture, son of Shu, Left Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs of Chen. His ancestors, from Chen commandery, served in the Jiangzuo and for generations were a leading clan; when Chen fell they moved to Guanzhong.
15
朗勤學，好屬文。 在陳，釋褐秘書郎，甚為尚書令江總所重。 嘗制千字詩，當時以為盛作。 陳後主聞而召入禁中，使為《月賦》，朗染翰立成。 後主曰：「觀此賦，謝希逸不能獨美於前矣！」 又使為《芝草》、《嘉蓮》二頌，深見優賞。 歷太子洗馬、德教殿學士，遷秘書丞。 陳亡，仕隋為尚書儀曹郎。 武德初，授齊王文學、祠部郎中，封汝南縣男，再轉給事中。 貞觀初卒官。 太宗為之廢朝一日，謂高士廉曰：「袁朗在任雖近，然其性謹厚，特使人傷惜。」 因敕給其喪事，並存問妻子。 有文集十四卷。
Lang studied diligently and loved literary composition. In Chen he entered service as Secretary Gentleman and was greatly valued by Department Director Jiang Zong. He once composed a thousand-character poem, which contemporaries regarded as a great work. Emperor Houzhu of Chen heard of this and summoned him into the inner palace, ordering him to compose a Rhapsody on the Moon; Lang took up the brush and completed it at once. Houzhu said, "Reading this rhapsody, Xie Xiyi cannot alone claim beauty before it!" He further had him compose two eulogies, On the Spirit Grass and On the Fine Lotus, and deeply favored and rewarded him. He served as Attendant of the Heir Apparent, Academic Scholar of the Hall of Virtuous Teaching, and was promoted to Assistant Director of the Secretariat. When Chen fell, he served Sui as Director in the Ministry of Rites. In the early Wude era he was appointed Literary Companion to Prince of Qi, Director in the Ministry of Sacrifices, enfeoffed as Baron of Runan, and twice transferred to Supervising Censor. In the early Zhenguan era he died in office. Taizong suspended court for one day on his account and said to Gao Shilian, "Yuan Lang's tenure, though brief, yet his nature was careful and generous—this especially makes one grieve." He then ordered his funeral provided and sent inquiries to wife and children. He had a collected works in fourteen scrolls.

Younger paternal cousin Chengxu
17
從父弟承序，陳尚書僕射憲之子。 武德中，齊王元吉聞其名，召為學士。 府廢，累轉建昌令。 在任清靜，士吏懷之。 高宗在籓，太宗選學行之士為其僚屬，謂中書侍郎岑文本曰：「梁、陳名臣，有誰可稱？ 復有子弟堪招引否？」 文本因言：「隋師入陳，百司奔散，莫有留者，唯袁憲獨在其主之傍。 王世充將受隋禪，群僚表請勸進，憲子給事中承家托疾，獨不署名。 此父子足稱忠烈。 承家弟承序，清貞雅操，實繼先風。」 由是召守晉王友，仍令侍讀，加授弘文館學士。 未幾，卒。
Younger paternal cousin Chengxu, son of Xian, Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs of Chen. In the Wude era Prince of Qi Li Yuanji heard his name and summoned him as Academic Scholar. When the princely establishment was abolished, he was repeatedly transferred to Magistrate of Jianchang. In office he was quiet and clean; officials and clerks cherished him. When Gaozong was still a prince, Taizong selected men of learning and conduct for his staff and said to Vice Director of the Secretariat Cen Wenben, "Among the famous ministers of Liang and Chen, who can be praised? Are there also sons and younger brothers worth recruiting?" Wenben then said, "When Sui's army entered Chen, the hundred offices fled and scattered—none remained; only Yuan Xian alone stayed at his lord's side. When Wang Shichong was about to accept Sui's abdication, the assembled officials memorialized urging him to advance; Xian's son Supervising Censor Chengjia feigned illness and alone did not sign. This father and son are enough to be called loyal and resolute. Chengjia's younger brother Chengxu, of pure and upright elegant conduct, truly continued the ways of his forebears." On this account he was summoned to serve as Friend to the Prince of Jin, and was further ordered to attend and read, with additional appointment as Academic Scholar of the Hongwen Hall. Before long he died.

從祖弟利貞
Younger paternal cousin of the same grandfather Li Zhen
19
朗從祖弟利貞，陳中書令敬之孫也。 高宗時為太常博士、周王侍讀。 永隆二年，王立為皇太子，百官上禮。 高宗將會百官及命婦於宣政殿，並設九部伎及散樂。 利貞上疏諫曰：「臣以前殿正寢，非命婦宴會之地； 象闕路門，非倡優進禦之所。 望詔命婦會於別殿，九部伎從東西門入，散樂一色，伏望停省。 若於三殿別所，自可備極恩私。 微臣庸蔽，不閑典則，忝預禮司，輕陳狂瞽。」 帝納其言，即令移於麟德殿。 至會日，酒酣，帝使中書侍郎薛元超謂利貞曰：「卿門承忠鯁，能抗疏直言，不加厚賜，何以獎勸！」 賜物百段。 俄遷祠部員外郎，卒。 中宗即位，以侍讀恩，追贈秘書少監。
Lang's younger paternal cousin of the same grandfather Li Zhen was grandson of Jing, Director of the Secretariat of Chen. Under Gaozong he was Erudite of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and Attendant Reader to the Prince of Zhou. In the second year of Yonglong the Zhou Prince was established as Heir Apparent; the hundred officials presented ritual observances. Gaozong was about to assemble the hundred officials and titled ladies at the Hall of Propagating Governance, and also set up the nine sections of music and miscellaneous entertainments. Li Zhen submitted a memorial remonstrating, saying, "Your subject considers that the main hall in the front is not a place for titled ladies' banquets; the Elephant Gate and Road Gate are not places where singers and actors should enter to perform for the throne. I hope an edict may order titled ladies to assemble in a separate hall, the nine sections of music to enter through the east and west gates, and the miscellaneous entertainments to be of one kind—I humbly hope they may be stopped and reduced. If in a separate hall of the three palaces, one may naturally prepare to the utmost private favor. Your humble subject, benighted and obstructed, unversed in canonical norms, presumptuously holding office in the Ministry of Rites, lightly states this rash blindness." The Emperor accepted his words and immediately ordered the assembly moved to Linde Hall. On the day of the assembly, when wine had gone deep, the Emperor had Vice Director of the Secretariat Xue Yuanchao say to Li Zhen, "Your clan has inherited loyal remonstrance; you were able to submit a forthright memorial—if we do not grant rich reward, how shall we encourage such conduct!" He granted one hundred lengths of goods. Soon he was promoted to Vice Director in the Ministry of Sacrifices and died. When Zhongzong took the throne, because of his service as Attendant Reader, he was posthumously promoted to Vice Director of the Secretariat.

朗十三代祖漢司徒滂，滂生魏國郎中、御史大夫渙，渙生晉尚書準，準生東晉右將軍、豫章太守沖，沖生司徒從事中郎耽，耽生瑯邪內史質，質生丹陽尹、宋公長史豹，豹生宋吳郡太守洵，累代有高名重位，前史有傳。 五代叔祖宋太尉淑，高祖父左僕射、雍州刺史顗，高祖司空察，皆死國難。 曾祖梁中書監、司空、穆公昂，仕齊為吳興太守，及梁高祖禪齊，久辭朝命。 父樞，叔父憲，仕陳，皆為陳僕射。 叔祖敬，中書令。 及陳亡，憲冒難扶護後主。 朗自以中外人物，為海內冠族，雖瑯邪王氏繼有臺鼎，而歷朝自為佐命，鄙之不以為伍。
Lang's thirteenth-generation ancestor Pang, Minister of Works of Han; Pang begot Huan, Director of the Bureau of Attendants and Censor-in-Chief of Wei; Huan begot Zhun, Director of the Secretariat of Jin; Zhun begot Chong, General of the Right and Prefect of Yuzhang of Eastern Jin; Chong begot Dan, Attendant of the Secretariat of the Ministry of Works; Dan begot Zhi, Interior Minister of Langye; Zhi begot Bao, Chief Administrator and Senior Clerk of Duke of Song; Bao begot Xun, Prefect of Wu of Song—for generations they had high fame and heavy rank, recorded in earlier histories. Shu, fifth-generation granduncle, Grand Commandant Shu of Song; great-grandfather Yan, Left Vice Director and Prefect of Yong of the Yuan clan; great-great-grandfather Cha, Minister of Works—all died for the state's peril. Great-grandfather Ang, Director of the Secretariat and Minister of Works, Duke Mu, served Qi as Prefect of Wuxing; when Liang's Founder abdicated Qi he long refused court appointment. Father Shu and paternal uncle Xian both served Chen and were both Vice Directors of the Department of State Affairs. Granduncle Jing was Director of the Secretariat. When Chen fell, Xian braved peril to support and protect Houzhu. Lang, considering himself among eminent figures within and without, the leading clan within the seas, though the Wang clan of Langye for generations had held the highest offices yet in every dynasty served as supporters of the throne—he despised them and would not rank himself with them.

Wang Bo. Styled Zi'an, he was a man of Longmen in Jiang Prefecture. Grandfather Tong, Records Clerk of the Household of Shu Commandery of Sui. In the late Daye era he abandoned office and returned home, making authorship and lecturing his occupation. Following the Spring and Autumn format, from after the Capture of the Lin stag through Qin and Han down to Later Wei he authored a chronicle book called the Original Classic. Ji immediately summoned five clerks, each ordered to hold brush; he dictated and divided the writing—all finished at once. Language and principle were canonical and ample; all sighed in admiration. Soon he was added Academic Scholar of the Hongwen Hall and concurrently Acting Vice Director of the Celestial Office. Ji quite wielded power and associated with improper sorts. In the second year of Wansui Tongtian, Qi Lianyao's plot at treason was exposed; Ji was punished for friendship with Yao, and together with younger brother Bi both were executed.
111
閟累官至涇州刺史。 神龍初，有詔追復苾、閟官位。
Bi was repeatedly promoted to Prefect of Jing Prefecture. In the early Shenlong era an edict restored the official ranks of Ji and Bi.
112
福畤，天後朝以子貴，累轉澤州長史，卒。
Fuchao, in the Heavenly Empress era because of his son's eminence was repeatedly transferred to Chief Administrator of Ze Prefecture and died.
113
初，吏部侍郎裴行儉典選，有知人之鑒，見苾與蘇味道，謂人曰：「二子亦當掌銓衡之任。」 李敬玄尤重楊炯、盧照鄰、駱賓王與勃等四人，必當顯貴。 行儉曰：「士之致遠，先器識而後文藝。 勃等雖有文才，而浮躁淺露，豈享爵祿之器耶！ 楊子沈靜，應至令長，余得令終為幸。」 果如其言。
Initially Vice Director of Personnel Pei Xingjian managed selection and had the discernment to know men; seeing Ji and Su Weidao he said to people, "These two sons also ought to hold the balance of selection." Li Jingxuan especially valued Yang Jiong, Lu Zhaolin, Luo Binwang, and Bo and the four men—they would surely reach eminence. Xingjian said, "For a scholar to reach far, first capacity and discernment, afterward literary art. Bo and the others though having literary talent were yet floating, shallow, and exposed—are they vessels to enjoy rank and salary? Yang Zi is calm and still—he ought to reach magistrate; for the rest to end as magistrate would be my fortune." It turned out as he said.
114
勃文章邁捷，下筆則成，尤好著書。 撰《周易發揮》五卷，及《次論》等書數部。 勃亡後，並多遺失。 有文集三十卷。 勃聰警絕眾，於推步歷算尤精，嘗作《大唐千歲歷》，言唐德靈長千年，不合承周、隋短祚。 其論大旨云：「以土王者，五十代而一千年； 金王者，四十九代而九百年； 水王者，二十代而六百年； 木王者，三十代而八百年； 火王者，二十代而七百年。 此天地之常期，符歷之數也。 自黃帝至漢，並是五運真主。 五行已遍，土運復歸，唐德承之，宜矣！ 魏、晉至於周、隋，鹹非正統，五行之沴氣也，故不可承之。」 大率如此。
Bo's writing was surpassingly quick; taking up the brush it was done; he especially loved authorship. He compiled Elaboration on the Changes of Zhou in five scrolls, and the Sequential Discourses and other books in several parts. After Bo's death many were lost. He had a collected works in thirty scrolls. Bo was clever and alert beyond the multitude; in calendrical calculation and reckoning he was especially precise; he once composed the Thousand-Year Calendar of the Great Tang, saying Tang's virtue would endure a thousand years and ought not inherit the short reigns of Zhou and Sui. The main argument said, "Those who rule by earth—fifty generations and one thousand years; those who rule by metal—forty-nine generations and nine hundred years; those who rule by water—twenty generations and six hundred years; those who rule by wood—thirty generations and eight hundred years; those who rule by fire—twenty generations and seven hundred years. These are heaven and earth's constant periods, the numbers of talisman and calendar. From the Yellow Emperor through Han—all were true lords of the Five Phases. The Five Phases having run their course, the earth phase returns; Tang virtue inherits it—fitting! Wei and Jin down to Zhou and Sui—all were not legitimate succession, the baleful qi of the Five Phases; therefore they cannot be inherited." The general drift was thus.

駱賓王
Luo Binwang
116
駱賓王，婺州義烏人。 少善屬文，尤妙於五言詩，嘗作《帝京篇》，當時以為絕唱。 然落魄無行，好與博徒遊。 高宗末，為長安主簿。 坐贓，左遷臨海丞，怏怏失誌，棄官而去。 文明中，與徐敬業於揚州作亂。 敬業軍中書檄，皆賓王之詞也。 敬業敗，伏誅，文多散失。 則天素重其文，遣使求之。 有兗州人郤雲卿集成十卷，盛傳於世。
Luo Binwang was a man of Yiwu in Wu Prefecture. In youth he was skilled at literary composition, especially marvelous at five-character poetry; he once composed On the Imperial Capital, which contemporaries regarded as a supreme song. Yet he was down-and-out without conduct and loved to roam with gamblers. In the late Gaozong era he was Chief Clerk of Chang'an. Punished for corruption he was demoted left Chief Clerk of Linhai; resentful and losing ambition he abandoned office and left. In the Wenming era he with Xu Jingye at Yang Prefecture raised rebellion. All proclamations in Jingye's army were Binwang's words. Jingye was defeated and executed; much of the writing was scattered and lost. Zetian had long valued his writing and sent envoys to seek it. A man of Yan Prefecture, Xi Yunqing, assembled ten scrolls, greatly transmitted in the world.

鄧玄挺
Deng Xuanting
118
鄧玄挺，雍州藍田人。 少善屬文，累遷左史。 坐與上官儀善，出為頓丘令。 有善政，璽書勞問。 累授中書舍人。 性俊辨，機捷過人，每有嘲謔，朝廷稱為口實。 則天臨朝，遷吏部侍郎，既不稱職，甚為時談所鄙。 又患消渴之疾，選人目為「鄧渴」，為榜於衢路。 自有唐已來，掌選之失，未有如玄挺者。 坐此左遷澧州刺史。 在州復以善政聞，遷晉州刺史，召拜麟臺少監，重為天官侍郎，其失又甚於前。 玄挺女為道王子諲妻，又與蔣王子煒相善。 諲謀迎中宗於房陵，以問玄挺。 煒又嘗謂玄挺曰：「欲作急計如何？」 玄挺雖皆不答，而不以告。 永昌元年得罪，下獄死。
Deng Xuanting was a man of Lantian in Yong Prefecture. In youth he was skilled at literary composition and was repeatedly promoted to Left Historian. Punished for friendship with Shangguan Yi he was sent out Magistrate of Dunqiu. He had good governance; an imperial letter commended and inquired. He was repeatedly appointed Palace Secretary Drafting Clerk. By nature handsome in debate, nimble and surpassing others—whenever there was mockery the court took it as a byword. When Zetian held court she promoted him Vice Director of Personnel; not competent in office, he was greatly despised in contemporary talk. Also suffering from wasting-thirst illness, selectors called him "Deng Thirsty" and posted placards on the public road. Since Tang began, failures in managing selection had none like Xuanting. On this account he was demoted left Prefect of Li Prefecture. In the prefecture again he was heard for good governance; he was promoted Prefect of Jin and summoned appointed Vice Director of the Lin Terrace, again Vice Director of the Celestial Office—the failures worse than before. Xuanting's daughter was wife to Prince of Dao Li Jin; she was also on good terms with Prince of Jiang Li Wei. Jin plotted to welcome Zhongzong from Fangling and asked Xuanting. Wei also once said to Xuanting, "Want to make an urgent plan—how?" Though Xuanting answered neither, he did not report them. In the first year of Yongchang he was punished, imprisoned and died.
