王玙，少習禮學，博求祠祭儀注以干時。 開元末，玄宗方尊道術，靡神不宗。 玙抗疏引古今祀典，請置春壇，祀青帝於國東郊，玄宗甚然之，因遷太常博士、侍御史，充祠祭使。 玙專以祀事希幸，每行祠禱，或焚紙錢，禱祈福祐，近於巫覡，由是過承恩遇。 肅宗即位，累遷太常卿，以祠禱每多賜賚。 乾元三年七月，兼蒲州刺史，充蒲、同、絳等州節度使。 中書令崔圓罷相，乃以玙為中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事。 人物時望，素不為眾所稱，及當樞務，聲問頓減。 玙又奏置太一神壇於南郊之東，請上躬行祀事。 肅宗嘗不豫，太卜云：「崇在山川。 」玙乃遣女巫分行天下，祈祭名山大川。 巫皆盛服乘傳而行，上令中使監之，因緣為奸，所至干托長吏，以邀賂遺。 一巫盛年而美，以惡少年數十自隨，尤為蠹弊，與其徒宿於黃州傳舍。 刺史左震晨至，驛門扃鐍，不可啟，震破鎖而入，曳女巫階下斬之，所從惡少年皆斃。 閱其贓賂數十萬，震籍以上聞，仍請贓錢代貧民租稅，其中使發遣歸京，肅宗不能詰。 肅宗親謁九宮神，慇勤於祠禱，皆玙所啟也。 歲餘，罷知政事，為刑部尚書。 上元二年，兼揚州長史、御史大夫。 兗淮南節度使。 肅宗南郊禮畢，以玙使持節都督越州諸軍事、越州刺史，充浙江東道節度觀察處置使，本官兼御史大夫，祠祭使如故。 入為太子少保，轉少師。 。 大曆三年六月卒。
Wang Yu studied ritual learning from his youth and assiduously collected sacrificial protocols and regulations in hopes of winning favor at court. In the closing years of the Kaiyuan reign, Emperor Xuanzong was exalting Daoist practices, and no deity went unhonored. Yu memorialized the throne, citing sacrificial precedents old and new, and asked that a spring altar be established to worship the Green Emperor in the eastern suburbs of the capital. Xuanzong strongly approved; Yu was promoted to Erudite of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and Attending Censor and made Commissioner of Sacrificial Rites. Yu made sacrificial business his path to favor. Whenever he conducted rites and prayers he would burn spirit money and plead for divine protection—conduct close to shamanism—and so won extravagant favor from the throne. After Suzong came to the throne, Yu rose in stages to Minister of Imperial Sacrifices and received frequent largesse for his ritual prayers. In the seventh month of Qianyuan 3 (760), he was also appointed Prefect of Pu and Military Commissioner over Pu, Tong, Jiang, and neighboring prefectures. When Chief Minister Cui Yuan left office, Yu was appointed Vice Director of the Secretariat and Associate Chief Minister. He had never enjoyed much esteem among his contemporaries, and once he took up the levers of power his reputation fell sharply. Yu further proposed erecting an altar to the Great Unity Spirit east of the southern suburban altar and asked that the emperor perform the rites in person. When Suzong fell ill, the Grand Diviner declared, "The malady stems from mountains and rivers." Yu then sent shamankesses throughout the empire to offer prayers and sacrifices at famous mountains and great rivers. The shamankesses traveled in grand attire on relay horses while palace envoys supervised them; both parties abused their posts, importuning local officials for bribes wherever they went. One young and beautiful shamankess traveled with several dozen rowdy youths and was especially abusive; she and her band lodged at the Huangzhou relay station. At dawn Prefect Zuo Zhen found the station gate bolted shut. He broke the lock, dragged the shamankess down the steps and executed her, and killed all her rowdy followers as well. He found bribes totaling several hundred thousand cash, reported them to the throne, and asked that the confiscated funds be applied to poor tenants' rent and taxes. He sent the supervising palace envoy back to the capital, and Suzong did not pursue the matter. Suzong's personal worship at the Nine Palaces shrines and his fervent ritual devotions were all Yu's doing. After little more than a year he left the chancellery and was appointed Minister of Justice. In Shangyuan 2 (761) he was also appointed Senior Administrator of Yangzhou and Censor-in-Chief. He also served as Huainan military commissioner. After Suzong completed the southern suburban sacrifices, Yu was sent with credentials as overall commander of Yuezhou forces and as Prefect of Yue, and was made Military, Observation, and Disposition Commissioner for Zhejiang East Circuit, retaining his posts as Censor-in-Chief and Commissioner of Sacrificial Rites. He was recalled to court as Junior Tutor of the Heir Apparent and later promoted to Junior Preceptor. . He died in the sixth month of Dali 3 (768).

玙以祭祀妖妄致位將相，時以左道進者，往往有之。 廣德二年八月，道士李國禎以道術見，因奏皇室仙系，宜修崇靈跡。 請於昭應縣南三十里山頂置天華上宮露台、大地婆父、三皇、道君、太古天皇、中古伏羲媧皇等祠堂，並置掃灑宮戶一百戶。 又於縣之東義扶谷故湫置龍堂，並許之。 時歲饑荒，人甚不安，昭應縣令梁鎮上表曰：
Yu had risen to general and chief minister through superstitious ritualism, and many others at the time advanced by similar heterodox means. In the eighth month of Guangde 2 (764), the Daoist Li Guozhen gained audience through his occult arts and urged that the imperial clan's immortal lineage be honored by restoring its sacred sites. He asked that shrines be built on a mountaintop thirty li south of Zhaoying County—including the Heavenly Flower Upper Palace terrace, the Great Earth Mother and Father, the Three Sovereigns, the Daoist Lord, the Most Ancient Celestial Emperor, and Fuxi and Nüwa of middle antiquity—and that one hundred households be assigned to maintain them. He also proposed a Dragon Hall at the old pool in Yifu Valley east of the county, and the court approved both plans. Famine had gripped the land and the people were in distress. 6
臣聞國以人為本，害其本則非國； 神以人為主，虐其主則非神。 故昔之聖王，所以極陳理道，明著祀典，將愛其人而慎用其財力，敬其神而虔恭於祠祭。 故神享其明德而降之福，人受其大賚而盡其力，然後神人以和，而國家可保也。 一昨蟊賊作孽，水旱為災，雖王畿皆遍，而臣縣最苦。 此則神之不能御大災明矣，又何力於陛下而得列祀典哉！ 用以殘弊之餘，當凶荒之歲，丁壯素出家入仕，羸老方飛芻輓粟，令但供億王事，已不堪命，更奔走鬼道，何以聊生？
I have heard that a state rests upon its people; harm the foundation and it ceases to be a state; and that spirits take the people as their masters; maltreat the masters and they are no longer worthy of worship. The sage kings of old therefore expounded the Way at length, defined the sacrificial canon clearly, cherished their people and husbanded their resources, and honored the spirits with sincere ritual devotion. Then the spirits accepted their bright virtue and sent down blessings, the people received royal bounty and gave their full effort, and only then could spirits and people live in harmony and the state endure. Rebels have ravaged the land and flood and drought have struck; though the capital region has suffered everywhere, my county has borne the worst of it. This proves plainly that the spirits cannot avert great calamities. What power have they to aid Your Majesty that they deserve a place in the state cult? To demand more of a people already broken, in years of famine, when the able-bodied have long since left for government service and the aged barely manage to haul grain—men who can scarcely meet the crown's levies—how can they also be driven to serve these ghostly cults and still survive?
7
臣又聞天地之神，尊之極者，掃地可祭，精意可饗。 陛下亦何必廢先王之典，崇俗巫之說，走南畝之客，殺東鄰之牛，而後冀非妄之福。 陛下雖欲為人祈福，福未至而人已困矣！ 其不可一也。 陛下不視昔者有道之君，至德之後，曷不卑宮室，惡飲食，恭己以遂萬物之性哉！ 陛下今違神亭育之心，竭人疲睏之力，如是又何從而致其福哉？ 此又不可二也。 又陛下宗廟之敬極矣，尚無一月三祭之禮； 今此獨為，則宗廟之靈，將等以親疏，校以厚薄，陛下又何以言哉？ 此又不可三也。 又大地婆父，祀典無文，言甚不經，義無可取。 若陛下待與大地建祖宗之廟，必上天貽向背之責，陛下又何以為詞哉？ 此又不可四也。 夫湫者，龍之所居也。 龍得水則神，無水則螻蟻之匹也。 故知水存則龍在，水竭則龍亡，此愚智之所同知矣。 今湫竭已久，龍安所存？ 陛下又崇飾祠宇，豐潔薦奠，為去龍之穴，破生人之產，人且怨矣，神何歆哉！ 此又不可五也。 其道君、三皇、五帝，則兩京及所都之處，皆建宮觀祠廟，時設齋醮饗祀，國有彝典，官有常禮，蓋無闕失，何勞神役靈？ 此又不可六也。 臣稽先王之典禮，觀前聖之軌躅，休咎豐凶，災祥禍福，必主帝王五事，不在山川百神。 此又不可七也。
I have also heard that the greatest spirits of Heaven and Earth may be worshipped with swept ground alone and received with pure sincerity. Why must Your Majesty abandon the rites of the ancient kings, exalt vulgar shamanism, drive farmers from their fields, slaughter the neighbor's ox, and only then hope for blessings that are not vain? Though Your Majesty wishes to bless the people, blessings have not arrived and the people are already exhausted! This is the first reason the proposal must be rejected. Does Your Majesty not consider the sage rulers of old, heirs of supreme virtue, who humbled their palaces, abhorred extravagance, and governed with reverent restraint so that all things might flourish? Your Majesty now defies the nurturing spirit of Heaven and exhausts a people already worn down—how can blessings possibly follow? This is the second reason it must be rejected. Your Majesty's devotion to the ancestral temple is already supreme, yet even there no rite calls for three sacrifices in a single month; yet this cult alone would be singled out for lavish worship. How then can the ancestral spirits be ranked by kinship and measured by favor? What defense can Your Majesty offer? This is the third reason it must be rejected. The cult of the Great Earth Mother and Father has no place in the canonical rites; the doctrine is absurd and the principle worthless. If Your Majesty erects an ancestral temple to the Great Earth, Heaven itself will hold you accountable for reversing proper order. What answer will Your Majesty give? This is the fourth reason it must be rejected. A sacred pool is the dragon's dwelling. A dragon with water is divine; without water it is no more than an ant. Where water remains, the dragon remains; where water fails, the dragon is gone—this fool and sage alike understand. The pool has been dry for years—where can any dragon abide? Yet Your Majesty would lavishly adorn shrines and heap offerings at a site the dragon has abandoned, ruining the livelihood of the living. The people already resent it—what spirit could accept such worship? This is the fifth reason it must be rejected. The Daoist Lord, the Three Sovereigns, and the Five Emperors already have temples and observatories in both capitals and wherever the court resides, with regular fasts and sacrifices prescribed by statute. Nothing is lacking—why summon spirits to do the work again? This is the sixth reason it must be rejected. I have studied the rites of the ancient kings and the example of the sages: fortune and misfortune, plenty and famine, omens good and ill—all depend on the sovereign's five duties, not on the myriad spirits of mountains and rivers. This is the seventh reason it must be rejected.
8
臣伏察此弊，頗知其由。 蓋以道士李國禎等動眾則得人，興工則獲利，祭祀則受胙，主執則弄權。 是以鼓動禁中，熒惑天聽，踰越險阻，負荷粢盛，以日系年，無時而息。 曾不謂神功力，空止竭人膏血，以使人神胥怨，災孽並生。 罔上害人，左道亂政，原情定罪，非殺而何！
I have looked closely into this abuse and understand its cause. Daoists such as Li Guozhen gain followers by mobilizing crowds, profit from construction, receive sacrificial portions from rites, and wield power once put in charge. Thus they agitate within the palace, mislead the emperor's ear, cross dangerous terrain, and burden the people with grain levies—day after day, year after year, without pause. They never credit the spirits with any power; they only drain the people's lifeblood until spirits and men alike resent the court and disasters multiply. They deceive the throne, harm the people, and subvert government by heterodox means. Judged by their intent, what punishment but death is fitting?
9
臣昨受命之時，親承聖旨，務存安緝，許逐權宜。 誠願沉鄴縣之巫，安流弊之俗，其所興兩祠土木之功、丹青之役、三六之祭、灑掃之戶，謹明宣旨，並以權宜停訖。 人吏百姓等，知陛下以從善為心，嫉惡為務，蠲除不急，劃革煩苛，皆喧呼於庭，抃躍於路，所徵糧糗，無不樂輸。 臣伏以國禎等並交結中貴，狡蠹成性，臣雖忘身許國，不懼讒構，終恐賄及豪右，復為奸惡。 其國禎等見據狀推勘，如獲贓狀，伏望許臣徵收，便充當縣郵館本用。 其湫既竭，不可更置祠堂，又不當為大地建立祖廟，臣並請停。 其三皇、道君、天皇、伏羲、女媧等，既先各有宮廟，望請並於本所依禮齋祭。
When I took office I received Your Majesty's instruction to preserve peace and was authorized to act as circumstances required. I earnestly wish to suppress the shamans as Ye County did and restore a people abused by corrupt custom. By Your Majesty's order I have halted construction on both shrines, suspended painting work, stopped the thrice-daily sacrifices, and disbanded the maintenance households. Officials and commoners alike, seeing that Your Majesty welcomes good counsel and abhors evil, rejoice in court and dance in the streets at the removal of needless burdens. Whatever levies remain, they pay gladly. Guozhen and his fellows consort with palace favorites and are corrupt by nature. Though I am ready to risk myself for the state and do not fear slander, I fear that once bribes reach the powerful they will resume their abuses. The evidence against Guozhen and his associates warrants confiscation as in a theft case. I beg leave to seize their property and apply it to the county relay station's operating costs. The pool is dry and no shrine should be built there; nor should an ancestral temple be raised to the Great Earth. I ask that both projects be halted. The Three Sovereigns, Daoist Lord, Celestial Emperor, Fuxi, Nüwa, and the rest already have temples elsewhere. I ask that they be worshipped only at those established sites according to proper rite.

李泌，字長源，其先遼東襄平人，西魏太保、八柱國司徒徒何弼之六代孫。 今居京兆吳房令承休之子。 少聰敏，博涉經史，精究《易象》，善屬文，尤工於詩，以王佐自負。 張九齡、韋虛心、張廷珪皆器重之。 泌操尚不羈，恥隨常格仕進。 天寶中，自嵩山上書論當世務，玄宗召見，令侍詔翰林，仍東宮供奉。 楊國忠忌其才辯，奏泌嘗為《感遇詩》，諷刺時政，詔於蘄春郡安置，乃潛遁名山，以習隱自適。 天寶末，祿山構難，肅宗北巡，至靈武即位，遣使訪召。 會泌自嵩、潁間冒難奔赴行在，至彭原郡謁見，陳古今成敗之機，甚稱旨，延致臥內，動皆顧問。 泌稱山人，固辭官秩，特以散官寵之，解褐拜銀青光祿大夫，俾掌樞務。 至於四言文狀、將相遷除，皆與泌參議，權逾宰相，仍判元帥廣平王軍司馬事。 肅宗每謂曰：「卿當上皇天寶中，為朕師友，下判廣平行軍，朕父子三人，資卿道義。 」其見重如此。 尋為中書令崔圓、幸臣李輔國害其能，將有不利於泌。 泌懼，乞游衡山，優詔許之，給以三品祿俸，遂隱衡岳，絕粒棲神。
Li Bi, courtesy name Changyuan, was descended from Xiangping in Liaodong—six generations removed from Tuohe Bi, Grand Guardian and Minister of Education, one of the Eight Pillars of Western Wei. His family now lived in the capital region; he was the son of Magistrate Chengxiu of Wufang. From youth he was brilliant, widely read in the classics and histories, and deeply versed in the Book of Changes. He wrote well and excelled especially at poetry, holding himself capable of ministering to a sage ruler. Zhang Jiuling, Wei Xuxin, and Zhang Tinggui all held him in high regard. Bi's temperament was free-spirited, and he disdained the conventional path to office. During the Tianbao reign he sent a memorial from Mount Song on affairs of state. Xuanzong summoned him, appointed him Hanlin attendant, and assigned him to the heir apparent's household. Yang Guozhong, jealous of his brilliance, accused him of satirizing the government in his "Poems on Encountering Fate." An edict banished him to Qichun, but Bi fled into the mountains and lived as a recluse. At the end of Tianbao, An Lushan rebelled. Suzong marched north, took the throne at Lingwu, and sent envoys to find him. Bi himself hurried from the Song and Ying region through the turmoil to the mobile court. At Pengyuan he had audience, expounded the patterns of rise and fall through history, and greatly pleased the emperor, who kept him at his bedside and consulted him on every matter. Bi called himself a mountain recluse and refused regular office, but the emperor honored him with a sinecure title, appointing him Silver-Gleaming Grand Master of Splendid Happiness and entrusting him with affairs of state. Memorials in four-character prose and appointments of generals and ministers were all discussed with Bi. His authority exceeded that of the chief minister, and he also served as army vice marshal on the staff of the Prince of Guangping. Suzong often told him, "In the Tianbao years under the Retired Emperor you were teacher and friend to me; now you direct the Prince of Guangping's army. We three—father and sons—depend on your counsel and integrity." Such was the esteem in which he was held. Before long Chief Minister Cui Yuan and the favorite Li Fuguo, jealous of his talent, plotted against him. Fearing for his life, Bi asked leave to retire to Mount Heng. A gracious edict granted his request with third-rank stipend, and he lived in seclusion on the sacred mountain, fasting and cultivating the spirit.
12
興元初，征赴行在，遷左散騎常侍。 貞元元年，除陝州長史，充陝、虢都防禦觀察使。 二年六月，泌奏：「虢州盧氏山冶，近出瑟瑟，請充獻，禁人開採。 」詔曰：「瑟瑟之寶，中土所無今產於近甸，實為靈貺。 朕不飾器玩，不尚珍奇，常思返朴之風，用明躬儉之節。 其出瑟瑟之處，任百姓求采，不宜禁止。 」就加泌檢校禮部尚書。 時陳、許戍邊卒三千自京西逃歸，至州境，泌潛師險隘，左右攻擊，盡誅之。 尋拜中書侍郎、平章事、集賢崇文館學士、修國史。 初，張延賞大減官員，人情咨怨，泌請復之，以從人欲，因是奏罷兼試額內佔闕等官，加百官俸料，隨閒劇加置手力課，上從之，人人以為便。 而竇參旁奏，遂改易，使同品之內，月俸多少累等。 泌又奏請罷拾遺、補闕，上雖不從，亦不授人，故諫司惟韓皋、歸登而已。 泌仍命收其署湌錢，令登等寓食於中書舍人，故時戲云：「韓諫議雖分左右，歸拾遺莫辨存亡。 」如是者三年。 至貞元五年，以前東都防禦判官、殿中侍御史、內供奉韋綬為左補闕，監察御史梁肅右補闕。 既復置，人心忻然。 順宗在春宮，妃蕭氏母郜國公主交通外人，上疑其有他，連坐貶黜者數人，皇儲亦危。 泌百端奏說，上意方解。
At the opening of the Xingyuan reign he was summoned to court and promoted to Left Regular Attendant. In Zhenyuan 1 (785) he was appointed Senior Administrator of Shanzhou and Military Commissioner of the Shan-Guo defense circuit. In the sixth month of the second year Bi memorialized: "The smeltery at Mount Lushi in Guozhou has lately yielded sese gems. I ask that they be reserved for tribute and that private mining be forbidden." The edict replied: "Sese gems are unknown in the central plains; that they now appear in our own domain is indeed a divine gift." We do not adorn ourselves with luxuries or prize rare curios; we ever seek the spirit of simplicity and practice personal frugality." Let the people mine sese where it is found; it should not be prohibited." Bi was thereupon additionally appointed Acting Minister of Rites. When three thousand Chen and Xu border troops deserted from west of the capital and entered his jurisdiction, Bi ambushed them in a narrow pass and killed them all. Soon after he was appointed Vice Director of the Secretariat, Chief Minister, Academician of the imperial libraries, and Compiler of National History. Earlier Zhang Yanshang had drastically cut the bureaucracy, provoking widespread resentment. Bi proposed restoring posts to satisfy public opinion, abolishing officials who held supernumerary slots, raising salaries, and adding clerical allowances scaled to each post's workload. The emperor approved, and all regarded the reforms as beneficial. But Dou Can intervened with a side memorial, and the plan was altered so that officials of the same rank received widely differing monthly salaries. Li Bi again asked to abolish the Reminder and Supplementation posts. The emperor refused, but he also appointed no one else, so the Remonstrance Bureau held only Han Gao and Gui Deng. Bi still confiscated their office meal allowances and made Deng and the others board and eat with the Secretariat drafters, whence the jest: "Han the Remonstrator may divide left from right, but for Gui the Reminder you cannot tell the living from the dead." This continued for three years. In the fifth year of Zhenyuan, Wei Shou—former staff judge of the Eastern Capital defense command, Palace Attendant censor, and inner attendant—was appointed Left Supplementation, and Investigating Censor Liang Su was appointed Right Supplementation. Once the offices were restored, public sentiment brightened. While Shunzong was heir apparent, the mother of his consort of the Xiao clan—Princess Gouguo—was found to have improper dealings with outsiders. The emperor suspected worse, several people were punished by association, and the crown prince himself was imperiled. Bi argued the matter from every angle in memorials, and the emperor's suspicions were finally allayed.

Bi had a reputation for blunt remonstrance, yet he also spoke of immortals and occult arts; rumor held that he had once consorted with Chisongzi, Wang Qiao, Anqi, and Xianmen. His contemporaries therefore looked down on him, and although he courted favor through esoteric talk, the rulers of his day did not truly esteem him. When Dezong first ascended the throne, he especially detested shamans, temple diviners, and bizarre occultists. Earlier, Suzong had favored yin-yang lore and temple divination, appointed the sorcerer Wang Yu as chief minister, and sometimes sent witch-women by courier post through the prefectures and counties to perform exorcistic rites. Every construction project or public work was hemmed in by taboos. Li Qian rose to capital magistrate through heterodox practices; he once assembled artisans inside the palace to weave pearl-embroidered imperial robes, then burned them upon completion as expiatory rites—hardly a month passed without such a ceremony. Dezong had learned of these practices as crown prince; once enthroned he stopped assembling monks at the inner chapel and abolished shaman rites. The responsible offices reported damage to the inner corridor of Xuanzheng Hall and asked permission to repair it. The emperor replied, "The Spring and Autumn Annals teach that work should follow the seasons—what Kui Ridge taboo is there?" He ordered the repairs carried out. By the late Jianzhong era, rebels pressed inward; Sang Daomao urged fortifying Fengtian; the emperor grew mindful of calendrical taboos and had long heard that Li Bi was skilled in occult arts, so he recalled him from exile to high office—an appointment public opinion did not welcome. In office as chief minister he simply bent with circumstances—nothing worth commending. He again surrounded himself with Gu Kuang and other frivolous men, who were constantly mocked by the court and brought him much ridicule. He died at sixty-eight and was posthumously appointed Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent, with unusually generous funeral gifts. Bi was free-spirited, quick-witted, and fond of bold pronouncements; from the time he moved in and out of the inner palace, powerful favorites repeatedly envied him, yet he always escaped harm through his wits; and in the end, through forceful counsel he won over the sage emperor and rose to the chief ministership. His collected works ran to twenty juan.

The emperor said, "Your words accord perfectly with my intent." On his return he was appointed Vice Director of the Bureau of Military Affairs and promoted to Junior Vice Governor of Hezhong.
24
建中初，張鎰為河中少尹。 鎰尋入相，二年七月，遷播給事中。 舊例，諸司甲庫，皆是胥吏掌知，為弊頗久，播始建議並以士人知之，至今稱當。 轉刑部侍郎、奉迎皇太后副使。 盧杞以播柔緩，冀其易制，驟稱薦之。 尋遷吏部侍郎，轉刑部尚書、知刪定。 奏上元中，詔擇古今名將十人於武成王廟配享，如文宣王廟之儀。 播以「太公古稱大賢，今其下稱亞聖，於義不安。 又孔子十哲，皆是當時弟子，今所擇名將，年代不同，於義既乖，於事又失。 臣請刪去名將配享之儀及十哲之稱。 」從之。
At the opening of the Jianzhong reign, Zhang Yi was junior vice governor of Hezhong. Yi soon became chief minister. In the seventh month of the second year Bo was promoted to Drafting Attendant. By long custom every bureau's archives had been run by clerks, a source of chronic abuse. Bo was first to propose placing them under literati, a reform still praised as sound. He was transferred to Vice Minister of Justice and deputy commissioner for welcoming the empress dowager. Lu Qi, finding Bo mild and pliable, hoped to control him easily and suddenly praised him to the throne. Soon he was promoted to Vice Minister of Personnel, then Minister of Justice with charge of legal revision. He noted that in the Shangyuan era an edict had named ten famous generals for secondary sacrifice in the Temple of the Martial Accomplishment King, following the model of Confucius's temple. Bo argued that "the Grand Duke was called a great sage in antiquity; to rank others beneath him as sub-sages is improper in principle." Confucius's Ten Wise Ones were all his own disciples, whereas the chosen generals belong to different ages—the principle is wrong and the practice mistaken. I ask that the generals' secondary sacrifice and the title Ten Wise Ones both be abolished. The emperor approved.
25
建中三年十月，拜銀青光祿大夫、中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事、集賢殿崇文館大學士、修國史。 時政事決在盧杞，播但斂衽取容而已。 乏於知人之鑑，好大言虛誕者，播必悅而親信之。 有李元平、陶公達、張愻、劉承誡，皆言談詭妄，讠誇大可立功名，亦有微材薄藝。 播累奏雲元平等皆可將相也，請閱試用之，上以為然，以元平為補闕。 會淮西節度李希烈叛亂，上以汝州要鎮，令選擇刺史。 播薦元平為汝州刺史，尋加檢校吏部郎中、汝州別駕，知州事。 元平至州旬日，為希烈所擒，汝州陷賊，中外哂之。 由是公達等未克任用。 播與盧杞等從駕幸奉天，既而杞、白志貞等並貶黜，播尚知政事，中外囂然，以為不可，遂罷相，改刑部尚書。 大臣韋倫等泣於朝曰：「宰相不能謀猷翊贊，以至今日，而尚為尚書，可痛心也！」
In the tenth month of Jianzhong 3 (782) he was appointed Silver-Gleaming Grand Master of Splendid Happiness, Vice Director of the Secretariat, Associate Chief Minister, Grand Academician of the imperial libraries, and Compiler of National History. State affairs rested with Lu Qi; Bo merely folded his hands and sought to please. He lacked discernment in judging men and delighted in boastful charlatans, whom he always favored and trusted. Among them were Li Yuanping, Tao Gongda, Zhang Xun, and Liu Chengjie—all given to wild talk and boasting of great deeds, though they possessed only slight talents. Bo repeatedly memorialized that Yuanping and the others were fit for general and ministerial rank and asked that they be tested. The emperor agreed and appointed Yuanping Supplementation. When Li Xilie of Huainan West rebelled, the emperor, deeming Ruzhou a critical post, ordered a prefect chosen. Bo recommended Yuanping as prefect of Ruzhou, then additionally as Acting Director of the Personnel Bureau and vice prefect in charge of the prefecture. Within ten days of reaching Ruzhou, Yuanping was captured by Xilie and the prefecture fell. Court and country alike mocked the appointment. Gongda and the others were never appointed. Bo followed Lu Qi and the court to Fengtian. After Qi and Bai Zhizhen were demoted, Bo still held power, provoking widespread outrage. He was removed from the chancellery and made Minister of Justice. Minister Wei Lun and others wept in open court, "The chief minister could neither plan nor assist, and brought us to this pass—yet he remains a minister! It is heartbreaking!"
26
貞元四年，回紇請和親，以咸安公主出降可汗，令播以本官加檢校右僕射、兼御史大夫，持節充送咸安公主及冊可汗使，奉使往來，皆清儉謹慎，蕃人悅之。 使回，遷兵部尚書，固辭疾，請罷官，改太子少師致仕。 播致仕之後，減去僮僕車騎，閉關守靜，不縈外事，士君子重之。 貞元十三年正月卒，時年七十九，廢朝一日，贈太子太保。
In Zhenyuan 4 (788) the Uyghurs sought a marriage alliance and Princess Xian'an was sent to wed the qaghan. Bo was appointed Acting Right Vice Director and Censor-in-Chief with credentials as envoy escorting the princess and investing the qaghan. On every mission he was frugal and careful, winning the foreigners' esteem. On his return he was promoted to Minister of Military Affairs but pleaded illness, asked to leave office, and was granted retirement as Junior Preceptor of the Heir Apparent. In retirement he reduced his household and retinue, shut his gates, and kept aloof from public affairs. Men of honor respected him for it. He died in the first month of Zhenyuan 13 (797) at the age of seventy-nine. The court suspended audiences for a day and posthumously enfeoffed him Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent.
27
李元平者，宗室子。 始為湖南觀察使蕭復判官，試大理評事。 性疏傲，敢大言，好論兵，天下賢士大夫無可其意者，以是人多銜怒。 關播奇重之，許以將帥。 時希烈反叛，朝廷以汝州與賊接壤，刺史韋光裔懦弱不任職，播乃盛稱元平，特召見，超左補闕，不數日，擢為檢校吏部郎中，兼汝州別駕，知州事。 既至部，募工徒繕理郛郭，希烈乃使勇士應募，執役板築，凡入數百人，元平不之覺。 希烈遣偽將李克誠以數百騎突至其城，先應募執役者應於內，縛元平馳去。 既見希烈，遺下污地。 希烈見其無須眇小，戲謂克誠曰：「使汝取李元平，何得將元平兒來？ 」因嫚罵曰：「盲宰相使汝當我，何待我淺耶！ 」偽署為御史中丞。 播聞元平得用，仍欺於人曰：「李生功業濟矣。 」言必能覆希烈而建功也。 居無何，希烈用為宰相，或告其有二者，乃斷一指以自誓。 希烈既死，或有人言在賊中微有謀慮，貸死流於珍州。 會赦得歸剡中，浙東觀察使皇甫政表聞其到，以發上怒，復流賀州而死。
Li Yuanping was a member of the imperial clan. He first served as staff judge to Hunan observation commissioner Xiao Fu and held the provisional title of Grand Court Reviewer. He was coarse and arrogant, spoke boldly, and loved to discourse on warfare. No worthy official in the realm met his standard, and many bore him resentment. Guan Bo thought him remarkable and promised him a general's command. When Xilie rebelled, Ruzhou bordered rebel territory and Prefect Wei Guangyi proved incompetent. Bo lavishly praised Yuanping, who was specially summoned, leap-promoted to Left Supplementation, and within days made Acting Director of the Personnel Bureau and vice prefect of Ruzhou in charge of the prefecture. On reaching his post he recruited laborers to repair the walls. Xilie sent warriors to enlist as laborers—several hundred in all—and Yuanping never noticed. Xilie sent his general Li Kecheng with several hundred horsemen to storm the city. The enlisted laborers opened the gates from within, seized Yuanping, and rode off with him. When brought before Xilie, he fouled himself on the ground. Xilie, seeing he was beardless and slight, joked to Kecheng, "I sent you for Li Yuanping—why have you brought his son?" He then cursed him, "A blind chief minister sent you against me—do you take me so lightly!" Xilie gave him the false title of Vice Censor-in-Chief. When Bo heard Yuanping had been appointed, he still told people, "Master Li's great deeds are about to succeed." He claimed Yuanping would surely overthrow Xilie and win glory. Before long Xilie made him chief minister. When someone accused him of divided loyalty, he cut off a finger to swear his faith. After Xilie's death someone reported that he had shown some counsel among the rebels. His life was spared and he was exiled to Zhenzhou. An amnesty allowed him to return to Kuai, but Zhedong observation commissioner Huangfu Zheng reported his arrival, provoking the emperor's wrath. He was exiled again to Hezhou, where he died.
28
史臣曰：蒸嘗礿祀，前王制以奉先； 怪力亂神，宣聖鄙而不語。 凡雲左道，固有舊章。 玙假於鬼神，乃至將相，既處代天之位，爰滋亂政之源。 國禎妖人疑眾，妄恢其祀典； 梁鎮正士抗疏，方悟其上心。 泌見可進而知難退，足為高率智辯之士； 居相位而談鬼神，乃見狂妄浮薄之蹤。 《王制》云：「執左道以亂政，殺。 」寧無畏乎！ 繁之醜行，棄於當時，竟陷非辜，諒由素履。 造為臣得禮，蒞事非能； 播居位取容，舉人敗事。 皆非國器，咸歷台司，失人者亡，國其危矣。
The historiographer writes: Summer and winter offerings and border sacrifices were instituted by the ancient kings to honor the ancestors; but strange powers and spirits disturbing order—the Sage himself despised and would not discuss. Heterodox ways have always been forbidden by statute. Yu invoked ghosts and spirits to rise to general and chief minister; once he stood in the place of Heaven's deputy, he became a fountainhead of misgovernment. Guozhen the sorcerer misled the people and wantonly expanded the state cult; only when the upright Liang Zhen submitted his defiant memorial did the emperor's mind awaken. Bi knew when to advance and when to withdraw—truly a man of lofty wisdom and eloquence; yet to hold the chancellery while discoursing on spirits revealed arrogant frivolity. The Royal Regulations state, "Whoever employs heterodox ways to subvert government shall be put to death." Did they feel no fear? Fan's vile conduct earned the scorn of his age; that he died unjustly was no doubt the fruit of his lifelong ways. Zao understood propriety as a minister but lacked capacity in office; Bo sought only to please in office and ruined affairs through his appointments. None were fit to govern the state, yet all held high office. When rulers lose the right men, the state perishes—and the realm was indeed in peril.
29
贊曰：玙、泌、造、播，俱非相材。 國禎左道，梁生直哉！
In summary: Yu, Bi, Zao, and Bo were none of them fit for the chancellery. Guozhen walked the heterodox path; Master Liang was upright indeed!
