王玙，少習禮學，博求祠祭儀注以干時。 開元末，玄宗方尊道術，靡神不宗。 玙抗疏引古今祀典，請置春壇，祀青帝於國東郊，玄宗甚然之，因遷太常博士、侍御史，充祠祭使。 玙專以祀事希幸，每行祠禱，或焚紙錢，禱祈福祐，近於巫覡，由是過承恩遇。 肅宗即位，累遷太常卿，以祠禱每多賜賚。 乾元三年七月，兼蒲州刺史，充蒲、同、絳等州節度使。 中書令崔圓罷相，乃以玙為中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事。 人物時望，素不為眾所稱，及當樞務，聲問頓減。 玙又奏置太一神壇於南郊之東，請上躬行祀事。 肅宗嘗不豫，太卜云：「崇在山川。 」玙乃遣女巫分行天下，祈祭名山大川。 巫皆盛服乘傳而行，上令中使監之，因緣為奸，所至干托長吏，以邀賂遺。 一巫盛年而美，以惡少年數十自隨，尤為蠹弊，與其徒宿於黃州傳舍。 刺史左震晨至，驛門扃鐍，不可啟，震破鎖而入，曳女巫階下斬之，所從惡少年皆斃。 閱其贓賂數十萬，震籍以上聞，仍請贓錢代貧民租稅，其中使發遣歸京，肅宗不能詰。 肅宗親謁九宮神，慇勤於祠禱，皆玙所啟也。 歲餘，罷知政事，為刑部尚書。 上元二年，兼揚州長史、御史大夫。 兗淮南節度使。 肅宗南郊禮畢，以玙使持節都督越州諸軍事、越州刺史，充浙江東道節度觀察處置使，本官兼御史大夫，祠祭使如故。 入為太子少保，轉少師。 。 大曆三年六月卒。
Wang Yu studied ritual learning from youth and assiduously collected manuals on sacrificial rites, hoping thereby to advance his career. By the late Kaiyuan period, Emperor Xuanzong had turned to Daoist practices and venerated every deity he encountered. Yu memorialized the throne with citations from ancient and contemporary ritual codes, urging a spring altar for worship of the Green Emperor east of the capital. Xuanzong warmly approved, and Yu was promoted to Erudite of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and Attending Censor, with additional duty as Commissioner of Sacrificial Rites. Yu made sacrificial office his path to favor. At every rite he would burn spirit money and pray for blessings in ways scarcely distinguishable from shamanism, and so won extravagant favor from the throne. After Suzong's accession, Yu rose step by step to Minister of Imperial Sacrifices and received lavish rewards for his prayers and sacrifices. In the seventh month of Qianyuan 3 (760), he was named concurrent Prefect of Pu and Military Commissioner over Pu, Tong, Jiang, and neighboring prefectures. When Chief Minister Cui Yuan left office, Yu was appointed Vice Director of the Secretariat and Associate Chief Minister. He had never enjoyed much public esteem, and once he entered the inner councils of state his reputation fell sharply. Yu further proposed an altar to the Great Unity Spirit east of the southern suburban shrine and asked that the emperor perform the rites in person. When Suzong fell ill, the Grand Diviner reported, "The remedy lies in honoring mountains and rivers." Yu thereupon sent female shamans throughout the empire to offer prayers at famous mountains and rivers. The shamans traveled in grand attire on imperial relay horses while palace eunuchs supervised them. Supervisors and shamans alike abused their authority, pressuring local officials for bribes wherever they went. One shaman, young and handsome, traveled with several dozen rowdy youths and was the worst of the lot. She and her followers lodged at the Huang Prefecture relay station. At dawn Prefect Zuo Zhen found the station gate bolted shut. He broke the lock, dragged the shaman down the steps and beheaded her, and had her rowdy followers executed as well. Zhen found bribes worth hundreds of thousands of cash, reported the case to the throne, and asked that the confiscated funds offset taxes owed by the poor. The supervising eunuch was sent back to Chang'an, and Suzong did not pursue the matter further. Suzong's personal worship at the Nine Palaces shrines and his fervent devotion to prayer were all Yu's doing. A year later he was dismissed from the chancellery and appointed Minister of Justice. In Shangyuan 2 (761) he was named concurrent Chief Administrator of Yangzhou and Censor-in-Chief. He also served as military commissioner of Huainan. After Suzong completed the southern suburban sacrifices, Yu was dispatched as commissioner with full authority over Yue prefecture, named Prefect of Yue and Military Commissioner of the Zhejiang Eastern Circuit, while retaining his posts as Censor-in-Chief and Commissioner of Sacrificial Rites. He returned to court as Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent and was later promoted to Junior Preceptor. " He died in the sixth month of Dali 3 (768).

玙以祭祀妖妄致位將相，時以左道進者，往往有之。 廣德二年八月，道士李國禎以道術見，因奏皇室仙系，宜修崇靈跡。 請於昭應縣南三十里山頂置天華上宮露台、大地婆父、三皇、道君、太古天皇、中古伏羲媧皇等祠堂，並置掃灑宮戶一百戶。 又於縣之東義扶谷故湫置龍堂，並許之。 時歲饑荒，人甚不安，昭應縣令梁鎮上表曰：
Yu had risen to ministerial rank through superstitious rites, and many others at the time advanced by similar heterodox means. In the eighth month of Guangde 2 (764), the Daoist Li Guozhen gained audience through his arts and urged that the imperial clan's immortal lineage deserved restored and venerated holy sites. He asked for shrines on a hilltop thirty li south of Zhaoying County—including the Heavenly Flower Upper Palace terrace, the Great Earth Mother and Father, the Three Sovereigns, the Daoist Lord, the Primordial Heavenly Sovereign, and Fuxi and Nüwa—plus one hundred households assigned to maintain them. He also proposed a Dragon Hall at the old pool in Yifu Valley east of the county, and the court approved both plans. Famine had left the people desperate. Magistrate Liang Zhen of Zhaoying submitted a memorial:
6
臣聞國以人為本，害其本則非國； 神以人為主，虐其主則非神。 故昔之聖王，所以極陳理道，明著祀典，將愛其人而慎用其財力，敬其神而虔恭於祠祭。 故神享其明德而降之福，人受其大賚而盡其力，然後神人以和，而國家可保也。 一昨蟊賊作孽，水旱為災，雖王畿皆遍，而臣縣最苦。 此則神之不能御大災明矣，又何力於陛下而得列祀典哉！ 用以殘弊之餘，當凶荒之歲，丁壯素出家入仕，羸老方飛芻輓粟，令但供億王事，已不堪命，更奔走鬼道，何以聊生？
I have heard that a state rests upon its people; harm the foundation and the state itself is undone; and spirits exist for the sake of the people; mistreat the people and the spirit is no spirit at all. The sage kings of antiquity therefore expounded the Way, codified ritual worship, cherished their subjects, husbanded public wealth, honored the gods, and approached sacrifice with sincere reverence. When the spirits received such virtue they bestowed blessings; when the people received such bounty they gave their full strength—only then were gods and men in harmony and the realm secure. Rebellion and flood and drought have ravaged the land. Though the capital region has suffered everywhere, my county has borne the worst of it. If the local spirits could not ward off such disasters, what power have they to deserve a place in the imperial ritual canon? In our ruined land, in years of famine, the strong have long since left for government service or the clergy while the old and weak haul fodder and grain. They can scarcely meet the demands of the throne—how can they also labor for these ghostly rites and still survive?
7
臣又聞天地之神，尊之極者，掃地可祭，精意可饗。 陛下亦何必廢先王之典，崇俗巫之說，走南畝之客，殺東鄰之牛，而後冀非妄之福。 陛下雖欲為人祈福，福未至而人已困矣！ 其不可一也。 陛下不視昔者有道之君，至德之後，曷不卑宮室，惡飲食，恭己以遂萬物之性哉！ 陛下今違神亭育之心，竭人疲睏之力，如是又何從而致其福哉？ 此又不可二也。 又陛下宗廟之敬極矣，尚無一月三祭之禮； 今此獨為，則宗廟之靈，將等以親疏，校以厚薄，陛下又何以言哉？ 此又不可三也。 又大地婆父，祀典無文，言甚不經，義無可取。 若陛下待與大地建祖宗之廟，必上天貽向背之責，陛下又何以為詞哉？ 此又不可四也。 夫湫者，龍之所居也。 龍得水則神，無水則螻蟻之匹也。 故知水存則龍在，水竭則龍亡，此愚智之所同知矣。 今湫竭已久，龍安所存？ 陛下又崇飾祠宇，豐潔薦奠，為去龍之穴，破生人之產，人且怨矣，神何歆哉！ 此又不可五也。 其道君、三皇、五帝，則兩京及所都之處，皆建宮觀祠廟，時設齋醮饗祀，國有彝典，官有常禮，蓋無闕失，何勞神役靈？ 此又不可六也。 臣稽先王之典禮，觀前聖之軌躅，休咎豐凶，災祥禍福，必主帝王五事，不在山川百神。 此又不可七也。
I have also heard that the greatest spirits of Heaven and Earth may be worshipped with a swept floor and pure intent alone. Why must Your Majesty abandon the rituals of the former kings, elevate vulgar shamanism, drive farmers from their fields, slaughter the neighbor's ox—as in the old proverb—and only then hope for blessings that are not vain? Though Your Majesty wishes to bless the people, the people are exhausted before any blessing can arrive! This is the first reason the project must be refused. Has Your Majesty forgotten how the sage rulers of old, heirs of supreme virtue, kept their palaces humble, their fare plain, and governed with restraint so that all things might flourish? Your Majesty now defies the nurturing spirit of Heaven, draining a people already spent—how can such conduct bring blessing? This is the second reason. Your Majesty's reverence for the ancestral temple is already supreme, yet even there the court does not perform three sacrifices in a single month; yet for these new shrines alone you would lavish worship. Would not the spirits of the ancestral temple be ranked by closeness and measured by favor? What could Your Majesty say in reply? This is the third reason. The Great Earth Mother and Father appear nowhere in the ritual canon. The proposal is absurd and without merit. If Your Majesty builds an ancestral temple to Earth, Heaven itself will hold you accountable for reversing the natural order. What defense could Your Majesty offer? This is the fourth reason. A pool is the dwelling of dragons. With water the dragon is divine; without water it is no more than an ant. When the water remains, the dragon remains; when the water dries up, the dragon is gone. Fool and sage alike understand this. The pool has been dry for years—where then is the dragon? Yet Your Majesty would adorn shrines and heap offerings at a site the dragon has abandoned, destroying the livelihood of the living. The people will resent it—what spirit could delight in such worship? This is the fifth reason. The Daoist Lord, the Three Sovereigns, and the Five Emperors already have temples and regular sacrifices in both capitals and wherever the court resides. The state has canonical rites and officials perform them without fail. Why summon spirits to labor anew? This is the sixth reason. I have examined the rituals of the former kings and the example of the sage rulers of old. Fortune and misfortune, blessing and calamity, depend upon the five duties of the sovereign—not upon the hundred spirits of mountains and rivers. This is the seventh reason.
8
臣伏察此弊，頗知其由。 蓋以道士李國禎等動眾則得人，興工則獲利，祭祀則受胙，主執則弄權。 是以鼓動禁中，熒惑天聽，踰越險阻，負荷粢盛，以日系年，無時而息。 曾不謂神功力，空止竭人膏血，以使人神胥怨，災孽並生。 罔上害人，左道亂政，原情定罪，非殺而何！
I have looked closely at this abuse and understand its cause. Daoists such as Li Guozhen gain followers by mobilizing crowds, profit by launching construction, sacrificial fees by officiating at rites, and power by controlling ceremonies. They stir the inner palace, dazzle the imperial ear, cross dangerous terrain, and burden the people with sacrificial supplies—day after day, year after year, without cease. They never speak of divine power, only drain the people's lifeblood until gods and men alike resent them and disasters multiply. They deceive the throne, harm the people, and disorder government through heterodox ways. Judged by their deeds, what punishment short of death could suffice?
9
臣昨受命之時，親承聖旨，務存安緝，許逐權宜。 誠願沉鄴縣之巫，安流弊之俗，其所興兩祠土木之功、丹青之役、三六之祭、灑掃之戶，謹明宣旨，並以權宜停訖。 人吏百姓等，知陛下以從善為心，嫉惡為務，蠲除不急，劃革煩苛，皆喧呼於庭，抃躍於路，所徵糧糗，無不樂輸。 臣伏以國禎等並交結中貴，狡蠹成性，臣雖忘身許國，不懼讒構，終恐賄及豪右，復為奸惡。 其國禎等見據狀推勘，如獲贓狀，伏望許臣徵收，便充當縣郵館本用。 其湫既竭，不可更置祠堂，又不當為大地建立祖廟，臣並請停。 其三皇、道君、天皇、伏羲、女媧等，既先各有宮廟，望請並於本所依禮齋祭。
When I took office I received Your Majesty's instruction to preserve peace and was permitted to act as circumstances required. I wished to suppress the Ye County shamans and calm these corrupt customs. I have announced Your Majesty's will and, acting on expedient authority, halted construction of both shrines, the painting work, the thrice-daily sacrifices, and the maintenance households. Officials and commoners alike, seeing that Your Majesty favors what is good and hates evil, rejoiced in the courtyards and danced in the streets at the removal of needless burdens. Whatever grain was levied, they paid gladly. Guozhen and his fellows are intimate with palace favorites and corruption is their nature. Though I would give my life for the state and do not fear slander, I fear their bribes will reach the powerful and their abuses resume. The evidence against Guozhen and his associates is as clear as proof of theft. I beg leave to confiscate their gains for the county post-house. The pool is dry and no shrine should be built there; no ancestral temple should be raised to Earth. I ask that both projects be halted. The Three Sovereigns, Daoist Lord, Heavenly Sovereign, Fuxi, Nüwa, and the rest already have temples elsewhere. I ask that sacrifice continue at those established sites according to ritual.

11
李泌，字長源，其先遼東襄平人，西魏太保、八柱國司徒徒何弼之六代孫。 今居京兆吳房令承休之子。 少聰敏，博涉經史，精究《易象》，善屬文，尤工於詩，以王佐自負。 張九齡、韋虛心、張廷珪皆器重之。 泌操尚不羈，恥隨常格仕進。 天寶中，自嵩山上書論當世務，玄宗召見，令侍詔翰林，仍東宮供奉。 楊國忠忌其才辯，奏泌嘗為《感遇詩》，諷刺時政，詔於蘄春郡安置，乃潛遁名山，以習隱自適。 天寶末，祿山構難，肅宗北巡，至靈武即位，遣使訪召。 會泌自嵩、潁間冒難奔赴行在，至彭原郡謁見，陳古今成敗之機，甚稱旨，延致臥內，動皆顧問。 泌稱山人，固辭官秩，特以散官寵之，解褐拜銀青光祿大夫，俾掌樞務。 至於四言文狀、將相遷除，皆與泌參議，權逾宰相，仍判元帥廣平王軍司馬事。 肅宗每謂曰：「卿當上皇天寶中，為朕師友，下判廣平行軍，朕父子三人，資卿道義。 」其見重如此。 尋為中書令崔圓、幸臣李輔國害其能，將有不利於泌。 泌懼，乞游衡山，優詔許之，給以三品祿俸，遂隱衡岳，絕粒棲神。
Li Bi, courtesy name Changyuan, was descended from Xiangping in Liaodong, sixth in line from Tuohe Bi, Grand Guardian and one of the Eight Pillars, Minister of Works of Western Wei. He was the son of Chengxiu, magistrate of Wufang in Jingzhao. From youth he was brilliant, widely read in the classics and histories, deeply versed in the Book of Changes, skilled in prose and especially gifted in poetry, and confident of his capacity to counsel rulers. Zhang Jiuling, Wei Xuxin, and Zhang Tinggui all held him in high regard. Bi's temperament was free and unconventional, and he disdained the usual path of official promotion. During the Tianbao era he memorialized from Mount Song on affairs of state. Xuanzong received him, named him Hanlin Academician Awaiting Imperial Draft, and assigned him to the crown prince's household. Yang Guozhong envied his eloquence and accused him of satirizing current policy in his Responsive to Encounter poems. An edict banished him to Qichun, but he fled into the mountains and took up the life of a recluse. At the end of the Tianbao reign An Lushan rebelled. Suzong withdrew north, took the throne at Lingwu, and sent envoys to find him. Bi meanwhile hurried from the Song and Ying region through the turmoil to the mobile court. At Pengyuan he had audience and expounded the patterns of success and failure through history. The emperor was greatly pleased, received him in his private quarters, and consulted him on every matter. Bi called himself a mountain recluse and firmly declined regular office. The emperor honored him with a nominal title, appointed him Silver-Gleam Grand Master of Splendid Happiness upon first entering service, and entrusted him with affairs of state. Four-character state papers and the appointment and dismissal of generals and ministers were all discussed with Bi. His authority exceeded that of the chief minister, and he also served as adjutant on the staff of the Prince of Guangping, commander of the armies. Suzong often told him, "In the Tianbao years of the Retired Emperor you were teacher and friend to me; below, you guide the Prince of Guangping's armies. Father and two sons—we three—all depend upon your counsel and integrity." Such was the esteem in which he was held. Before long Chief Minister Cui Yuan and the favorite Li Fuguo, envying his talent, plotted against him. Bi feared for his life and asked leave to retire to Mount Heng. A gracious edict granted his request with third-rank stipend. He withdrew to the sacred peak, abstained from grain, and devoted himself to spiritual cultivation.
12
興元初，征赴行在，遷左散騎常侍。 貞元元年，除陝州長史，充陝、虢都防禦觀察使。 二年六月，泌奏：「虢州盧氏山冶，近出瑟瑟，請充獻，禁人開採。 」詔曰：「瑟瑟之寶，中土所無今產於近甸，實為靈貺。 朕不飾器玩，不尚珍奇，常思返朴之風，用明躬儉之節。 其出瑟瑟之處，任百姓求采，不宜禁止。 」就加泌檢校禮部尚書。 時陳、許戍邊卒三千自京西逃歸，至州境，泌潛師險隘，左右攻擊，盡誅之。 尋拜中書侍郎、平章事、集賢崇文館學士、修國史。 初，張延賞大減官員，人情咨怨，泌請復之，以從人欲，因是奏罷兼試額內佔闕等官，加百官俸料，隨閒劇加置手力課，上從之，人人以為便。 而竇參旁奏，遂改易，使同品之內，月俸多少累等。 泌又奏請罷拾遺、補闕，上雖不從，亦不授人，故諫司惟韓皋、歸登而已。 泌仍命收其署湌錢，令登等寓食於中書舍人，故時戲云：「韓諫議雖分左右，歸拾遺莫辨存亡。 」如是者三年。 至貞元五年，以前東都防禦判官、殿中侍御史、內供奉韋綬為左補闕，監察御史梁肅右補闕。 既復置，人心忻然。 順宗在春宮，妃蕭氏母郜國公主交通外人，上疑其有他，連坐貶黜者數人，皇儲亦危。 泌百端奏說，上意方解。
At the opening of the Xingyuan era he was summoned to court and promoted to Left Regular Attendant. In Zhenyuan 1 (785) he was named Chief Administrator of Shanzhou and Defense and Observation Commissioner of Shan and Guo. In the sixth month of the second year Bi reported, "A smelting site on Mount Lushi in Guo Prefecture has lately yielded sese gems. I ask that they be submitted as tribute and that private mining be forbidden." The edict replied, "Sese gems are unknown in the central plains. That they now appear in our own domain is truly a numinous gift." We do not adorn vessels or prize rarities, but ever seek the spirit of simplicity and practice personal frugality." Let the people mine sese where it is found. It should not be forbidden." Bi was thereupon named Acting Minister of Rites. Three thousand garrison troops from Chen and Xu who had deserted west of the capital fled homeward. When they reached his jurisdiction, Bi ambushed them in a narrow pass and had them all executed. He was soon appointed Vice Director of the Secretariat, Chief Minister, Academician of the Hall of Assembled Worthies and Hall of Venerated Literature, and Compiler of National History. Earlier Zhang Yanshang had drastically cut the bureaucracy, provoking widespread resentment. Bi persuaded the emperor to restore the ranks, abolish redundant concurrent posts, raise official salaries, and assign clerical staff according to each office's workload. The emperor agreed, and all regarded the reforms as beneficial. Dou Can intervened with a side memorial, however, and the plan was altered so that officials of the same rank received widely differing monthly salaries. Bi also proposed abolishing the Reminder and Supplementation posts. Though the emperor did not agree, he appointed no one to fill them, so the Remonstrance Bureau held only Han Gao and Gui Deng. Bi further ordered their office meal allowances seized and had Deng and the others board with Secretariat Drafters. Wits of the day quipped, "Han the Remonstrator may divide left from right, but Gui the Reminder cannot tell the living from the dead." This went on for three years. In Zhenyuan 5, Wei Shou, former staff judge of the Eastern Capital Defense, Palace Attending Censor, and Inner Attendant, was named Left Supplementation, and Investigating Censor Liang Su Right Supplementation. Once the posts were restored, public sentiment rejoiced. While Shunzong was crown prince, his consort Lady Xiao's mother, the Princess of Gao, was found consorting with outsiders. The emperor suspected intrigue; several people were implicated and demoted, and the heir himself was endangered. Bi argued the case from every angle until the emperor's suspicions were finally allayed.

Bi had a reputation for blunt integrity, yet he spoke of immortals and occult arts, and rumor held that he had consorted with Master Red Pine, Wang Qiao, Anqi, and Xianmen. His age therefore held him lightly in esteem. Though he courted favor through heterodox teachings, contemporary rulers did not truly value him. When Dezong first ascended the throne, he especially detested shamans, prayer-masters, and purveyors of the bizarre. Under Suzong, yin-yang doctrine and sacrificial prayer had held sway; the sorcerer Wang Yu had served as chief minister, and shamanesses had been sent by relay post through the provinces to perform exorcistic rites. Every construction project was bound by ritual taboos. Li Gan had risen to Magistrate of the Capital through heterodox arts. He once assembled artisans within the palace to weave pearl-embroidered imperial robes, then burned them as exorcistic offerings—month after month without pause. Dezong as crown prince had known these abuses well. Upon his accession he ended the gathering of monks in the inner chapel and abolished shamanistic sacrifices. Officials reported that the inner corridor of Xuanzheng Hall had collapsed and requested repairs. The Grand Diviner objected, "The first month of winter is the Kui Ridge—unfavorable for construction. Please choose another month." The emperor replied, "The Spring and Autumn Annals teach that work should follow the season. What Kui Ridge is there?" He ordered the repairs to proceed. Asked why, officials replied, "Your Majesty's natal hour is wu, so we dared not place the carriage in its path." The emperor wept aloud, "How can one bend the spirit carriage to seek personal advantage?" He ordered the carriage to proceed straight through the wu hour. By the late Jianzhong era, rebels threatened within and Sang Daomao had prophesied fortifying Fengtian. The emperor grew mindful of calendrical taboos, yet had long heard that Bi excelled in occult arts. His recall from exile to high office therefore satisfied few observers. Once in office he bent with every wind and left little worth praise. He again patronized frivolous men such as Gu Kuang, who were constantly mocked by court officials and brought ridicule upon himself. He died at sixty-eight and was posthumously named Grand Preceptor of the Heir Apparent with enhanced funeral gifts. Bi was free-spirited, quick-witted, and fond of bold pronouncements. From his first entry into the inner palace he was repeatedly envied by powerful favorites, yet always escaped harm through his wits; and in the end, through far-ranging discourse, won the sage sovereign's trust and rose to the chancellorship. His collected works ran to twenty scrolls.

24
建中初，張鎰為河中少尹。 鎰尋入相，二年七月，遷播給事中。 舊例，諸司甲庫，皆是胥吏掌知，為弊頗久，播始建議並以士人知之，至今稱當。 轉刑部侍郎、奉迎皇太后副使。 盧杞以播柔緩，冀其易制，驟稱薦之。 尋遷吏部侍郎，轉刑部尚書、知刪定。 奏上元中，詔擇古今名將十人於武成王廟配享，如文宣王廟之儀。 播以「太公古稱大賢，今其下稱亞聖，於義不安。 又孔子十哲，皆是當時弟子，今所擇名將，年代不同，於義既乖，於事又失。 臣請刪去名將配享之儀及十哲之稱。 」從之。
At the beginning of the Jianzhong era, Zhang Yi was Vice Governor of Hezhong. Yi soon entered the chancellery, and in the seventh month of the second year Bo was promoted to Drafting Attendant. By old precedent the document stores of all bureaus were managed by clerks, a long-standing abuse. Bo was the first to propose that literati manage them instead, a reform still regarded as sound. He was transferred to Vice Minister of Justice and Deputy Commissioner for Welcoming the Empress Dowager. Lu Qi, finding Bo gentle and pliable, hoped to control him easily and suddenly praised and recommended him. He was soon promoted to Vice Minister of Personnel, then Minister of Justice with charge of editorial revision. He memorialized that during the Shangyuan era an edict had selected ten famous generals from antiquity and recent times for secondary sacrifice at the Martial Completion King Temple, following the rites of the Confucian temple. Bo argued, "The Grand Duke was called a great sage in antiquity; to call those beneath him secondary sages is ritually improper." Confucius's Ten Wise Ones were all his own disciples, whereas the selected generals belong to different eras. The parallel is wrong in principle and mistaken in practice. I ask that the rite of secondary sacrifice for famous generals and the title of Ten Wise Ones both be abolished. The emperor approved.
25
建中三年十月，拜銀青光祿大夫、中書侍郎、同中書門下平章事、集賢殿崇文館大學士、修國史。 時政事決在盧杞，播但斂衽取容而已。 乏於知人之鑑，好大言虛誕者，播必悅而親信之。 有李元平、陶公達、張愻、劉承誡，皆言談詭妄，讠誇大可立功名，亦有微材薄藝。 播累奏雲元平等皆可將相也，請閱試用之，上以為然，以元平為補闕。 會淮西節度李希烈叛亂，上以汝州要鎮，令選擇刺史。 播薦元平為汝州刺史，尋加檢校吏部郎中、汝州別駕，知州事。 元平至州旬日，為希烈所擒，汝州陷賊，中外哂之。 由是公達等未克任用。 播與盧杞等從駕幸奉天，既而杞、白志貞等並貶黜，播尚知政事，中外囂然，以為不可，遂罷相，改刑部尚書。 大臣韋倫等泣於朝曰：「宰相不能謀猷翊贊，以至今日，而尚為尚書，可痛心也！」
In the tenth month of Jianzhong 3 he was appointed Silver-Gleam Grand Master of Splendid Happiness, Vice Director of the Secretariat, Associate Chief Minister, Grand Academician of the Hall of Assembled Worthies and Hall of Venerated Literature, and Compiler of National History. Government affairs were decided by Lu Qi; Bo merely drew in his robes and sought to please him. He lacked discernment in judging men and delighted in those who spoke grandly and extravagantly, whom he invariably favored and trusted. Among them were Li Yuanping, Tao Gongda, Zhang Xun, and Liu Chengjie—all given to bizarre and delusive talk, boasting they could win merit and fame, though they possessed only slight talent. Bo repeatedly memorialized that Yuanping and the others were fit for generalship and ministerial rank and asked that they be examined and tried. The emperor agreed and named Yuanping Supplementation. When Huaixi Military Commissioner Li Xilie rebelled, the emperor, recognizing Ruzhou as a strategic post, ordered selection of a prefect. Bo recommended Yuanping as Prefect of Ru, soon adding Acting Director in the Ministry of Personnel and Vice Prefect of Ru with charge of prefectural affairs. Within ten days of reaching the prefecture Yuanping was captured by Xilie and Ruzhou fell to the rebels. Court and country alike mocked the appointment. Gongda and the others were therefore never employed. Bo followed Lu Qi and others to Fengtian. After Qi, Bai Zhizhen, and others were demoted, Bo still held the chancellery. Court and country were in uproar at the thought, and he was dismissed from the chancellery and made Minister of Justice. Great ministers such as Wei Lun wept in open court, "The chief minister could not counsel and support the throne, bringing us to this pass—and he remains a minister! It is heartbreaking!"
26
貞元四年，回紇請和親，以咸安公主出降可汗，令播以本官加檢校右僕射、兼御史大夫，持節充送咸安公主及冊可汗使，奉使往來，皆清儉謹慎，蕃人悅之。 使回，遷兵部尚書，固辭疾，請罷官，改太子少師致仕。 播致仕之後，減去僮僕車騎，閉關守靜，不縈外事，士君子重之。 貞元十三年正月卒，時年七十九，廢朝一日，贈太子太保。
In Zhenyuan 4 the Uyghurs sought a marriage alliance and the Princess of Xian'an was sent to wed the khan. Bo was named Acting Right Vice Director and concurrent Censor-in-Chief, commissioner escorting the princess and enfeoffing the khan. On his missions he conducted himself with frugal propriety, and the frontier peoples were pleased. On his return he was promoted to Minister of War but firmly declined on grounds of illness and requested retirement, and was named Junior Preceptor of the Heir Apparent with permission to retire. After retirement he reduced his servants and equipage, closed his gates, and kept to quiet seclusion without entangling himself in outside affairs. Men of honor respected him for it. He died in the first month of Zhenyuan 13 at the age of seventy-nine. The court suspended audience for one day and posthumously named him Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent.
27
李元平者，宗室子。 始為湖南觀察使蕭復判官，試大理評事。 性疏傲，敢大言，好論兵，天下賢士大夫無可其意者，以是人多銜怒。 關播奇重之，許以將帥。 時希烈反叛，朝廷以汝州與賊接壤，刺史韋光裔懦弱不任職，播乃盛稱元平，特召見，超左補闕，不數日，擢為檢校吏部郎中，兼汝州別駕，知州事。 既至部，募工徒繕理郛郭，希烈乃使勇士應募，執役板築，凡入數百人，元平不之覺。 希烈遣偽將李克誠以數百騎突至其城，先應募執役者應於內，縛元平馳去。 既見希烈，遺下污地。 希烈見其無須眇小，戲謂克誠曰：「使汝取李元平，何得將元平兒來？ 」因嫚罵曰：「盲宰相使汝當我，何待我淺耶！ 」偽署為御史中丞。 播聞元平得用，仍欺於人曰：「李生功業濟矣。 」言必能覆希烈而建功也。 居無何，希烈用為宰相，或告其有二者，乃斷一指以自誓。 希烈既死，或有人言在賊中微有謀慮，貸死流於珍州。 會赦得歸剡中，浙東觀察使皇甫政表聞其到，以發上怒，復流賀州而死。
Li Yuanping was a member of the imperial clan. He first served as staff judge to Hunan Observation Commissioner Xiao Fu and as trial Grand Court Reviewer. He was arrogant by nature, spoke boldly, and loved to discuss military affairs. No worthy gentleman in the realm could satisfy him, and many harbored resentment. Guan Bo regarded him as extraordinary and promised him a general's command. When Xilie rebelled, the court saw that Ruzhou bordered rebel territory and that Prefect Wei Guangyi was too weak for the post. Bo lavishly praised Yuanping, who was specially summoned, leap-promoted to Left Supplementation, and within days named Acting Director in the Ministry of Personnel and Vice Prefect of Ru with charge of prefectural affairs. On reaching his post he recruited laborers to repair the walls. Xilie sent warriors to enlist, who entered as laborers in the hundreds. Yuanping noticed nothing. Xilie sent his general Li Kecheng with several hundred horsemen to storm the city. The enlisted laborers within seized Yuanping and carried him off. When brought before Xilie, he soiled the ground in fear. Xilie, seeing him beardless and slight of stature, jested to Kecheng, "I sent you to fetch Li Yuanping—why have you brought me Yuanping's son?" He then reviled him, "A blind chief minister sent you to oppose me—as if I were someone to be trifled with!" Yuanping was given the false title of Vice Censor-in-Chief. Hearing that Yuanping had been appointed, Bo still deceived others, saying, "Master Li's achievements are assured." He declared that Yuanping would surely overthrow Xilie and win glory. Before long Xilie made him chief minister. When someone reported divided loyalty, he cut off a finger to swear his fidelity. After Xilie's death someone reported that he had shown slight strategic sense among the rebels. His death sentence was commuted to exile in Zhen Prefecture. An amnesty allowed him to return to Shanzhong. Zhedong Observation Commissioner Huangfu Zheng reported his arrival, provoking the emperor's anger. He was exiled again to He Prefecture, where he died.
28
史臣曰：蒸嘗礿祀，前王制以奉先； 怪力亂神，宣聖鄙而不語。 凡雲左道，固有舊章。 玙假於鬼神，乃至將相，既處代天之位，爰滋亂政之源。 國禎妖人疑眾，妄恢其祀典； 梁鎮正士抗疏，方悟其上心。 泌見可進而知難退，足為高率智辯之士； 居相位而談鬼神，乃見狂妄浮薄之蹤。 《王制》云：「執左道以亂政，殺。 」寧無畏乎！ 繁之醜行，棄於當時，竟陷非辜，諒由素履。 造為臣得禮，蒞事非能； 播居位取容，舉人敗事。 皆非國器，咸歷台司，失人者亡，國其危矣。
The historiographer writes: seasonal and autumnal sacrifices were instituted by the former kings to honor the ancestors; strange powers and spirits that disorder the world—the Sage despised them and would not discuss them. Heterodox ways have always been forbidden by established law. Yu borrowed the authority of ghosts and spirits to reach ministerial rank. Having occupied a position that acts for Heaven, he became a source of governmental disorder. Guozhen the sorcerer deluded the people and wantonly expanded the ritual canon; only the forthright memorial of Liang Zhen awakened the sovereign's mind. Bi knew when to advance and when to withdraw, and was a man of lofty wisdom and eloquence; yet in the chancellery he spoke of ghosts and spirits, revealing arrogance and frivolity. The Royal Regulations states, "Those who wield heterodox ways to disorder government shall be put to death." Did they not fear this law? Fan's vile conduct was despised in his own day; that he fell into undeserved guilt was surely the fruit of his lifelong conduct. Zao understood ritual propriety as a minister but lacked capacity in affairs of state; Bo in office sought only to please; in raising men he ruined affairs. None were fit to govern the state, yet all held high office. When rulers lose the right men, the state perishes—and the realm was indeed in peril.
29
贊曰：玙、泌、造、播，俱非相材。 國禎左道，梁生直哉！
Encomium: Yu, Bi, Zao, and Bo—none possessed the makings of a chief minister. Guozhen practiced heterodox ways; Master Liang was upright indeed!
