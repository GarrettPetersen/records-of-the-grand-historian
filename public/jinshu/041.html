1
魏舒，字陽元，任城樊人也。 少孤，為外家寧氏所養。 寧氏起宅，相宅者云：「當出貴甥。 」外祖母以魏氏甥小而慧，意謂應之。 舒曰：「當為外氏成此宅相。 」久乃別居。 身長八尺二寸，姿望秀偉，飲酒石餘，而遲鈍質樸，不為鄉親所重。 從叔父吏部郎衡，有名當世，亦不之知，使守水碓，每歎曰：「舒堪數百戶長，我願畢矣！ 」舒亦不以介意。 不修常人之節，不為皎曆之事，每欲容才長物，終不顯人之短。 性好騎射，著韋衣。 入山澤，以漁獵為事。 唯太原王乂謂舒曰：「卿終當為台輔，然今未能令妻子免饑寒，吾當助卿營之。 」常振其匱乏，舒受而不辭。 舒嘗詣野王，主人妻夜產，俄而聞車馬之聲，相問曰：「男也，女也？ 」曰：「男，書之，十五以兵死。 」復問：「寢者為誰？ 」曰：「魏公舒。 」後十五載，詣主人，問所生兒何在，曰：「因條桑為斧傷而死。 」舒自知當為公矣。
Wei Shu, courtesy name Yangyuan, was a native of Fan in Rencheng Commandery. He lost his father while still a boy and was brought up by his maternal kin, the Ning family. When the Nings built a residence, a geomancer said: "This house will yield an eminent nephew." His maternal grandmother assumed the prophecy meant her clever little Wei grandson. Wei Shu said: "It will be my mother's family for whom this lucky layout comes true." Only much later did he move out and live separately. He stood eight chi two cun tall, with a striking presence, and could drink more than a shi of wine at a sitting; yet he seemed slow and unpolished, so neighbors did not think much of him. His cousin Heng, a Personnel Bureau director celebrated in their day, still failed to appreciate him and left him tending the water mill, sighing: "Wei Shu is enough for a headman over a few hundred households—that satisfies me." Wei Shu did not resent it. He did not fuss over ordinary propriety or seek flashy accomplishments; he preferred to make room for others' abilities and strengths, and never aired their faults. He loved riding and shooting and dressed in leather hunting clothes. He spent his time fishing and hunting in the hills and wetlands. Only Wang Yi of Taiyuan told him: "You are destined for the highest offices, but right now you cannot keep your family from want—I will help you set yourself up." Wang Yi repeatedly eased his straits, and Wei Shu accepted without refusing. Once when Wei Shu visited Yewang, the host's wife went into labor at night; soon voices outside sounded like horses and carriages, asking: "Is it a boy or a girl?" The reply came: "A boy—note this: he will die by arms at fifteen." They asked again: "Who is sleeping here?" The answer was: "Wei Shu." Fifteen years later Wei Shu called on the host and asked after that child; he was told the boy had died when an axe cut him while he was stripping mulberry branches. Wei Shu understood then that he would rise to ducal rank.
2
年四十餘，郡上計掾察孝廉。 宗党以舒無學業，勸令不就，可以為高耳。 舒曰：「若試而不中，其負在我，安可虛竊不就之高以為己榮乎！ 」於是自課。 百日習一經，因而對策升第。 除澠池長，遷浚儀令，入為尚書郎。 時欲沙汰郎官。 非其才者罷之。 舒曰：「吾即其人也。 」襆被而出。 同僚素無清論者咸有愧色，談者稱之。
Past forty, he served as the commandery clerk on the annual accounting mission and was recommended as Filial and Incorrupt. His clansmen, noting he had little formal learning, urged him to decline the nomination and preserve a reputation for lofty refusal. Wei Shu replied: "If I sit the examination and fail, the blame is mine; I must not borrow fake 'high-minded' refusal just to polish my name." So he drilled himself. He mastered one classic every hundred days, passed the policy examination, and advanced. He was named magistrate of Mianchi, then of Junyi, and eventually entered court as a Gentleman of the Masters of Writing. The court then planned to purge underperforming palace gentlemen. Anyone judged inadequate would be removed. Wei Shu said: "That describes me." He packed his bedding and walked out. Coworkers who lacked sterling reputations were ashamed; commentators praised him.
3
累遷後將軍鐘毓長史，毓每與參佐射，舒常為畫籌而已。 後遇朋人不足，以舒滿數。 毓初不知其善射。 舒容范閒雅，發無不中，舉坐愕然。 莫有敵者。 毓歎而謝曰：「吾之不足以盡卿才，有如此射矣，豈一事哉！ 」轉相國參軍，封劇陽子。 府朝碎務，未嘗見是非； 至於廢興大事，眾人莫能斷者，舒徐為籌之，多出眾議之表。 文帝深器重之，每朝會坐罷，目送之曰：「魏舒堂堂，人之領袖也。 」遷宜陽、滎陽二郡太守，甚有聲稱。 徵拜散騎常侍。 出為冀州刺史，在州三年，以簡惠稱。 入為侍中。 武帝以舒清素，特賜絹百匹。 遷尚書，以公事當免官，詔以贖論。 舒三娶妻皆亡，是歲自表乞假還本郡葬妻，詔賜葬地一頃，錢五十萬。
He rose to chief clerk under Rear General Zhong Yu; when Zhong practiced archery with his staff, Wei Shu usually only kept score. Later, when there were not enough partners for the round, they added Wei Shu to make up the party. Zhong Yu had not realized he could shoot. Wei Shu carried himself with calm elegance and never missed; everyone at the table sat dumbstruck. No one could beat him. Zhong Yu sighed and said: "If I failed to use your abilities fully in archery, how much more elsewhere?" He was reassigned as aide to the minister of state and enfeoffed as Baron of Ju. In routine office trivia he never picked fights; but on weighty questions of state where others could not decide, he thought them through calmly and often surpassed every other proposal. Prince Wen of Wei valued him highly; after each court session he watched him leave and said: "Wei Shu has true presence—he is born to lead men." He became governor of Yiyang and Xingyang and earned wide acclaim. He was recalled and named Regular Palace Attendant. He served three years as Inspector of Ji Province and was noted for straightforward, humane rule. He was transferred to Palace Attendant. Emperor Wu admired his austerity and gave him a special gift of one hundred bolts of silk. He rose to Master of Writing; a bureaucratic fault should have cost him his post, but an edict allowed him to commute the penalty. All three of Wei Shu's wives had died; that year he asked leave to return home and bury them, and the throne granted one qing of burial ground and five hundred thousand cash.
4
太康初，拜右僕射。 舒與衛瓘、山濤、張華等以六合混一，宜用古典封禪東嶽，前後累陳其事，帝謙讓不許。 以舒為左僕射，領吏部。 舒上言：「今選六宮，聘以玉帛，而舊使禦府丞奉聘，宣成嘉禮，贄重使輕。 以為拜三夫人宜使卿，九嬪使五官中郎將，美人、良人使謁者，於典制為弘。 」有詔詳之，眾議異同，遂寢。 加右光祿大夫、儀同三司。
Early in the Taikang era he became Right Vice Director of the Masters of Writing. Wei Shu joined Wei Guan, Shan Tao, and Zhang Hua in arguing that, with the realm reunited, the classical Eastern Peak feng and shan sacrifices should be held; they petitioned repeatedly, but the emperor modestly refused. Wei Shu was appointed Left Vice Director of the Masters of Writing and oversaw the Ministry of Personnel. Wei Shu memorialized: "When filling the six palaces we send jade and silk as bride-price, yet we still send the imperial treasury assistant to deliver it—the ceremony is grand but the envoy too lowly." He proposed that investitures for the Three Ladies use a minister, for the Nine Concubines a commander of household gentlemen for all purposes, and for Beauties and Good Women court heralds—better fitting the statutes." The throne ordered discussion, but opinions conflicted and the reform stalled. He was additionally named Right Household Grandee with privileges matching the Three Dukes.
5
及山濤薨，以舒領司徒，有頃即真。 舒有威重德望，祿賜散之九族，家無餘財。 陳留周震累為諸府所辟，辟書既下，公輒喪亡，僉號震為殺公掾，莫有辟者。 舒乃命之，而竟無患，識者以此稱其達命。 以年老，每稱疾遜位。 中復暫起，署兗州中正，尋又稱疾。 尚書左丞郤詵與舒書曰：「公久疾小差，視事是也，唯上所念。 何竟起訖還臥，曲身回法，甚失具瞻之望。 公少立巍巍，一旦棄之，可不惜哉！ 」舒稱疾如初。 後以災異遜位，帝不聽。 後因正旦朝罷還第，表送章綬。 帝手詔敦勉。 而舒執意彌固，乃下詔曰：「司徒、劇陽子舒，體道弘粹，思量經遠，忠肅居正，在公盡規。 入管銓衡，官人允敘； 出贊袞職，敷弘五教。 惠訓播流，德聲茂著，可謂朝之俊乂者也。 而屢執沖讓，辭旨懇誠，申覽反覆，省用憮然。 蓋成人之美，先典所與，難違至情。 今聽其所執，以劇陽子就第，位同三司，祿賜如前。 几杖不朝，賜錢百萬，床帳簟褥自副。 以舍人四人為劇陽子舍人，置官騎十人。 使光祿勳奉策，主者詳案典禮，令皆如舊制。 」於是賜安車駟馬，門施行馬。 舒為事必先行而後言，遜位之際，莫有知者。 時論以為晉興以來，三公能辭榮善終者，未之有也。 司空衛瓘與舒書曰：「每與足下共論此事，日日未果，可謂瞻之在前，忽焉在後矣。 」太熙元年薨，時年八十二。 帝甚傷悼，賵賻優厚，諡曰康。
After Shan Tao died, Wei Shu headed the Ministry of Education ad interim and soon received the full appointment. Wei Shu commanded respect; he shared stipends and gifts across his extended clan and kept nothing for himself. Zhou Zhen of Chenliu had been invited by several offices, but each patron died as soon as his appointment arrived; people called Zhou the "official-killer" and no one would hire him. Wei Shu hired him anyway, and nothing happened; observers praised his grasp of destiny. Advancing in years, he repeatedly pleaded illness to resign. He briefly returned to serve as provincial evaluator for Yan Province, then again pleaded illness. Xi Shen, Left Aide to the Masters of Writing, wrote: "You have been ill but are a little better—returning to duty is appropriate; that is what the court expects." Why get up only to go back to bed, bending rules to suit yourself? You disappoint everyone who looks to you. You built an eminent reputation long ago; to throw it away now would be a shame! Wei Shu again pleaded illness as before. He later cited omens and resigned; the emperor refused. After New Year's court he went home and formally returned his seals of office. The emperor answered with a handwritten note urging him to stay. Wei Shu remained adamant, so the throne issued an edict: "Minister of Education Wei Shu, Baron of Ju, embodies the Way with breadth and purity; he thinks far ahead; he is loyal, grave, and upright; he gives honest counsel in office. Within the ministries he ranks candidates fairly; as minister he advances the Five Teachings among the people. His humane counsel spreads wide and his moral renown shines—he is truly one of the court's foremost pillars. Yet he insistently asks to step down; his words are heartfelt and sincere. Each time I read his petitions my heart sinks. The classics urge us to honor another's good intentions; I cannot ignore his deepest wishes. We grant his request: the Baron of Ju may retire home with rank equal to the Three Dukes and the same salary and gifts as before. He need not attend court; he receives one million cash, plus bedding, curtains, and mats. Four gentlemen attendants will serve his household, with ten official mounted escorts. The Superintendent of the Household shall deliver the patent; officials shall verify everything against precedent. He was given a comfortable carriage and team and permission to mount barrier poles at his gate. Wei Shu always acted before he spoke; when he resigned, nobody had seen it coming. Commentators said that since the Jin founding no Three Duke had ever relinquished honor so gracefully and died at peace. Wei Guan, Minister of Works, wrote: "We spoke of this again and again yet never resolved it—the goal seemed within reach, then slipped away." He died in the first Taixi year at eighty-two. The emperor grieved deeply; funeral subsidies were lavish; his posthumous name was Kang ("Peaceful").
6
子混，字延廣，清惠有才行，為太子舍人。 年二十七，先舒卒，朝野咸為舒悲惜。 舒每哀慟，退而歎曰：「吾不及莊生遠矣，豈以無益自損乎！ 」於是終服不復哭。 詔曰：「舒惟一子，薄命短折。 舒告老之年，處窮獨之苦，每念怛然，為之嗟悼。 思所以散愁養氣，可更增滋味品物。 仍給賜陽燧四望繐窗戶皁輪車牛一乘，庶出入觀望，或足散憂也。 」以庶孫融嗣。 又早卒，從孫晃嗣。
His son Wei Hun, courtesy name Yanguang, was gentle and talented and served as attendant to the heir apparent. He died at twenty-seven, predeceasing his father; officials and commoners alike grieved for Wei Shu. Wei Shu mourned deeply, then sighed: "I am nowhere near Zhuangzi—why wound myself over what cannot help?" After the mourning period ended he did not weep again. An edict read: "Wei Shu had but one son, and fate cut him off young." In his retirement Wei Shu endures loneliness and loss; each thought pains us, and we mourn with him. We should find ways to lift his spirits and care for his health—send richer foods and delicacies. Also grant him a sun-mirror carriage with four-view gauze windows, black wheels, and a pair of oxen, so outings might ease his sorrow. His grandson Rong (born of a concubine's line) inherited the title. Rong also died young; the heir became his cousin once removed, Huang.
7
李憙，字季和，上黨銅鞮人也。 父牷，漢大鴻臚。 憙少有高行，博學研精，與北海管甯以賢良征，不行。 累辟三府，不就。 宣帝復辟憙為太傅屬，固辭疾，郡縣扶輿上道，時憙母疾篤，乃竊逾泫氏城而徒還，遂遭母喪，論者嘉其志節。 後為并州別駕，時驍騎將軍秦朗過并州，州將畢軌敬焉。 令乘車至閣。 憙固諫以為不可，軌不得已從之。
Li Xi, courtesy name Jihe, was a native of Tongdi in Shangdang Commandery. His father Li Quan had served as Grand Herald under the Han. Li Xi showed sterling character early; he studied widely and deeply. He and Guan Ning of Beihai were summoned as Worthy and Good candidates, but he declined. Several high offices summoned him; he refused each time. Emperor Xuan summoned him as an aide to the Grand Tutor; he pleaded illness. Local officials carried him toward the capital in a litter, but his mother lay dying, so he slipped away over the wall at Xuanshi and walked home. She died soon after, and observers praised his devotion. He became Bing Province aide; when General of Agile Cavalry Qin Lang traveled through the region, provincial commander Bi Gui showed him full courtesy. Bi Gui had him ride in a carriage up to the gate pavilion. Li Xi insistently objected; Bi Gui had no choice but to yield.
8
景帝輔政，命憙為大將軍從事中郎，憙到，引見，謂憙曰：「昔先公辟君而君不應，今孤命君而君至，何也？ 」對曰：「先君以禮見待，憙得以禮進退。 明公以法見繩，憙畏法而至。 」帝甚重之。 轉司馬，尋拜右長史。 從討毌丘儉還，遷御史中丞。 當官正色，不憚強禦，百僚震肅焉。 薦樂安孫璞，亦以道德顯，時人稱為知人。 尋遷大司馬，以公事免。
Prince Jing, assisting with government, named Li Xi aide to the Grand General. At his audience the prince asked: "Once my father summoned you and you stayed away; now I summon you and you appear—why is that?" Li Xi answered: "Your father treated me according to ritual, so I could accept or decline on those terms." Your Highness measures men by statute; I respected the law and came. The emperor thought highly of him. He became an army marshal and soon chief clerk on the right. After the campaign against Guanqiu Jian he was promoted to Palace Assistant Imperial Clerk. He faced duty with a stern air and did not flinch from the mighty; the bureaucracy stood in awe. He recommended Sun Pu of Le'an, who likewise rose on virtue and conduct; contemporaries said Li Xi could judge character. He was soon promoted to Grand Marshal but later removed over an administrative matter.
9
司馬伷為甯北將軍，鎮鄴，以憙為軍司。 頃之，除涼州刺史，加揚威將軍、假節，領護羌校尉，綏禦華夷，甚有聲績。 羌虜犯塞，憙因其隙會，不及啟聞，輒以便宜出軍深入，遂大克獲，以功重免譴，時人比之漢朝馮、甘焉。 於是請還，許之。 居家月餘，拜冀州刺史，累遷司隸校尉。 及魏帝告禪于晉，憙以本官行司徒事，副太尉鄭沖奉策。 泰始初，封祁侯。
When Sima Zhou served as General Who Guards the North at Ye, he named Li Xi army clerk. Before long he became Inspector of Liangzhou with the additional titles General Who Displays Might and credential-bearing Colonel Protecting the Qiang; he kept Chinese and frontier peoples in check with notable success. When Qiang raiders breached the frontier, Li Xi exploited the moment without waiting for court approval and struck deep on discretionary authority, scoring a major victory. Merit outweighed his breach of protocol, so he escaped sanction; people compared him to Feng Fengshi and Gan Yanshou of the Han. He then asked to retire from the post and was allowed. After little more than a month at home he became Inspector of Ji Province and eventually Metropolitan Commandant. When the Wei emperor abdicated in favor of Jin, Li Xi handled Minister of Education duties in his current rank and assisted Grand Commandant Zheng Chong in presenting the abdication patent. Early in the Taishi era he was enfeoffed as Marquis of Qi.
10
憙上言：「故立進令劉友、前尚書山濤、中山王睦、故尚書僕射武陔各占官三更稻田，請免濤、睦等官。 陔已亡，請貶諡。 」詔曰：「法者，天下取正，不避親貴，然後行耳，吾豈將枉縱其間哉！ 然案此事皆是友所作，侵剝百姓，以繆惑朝士。 奸吏乃敢作此，其考竟友以懲邪佞。 濤等不貳其過者，皆勿有所問。 《易》稱'王臣蹇蹇，匪躬之故'。 今憙亢志在公，當官而行，可謂'邦之司直'者矣。 光武有云：'貴戚且斂手以避二鮑'。 豈其然乎！ 其申敕群僚，各慎所司，寬宥之恩，不可數遇也。 」憙為二代司隸，朝野稱之。 以公事免。
Li Xi reported that Liu You, former magistrate of Lijin, Shan Tao, Prince Mu of Zhongshan, and the late Vice Director Wu Ling had each seized government rice lands registered for three irrigation shifts; he asked that Shan Tao, the prince, and others be stripped of office. Wu Ling was dead, so Li Xi asked that his posthumous title be lowered. The response read: "Law is the standard for all under Heaven; it must reach kin and nobles alike. Do you think I would twist it to spare anyone?" Yet this affair was Liu You's doing: he seized land from the people and misled officials at court. Corrupt clerks dared this malfeasance; try Liu You to the full extent to punish deceit. Shan Tao and others who were not accomplices need not be prosecuted. The Changes says: "The king's ministers labor in hardship—not for private ends." Li Xi stands tall for the common good and does his duty—he is truly the "straight man of the state." Emperor Guangwu said even imperial in-laws kept their hands folded to stay clear of Bao Yong and Bao De. Was it not exactly so? Tell every official again to mind his duties carefully—such leniency cannot be expected often. Li Xi served as Metropolitan Commandant under two reigns and won praise at court and beyond. He was later dismissed over an administrative matter.
11
其年，皇太子立，以憙為太子太傅。 自魏明帝以後，久曠東宮，制度廢闕，官司不具，詹事、左右率、庶子、中舍人諸官並未置，唯置衛率令典兵，二傅並攝眾事。 憙在位累年，訓道盡規。 遷尚書僕射，拜特進、光祿大夫，以年老遜位。 詔曰：「光祿大夫、特進李憙，杖德居義，當升台司。 毗亮朕躬，而以年尊致仕。 雖優遊無為，可以頤神，而虛心之望，能不憮然！ 其因光祿之號，改假金紫，置官騎十人，賜錢五十萬，祿賜班禮，一如三司，門施行馬。」
The same year the heir apparent was installed, Li Xi became his Grand Tutor. Since Wei Mingdi the heir's residence had stood empty for years; regulations had lapsed and offices were unfilled—no Household Superintendent, Left or Right Guard superintendents, household sons, or inner gentlemen—only a Guard commander led troops while both tutors handled everything else. Li Xi held the post for years, instructing the heir and offering thorough counsel. He rose to Vice Director of the Masters of Writing and received Exceptional Advance and Household Grandee titles before yielding office to age. An edict declared: "Household Grandee and Exceptional Advance Li Xi upholds virtue and righteousness and ought to join the highest ministers. He has aided and enlightened Us, yet advanced years have led him to retire. Leisure may nourish him, yet Our hopes for his counsel leave Us deeply regretful! Retain his Household Grandee title but confer provisional gold ribbon and purple robe, assign ten mounted attendants, grant five hundred thousand cash, and extend stipends and ceremonial honors equal to the Three Dukes, including barrier poles at his gate."
12
初，憙為僕射時，涼州虜寇邊，憙唱義遣軍討之。 朝士謂出兵不易，虜未足為患，竟不從之。 後虜果大縱逸，涼州覆沒，朝廷深悔焉。 以憙清素貧儉，賜絹百匹。 及齊王攸出鎮，憙上疏諫爭，辭甚懇切。 憙自曆仕，雖清非異眾，而家無儲積，親舊故人乃至分衣共食，未嘗私以王官。 及卒，追贈太保，諡曰成。 子贊嗣。
Earlier, while Li Xi was Vice Director, tribes raided Liangzhou; he took the lead in proposing a punitive expedition. Court officials argued that campaigning was difficult and the raid no real threat, so his plan was rejected. The tribes later swept the region unchecked and Liangzhou fell; the court bitterly regretted ignoring him. Because Li Xi lived plainly and in poverty, he was granted one hundred bolts of silk. When Prince Qi Sima You left for his assignment, Li Xi submitted an earnest memorial arguing against it. Across his career Li Xi was honest like many yet kept no savings; relatives and old friends sometimes shared clothes and food, and he never abused imperial appointment for private ends. After his death he was posthumously honored as Grand Guardian with the posthumous name Cheng ("Accomplished"). His son Li Zan inherited the title.
13

劉寔，字子真，平原高唐人也。 漢濟北惠王壽之後也，父廣，斥丘令。 寔少貧苦，賣牛衣以自給。 然好學，手約繩，口誦書，博通古今。 清身潔己，行無瑕玷。 郡察孝廉，州舉秀才，皆不行。 以計吏入洛，調為河南尹丞，遷尚書郎、廷尉正。 後曆吏部郎，參文帝相國軍事，封循陽子。
Liu Shi, courtesy name Zizhen, was a native of Gaotang in Pingyuan Commandery. He descended from Prince Hui of Jibei (Liu Shou) under the Han; his father Liu Guang was magistrate of Chiqiu. Liu Shi grew up destitute and sold straw coats for cattle to get by. Yet he loved learning, knotting cords as he studied and reciting aloud until he mastered classical and recent scholarship. He kept himself scrupulously clean and his conduct blameless. The commandery nominated him Filial and Incorrupt and the province as Cultivated Talent; he declined both. He entered Luoyang as an accounting clerk, became assistant to the Henan governor, then Gentleman of the Masters of Writing and director at the Court of Justice. He later headed the appointments bureau, joined Prince Wen's minister-of-state staff, and was enfeoffed as Baron of Xun.

When Zhong Hui and Deng Ai invaded Shu, a visitor asked Liu Shi whether the two generals would conquer it. Liu Shi replied: "They will crush Shu, but neither will come back." Asked why, he smiled and gave no answer; events unfolded exactly as he predicted. His prescience was often of this sort.

Seeing people scramble for promotion while modest yielding had vanished, he wrote "Treatise Honoring Yielding" to correct the trend. It reads:
17
古之聖王之化天下，所以貴讓者，欲以出賢才，息爭競也。 夫人情莫不欲已之賢也，故勸令讓賢以自明賢也，豈假讓不賢哉！ 故讓道興，賢能之人不求而自出矣，至公之舉自立矣，百官之副亦豫具矣。 一官缺，擇眾官所讓最多者而用之，審之道也。 在朝之士相讓於上，草廬之人咸皆化之，推賢讓能之風從此生矣。 為一國所讓，則一國士也； 天下所共推，則天下士也。 推讓之風行，則賢與不肖灼然殊矣。 此道之行，在上者無所用其心，因成清議，隨之而已。 故曰，蕩蕩乎堯之為君，莫之能名。 言天下自安矣，不見堯所以化之，故不能名也。 又曰，舜禹之有天下而不與焉，無為而化者其舜也歟。 賢人相讓于朝，大才之人恆在大官，小人不爭於野，天下無事矣。 以賢才化無事，至道興矣。 已仰其成，復何與焉！ 故可以歌《南風》之詩，彈五弦之琴也。 成此功者非有他，崇讓之所致耳。 孔子曰，能以禮讓為國，則不難也。
The ancient sage kings prized yielding because it drew forth worthy men and stilled rivalry. Everyone wishes to be thought worthy, so we urge men to yield posts to worthier candidates and thereby prove their own worth—it is no sham yielding to the unworthy! When yielding prevails, able men appear without being sought, the most impartial appointments take care of themselves, and deputy posts are filled in advance. When a post opens, pick the candidate whom most colleagues recommended through yielding—that is sound policy. Ministers yield to one another before the throne, common scholars follow suit, and the custom of recommending worthier men begins. One whom a whole kingdom yields to is that kingdom's leading gentleman; one whom all under Heaven recommends is the foremost gentleman of the realm. When yielding spreads, the worthy and unworthy stand clearly apart. Under this practice rulers need plot little: pure public opinion forms and they need only follow it. Hence the saying: "So vast was Yao as ruler that none could put a name to his deeds." It means the realm was tranquil without anyone pointing to how Yao wrought it, so his virtue could not be labeled. It also says Shun and Yu possessed the realm yet acted as if they did not; Shun transformed all by nonassertive rule. When worthies yield to one another at court, great talent fills high office and common folk cease quarreling in the countryside—the realm is untroubled. When worthy talent brings effortless peace, the highest Way prevails. The ruler need only rely on their success—what more must he do? Thus he could sing the "South Wind" ode and play the five-string lute. No other force produced such success—only the elevation of yielding. Confucius said: "Governing a state through ritual deference is no difficult thing."
18
在朝之人不務相讓久矣，天下化之。 自魏代以來，登進辟命之士，及在職之吏，臨見受敘，雖自辭不能，終莫肯讓有勝己者。 夫推讓之風息，爭競之心生。 孔子曰，上興讓則下不爭，明讓不興下必爭也。 推讓之道興，則賢能之人日見推舉； 爭競之心生，則賢能之人日見謗毀。 夫爭者之欲自先，甚惡能者之先，不能無毀也。 故孔墨不能免世之謗己，況不及孔墨者乎！ 議者僉然言，世少高名之才，朝廷不有大才之人可以為大官者。 山澤人小官吏亦復雲，朝廷之士雖有大官名德，皆不及往時人也。 餘以為此二言皆失之矣。 非時獨乏賢也，時不貴讓。 一人有先眾之譽，毀必隨之，名不得成使之然也。 雖令稷契復存，亦不復能全其名矣。 能否混雜，優劣不分，士無素定之價，官職有缺，主選之吏不知所用，但案官次而舉之。 同才之人先用者，非勢家之子，則必為有勢者之所念也。 非能獨賢，因其先用之資，而復遷之無已。 遷之無已，不勝其任之病發矣。 觀在官之人，政績無聞，自非勢家之子，率多因資次而進也。
Court officials have long ceased yielding to one another, and the realm has followed suit. Since the Wei dynasty, appointed scholars and serving clerics, when summoned and ranked, even when nominally declining always refuse to yield to anyone better qualified. When yielding dies, the scramble for place begins. Confucius said: "When those above honor yielding, those below do not fight; when yielding does not flourish below, they inevitably fight." When yielding prevails, worthy men are recommended day by day; when rivalry grows, worthy men face daily slander. Those who seek priority resent anyone ahead of them and cannot refrain from tearing him down. Even Confucius and Mozi could not escape vilification—what hope for lesser men? Critics all complain that the age lacks celebrated talent and the court has no great men fit for high office. Rustic clerks echo that today's ministers, however titled or reputed, fall short of earlier generations. I believe both judgments miss the mark. The problem is not missing worthies but failing to honor yielding. Anyone praised ahead of the pack attracts slander; reputations founder for that reason. Even if Ji and Xie lived again, they could not keep their names intact. Able and incapable blur together; gentlemen lack fixed standing; when posts open, personnel officers pick blindly by seniority. Among equals the first chosen are either sons of powerful houses or protégés of the mighty. They are not uniquely worthy; once favored they rise without cease. Endless promotion breeds failures from incompetence. Look at officials today: few distinguished records; aside from sons of influence most advance by mere seniority.
19
向令天下貴讓，士必由於見讓而後名成，名成而官乃得用之。 諸名行不立之人，在官無政績之稱，讓之者必矣，官無因得而用之也。 所以見用不息者，由讓道廢，因資用人之有失久矣。 故自漢魏以來，時開大舉，令眾官各舉所知，唯才所任，不限階次，如此者甚數矣。 其所舉必有當者，不聞時有擢用，不知何誰最賢故也。 所舉必有不當，而罪不加，不知何誰最不肖也。 所以不可得知，由當時之人莫肯相推，賢愚之名不別，令其如此。 舉者知在上者察不能審，故敢漫舉而進之。 或舉所賢，因及所念，一頓而至，人數猥多，各言所舉者賢，加之高狀，相似如一，難得而分矣。 參錯相亂，真偽同貫，更復由此而甚。 雖舉者不能盡忠之罪，亦由上開聽察之路濫，令其爾也。 昔齊王好聽竽聲，必令三百人合吹而後聽之，廩以數人之俸。 南郭先生不知吹竽者也，以三百人合吹可以容其不知，因請為王吹竽，虛食數人之俸。 嗣王覺而改之，難彰先王之過。 乃下令曰：「吾之好聞竽聲有甚于先王，欲一一列而聽之。 」先生于此逃矣。 推賢之風不立，濫舉之法不改，則南郭先生之徒盈於朝矣。 才高守道之士日退，馳走有勢之門日多矣。 雖國有典刑，弗能禁矣。
If yielding were honored across the realm, a scholar would first win renown by being publicly endorsed, and only then could office legitimately take him up. Men with no standing reputation and no record of achievement would find no sponsors; the government would have no warrant to appoint them. Endless recycling of unfit officials stems from the collapse of yielding and from long reliance on mere seniority. Since Han and Wei, the court often ordered mass recommendations without rank restrictions—purely talent-based—and did so repeatedly. Some nominees must have been fit, yet timely promotions are unheard of because no one can tell who is worthiest. Some nominees must be unsuitable, yet no one is punished because no one can single out the worst. The confusion persists because contemporaries refuse to recommend others and blur the worthy with the foolish. Recommenders know superiors cannot vet every nominee, so they nominate recklessly. People nominate favorites and pile on florid praise until every résumé sounds alike and distinctions vanish. Truth and falsehood tangle until the mess grows worse. Nominees share blame, yet rulers invite abuse by opening indiscriminate channels for scrutiny. The king of Qi loved ensemble yu pipes and listened only when three hundred played together, paying them like several dozen salaries. Mr. Nanguo could not play, but hiding in a choir of three hundred hid his ignorance; he drew several salaries for nothing. The next king saw the problem yet spared his father's reputation. He decreed: "I love the yu even more than my predecessor and will hear each player solo." At that Mr. Nanguo fled. Until recommending worthies matters and sham nominations stop, the court will swarm with Mr. Nanguos. Able men withdraw while climbers flock to powerful houses. Statutes alone cannot stop it.
20
夫讓道不興之弊，非徒賢人在下位，不得時進也，國之良臣荷重任者，亦將以漸受罪退矣。 何以知其然也？ 孔子以為顏氏之子不貳過耳，明非聖人皆有過。 寵貴之地欲之者多矣，惡賢能者塞其路，其過而毀之者亦多矣。 夫謗毀之生，非徒空設，必因人之微過而甚之者也。 譭謗之言數聞，在上者雖欲弗納，不能不杖所聞，因事之來而微察之也，無以，其驗至矣。 得其驗，安得不理其罪。 若知而縱之，王之威日衰，令之不行自此始矣。 知而皆理之，受罪退者稍多，大臣有不自固之心。 夫賢才不進，貴臣日疏，此有國者之深憂也。 《詩》曰：「受祿不讓，至於已斯亡。 」不讓之人憂亡不暇，而望其益國朝，不亦難乎！
When yielding fails, not only do juniors stall; senior ministers themselves suffer reprimands and fall away. How do we know? Confucius praised Yan Hui for not repeating mistakes—proof that even worthies err. Coveted posts attract crowds; rivals block able men and seize on any slip to slander them. Slander is seldom invented whole cloth—it magnifies small faults. Rumors pile up; rulers may wish to ignore them yet must note each report and investigate—evidence soon surfaces. Once proof appears, crimes must be punished. If rulers know yet indulge it, royal authority ebbs and orders cease to run. If every charge is prosecuted, dismissed ministers multiply and senior officials lose confidence. Blocked talent and estranged ministers are a ruler's gravest concern. The Odes says: "Those who take emoluments without yielding bring ruin on themselves." Men who refuse to yield fret over survival—how can they benefit the state?
21
竊以為改此俗甚易耳。 何以知之？ 夫一時在官之人，雖雜有凡猥之才，其中賢明者亦多矣，豈可謂皆不知讓賢為貴邪！ 直以其時皆不讓，習以成俗，故遂不為耳。 人臣初除，皆通表上聞，名之謝章，所由來尚矣。 原謝章之本意，欲進賢能以謝國恩也。 昔舜以禹為司空，禹拜稽首，讓於稷契及咎繇。 使益為虞官，讓於硃虎、熊、羆。 使伯夷典三禮，讓于夔龍。 唐虞之時，眾官初除，莫不皆讓也。 謝章之義，蓋取於此。 《書》記之者，欲以永世作則。 季世所用，不賢不能讓賢，虛謝見用之恩而已。 相承不變，習俗之失也。
Changing this habit would be easy. Why? Today's officials include mediocrities, yet many are wise—they know yielding is honorable. They merely follow habit because no one yields anymore. New appointees always memorialize the throne—the "gratitude memorial"—an ancient practice. Originally it meant recommending worthier men to repay imperial favor. When Shun named Yu Minister of Works, Yu kowtowed and yielded to Ji, Qi, and Gao Yao. When Yi became superintendent of forests he yielded to Zhu Hu, Xiong, and Pi. When Boyi took charge of ritual he yielded to Kui and Long. Under Yao and Shun every new minister yielded. The gratitude memorial surely derives from this precedent. The Documents preserved these acts as models for all ages. Later generations hollowed it into empty thanks unless one were worthy enough to yield. Unchanging habit corrupted the rite.
22
夫敘用之官得通章表者，其讓賢推能乃通，其不能有所讓徒費簡紙者，皆絕不通。 人臣初除，各思推賢能而讓之矣，讓之文付主者掌之。 三司有缺，擇三司所讓最多者而用之。 此為一公缺，三公已豫選之矣。 且主選之吏，不必任公而選三公，不如令三公自共選一公為詳也。 四征缺，擇四征所讓最多而用之，此為一征缺，四征已豫選之矣，必詳於停缺而令主者選四征也。 尚書缺，擇尚書所讓最多者而用之，此為八尚書共選一尚書，詳于臨缺令主者選八尚書也。 郡守缺，擇眾郡所讓最多者而用之，詳于任主者令選百郡守也。
Personnel officials may forward only memorials that nominate worthier men; empty paperwork is refused. Every new appointee must propose a worthier substitute; the nominating text stays on file. When a Three Ducal seat opens, pick the candidate whom the three dukes most often yielded to. Thus one vacancy yields three advance nominees. Better than letting personnel pick dukes is to let the three dukes jointly fill one seat—that yields finer judgment. Apply the same rule to the four expedition generals: let them nominate among themselves rather than leaving posts frozen while clerks guess. When a Masters of Writing post opens, choose from nominees whom serving ministers most endorsed—better than freezing eight seats while clerks decide. For governors, pick from empire-wide endorsements rather than letting one clerk choose among hundreds.
23
夫以眾官百郡之讓，與主者共相比，不可同歲而論也。 雖復令三府參舉官，本不委以舉選之任，各不能以根其心也。 其所用心者裁之不二三，但令主者案官次而舉之，不用精也。 賢愚皆讓，百姓耳目盡為國耳目。 夫人情爭則欲毀己所不知，讓則競推於勝己。 故世爭則毀譽交錯，優劣不分，難得而讓也。 時讓則賢智顯出，能否之美歷歷相次，不可得而亂也。 當此時也，能退身修已者，讓之者多矣。 雖欲守貧賤，不可得也。 馳騖進趣而欲人見讓，猶卻行而求前也。 夫如此，愚智咸知進身求通，非修之於己則無由矣。 游外求者，於此相隨而歸矣。 浮聲虛論，不禁而自息矣。 人人無所用其心，任眾人之議，而天下自化矣。 不言之化行，巍巍之美於此著矣。 讓可以致此，豈可不務之哉！
Collective judgment across counties outweighs a single clerk's opinion—it is not comparable. Even three bureaus nominating together lack full stake—they cannot commit wholeheartedly. They skim candidates and leave personnel to promote by roster—no refinement. When everyone yields recommendations, the people become the ruler's eyes and ears. Rivalry breeds slander of unknown rivals; yielding urges promotion of betters. Strife scrambles praise and blame until yielding is impossible. When yielding prevails, talent stands out in clear order—nothing confuses it. Then those who cultivate themselves attract many sponsors. They cannot stay obscure even if they wish. Scheming for promotion while expecting others to yield you is like walking backward to advance. Then everyone sees that advancement requires self-cultivation—there is no other path. Opportunists fade away. Empty gossip dies without edicts. Rulers need not scheme—trust public consensus and the realm orders itself. Silent transformation unfolds—great majesty appears. Yielding achieves this—how can we neglect it?
24
《春秋傳》曰：「范宣子之讓，其下皆讓。 欒黶雖汰，弗敢違也。 晉國以平，數世賴之。 」上世之化也，君子尚能而讓其下，小人力農以事其上，上下有禮，讒慝遠黜，由不爭也。 及其亂也，國家之弊，恆必由之。 篤論了了如此。 在朝君子典選大官，能不以人廢言，舉而行之，各以讓賢舉能為先務，則群才猥出，能否殊別，蓋世之功，莫大於此。
The Zuo Tradition says: "When Fan Xuanzi yielded, his subordinates yielded too." Even the arrogant Luan Yan did not dare disobey. Jin regained peace and enjoyed stability for generations. In high antiquity gentlemen honored ability and yielded to juniors; commoners farmed to serve superiors; ritual ran above and below; slander vanished because none quarreled. When order collapses, national ruin always stems from contention. Sound doctrine states it plainly. If ministers who manage appointments heed this counsel and put recommending worthier men first, talent will multiply and ranks will sort themselves—no reform would surpass it.

Early in Taishi he rose from baron to earl and eventually Privy Treasurer. During Xianning he served as Minister of Ceremonies. He was transferred to Master of Writing. During Du Yu's conquest of Wu, Liu Shi served as army clerk to the Guard South command at his current rank.

Unable to refuse, he married into the Huas and fathered Xia. Liu Shi lost office when Xia took bribes. Soon restored as Minister of Agriculture, he was dismissed again over Xia's crimes.

Whenever Liu Shi visited home, villagers brought wine and meat to greet him. Loath to refuse, he shared the meal and sent back what remained. Someone said: "Your integrity towers above the age, yet your sons do not follow it. Why not instruct them daily until they mend their faults? Liu Shi replied: "What I follow comes from what I saw and heard—it was not handed down by habit; lectures cannot replicate that." People judged his reply sound.
28
後起為國子祭酒、散騎常侍。 湣懷太子初封廣陵王，高選師友，以寔為師。 元康初，進爵為侯，累遷太子太保，加侍中、特進、右光祿大夫、開府儀同三司，領冀州都督。 九年，策拜司空，遷太保，轉太傅。 太安初，寔以老病遜位，賜安車駟馬、錢百萬，以侯就第。 及長沙成都之相攻也，寔為軍人所掠，潛歸鄉里。
He was later recalled as Libationer of the Imperial Academy and Regular Palace Attendant. When Crown Prince Minhuai was first invested as Prince of Guangling, Liu Shi was chosen as his tutor. Early in Yuankang he advanced to marquis, rose to Crown Prince Grand Guardian, added Palace Attendant, Exceptional Advance, Right Household Grandee, establishment equal to the Three Dukes, and concurrent Ji Province commander. In the ninth year he was appointed Minister of Works, then Grand Guardian, then Grand Tutor. Early in Taian he resigned for age and illness, receiving a carriage, team, one million cash, and retired home as marquis. When the princes of Changsha and Chengdu fought, soldiers plundered Liu Shi, who slipped home.

三年，詔曰：「昔虞任五臣，致垂拱之化，漢相蕭何，興寧一之譽，故能光隆於當時，垂裕於百代。 朕紹天明命，臨御萬邦，所以崇顯政道者，亦賴之於元臣庶尹，畢力股肱，以副至望。 而君年耆告老，確然難違。 今聽君以侯就第，位居三司之上，秩祿准舊，賜几杖不朝及宅一區。 國之大政，將就諮於君，副朕意焉。 」歲余薨，時年九十一，諡曰元。
In the third year an edict ran: "Ancient Yu employed five ministers and achieved effortless rule; Han Chancellor Xiao He won fame for securing unity—thus they shone in their day and blessed later ages. We have received Heaven's charge and rule all lands; exalted governance depends on chief ministers who serve as Our arms and legs to meet Our hopes. Yet you cite age and wish to retire—We cannot refuse resolutely. We permit you to retire home as marquis with precedence above the Three Dukes, stipends as before, a stool and staff excusing court attendance, and one residence. We shall consult you on great affairs of state so Our wishes may be fulfilled. He died a little over a year later at ninety-one with the posthumous name Yuan ("Primary").
30
寔少貧窶，杖策徒行，每所憩止，不累主人，薪水之事，皆自營給。 及位望通顯，每崇儉素，不尚華麗。 嘗詣石崇家，如廁，見有絳紋帳，裀褥甚麗，兩婢持香囊。 寔便退，笑謂崇曰：「誤入卿內。 」崇曰：「是廁耳。 」寔曰：「貧士未嘗得此。 」乃更如他廁。 雖處榮寵，居無第宅，所得俸祿，贍恤親故。 雖禮教陵遲，而行己以正。 喪妻為廬杖之制，終喪不禦內。 輕薄者笑之，寔不以介意。 自少及老，篤學不倦，雖居職務，卷弗離手。 尤精《三傳》，辨正《公羊》，以為衛輒不應辭以王父命，祭仲失為臣之節，舉此二端以明臣子之體，遂行於世。 又撰《春秋條例》二十卷。
Liu Shi grew up destitute, walking with a staff, and whenever he lodged he took nothing from hosts, gathering fuel and water himself. After he rose to eminence he still favored plain simplicity over display. Visiting Shi Chong, he entered a lavatory hung with crimson brocade, fine cushions, and two maids holding sachets. Liu Shi stepped back and said with a smile: "I blundered into your private rooms." Shi Chong said: "That is the privy." Liu Shi answered: "A poor scholar like me has never seen the like." He then used a different privy. Despite high rank he kept no grand house, spending his salary on kin and old friends. Even as ritual declined he lived uprightly. Mourning his wife he kept mourning hut and staff rites and avoided the inner chambers until the period ended. The shallow mocked him; Liu Shi ignored them. From youth to age he studied tirelessly and never set a book aside even in office. He argued that Prince Zhe of Wei should not refuse office citing his grandfather's command and that Jizhong lacked the standard of a minister; these two examples clarified the duties of subject and son and won acceptance across the realm. He also wrote twenty fascicles of Chunqiu regulations.
31
有二子，躋、夏。 躋字景雲，官至散騎常侍。 夏以貪污棄放於世。
He had two sons, Ji and Xia. Liu Ji, courtesy name Jingyun, rose to Regular Palace Attendant. Liu Xia was disgraced and cast out for corruption.
32
弟智，字子房，貞素有兄風。 少貧窶，每負薪自給，讀誦不輟，竟以儒行稱。 曆中書黃門吏部郎，出為潁川太守。 平原管輅嘗謂人曰：「吾與劉潁川兄弟語，使人神思清發，昏不假寐。 自此之外，殆白日欲寢矣。 」入為秘書監，領南陽王師，加散騎常侍，遷侍中、尚書、太常。 著《喪服釋疑論》，多所辨明。 太康末卒，諡曰成。
His younger brother Liu Zhi, courtesy name Zifang, shared his brother's integrity. He was poor in youth, carried firewood to live, never stopped reading, and was praised for Confucian conduct. He served in the Palace Bureau, at the Yellow Gates, and as Personnel Director, then became governor of Yingchuan. Guan Lu of Pingyuan said: "Conversation with the Liu brothers of Yingchuan clears the mind; I need no nap by day." With anyone else I could nap all afternoon." He became Secretary Supervisor, tutor to the Prince of Nanyang, Regular Palace Attendant, then Palace Attendant, Master of Writing, and Minister of Ceremonies. He wrote "Treatise Clarifying Doubts on Mourning Dress," settling many questions. He died near the end of Taikang with the posthumous name Cheng.
33
高光，字宣茂，陳留圉城人，魏太尉柔之子也。 光少習家業，明練刑理。 初以太子舍人累遷尚書郎，出為幽州刺史、潁州太守。 是時武帝置黃沙獄，以典詔囚。 以光曆世明法，用為黃沙御史，秩與中丞同，遷廷尉。 元康中，拜尚書，典三公曹。 時趙王倫篡逆，光於其際，守道全貞。 及倫賜死，齊王冏輔政，復以光為廷尉，遷尚書，加奉車都尉。 後從駕討成都王穎有勳，封延陵縣公，邑千八百戶。 于時朝廷咸推光明于用法，故頻典理官。 惠帝為張方所逼，幸長安，朝臣奔散，莫有從者，光獨侍帝而西。 遷尚書左僕射，加散騎常侍。 光兄誕為上官巳等所用，曆徐、雍二州刺史。 誕性任放無倫次，而決烈過人，與光異操。 常謂光小節，恆輕侮之，光事誕愈謹。 帝既還洛陽，時太弟新立，重選傅訓，以光為少傅，加光祿大夫，常侍如故。 及懷帝即位，加光祿大夫金章紫綬，與傅祗並見推崇。 尋為尚書令，本官如故。 以疾卒，贈司空、侍中。 屬京洛傾覆，竟未加諡。
Gao Guang, courtesy name Xuanmao, was from Yucheng in Chenliu and son of Wei Grand Commandant Gao Rou. He studied his family's legal tradition from youth and mastered penal doctrine. He began as crown prince attendant, rose to Gentleman of the Masters of Writing, then served as Inspector of Youzhou and governor of Yingzhou. Emperor Wu then founded the Yellow Sand prison for imperial-order cases. For his family's legal expertise he became Yellow Sand Imperial Clerk with rank equal to the Palace Assistant Clerk, then Director of Justice. During Yuankang he became Master of Writing and headed the Three Ducal bureau. When Prince Zhao Lun usurped the throne, Gao Guang kept faith and integrity. After Lun was forced to die, Prince of Qi Sima Jiong took charge and reappointed Gao Guang Director of Justice, then Master of Writing with the additional title Colonel of Chariots. He later accompanied the emperor against Prince Chengdu Sima Ying and earned merit, receiving the rank Duke of Yanling with eighteen hundred households. The court praised his clarity in applying law and repeatedly placed him over justice posts. When Emperor Hui was driven by Zhang Fang to Chang'an and ministers fled, only Gao Guang accompanied him west. He rose to Left Vice Director of the Masters of Writing and Regular Palace Attendant. Gao Dan worked for Shangguan Si and successively governed Xu and Yong. Gao Dan was reckless and undisciplined yet bolder than most—a temper unlike his brother. He sneered at Gao Guang's caution, yet Guang treated him with increasing respect. After the emperor returned to Luoyang and the heir apparent was newly installed, tutors were chosen carefully; Gao Guang became Junior Tutor with Household Grandee and continued as Regular Attendant. When Emperor Huai took the throne, Gao Guang received Household Grandee with gold seal and purple ribbon alongside Fu Zhi in high esteem. Soon afterward he became Director of the Masters of Writing while keeping prior duties. He died of illness and was posthumously honored as Minister of Works and Palace Attendant. The fall of Luoyang prevented conferral of a posthumous name.
34
子韜字子遠，放佚無檢。 光為廷尉時，韜受貨賕，有司奏案之，而光不知。 時人雖非光不能防閑其子，以其用心有素，不以為累。 初，光詣長安留台，以韜兼右衛將軍。 韜與殿省小人交通，及光卒，仍於喪中往來不絕。 時東海王越輔政，不朝觀。 韜知人心有望，密與太傅參軍薑賾、京兆杜概等謀討越，事泄伏誅。
His son Gao Tao, courtesy name Ziyuan, was dissolute and unrestrained. While Gao Guang headed the Court of Justice, Tao took bribes; authorities impeached him but Guang knew nothing. Critics faulted him for not controlling his son, yet his lifelong integrity spared him lasting blame. Earlier, when Gao Guang attended the provisional court at Chang'an, he named Tao concurrent Right Guard general. Tao consorted with palace underlings and, after Guang died, continued those visits even in mourning. Prince Donghai Sima Yue held power but skipped court audiences. Reading popular resentment, Tao secretly joined Grand Tutor aide Jiang Ze and Du Gai of Jingzhao to move against Yue; the plot leaked and he was executed.
35
史臣曰：下士競而文，中庸靜而質，不若進不足而退有餘也。 魏舒、劉寔發慮精華，結綬登槐，覽止成務。 季和切問近對，當官正色。 詩云「貪人敗類」，豈劉夏之謂歟！
The historians write: petty scholars strive for polish; the moderate stay plain—it is better to advance modestly and retire amply. Wei Shu and Liu Shi distilled keen insight, entered high office, grasped essentials, and delivered results. Li Xi answered probing questions plainly and faced duty with a stern air. The Odes says "greedy men ruin their kin"—did it mean Liu Xia?
36
贊曰：舒言不矜，憙對千乘。 子真、宣茂，雅志難陵。 進忠能舉，退讓攸興。 皎皎瑚器，來光玉繩。
The eulogy reads: Wei Shu spoke without arrogance; Li Xi answered princes to their faces. Liu Zizhen and Gao Xuanmao pursued lofty aims none could eclipse. They advanced loyal talent and welcomed yielding when stepping back. Bright as coral vessels, they brought luster like stars along the Jade Cord.
