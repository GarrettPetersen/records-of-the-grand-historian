1
魏舒，字陽元，任城樊人也。 少孤，為外家寧氏所養。 寧氏起宅，相宅者云：「當出貴甥。 」外祖母以魏氏甥小而慧，意謂應之。 舒曰：「當為外氏成此宅相。 」久乃別居。 身長八尺二寸，姿望秀偉，飲酒石餘，而遲鈍質樸，不為鄉親所重。 從叔父吏部郎衡，有名當世，亦不之知，使守水碓，每歎曰：「舒堪數百戶長，我願畢矣！ 」舒亦不以介意。 不修常人之節，不為皎曆之事，每欲容才長物，終不顯人之短。 性好騎射，著韋衣。 入山澤，以漁獵為事。 唯太原王乂謂舒曰：「卿終當為台輔，然今未能令妻子免饑寒，吾當助卿營之。 」常振其匱乏，舒受而不辭。 舒嘗詣野王，主人妻夜產，俄而聞車馬之聲，相問曰：「男也，女也？ 」曰：「男，書之，十五以兵死。 」復問：「寢者為誰？ 」曰：「魏公舒。 」後十五載，詣主人，問所生兒何在，曰：「因條桑為斧傷而死。 」舒自知當為公矣。
Wei Shu, whose courtesy name was Yangyuan, came from Fan in Rencheng commandery. Orphaned while still a boy, he was brought up in the household of his mother's people, the Nings. As the Nings raised a new residence, a geomancer told them it would produce a distinguished nephew. His maternal grandmother, seeing the Wei boy as bright despite his youth, assumed the prophecy pointed to him. Shu replied, "Then it falls to our mother's family to make good on that sign." Only much later did he move out and establish a separate household. He stood eight chi two cun tall, cut a striking figure, and could put away more than a picul of wine, yet he seemed slow and unpolished, so neighbors and kin thought little of him. His father's cousin Heng, a celebrated Director of Personnel, never saw his talent either and put him in charge of the water mill, sighing that if Shu could manage a few hundred households his own ambitions would be satisfied. Shu did not let it trouble him. He neither fussed over petty proprieties nor sought flashy renown; he preferred to make room for others' gifts and never aired their faults. He loved riding and archery and went about in leather hunting dress. He spent his time in hills and wetlands, fishing and hunting for a living. Only Wang Yi of Taiyuan told him, "You are destined for the highest councils, yet today you cannot keep your household from want; I will help you see to their needs." Wang regularly relieved his straits, and Shu accepted without demur. Once when Shu was staying in Yewang, the host's wife labored at night; soon the clatter of horses and wheels was heard, and voices called back and forth: a boy or a girl? The answer came: a boy—write it down—he will die by the sword at fifteen. Then they asked who was sleeping in the house. "Wei Shu," came the reply. Fifteen years later Shu looked up his host and asked after the child; the man said the boy had been killed by an axe while trimming mulberry. Shu understood then that he was fated to rise to the rank of duke.
2
年四十餘，郡上計掾察孝廉。 宗党以舒無學業，勸令不就，可以為高耳。 舒曰：「若試而不中，其負在我，安可虛竊不就之高以為己榮乎！ 」於是自課。 百日習一經，因而對策升第。 除澠池長，遷浚儀令，入為尚書郎。 時欲沙汰郎官。 非其才者罷之。 舒曰：「吾即其人也。 」襆被而出。 同僚素無清論者咸有愧色，談者稱之。
After he turned forty, the commandery sent him up as accounts clerk and nominated him Filial and Incorrupt. Kinsmen thought him without book learning and urged him to stay home, which would look admirably high-minded. Shu answered, "If I sit the exam and fail, the blame is mine; I will not borrow a false reputation by refusing what I have not earned." He then imposed a strict course of study on himself. Within a hundred days he had worked through one classic, passed the palace examination, and won placement. He was named magistrate of Mianchi, then of Junyi, and finally entered court service as a Gentleman of the Masters of Writing. A purge of the gentleman clerks was under way. Anyone judged unfit was to be dismissed. "I am one of those," said Shu. He rolled up his bedding and walked out. Fellow officials who had never passed for men of principle colored with shame, while observers praised his integrity.
3
累遷後將軍鐘毓長史，毓每與參佐射，舒常為畫籌而已。 後遇朋人不足，以舒滿數。 毓初不知其善射。 舒容范閒雅，發無不中，舉坐愕然。 莫有敵者。 毓歎而謝曰：「吾之不足以盡卿才，有如此射矣，豈一事哉！ 」轉相國參軍，封劇陽子。 府朝碎務，未嘗見是非； 至於廢興大事，眾人莫能斷者，舒徐為籌之，多出眾議之表。 文帝深器重之，每朝會坐罷，目送之曰：「魏舒堂堂，人之領袖也。 」遷宜陽、滎陽二郡太守，甚有聲稱。 徵拜散騎常侍。 出為冀州刺史，在州三年，以簡惠稱。 入為侍中。 武帝以舒清素，特賜絹百匹。 遷尚書，以公事當免官，詔以贖論。 舒三娶妻皆亡，是歲自表乞假還本郡葬妻，詔賜葬地一頃，錢五十萬。
Promoted in stages to chief clerk under General of the Rear Zhong Yu, Shu usually only tallied arrows when Yu and his aides shot for sport. Once the archery party lacked a man, they added Shu to round out the teams. Zhong Yu had not known he could shoot. Shu stood at ease, every arrow found its mark, and the company sat thunderstruck. No one there could rival him. Yu sighed and apologized: "If I have failed to use your gifts fully, archery alone proves it—this cannot be the only field." He was moved to adviser on the minister of state's staff and enfeoffed as Baron of Juyang. On petty bureau business he never tipped his hand; yet on great questions of state that left the staff divided, he would quietly think them through and often outstrip every other proposal. Emperor Wen (Sima Zhao) valued him deeply; as court broke up he would watch Shu leave and murmur, "Wei Shu has true presence—he is the sort men follow." He was promoted to governor of Yiyang and Xingyang, earning wide acclaim in both posts. He was recalled to the capital as Cavalier Attendant-in-Ordinary. Sent out as provincial inspector of Ji, he governed three years and was known for a light touch and humane rule. He returned to court as Palace Attendant. Emperor Wu, noting his austerity, presented him with a hundred bolts of silk as a special mark of favor. Raised to Masters of Writing, he faced dismissal over an official matter; an edict allowed him to commute the penalty with a fine. All three of Shu's wives had died; that year he asked leave to go home and bury the last, and the throne granted him a hectare of burial ground and half a million cash.
4
太康初，拜右僕射。 舒與衛瓘、山濤、張華等以六合混一，宜用古典封禪東嶽，前後累陳其事，帝謙讓不許。 以舒為左僕射，領吏部。 舒上言：「今選六宮，聘以玉帛，而舊使禦府丞奉聘，宣成嘉禮，贄重使輕。 以為拜三夫人宜使卿，九嬪使五官中郎將，美人、良人使謁者，於典制為弘。 」有詔詳之，眾議異同，遂寢。 加右光祿大夫、儀同三司。
Early in the Taikang era he was appointed Right Vice Director of the Secretariat. Shu joined Wei Guan, Shan Tao, Zhang Hua, and others in urging that, with the realm reunited, the court ought to perform the classical Feng and Shan sacrifices on Mount Tai; they memorialized again and again, but the emperor modestly refused. He was made Left Vice Director of the Secretariat with acting charge of the Ministry of Personnel. Shu memorialized: "Palace ladies are chosen with jade and silk as bride-price, yet we still send the assistant from the imperial workshops to carry the gifts—the ritual is grand but the envoy beneath it." "Investitures of the Three Ladies should go through ministers, the Nine Concubines through the leader of court gentlemen for domestic service, the Beauties and Worthies through reception officers—then the statutes would match the dignity of the rite." The court ordered study, opinions split, and the proposal lapsed. He was additionally named Grand Master of the Right with ceremonial rank equal to the Three Dukes.
5
及山濤薨，以舒領司徒，有頃即真。 舒有威重德望，祿賜散之九族，家無餘財。 陳留周震累為諸府所辟，辟書既下，公輒喪亡，僉號震為殺公掾，莫有辟者。 舒乃命之，而竟無患，識者以此稱其達命。 以年老，每稱疾遜位。 中復暫起，署兗州中正，尋又稱疾。 尚書左丞郤詵與舒書曰：「公久疾小差，視事是也，唯上所念。 何竟起訖還臥，曲身回法，甚失具瞻之望。 公少立巍巍，一旦棄之，可不惜哉！ 」舒稱疾如初。 後以災異遜位，帝不聽。 後因正旦朝罷還第，表送章綬。 帝手詔敦勉。 而舒執意彌固，乃下詔曰：「司徒、劇陽子舒，體道弘粹，思量經遠，忠肅居正，在公盡規。 入管銓衡，官人允敘； 出贊袞職，敷弘五教。 惠訓播流，德聲茂著，可謂朝之俊乂者也。 而屢執沖讓，辭旨懇誠，申覽反覆，省用憮然。 蓋成人之美，先典所與，難違至情。 今聽其所執，以劇陽子就第，位同三司，祿賜如前。 几杖不朝，賜錢百萬，床帳簟褥自副。 以舍人四人為劇陽子舍人，置官騎十人。 使光祿勳奉策，主者詳案典禮，令皆如舊制。 」於是賜安車駟馬，門施行馬。 舒為事必先行而後言，遜位之際，莫有知者。 時論以為晉興以來，三公能辭榮善終者，未之有也。 司空衛瓘與舒書曰：「每與足下共論此事，日日未果，可謂瞻之在前，忽焉在後矣。 」太熙元年薨，時年八十二。 帝甚傷悼，賵賻優厚，諡曰康。
When Shan Tao died, Shu took provisional charge of the Ministry of Education and soon received the full appointment. A man of formidable presence and reputation, he shared every stipend and gift with his extended kin and kept nothing in reserve. Zhou Zhen of Chenliu had been summoned to several bureaus, yet each time the commission arrived his new chief died; people called him the clerk who killed his superiors, and no one would hire him. Shu offered him a post anyway, and no calamity followed; observers said this showed his understanding of destiny. Growing old, he repeatedly cited illness to resign. He briefly returned to serve as provincial personnel evaluator for Yan, then pleaded illness again. Xi Shen, assistant director on the left of the Masters of Writing, wrote: "Your long illness has eased; resuming duty would be right—only the throne's wishes matter." "To rise only to go straight back to bed, bending every rule, betrays what the realm expects of you." "You built a towering name in your prime; to throw it away now would be a waste beyond words." Shu again pleaded illness as he had before. He later cited ill omens to step down; the emperor would not hear of it. After New Year's court he went home and returned his seals of office. The emperor answered with a personal edict urging him to remain. Shu would not budge, so the court proclaimed: "Minister of Education and Baron of Juyang, Wei Shu, embodies the Way in its breadth and purity, plans with a long view, serves with loyal gravity, and in office never fails to speak plain truth." "Within the capital he ran appointments and ranked officials fairly;" "beyond it he aided the highest ministers and spread the five teachings abroad." "His gentle counsel has gone out like water, and his virtue is renowned—he is truly one of the court's great pillars." "Yet he has pressed his modest retirements again and again in language so earnest that each reading leaves us troubled." "The classics bless us when we honor another man's chosen course; deep feeling is not lightly refused." "We therefore grant his wish: the Baron of Juyang may retire to private life with rank matching the Three Dukes and keep his former salary and perquisites." "He is excused from court even on staff-and-cushion occasions; we add a million cash, with bed hangings, mats, and bedding supplied in full." "Four household gentlemen will attend the baronial household, with ten official escort riders." "The Master of Ceremonies shall deliver the patent; bureaus are to check precedent so every detail follows standing ritual." He received a secure carriage, a four-horse team, and the crossbars before his gate that mark ministerial rank. Shu always acted before he spoke; when he resigned, no one had seen it coming. People said that since the Jin dynasty began, no Three Duke had ever surrendered honor and finished his days in peace—until Shu. Minister of Works Wei Guan wrote: "We spoke of this again and again, yet each day it remained undone—always seeming within reach, then slipping away." He died in the first year of the Taixi era, aged eighty-two. The emperor mourned him deeply, sent lavish funeral goods, and gave him the posthumous name Kang, "Peaceful."
6
子混，字延廣，清惠有才行，為太子舍人。 年二十七，先舒卒，朝野咸為舒悲惜。 舒每哀慟，退而歎曰：「吾不及莊生遠矣，豈以無益自損乎！ 」於是終服不復哭。 詔曰：「舒惟一子，薄命短折。 舒告老之年，處窮獨之苦，每念怛然，為之嗟悼。 思所以散愁養氣，可更增滋味品物。 仍給賜陽燧四望繐窗戶皁輪車牛一乘，庶出入觀望，或足散憂也。 」以庶孫融嗣。 又早卒，從孫晃嗣。
His son Hun, courtesy name Yanguang, was clear-minded, generous, and talented, and served as gentleman attendant on the heir apparent. He died at twenty-seven, before his father; court and capital alike grieved for the old man. Shu mourned fiercely, then withdrew and sighed, "I am nothing beside Master Zhuang—why should I injure myself for what cannot help?" After that he finished the mourning rites and did not weep again. An edict read: "Shu had only one son, and fate cut him off young." In the years when he seeks to step down he endures the bitterness of solitude without heir; the thought pains us, and we grieve for him. We would find ways to ease his sorrow and restore his strength—let richer foods and dainties be added to his table. Further grant him a carriage fitted with a bronze fire-mirror, four gauze windows, black wheels, and a yoke of oxen, so he may ride out, take in the view, and perhaps lighten his grief. His grandson Rong, born of a concubine's line, was named heir. Rong also died young, and a grand-nephew, Huang, inherited the title.
7
李憙，字季和，上黨銅鞮人也。 父牷，漢大鴻臚。 憙少有高行，博學研精，與北海管甯以賢良征，不行。 累辟三府，不就。 宣帝復辟憙為太傅屬，固辭疾，郡縣扶輿上道，時憙母疾篤，乃竊逾泫氏城而徒還，遂遭母喪，論者嘉其志節。 後為并州別駕，時驍騎將軍秦朗過并州，州將畢軌敬焉。 令乘車至閣。 憙固諫以為不可，軌不得已從之。
Li Xi, courtesy name Jihe, was a native of Tongdi in Shangdang commandery. His father, Li Quan, had been Grand Herald under the Han. In youth he carried himself with uncommon rectitude, read widely and to the point, and when he and Guan Ning of Beihai were summoned together as men of worth, he did not go. The Three Offices called him repeatedly; he declined each time. Emperor Xuan summoned him as an aide to the Grand Tutor; he pleaded illness and refused, but local officials forced him into a litter. His mother lay dying, so he slipped over the wall at Xuanshi and walked home; she died soon after, and observers praised his steadfast devotion. He later became chief clerk of Bing Province; when General of Agile Cavalry Qin Lang passed through, provincial commander Bi Gui treated him with marked respect. He had Xi brought by carriage right to the vestibule. Xi argued firmly against the honor; Bi Gui yielded only because he had to.
8
景帝輔政，命憙為大將軍從事中郎，憙到，引見，謂憙曰：「昔先公辟君而君不應，今孤命君而君至，何也？ 」對曰：「先君以禮見待，憙得以禮進退。 明公以法見繩，憙畏法而至。 」帝甚重之。 轉司馬，尋拜右長史。 從討毌丘儉還，遷御史中丞。 當官正色，不憚強禦，百僚震肅焉。 薦樂安孫璞，亦以道德顯，時人稱為知人。 尋遷大司馬，以公事免。
Regent Emperor Jing named him adviser on the general-in-chief's staff. At their first meeting he asked, "Your father once called you forth and you stayed away; I summon you and you appear—what changed?" Xi answered, "Your father received me with courtesy, so I could answer with courtesy—coming or staying away." You hold me to the law; I come because I fear it." The regent esteemed him all the more. He moved to the marshal's office and was soon named senior clerk on the right. After the campaign against Guanqiu Jian he was raised to palace assistant secretary. He held his post with unflinching dignity and showed no fear of the mighty; the whole bureaucracy stood in awe. He recommended Sun Pu of Le'an, who likewise won fame on moral grounds; contemporaries said he had an eye for talent. Soon he rose to Grand Marshal, only to be dismissed over an official matter.
9
司馬伷為甯北將軍，鎮鄴，以憙為軍司。 頃之，除涼州刺史，加揚威將軍、假節，領護羌校尉，綏禦華夷，甚有聲績。 羌虜犯塞，憙因其隙會，不及啟聞，輒以便宜出軍深入，遂大克獲，以功重免譴，時人比之漢朝馮、甘焉。 於是請還，許之。 居家月餘，拜冀州刺史，累遷司隸校尉。 及魏帝告禪于晉，憙以本官行司徒事，副太尉鄭沖奉策。 泰始初，封祁侯。
Sima Zhou, as General Who Guards the North at Ye, appointed him army director. Shortly after he became inspector of Liang, with the added rank of General Who Spreads Might, ceremonial axe, and concurrent Qiang command—he kept Hu and Han in order and earned wide praise. Qiang raiders broke the frontier; Xi acted on the opening without awaiting orders, drove deep, and routed them. Merit outweighed breach of protocol, so he went unpunished, and men likened him to Feng Fengshi and Gan Yanshou of Han. He asked to retire from the frontier, and the court agreed. A month at home, then appointment as inspector of Ji; step by step he rose to metropolitan commandant. When the Wei throne yielded to Jin, he discharged the minister of education's duties in his existing rank and, assisting Grand Commandant Zheng Chong, presented the abdication patent. Early in Taishi he received the marquisate of Qi.
10
憙上言：「故立進令劉友、前尚書山濤、中山王睦、故尚書僕射武陔各占官三更稻田，請免濤、睦等官。 陔已亡，請貶諡。 」詔曰：「法者，天下取正，不避親貴，然後行耳，吾豈將枉縱其間哉！ 然案此事皆是友所作，侵剝百姓，以繆惑朝士。 奸吏乃敢作此，其考竟友以懲邪佞。 濤等不貳其過者，皆勿有所問。 《易》稱'王臣蹇蹇，匪躬之故'。 今憙亢志在公，當官而行，可謂'邦之司直'者矣。 光武有云：'貴戚且斂手以避二鮑'。 豈其然乎！ 其申敕群僚，各慎所司，寬宥之恩，不可數遇也。 」憙為二代司隸，朝野稱之。 以公事免。
Xi reported that Liu You, Shan Tao, Prince Mu of Zhongshan, and the late Wu Gai had each seized three qing of government paddy; he asked that Shan Tao and the prince be stripped of their posts. Wu Gai being dead, he asked that his posthumous name be lowered. The edict read: "Law is the measure of the empire; it must spare neither kin nor grandees—or it is nothing. I will not twist it for favor." Yet the inquiry showed Liu You alone had done it—preying on commoners and deceiving the court with lies. Let that corrupt clerk be tried to the full sentence, to chastise treachery and flattery. Shan Tao and others who did not share his guilt are not to be prosecuted. The Changes says the king's ministers brave hardship—not for private ends. Xi sets the common good above himself and does the duty before him—he is the 'straight staff of the state.' Guangwu said the great families should stand aside for men like the two Baos. Is that not exactly our case? Charge every official to mind his brief—pardons must not become routine. Xi held the metropolitan inspectorship under two houses, and all the realm approved. He stepped down over an official matter.
11
其年，皇太子立，以憙為太子太傅。 自魏明帝以後，久曠東宮，制度廢闕，官司不具，詹事、左右率、庶子、中舍人諸官並未置，唯置衛率令典兵，二傅並攝眾事。 憙在位累年，訓道盡規。 遷尚書僕射，拜特進、光祿大夫，以年老遜位。 詔曰：「光祿大夫、特進李憙，杖德居義，當升台司。 毗亮朕躬，而以年尊致仕。 雖優遊無為，可以頤神，而虛心之望，能不憮然！ 其因光祿之號，改假金紫，置官騎十人，賜錢五十萬，祿賜班禮，一如三司，門施行馬。」
The same year the crown prince was named, Xi became his grand tutor. Since Wei Mingdi the heir's residence had stood empty: regulations lapsed, posts went unfilled—no steward, no guard captains, no household staff—only a guard command held the troops while both tutors managed everything else. Xi held the office for years, teaching the prince and speaking plainly whenever duty required. He rose to vice director of the Masters of Writing, took the titles Specially Advanced and Grand Master of Splendid Happiness, then retired on grounds of age. An edict proclaimed: "Grand Master Li Xi, Specially Advanced, has leaned on virtue and kept to the right—he should have joined the highest council." "He has steadied Our rule, yet age has led him to retire." "Leisure may restore him, but Our wish to keep him near leaves Us heavy-hearted." "Retain his splendid-happiness title, add the gold seal and purple ribbon, assign ten escort riders, grant half a million cash, and match the Three Dukes in salary, gifts, and ceremony, with crossbars before his gate."
12
初，憙為僕射時，涼州虜寇邊，憙唱義遣軍討之。 朝士謂出兵不易，虜未足為患，竟不從之。 後虜果大縱逸，涼州覆沒，朝廷深悔焉。 以憙清素貧儉，賜絹百匹。 及齊王攸出鎮，憙上疏諫爭，辭甚懇切。 憙自曆仕，雖清非異眾，而家無儲積，親舊故人乃至分衣共食，未嘗私以王官。 及卒，追贈太保，諡曰成。 子贊嗣。
While Xi served as vice director, tribes struck Liangzhou; he was the first to urge a punitive expedition. Courtiers called war too costly and the raiders a minor nuisance; they ignored him. The tribes then swept the frontier, Liangzhou collapsed, and the court bitterly regretted its refusal. Seeing his austerity and want, the throne sent him a hundred bolts of silk. When Prince You of Qi was sent to hold a frontier post, Xi memorialized in blunt, heartfelt protest. Across a long career he stayed as honest as any man yet kept no surplus; with friends and family he shared coat and bowl, and never traded on a royal appointment. At his death he was posthumously named grand guardian with the epithet Cheng, "Accomplished." His son Zan inherited the title.
13
少子儉，字仲約，曆左積弩將軍、屯騎校尉。 儉子弘，字世彥，少有清節，永嘉末，曆給事黃門侍郎、散騎常侍。
A younger son, Jian, courtesy name Zhongyue, served as general of the left stacked crossbows and colonel of garrison cavalry. Jian's son Hong, courtesy name Shiyan, showed integrity early; late in Yongjia he held posts as palace attendant and cavalier attendant-in-ordinary.
14
劉寔，字子真，平原高唐人也。 漢濟北惠王壽之後也，父廣，斥丘令。 寔少貧苦，賣牛衣以自給。 然好學，手約繩，口誦書，博通古今。 清身潔己，行無瑕玷。 郡察孝廉，州舉秀才，皆不行。 以計吏入洛，調為河南尹丞，遷尚書郎、廷尉正。 後曆吏部郎，參文帝相國軍事，封循陽子。
Liu Shi, courtesy name Zizhen, came from Gaotang in Pingyuan commandery. He descended from Prince Hui of Jibei under the Han; his father Guang had been magistrate of Chiqiu. He grew up in deep poverty, patching his living by selling straw cattle-mats. Still he studied obsessively, twisting rope as he walked and reciting texts until he commanded the whole tradition, past and present. He kept himself spotless; not a stain touched his name. The commandery nominated him Filial and Incorrupt, the province as Flourishing Talent; he accepted neither summons. He reached Luoyang as a fiscal clerk, became aide to the Henan governor, then rose to gentleman of the Masters of Writing and rectifier at the commandant of justice. He went on to the Ministry of Personnel, served on Sima Zhao's minister-of-state staff, and was enfeoffed as Baron of Xunyang.

鐘會、鄧艾之伐蜀也，有客問寔曰：「二將其平蜀乎？ 」寔曰：「破蜀必矣，而皆不還。 」客問其故，笑而不答，竟如其言。 寔之先見，皆此類也。
During Zhong Hui and Deng Ai's invasion of Shu, a visitor asked whether the two generals would conquer the west. Shi said Shu would fall, but neither commander would come back. Asked why, he smiled and held his tongue—and events proved him right. His foresight was always of that order.
16
以世多進趣，廉遜道闕，乃著《崇讓論》以矯之。 其辭曰：
Seeing a world mad for promotion and shy of modesty, he wrote his "Treatise on Venerating Yielding" to set it right. It begins:
17
古之聖王之化天下，所以貴讓者，欲以出賢才，息爭競也。 夫人情莫不欲已之賢也，故勸令讓賢以自明賢也，豈假讓不賢哉！ 故讓道興，賢能之人不求而自出矣，至公之舉自立矣，百官之副亦豫具矣。 一官缺，擇眾官所讓最多者而用之，審之道也。 在朝之士相讓於上，草廬之人咸皆化之，推賢讓能之風從此生矣。 為一國所讓，則一國士也； 天下所共推，則天下士也。 推讓之風行，則賢與不肖灼然殊矣。 此道之行，在上者無所用其心，因成清議，隨之而已。 故曰，蕩蕩乎堯之為君，莫之能名。 言天下自安矣，不見堯所以化之，故不能名也。 又曰，舜禹之有天下而不與焉，無為而化者其舜也歟。 賢人相讓于朝，大才之人恆在大官，小人不爭於野，天下無事矣。 以賢才化無事，至道興矣。 已仰其成，復何與焉！ 故可以歌《南風》之詩，彈五弦之琴也。 成此功者非有他，崇讓之所致耳。 孔子曰，能以禮讓為國，則不難也。
The ancient sage kings prized yielding because it drew talent forth and stilled strife. Every heart wants to seem worthy, so the wise urged men to yield place to the better—proving their own worth, not pretending virtue they lacked. When yielding is honored, talent steps forward unbidden, the fairest choices take care of themselves, and understudies for every post stand ready. Fill a vacancy by promoting the man others most often stepped aside for—that is sound appointment. Courtiers defer to one another before the throne, rustics take the cue, and a culture of recommending the able begins. The man a whole kingdom yields to is that kingdom's true gentleman; the man the whole realm recommends is a gentleman of the realm. Once yielding spreads, worthy men and fools are plain to tell apart. Under that dispensation rulers need not scheme: clear public opinion forms, and they have only to follow it. Hence the saying: Yao ruled so effortlessly that no one could describe how he did it. The empire rested easy; people never saw his method, so they could not name it. Again: Shun and Yu held the realm as if it were not theirs; Shun, perhaps, ruled by doing nothing yet changing everything. Worthies defer in the hall, great talent fills high posts, small men do not squabble in the fields, and the realm is calm. When worthies shape a world that needs no meddling, the supreme Way prevails. The ruler has only to trust the outcome—what is left to do? Then he may sing the "South Wind" and strum the five-string lute in peace. None of this comes from anything but honoring yielding. Confucius said ruling a state through courteous yielding should not be hard.
18
在朝之人不務相讓久矣，天下化之。 自魏代以來，登進辟命之士，及在職之吏，臨見受敘，雖自辭不能，終莫肯讓有勝己者。 夫推讓之風息，爭競之心生。 孔子曰，上興讓則下不爭，明讓不興下必爭也。 推讓之道興，則賢能之人日見推舉； 爭競之心生，則賢能之人日見謗毀。 夫爭者之欲自先，甚惡能者之先，不能無毀也。 故孔墨不能免世之謗己，況不及孔墨者乎！ 議者僉然言，世少高名之才，朝廷不有大才之人可以為大官者。 山澤人小官吏亦復雲，朝廷之士雖有大官名德，皆不及往時人也。 餘以為此二言皆失之矣。 非時獨乏賢也，時不貴讓。 一人有先眾之譽，毀必隨之，名不得成使之然也。 雖令稷契復存，亦不復能全其名矣。 能否混雜，優劣不分，士無素定之價，官職有缺，主選之吏不知所用，但案官次而舉之。 同才之人先用者，非勢家之子，則必為有勢者之所念也。 非能獨賢，因其先用之資，而復遷之無已。 遷之無已，不勝其任之病發矣。 觀在官之人，政績無聞，自非勢家之子，率多因資次而進也。
Courtiers have ceased deferring to one another, and the whole realm has taken the habit. Since Wei, appointees and serving clerks—however they may protest incompetence at audience—refuse to step aside for anyone better. When yielding dies, rivalry is born. Confucius said when superiors honor yielding, inferiors do not fight—so if yielding is not fostered at the top, strife follows below. Where yielding thrives, the able are lifted up each day; where rivalry rules, the able are torn down every day. Schemers must be first and hate anyone ahead of them; slander follows. Confucius and Mozi could not escape slander—what hope for lesser men? Everyone laments a shortage of famous talent and claims no one at court is fit for the highest posts. Even lowly clerks say today's grandees, however titled, cannot match the old stock. Both verdicts, I believe, are wrong. The problem is not missing worthies but a climate that scorns yielding. Outshine the pack and attack dogs follow; reputations never mature—that is why. Even Ji and Xie reborn could not keep their names whole. Able and useless blur together, no fixed scale judges men, and when a post opens selectors fall back on seniority because they cannot tell talent from rot. Among equals, the first picked is always a magnate's son or a favorite of magnates. Not because he alone excels—early promotion becomes a ladder without end. Endless promotion soon exposes men who cannot bear the load. Look at serving officials—few distinguished records; unless born to power, most rose by stacking years.
19
向令天下貴讓，士必由於見讓而後名成，名成而官乃得用之。 諸名行不立之人，在官無政績之稱，讓之者必矣，官無因得而用之也。 所以見用不息者，由讓道廢，因資用人之有失久矣。 故自漢魏以來，時開大舉，令眾官各舉所知，唯才所任，不限階次，如此者甚數矣。 其所舉必有當者，不聞時有擢用，不知何誰最賢故也。 所舉必有不當，而罪不加，不知何誰最不肖也。 所以不可得知，由當時之人莫肯相推，賢愚之名不別，令其如此。 舉者知在上者察不能審，故敢漫舉而進之。 或舉所賢，因及所念，一頓而至，人數猥多，各言所舉者賢，加之高狀，相似如一，難得而分矣。 參錯相亂，真偽同貫，更復由此而甚。 雖舉者不能盡忠之罪，亦由上開聽察之路濫，令其爾也。 昔齊王好聽竽聲，必令三百人合吹而後聽之，廩以數人之俸。 南郭先生不知吹竽者也，以三百人合吹可以容其不知，因請為王吹竽，虛食數人之俸。 嗣王覺而改之，難彰先王之過。 乃下令曰：「吾之好聞竽聲有甚于先王，欲一一列而聽之。 」先生于此逃矣。 推賢之風不立，濫舉之法不改，則南郭先生之徒盈於朝矣。 才高守道之士日退，馳走有勢之門日多矣。 雖國有典刑，弗能禁矣。
If yielding were prized, a man would earn his name only after others stepped aside for him, and only then would office follow. Men without standing or record would find others yielding their turn, and government would have no cause to use them. They keep their posts because yielding died out and seniority-based appointment has gone uncorrected for ages. Since Han and Wei, repeated edicts told every official to nominate talent without regard to rank—again and again. Some nominees must be fit, yet none are heard of being raised—no one can tell who is best. Some must be unfit, yet no one is punished—no one can pick out the worst. The reason is simple: no one defers, worthy and fool look the same, so judgment collapses. Nominators know superiors cannot vet closely, so they toss names forward at random. Some push favorites and hangers-on in a single flood; every file swears its man is brilliant, the praise reads the same, and no one can sort them. Truth and humbug tangle until the mess grows worse. Recommenders share the blame, but the throne invited the abuse by listening too wide. The king of Qi insisted on three hundred yu players at once and paid each as several common musicians. Mr. Nanguo could not play, but the massed band hid him; he drew several musicians' pay for nothing. The next king saw the fraud but hesitated to shame his father. He decreed, "I love the yu more than my father—bring the players forward one at a time." Nanguo ran. Without real recommendation and reform, the court fills with Nanguos. True talent retreats while careerists crowd the mighty. Statutes alone cannot stop it.
20
夫讓道不興之弊，非徒賢人在下位，不得時進也，國之良臣荷重任者，亦將以漸受罪退矣。 何以知其然也？ 孔子以為顏氏之子不貳過耳，明非聖人皆有過。 寵貴之地欲之者多矣，惡賢能者塞其路，其過而毀之者亦多矣。 夫謗毀之生，非徒空設，必因人之微過而甚之者也。 譭謗之言數聞，在上者雖欲弗納，不能不杖所聞，因事之來而微察之也，無以，其驗至矣。 得其驗，安得不理其罪。 若知而縱之，王之威日衰，令之不行自此始矣。 知而皆理之，受罪退者稍多，大臣有不自固之心。 夫賢才不進，貴臣日疏，此有國者之深憂也。 《詩》曰：「受祿不讓，至於已斯亡。 」不讓之人憂亡不暇，而望其益國朝，不亦難乎！
When yielding fails, not only do the worthy stall below—even trusted ministers fall, step by step, to disgrace. How do we know? Confucius said only Yan Hui never repeated a mistake—so everyone short of a sage errs. Everyone covets high place; rivals block the able and pounce on every slip. Slander never comes from nowhere—it magnifies a speck into a stain. Rumors pile up; rulers may wish to ignore them yet must act on what they hear, probing each case until the evidence lands. Proof in hand, punishment follows. To know and wink at it drains royal authority and orders cease to run. Punish every case and dismissed ministers multiply; the great officers lose their footing. Talent blocked and pillars estranged—any ruler's nightmare. The Songs warn: grasp pay without yielding and ruin follows. Men who never yield busy themselves with survival—can they serve the dynasty?
21
竊以為改此俗甚易耳。 何以知之？ 夫一時在官之人，雖雜有凡猥之才，其中賢明者亦多矣，豈可謂皆不知讓賢為貴邪！ 直以其時皆不讓，習以成俗，故遂不為耳。 人臣初除，皆通表上聞，名之謝章，所由來尚矣。 原謝章之本意，欲進賢能以謝國恩也。 昔舜以禹為司空，禹拜稽首，讓於稷契及咎繇。 使益為虞官，讓於硃虎、熊、羆。 使伯夷典三禮，讓于夔龍。 唐虞之時，眾官初除，莫不皆讓也。 謝章之義，蓋取於此。 《書》記之者，欲以永世作則。 季世所用，不賢不能讓賢，虛謝見用之恩而已。 相承不變，習俗之失也。
I believe this habit could be broken with ease. Why? Officialdom always mixes mediocrities with many bright men—they know yielding matters. Habit alone stops them—no one yields, so no one yields. New appointees always submit a memorial of thanks—an old rite. The point was to recommend better men while thanking the throne. Shun named Yu minister of works; Yu kowtowed and ceded to Ji, Xie, and Gao Yao. Yi was set over forests and marshes and yielded to Zhu Hu, Xiong, and Pi. Boyi took the three rites and deferred to Kui and Long. Under Yao and Shun every new officer yielded. The thanks memorial comes from that spirit. The Classic records it as a pattern for all time. Later ages hollowed it out—unworthy men cannot yield, so they mouth thanks and mean nothing. Custom froze, and the rite rotted.
22
夫敘用之官得通章表者，其讓賢推能乃通，其不能有所讓徒費簡紙者，皆絕不通。 人臣初除，各思推賢能而讓之矣，讓之文付主者掌之。 三司有缺，擇三司所讓最多者而用之。 此為一公缺，三公已豫選之矣。 且主選之吏，不必任公而選三公，不如令三公自共選一公為詳也。 四征缺，擇四征所讓最多而用之，此為一征缺，四征已豫選之矣，必詳於停缺而令主者選四征也。 尚書缺，擇尚書所讓最多者而用之，此為八尚書共選一尚書，詳于臨缺令主者選八尚書也。 郡守缺，擇眾郡所讓最多者而用之，詳于任主者令選百郡守也。
Let every appointing officer who may memorialize do so only to recommend a better man; empty thanks waste paper and go nowhere. At each new posting, men should nominate superiors and defer; those writings go to the registrar. Fill a Three Duke vacancy from the man the three most often stepped aside for. One seat opens, but the three have already picked his successor. Selectors need not be dukes themselves; better the Three Dukes jointly pick the third—that is sounder. One expeditionary post empty? The four generals have already ranked their choices—far better than leaving the job open while a clerk picks among them. For a Masters of Writing seat, let the eight ministers jointly elevate the colleague most deferred to—better than a hurried search when the chair is empty. Governors should be the man the commanderies most often yielded to—not a central clerk paging through a hundred names.
23
夫以眾官百郡之讓，與主者共相比，不可同歲而論也。 雖復令三府參舉官，本不委以舉選之任，各不能以根其心也。 其所用心者裁之不二三，但令主者案官次而舉之，不用精也。 賢愚皆讓，百姓耳目盡為國耳目。 夫人情爭則欲毀己所不知，讓則競推於勝己。 故世爭則毀譽交錯，優劣不分，難得而讓也。 時讓則賢智顯出，能否之美歷歷相次，不可得而亂也。 當此時也，能退身修已者，讓之者多矣。 雖欲守貧賤，不可得也。 馳騖進趣而欲人見讓，猶卻行而求前也。 夫如此，愚智咸知進身求通，非修之於己則無由矣。 游外求者，於此相隨而歸矣。 浮聲虛論，不禁而自息矣。 人人無所用其心，任眾人之議，而天下自化矣。 不言之化行，巍巍之美於此著矣。 讓可以致此，豈可不務之哉！
Collective yielding from every office and commandery dwarfs what one appointing clerk can know. Even when the three bureaus "help" nominate, they lack real selection power and never invest their hearts in it. They skim two or three names and tell the clerk to follow seniority—no real thought. When everyone defers, the people become the ruler's eyes and ears. Rivalry breeds slander of the unknown; yielding lifts the better man. In a contentious age praise and blame tangle and no one yields. When yielding rules, talent shines and competence sorts itself unmistakably. Then men who step back and refine themselves win the most deferrals. They cannot stay poor and obscure if they try. Scrambling for promotion while expecting deference is walking backward and calling it forward. Then fool and sage alike see that advancement rides on self-cultivation, not intrigue. Adventurers hunting outside favor come home to inward work. Hollow fame dies without a ban. Rulers need not scheme—trust public judgment and the realm rights itself. Silent transformation unfolds and true majesty appears. Yielding achieves this—how can we neglect it?
24
《春秋傳》曰：「范宣子之讓，其下皆讓。 欒黶雖汰，弗敢違也。 晉國以平，數世賴之。 」上世之化也，君子尚能而讓其下，小人力農以事其上，上下有禮，讒慝遠黜，由不爭也。 及其亂也，國家之弊，恆必由之。 篤論了了如此。 在朝君子典選大官，能不以人廢言，舉而行之，各以讓賢舉能為先務，則群才猥出，能否殊別，蓋世之功，莫大於此。
The Zuo says when Fan Xuanzi stepped aside, his subordinates did the same. Even arrogant Luan Yan dared not refuse. Jin grew calm and prospered for generations on that habit. That was high antiquity: the great deferred to subordinates, farmers served their betters, ritual ran top to bottom, and slander fled—because no one fought rank. When order broke, state's ills always traced to losing that. The moral is plain. If high selectors heed this essay and put yielding first, talent will flood forth, competence will sort itself, and no policy could do more for an age.
25
泰始初，進爵為伯，累遷少府。 咸甯中為太常。 轉尚書。 杜預之伐吳也，寔以本官行鎮南軍司。
Early in Taishi he was raised to earl and rose through the ministry of the lesser treasury. Under Xianning he served as chamberlain for ceremonials. He moved to the Masters of Writing. During Du Yu's conquest of Wu he served as army director on the southern command staff in his existing rank.
26
初，寔妻盧氏生子躋而卒，華氏將以女妻之。 寔弟智諫曰：「華家類貪，必破門戶。 」辭之不得，竟婚華氏而生子夏。 寔竟坐夏受賂，免官。 頃之為大司農，又以夏罪免。
His first wife, Lady Lu, bore a son, Ji, then died; the Huas offered him a daughter. His brother Zhi warned, "The Huas are a grasping clan—they will break our house." He could not refuse; he wed the Hua woman and she bore Xia. Xia's bribery cost Shi his post. Soon he was minister of agriculture, then lost that office too over Xia's crimes.
27
寔每還州裏，鄉人載酒肉以候之。 寔難逆其意，輒共啖而返其餘。 或謂寔曰：「君行高一世，而諸子不能遵。 何不旦夕切磋，使知過而自改邪！ 」寔曰：「吾之所行，是所聞見，不相祖習，豈復教誨之所得乎！ 」世以寔言為當。
Each time he came home, neighbors met him with wine and meat. Unwilling to refuse their kindness, he shared the meal and sent the leftovers back. Someone told him, "Your virtue outshines the age, yet your sons do not follow it." Why not coach them morning and night until they mend their ways?" Shi answered, "What I do I learned by sight and sound, not by rote from elders—lectures will not graft that onto my sons." People judged him right.
28
後起為國子祭酒、散騎常侍。 湣懷太子初封廣陵王，高選師友，以寔為師。 元康初，進爵為侯，累遷太子太保，加侍中、特進、右光祿大夫、開府儀同三司，領冀州都督。 九年，策拜司空，遷太保，轉太傅。 太安初，寔以老病遜位，賜安車駟馬、錢百萬，以侯就第。 及長沙成都之相攻也，寔為軍人所掠，潛歸鄉里。
He was later recalled as libationer of the national university and cavalier attendant-in-ordinary. When Crown Prince Minhuai was first prince of Guangling, the court chose eminent tutors; Shi became his teacher. Early in Yuankang he became a marquis, rose to junior tutor to the heir apparent, and accumulated palace attendant, Specially Advanced, grand master of the right, opener of office equal to the Three Dukes, and area command over Ji. In the ninth year he received the minister of works patent, then grand tutor, then grand preceptor. Early in Tai'an age and illness drove him to retire; the throne sent a carriage, four horses, a million cash, and let him close out his days as marquis at home. When the princes of Changsha and Chengdu turned on each other, soldiers robbed him; he slipped home to the country.
29
三年，詔曰：「昔虞任五臣，致垂拱之化，漢相蕭何，興寧一之譽，故能光隆於當時，垂裕於百代。 朕紹天明命，臨御萬邦，所以崇顯政道者，亦賴之於元臣庶尹，畢力股肱，以副至望。 而君年耆告老，確然難違。 今聽君以侯就第，位居三司之上，秩祿准舊，賜几杖不朝及宅一區。 國之大政，將就諮於君，副朕意焉。 」歲余薨，時年九十一，諡曰元。
A third-year edict read: "Shun's five ministers brought effortless rule; Xiao He won Han fame for unity—so they glorified their times and left a legacy for ages." We hold Heaven's mandate over the realm; good government rests on pillar ministers giving their all as Our arms and legs." Yet you are old and ask to retire—your resolve brooks no refusal. We grant your wish to close out life as marquis at home, rank above the Three Dukes, salary unchanged, with staff-and-cushion privilege, exemption from court, and a house. On weighty affairs We will still seek your counsel—such is Our mind." A year later he died at ninety-one with the posthumous name Yuan, "Primordial."
30
寔少貧窶，杖策徒行，每所憩止，不累主人，薪水之事，皆自營給。 及位望通顯，每崇儉素，不尚華麗。 嘗詣石崇家，如廁，見有絳紋帳，裀褥甚麗，兩婢持香囊。 寔便退，笑謂崇曰：「誤入卿內。 」崇曰：「是廁耳。 」寔曰：「貧士未嘗得此。 」乃更如他廁。 雖處榮寵，居無第宅，所得俸祿，贍恤親故。 雖禮教陵遲，而行己以正。 喪妻為廬杖之制，終喪不禦內。 輕薄者笑之，寔不以介意。 自少及老，篤學不倦，雖居職務，卷弗離手。 尤精《三傳》，辨正《公羊》，以為衛輒不應辭以王父命，祭仲失為臣之節，舉此二端以明臣子之體，遂行於世。 又撰《春秋條例》二十卷。
Youth left him threadbare; he walked with a stick, imposed on no host for fuel or water, and saw to his own needs. Even when powerful and famous he clung to plain living and scorned display. Visiting Shi Chong, he stepped into a lavatory hung with crimson silk, rich cushions, and two maids bearing perfume bags. He stepped back and laughed, "I have wandered into your private rooms by mistake." Chong said, "That is only the toilet." Shi replied, "A poor scholar never sees the like." He used another lavatory instead. Despite favor at court he owned no grand house and sent every salary to kin and friends. While public morals slid, he kept his own conduct straight. For his wife's death he built the mourning hut and carried the staff, and until mourning ended he did not visit the women's quarters. Wits mocked him; he ignored them. From boyhood to age he never tired of books; even in office he read constantly. He mastered the Three Commentaries and challenged the Gongyang: Duke Zhu of Wei was wrong to cite his grandfather's command, and Jizhong of Zheng broke a minister's duty. Those two cases became his standard for subject and son, and his reading spread. He also wrote twenty chapters of "Spring and Autumn Regulations."
31
有二子，躋、夏。 躋字景雲，官至散騎常侍。 夏以貪污棄放於世。
He had two sons: Ji and Xia. Ji, courtesy name Jingyun, reached the post of cavalier attendant-in-ordinary. Xia was ruined by greed and cast out of respectable society.
32
弟智，字子房，貞素有兄風。 少貧窶，每負薪自給，讀誦不輟，竟以儒行稱。 曆中書黃門吏部郎，出為潁川太守。 平原管輅嘗謂人曰：「吾與劉潁川兄弟語，使人神思清發，昏不假寐。 自此之外，殆白日欲寢矣。 」入為秘書監，領南陽王師，加散騎常侍，遷侍中、尚書、太常。 著《喪服釋疑論》，多所辨明。 太康末卒，諡曰成。
His brother Zhi, courtesy name Zifang, was as upright and austere as his elder brother. Poor in youth, he hauled firewood to live yet never stopped studying, and won fame as a Confucian gentleman. He served in the secretariat, at yellow gates, and in the Ministry of Personnel, then became governor of Yingchuan. Guan Lu of Pingyuan said, "Talking with the Liu brothers of Yingchuan clears the mind; you need no nap at dusk." With anyone else I could nap in broad daylight." He was recalled as director of the palace library and tutor to the prince of Nanyang, added as cavalier attendant-in-ordinary, then rose to palace attendant, Masters of Writing, and chamberlain for ceremonials. His "Discourse Resolving Doubts on Mourning Dress" settled many disputed points. He died late in Taikang with the posthumous name Cheng.
33
高光，字宣茂，陳留圉城人，魏太尉柔之子也。 光少習家業，明練刑理。 初以太子舍人累遷尚書郎，出為幽州刺史、潁州太守。 是時武帝置黃沙獄，以典詔囚。 以光曆世明法，用為黃沙御史，秩與中丞同，遷廷尉。 元康中，拜尚書，典三公曹。 時趙王倫篡逆，光於其際，守道全貞。 及倫賜死，齊王冏輔政，復以光為廷尉，遷尚書，加奉車都尉。 後從駕討成都王穎有勳，封延陵縣公，邑千八百戶。 于時朝廷咸推光明于用法，故頻典理官。 惠帝為張方所逼，幸長安，朝臣奔散，莫有從者，光獨侍帝而西。 遷尚書左僕射，加散騎常侍。 光兄誕為上官巳等所用，曆徐、雍二州刺史。 誕性任放無倫次，而決烈過人，與光異操。 常謂光小節，恆輕侮之，光事誕愈謹。 帝既還洛陽，時太弟新立，重選傅訓，以光為少傅，加光祿大夫，常侍如故。 及懷帝即位，加光祿大夫金章紫綬，與傅祗並見推崇。 尋為尚書令，本官如故。 以疾卒，贈司空、侍中。 屬京洛傾覆，竟未加諡。
Gao Guang, courtesy name Xuanmao, came from Yucheng in Chenliu; he was the son of Wei grand commandant Gao Rou. He trained in his father's legal tradition and mastered penal law. From gentleman attendant on the heir apparent he rose to gentleman of the Masters of Writing, then served as inspector of You and governor of Ying commandery. Emperor Wu then founded the Yellow Sand prison for edict cases. His family's legal expertise won him the Yellow Sand inspectorship, rank equal to the assistant secretary, then the commandant of justice. Under Yuankang he joined the Masters of Writing in charge of the Three Dukes section. When Sima Lun seized the throne, Guang held to principle and kept his honor clean. After Lun's forced suicide and under Prince Jiong's regency he returned as commandant of justice, then Masters of Writing, with the title commandant of the imperial coach. He campaigned against Prince Ying of Chengdu with distinction and received the duchy of Yanling, eighteen hundred households. The court deemed him a master of the law, so he kept judicial posts. Forced west to Chang'an by Zhang Fang, Emperor Hui found every courtier had fled—only Guang stayed at his side. He became left vice director of the Masters of Writing and cavalier attendant-in-ordinary. His brother Dan served Shangguan Si's faction and held the inspectorships of Xu and Yong. Dan was wild and disorderly yet bold beyond most—a temper nothing like Guang's. He sneered at Guang as fussy and slighted him; Guang only served him more dutifully. Back in Luoyang, with a new heir presumptive, the court re-picked tutors: Guang became junior tutor, grand master of splendid happiness, and kept his attendant post. Emperor Huai added the gold seal and purple ribbon to his splendid-happiness title; he and Fu Zhi stood equally honored. Soon he was director of the Masters of Writing while keeping his other titles. He died of illness; the court posthumously named him minister of works and palace attendant. The fall of Luoyang left him without a posthumous name.
34
子韜字子遠，放佚無檢。 光為廷尉時，韜受貨賕，有司奏案之，而光不知。 時人雖非光不能防閑其子，以其用心有素，不以為累。 初，光詣長安留台，以韜兼右衛將軍。 韜與殿省小人交通，及光卒，仍於喪中往來不絕。 時東海王越輔政，不朝觀。 韜知人心有望，密與太傅參軍薑賾、京兆杜概等謀討越，事泄伏誅。
His son Tao, courtesy name Ziyuan, was reckless and ungovernable. While Guang headed justice, Tao took bribes; the bureaus impeached him, yet Guang knew nothing. Critics faulted him for not controlling Tao, but his lifelong integrity spared him real blame. When Guang joined the Chang'an exiled court he gave Tao a concurrent post as general of the right guard. Tao consorted with palace underlings and, even during mourning for his father, kept up those visits. Prince Yue of Donghai held power and skipped court. Reading popular resentment, Tao secretly joined Jiang Ze, Du Gai, and others to move against Yue; the plot leaked and he died on the block.
35
史臣曰：下士競而文，中庸靜而質，不若進不足而退有餘也。 魏舒、劉寔發慮精華，結綬登槐，覽止成務。 季和切問近對，當官正色。 詩云「貪人敗類」，豈劉夏之謂歟！
The historians write: petty men chase polish; the balanced path is quiet and plain—better to fall short going forward than to lack room when stepping back. Wei Shu and Liu Shi distilled deep judgment, took high office, and knew when to stop—finishing what they began. Li Xi answered sharp questions plainly and held office with unbending dignity. The Songs call the grasping man the ruin of his kind—was that not Liu Xia?
36
贊曰：舒言不矜，憙對千乘。 子真、宣茂，雅志難陵。 進忠能舉，退讓攸興。 皎皎瑚器，來光玉繩。
Encomium: Wei Shu spoke without swagger; Li Xi faced a prince regnant with plain truth. Liu Shi and Gao Guang—purposes too high to be trampled. They advanced loyalty and lifted the able; in yielding office they set the tone. Like polished ritual coral they shone; like stars on the Jade Cord they lit the age.
