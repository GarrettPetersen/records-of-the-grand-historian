王渾，字玄沖，太原晉陽人也。 父昶，魏司空。 渾沈雅有器量。 襲父爵京陵侯，辟大將軍曹爽掾。 爽誅，隨例免。 起為懷令，參文帝安東軍事，累遷散騎黃門侍郎、散騎常侍。 咸熙中為越騎校尉。 武帝受禪，加揚烈將軍，遷徐州刺史。 時年荒歲饑，渾開倉振贍，百姓賴之。 泰始初，增封邑千八百戶。 久之，遷東中郎將，監淮北諸軍事，鎮許昌。 數陳損益，多見納用。
Wang Hun, courtesy name Xuanzhong, was a native of Jinyang in Taiyuan. His father, Wang Chang, had served Wei as Minister of Works. Wang Hun was grave, cultivated, and of generous measure. He succeeded to his father's title as Marquis of Jingling and joined the staff of General-in-chief Cao Shuang. When Cao Shuang was executed, Wang Hun lost his post like others swept up in the purge. Recalled as magistrate of Huai, he served on the staff for Prince Wen's eastern command, then rose to gentleman attendant at the Yellow Gate and regular attendant-in-ordinary. During the Xianxi years he was appointed colonel of the élite Yue cavalry. When Emperor Wu took the throne by abdication, Wang Hun was named General Who Spreads Might and inspector of Xuzhou. Famine gripped the region; he opened government granaries for relief, and the people depended on him to survive. Early in Taishi his appanage was enlarged by eighteen hundred households. He was later promoted general of the eastern palace guard, put in charge of forces north of the Huai, and stationed at Xuchang. He often submitted practical recommendations, and the court adopted most of them.
3
轉征虜將軍、監豫州諸軍事、假節，領豫州刺史。 渾與吳接境，宣佈威信，前後降附甚多。 吳將薛瑩、魯淑眾號十萬，淑向弋陽，瑩向新息。 時州兵並放休息，眾裁一旅，浮淮潛濟，出其不意，瑩等不虞晉師之至。 渾擊破之，以功封次子尚為關內侯。 遷安東將軍、都督揚州諸軍事，鎮壽春。 吳人大佃皖城，圖為邊害。 渾遣揚州刺史應綽督淮南諸軍攻破之，並破諸別屯，焚其積穀百八十餘萬斛、稻苗四千餘頃、船六百餘艘。 渾遂陳兵東疆，視其地形險易，曆觀敵城，察攻取之勢。
He became general who conquers foes, military overseer of Yuzhou with ceremonial baton, while serving as Yuzhou's regional inspector. His territory lay against Wu; he projected authority and good faith, and enemy soldiers defected to him in large numbers over time. Wu commanders Xue Ying and Lu Shu led a host said to number one hundred thousand: Lu Shu drove on Yiyang, Xue Ying on Xinxi. With provincial troops stood down and barely one brigade at hand, he crossed the Huai in secret and caught the enemy unprepared; Xue Ying had not expected Jin forces to appear. Wang Hun shattered their army and, for this merit, had his second son Wang Shang enfeoffed as marquis within the passes. He was made general who pacifies the east and area commander for Yangzhou, with headquarters at Shouchun. Wu had developed a major military farm at Wancheng, intending to harass the frontier. Wang Hun sent Yangzhou's regional inspector Ying Chuo at the head of the Huainan armies to overrun the position, destroy satellite camps, and burn over 1.8 million hu of grain, more than four thousand qing of paddy, and upward of six hundred boats. He then deployed along the eastern border, studied the ground, toured enemy towns, and assessed how an offensive might unfold.
4
及大舉伐吳，渾率師出橫江，遣參軍陳慎、都尉張喬攻尋陽瀨鄉，又擊吳牙門將孔忠，皆破之，獲吳將周興等五人。 又遣殄吳護軍李純據高望城，討吳將俞恭，破之，多所斬獲。 吳曆武將軍陳代、平虜將軍硃明懼而來降。 吳丞相張悌、大將軍孫震等率眾數萬指城陽，渾遣司馬孫疇、揚州刺史周浚擊破之，臨陣斬二將，及首虜七千八百級，吳人大震。
In the full-scale invasion of Wu, Wang Hun advanced from Hengjiang, sent staff officer Chen Shen and commandant Zhang Qiao against Laixiang in Xunyang, and also defeated Wu gate general Kong Zhong, taking five captives including commander Zhou Xing. He also ordered protector Li Chun of the Wu-extermination force to seize Gaowang, where he routed Wu general Yu Gong with heavy casualties and prisoners. Wu commanders Chen Dai and Zhu Ming, unnerved by the Jin advance, crossed over and surrendered. Chancellor Zhang Ti and great general Sun Zhen of Wu marched tens of thousands toward Chengyang; Wang Hun sent major Sun Chou and inspector Zhou Jun to crush them, killing both commanders on the field and claiming 7,800 counted heads and prisoners, which stunned Wu.

孫皓司徒何植、建威將軍孫晏送印節詣渾降。 既而王濬破石頭，降孫皓，威名益振。 明日，渾始濟江，登建鄴宮，釃酒高會。 自以先據江上，破皓中軍，案甲不進，致在王濬之後。 意甚愧恨，有不平之色，頻奏濬罪狀，時人譏之。 帝下詔曰：「使持節、都督揚州諸軍事、安東將軍、京陵侯王渾，督率所統，遂逼秣陵，令賊孫皓救死自衛，不得分兵上赴，以成西軍之功，又摧大敵，獲張悌，使皓途窮勢盡，面縛乞降。 遂平定秣陵，功勳茂著。 其增封八千戶，進爵為公，封子澄為亭侯、弟湛為關內侯，賜絹八千匹。 」轉征東大將軍，復鎮壽陽。 渾不尚刑名，處斷明允。 時吳人新附，頗懷畏懼。 渾撫循羈旅，虛懷綏納，座無空席，門不停賓。 於是江東之士莫不悅附。
Sun Hao's minister He Zhi and general Sun Yan brought the seals of office to Wang Hun and capitulated. Soon Wang Jun took Shitou and forced Sun Hao to yield, and his renown eclipsed others'. Only the next day did Wang Hun cross the Yangzi, enter the Jianye palace, and hold a lavish victory banquet. He believed he had held the river line first and broken Sun Hao's main force, yet by holding back he let Wang Jun finish the conquest ahead of him. Bitter and humiliated, he showed his pique and filed repeated charges against Wang Jun, for which contemporaries ridiculed him. The emperor proclaimed: Bearer of the baton and area commander for Yangzhou, General Who Pacifies the East, Marquis of Jingling Wang Hun pinned Moling so tightly that Sun Hao could only defend himself and could not shift troops upstream—thus clearing the way for the western column's success—while also shattering a major enemy force, capturing Zhang Ti, and driving Sun Hao to rope himself and offer surrender. In this way Moling was secured; his achievement stood out in full measure. His fief rose by eight thousand households, his title advanced to duke, his son Wang Cheng made village marquis and his brother Wang Zhan marquis within the passes, with a gift of eight thousand bolts of silk. He was reassigned general who conquers the east and returned to headquarters at Shouyang. Wang Hun was no legalist pedant; his judgments were lucid and even-handed. The newly submitted people of Wu were still anxious. He put displaced gentry at ease, received them with open courtesy, kept every seat filled, and turned no caller from his door. Jiangdong notables flocked to him willingly.
6
徵拜尚書左僕射，加散騎常侍。 會朝臣立議齊王攸當之籓，渾上書諫曰：「伏承聖詔，憲章古典，進齊王攸為上公，崇其禮儀，遣攸之國。 昔周氏建國，大封諸姬，以籓帝室，永世作憲。 至於公旦，武王之弟，左右王事，輔濟大業，不使歸籓。 明至親義著，不可遠朝故也。 是故周公得以聖德光弼幼主，忠誠著于《金縢》，光述文武仁聖之德。 攸于大晉，姬旦之親也。 宜贊皇朝，與聞政事，實為陛下腹心不貳之臣。 且攸為人，修潔義信，加以懿親，志存忠貞。 今陛下出攸之國，假以都督虛號，而無典戎幹方之實，去離天朝，不預王政。 傷母弟至親之體，虧友于款篤之義，懼非陛下追述先帝、文明太后待攸之宿意也。 若以攸望重，於事宜出者，今以汝南王亮代攸。 亮，宣皇帝子，文皇帝弟，伷、駿各處方任，有內外之資，論以後慮，亦不為輕。 攸今之國，適足長異同之論，以損仁慈之美耳。 而令天下窺陛下有不崇親親之情，臣竊為陛下不取也。 若以妃後外親，任以朝政，則有王氏傾漢之權，呂產專朝之禍。 若以同姓至親，則有吳楚七國逆亂之殃。 曆觀古今，苟事輕重，所在無不為害也。 不可事事曲設疑防，慮方來之患者也。 唯當任正道而求忠良。 若以智計猜物，雖親見疑，至於疏遠者亦何能自保乎！ 人懷危懼，非為安之理。 此最有國有家者之深忌也。 愚以為太子太保缺，宜留攸居之，與太尉汝南王亮、衛將軍楊珧共為保傅，幹理朝事。 三人齊位，足相持正，進有輔納廣義之益，退無偏重相傾之勢。 令陛下有篤親親之恩，使攸蒙仁覆之惠。 臣同國休戚，義在盡言，心之所見，不能默已。 私慕魯女存國之志，敢陳愚見，觸犯天威。 欲陛下事每盡善，冀萬分之助。 臣而不言，誰當言者。 」帝不納。
He was recalled as senior vice-president of the Secretariat and given the concurrent title of regular attendant. When ministers argued that Prince You of Qi must go to his princedom, Wang Hun memorialized in protest: I have heard Your Majesty's edict, framed on ancient precedent, raising Prince You of Qi to grand duke, honoring his rites, and sending him to his domain. When the Zhou founded their rule they invested many Ji kinsmen as bulwarks of the throne, a pattern meant to last for ages. The Duke of Zhou, brother to King Wu, stayed at court to govern and never was packed off to a distant fief. That shows how close kin whose virtue is plain must not be banished from the capital. Thus the Duke of Zhou could guide the boy king with sage virtue; his loyalty shines in the Metal-bound Coffer ode, carrying forward the humane renown of Kings Wen and Wu. For Great Jin, Prince You stands in the same relation as the Duke of Zhou did to the Zhou. He should help govern at court and hear state business; he is in truth an intimate minister on whom Your Majesty can rely without reserve. Besides, Prince You is scrupulous, upright, and trustworthy, and as imperial kin his loyalty is beyond question. To banish him to his fief with only a nominal area command—no real military authority—and cut him off from court and policy-making is another matter. It wounds the bond between mother and younger son and frays brotherly affection; I doubt it matches what the late emperor and Empress Dowager Wénmíng intended for Prince You. If senior standing truly requires someone to hold an external post, appoint Prince Liang of Runan in Prince You's stead. Prince Liang is a son of Emperor Xuan and brother to Emperor Wen; princes Zhou and Jun already hold key posts inside and out, so the dynasty would still be well covered. Sending Prince You away now would only fuel factional talk and tarnish Your Majesty's reputation for cherishing kin. It would suggest to the realm that you slight close family; I respectfully urge you not to do it. Entrusting the consorts' affines with power invites a Wang Mang-style takeover or a Lü-clique stranglehold on government. Relying solely on imperial clansmen risks another revolt like the seven kingdoms of Wu and Chu. History shows that every such expedient, whichever way the scales tilt, ends badly. You cannot hedge every policy against imaginary future threats. The right course is to uphold proper principle and choose loyal, capable men. If you govern by suspicion, even kin become suspects, and those farther out cannot feel safe either. A court of frightened men is no recipe for stability. That is the gravest fear of any ruler with a realm to keep. I suggest leaving the vacant post of heir-apparent grand guardian to Prince You, sharing guardianship of the heir with Grand Commandant Prince Liang of Runan and Guards General Yang Yao while they jointly administer court business. The three would balance one another—cooperating for the heir's good without letting any one faction dominate. You would show true affection for kin while Prince You enjoys your protecting favor. As I share the dynasty's fortunes, duty compels me to speak my mind; I cannot hold my tongue. Like the woman of Lu who spoke to save her state, I risk your displeasure by offering these blunt views. I wish every decision of yours to be sound and hope this may help in however small a measure. If I stay silent, who will tell you the truth? The emperor rejected his advice.

太熙初，遷司徒。 惠帝即位，加侍中，又京陵置士官，如睢陵比。 及誅楊駿，崇重舊臣，乃加渾兵。 渾以司徒文官，主史不持兵，持兵乃吏屬絳衣。 自以偶因時寵，權得持兵，非是舊典，皆令皁服。 論者美其謙而識體。
Early in Taixi he was appointed minister of education. Under Emperor Hui he gained the concurrent title of palace attendant, and a corps of household guards was authorized for the Jingling marquisate on the Suiling model. After Yang Jun's execution, as the court elevated senior officials, Wang Hun was granted a military guard. As minister of education—a civil post—his chief clerk had not carried arms; armed attendants had been the business of crimson-robed staff. He considered his armed escort a temporary favor, not precedent, and had the guards dress in black like ordinary household troops. Observers commended his humility and sense of propriety.

楚王瑋將害汝南王亮等也。 公孫宏說瑋曰：「昔宣帝廢曹爽，引太尉蔣濟參乘，以增威重。 大王今舉非常事，宜得宿望，鎮厭眾心。 司徒王渾宿有威名，為三軍所信服，可請同乘，使物情有憑也。 」瑋從之。 渾辭疾歸第，以家兵千餘人閉門距瑋。 瑋不敢逼。 俄而瑋以矯詔伏誅，渾乃率兵赴官。 帝嘗訪渾元會問郡國計吏方俗之宜，渾奏曰：「陛下欽明聖哲，光於遠近，明詔沖虛，詢及芻蕘，斯乃周文疇咨之求，仲尼不恥下問也。 舊三朝元會前計吏詣軒下，侍中讀詔，計吏跪受。 臣以詔文相承已久，無他新聲，非陛下留心方國之意也。 可令中書指宣明詔，問方土異同，賢才秀異，風俗好尚，農桑本務，刑獄得無冤濫，守長得無侵虐。 其勤心政化興利除害者，授以紙筆，盡意陳聞。 以明聖指垂心四遠，不復因循常辭。 且察其答對文義，以觀計吏人才之實。 又先帝時，正會後東堂見征鎮長史司馬、諸王國卿、諸州別駕。 今若不能別見，可前詣軒下，使侍中宣問，以審察方國，於事為便。 」帝然之。 又詔渾錄尚書事。
Prince Wei of Chu was plotting to kill Prince Liang of Runan and his allies. Gongsun Hong urged him: When Emperor Xuan eliminated Cao Shuang, he had Grand Commandant Jiang Ji ride in his carriage to lend the coup solemn weight. Your Highness is staging a coup; you need a figure of established prestige at your side to steady public opinion. Minister Wang Hun's name carries authority; the troops trust him—invite him into your carriage so the act looks legitimate. Prince Wei agreed. Wang Hun pleaded illness, went home, and barred his gates with over a thousand family retainers against the prince. Prince Wei did not dare force an entry. Soon Prince Wei was executed for forging an edict, and Wang Hun then marched his guard to resume his duties. Once at the New Year audience the emperor asked Wang Hun how to question the regional clerks about local conditions. Wang Hun replied: Your sagely brilliance reaches everywhere; your modest edict consults even the humblest—like King Wen seeking advice in the fields or Confucius unashamed to learn from inferiors. Formerly, before the triple audience at New Year, clerks stood below the gallery while an attendant read the formulaic rescript and each clerk knelt to receive it. That rote wording has been handed down for ages; it does not show Your Majesty's real interest in the provinces. Have the Secretariat publish a clear instruction: ask about local conditions, notable talent, popular customs, farming and sericulture, miscarriages of justice, and oppressive magistrates. Give paper and ink to those eager to report what helps or harms good government and let them write everything out. That would show the throne's concern for the four quarters instead of recycling empty phrases. You can also judge the clerks' quality from the sense of their written answers. Under the late emperor, after the main audience, clerks from commands and garrisons, princely ministers, and prefectural deputies were received in the eastern hall. If private audiences are impractical, bring them below the gallery beforehand and let an attendant put your questions—an efficient way to learn about the localities. The emperor approved. He also ordered Wang Hun to serve as recording secretary for the Secretariat.

渾所曆之職，前後著稱，及居台輔，聲望日減。 薨，時年七十五，諡曰元。 長子尚早亡，次子濟嗣。
Wang Hun had won praise in every post he held, but once he reached the highest councils his standing steadily declined. He died at seventy-five and received the posthumous epithet Yuan. His firstborn, Wang Shang, had died young; the succession passed to his second son, Wang Ji.

=濟字武子。 少有逸才，風姿英爽，氣蓋一時，好弓馬，勇力絕人，善《易》及《莊》、《老》，文詞俊茂，伎藝過人，有名當世，與姊夫和嶠及裴楷齊名。 尚常山公主。 年二十，起家拜中書郎，以母憂去官。 起為驍騎將軍，累遷侍中，與侍中孔恂、王恂、楊濟同列，為一時秀彥。 武帝嘗會公卿籓牧於式乾殿，顧濟、恂而謂諸公曰：「朕左右可謂恂恂濟濟矣！ 」每侍見，未嘗不諮論人物及萬機得失。 濟善於清言，修飾辭令，諷議將順，朝臣莫能尚焉。 帝益親貴之。 仕進雖速，論者不以主婿之故，咸謂才能致之。 然外雖弘雅，而內多忌刻，好以言傷物，儕類以此少之。 以其父之故，每排王濬，時議譏焉。
Wang Ji, courtesy name Wuzi. Even as a youth he displayed rare ability—carriage keen and commanding, a presence that outshone his contemporaries. He delighted in archery and riding and surpassed others in bold strength. He was versed in the *Changes*, the *Zhuangzi*, and the *Laozi*; his prose was brilliant, his accomplishments exceptional. He ranked among the celebrated men of the day alongside his brother-in-law He Qiao and Pei Kai. He took the Princess of Changshan as his wife. At twenty he entered service as a Gentleman of the Palace Secretariat, then resigned to observe mourning for his mother. Recalled as General of Agile Cavalry, he rose through several posts to Palace Attendant, serving alongside Kong Xun, Wang Xun, and Yang Ji as one of the day’s most distinguished officials. Emperor Wu once gathered high ministers and regional governors in Shiqian Hall, glanced at Wang Ji and Kong Xun, and told the company, “The men at my side are truly orderly and abounding in talent.” Whenever he attended the throne, he invariably joined in weighing character and the merits or faults of state affairs. Wang Ji excelled at *qingtan* discourse, shaped his speech with care, and framed advice so as to follow the emperor’s bent—none at court could match him in that art. The sovereign came to favor and esteem him all the more. Though his career rose swiftly, observers attributed his success to ability rather than merely to his status as imperial son-in-law. Outwardly magnanimous and cultivated, he was inwardly suspicious and cutting, prone to barbed remarks; contemporaries held him somewhat the less for it. Owing to his father’s quarrel with Wang Jun, he repeatedly worked against Jun, and opinion at court mocked him for it.

齊王攸當之籓，濟既陳請，又累使公主與甄德妻長廣公主俱入，稽顙泣請帝留攸。 帝怒謂侍中王戎曰：「兄弟至親，今出齊王，自是朕家事，而甄德、王濟連遣婦來生哭人！ 」以忤旨，左遷國子祭酒，常侍如故。 數年，入為侍中。 時渾為僕射，主者處事或不當，濟性峻厲，明法繩之。 素與從兄佑不平，佑黨頗謂濟不能顧其父，由是長同異之言。 出為河南尹，未拜，坐鞭王官吏免官。 而王佑始見委任。 而濟遂被斥外，於是乃移第北芒山下。
As Prince Sima You of Qi prepared to depart for his princedom, Wang Ji had already petitioned against it; he also had the princess join Zhen De’s wife, the Princess of Changuang, in entering the palace, prostrating themselves in tears to beg the emperor to keep You at court. The emperor angrily told Palace Attendant Wang Rong, “Brothers are my closest kin. Sending the Prince of Qi away is my own family matter—yet Zhen De and Wang Ji keep dispatching their wives to wail at me as if I were dead!” For this breach of imperial will he was demoted to Libationer of the Imperial Academy while retaining his standing as a regular attendant. Several years later he was recalled as Palace Attendant. Wang Hun then headed the Secretariat; when subordinates mishandled business, Wang Ji—by nature strict—insisted on clear application of statute. He had never been on good terms with his cousin Wang You; You’s partisans claimed Wang Ji failed in filial duty toward his father, and partisan whispers of division persisted. He was named Governor of Henan but, before taking up the post, was stripped of rank for having had a Wang-clan official flogged. Meanwhile Wang You began to win the court’s trust and real authority. Wang Ji, relegated to the margins, thereupon relocated his household to the northern foot of Mount Mang.

性豪侈，麗服玉食。 時洛京地甚貴，濟買地為馬埒，編錢滿之，時人謂為「金溝」。 王愷以帝舅奢豪，有牛名「八百里駁」，常瑩其蹄角。 濟請以錢千萬與牛對射而賭之。 愷亦自恃其能，令濟先射。 一發破的，因據胡床，叱左右速探牛心來，須臾而至，一割便去。 和嶠性至儉，家有好李，帝求之，不過數十。 濟候其上直，率少年詣園，共啖畢，伐樹而去。 帝嘗幸其宅，供饌甚豐，悉貯琉璃器中。 蒸肫甚美，帝問其故，答曰：「以人乳蒸之。 」帝色甚不平，食未畢而去。
He lived ostentatiously, draped in finery and dining with the costliest fare. Luoyang real estate being exorbitant, he purchased a tract for a riding ring and paved it with strung cash—contemporaries dubbed it the “golden ditch.” Wang Kai, the emperor’s uncle by marriage, rivaled others in lavish display; he owned a famous ox called “Eight-hundred-*li* Piebald” and kept its hooves and horns burnished. 濬自以功大，而為渾父子及豪強所抑，屢為有司所奏，每進見，陳其攻伐之勞，及見枉之狀，或不勝忿憤，徑出不辭。 帝每容恕之。 益州護軍範通，濬之外親也。 謂濬曰：「卿功則美矣，然恨所以居美者，未盡善也。 」濬曰：「何謂也？ 」通曰：「卿旋旆之日，角巾私第，口不言平吳之事。 若有問者，輒曰：'聖主之德，群帥之力，老夫何力之有焉！ '如斯，顏老之不伐，龔遂之雅對，將何以過之。 藺生所以屈廉頗，王渾能無愧乎！ 」濬曰：「吾始懼鄧艾之事，畏禍及，不得無言，亦不能遣諸胸中，是吾偏也。 」時人咸以濬功重報輕，博士秦秀、太子洗馬孟康、前溫令李密等並表訟濬之屈。 帝乃遷濬鎮軍大將軍，加散騎常侍，領後軍將軍。 王渾詣濬，濬嚴設備衛，然後見之，其相猜防如此。
Convinced his deeds outweighed his rewards, yet blocked by the Hun clan and local magnates and hauled before the ministry again and again, he would recount his campaigns and grievances at audience—or storm out speechless with rage. The sovereign each time overlooked it. Fan Tong, army protector in Yi, was a kinsman by marriage. He told Jun that his achievement was splendid but the way he wore that glory was not yet beyond reproach. Jun asked what he meant. Fan Tong said that on the day Jun turned his banners homeward he should don a scholar’s kerchief in his private residence and never speak of the conquest of Wu. If anyone asked, he should always answer that it was the sage ruler’s virtue and his commanders’ strength—what had a greybeard like him done? That would match Yan Hui’s refusal to boast or Gong Sui’s tactful replies—who could improve on it? It was thus that Lin Xiangru humbled Lian Po—could Wang Hun then be free of shame?” Jun said he had first feared the fate of Deng Ai, dreaded calamity reaching him, and could not but speak; neither could he drive the feeling from his breast—that was his bias.” People of the day still thought Jun’s merit heavy and his reward slight; Erudite Qin Xiu, Household Assistant to the Heir Apparent Meng Kang, former magistrate of Wen Li Mi, and others jointly memorialized to redress his wrongs. The emperor then promoted him to General Who Garrisons the Army, added cavalier attendant at leisure, and left him General of the Rear Army as well. When Wang Hun called, Jun surrounded himself with arms before receiving him—such was their mutual distrust.
31
濬平吳之後，以勳高位重，不復素業自居，乃玉食錦服，縱奢侈以自逸。 其有辟引，多是蜀人，示不遺故舊也。 後又轉濬撫軍大將軍、開府儀同三司，加特進，散騎常侍、後軍將軍如故。 卒，時年八十，諡曰武。 葬柏谷山，大營塋域，葬垣周四十五里，面別開一門，松柏茂盛。 子矩嗣。
After Wu fell, Jun—now powerful and proud—abandoned frugal habits for fine fare, silk robes, and open luxury. His appointments favored Shu men to prove he had not forgotten old ties. He was later moved to General Who Sooths the Army with independent command matching the Three Dukes, honored as Exalted Marcher, and kept his previous attendant and rear-army posts. He died at eighty with the posthumous name “Martial.” His tomb on Baigu Mountain sprawled over a forty-five-*li* park with its own gate and thick pine and cypress groves. His son Ju succeeded to the title.
32
矩弟暢，散騎郎。 暢子粹，，武帝詔粹尚潁川公主，仕至魏郡太守。
Ju’s brother Chang served as Gentleman Cavalier Attendant. Chang’s son Cui was ordered by Emperor Wu to marry the Princess of Yingchuan and rose to prefect of Wei commandery.
33
濬有二孫，過江不見齒錄。 安西將軍恆溫鎮江陵，表言之曰：「臣聞崇德賞功，為政之所先； 興滅繼絕，百王之所務。 故德參時雍，則奕世承祀； 功烈一代，則永錫祚胤。 案故撫軍王濬曆職內外，任兼文武，料敵制勝，明勇獨斷，義存社稷之利，不顧專輒之罪。 荷戈長鶩，席捲萬里，僭號之吳，面縛象魏，今皇澤被于九州，玄風洽于區外，襄陽之封，廢而莫續； 恩寵之號，墜於近嗣。 遐邇酸懷，臣竊悼之。 濬今有二孫，年出六十，室如懸磬，糊口江濱，四節蒸嘗，菜羹不給。 昔漢高定業，求樂毅之嗣； 世祖旌賢，建葛亮之胤。 夫效忠異代，立功異國，尚通天下之善，使不泯棄，況濬建元勳于當年，著喜慶於身後，靈基托根于南垂，皇祚中興于江左，舊物克彰，神器重耀，豈不由伊人之功力也哉！ 誠宜加恩，少垂矜憫，追錄舊勳，纂錫茅土。 則聖朝之恩，宣暢于上，忠臣之志，不墜於地矣。 」卒不見省。
Jun left two grandsons who, once south of the Yangtze, were struck from the noble registers. Huan Wen, General Who Pacifies the West, garrisoned Jiangling and submitted a memorial saying that to honor virtue and reward merit was the first concern of government, and that to restore ruined lines and continue severed sacrifices was the business of every sage king. When virtue matched an age of harmony, offerings passed down generation after generation; when merit blazed for one reign, blessings extended forever to posterity. The late General Who Sooths the Army Wang Jun had served at court and in the field, joining civil and martial charge; he sized up the enemy, seized victory, and acted with clear courage and independent resolve, seeking the altars’ good and not counting the offense of acting on his own authority. Arms in hand he rolled up ten thousand *li*, and the Wu that had usurped the title came bound to the imperial gate. Now royal grace bathes the nine provinces and the transforming wind reaches beyond the borders, yet the Xiangyang enfeoffment stands abolished without renewal; marks of favor and honor have slipped from the nearest heirs. Near and far harbor bitter grief; your subject mourns it in private. Jun’s two grandsons are past sixty, their homes bare as a hung chime-stone; they scrape a living along the river and cannot supply even vegetable gruel for the seasonal offerings. Han Gaozu, founding the enterprise, sought out the descendants of Yue Yi; Emperor Guangwu raised the worthy and ennobled Zhuge Liang’s line. Loyalty shown under another sovereign and merit won in another realm still links to the good of all under Heaven and keeps their memory from extinction—how much more Jun, who wrought the founding merit of that age and left blessing on his person after death? His spiritual roots rest on the southern march; the house’s mandate renews east of the Yangtze; old glories reappear and the sacred regalia shines again—was that not this man’s labor and strength? It is truly right to show added grace, extend a measure of pity, record his old merit, and renew a grant of fief and land. Then the sage court’s kindness would shine abroad above, and the will of loyal ministers would not fall forgotten to the earth. In the end the memorial was not heeded.

唐彬
Tang Bin
35
唐彬，字儒宗，魯國鄒人也。 父台，太山太守。 彬有經國大度，而不拘行檢。 少便弓馬，好遊獵，身長八尺，走及奔鹿，強力兼人。 晚乃敦悅經史，尤明《易經》，隨師受業，還家教授，恆數百人。 初為郡門下掾，轉主簿。 刺史王沈集諸參佐，盛論距吳之策，以問九郡吏。 彬與譙郡主張惲俱陳吳有可兼之勢，沈善其對。 又使彬難言吳未可伐者，而辭理皆屈。 還遷功曹，舉孝廉，州辟主簿，累遷別駕。
Tang Bin, *zi* Ruzong, came from Zou in Lu. His father, Tang Tai, served as grand administrator of Taishan. Tang Bin had the breadth of mind to govern the realm but cared little for conventional propriety. As a youth he excelled at riding and archery and loved the chase; he stood eight feet tall, could outrun a bolting stag, and possessed strength beyond ordinary men. Later he turned seriously to the classics and histories, with particular mastery of the *Zhou yi*; he studied under a tutor, then taught at home to an audience routinely numbering in the hundreds. He began as a junior clerk in the commandery administration and was promoted to chief registrar. Inspector Wang Shen convened his staff for a wide-ranging discussion of how to oppose Wu and polled the recorders of the nine commanderies. Tang Bin and Zhang Yun, registrar of Qiao, both argued that Wu could be overrun; Wang Shen commended their answers. He then had Tang Bin refute those who claimed Wu was not ripe for attack, and each opponent’s argument collapsed. He was moved up to merit evaluator, nominated filial and incorrupt, appointed provincial registrar, and eventually promoted to governor’s chief of staff.
36
彬忠肅公亮，盡規匡救，不顯諫以自彰，又奉使詣相府計事，于時僚佐皆當世英彥，見彬莫不欽悅，稱之于文帝，薦為掾屬。 帝以問其參軍孔顥，顥忌其能，良久不答。 陳騫在坐，斂板而稱曰：「彬之為人，勝騫甚遠。 」帝笑曰：「但能如卿，固未易得，何論於勝。 」因辟彬為鎧曹屬。 帝問曰：「卿何以致辟？ 」對曰：「修業陋巷，觀古人之遺跡，言滿天下無口過，行滿天下無怨惡。 」帝顧四坐曰：「名不虛行。 」他日，謂孔顥曰：「近見唐彬，卿受蔽賢之責矣。」
Tang Bin was loyal, grave, and upright; he remonstrated tirelessly to set things right without parading his counsel for fame. Sent to the chancellor’s headquarters on business, he impressed every eminent man on staff, who spoke of him to Emperor Wen and secured him a post among the clerks. The emperor turned to his adviser Kong Hao, who resented Tang Bin’s talent and remained silent. Chen Qian, who was present, straightened his tablet and said, “Tang Bin is far my superior as a man. ” The emperor smiled and replied, “Merely to match you would be rare enough; we need not speak of surpassing. ” He thereupon appointed Tang Bin to the armory bureau. The emperor asked, “What earned you this appointment?” ” He answered, “Studying in obscurity and pondering the example of the ancients: one may speak before the world without indiscretion and act before the world without earning ill will. ” The emperor glanced at those seated and said, “His reputation is no empty boast. ” On another occasion he told Kong Hao, “I have lately met Tang Bin; you stand charged with hiding talent from the throne.”
37
初，鄧艾之誅也，文帝以艾久在隴右，素得士心，一旦夷滅，恐邊情搔動，使彬密察之。 彬還，白帝曰：「鄧艾忌克詭狹，矜能負才，順從者謂為見事，直言者謂之觸迕。 雖長史司馬，參佐牙門，答對失指，輒見罵辱。 處身無禮，大失人心。 又好施行事役，數勞眾力。 隴右甚患苦之，喜聞其禍，不肯為用。 今諸軍已至，足以鎮壓內外，願無以為慮。」
When Deng Ai was put to death, Emperor Wen feared that the Longxi frontier, where Deng had long served and enjoyed the troops’ devotion, might grow restless if he were suddenly destroyed, and secretly dispatched Tang Bin to assess the mood. Tang Bin reported, “Deng Ai was suspicious, harsh, and narrow; he prized his own abilities—flatterers he deemed astute, frank men he treated as insubordinate. Even chief clerk, major, and camp staff faced abuse if their replies displeased him. He treated others without courtesy and forfeited the army’s goodwill. He also habitually imposed corvée and overworked the people. Longxi had groaned under his rule; they welcomed his fall and would not rally to him again. The imperial forces now on the scene are ample to hold the region; Your Majesty need not worry.”
38
俄除尚書水部郎。 泰始初，賜爵關內侯。 出補鄴令，彬道德齊禮，期月化成。 遷弋陽太守，明設禁防，百姓安之。 以母喪去官。 益州東接吳寇，監軍位缺，朝議用武陵太守楊宗及彬。 武帝以問散騎常侍文立，立曰：「宗、彬俱不可失。 然彬多財欲，而宗好酒，惟陛下裁之。 」帝曰：「財欲可足，酒者難改。 」遂用彬。 尋又詔彬監巴東諸軍事，加廣武將軍。 上征吳之策，甚合帝意。
Shortly afterward he was named director of the Water Section in the Secretariat. In the first year of the Taishi era he received the title of secondary marquis. Posted as magistrate of Ye, he led by moral example and ritual, and within a year the district was transformed. Promoted to grand administrator of Yiyang, he enforced clear laws and defenses, and the people lived at ease. He resigned to observe mourning for his mother. Yizhou bordered Wu on the east; with the army supervisor’s post empty, the court debated appointing either Yang Zong, grand administrator of Wuling, or Tang Bin. Emperor Wu consulted Wen Li, Cavalier Attendant-in-ordinary, who replied, “You cannot afford to lose either Yang Zong or Tang Bin. Yet Tang Bin is acquisitive, while Yang Zong drinks heavily—the choice is yours.” ” The emperor said, “Greed can be curbed; a drunkard is harder to reform.” ” He chose Tang Bin. Soon an edict named him supervisor of military affairs in Badong with the additional title General Who Extends Martial Might. His strategy for the Wu campaign matched the emperor’s wishes.
39
後與王濬共伐吳，彬屯據沖要，為眾軍前驅。 每設疑兵，應機制勝，陷西陵、樂鄉，多所擒獲。 自巴陵、沔口以東，諸賊所聚，莫不震懼，倒戈肉袒。 彬知賊寇已殄，孫皓將降，未至建鄴二百里，稱疾遲留，以示不競。 果有先到者爭物，後到者爭功，于時有識莫不高彬此舉。 吳平，詔曰：「廣武將軍唐彬受任方隅，東禦吳寇，南監蠻越，撫寧疆埸，有綏禦之績。 又每慷慨，志在立功。 頃者征討，扶疾奉命，首啟戎行，獻俘授馘，勳效顯著。 其以彬為右將軍、都督巴東諸軍事。 」徵拜翊軍校尉，改封上庸縣侯，食邑六千戶，賜絹六千匹。 朝有疑議，每參預焉。
Later he campaigned against Wu alongside Wang Jun, holding the key approaches and leading the van. He repeatedly used feints, seized opportunities, and took Xiling and Lexiang with heavy captures. East of Baling and the Han estuary every Wu stronghold quailed; defenders threw down their arms and came out bare-chested to surrender. Seeing Wu’s forces broken and Sun Hao ready to yield, he halted two hundred *li* short of Jianye, pleading illness, to avoid the scramble for glory. As it happened, the first arrivals squabbled over booty and the latecomers over credit; informed observers all praised Tang Bin’s restraint. After Wu fell, an edict declared, “General Who Extends Martial Might Tang Bin held a border command: he faced Wu in the east, watched the southern tribes, and kept the marches tranquil—true pacification and control. He was ardent by nature and bent on achievement. In the recent campaign he took the field despite illness, opened the advance, delivered prisoners and trophies, and his service stood out. He is appointed General of the Right and area commander for all Badong forces. ” He was recalled as Colonel of the Wings of the Army, enfeoffed as marquis of Shangyong with six thousand households and a gift of six thousand bolts of silk. Whenever court debate stalled on a point, he was called in.
40
北虜侵掠北平，以彬為使持節、監幽州諸軍事、領護烏丸校尉、右將軍。 彬既至鎮，訓卒利兵，廣農重稼，震威耀武，宣喻國命，示以恩信。 於是鮮卑二部大莫廆、擿何等並遣侍子入貢。 兼修學校，誨誘無倦，仁惠廣被。 遂開拓舊境，卻地千里。 復秦長城塞，自溫城洎于碣石，綿亙山谷且三千里，分軍屯守，烽堠相望。 由是邊境獲安，無犬吠之警，自漢魏征鎮莫之比焉。 鮮卑諸種畏懼，遂殺大莫廆。 彬欲討之，恐列上俟報，虜必逃散，乃發幽冀車牛。 參軍許祗密奏之。 詔遣御史檻車征彬付廷尉，以事直見釋。 百姓追慕彬功德，生為立碑作頌。
Northern tribes raided Beiping; Tang Bin received the credentials, took charge of Youzhou forces, held the concurrent post of Colonel Protector of the Wuhuan, and kept his rank as General of the Right. At his post he trained soldiers, honed weapons, promoted farming, staged demonstrations of force, published imperial policy, and combined awe with good faith. Two Xianbei confederations led by Damowei and Tihe then sent hostages and tribute. He restored schools, taught tirelessly, and his kindness reached widely. He recovered old ground and pushed the frontier back a thousand *li*. He rebuilt the Qin line of wall from Wencheng to Jieshi, nearly three thousand *li* through hill country, with garrisons and beacons linked along its length. The border knew such calm that dogs seldom barked at alarm—no frontier command since Han and Wei had matched it. The Xianbei, cowed by him, turned on Damowei and slew him. Tang Bin meant to strike them but knew a memorial and wait for approval would let the tribes scatter, so he requisitioned transport oxen from You and Ji without prior clearance. His adviser Xu Zhi reported this secretly to the throne. The court sent a censor to haul him to the capital in a prison cart, but the commandant of justice cleared him as acting within right. The people, grateful for his rule, erected a monument and composed a eulogy while he still lived.
41
彬初受學于東海閻德，門徒甚多，獨目彬有廊廟才。 及彬官成，而德已卒，乃為之立碑。
Tang Bin had studied under Yan De of Donghai amid a large school; alone Yan marked him as material for high office. By the time Tang Bin’s career flourished, Yan De was dead; Tang therefore raised a stele in his honor.
42
元康初，拜使持節、前將軍、領西戎校尉、雍州刺史。 下教曰：「此州名都，士人林藪。 處士皇甫申叔、嚴舒龍、姜茂時、梁子遠等，並志節清妙，履行高潔。 踐境望風，虛心饑渴，思加延致，待以不臣之典。 幅巾相見，論道而已，豈以吏職，屈染高規。 郡國備禮發遣，以副於邑之望。 」於是四人皆到，彬敬而待之。 卒官，時年六十，諡曰襄，賜絹二百匹，錢二十萬。 長子嗣，官至廣陵太守。 少子岐，征虜司馬。
Early in Yuankang he became credential bearer, General of the Van, concurrent Colonel of the Western Rong, and inspector of Yongzhou. He proclaimed, “Yongzhou is a renowned seat; its scholars gather like trees in a grove.” The recluses Huangfu Shenshen, Yan Shulong, Jiang Maoshi, and Liang Ziyuan combine sterling principle with spotless lives.” I have long admired them and wish to invite them with the etiquette reserved for guests, not subjects.” We shall meet informally and speak of principle; I would not demean their high standard with petty bureaucratic demands.” Let each district prepare proper escort and send them forward, as the capital expects.” ” All four came; Tang Bin received them with respect. He died in post at sixty, posthumously titled Xiang, with two hundred bolts of silk and two hundred thousand cash granted for his funeral. His eldest son succeeded him and rose to grand administrator of Guangling. His younger son Tang Qi became a major on the staff of the General Who Conquers the Barbarians.

史評
Shiping
44
史臣曰：孫氏負江山之阻隔，恃牛鬥之妖氛，奄有水鄉，抗衡上國。 二王屬當戎旅，受律遄征，渾既獻捷橫江，濬亦克清建鄴。 于時討吳之役，將帥雖多，定吳之功，此焉為最。 向使弘范父之不伐，慕陽夏之推功，上稟廟堂，下憑將士。 豈非茂勳茂德，善始善終者歟！ 此而不存，彼焉是務。 或矜功負氣，或恃勢驕陵，競構南箕，成茲貝錦。 遂乃喧黷宸扆，斁亂彝倫，既為戒于功臣，亦致譏於清論，豈不惜哉！ 王濟遂驕父之褊心，乖爭子之明義，俊材雖多，亦奚以為也。 唐彬畏避交爭，屬疾遲留，退讓之風，賢於渾濬遠矣。 傳云「不拘行檢」，安得長者之行哉！
The historians remark: The Sun house leaned on the Yangtze’s barrier and the dire omens over the Wu stars, seized the lake country, and defied the central court. The two Wangs took the field under orders: Wang Hun won at Hengjiang, Wang Jun swept Jianye. Among the many commanders in the Wu expedition, theirs was the decisive share in finishing the war. Had they honored Fan Wenzi’s habit of disclaiming merit and the Yangxia gentry’s willingness to yield credit, informing the high ministers above and trusting the commanders and troops below, would they not have paired brilliant achievement with lasting virtue and matched a worthy end to a worthy beginning? Instead they abandoned that path and chased lesser quarrels. Some swaggered over their feats, others threw their weight about; together they wove slanders like the “Southern Dipper” ode and the “shell-patterned brocade” calumny. They fouled the throne with wrangling, broke orderly relations, warned future generals against pride, and earned the censure of every fair judge—a pity indeed. Wang Ji aped his father’s pettiness and abandoned the decency a son owes—talent avails little without character. Tang Bin shunned the quarrel, lingered on pretext of illness, and showed a humility that puts Wang Hun and Wang Jun to shame. Yet the text calls him “unconcerned with propriety”—that is hardly the mark of a true gentleman.
45
贊曰：二王總戎，淮海攸同。 渾既害善，濬亦矜功。 武子豪桀，夙參朝列。 逞欲牛心，紆情馬埒。 儒宗知退，避名全節。
The encomium runs: “Two Wangs held the hosts; Huai and sea bowed as one.” Hun injured the worthy; Jun preened over his deeds. Wang Ji, a swaggering blade, long sat in court. He chased rare dishes and poured passion into the polo bank. Tang Bin, the gentleman who knew retreat, shunned fame and kept his honor whole.
