1
王羲之
Wang Xizhi.
2
王羲之，字逸少，司徒導之從子也。 祖正，尚書郎。 父曠，淮南太守。 元帝之過江也，曠首創其議。 羲之幼訥於言，人未之奇。 年十三，嘗謁周顗，顗察而異之。 時重牛心炙，坐客未啖，顗先割啖羲之，於是始知名。 及長，辯贍，以骨鯁稱，尤善隸書，為古今之冠，論者稱其筆勢，以為飄若浮雲，矯若驚龍。 深為從伯敦、導所器重。 時陳留阮裕有重名，為敦主簿。 敦嘗謂羲之曰：「汝是吾家佳子弟，當不減阮主簿。 」裕亦目羲之與王承、王悅為王氏三少。 時太尉郗鑒使門生求女婿于導，導令就東廂遍觀子弟。 門生歸，謂鑒曰：「王氏諸少並佳，然聞信至，咸自矜持。 惟一人在東床坦腹食，獨若不聞。 」鑒曰：「正此佳婿邪！ 」訪之，乃羲之也，遂以女妻之。
Wang Xizhi, whose courtesy name was Yishao, was a nephew of Wang Dao, the Minister of Education. His grandfather Wang Zheng had served as a Gentleman of the Secretariat. His father Wang Kuang was the Administrator of Huainan commandery. When Emperor Yuan crossed to the south, Wang Kuang had been the first to advance the plan. As a boy Wang Xizhi was tongue-tied, and no one thought him extraordinary. At thirteen he paid a call on Zhou Yi, who sized him up and saw something uncommon in him. Ox-heart roast was then considered a great delicacy; before the other guests had tasted it, Zhou Yi carved a portion for Wang Xizhi—through that gesture the youth first won a name. As an adult he was fluent and well spoken, praised for moral backbone, and above all a master of clerical script—the finest of his age. Connoisseurs described his brushwork as drifting like clouds and startling as a twisting dragon. His uncles Wang Dun and Wang Dao held him in the highest regard. Ruan Yu of Chenliu enjoyed enormous prestige at the time and served as Wang Dun's chief clerk. Wang Dun once told him, "You are one of the best youths in our clan; you should prove no less capable than Clerk Ruan." Ruan Yu likewise grouped Wang Xizhi with Wang Cheng and Wang Yue as the three rising stars of the Wang family. When Grand Commandant Xi Jian dispatched a dependent to choose a son-in-law among Wang Dao's nephews, Wang Dao told the man to tour the east wing and look over every candidate. The messenger reported back to Xi Jian, "The Wang boys are all impressive, but the moment they heard you had sent someone, each put on a stiff, careful air." Only one stayed on the eastern couch, shirt open, eating as if he had not heard a thing. Xi Jian said, "That is exactly the son-in-law I want." They identified him as Wang Xizhi, and Xi Jian married his daughter to him.
3
起家秘書郎，征西將軍庾亮請為參軍，累遷長史。 亮臨薨，上疏稱羲之清貴有鑒裁。 遷甯遠將軍、江州刺史。 羲之既少有美譽，朝廷公卿皆愛其才器，頻召為侍中、吏部尚書，皆不就。 復授護軍將軍，又推遷不拜。 揚州刺史殷浩素雅重之，勸使應命，乃遺羲之書曰：「悠悠者以足下出處足觀政之隆替，如吾等亦謂為然。 至如足下出處，正與隆替對，豈可以一世之存亡，必從足下從容之適？ 幸徐求眾心。 卿不時起，復可以求美政不？ 若豁然開懷，當知萬物之情也。 」羲之遂報書曰：「吾素自無廊廟志，直王丞相時果欲內吾，誓不許之，手跡猶存，由來尚矣，不於足下參政而方進退。 自兒娶女嫁，便懷尚子平之志，數與親知言之，非一日也。 若蒙驅使，關隴、巴蜀皆所不辭。 吾雖無專對之能，直謹守時命，宣國家威德，固當不同於凡使，必令遠近咸知朝廷留心於無外，此所益殊不同居護軍也。 漢末使太傅馬日磾慰撫關東，若不以吾輕微，無所為疑，宜及初冬以行，吾惟恭以待命。」
He entered service as a palace secretary, joined General Who Guards the West Yu Liang's staff as an adviser, and rose step by step to chief clerk. On his deathbed Yu Liang forwarded a memorial commending Wang Xizhi's integrity, fastidious taste, and sound judgment. He was advanced to General Who Pacifies the Distance and governor of Jiang Province. Wang Xizhi had enjoyed an excellent reputation from his youth, and high officials at court prized his gifts; they summoned him repeatedly for posts such as palace attendant and minister of personnel, but he refused every offer. When the generalship of the Guards was offered him once more, he again demurred and never took up the commission. Yin Hao, the Yangzhou inspector, had always respected him and pressed him to accept appointment; he wrote Wang Xizhi a letter saying, "Casual observers claim that whether you take office mirrors the health of the state—and we think they are right. Your choice to serve or withdraw stands opposite the dynasty's fortune; surely the fate of an age cannot turn solely on what suits your ease. You should proceed patiently and win the people's hearts. If you refuse to step forward now, how will good government ever be achieved? If you set aside reserve and engage wholeheartedly, you will grasp what moves the world. " Wang Xizhi answered: "I have never coveted ministerial office. When Chancellor Wang tried to pull me into court I refused on oath; the note still survives, and that resolve is old—it is not because you entered government that I am only now debating whether to serve. Ever since my children married off I have nursed Shang Ziping's dream of retiring; I have told friends and family again and again, and not just lately. If the court sends me abroad, I would not shrink from duty even to the Guanlong frontier or the Bashu basin. I may lack polished embassy manners, yet if I simply obey orders and proclaim imperial dignity, I would still surpass the usual envoy and show every quarter that the throne remembers its outer domains—that good exceeds whatever came from holding the Guards command. Late in the Han they dispatched Grand Tutor Ma Midi to pacify the east; unless you despise me as too slight a man, send me out at the beginning of winter—I await your orders respectfully."
4
羲之既拜護軍，又苦求宣城郡，不許，乃以為右軍將軍、會稽內史。 時殷浩與桓溫不協，羲之以國家之安在於內外和，因以與浩書以戒之，浩不從。 及浩將北伐，羲之以為必敗，以書止之，言甚切至。 浩遂行果為姚襄所敗。 復圖再舉，又遺浩書曰：
After Wang Xizhi took the Guards post he pleaded bitterly for Xuancheng; when that was denied he received instead the posts of General of the Right Army and interior minister of Kuaiji. Yin Hao and Huan Wen were at odds; Wang Xizhi believed the realm depended on concord between court and commanders, so he warned Yin Hao in a letter—advice Yin ignored. When Yin Hao prepared a northern expedition Wang Xizhi foresaw disaster and wrote to dissuade him in the strongest terms. Yin Hao marched anyway and, as predicted, suffered defeat at the hands of Yao Xiang. When Yin Hao plotted another campaign, Wang Xizhi again wrote him a letter that read:
5
知安西敗喪，公私惋怛，不能須臾去懷，以區區江左，所營綜如此，天下寒心，固以久矣，而加之敗喪，此可熟念。 往事豈復可追，顧思弘將來，令天下寄命有所，自隆中興之業。 政以道勝寬和為本，力爭武功，作非所當，因循所長，以固大業，想識其由來也。
News of General Who Pacifies the West's defeat appalled everyone; grief would not lift for an instant. Our narrow foothold south of the Yangzi has been strained to this point—the empire has despaired for ages—and fresh disaster makes reflection imperative. The past cannot be undone; look rather to what lies ahead so that people again have something to live for and the revival may proceed. Rule should prevail through moral authority grounded in tolerance and peace; leaning on force alone is wrong. Play to existing strengths to secure the larger task—you know the reasoning as well as I.
6
自寇亂以來，處內外之任者，未有深謀遠慮，括囊至計，而疲竭根本，各從所志，竟無一功可論，一事可記，忠言嘉謀棄而莫用，遂令天下將有土崩之勢，何能不痛心悲慨也。 任其事者，豈得辭四海之責！ 追咎往事，亦何所復及，宜更虛己求賢，當與有識共之，不可復令忠允之言常屈於當權。 今軍破於外，資竭於內，保淮之志非復所及，莫過還保長江，都督將各復舊鎮，自長江以外，羈縻而已。 任國鈞者，引咎責躬，深自貶降以謝百姓。 更與朝賢思布平政，除其煩苛，省其賦役，與百姓更始。 庶可以允塞群望，救倒懸之急。
Since the barbarian wars, officials at court and in the provinces have shown neither foresight nor caution—always hoarding schemes yet bleeding the realm dry, each chasing private aims until nothing praiseworthy remains and worthy counsel goes unheard. The empire now teeters toward ruin; the heart breaks with rage. Those who hold power cannot escape answering to the whole realm. Recrimination fixes nothing; humble yourself, recruit wise men, and share the burden—and stop letting blunt honesty bow to faction. Defeat abroad has emptied the treasury; we can no longer cling to the Huai line—better withdraw behind the Yangzi, restore each commander to former posts, and beyond the river merely maintain a light touch. National leaders must own the fault, impose penance on themselves, and step down far enough to apologize to the common people. Then join conscientious ministers to widen policy, cut red tape and harsh imposts, lighten taxes and labor, and give the people a fresh start. Only thus may popular expectation be met and the people saved from their desperate plight.
7
使君起于布衣，任天下之重，尚德之舉，未能事事允稱。 當董統之任而敗喪至此，恐闔朝群賢未有與人分其謗者。 今亟修德補闕，廣延群賢，與之分任，尚未知獲濟所期。 若猶以前事為未工，故復求之於分外，宇宙雖廣，自容何所！ 知言不必用，或取怨執政，然當情慨所在，正自不能不盡懷極言。 若必親征，未達此旨，果行者，愚智所不解也。 願復與眾共之。
You began as a commoner yet shoulder the empire; even your esteemed appointments have not always lived up to their promise. Entrusted with supreme leadership you led us to catastrophe; I doubt any colleague will shoulder part of the reproach for you. Repair your virtue without delay, widen your circle of advisers, and divide responsibility—and even then success is uncertain. If you decide earlier efforts were too tame and grasp for still more—even the wide world may have no place left for you. I realize frank counsel may win no hearing or only anger the mighty; still, feeling leaves no choice but to speak plainly. Should you insist on leading the army yourself without seeing this point, the march itself will baffle everyone, wise or simple. I hope this may be aired openly among our peers.
8
復被州符，增運千石，征役兼至，皆以軍期，對之喪氣，罔知所厝。 自頃年割剝遺黎，刑徒竟路，殆同秦政，惟未加參夷之刑耳，恐勝廣之憂，無復日矣。
Fresh orders from the province pile another thousand shi of grain transport on us, every duty stamped urgent as war—one feels only despair, at a loss where to turn. For years the remnant population has been fleeced until convicts choke the highways—policy nearly as cruel as the Qin's, short only of wholesale clan executions. I dread a popular explosion may come any morning.
9
又與會稽王箋陳浩不宜北伐，並論時事曰：
He also sent Prince Sima Yu of Kuaiji a memorandum arguing against Yin Hao's northern expedition and commenting on current affairs:
10
古人恥其君不為堯舜，北面之道，豈不願尊其所事，比靈斯往代，況遇千載一時之運？ 顧智力屈于當年，何得不權輕重而處之也。 今雖有可欣之會，內求諸己，而所憂乃重於所欣。 《傳》云：「自非聖人，外甯必有內憂。 」今外不寧，內憂已深。 古之弘大業者，或不謀於眾，傾國以濟一時功者，亦往往而有之。 誠獨運之明足以邁眾，暫勞之弊終獲永逸者可也。 求之於今，可得擬議乎！
Our forebears blushed if their lord fell short of Yao or Shun; as loyal ministers we too long to ennoble our sovereign and match earlier ages—above all when fortune seems uniquely ripe. Yet wit and strength have limits in any reign; one must weigh priorities and choose pragmatically. Even where cause for cheer appears, inwardly anxiety outweighs satisfaction. The classics remark that except for sages, outward peace conceals inner peril. Today we lack even outward calm, and inward troubles run deep. Ancient founders sometimes acted without polling everyone; rulers did gamble the realm on single decisive strokes. That works only when unilateral genius truly outshines the crowd and short-term strain buys lasting peace. Look at our situation now—can anyone honestly claim such conditions hold?
11
夫廟算決勝，必宜審量彼我，萬全而後動。 功就之日，便當因其眾而即其實。 今功未可期，而遺黎殲盡，萬不餘一。 且千里饋糧，自古為難，況今轉運供繼，西輸許洛，北入黃河。 雖秦政之弊，未至於此，而十室之憂，便以交至。 今運無還期，徵求日重，以區區吳越經緯天下十分之九，不亡何待！ 而不度德量力，不弊不已，此封內所痛心歎悼而莫敢吐誠。
War planning demands cold comparison of strengths; move only when every contingency is covered. After victory you may lean on popular strength and consolidate real gains. Today success is nowhere in sight while civilians are massacred until scarcely one in ten thousand survives. Feeding armies across a thousand miles has always been hellishly hard—especially now that convoys must run west toward Luoyang and north across the Yellow River. Even Qin tyranny stopped short of this; now every village faces simultaneous ruin. Carriers never come home, levies swell daily, and this small Wu-Yue patch must supply nine-tenths of the realm—if that is not a recipe for extinction, what is? Still power ignores its limits and drives until collapse—the whole heartland groans yet no one dares speak openly.
12
往者不可諫，來者猶可追，願殿下更垂三思，解而更張，令殷浩、荀羨還據合肥、廣陵，許昌、譙郡、梁、彭城諸軍皆還保淮，為不可勝之基，鬚根立勢舉，謀之未晚，此實當今策之上者。 若不行此，社稷之憂可計日而待。 安危之機，易於反掌，考之虛實，著於目前，願運獨斷之明，定之於一朝也。
The past is beyond advice, but the future can still be shaped—please reconsider three times, change course, pull Yin Hao and Xun Xian back to Hefei and Guangling, withdraw Xuchang, Qiao, Liang, and Pengcheng troops behind the Huai, and build an impregnable line; once foundations firm you may strike—not too late. That is today's paramount strategy. Ignore this and the dynasty's doom can be numbered in days. Safety and disaster pivot as quickly as turning a hand—the facts lie plain before you; I pray you exercise decisive clarity and settle policy at once.
13
地淺而言深，豈不知其未易。 然古人處閭閻行陣之間，尚或幹時謀國，評裁者不以為譏，況廁大臣末行，豈可默而不言哉！ 存亡所系，決在行之，不可復持疑後機，不定之於此，後欲悔之，亦無及也。
I am a minor figure presuming to speak weighty truth—I know how risky that is. Yet ancients meddled from marketplace or camp for the nation's sake without shame—how much more may a senior minister speak up? Whether we live or die turns on whether you act; hesitate now and remorse will arrive too late.
14
殿下德冠宇內，以公室輔朝，最可直道行之，致隆當年，而未允物望，受殊遇者所以寤寐長歎，實為殿下惜之。 國家之慮深矣，常恐伍員之憂不獨在昔，麋鹿之游將不止林藪而已。 願殿下暫廢虛遠之懷，以救倒懸之急，可謂以亡為存，轉禍為福，則宗廟之慶，四海有賴矣。
Your virtue caps the realm and you guide the throne as imperial kin—you could rule straight down the middle and lift the age, yet popular hope remains unsated; even favored ministers lie awake sighing for love of you. The dynasty's peril runs deep; Wu Zixu's warning may recur, and court refugees may flee beyond mere woodland. Suspend lofty detachment long enough to save people dangling by their heels—turn extinction into survival and curse into blessing—and the temples will rejoice and the realm will trust you.
15
時東土饑荒，羲之輒開倉振貸。 然朝廷賦役繁重，吳會憂甚，羲之每上疏爭之，事多見從。 又遺尚書僕射謝安書曰：
When famine struck the east Wang Xizhi opened government granaries for relief loans. Court taxes and labor gangs remained crushing—Kuaiji suffered worst—yet Wang Xizhi kept memorializing against abuses and usually won concessions. He also wrote Vice Director Xie An:
16
頃所陳論，每蒙允納，所以令下小得蘇息，各安其業。 若不耳，此一郡久以蹈東海矣。
The proposals I have lately laid before you have mostly been approved, so local directives eased slightly and people could tend their livelihoods. Without that indulgence this prefecture would long ago have slid into the ocean—meaning utter ruin.
17
今事之大者未布，漕運是也。 吾意望朝廷可申下定期，委之所司，勿復催下，但當歲終考其殿最。 長吏尤殿，命檻車送詣天臺。 三縣不舉，二千石必免，或可左降，令在疆塞極難之地。
Among unfinished business none looms larger than grain transport by water. I urge the court to set firm deadlines, leave execution to the proper bureaus, stop hectored micromanagement, and judge officers solely by year-end performance. The worst magistrates should ride prison wagons to the capital. Fail three districts under your charge and a two-thousand-bushel official must be cashiered—or demoted to some miserable border post.
18
又自吾到此，從事常有四五，兼以台司及都水御史行台文符如雨，倒錯違背，不復可知。 吾又瞑目循常推前，取重者及綱紀，輕者在五曹。 主者蒞事，未嘗得十日，吏民趨走，功費萬計。 卿方任其重，可徐尋所言。 江左平日，揚州一良刺史便足統之，況以群才而更不理，正由為法不一，牽制者眾，思簡而易從，便足以保守成業
Since I reached this post four or five aides jostle about while Secretariat, capital hydrology, and touring inspectors shower contradictory orders—nothing lines up. Closing my eyes I simply follow routine: weighty cases go to senior clerks, light ones to the five departments. No chief officer lasts ten days straight; runners and townsfolk dash until wasted effort mounts astronomically. You now shoulder that burden—please review what I have said at leisure. In normal times south of the Yangzi a single competent Yangzhou inspector could hold everything together—yet now, rich in talent, we still misrule because laws conflict and too many hands tug the reins; simplify procedure so people can comply and we might keep what we have.
19
倉督監耗盜官米，動以萬計，吾謂誅翦一人，其後便斷，而時意不同。 近檢校諸縣，無不皆爾。 余姚近十萬斛，重斂以資奸吏，令國用空乏，良可歎也。
Granary clerks embezzle tens of thousands of hu of state grain; I argued executing one ringleader would stop it—but opinion upstairs disagreed. Recent audits show every county guilty of the same. Yuyao alone sits on nearly one hundred thousand hu while cruel taxes feed corrupt officers—state stocks drain dry; it breaks the heart.
20
自軍興以來，征役及充運死亡叛散不反者眾，虛耗至此，而補代循常，所在凋困，莫知所出。 上命所差，上道多叛，則吏及叛者席捲同去。 又有常制，輒令其家及同伍課捕。 課捕不擒，家及同伍尋復亡叛。 百姓流亡，戶口日減，其源在此。 又有百工醫寺，死亡絕沒，家戶空盡，差代無所，上命不絕，事起成十年、十五年，彈舉獲罪無懈息而無益實事，何以堪之！ 謂自今諸死罪原輕者及五歲刑，可以充此，其減死者，可長充兵役，五歲者，可充雜工醫寺，皆令移其家以實都邑。 都邑既實，是政之本，又可絕其亡叛。 不移其家，逃亡之患復如初耳。 今除罪而充雜役，盡移其家，小人愚迷，或以為重於殺戮，可以絕奸。 刑名雖輕，懲肅實重，豈非適時之宜邪！
Since the wars began, draftees and river crews die, desert, or vanish without return; the treasury is hollow yet replacements continue by rote until districts collapse with no idea how to cope. Men drafted by imperial command often desert along the way; then officials and deserters flee together in a rout. Standing rules also make each man's family and his five-household mutual-responsibility group hunt him down. When those patrols fail, the families and neighbors soon desert too. Commoners scatter as refugees while the census shrinks daily—and this is why. Craftsmen and temple healers die without heirs until workshops stand empty, yet quotas never stop—cases drag ten or fifteen years of endless accusations that punish clerks without fixing anything. Who can bear it? Henceforth commute lighter capital cases and five-year sentences into these roles: spare the rope by drafting convicts into the army, assign five-year men to workshops and temple chores, and relocate their families to swell the capital population. Stocking the metropolis is fundamental policy and stops people from running away. Skip forced relocation and desertion starts all over again. Replacing execution with penal labor plus resettlement looks cruel to simple folk—yet it deters crime better than the axe. The sentence sounds mild but bites harder—is that not exactly what the moment needs?
21
羲之雅好服食養性，不樂在京師，初渡浙江，便有終焉之志。 會稽有佳山水，名士多居之，謝安未仕時亦居焉。 孫綽、李充、許詢、支遁等皆以文義冠世，並築室東土，與羲之同好。 嘗與同志宴集于會稽山陰之蘭亭，羲之自為之序以申其志，曰：
Wang Xizhi doted on alchemical regimen and quietist longevity; he disliked Luoyang, and once he crossed into Zhedong he resolved to finish his life there. Kuaiji's landscape drew celebrated scholars—Xie An lived there too before he entered government. Sun Chuo, Li Chong, Xu Xun, Zhi Dun, and their circle led the age in letters; each raised a villa in the east and kept Wang Xizhi company. He once joined kindred spirits for a banquet at the Orchid Pavilion south of Shanyin and drafted the famous preface himself; it begins:
22
永和九年，歲在癸丑，暮春之初，會於會稽山陰之蘭亭，修禊事也。 群賢畢至，少長咸集。 此地有崇山峻嶺，茂林修竹，又有清流激湍，映帶左右，引以為流觴曲水，列坐其次。 雖無絲竹管弦之盛，一觴一詠，亦足以暢敘幽情。

Peaks rose sheer, bamboo groves thickened, and bright rapids curled past us on either side; we turned them into a meandering stream for the wine-cup game and sat along its banks. Even without full orchestras, each cup paired with a verse was enough to voice the heart's hidden moods.

Men move through a single lifetime in countless ways: some pour out private thoughts in sealed rooms; others chase passions abroad and forget the body. Preferences diverge—some restless, some still—but whenever joy strikes and we clutch that moment, we feel utterly content and forget age stealing on us. When satiation fades, mood swings with changing affairs—and regret follows. Joy that thrilled us moments ago turns instantly into dusty memory, yet memory still moves us. Life spans long or short obey fate's turning—and every span ends in extinction. The wise called death and life the greatest of matters—how can we not grieve?

Whenever I read old laments I feel the author speaks straight to my heart—yet words can never fully carry that ache. So I reject Zhuangzi's fantasy that life and death are the same or that long life equals early death; future readers will pity us just as we pity antiquity—what sorrow! Here I list today's poets and preserve their verses; ages may change, but the cause of our moods stays one. Later readers will yet respond to these lines.

Watching retainers gamble at chupu as a child he remarked, "The southern army lacks fight. A student retorted, "This boy squints through a bamboo tube—once in a while he spots a stripe. Wang Xianzhi flared, "Compared with Xun Can I fall short from afar; beside Liu Tan I feel ashamed up close. He swept out in a huff. Visiting Xie An with Wang Huizhi and Wang Caozhi, the elders prattled of trivia while Xianzhi offered only courteous small talk. After they left someone asked which brother impressed him; Xie An named the youngest. Guests pressed why, Xie said, "Virtuous men speak little—that reserve told me." Once fire erupted while he roomed with Wang Huizhi; the elder bolted barefoot. Wang Xianzhi stayed serene and let servants walk him out slowly. Thieves once stripped his bedroom while he slept. He murmured, "Friends—the blue felt mat is an heirloom—kindly leave it. They fled in panic.
49
工草隸，善丹青。 七八歲時學書，羲之密從後掣其筆不得，歎曰：「此兒後當復有大名。 」嘗書壁為方丈大字，羲之甚以為能，觀者數百人。 桓溫嘗使書扇，筆誤落，因畫作烏駁牸牛，甚妙。
He mastered cursive and clerical scripts and excelled at painting. At seven or eight Wang Xizhi tried snatching his brush from behind and could not—then sighed that the boy would earn a great name. He once brushed characters a yard tall on a wall; hundreds came to stare and his father praised the feat. When Huan Wen asked for fans Wang Xianzhi blotted the silk—then turned the slip into a splendid piebald cow.
50
起家州主簿、秘書郎，轉丞，以選尚新安公主。 嘗經吳郡，聞顧辟彊有名園。 先不相識，乘平肩輿徑入。 時辟彊方集賓友，而獻之遊歷既畢，傍若無人。 辟彊勃然數之曰：「傲主人，非禮也。 以貴驕士，非道也。 失是二者，不足齒之傖耳。 」便驅出門。 獻之傲如也，不以屑意。
He entered service as prefect recorder and palace secretary, rose to deputy director of the secretariat, and married the Princess of Xin'an by imperial selection. Passing through Wu he heard Gu Piqiang owned a celebrated estate. They had never met, yet Wang rode an open sedan straight onto the grounds. Gu was entertaining guests; Wang toured the grounds as if alone. Gu exploded, "Insulting your host breaks propriety. Throwing princely rank at scholars violates the Way. Miss both marks and you are beneath contempt. He had Wang thrown out. Wang Xianzhi stayed lordly and shrugged it off.
51
謝安甚欽愛之，請為長史。 安進號衛將軍，復為長史。 太元中，新起太極殿，安欲使獻之題榜，以為萬代寶，而難言之，試謂曰：「魏時陵雲殿榜未題，而匠者誤釘之，不可下，乃使韋仲將懸橙書之。 比訖，須鬢盡白，裁餘氣息。 還語子弟，宜絕此法。 」獻之揣知其旨，正色曰：「仲將，魏之大臣，甯有此事！ 使其若此，有以知魏德之不長。 」安遂不之逼。 安又問曰：「君書何如君家尊？ 」答曰：「故當不同。 」安曰：「外論不爾。 」答曰：「人那得知！ 」尋除建威將軍、吳興太守，徵拜中書令。
Xie An esteemed him and named him chief clerk. When Xie became General Who Guards the Wei Wang stayed chief clerk. During Taiyuan, Xie wanted Wang to brush the new Taiji Hall plaque—a heirloom—but feared to command bluntly, so hinted with an old tale: Wei craftsmen once nailed the Lingyun plaque prematurely and forced minister Wei Zhongjiang to ink it while dangling from a scaffold. Wei descended white-haired and gasping. He forbade his heirs ever to repeat such humiliation. Wang Xianzhi saw through the ploy and snapped, "Wei Zhongjiang was a Wei minister—that story is absurd! Had it happened it would prove Wei lacked lasting virtue. Xie An dropped the subject. Xie later asked whether his calligraphy matched his father's. He answered, "They should differ." Xie said critics disagree. Wang replied, "How would outsiders know?" Soon he became General Who Establishes Might, prefect of Wuxing, then director of the palace secretariat.
52
未幾，獻之遇疾，家人為上章，道家法應首過，問其有何得失。 對曰：「不覺餘事，惟憶與郗家離婚。 」獻之前妻，郗曇女也。 俄而卒於官。 安僖皇后立，以後父追贈侍中、特進、光祿大夫、太宰，諡曰憲。 無子，以兄子靜之嗣，位至義興太守。 時議者以為羲之草隸，江左中朝莫有及者，獻之骨力遠不及父，而頗有媚趣。 桓玄雅愛其父子書，各為一帙，置左右以玩之。 始羲之所與共游者許邁。
Falling ill, his household filed a Daoist merit petition requiring confession of sins. He said he recalled nothing except divorcing Lady Xi. That wife had been Xi Tan's daughter. He died in post soon after. When Empress Wang became consort her father received posthumous ranks culminating in grand tutor with the epithet Xian. Childless, he adopted his nephew Wang Jingzhi, who rose to prefect of Yixing. Critics held Wang Xizhi's grass and clerical hands unrivaled south of the Yangzi; Wang Xianzhi lacked his father's structural power but showed seductive charm. Huan Xuan treasured both men's works, keeping separate albums at hand. Among Wang Xizhi's early companions was Xu Mai.

附許邁
Appendix: Xu Mai.
54
許邁，字叔玄，一名映，丹陽句容人也。 家世士族，而邁少恬靜，不慕仕進。 未弱冠，嘗造郭璞，璞為之筮，遇《泰》之《大畜》，其上六爻發。 璞謂曰：「君元吉自天，宜學升遐之道。 」時南海太守鮑靚隱跡潛遁，人莫之知。 邁乃往候之，探其至要。 父母尚存，未忍違親。 謂余杭懸霤山近延陵之茅山，是洞庭西門，潛通五嶽，陳安世、茅季偉常所遊處，於是立精舍於懸霤，而往來茅嶺之洞室，放絕世務，以尋仙館，朔望時節還家定省而已。 父母既終，乃遣婦孫氏還家，遂攜其同志遍游名山焉。
Xu Mai, whose courtesy name was Shuxuan and who also took the name Ying, came from Jurong in Danyang commandery. Though born to a gentry family he remained placid as a youth and sought no career. Before his capping he consulted Guo Pu, who cast yarrow stalks and drew Peace turning into Great Taming with the top line changing. Guo Pu said, "Heaven grants you supreme good fortune—you should pursue transcendence. Meanwhile Bao Jing, prefect of Nanhai, lived in hiding where no one could find him. Xu Mai sought him out and drew out his deepest teachings. His parents still lived, so he could not abandon them. He held that Xuansliu Mountain in Yuhang, near Maoshan at Yanling, formed the western portal of Dongting and linked to the Five Peaks—the haunt of adepts Chen Anshi and Mao Jiwei—so he built a hermitage there, shuttled to Maoshan's caves, cut worldly ties to pursue immortality, and came home only at each fortnight to visit his parents. After they died he sent his wife the Lady Sun home and roamed sacred peaks with fellow seekers.
55
初采藥於桐廬縣之桓山，餌術涉三年，時欲斷穀。 以此山近人，不得專一，四面籓之，好道之徒欲相見者，登樓與語，以此為樂。 常服氣，一氣千餘息。 永和二年，移入臨安西山，登岩茹芝，眇爾自得，有終焉之志。 乃改名玄，字遠遊。 與婦書告別，又著詩十二首，論神仙之事焉。 羲之造之，未嘗不彌日忘歸，相與為世外之交。 玄遺羲之書云：「自山陰南至臨安，多有金堂玉室，仙人芝草，左元放之徒，漢末諸得道者皆在焉。 」羲之自為之傳，述靈異之跡甚多，不可詳記。 玄自後莫測所終，好道者皆謂之羽化矣。
He first gathered drugs on Mount Huan in Tonglu, ate atractylodes for three years, and tried grain abstention. Because the peak stood too close to villages he fenced it on four sides; admirers who wished an audience spoke with him only from a lookout—a sport he enjoyed. He practiced breath retention until one cycle counted over a thousand counts. In Yonghe 2 he relocated to the western hills of Lin'an, climbing cliffs and chewing ganoderma in quiet contentment, resolved to die there. He renamed himself Xuan with the courtesy name Yuanyou. He sent his wife a parting letter and twelve poems on transcendence. Wang Xizhi's visits lasted whole days; they formed a friendship outside mundane life. Xuan wrote Wang Xizhi that south from Shanyin to Lin'an lay golden halls and jade chambers, immortal fungi, and masters like Zuo Ci—all the Han-era transcendents dwelt there. Wang Xizhi drafted his biography, crowded with marvels too numerous to list. None knew Xu Xuan's ultimate fate; Daoists declared him translated by feather wings.
56
贊曰：書契之興，肇乎中古，繩文鳥跡，不足可觀。 末代去朴歸華，舒箋點翰，爭相跨尚，競其工拙。 伯英臨池之妙，無復餘蹤； 師宜懸帳之奇，罕有遺跡。 逮乎鐘王以降，略可言焉。 鐘雖擅美一時，亦為回絕，論其盡善，或有所疑。 至於布纖濃，分疏密，霞舒雲卷，無所間然。 但其體則古而不今，字則長而逾制，語其大量，以此為瑕。 獻之雖有父風，殊非新巧。 觀其字勢疏瘦，如隆冬之枯樹； 覽其筆蹤拘束，若嚴家之餓隸。 其枯樹也，雖槎枿而無屈伸； 其餓隸也，則羈羸而不放縱。 兼斯二者，故翰墨之病歙！ 子雲近出，擅名江表，然僅得成書，無丈夫之氣，行行若縈春蚓，字字如綰秋蛇； 臥王濛於紙中，坐徐偃於筆下； 雖禿千兔之翰，聚無一毫之筋，窮萬谷之皮，斂無半分之骨； 以茲播美，非其濫名邪！ 此數子者，皆譽過其實。 所以詳察古今，研精篆素，盡善盡美，其惟王逸少乎！ 觀其點曳之工，裁成之妙，煙霏露結，狀若斷而還連； 鳳翥龍蟠，勢如斜而反直。 玩之不覺為倦，覽之莫識其端，心慕手追，此人而已。 其餘區區之類，何足論哉！
The historian remarks: script arose in middle antiquity from knotted cords and bird traces hardly worth the eye. Later ages forsook plain forms for ornament; brush danced across paper as rivals outdid one another in finesse. Zhang Zhi's miracle by the ink-darkened pool leaves barely a trace; Shi Yi's feat writing through a suspended curtain survives almost nowhere. Only from Zhong Yao and Wang Xizhi onward may one judge with confidence. Zhong Yao dominated his age yet belongs to a solitary pinnacle; whether even he reached flawless mastery invites debate. His modulation of thick and thin, loose and tight—mist veiling, dew beading—looks broken yet flows unbroken; yet his manner feels archaic rather than modern and his glyphs run overlong against rule—that is his blemish. Wang Xianzhi echoes his father yet lacks fresh ingenuity. His stroke momentum looks gaunt as leafless midwinter trees; his brush moves tight as starved house slaves under a harsh master. Those winter trees sprout snags yet never flex; those famished servants stay leashed and gaunt, never loose. Combine both faults and the ink itself grows sickly! Xiao Ziyun lately rose to fame south of the river but masters only tidy copy—it lacks masculine vigor; each row coils like spring earthworms, each graph knots like autumn snakes; it stretches Wang Meng supine on the page and seats Xu Yan rigid beneath the brush; though it wears down a thousand rabbit brushes it gathers no fiber of tendon; though it stacks ten thousand valleys of hides it collects no splinter of bone; to broadcast such work as virtue—is that not hollow fame? These worthies are overrated one and all. Survey past and present, refine seal and draft script, and find perfection solely in Wang Xizhi! Study his dots and sweeps, his shaped compositions—mist and dew interwoven—seeming severed yet endlessly linked; phoenix lifting, dragon curling—energy seems slanted yet resolves upright. One handles his work without tiring, gazes without finding its limit—only he draws mind and hand together. The lesser scribblers scarcely merit mention!
