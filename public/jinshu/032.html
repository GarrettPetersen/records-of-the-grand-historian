1
元敬虞皇后
Empress Yu the Respectful
2
元敬虞皇后諱孟母，濟陽外黃人也。 父豫，見《外戚傳》。 帝為琅邪王，納后為妃，無子。 永嘉六年薨，時年三十五。
Empress Yu the Respectful, whose personal name was Mengmu, came from Waihuang in Jiyang. Her father was Yu Yu; his life is recorded under the consort kin. While he was still Prince of Langya, he married her as his consort, and she had no children. She died in 312, at the age of thirty-five.
3
帝為晉王，追尊為王后。 有司奏王后應別立廟。 令曰：「今宗廟未成，不宜更興作，便修飾陵上屋以為廟。 」太興三年，冊曰：「皇帝咨前琅邪王妃虞氏：朕祗順昊天成命，用陟帝位。 悼妃夙徂，徽音潛翳，御于家邦，靡所儀刑，陰教有虧，用傷于懷。 追號制諡，先王之典。 今遣使持節兼太尉萬勝奉冊贈皇后璽綬，祀以太牢。 魂而有靈，嘉茲寵榮。 」乃祔於太廟，葬建平陵。
Once he was invested as Prince of Jin, he posthumously raised her to the rank of queen consort. Officials proposed that she receive a shrine of her own, apart from the main imperial cult. He issued an order: “The imperial ancestral temple is still unfinished, so we should not start another major project; renovate the structures on her mound and let that serve as her shrine.” ” In 320, the formal patent ran: “The Emperor speaks to Lady Yu, once consort to the Prince of Langya: I have bowed to Heaven’s Mandate and taken the throne.” My consort was taken too soon; her virtuous memory has faded; within the palace and across the realm there is no one left to embody her example, and the teaching proper to the inner quarters stands impaired—this grieves me deeply. Bestowing a posthumous name and style is what the sages of old established as right. I therefore send Wan Sheng, credential-bearing envoy and concurrent Grand Commandant, to deliver the patent and the empress’s seals and ribbons, with sacrifice of the highest grade. Should your spirit endure, take comfort in this honor we pay you. She was then enshrined with the imperial ancestors and laid to rest at Jianping Mausoleum.
4
太寧中，明帝追懷母養之恩，贈豫妻王氏為䢵陽縣君，從母散騎常侍新野王罕妻為平陽鄉君。
In the Taining years, Emperor Ming remembered how his mother had raised him and invested Yu’s widow, Lady Wang, as Mistress of Yinyang county, and his aunt—the wife of Wang Han, Prince of Xinye and Regular Cavalier Attendant-in-Ordinary—as Mistress of Pingyang township.
5
荀豫章君
Lady Xun of Yuzhang
6
豫章君荀氏，元帝宮人也。 初有寵，生明帝及琅邪王裒，由是為虞后所忌。 自以位卑，每懷怨望，為帝所譴，漸見疏薄。 及明帝即位，封建安君，別立第宅。 太寧元年，帝迎還臺內，供奉隆厚。 及成帝立，尊重同于太后。 咸康元年薨。 詔曰：「朕少遭憫凶，慈訓無稟，撫育之勤，建安君之仁也。 一旦薨殂，實思報復，永惟平昔，感痛哀摧。 其贈豫章郡君，別立廟于京都。」
Lady Xun of Yuzhang, from the Xun family, had entered Emperor Yuan’s harem. She was favored early on and gave birth to Emperor Ming and Sima Pou, Prince of Langya, which drew the jealousy of Empress Yu. Conscious of her low standing, she nursed grievances until the emperor reproached her; thereafter he grew steadily cooler toward her. After Emperor Ming took the throne, he created her Lady of Jian’an and set aside a private mansion for her. In 323 he brought her back into the palace precincts and treated her with exceptional generosity. Under Emperor Cheng she was deferred to as if she were the empress dowager herself. She died in 335. The edict read: “I lost my parents while young and had no mother’s guidance; it was Lady of Jian’an who tirelessly raised me—that was her kindness.” Now that she is gone, I long to repay her; when I think back on those years, sorrow overwhelms me. “Posthumously invest her as Mistress of Yuzhang commandery and build her an independent shrine in the capital.”
7
明穆庾皇后
Empress Yu the Solemn and Clear-minded
8
明穆庾皇后諱文君，潁川鄢陵人也。 父琛，見《外戚傳》。 后性仁慈，美姿儀。 元帝聞之，聘為太子妃，以德行見重。
Empress Yu the Solemn, whose personal name was Wenjun, hailed from Yanling in Yingchuan. Her father was Yu Chen; see the consort-kin biographies. She was gentle and kind by nature, and strikingly graceful. Emperor Yuan, hearing of her reputation, chose her as his heir’s consort; she was valued for character and deportment.
9
明帝即位，立為皇后。 冊曰：「妃庾氏昔承明命，作嬪東宮，虔恭中饋，思媚軌則。 履信思順，以成肅雝之道； 正位閨房，以著協德之美。 朕夙罹不造，煢煢在疚。 群公卿士，稽之往代，僉以崇嫡明統，載在典謨，宜建長秋，以奉宗廟。 是以追述先志，不替舊命，使使持節兼太尉授皇后璽綬。 夫坤德尚柔，婦道承姑，崇粢盛之禮，敦螽斯之義。 是以利在永貞，克隆堂基，母儀天下，潛暢陰教。 鑒于六列，考之篇籍，禍福無門，盛衰由人，雖休勿休。 其敬之哉，可不慎歟！」
When Emperor Ming took the throne, he made her empress. The investiture declared: “Lady Yu once accepted the imperial charge and entered the Eastern Palace as consort; she kept the inner household with devotion and strove to embody every duty expected of a wife.” She walked in good faith and humility, fulfilling that harmonious virtue praised in the songs; She held her rightful place in the women’s quarters and showed what it means for spouses to match each other in virtue. I have known bereavement from youth and stand orphaned in grief. My ministers, looking to precedent, agree that raising the legitimate heir and securing the line—as the classics demand—means installing a chief consort worthy to assist the ancestral cult. I therefore honor my late father’s wish and renew the earlier appointment, sending a credential-bearing Grand Commandant to invest you with the empress’s seals and ribbons. Earth’s virtue lies in yielding; a wife’s duty includes serving her husband’s mother; honor the sacrifices and cherish the blessing of abundant heirs. Perseverance in virtue will strengthen the dynasty; as mother of the realm, let your quiet influence spread the teaching of the inner quarters. Read the lesson of the six grades of consorts and the histories: fortune follows no single path; rise and fall rest on human conduct; even in prosperity, never grow complacent. Revere these charges—could anything demand greater care?”
10
成恭杜皇后
Empress Du the Accomplished and Reverent
11
成恭杜皇后諱陵陽[2]，京兆人，鎮南將軍預之曾孫也。 父乂，見《外戚傳》。 成帝以后奕世名德，咸康二年備禮拜為皇后，即日入宮。 帝御太極前殿，群臣畢賀，晝漏盡，懸籥，百官乃罷。 后少有姿色，然長猶無齒，有來求婚者輒中止。 及帝納采之日，一夜齒盡生。 改宣城陵陽縣為廣陽縣。 七年三月，后崩，年二十一。 外官五日一臨，內官旦一入，葬訖止。 后在位六年，無子。
Empress Du the Accomplished and Reverent, whose personal name was Lingyang, came from the Jingzhao area; she was a great-granddaughter of Du Yu, General Who Guards the South. Her father was Du Yi; his biography appears among the consort kin. Because her clan had been noted for virtue for generations, Emperor Cheng in 336 invested her as empress with full ceremony, and she moved into the palace that very day. The emperor received the court in the Taiji Hall’s forward chamber; after the full ritual of felicitations, the water-clock ran dry, the palace gates were sealed for the night, and the officials withdrew. She had been pretty as a girl, but even when grown she still had no teeth, so every match that was proposed fell through. On the night before the formal betrothal, her teeth came in all at once. The county of Lingyang in Xuancheng was redesignated Guangyang, to avoid writing her name. She died in the third month of 341, at twenty-one. Outer-court officials were to visit the bier every fifth day, inner staff every morning, until the funeral ended. She had been empress for six years and bore no children.
12
先是，三吳女子相與簪白花，望之如素柰，傳言天公織女死，為之著服，至是而后崩。 帝下詔曰：「吉凶典儀，誠宜備設； 然豐約之度，亦當隨時，況重壤之下，而崇飾無用邪！ 今山陵之事，一從節儉，陵中唯潔掃而已，不得施塗車芻靈。 」有司奏造凶門柏歷及調挽郎，皆不許。 又禁遠近遣使。 明年元會，有司奏廢樂。 詔廢管絃，奏金石如故。
Earlier, women east of the capital had taken to wearing white blossoms so thick they looked like drifts of pale crabapple; gossip said heaven’s Weaver Star had died and they were in mourning for her—then the empress passed away. The emperor decreed: “Funeral rites ought to be complete in form; but scale should suit the times. How can we pile useless finery on someone who lies deep under the soil?” For her tomb, let everything be spare: the chamber need only be swept clean—no straw carriages or straw effigies.” When officials asked to build the usual funeral gate and cypress biers and to draft professional mourners, he refused every request. He also barred localities from sending delegations. At the following New Year audience, the ministry asked to cancel the musical program. He allowed only bells and chimes—strings and winds were dropped.
13
孝武帝立，寧康二年，以后母裴氏為廣德縣君。 裴氏名穆，長水校尉綽孫，太傅主簿遐女，太尉王夷甫外孫。 中表之美，高於當世。 遐隨東海王越遇害，無子。 唯穆渡江，遂享榮慶，立第南掖門外，世所謂杜姥宅云。
After Emperor Xiaowu came to the throne, in 374 he ennobled the late empress’s mother, Lady Pei, as Mistress of Guangde county. Lady Pei, personal name Mu, was the granddaughter of Pei Chuo, Colonel of the Chang Waters; her father was Pei Xia, senior clerk to the Grand Tutor; her mother was a daughter of Wang Yan, the Grand Commandant known as Yifu. Her relatives on both sides were among the most distinguished families of the day. Pei Xia had been killed while in Sima Yue’s train and left no male heir. Only Lady Mu made the crossing south and lived to see honor and comfort; her house stood just outside the southern palace gate—the mansion people still called “Old Lady Du’s place.”
14
周太妃
Grand Consort Zhou
15
章太妃周氏以選入成帝宮，有寵，生哀帝及海西公。 始拜為貴人。 哀帝即位，詔有司議貴人位號，太尉桓溫議宜稱夫人，尚書僕射江虨議應曰太夫人。 詔崇為皇太妃，儀服與太后同。 又詔「朝臣不為太妃敬，合禮典不」。 太常江逌議「位號不極，不應盡敬」。 興寧元年薨。 帝欲服重，江虨啟應緦麻三月。 詔欲降為期年，虨又啟「厭屈私情，所以上嚴祖考」，帝從之。
Lady Zhou, titled Grand Consort of Zhang, had entered Emperor Cheng’s harem as a chosen attendant; favored, she gave birth to Emperor Ai and Sima Yi, the Prince of Haixi. She was first ranked as a noble lady. After Emperor Ai took the throne, he told officials to fix a proper title for her: Huan Wen, as Grand Commandant, proposed “Lady”; Jiang Bin, Vice Director of the Secretariat, argued for “Grand Lady.” The throne instead elevated her to imperial grand consort, with regalia matching an empress dowager’s. Another edict posed the question: “If courtiers refuse to bow to the grand consort, does that satisfy the ritual code?” Jiang You, Minister of Ceremonies, replied that her title fell short of the highest rank, so the full obeisance owed a dowager was not appropriate. She died in 363. The emperor wanted to observe the heaviest mourning; Jiang Bin advised the three-month fine-hemp obligation instead. When the court suggested cutting it to one year, Jiang Bin argued that restraining private grief showed proper reverence toward the imperial ancestors; the emperor accepted his view.
16
康獻褚皇后
Empress Chu the Secure and Exemplary
17
康獻褚皇后諱蒜子，河南陽翟人也。 父裒，見《外戚傳》。 后聰明有器識，少以名家入為琅邪王妃。 及康帝即位，立為皇后，封母謝氏為尋陽鄉君。
Empress Chu the Secure and Exemplary, whose personal name was Suanzi, came from Yangzhai in Henan. Her father was Chu Pou; see the consort-kin section. She was bright and judicious, and as a girl from a great clan became consort to the Prince of Langya. When Emperor Kang succeeded, he made her empress and invested her mother, Lady Xie, as Mistress of Xunyang township.
18
及穆帝即位，尊后曰皇太后。 時帝幼沖，未親國政。 領司徒蔡謨等上奏曰：「嗣皇誕哲岐嶷，繼承天統，率土宅心，兆庶蒙賴。 陛下體茲坤道，訓隆文母。 昔塗山光夏，簡狄熙殷，實由宣哲，以隆休祚。 伏惟陛下德侔二媯，淑美關雎，臨朝攝政，以寧天下。 今社稷危急，兆庶懸命，臣等章惶，一日萬機，事運之期，天祿所鍾，非復沖虛高讓之日。 漢和熹、順烈，並亦臨朝，近明穆故事，以為先制。 臣等不勝悲怖，謹伏地上請。 乞陛下上順祖宗，下念臣吏，推公弘道，以協天人，則萬邦承慶，群黎更生。 」太后詔曰：「帝幼沖，當賴群公卿士將順匡救，以酬先帝禮賢之意，且是舊德世濟之美，則莫重之命不墜，祖宗之基有奉，是其所以欲正位于內而已。 所奏懇到，形于翰墨，執省未究，以悲以懼。 先后允恭謙抑，思順坤道，所以不距群情，固為國計。 豈敢執守沖闇，以違先旨。 輒敬從所奏。 」於是臨朝稱制。 有司奏，謝夫人既封，荀、卞二夫人亦應追贈，皆后之前母也。 太后不許。 太常殷融議依鄭玄義，衛將軍裒在宮庭則盡臣敬，太后歸寧之日自如家人之禮。 太后詔曰：「典禮誠所未詳，如所奏，是情所不能安也，更詳之。 」征西將軍翼、南中郎尚議謂「父尊盡於一家，君敬重於天下，鄭玄義合情禮之中」。 太后從之。 自後朝臣皆敬裒焉。
After Emperor Mu came to the throne, she was honored as empress dowager. The boy emperor was too young to rule in his own right. Cai Mo, acting as Minister of Education, led a memorial: “The young sovereign is precocious and fit for the Mandate; the realm already looks to him and the people depend on his reign.” Your Majesty embodies the yielding power of Earth and carries forward the teaching of King Wen’s mother. Ancient consorts of Yu and of Shang’s house brought glory to their dynasties through wisdom—such virtue magnifies the throne’s blessing. We beg you: your virtue rivals the consorts of Shun; your grace recalls the ode ‘Guanju.’ Take the regency and steady the realm. The altars are in peril and every life hangs in the balance; we ministers tremble at the myriad decisions a single day brings. This is the moment Heaven has chosen—no time for demurring modesty. Han’s Deng and Liang empresses both governed from behind the screen; our own late Empress Yu the Solemn offers the nearest precedent. Overcome with fear and sorrow, we prostrate ourselves and implore you. Honor the ancestors, remember your officials, and rule with impartial sagacity so Heaven and humanity align; every state will rejoice and the people will be reborn from peril. The dowager replied: “The emperor is a child; he must lean on you ministers to guide and correct the court—that fulfills his late father’s wish to honor worthy men. Our house’s accumulated virtue must not let the great Mandate slip; someone must guard the ancestral enterprise. That is all I mean by wishing to settle rightful authority within the palace.” Your words are earnest and fill page after page; as I read them through, grief and apprehension mingle. My predecessors were humble and yielded to circumstance in the spirit of Earth; they did not refuse the court’s pleas when the realm required it. I cannot cling to youthful modesty and defy what those before me intended. I accept your request. With that, she held court and issued orders in the emperor’s name. Officials noted that Lady Xie had received a title and asked that Lady Xun and Lady Bian—the empress’s two stepmothers—be honored posthumously as well. The dowager refused. Yin Rong, Minister of Ceremonies, cited Zheng Xuan: Chu Pou should observe full court etiquette while at court, but when the dowager visited her family home, they could behave as ordinary kin. The dowager replied: “The classics do not spell this out clearly; your proposal would leave me uneasy. Think it through again.” Yu Yi, the western expedition commander, and Yu Shang, South Household Gentlemen, cited Zheng Xuan: paternal authority stops at the household, whereas the ruler’s dignity belongs to the realm—their view balanced duty and affection.” The dowager accepted their opinion. After that, every courtier treated Chu Pou with the full deference due a minister.
19
帝既冠，太后詔曰：「昔遭不造，帝在幼沖，皇緒之微，眇若贅旒。 百辟卿士率遵前朝，勸喻攝政。 以社稷之重，先代成義，僶俛敬從，弗遑固守。 仰憑七廟之靈，俯仗群后之力，帝加元服，禮成德備，當陽親覽，臨御萬國。 今歸事反政，一依舊典。 」于是居崇德宮，手詔群公曰：「昔以皇帝幼沖，從群后之議，既以闇弱，又頻丁極艱，銜恤歷祀，沈憂在疚。 司徒親尊德重，訓救其弊，王室之不壞，實公是憑。 帝既備茲冠禮，而四海未一，五胡叛逆，豺狼當路，費役日興，百姓困苦。 願諸君子思量遠算，戮力一心，輔翼幼主，匡救不逮。 未亡人永歸別宮，以終餘齒。 仰惟家國，故以一言託懷。」
Once the emperor came of age, the dowager proclaimed: “In those dark years the boy on the throne was all that stood between the dynasty and collapse.” The high ministers, holding to precedent, pressed me to govern in his name.” The altars left me no choice; I bowed to ancient example and took the regency, though I never wished to hold power for myself.” Heaven and our ancestors preserved us; with your help he has reached his majority and is ready to rule face-to-face with the realm.” I therefore return the government to him, following the old statutes to the letter.” She moved to Chongde Palace and sent a personal note to the great officers: “I took the regency only because he was a child; I am myself frail, and year after year of mourning and crisis have worn me down.” The Minister of Education, noble in both blood and character, steadied us when we faltered; the house of Sima owes him its survival.” He is a man grown, yet the empire is still torn: barbarian armies prowl the roads and the people stagger under endless exactions.” I ask you to look beyond the day, stand together, support the young sovereign, and mend every gap you find.” I shall withdraw to another palace and live out what years remain to me.” I mention this only because the realm and the clan weigh on my heart.”
20
及哀帝、海西公之世，太后復臨朝稱制[3]。 桓溫之廢海西公也，太后方在佛屋燒香，內侍啟云「外有急奏」，太后乃出。 尚倚戶前視奏數行，乃曰「我本自疑此」，至半便止，索筆答奏云：「未亡人罹此百憂，感念存沒，心焉如割。 」溫始呈詔草，慮太后意異，悚動流汗，見于顏色。 及詔出，溫大喜。
During the reigns of Emperor Ai and the deposed Prince of Haixi, she took up the regency once more. Huan Wen’s coup caught her at prayer in the chapel; a eunuch whispered that urgent papers had arrived, and she came out to receive them. She skimmed the opening lines at her doorway and murmured that she had half expected the news; she broke off midway, took brush in hand, and wrote that widowhood had brought every sorrow imaginable and that her heart felt torn out.” Huan Wen, presenting his draft abdication edict, was terrified she might balk—his face ran with sweat.” Once she sealed the document, Huan Wen was elated.
21
簡文帝即位，尊后為崇德太后。 及帝崩，孝武帝幼沖，桓溫又薨。 群臣啟曰：「王室多故，禍艱仍臻，國憂始周，復喪元輔，天下惘然，若無攸濟。 主上雖聖資奇茂，固天誕縱。 而春秋尚富，如在諒闇，蒸蒸之思，未遑庶事。 伏惟陛下德應坤厚，宣慈聖善，遭家多艱，臨朝親覽。 光大之美，化洽在昔，謳歌流詠，播溢無外。 雖有莘熙殷，妊姒隆周，未足以喻。 是以五謀克從，人鬼同心，仰望來蘇，懸心日月。 夫隨時之義，周易所尚，寧固社稷，大人之任。 伏願陛下撫綜萬機，釐和政道，以慰祖宗，以安兆庶。 不勝憂國喁喁至誠。 」太后詔曰：「王室不幸，仍有艱屯。 覽省啟事，感增悲歎。 內外諸君，並以主上春秋沖富，加蒸蒸之慕，未能親覽，號令宜有所由。 苟可安社稷，利天下，亦豈有所執，輒敬從所啟。 但闇昧之闕，望盡弼諧之道。 」於是太后復臨朝。 帝既冠，乃詔曰：「皇帝婚冠禮備，遐邇宅心，宜當陽親覽，緝熙惟始。 今歸政事，率由舊典。 」於是復稱崇德太后。
Emperor Jianwen raised her to Grand Dowager of Chongde. Jianwen soon died, leaving Xiaowu a minor; then Huan Wen died as well. The ministers wrote: “Disaster has followed disaster; we have scarcely buried one sovereign before we lose our chief minister—the court is adrift.” The emperor is gifted beyond measure—Heaven has showered him with talent—but he is still a boy. He remains in deep mourning for his father and has no leisure yet for the business of state.” We beg you, whose kindness matches the earth itself, to resume the regency you held when crises multiplied.” The glory you brought to the government in those years still fills every corner of the realm with praise.” Not even the mothers of Shang and Zhou outshone your record.” Every voice joined in pleading; high and low look to you as they would to the sun.” The Book of Changes honors adapting to the hour; guarding the dynasty is the duty of those who lead.” Take the reins again, set policy to rights, comfort the ancestral spirits, and give the people peace.” We speak from urgent, heartfelt concern for the realm.” She answered: “The dynasty’s misfortunes are not yet over.” Your words add fresh sorrow to all I bear.” He is young and still wrapped in filial grief; someone must speak in his name.” If returning to the regency steadies the state, I will not refuse—I accept.” Where I am blind, rely on you to counsel and correct me.” With that she resumed the regency. She died in 384 in Xianyang Hall, aged sixty-one, after forty years at the center of power. For Emperor Xiaowu she was a cousin’s widow, and ministers quarreled over what mourning he owed. Xu Zao of the National University cited the canon: reverence for father matches reverence for ruler. The ritual also says that when a husband stands in the place of a father to you, his wife stands as a mother—by the same token, a consort who acts as sovereign demands the mourning due a mother.” Therefore one wears the graded hemp for a mother toward her.” Lu’s condemnation of improper sacrifice shows how rank must be kept straight.” The emperor already honors Kang, Mu, Ai, and Empress Jing as he would his lords—he cannot treat them as rulers in the temple yet shirk kinship mourning for the woman who stood as his mother in law.” He should observe one year’s hemmed sackcloth.” The court agreed.

Empress He the Solemn and Illustrious, personal name Fanni, came from Qian in Lujiang. Her father was He Zhun, recorded among the consort kin. She entered the selection as a daughter of a great clan. In 357 an imperial rescript addressed He Qi: marriage orders society and sustains the sacrifices to Heaven, earth, and ancestors.” After consulting the ministers, all agreed the ancient betrothal rites should be followed.” He therefore sent Wang Biaozhi and Wang Zong, with credentials, to perform the betrothal ceremony.” He Qi answered with the prescribed self-abasing formula accepting the imperial command.” He thanked the throne for deigning to seek a bride among his kin.” He offered his cousin’s daughter, the child left by the late He Zhun, still untrained but presentable.” He pledged to observe every detail of ritual.” The court then sent Sima Xi and Wang Qia to invest her as empress with the formal patent.”

When he took the throne, she became empress. She died in 371 and was buried at Jingping Mausoleum. When he was demoted to Prince of Haixi, she was stripped of her rank and titled merely his consort. 簡文宣鄭太后
Dowager Zheng of Jianwen and the Exalted
30
簡文宣鄭太后諱阿春，河南滎陽人也。 世為冠族。 祖合，臨濟令。 父愷，字祖元，安豐太守。
Dowager Zheng, personal name Achun, came from Xingyang in Henan. Her family had been eminent for generations. Her grandfather Zheng He had served as magistrate of Linji. Her father Zheng Kai, courtesy Zuyuan, was governor of Anfeng.
31
后少孤，無兄弟，唯姊妹四人，后最長。 先適渤海田氏，生一男而寡，依于舅濮陽吳氏。 元帝為丞相，敬后先崩，將納吳氏女為夫人。 后及吳氏女並游後園，或見之，言於帝曰：「鄭氏女雖嫠，賢於吳氏遠矣。 」建武元年，納為琅邪王夫人，甚有寵。 后雖貴幸，而恒有憂色。 帝問其故，對曰：「妾有妹，中者已適長沙王褒，餘二妹未有所適，恐姊為人妾，無復求者。 」帝因從容謂劉隗曰：「鄭氏二妹，卿可為求佳對，使不失舊。 」隗舉其從子傭娶第三者，以小者適漢中李氏，皆得舊門。 帝召王褒為尚書郎，以悅后意。 后生琅邪悼王、簡文帝、尋陽公主。 帝稱尊號，后雖為夫人，詔太子及東海、武陵王皆母事之。 帝崩，后稱建平國夫人。
She lost her parents early, had no brothers, and was the eldest of four sisters. Her first marriage, to a man of Bohai’s Tian family, left her a widow with one son; she then lived with her uncle’s household in Puyang. While still Prince of Langya and serving as chancellor, he had lost Empress Yu; he planned to take a Wu daughter as his consort. Lady Zheng and the Wu maiden toured the rear garden together; an observer told him the widowed Zheng lady far outshone his intended bride.” In 317 he took her as his consort instead and favored her deeply. Even at the height of favor she often looked troubled. Asked why, she said her middle sister was already wife to Sima Bao, Prince of Changsha, while the other two were still unwed; she worried that because she herself was a concubine, no one would ask for them.” The prince quietly asked Liu Wei to find honorable husbands for the two younger girls.” Liu Wei gave his nephew Yong the third sister and married the youngest into the Li family of Hanzhong—both matches kept the girls within the elite.” To please her, he gave Wang Bao a post among the Masters of Writing secretaries. She gave birth to the Prince of Langya who died young, to Emperor Jianwen, and to the Princess of Xunyang. Once he took the throne, she remained a consort in rank, yet he commanded the heir apparent and the eastern princes to honor her as they would a mother. After his death she was known as Lady of the Jianping fief.

咸和元年薨，簡文帝時為琅邪王，制服重。 有司以王出繼，宜降所生，國臣不能匡正，奏免國相諸葛頤。 王上疏曰：「亡母生臨臣國，沒留國第，臣雖出後，亦無所厭，則私情得敘。 昔敬后崩，孝王已出繼，亦還服重。 此則明比，臣所憲章也。 」明穆皇后不奪其志，乃徙琅邪王為會稽王，追號后曰會稽太妃。 及簡文帝即位，未及追尊。 臨崩，封皇子道子為琅邪王，領會稽國，奉太妃祀。
She died in 326 while Sima Yu was still Prince of Langya; he observed the full mourning grade. Officials argued that as an adopted heir he should lessen mourning for his natural mother; blaming the princely household staff, they impeached minister Zhuge Yi. He replied: “My mother lived and died within my princedom; though I was posthumously adopted out, I still owe her full grief.” When Empress Jing passed away, Prince Xiao had likewise been adopted elsewhere, yet he still came back to mourn her fully. That precedent is plain, and it is the rule I follow. Empress Yu the Solemn accepted his plea: Sima Yu became Prince of Kuaiji, and Lady Zheng was honored posthumously as Grand Consort of Kuaiji. Emperor Jianwen had no chance to elevate her title before other business crowded in. Dying, he made Sima Daozi Prince of Langya with Kuaiji income to maintain her shrine.

太元十九年，孝武帝下詔曰：「會稽太妃文母之德，徽音有融，誕載聖明，光延于晉。 先帝追尊聖善，朝議不一，道以疑屈。 朕述遵先志，常惕于心。 今仰奉遺旨，依陽秋二漢孝懷皇帝故事，上太妃尊號曰簡文太后。 」[8]于是立廟于太廟路西，陵曰嘉平。 時群臣希旨，多謂鄭太后應配食于元帝者。 帝以問太子前率徐邈，邈曰：「臣案陽秋之義，母以子貴。 魯隱尊桓母，別考仲子之宮而不配食于惠廟。 又平素之時，不伉儷于先帝，至于子孫，豈可為祖考立配？ 其崇尊盡禮，由於臣子，故得稱太后，陵廟備典。 若乃祔葬配食，則義所不可。 」從之。
In 394 Emperor Xiaowu proclaimed Lady Zheng’s motherly virtue and her role in rearing Jianwen. Jianwen had tried to honor her, but ministers disagreed and the matter stalled. I mean to finish what he began and have long felt uneasy about the delay. He cited Han and chronicle precedent and raised her to Dowager Empress Jianwen. A shrine went up west of the imperial temple; she was buried at Jiaping Mausoleum. Flatterers urged that she share Emperor Yuan’s altar in the ancestral temple. Xu Miao told him the classic rule: a woman’s rank rises with her son. Lu’s Duke Yin built a side shrine for Lady Zhongzi instead of seating her beside Duke Hui. Lady Zheng had never been Yuan’s principal wife; she could not be retrofitted as his temple consort. Her titles and tomb were gifts of a grateful son, which is why she could be called dowager at all. Joint burial beside Yuan, however, was ritually improper. The throne accepted his reasoning.

Lady Li Lingrong, mother of Emperor Xiaowu, began life in the lowest palace ranks. As Prince of Kuaiji he had three boys, none of whom survived. After one heir was cast aside and another prince died in youth, his concubines bore no children for almost a decade. Hu Qian divined that a palace woman would bear two sons, one destined to revive the dynasty. Lady Xu had already given him the Princess of Xin’an and was his favorite. He longed for a child from her, but year after year she conceived nothing. The Daoist Xu Mai was widely rumored to possess transcendental arts. Xu Mai admitted he was no thaumaturge. He urged the prince to trust Hu Qian’s advice and cast his net wider among the women. Jianwen agreed and broadened the search. Years later, still childless, he lined up every favorite and then every maid for a face-reader; none fit the omen until the very last ranks. Lady Li worked in the textile ward—tall and dark-skinned, the others nicknamed her “Kunlun.” The reader took one look and cried, “Here she is.” For the sake of the succession he called her to his couch. She dreamed of dragons cradling her knees and heavenly bodies in her womb; when she told the others, Jianwen listened—and she bore Xiaowu, Sima Daozi, and the long princess of Poyang.

Empress Chu the Reverent and Thoughtful
47
史臣曰：方祇體安，儷乾儀而合德； 圓舒循晷，配羲曜以齊明。 故知陽爍陰凝，萬物假其陶鑄； 火炎水潤，六氣由其調理。 取譬賢淑，作伉文思，靈根式固，實資於此。 宣穆閱禮，偶德潛鱗，翊天造之艱虞，嗣塗山之逸響，寶運歸其後胤，蓋有母儀之助焉。 武元楊氏預聞朝政，明不逮遠，愛溺私情，深杜衛瓘之言，不曉張泓之詐，運其陰沴，韜映乾明，晉道中微，基于是矣。 惠皇稟質，王縱其嚚，識暗鳴蛙，智昏文蛤。 南風肆狡，扇禍稽天。 初踐椒宮，逞梟心于長樂； 方觀梓樹，頒鴆羽於離明。 褒后滅周，方之蓋小； 妹妃傾夏，曾何足喻。 中原陷於鳴鏑，其兆彰於此焉。 昔者高宗諒闇，總百官於元老； 成王沖眇，託萬機於上公。 太后御宸，諒知非古。 而明穆、康獻，仍世臨朝，時屬委裘，躬行負扆。 各免華陽之釁，竟躡和熹之蹤，保陵遲以克終，所幸實為多矣。
The annalist writes: earth rests square and mates with heaven’s design, sharing one virtue. Moon and sun trace each other’s course and light the cosmos together. From yang’s fire and yin’s deep all things take shape; as flame and flood temper the six seasonal breaths. Wise wives mirror heaven’s pairing; the dynasty’s taproot holds fast because of them. Zhang the August and Solemn walked in rites beside a rising dragon, endured the founding’s perils, and left a line that inherited the throne—such is the power of true motherhood. Empress Yang meddled in Emperor Wu’s court: blinded by favor, she silenced Wei Guan and missed Zhang Hong’s plot, letting dark intrigue eclipse the throne—the mid-Jin slump began with her. Emperor Hui was a weak vessel; Empress Jia stoked his cruelty until he could neither see nor think straight. Jia Nanfeng’s malice fanned disaster across the realm. From the harem she nursed a predator’s heart and struck first at Changle Palace; then, beside the heir’s courtyard, she dealt poison in broad daylight. Baosi’s ruin of Zhou pales beside this; Moxi’s toppling of Xia is hardly a closer match. The heartland’s fall to steppe armies was foreshadowed in these palace crimes. High Shang’s king mourned in silence while veterans ran the ministries; the boy King Cheng gave every thread of policy to the Duke of Zhou. Women on the throne was never classical practice—yet it happened. Still Empresses Yu and Chu ruled in succession when the boy emperors could not, each holding the regency in person. They skirted the worst coups, followed Han’s able dowagers, and brought unstable times to a peaceful end—no small mercy.
48
贊曰：二妃光舜，三母翼周。 末升夷癸，褒進亡幽。 家邦興滅，職此之由。 穆后沈斷，忘情執爨。 故劍辭恩，池蒲起歎。 崇化繁祉，肇基商亂。 二楊繼寵，福極災生。 南風熾虐，國喪身傾。 獻容幸亂，居辱疑榮。 援筆廢主，持尺威帝。 契闊終罹，殷憂以斃。 芬實窈窕，芳菲婉嫕。 呂妾變嬴，黃姬化羋。 石文遠著，金行潛徙。 婦德傾城，迷朱奪紫。
The verdict runs: Shun’s wives brought light; Zhou’s three mothers lifted the throne. The last evils of Xia’s court met Shang’s ruin; Baosi’s favor buried You in darkness. Dynasties rise or fall on choices such as these. Empress Yu Wenjun was resolute: she set private ties aside and kept to humble duty. She remembered the “old sword” and spurned new favorites—hence the poem’s lament. Her virtue brought blessings, yet it also laid the tinder for later chaos. Two Yang consorts rose in turn; fortune peaked—and calamity followed. Nanfeng’s tyranny broke the realm and destroyed herself. Yang Xianrong traded on turmoil, mistaking humiliation for honor. She drafted edicts to unthrone emperors and brandished regalia to cow the throne. Separation and sorrow finished her; anxiety at last took her life. Lady Zuo Fen—slender grace, gentle perfume. Like Lü Zhi swapping the imperial surname or the Mi women shifting Chu’s bloodline, blood and throne mixed. Portents were carved in stone while the Jin “metal” Mandate silently slipped away. A woman’s wiles could shake a capital, swapping rightful rank for usurped purple.

校勘記
Textual notes
50
「安陵縣君」斠注：《御覽·二〇二》引《中興書》「安陵」作「安陽」。 按：晉無安陵縣，而有東安陵縣，疑此當作「安陽縣君」。
A collation note: the Zhongxing shu reads “Anyang” instead of “Anling.” Jin geography had no Anling; editors suspect “Anyang” was intended.
51
「諱陵陽」考異：《宋書·州郡志》杜皇后諱陵，此衍「陽」字。 咸康四年以後諱改宣城之陵陽縣為廣陽，可證后名無「陽」字。
The Song history records her name as Ling, not Lingyang; “yang” is likely a scribal addition. The 338 renaming of Lingyang county proves the extra character is wrong.
52
「及哀帝、海西公之世，太后復臨朝稱制」李校：《哀帝紀》惟興寧二年帝以服藥致疾，崇德太后復臨朝攝政，至《海西公紀》不言有臨朝事，「世」字當是「際」字之誤。
Editorial note: only Emperor Ai’s reign shows a return to regency; the word translated as eras should probably read at the juncture (between the two reigns).
53
「以明尊卑」：《禮志·中》、《宋志·二》、《通典·八〇》、《通考·一二一》「尊卑」皆作「尊尊」，乃用穀梁傳文公二年「君子不以親親害尊尊」之義。 徐藻、徐邈父子治穀梁，此作「尊尊」為是。
Variant: other sources read zunzun (priority of public rank) per the Guliang commentary. Because the Xu family specialized in Guliang, the zunzun reading is preferred.
54
元興三年崩：「元興」原作「永興」。 商榷：「永興」當作「元興」，自穆帝升平元年至安帝元興三年，正四十八年。 按：《安紀》、《建康實錄·一〇》皆在元興三年，今據改。
Colophon: the era name was miswritten as Yongxing instead of Yuanxing. Scholars note forty-eight years on the throne matches Yuanxing 3, not Yongxing. Standard histories agree on Yuanxing 3; the edition adopts that date.
55
「太元十一年海西公薨」：「十一年」原作「九年」。 《海西公紀》、《孝武紀》、《建康實錄·九》、《六朝事跡類編》俱作「太元十一年」，今據改。
Colophon: Taiyuan 11 was misprinted as Taiyuan 9. Parallel sources unanimously give Taiyuan 11.
56
「簡文太后」斠注：當從《孝武紀》作「簡文宣太后」。 按：《冊府·二九》亦有「宣」字。
Title should include “Xuan” as in the Xiaowu annals. The Cefu yuangui preserves the “Xuan” character as well.
57
「孔安國」：據《孔安國傳》，安國時為領軍，「孔安國」上疑脫「領軍」二字。
Kong Anguo’s entry suggests the title Commander of the Guards probably dropped out before his name.
