曹志
Cao Zhi
2
曹志，字允恭，譙國譙人，魏陳思王植之孽子也。 少好學，以才行稱，夷簡有大度，兼善騎射。 植曰：「此保家主也。 」立以為嗣。 後改封濟北王。 武帝為撫軍將軍，迎陳留王於鄴，志夜謁見，帝與語，自暮達旦，甚奇之。 及帝受禪，降為鄄城縣公。 詔曰：「昔在前世，雖曆運迭興，至於先代苗裔，傳祚不替，或列籓九服，式序王官。 選眾命賢，惟德是與，蓋至公之道也。 魏氏諸王公養德藏器，壅滯曠久，前雖有詔，當須簡授，而自頃眾職少缺，未得式敘。 前濟北王曹志履德清純，才高行潔，好古博物，為魏宗英，朕甚嘉之。 其以志為樂平太守。 」志在郡上書，以為宜尊儒重道，請為博士置吏卒。 遷章武、趙郡太守。 雖累郡職，不以政事為意，晝則遊獵，夜誦《詩》《書》，以聲色自娛，當時見者未能審其量也。
Cao Zhi, style Yonggong, came from Qiao in the kingdom of Qiao. He was a younger son born to a concubine of Wei’s Prince of Chensi, the celebrated poet whose personal name was homophonous with his son’s but written with a different graph. From boyhood he loved study and was esteemed for ability and character: open, unpretentious, and large-minded, and a capable horseman and archer as well. His father the prince said, “This is the son who will keep our house secure.” He was named heir. Later he was transferred to the title of Prince of Jibei. While Emperor Wu still held the post of General Who Pacifies the Army and had gone to Ye to welcome the Prince of Chenliu, Cao Zhi sought a night audience. They talked from evening until dawn, and the future emperor was deeply impressed. After the abdication he was reduced in rank to Duke of Juancheng. An edict declared: “Under earlier dynasties, even as Heaven’s mandate shifted from house to house, scions of fallen lines often kept their titles, whether as feudal lords across the realm or as officers in the royal administration.” To elevate the worthy from among the many and bind oneself to nothing but merit is the path of perfect fairness.” Wei’s imperial clansmen have long kept their abilities in reserve while appointments were blocked. Earlier orders called for careful selection, yet many offices still stand thinly staffed, and proper order in ennoblement has not been achieved. The former Prince of Jibei, Cao Zhi, walks a path of unstained integrity, combines high talent with spotless conduct, and cherishes the classical tradition—an ornament of the Wei bloodline whom I hold in high regard. Let Cao Zhi be appointed Administrator of Leping.” While serving in the commandery he petitioned the throne to exalt Confucian learning and the Way, and asked that the court assign attendants to the academicians.” He was promoted in turn to Administrator of Zhangwu and of Zhao. Though he served several terms as a commandery governor, he showed little interest in routine administration: days in the hunt, evenings with the *Classic of Poetry* and the *Book of Documents*, and his own pleasures in music and company—so contemporaries mistook him for a man of no depth.
3
咸甯初，詔曰：「鄄城公曹志，篤行履素，達學通識，宜在儒林，以弘胄子之教。 其以志為散騎常侍、國子博士。 」帝嘗閱《六代論》，問志曰：「是卿先王所作邪？ 」志對曰：「先王有手所作目錄，請歸尋按。 」還奏曰：「按錄無此。 」帝曰：「誰作？ 」志曰：「以臣所聞，是臣族父冏所作。 以先王文高名著，欲令書傳於後，是以假託。 」帝曰：「古來亦多有是。 」顧謂公卿曰：「父子證明，足以為審。 自今已後，可無復疑。」
Early in the Xianning era (275–280) an edict read: “Duke of Juancheng Cao Zhi lives plainly and studies widely; he belongs among Confucian scholars to broaden the education of the heir-apparent’s companions.” Appoint him Cavalier Attendant-in-ordinary and Erudit of the National University.” The emperor once read the *Treatise on the Six Dynasties* and asked Cao Zhi, “Did your royal father write this?” Cao Zhi answered, “My father kept a handwritten catalogue of his works. Allow me to go home and check it.” He returned and reported, “That piece is not listed among his papers.” The emperor asked, “Then who wrote it?” Cao Zhi said, “From what I have learned, it was the work of my kinsman Cao Jiong.” Because my father’s literary fame stood so high, Cao Jiong attached the essay to his name so that it might survive.” The emperor replied, “Such pious forgeries are nothing new in history.” Turning to the high ministers he added, “When father and son agree like this, the matter is settled.” Let no one doubt it again.”
4
後遷祭酒。 齊王攸將之國，下太常議崇錫文物。 時博士秦秀等以為齊王宜內匡朝政，不可之籓。 志又常恨其父不得志于魏，因愴然歎曰：「安有如此之才，如此之親，不得樹本助化，而遠出海隅？ 晉朝之隆，其殆乎哉！ 」乃奏議曰：「伏聞大司馬齊王當出籓東夏，備物盡禮，同之二伯。 今陛下為聖君，稷、契為賢臣，內有魯、衛之親，外有齊、晉之輔，坐而守安，此萬世之基也。 古之夾輔王室，同姓則周公其人也，異姓則太公其人也，皆身在內，五世反葬。 後雖有五霸代興，桓、文譎主，下有請隧之僭，上有九錫之禮，終於譎而不正，驗於尾大不掉，豈與召公之歌《棠棣》，周詩之詠《鴟鴞》同日論哉！ 今聖朝創業之始，始之不諒，後事難工。 幹植不強，枝葉不茂； 骨骾不存，皮膚不充。 自羲皇以來，豈是一姓之獨有！ 欲結其心者，當有磐石之固。 夫欲享萬世之利者，當與天下議之。 故天之聰明，自我人之聰明。 秦、魏欲獨擅其威，而財得沒其身； 周、漢能分其利，而親疏為之用。 此自聖主之深慮，日月之所照。 事雖淺，當深謀之； 言雖輕，當重思之。 志備位儒官，若言不及禮，是志寇竊。 知忠不言，議所不敢。 志以為當如博士等議。 」議成當上，見其從弟高邑公嘉。 嘉曰：「兄議甚切，百年之後必書晉史，目下將見責邪。 」帝覽議，大怒曰：「曹志尚不明吾心，況四海乎！ 」以議者不指答所問，橫造異論，策免太常鄭默。 於是有司奏收志等結罪，詔惟免志官，以公還第，其餘皆付廷尉。
He was later promoted to Libationer of the National University. When Prince Sima You of Qi was ordered to his fief, the court instructed the Ministry of Ceremonies to debate what honors and ritual gear should attend his departure. Erudits such as Qin Xiu argued that the prince ought to remain at court to steady the government rather than leave for his appanage. Cao Zhi still brooded over how his father had never won his way under Wei. He once said in anguish, “How could a man of such gifts and such nearness to the throne be denied a role at the heart of government, and instead be packed off to the empire’s edge?” If that is how Jin treats its own, what hope is there for the dynasty’s greatness!” He therefore drafted a memorial: “I learn that the Grand Marshal, Prince of Qi, is to take up his fief in the east with every ceremonial honor, as though he were one of the ancient ‘two elders’ who aided the Son of Heaven.” Your Majesty is a sage ruler served by men worthy to be called Ji and Xie; within the palace you have the bone-ties of Lu and Wei, beyond it ministers strong as Qi and Jin once were. Peace kept from the capital is a foundation meant to last ages.” Those who steadied the Zhou house—the Duke of Zhou among kin, the Grand Duke of Qi among outsiders—stayed at court, and even after five generations their bones came home to the royal domain for burial.” Later ages produced the Five Hegemons: rulers like Duke Huan and Duke Wen who ruled by guile, vassals who dared ask for royal funeral rites, and courts that piled the nine insignia on a subject—power that grew too heavy to control. That world has nothing in common with the Duke of Shao’s hymn to brotherhood in the *Book of Odes*, or with the poem that warns a young king to mind his kin.” At the founding of a dynasty, what is done amiss at the outset cannot easily be set right afterward.” A weak trunk cannot bear lush branches.” Strip the bone from the body and the skin has nothing to cling to.” Since high antiquity no single lineage has monopolized Heaven’s favor forever.” If you would win men’s loyalty, your foundations must be rock-solid.” A ruler who means to pass down lasting good must take counsel with the realm itself.” Heaven hears and sees through the ears and eyes of the people.” Qin and Cao Wei tried to hoard all authority and perished with it.” Zhou and Han prospered by sharing power so that both kin and ministers could serve the throne.” That is the far-sighted policy of a true king, plain as sunlight.” Even a small matter deserves deep thought.” Even casual words should be weighed with care.” I hold a scholar’s title: if I stay silent on a question of ritual, I am nothing but a thief of office.” To see what is right and hold my tongue is what I dare not do.” I believe Your Majesty should accept the erudits’ opinion.” When the draft was finished and ready for the throne, he met his cousin Cao Jia, Duke of Gaoyi.” Cao Jia warned him, “Brother, this memorial cuts deep. Future historians will praise you, but what about the emperor’s anger today?” When the emperor read it, he flew into a rage. “If Cao Zhi cannot read my mind,” he cried, “what can I expect of the rest of the realm?” Because the memorialists had ignored the question put to them and veered into dissent, he removed Minister of Ceremonies Zheng Mo by edict.” Officials asked to arrest Cao Zhi and his allies on conspiracy charges. The emperor stripped only Cao Zhi of office and sent him home as a duke, while the others were remanded to the commandant of justice.”
5
頃之，志復為散騎常侍。 遭母憂，居喪過禮，因此篤病，喜怒失常。 九年卒，太常奏以惡諡。 崔褒歎曰：「魏顆不從亂，以病為亂故也。 今諡曹志而諡其病，豈謂其病不為亂乎！ 」於是諡為定。
Soon afterward Cao Zhi was reappointed Cavalier Attendant-in-ordinary. His mother’s death left him mourning beyond the prescribed rites; he fell gravely ill and lost control of his moods. He died in the ninth year of the era (probably 288). The Ministry of Ceremonies proposed a harsh posthumous name. Cui Bao objected: “Wei Ke earned a fair posthumous title because his disobedience had not been mutiny but illness mistaken for mutiny.” If you name Cao Zhi’s sickness ‘rebellion,’ are you saying his sickness was not a kind of rebellion against good order?” The court therefore settled on the posthumous name “Ding” (“the Determined”).”

庾峻
Yu Jun
7
庾峻，字山甫，潁川鄢陵人也。 祖乘，才學洽聞，漢司徒辟，有道征，皆不就。 伯父嶷，中正簡素，仕魏為太僕。 父道，廉退貞固，養志不仕。 牛馬有踶齧者，恐傷人，不貨於市。 及諸子貴，賜拜太中大夫。 峻少好學，有才思。 嘗游京師，聞魏散騎常侍蘇林老疾在家，往候之。 林嘗就乘學，見峻流涕，良久曰：「尊祖高才而性退讓，慈和泛愛，清靜寡欲，不營當世，惟修德行而已。 鄢陵舊五六萬戶，聞今裁有數百。 君二父孩抱經亂，獨至今日，尊伯為當世令器，君兄弟復俊茂，此尊祖積德之所由也。」
Yu Jun, style Shanfu, was a native of Yanling in Yingchuan commandery. His grandfather Yu Cheng was learned and widely read. The Han court summoned him as Minister of Education and again as a man of recognized virtue; he declined both calls. His uncle Yu Yi, a plain and upright local evaluator, served Wei as Grand Coachman. His father Yu Dao lived modestly and refused every office, intent on cultivating his own integrity. He would not sell at market any ox or horse known to kick or bite, lest it harm someone. When his sons rose to high rank, the court honored him with the title of Grand Palace Grandee. Yu Jun loved learning from youth and showed literary talent. On a visit to Luoyang he heard that the Wei scholar Su Lin lay old and sick at home, and called on him. Su Lin had once studied under Yu Cheng. Seeing Yu Jun, he wept and said at length, “Your grandfather was a man of towering gifts yet humble temper—kind, generous, quiet, and free of ambition. He never angled for place; he only perfected his character.” Yanling once counted fifty or sixty thousand households; today barely a few hundred remain.” Your two fathers were carried as infants through the wars yet lived to see this day; your uncle is one of the age’s finest men; you and your brothers shine with promise—this is the harvest of your grandfather’s hidden virtue.”
8
曆郡功曹，舉計掾，州辟從事。 太常鄭袤見峻，大奇之，舉為博士。 時重《莊》《老》而輕《經》《史》，駿懼雅道陵遲，乃潛心儒典。 屬高貴鄉公幸太學，問《尚書》義于峻，峻援引師說，發明經旨，申暢疑滯，對答詳悉。 遷秘書丞。 長安有大獄，久不決，拜峻侍御史，往斷之，朝野稱允。 武帝踐阼，賜爵關中侯，遷司空長史，轉秘書監、御史中丞，拜侍中，加諫議大夫。 常侍帝講《詩》，中庶子何劭論《風》《雅》正變之義，峻起難往反，四坐莫能屈之。
He served as commandery merit assessor, was recommended as accounting clerk for the capital report, and was hired as a provincial retainer. Zheng Mao, Minister of Ceremonies, was so struck by him that he nominated Yu Jun as an erudit. When fashionable opinion exalted Zhuangzi and Laozi at the expense of the classics and histories, Yu Jun feared the loss of true culture and threw himself into Confucian study. When Duke Cao Mao of Gaoxiang visited the National University and questioned him on the *Book of Documents*, Yu Jun drew on his teachers’ glosses, unfolded knotty passages, and answered with meticulous clarity. He was promoted to assistant director of the Palace Library. A major criminal case at Chang’an had dragged on unresolved; Yu Jun was named Attendant Censor and sent to settle it, and both court and commoners approved his judgment. After Emperor Wu took the throne Yu Jun received the rank of Marquis Within the Passes, rose to chief clerk to the Minister of Works, then served as director of the Palace Library and as palace assistant secretary, became a palace attendant, and was given the additional title of Grandee Remonstrant. He regularly joined the emperor’s lectures on the *Classic of Poetry*. When He Shao, the junior mentor to the heir apparent, spoke on “orthodox” and “mutated” poems in the *Airs* and *Elegances*, Yu Jun rose to debate him to and fro, and no one in the hall could get the better of him.

He also despised hollow display and the neglect of genuine reputation, and wrote a lengthy essay against it—the text is omitted here as too long. He died in the ninth year (293). The court sent a full set of court robes, another suit, and three hundred thousand cash for his funeral. On his deathbed he told his son Yu Min to bury him the same evening he died, in plain cloth and headcloth, without waiting to choose a lucky day. Yu Min obeyed: the body was dressed only in everyday clothes. He had two sons, Yu Min and Yu Ai.

Son: Yu Min

=瑉字子琚。 性淳和好學，行己忠恕。 少曆散騎常侍、本國中正、侍中，封長岑男。 懷帝之沒劉元海也，瑉從在平陽。 元海大會，因使帝行酒，瑉不勝悲憤，再拜上酒，因大號哭，賊惡之。 會有告瑉及王儁等謀應劉琨者，元海因圖弑逆，瑉等並遇害。 初，洛陽之未陷也，瑉為侍中，直於省內，謂同僚許遐曰：「世路如此，禍難將及，吾當死乎此屋耳！ 」及是，竟不免焉。 太元末，追諡曰貞。
Yu Min, courtesy name Ziju, was his son. He was gentle, studious, and lived by loyalty and forbearance. While young he served as Cavalier Attendant-in-ordinary, local merit evaluator for his commandery, and palace attendant, and was ennobled as Baron of Changcen. When Emperor Huai fell into the hands of Liu Yuan (Liu Yuanhai), Yu Min followed him to Pingyang. At a banquet Liu Yuan ordered the captive emperor to pour wine for the guests. Yu Min, unable to contain his grief, presented the cup with a formal bow and broke into loud weeping; the conquerors detested him for it. Someone accused Yu Min, Wang Jun, and others of plotting to aid Liu Kun. Liu Yuan seized on the charge to frame them for regicide, and Yu Min was executed with the rest. Before Luoyang fell, Yu Min was on duty in the palace as palace attendant. He told his colleague Xu Xia, “The times are turning this dark—trouble is coming. I expect to die in this very hall!” In the end he could not escape that fate.” Late in the Taiyuan era (376–396) the court posthumously titled him “Zhen” (“the Upright”).

瑉弟敳
Yu Min’s brother, Yu Kai
16
=敳字子嵩。 長不滿七尺，而腰帶十圍，雅有遠韻。 為陳留相，未嘗以事嬰心，從容酣暢，寄通而已。 處眾人中，居然獨立。 嘗讀《老》《莊》，曰：「正與人意暗同。 」太尉王衍雅重之。
Yu Kai, courtesy name Zisong, was his younger brother. He stood under seven feet tall yet girt his waist with ten hand-spans of belt, carrying an air of lofty detachment. As chancellor of Chenliu he never let paperwork fret him; he moved through office in relaxed ease, letting things take their own course. In any crowd he seemed to stand apart, self-contained. Reading the *Laozi* and *Zhuangzi*, he remarked, “These texts voice what we already feel without knowing it.” Grand Commandant Wang Yan held him in the highest regard.
17
敳見王室多難，終知嬰禍，乃著《意賦》以豁情，猶賈誼之《服鳥》也。 其詞曰：「至理歸於渾一兮，榮辱固亦同貫。 存亡既已均齊兮，正盡死復何歎。 物咸定于無初兮，俟時至而後驗。 若四節之素代兮，豈當今之得遠？ 且安有壽之與夭兮，或者情橫多戀。 宗統竟初不別兮，大德亡其情願。 蠢動皆神之為兮，癡聖惟質所建。 真人都遣穢累兮，性茫蕩而無岸。 縱驅於遼廓之庭兮，委體乎寂寥之館。 天地短於朝生兮，億代促於始旦。 顧瞻宇宙微細兮，眇若豪鋒之半。 飄搖玄曠之域兮，深漠暢而靡玩。 兀與自然並體兮，融液忽而四散。 」從子亮見賦，問曰：「若有意也，非賦所盡； 若無意也，復何所賦？ 」答曰：「在有無之間耳！」
Seeing endless trouble for the throne, Yu Kai knew he would be swept up in it, so he wrote the *Rhapsody on Intent* to clear his heart, much as Jia Yi once wrote *The Owl*. It begins: “The highest truth folds back into the undivided whole; honor and disgrace run on the same string.” Life and death already balance one another; when integrity is spent in dying, what is left to lament?” All things rest in a beginningless fixity; only when the moment comes is the pattern proved.” They turn like the four seasons in their steady round—how could the present hour escape that wheel?” Where is the line between a full span and an early end? Passion clings where reason should let go.” Lineage and extinction are one at root; the greatest power forgets what the heart prefers.” Every twitch of life is spirit’s play; fool and sage alike are shaped by the same stuff.” The realized man sheds every stain; his nature is shoreless, open sky.” He ranges the court of boundless space and lodges his body in utter stillness.” Heaven and earth are briefer than the mayfly’s dawn; eons shrink beside the first light.” Look back on the cosmos and it is smaller than half a spearpoint’s gleam.” He drifts the dark expanse, depth without bottom, freedom without a toy to grasp.” He stands with Nature as one body, then melts and scatters like dew on the wind.” His nephew Yu Liang read the piece and asked, “If you really mean this, no poem can say it all; if you do not, why bother writing a rhapsody at all?” Yu Kai answered, “Somewhere between caring and not caring—that is all.”
18
遷吏部郎。 是時天下多故，機變屢起，敳常靜默無為。 參東海王越太傅軍事，轉軍諮祭酒。 時越府多雋異，敳在其中，常自袖手。 豫州牧長史河南郭象善《老》《莊》，時人以為王弼之亞。 敳甚知之，每曰：「郭子玄何必減瘐子嵩」。 象後為太傅主簿，任事專勢。 敳謂象曰：「卿自是當世大才，我疇昔之意都已盡矣。」
He was promoted to director in the Ministry of Personnel. The realm was in constant upheaval and intrigue, yet Yu Kai stayed quiet and let events run their course. He served on the staff of Sima Yue, Prince of the Eastern Seas, as Grand Mentor, then became army advisory libationer. Sima Yue’s headquarters teemed with brilliant men, but Yu Kai usually stood by with his hands tucked in his sleeves. Guo Xiang of Henan, chief clerk on the staff of the governor of Yuzhou, was a master of the *Laozi* and *Zhuangzi* whom contemporaries ranked just below Wang Bi. Yu Kai understood him well and used to say, “Guo Zixuan is nobody’s inferior to Yu Zisong.” Later, as chief clerk to the Grand Mentor, Guo Xiang monopolized influence over every decision. Yu Kai told him, “You are a leading talent of our day; whatever regard I once had for you is spent.”

The court rejected his proposal, yet everyone who heard it shivered.

秀性忌讒佞，疾之如仇，素輕鄙賈充，及伐吳之役，聞其為大都督，謂所親者曰：「充文案小才，乃居伐國大任，吾將哭以送師。 」或止秀曰：「昔蹇叔知秦軍必敗，故哭送其子耳。 今吳君無道，國有自亡之形，群率踐境，將不戰而潰。 子之哭也，既為不智，乃不赦之罪。 」於是乃止。 及孫皓降于王濬，充未之知，方以吳未可平，抗表請班師。 充表與告捷同至，朝野以充位居人上，智出人下，僉以秀為知言。
Qin Xiu detested flatterers and despised Jia Chong. When he learned that Jia had been named grand commander against Wu, he told friends, “That man is a petty clerk, not the leader of a conqueror’s host—I mean to weep the armies on their way as Jian Shu wept for Qin.” Friends urged him, “Jian Shu wept only because he foresaw disaster for his own son’s campaign.” Wu’s tyrant has doomed his state; our hosts already cross his borders and will collapse him without a fight.” Your mourning would be folly and a capital offense.” He gave up the idea. When Sun Hao had already surrendered to Wang Jun, Jia Chong still thought Wu unbeaten and memorialized to recall the army. His recall petition arrived with the victory dispatch, and everyone agreed that Jia Chong outranked all men in office but fell below them in sense—proof that Qin Xiu had spoken true.
42
及充薨，秀議曰：「充舍宗族弗授，而以異姓為後，悖禮溺情，以亂大倫。 昔鄫養外孫莒公子為後，《春秋》書'莒人滅鄫'。 聖人豈不知外孫親邪！ 但以義推之，則無父子耳。 又案詔書'自非功如太宰，始封無後如太宰，所取必己自出如太宰，不得以為比'。 然則以外孫為後，自非元功顯德，不之得也。 天子之禮，蓋可然乎？ 絕父祖之血食，開朝廷之禍門。 《諡法》'昏亂紀度曰荒'，請諡荒公。 」不從。
After Jia Chong’s death Qin Xiu argued, “He passed over his own kin to adopt an outsider as heir—sentiment trumping ritual and overturning the natural order.” When the state of Zeng made a grandson of Ju its heir, the *Spring and Autumn Annals* recorded that ‘the men of Ju destroyed Zeng.’” The sage was hardly blind to affection for a daughter’s son!” Measured by duty, that line severs the tie between father and son.” The edict itself says that unless merit equals the Grand Preceptor’s, unless a first enfeoffment truly lacks heirs, and unless the heir is still one’s own blood—as in the Grand Preceptor’s case—no precedent applies.” Adopting a daughter’s son is lawful only for founders of towering merit.” Can the rites of the Son of Heaven condone this?” It ends the sacrifices to his ancestors and opens the court to future usurpation.” The canon of posthumous names calls deliberate confusion of law ‘reckless’; let him be styled Duke of Recklessness.” The court refused.
43
王濬有平吳之勳，而為王渾所譖毀。 帝雖不從，無明賞罰，以濬為輔國大將軍，天下咸為之怨。 秀乃上言曰：「自大晉啟祚，輔國之號，率以舊恩。 此為王濬無功之時，受九列之顯位，立功之後更得寵人之辱號也。 四海視之，孰不失望！ 蜀小吳大，平蜀之後，二將皆就加三事，今濬還而降等，天下安得不惑乎！ 吳之未亡也，雖以三祖之神武，猶躬受其屈。 以孫皓之虛名，足以驚動諸夏，每一小出，雖聖心知其垂亡，然中國輒懷惶怖。 當爾時，有能借天子百萬之眾，平而有之，與國家結兄弟之交，臣恐朝野實皆甘之耳。 今濬舉蜀、漢之卒，數旬而平吳，雖舉吳人之財寶以與之，本非己分有焉，而遽與計校乎？」
Wang Jun had conquered Wu, yet Wang Hun slandered him. The emperor took no clear stand: he named Wang Jun General Who Aids the State, a title the realm read as insult, not reward. Qin Xiu therefore wrote, “Since our dynasty began, the title General Who Aids the State has gone to men honored for past favors, not for fresh merit.” Wang Jun already held one of the nine top posts before he won any glory; to give him, after victory, the same empty title reserved for court favorites adds insult to his labors.” The whole empire sees it—who would not feel betrayed?” Shu was the lesser foe and Wu the greater: after Shu fell, both generals who took part were raised to the three dukedoms, yet Wang Jun comes home from the greater conquest with a demotion—how can the realm not call that madness?” While Wu still stood, even our dynasty’s three martial founders bowed to its strength in person.” Sun Hao’s hollow prestige still shook the Central Plains: every minor sortie left the court, though convinced he was doomed, braced in dread.” Had anyone then been able to borrow the imperial armies, crush Wu, keep it, and treat the throne as an ally, I suspect every man in and out of court would have welcomed the bargain.” Wang Jun led the veterans of Shu and conquered Wu in weeks. You could heap on him every treasure Wu owned—none of it would be more than his due—yet you haggle over his reward?”
44
後與劉暾等同議齊王攸事，忤旨，除名。 尋復起為博士。 秀性悻直，與物多忤。 為博士前後垂二十年，卒於官。
Later he joined Liu Tun and others in debating Prince Sima You’s case, crossed the emperor, and was dismissed from the rolls. Soon he was recalled as an erudit. Qin Xiu was blunt and uncompromising and often collided with the world. He spent almost twenty years as an erudit and died in that post.
45
【史評】
Historians’ commentary
46
史臣曰：齊獻王以明德茂親，經邦論道，允厘庶績，式敘彝倫。 武帝納奸諂之邪謀，懷紹終之遠慮，遂乃君茲青土，作牧東籓。 遠邇驚嗟，朝野失望。 曹志等服膺教義，方軌儒門，蹇蹇匪躬，慺慺體國。 故能抗言鳳闕，忤犯龍鱗，身雖暫屈，道亦弘矣！ 庾氏世載清德，見稱於世，汝潁之多奇士，斯焉取斯。 謀甫素疾佞邪，而發因醉飽，投鼠忌器，豈易由言。 竊人之財，猶謂之盜，子玄假譽攘善，將非盜乎！
The historians write: Prince Sima You of Qi, a kinsman of shining virtue, set policy for the realm, brought order to every branch of government, and gave shape to moral relations. Emperor Wu listened to sycophants, brooded on securing his line, and sent that prince to rule the green fields of Qi as shepherd of the eastern marches. Near and far were stunned; court and country alike despaired. Cao Zhi and his fellows took Confucian teaching to heart, walked the scholar’s path, risked themselves without flinching, and burned for the dynasty’s good. So they could speak truth in the phoenix halls and stroke the dragon the wrong way: though their careers suffered for it, the Way they served grew only greater.” The Yu house bred generation on generation of unstained honor and produced the sort of rare talent the Run-Ying country was famous for.” Yu Chun always despised sycophants but burst out in his cups—one fears to strike the rat beside the priceless vase, and frank speech is never easy.” To steal another’s goods is theft: Guo Xiang stole another’s reputation—is that not theft as well?”
47
贊曰：魏氏維城，濟北知名。 潁川多士，峻亦飛英。 長岑徇義，祭酒遺榮。 謀甫三爵，酗醟斯作。 象既攘善，秀惟癉惡。 旉獻嘉謀，幾趨鼎鑊。
Encomium: As bulwark of the house of Wei, the Prince of Jibei won lasting fame. Yingchuan bred many great men, and Yu Jun rose among them. The Baron of Changcen died for loyalty; the Libationer turned his back on empty glory. Yu Moufu’s third cup loosed the drunken brawl. Guo Xiang stole credit; Qin Xiu lashed out at vice. Yu Bin offered wise counsel and nearly landed in the execution pot.
