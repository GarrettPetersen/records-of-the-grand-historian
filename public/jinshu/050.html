Cao Zhi
2
曹志，字允恭，譙國譙人，魏陳思王植之孽子也。 少好學，以才行稱，夷簡有大度，兼善騎射。 植曰：「此保家主也。 」立以為嗣。 後改封濟北王。 武帝為撫軍將軍，迎陳留王於鄴，志夜謁見，帝與語，自暮達旦，甚奇之。 及帝受禪，降為鄄城縣公。 詔曰：「昔在前世，雖曆運迭興，至於先代苗裔，傳祚不替，或列籓九服，式序王官。 選眾命賢，惟德是與，蓋至公之道也。 魏氏諸王公養德藏器，壅滯曠久，前雖有詔，當須簡授，而自頃眾職少缺，未得式敘。 前濟北王曹志履德清純，才高行潔，好古博物，為魏宗英，朕甚嘉之。 其以志為樂平太守。 」志在郡上書，以為宜尊儒重道，請為博士置吏卒。 遷章武、趙郡太守。 雖累郡職，不以政事為意，晝則遊獵，夜誦《詩》《書》，以聲色自娛，當時見者未能審其量也。
Cao Zhi, courtesy name Yunggong, came from Qiao in Qiao commandery. He was a younger son of Cao Zhi, the Wei prince known as the Prince of Chensi. From boyhood he loved study and won praise for ability and character—open and even-tempered, broad-minded, and adept both on horseback and with the bow. His father Cao Zhi said: "This boy will be the one to safeguard our house." He was therefore named heir. Later his title was changed to Prince of Jibei. While Emperor Wu still held the post of General Who Upholds the Army and had gone to Ye to welcome the Prince of Chenliu, Cao Zhi sought a nighttime audience. The two talked from dusk until dawn, and the emperor was deeply impressed. After the abdication he was demoted to Duke of Juancheng county. An edict declared: "In ages past, though fate turned and dynasties rose and fell in succession, scions of earlier ruling houses often kept their lines unbroken—some held fiefs across the realm, others held titled posts at court. To elevate the worthy from among the many and grant office solely on grounds of virtue—that is the principle of perfect fairness." The Wei imperial princes have long kept their talents in reserve while blocked from advancement. Though an earlier edict promised they would be chosen for posts when appropriate, vacancies have lately been scarce and orderly promotions have still not gone forward. The former Prince of Jibei, Cao Zhi, embodies unsullied virtue, combines rare talent with impeccable conduct, and delights in antiquity and wide learning—truly a distinguished member of the Wei house. I applaud him warmly. Let Cao Zhi be appointed governor of Leping. Once in office he memorialized the throne, arguing that Confucian scholarship and the moral Way deserved greater emphasis, and asking that each academic erudite be allotted clerks and attendants. He was promoted to governor successively at Zhangwu and Zhao commandery. Though he served in several commanderies in succession, he showed little zeal for paperwork—hunting by day, reading the "Poetry" and "Documents" by night, and diverting himself with music and company—so contemporaries could not yet gauge his true depth.

咸甯初，詔曰：「鄄城公曹志，篤行履素，達學通識，宜在儒林，以弘胄子之教。 其以志為散騎常侍、國子博士。 」帝嘗閱《六代論》，問志曰：「是卿先王所作邪？ 」志對曰：「先王有手所作目錄，請歸尋按。 」還奏曰：「按錄無此。 」帝曰：「誰作？ 」志曰：「以臣所聞，是臣族父冏所作。 以先王文高名著，欲令書傳於後，是以假託。 」帝曰：「古來亦多有是。 」顧謂公卿曰：「父子證明，足以為審。 自今已後，可無復疑。」
Early in the Xianning era an edict ran: "The Duke of Juancheng, Cao Zhi, leads an austere life of steadfast principle, with broad erudition and judgment. He belongs among classical scholars to strengthen the education of young nobles at school. Appoint him Palace Attendant-in-Ordinary and Erudite of the Imperial Academy." ": The emperor once read the "Treatise on the Six Ages" and asked Cao Zhi, "Did your late royal father write this? ": Cao Zhi answered, "My father kept a handwritten catalogue of his own works. Permit me to go home and check it. ": He reported back: "It does not appear on his list. ": "Then who wrote it?" ": "From what I have learned, my clansman Cao Jiong composed it. Because my late father's literary reputation stood so high, he wanted the essay to survive under that name, so he lent him the authorship. ": "Such borrowing of a famous name has plenty of precedent in history. ": Turning to the high ministers he added, "Father and son have both spoken—there is enough here to settle the matter. Hereafter no one should doubt it again.
4
後遷祭酒。 齊王攸將之國，下太常議崇錫文物。 時博士秦秀等以為齊王宜內匡朝政，不可之籓。 志又常恨其父不得志于魏，因愴然歎曰：「安有如此之才，如此之親，不得樹本助化，而遠出海隅？ 晉朝之隆，其殆乎哉！ 」乃奏議曰：「伏聞大司馬齊王當出籓東夏，備物盡禮，同之二伯。 今陛下為聖君，稷、契為賢臣，內有魯、衛之親，外有齊、晉之輔，坐而守安，此萬世之基也。 古之夾輔王室，同姓則周公其人也，異姓則太公其人也，皆身在內，五世反葬。 後雖有五霸代興，桓、文譎主，下有請隧之僭，上有九錫之禮，終於譎而不正，驗於尾大不掉，豈與召公之歌《棠棣》，周詩之詠《鴟鴞》同日論哉！ 今聖朝創業之始，始之不諒，後事難工。 幹植不強，枝葉不茂； 骨骾不存，皮膚不充。 自羲皇以來，豈是一姓之獨有！ 欲結其心者，當有磐石之固。 夫欲享萬世之利者，當與天下議之。 故天之聰明，自我人之聰明。 秦、魏欲獨擅其威，而財得沒其身； 周、漢能分其利，而親疏為之用。 此自聖主之深慮，日月之所照。 事雖淺，當深謀之； 言雖輕，當重思之。 志備位儒官，若言不及禮，是志寇竊。 知忠不言，議所不敢。 志以為當如博士等議。 」議成當上，見其從弟高邑公嘉。 嘉曰：「兄議甚切，百年之後必書晉史，目下將見責邪。 」帝覽議，大怒曰：「曹志尚不明吾心，況四海乎！ 」以議者不指答所問，橫造異論，策免太常鄭默。 於是有司奏收志等結罪，詔惟免志官，以公還第，其餘皆付廷尉。
He was later promoted to Libationer of the Imperial Academy. When Prince You of Qi was ordered to his princedom, the court instructed the Minister of Ceremonies to decide what honors and insignia should attend his departure. Erudite Qin Xiu and others argued that the prince ought to remain at court to steady the government rather than depart for his territorial seat. Cao Zhi still smarted over how his own father had been denied his due under Wei. He sighed in grief: "How can a man of such gifts and such royal kinship be barred from strengthening the dynasty at its core and sent instead to the rim of the empire? If this is how Jin treats its own, what hope has the dynasty of ever prospering? ": He therefore submitted a memorial: "I understand that the Prince of Qi, in his capacity as Grand Marshal, is being dispatched to his eastern princedom with every ritual honor due to the ancient archetypes of the two elder guardians of state. Your Majesty is a sage ruler served by ministers worthy of Ji and Xie; within the palace you have the intimacy of Lu and Wei princes, beyond it the bulwark once provided by Qi and Jin. Secure the realm from the center and you lay the foundation for ages to come. Those who propped up the house of Zhou—same-clan kin like the Duke of Zhou, affinal ministers like the Grand Duke Jiang—all remained at the capital, generation after generation, to be buried beside their kings. Later hegemons such as Huan of Qi and Wen of Jin ruled through guile; vassals pressed for kingly privileges while courts handed out the nine imperial gifts—power without legitimacy, the classic "tail too heavy to wag" problem. That record cannot stand beside the brotherly loyalty praised in the "Tangdi" ode or the warning woven through the "Owl". This dynasty is still laying its first stones; begin without good faith and nothing built afterward will stand straight. A weak trunk cannot sustain lush branches; strip away moral spine and the outward show collapses. Since mythic antiquity no single lineage has ever monopolized Heaven's favor forever. Win men's loyalty only when your foundations stand like bedrock. Whoever would secure lasting advantage must weigh policy openly with the realm. Heaven hears and sees through the hearing and sight of the people. Qin and Wei tried to hoard all authority and ended only by drowning themselves; Zhou and Han shared power with kin and allies near and far, and those bonds served them well. Far-sighted rulers understood this; it is plain as sunlight. Even small matters deserve long forethought; even casual words should be weighed with care. I hold a place among classical scholars: if my counsel falls short of ritual propriety, then I am nothing but a fraud occupying an office. To see what loyalty requires and stay silent is what I dare not do in council. I believe we should adopt the erudites' recommendation. ": When the draft memorial was ready to go up, he showed it to his cousin Jia, Duke of Gaoyi. Jia replied: "Brother, this argument cuts deep. Historians a century hence may praise you; those at court today may blame you instead. ": The emperor read the memorial and flared with anger: "If even Cao Zhi cannot read my intent, what can I expect from the realm? ": Deeming the authors evasive and argumentative rather than responsive, he cashiered Minister of Ceremonies Zheng Mo by edict. Authorities then sought to arrest Cao Zhi and his allies on conspiracy charges; an edict stripped only Cao Zhi of office and allowed him to retire to his ducal mansion as commoner nobility, while the others were remanded to the Minister of Justice.

頃之，志復為散騎常侍。 遭母憂，居喪過禮，因此篤病，喜怒失常。 九年卒，太常奏以惡諡。 崔褒歎曰：「魏顆不從亂，以病為亂故也。 今諡曹志而諡其病，豈謂其病不為亂乎！ 」於是諡為定。
Soon afterward Cao Zhi was again appointed Palace Attendant-in-Ordinary. His mother's death plunged him into mourning so severe it exceeded prescribed rites; grief broke his health and unsettled his temper. He died in the ninth year of the era; the Minister of Ceremonies proposed a harsh posthumous name. Cui Bao protested: "Wei Ke defied a deranged command because he knew illness—not malice—had given it." If the court censures Cao Zhi for derangement in mourning, are we saying grief-induced illness counts as moral rebellion? ": The court therefore settled on the posthumous name "Ding" (Steadfast).

庾峻，字山甫，潁川鄢陵人也。 祖乘，才學洽聞，漢司徒辟，有道征，皆不就。 伯父嶷，中正簡素，仕魏為太僕。 父道，廉退貞固，養志不仕。 牛馬有踶齧者，恐傷人，不貨於市。 及諸子貴，賜拜太中大夫。 峻少好學，有才思。 嘗游京師，聞魏散騎常侍蘇林老疾在家，往候之。 林嘗就乘學，見峻流涕，良久曰：「尊祖高才而性退讓，慈和泛愛，清靜寡欲，不營當世，惟修德行而已。 鄢陵舊五六萬戶，聞今裁有數百。 君二父孩抱經亂，獨至今日，尊伯為當世令器，君兄弟復俊茂，此尊祖積德之所由也。」
Yu Jun, courtesy name Shanfu, came from Yanling in Yingchuan commandery. His grandfather Yu Cheng was widely read and accomplished; the Han court summoned him as Minister of Education and again as a candidate renowned for virtue—he declined every offer. His uncle Yi served locally as regional merit evaluator, lived plainly, and rose under Wei to Minister Coachman. His father Dao lived modestly and uprightly, devoted to study and never taking office. He refused to sell at market any ox or horse known to kick or bite, lest it harm someone. Once his sons rose to honor, the court ennobled him as Grand Palace Grandee. Yu Jun loved scholarship from youth and showed literary gifts. During a stay in the capital he learned that the veteran Wei scholar Su Lin lay ill at home and went to visit him. Su Lin had studied under Yu Cheng's household. Seeing Yu Jun he wept and finally said: "Your grandfather combined brilliance with humility—gentle, generous, detached from ambition and desire. He sought no office in his own day; he simply cultivated character. Yanling once counted fifty or sixty thousand households; today rumors say only a few hundred remain. Your fathers survived chaos as infants and lived to see better days; your uncle ranks among today's finest talents; you younger men shine as well—all this springs from the virtue your grandfather stored up."
8
曆郡功曹，舉計掾，州辟從事。 太常鄭袤見峻，大奇之，舉為博士。 時重《莊》《老》而輕《經》《史》，駿懼雅道陵遲，乃潛心儒典。 屬高貴鄉公幸太學，問《尚書》義于峻，峻援引師說，發明經旨，申暢疑滯，對答詳悉。 遷秘書丞。 長安有大獄，久不決，拜峻侍御史，往斷之，朝野稱允。 武帝踐阼，賜爵關中侯，遷司空長史，轉秘書監、御史中丞，拜侍中，加諫議大夫。 常侍帝講《詩》，中庶子何劭論《風》《雅》正變之義，峻起難往反，四坐莫能屈之。
He served as merit clerk in his commandery, rose as accounting clerk on recommendation, and entered provincial service as an attendant. Minister of Ceremonies Zheng Mao thought him extraordinary and nominated him as an imperial erudite. Fashion favored Zhuangzi and Laozi while slighting the Classics and histories; Yu Jun feared the erosion of serious learning and immersed himself in Confucian texts. When Duke Gaoguixiang toured the Imperial Academy and questioned him on the "Book of Documents," Yu Jun drew on established commentary, clarified knotty passages, and answered with meticulous thoroughness. He rose to Assistant Director of the Palace Library. A major Chang'an lawsuit had stalled for years; Yu Jun received appointment as attendant inspector and settled it to universal acclaim. After Emperor Wu took the throne Yu Jun received the title Marquis Within-the-Passes, advanced to chief clerk of the Minister of Works, then Director of the Palace Library, vice-director of the imperial clerks, palace attendant, and concurrent Grand Counselor of Remonstrance. When he attended the emperor's lectures on the "Classic of Poetry," He Shao, attendant to the heir, argued whether certain "Airs" and "Elegances" were canonical or decadent; Yu Jun rose to debate him round after round, and no one in the hall could best him.

He likewise despised fashionable vanity and the neglect of genuine achievement, writing a lengthy critique not reproduced here. He died in the ninth year of the era; an edict granted court robes, a burial garment, and three hundred thousand cash. On his deathbed he told his son Yu Min to hold a same-day burial if he died before nightfall, to dress him only in plain cloth, and not to wait for an astrologically chosen burial day. Yu Min obeyed, dressing the body for burial in everyday clothes as instructed. He had two sons, Yu Min and Yu Ai.

=瑉字子琚。 性淳和好學，行己忠恕。 少曆散騎常侍、本國中正、侍中，封長岑男。 懷帝之沒劉元海也，瑉從在平陽。 元海大會，因使帝行酒，瑉不勝悲憤，再拜上酒，因大號哭，賊惡之。 會有告瑉及王儁等謀應劉琨者，元海因圖弑逆，瑉等並遇害。 初，洛陽之未陷也，瑉為侍中，直於省內，謂同僚許遐曰：「世路如此，禍難將及，吾當死乎此屋耳！ 」及是，竟不免焉。 太元末，追諡曰貞。
Yu Min's courtesy name was Ziju. He was mild, cordial, studious, and lived by loyalty and reciprocity. While young he served as Palace Attendant-in-Ordinary, regional merit evaluator for his home commandery, palace attendant, and Baron of Changcen. When Emperor Huai fell captive to Liu Yuan (Liu Yuanhai), Yu Min accompanied him to Pingyang. At a banquet Liu Yuan ordered the Jin emperor to pour wine for his guests; Yu Min, overwhelmed with grief and rage, presented his cup with a double bow and burst into loud weeping—an affront his captors resented. When informants accused Yu Min, Wang Jun, and others of plotting to aid Liu Kun, Liu Yuan seized the excuse to stage a coup; Yu Min and the rest were killed. Before Luoyang fell, Yu Min was serving as palace attendant on duty in the palace offices. He told fellow attendant Xu Xia, "Affairs have come to this—calamity is near. I shall probably die inside these walls. ": In the end his words proved true. Late in the Taiyuan era the court posthumously titled him "Zhen" (Steadfast).

=敳字子嵩。 長不滿七尺，而腰帶十圍，雅有遠韻。 為陳留相，未嘗以事嬰心，從容酣暢，寄通而已。 處眾人中，居然獨立。 嘗讀《老》《莊》，曰：「正與人意暗同。 」太尉王衍雅重之。
Yu Ai was styled Zisong. Though barely seven chi tall, his girth measured ten hand-spans around the waist, and he carried an air of distant refinement. As chancellor of Chenliu he refused to let paperwork burden him—he lived at ease, drank freely, and drifted with circumstance. In any gathering he seemed to stand utterly apart from the rest. Reading the "Laozi" and "Zhuangzi" he remarked, "This speaks quietly to what I have always felt." Grand Commandant Wang Yan held him in the highest regard.
17
敳見王室多難，終知嬰禍，乃著《意賦》以豁情，猶賈誼之《服鳥》也。 其詞曰：「至理歸於渾一兮，榮辱固亦同貫。 存亡既已均齊兮，正盡死復何歎。 物咸定于無初兮，俟時至而後驗。 若四節之素代兮，豈當今之得遠？ 且安有壽之與夭兮，或者情橫多戀。 宗統竟初不別兮，大德亡其情願。 蠢動皆神之為兮，癡聖惟質所建。 真人都遣穢累兮，性茫蕩而無岸。 縱驅於遼廓之庭兮，委體乎寂寥之館。 天地短於朝生兮，億代促於始旦。 顧瞻宇宙微細兮，眇若豪鋒之半。 飄搖玄曠之域兮，深漠暢而靡玩。 兀與自然並體兮，融液忽而四散。 」從子亮見賦，問曰：「若有意也，非賦所盡； 若無意也，復何所賦？ 」答曰：「在有無之間耳！」
Seeing the house of Jin besieged by troubles, Yu Ai knew catastrophe would catch him in time; he wrote his "Rhapsody on Intention" to unburden his heart, in the spirit of Jia Yi's "Owl." It begins: "When the highest truth folds back into oneness, honor and disgrace run on the same string." Life and death already balance out—why lament once your portion is spent?" All things rest in that undivided origin and prove themselves only when their hour arrives." They turn like the four seasons in steady succession—how could anyone escape that rhythm now?" Where is the difference between long life and short if craving skews every heart?" Lineage begins undivided; the greatest virtue forgets private longing." Every twitch of life issues from spirit; blur saint and fool and you see only raw substance at work." The perfected shed every stain; nature stretches shoreless and wide." They roam the boundless court and yield their bodies to the silent inn." Heaven and earth shrink beside the mayfly; aeons vanish in a single morning." The cosmos shrinks to half the tip of the finest hair." You drift through dark immensity—deep, hollow, free, with nothing left to grasp." You stand dumb with the unnamed Dao, then melt away on every side." His nephew Yu Liang read the piece and asked: "If there is a point here, no poem can finish it; if there is none, why write a rhapsody at all?" Yu Ai answered: "Somewhere between yes and no—that is all."

遷吏部郎。 是時天下多故，機變屢起，敳常靜默無為。 參東海王越太傅軍事，轉軍諮祭酒。 時越府多雋異，敳在其中，常自袖手。 豫州牧長史河南郭象善《老》《莊》，時人以為王弼之亞。 敳甚知之，每曰：「郭子玄何必減瘐子嵩」。 象後為太傅主簿，任事專勢。 敳謂象曰：「卿自是當世大才，我疇昔之意都已盡矣。」
He was promoted to section director in the Ministry of Personnel. The realm seethed with intrigue, yet Yu Ai stayed quiet and took no initiative. He served on Prince Sima Yue of Donghai's staff as Grand Tutor's army adviser, then became army Libationer-delegate. Prince Yue's headquarters overflowed with talent, but Yu Ai usually stood back with folded arms. Guo Xiang of Henan, chief clerk to the Yuzhou governor, excelled at the "Laozi" and "Zhuangzi"; contemporaries ranked him just below Wang Bi. Yu Ai admired him and often said, "Guo Zixuan need not take second place to Yu Zisong." Later Guo Xiang became chief clerk to the Grand Tutor and wielded concentrated authority. Yu Ai told him: "You are a giant of our age; whatever fond hopes I once nursed for you are spent."

敳有重名，為搢紳所推，而聚斂積實，談者譏之。 都官從事溫嶠奏之，敳更器嶠，目嶠森森如千丈松，雖礧砢多節，施之大廈，有棟樑之用。 時劉輿見任于越，人士多為所構，惟敳縱心事外，無跡可間。 後以其性儉家富，說越令就換錢千萬，冀其有吝，因此可乘。 越于眾坐中問於敳，而敳乃穨然已醉，幘墮機上，以頭就穿取，徐答-{云}-：「下官家有二千萬，隨公所取矣。 」輿於是乃服。 越甚悅，因曰：「不可以小人之慮度君子之心。 」王衍不與敳交，敳卿之不置。 衍曰：「君不得為耳。 」敳曰：「卿自君我，我自卿卿。 我自用我家法，卿自用卿家法。 」衍甚奇之。 石勒之亂，與衍俱被害，時年五十。
Yu Ai enjoyed immense prestige among officialdom yet piled up private wealth, and wits mocked him for it. When Zeng adopted a Ju grandson, the "Spring and Autumn" recorded it as "Ju destroying Zeng." The sages knew grandsons were dear! Ritual kinship, not blood, defines father and son." Edicts allowed alien heirs only for peers of the Grand Tutor's merit and circumstance—Chong cannot invoke that precedent." Adopting a daughter's son without supreme merit is forbidden. Can the Son of Heaven's rites condone it? It ends ancestral offerings and invites dynastic disaster. The canons style such men "Wild"—give him the posthumous name Duke Huang. The court refused.
43
王濬有平吳之勳，而為王渾所譖毀。 帝雖不從，無明賞罰，以濬為輔國大將軍，天下咸為之怨。 秀乃上言曰：「自大晉啟祚，輔國之號，率以舊恩。 此為王濬無功之時，受九列之顯位，立功之後更得寵人之辱號也。 四海視之，孰不失望！ 蜀小吳大，平蜀之後，二將皆就加三事，今濬還而降等，天下安得不惑乎！ 吳之未亡也，雖以三祖之神武，猶躬受其屈。 以孫皓之虛名，足以驚動諸夏，每一小出，雖聖心知其垂亡，然中國輒懷惶怖。 當爾時，有能借天子百萬之眾，平而有之，與國家結兄弟之交，臣恐朝野實皆甘之耳。 今濬舉蜀、漢之卒，數旬而平吳，雖舉吳人之財寶以與之，本非己分有焉，而遽與計校乎？」
Wang Jun conquered Wu yet Wang Hun slandered him. The emperor took no clear side—named Wang Jun merely General Who Guards the State—and the realm seethed. Qin Xiu memorialized: "Since Great Jin began, the title General Who Guards the State has honored elder statesmen. Wang Jun held exalted Nine Ministers rank before any deed; after victory he receives the condescending title meant for court favorites. The whole realm watched—who was not disheartened? Shu was the lesser prize, Wu the greater: after Shu fell both generals won the three excellencies, yet Wang Jun, who actually took Wu, is downgraded—how can the realm not be baffled? While Wu survived even our three sage-warrior founders endured setbacks. Sun Hao's hollow prestige still terrified the Central Plains—each sortie spread dread though the throne knew Wu was doomed. Had anyone raised a million imperial troops, conquered Wu, and sealed brotherhood with the throne, court and countryside alike would have rejoiced. Wang Jun led Shu and Han veterans and crushed Wu within weeks; reward him with Wu's entire hoard—it was never his private spoil—why quibble now?"
44
後與劉暾等同議齊王攸事，忤旨，除名。 尋復起為博士。 秀性悻直，與物多忤。 為博士前後垂二十年，卒於官。
Later he joined Liu Tun in debating Prince You of Qi's case, offended the emperor, and was stricken from the rolls. He was soon recalled as an imperial erudite. Qin Xiu was blunt and quarreled often with the world. He spent nearly twenty years as an erudite and died in office.
45
【史評】
Historians' appraisal
46
史臣曰：齊獻王以明德茂親，經邦論道，允厘庶績，式敘彝倫。 武帝納奸諂之邪謀，懷紹終之遠慮，遂乃君茲青土，作牧東籓。 遠邇驚嗟，朝野失望。 曹志等服膺教義，方軌儒門，蹇蹇匪躬，慺慺體國。 故能抗言鳳闕，忤犯龍鱗，身雖暫屈，道亦弘矣！ 庾氏世載清德，見稱於世，汝潁之多奇士，斯焉取斯。 謀甫素疾佞邪，而發因醉飽，投鼠忌器，豈易由言。 竊人之財，猶謂之盜，子玄假譽攘善，將非盜乎！
The historians say: Prince Xian of Qi, both virtuous and royally close, should have governed the realm and set policy—ordering every task and securing moral relations. Emperor Wu listened to sycophants and brooded on securing his own succession, so he invested him with Qi and sent him east as a frontier prince. Near and far murmured in shock; court and countryside were dismayed. Cao Zhi and his fellows absorbed Confucian teaching, walked the scholars' path, risked themselves unstintingly, and trembled for the state. Thus they could speak boldly at the gate of the palace and brush the imperial dragon—though their persons suffered briefly, the Way they upheld shone great. The Yu clan bore unstained virtue for generations and won renown—the Ru–Ying region bred rare talents, and here they proved it. Yu Chun always despised sycophancy yet erupted while drunk—like hunting rats beside sacred vessels, wise counsel comes hard. Theft of coin is theft—Guo Zixuan stole another's fame and claimed his virtue as his own; is that not robbery?
47
贊曰：魏氏維城，濟北知名。 潁川多士，峻亦飛英。 長岑徇義，祭酒遺榮。 謀甫三爵，酗醟斯作。 象既攘善，秀惟癉惡。 旉獻嘉謀，幾趨鼎鑊。
Encomium: The Wei house leaned on him as its bulwark—famous as Prince of Jibei. Yingchuan teemed with talents; Yu Jun too rose in splendor. Baron of Changcen died for honor; the Libationer cast aside glory. Three cups undid Moufu—drunken brawling followed. Guo Xiang stole credit for good; Qin Xiu punished evil relentlessly. Yu Fen offered worthy counsel and nearly landed in the execution cauldron.
