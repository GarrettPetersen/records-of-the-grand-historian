1
慕容垂
Murong Chui.
2
慕容垂，字道明，皝之第五子也。 少岐嶷有器度，身長七尺七寸，手垂過膝。 皝甚寵之，常目而謂諸弟曰：「此兒闊達好奇，終能破人家，或能成人家。 」故名霸，字道業，恩遇逾於世子俊，故俊不能平之。 以滅宇文之功，封都鄉侯。 石季龍來伐，既還，猶有兼併之志，遣將鄧恆率眾數萬屯于樂安，營攻取之備。 垂戍徒河，與恆相持，恆憚而不敢侵。 垂少好畋遊，因獵墜馬折齒，慕容俊僭即王位，改名𡙇，外以慕郤𡙇為名，內實惡而改之。 尋以讖記之文，乃去「夬」，以「垂」為名焉。
Murong Chui, styled Daoming, was Murong Huang's fifth son. As a boy he was sharp and commanding; he grew to seven feet seven inches, with hands that reached below his knees. Huang doted on him. He would often stare at the boy and tell his other sons, "This one is open-handed and restless; in time he may wreck a house—or build one. So he was named Ba, with the style Daoye, and was favored even above the heir Jun—something Jun never forgave. For the campaign that destroyed the Yuwen, he was made marquis of the capital district. After Shi Hu invaded and withdrew, Huang still meant to swallow more territory. He sent Deng Heng with tens of thousands of men to camp at Le'an and prepare an assault. Chui held Tuhe and faced Heng across the lines; Heng was afraid to press the attack. Chui loved the chase in his youth and once broke his teeth in a fall from his horse. When Murong Jun declared himself king, he renamed Chui Guai—ostensibly after Xi Que, in truth because he loathed the old name. Soon a prophecy led him to drop the guai radical and fix the name as Chui.
3
石季龍之死也，趙魏亂，垂謂俊曰：「時來易失，赴機在速，兼弱攻昧，今其時矣。 」俊以新遭大喪，不許。 慕輿根言於俊曰：「王子之言，千載一時，不可失也。 」俊乃從之，以垂為前鋒都督。 俊既克幽州，將坑降卒，垂諫曰：「吊伐之義，先代常典。 今方平中原，宜綏懷以德，坑戮之刑不可為王師之先聲。 」俊從之。 及俊僭稱尊號，封垂吳王，徙鎮信都，以侍中、右禁將軍錄留台事，大收東北之利。 又為征南將軍、荊、兗二州牧，有聲于梁、楚之南。 再為司隸，偽王公已下莫不累跡。 時莫容暐嗣偽位，慕容恪為太宰。 恪甚重垂，常謂暐曰：「吳王將相之才十倍於臣，先帝以長幼之次，以臣先之，臣死之後，願陛下委政吳王，可謂親賢兼舉。 」及敗桓溫於枋頭，威名大振。 慕容評深忌惡之，乃謀誅垂。 垂懼禍及己，與世子全奔於苻堅。
At Shi Hu's death the north collapsed into chaos. Chui told Jun, "Moments slip away; speed is everything. Strike the weak while they are blind—that hour is now. Jun, still deep in mourning, refused. Murong Gen urged Jun, "What the prince says is a once-in-a-thousand-years chance. Let it not pass. Jun relented and named Chui commander of the vanguard. When Jun seized Youzhou he meant to massacre the captives. Chui protested, "Succoring the fallen and punishing the guilty was the old way of kings. As we move to pacify the heartland, win men with kindness. Mass killing must not be the first sound our armies make. Jun agreed. When Jun took the imperial title he made Chui prince of Wu and posted him at Xindu as palace attendant, right forbidden general, and recorder of the eastern capital, drawing heavy revenue from the northeast. He was also named general who campaigns south and governor of Jing and Yan, famed throughout the lands south of Liang and Chu. A second term as metropolitan commandant followed; every prince and minister in the realm trailed in his wake. Murong Wei now held the throne and Murong Ke served as grand preceptor. Ke esteemed Chui and often told Wei, "The Prince of Wu has ten times my gifts as statesman and commander. Our late lord, respecting age, set me ahead of him. When I am gone, entrust the realm to the Prince of Wu—that is how to honor talent and kin together. After he shattered Huan Wen at Fangtou, his fame soared. Murong Ping hated him bitterly and plotted his death. Fearing for his life, Chui fled with his heir Murong Quan to Fu Jian.
4
自恪卒後，堅密有圖暐之謀，憚垂威名而未發。 及聞其至，堅大悅，郊迎執手，禮之甚重。 堅相王猛惡垂雄略，勸堅殺之。 堅不從，以為冠軍將軍，封賓都侯，食華陰之五百戶。 王猛伐洛，引全為參軍。 猛乃令人詭傳垂語于全曰：「吾已東還，汝可為計也。 」全信之，乃奔暐。 猛表全叛狀，垂懼而東奔，及藍田，為追騎所獲。 堅引見東堂，慰勉之曰：「卿家國失和，委身投朕。 賢子志不忘本，猶懷首丘。 《書》不云乎：「父父子子，無相及也。 」卿何為過懼而狼狽若斯也！ 」於是復垂爵位，恩待如初。
After Ke died, Jian quietly planned to destroy Yan but held back for fear of Chui's name. Learning Chui had come, Jian went out to meet him, clasped his hand, and honored him lavishly. Wang Meng, Jian's chief minister, feared Chui's genius and urged execution. Jian refused. He made Chui general of the champions, marquis of Bin Capital, with five hundred households in Huayin. When Wang Meng marched on Luoyang he took Quan on his staff. Meng forged a message to Quan in Chui's name: "I have gone east. Act as you see fit. Quan believed it and fled back to Murong Wei. Meng reported Quan's defection. Chui fled east in alarm and was caught at Lantian by pursuers. Jian received him in the eastern hall and said, "Your house fell out and you came to me. Your fine son remembered his roots and longed for home. Does not the Book say, "Father and son each keep his place—guilt does not cross generations"? Why such panic? Chui's rank was restored and he was favored as before.
5
及堅擒暐，垂隨堅入鄴，收集諸子，對之悲慟，見其故吏，有不悅之色。 前郎中令高弼私於垂曰：「大王以命世之姿，遭無妄之運，迍邅棲伏，艱亦至矣。 天啟嘉會，靈命暫遷，此乃鴻漸之始，龍變之初，深願仁慈有以慰之。 且夫高世之略必懷遺俗之規，方當網漏吞舟，以弘苞養之義； 收納舊臣之胄，以成為山之功，奈何以一怒捐之？ 竊為大王不取。 」垂深納之。 垂在堅朝，歷位京兆尹，進封泉州侯，所在征伐，皆在大功。
When Jian took Ye, Chui entered with him, gathered his sons, and wept before them; some old officers he greeted with a scowl. Gao Bi, former director of palace attendants, said privately, "Great King, born for an age of turmoil, you have suffered misfortune and lived in hiding—the trial has been severe. Heaven now offers a turn of fortune; for a moment the mandate is yours—the wild goose begins its flight, the dragon stirs. Show mercy and comfort these men. Great designs require magnanimity. Loosen the net to take big fish—enlarge the grace of sheltering and nurturing; gather the sons of your old officers and build a mountain of loyalty. Why cast them off in anger? I beg Great King not to do this. Chui took the advice to heart. Under Jian he governed Jingzhao, rose to marquis of Quanzhou, and in every campaign carried off the chief honors.
6
堅之敗於淮南也，垂軍獨全，堅以千餘騎奔垂。 垂世子寶言於垂曰：「家國傾喪，皇綱廢馳，至尊明命著之圖籙，當隆中興之業，建少康之功。 但時來之運未至，故韜光俟奮耳。 今天厭亂德，凶眾土崩，可謂乾啟神機，授之於我。 千載一時，今其會也，宜恭承皇天之意，因而取之。 且夫立大功者不顧小節，行大仁者不念小惠。 秦既蕩覆二京，空辱神器，仇恥之深，莫甚於此，願不以意氣微恩而忘社稷之重。 五木之祥，今其至矣。 」垂曰：「汝言是也。 然彼以赤心投命，若何害之！ 苟天所棄，圖之多便。 且縱令北還，更待其釁，既不負宿心，可以義取天下。 」垂弟德進曰：「夫鄰國相吞，有自來矣。 秦強而並燕，秦弱而圖之，此為報仇雪辱，豈所謂負宿心也！ 昔鄧祁侯不納三甥之言，終為楚所滅； 吳王夫差違子胥之諫，取禍句踐。 前事之不忘，後事之師表也。 願不棄湯、武之成蹤，追韓信之敗跡，乘彼土崩，恭行天罰，斬逆氐，復宗祀，建中興，繼洪烈，天下大機，弗宜失也。 若釋數萬之眾，授幹將之柄，是郤天時而待後害，非至計也。 語曰：'當斷不斷，反受其亂。 '願兄無疑。 」垂曰：「吾昔為太傅所不容，投身于秦主，又為王猛所譖，復見昭亮，國士之禮每深，報德之分未一。 如使秦運必窮，歷數歸我者，授首之便，何慮無之。 關西之地，會非吾有，自當有擾之者，吾可端拱而定關東。 君子不怙亂，不為禍先，且可觀之。 」乃以兵屬堅。 初，寶在長安，與韓黃、李根等因讌摴蒱，寶危坐整容，誓之曰：「世云摴蒱有神，豈虛也哉！ 若富貴可期，頻得三盧。 」於是三擲盡盧，寶拜而受賜，故云五木之祥。
At the defeat on the Huai, only Chui's corps held together. Jian fled to him with a thousand horsemen. Bao urged Chui, "Our house is ruined and the throne empty, yet heaven's mandate is written in the charts. Raise the restoration as Shaokang did. The hour had not come, so you hid your light and waited. Now heaven hates Qin's crimes and its hosts crumble. The divine moment is ours. A once-in-a-thousand-years chance is here. Receive heaven's will and seize it. Great deeds ignore small scruples; great kindness forgets petty debts. Qin wrecked the two capitals and defiled the throne—the shame is beyond measure. Do not let one man's kindness outweigh the altars of Yan. The omen of the five tablets has come true. Chui said, "You are right. Yet he came to us in good faith—how can I strike him down! If heaven has cast him off, we will have many chances to act. Let him go north and wait for his next mistake. Keep faith with him for now, and take the realm by righteous means later." His brother Murong De pressed him: "States have always swallowed their neighbors. Qin swallowed Yan when it was strong; now that Qin is weak we strike back. That is vengeance, not betrayal! The Marquis of Deng ignored his nephews' warning and was destroyed by Chu; King Fuchai of Wu spurned Wu Zixu and fell to Goujian. The past is the mirror of the future. Do not spurn the example of Tang and Wu for Han Xin's folly. While Qin collapses, execute heaven's judgment, destroy the Di, restore our altars, and rebuild the dynasty. Do not lose the realm's great chance. Release tens of thousands of men and hand Jian the sword—that rejects heaven's season and invites later ruin. It is no master plan. As the proverb says, "He who will not decide when he must will be undone by disorder." Brother, have no doubts." Chui said, "Ke once drove me out; Jian took me in. Wang Meng slandered me, yet Jian cleared me. He treated me as a peer of the realm, and I have not repaid him. If Qin's fate is spent and the mandate returns to us, his head will be easy to take. The west was never ours; others will fight over it. I can sit at ease and secure the east. A gentleman does not profit from chaos or strike first. Let us watch." He gave his troops back to Jian. Earlier in Chang'an, Bao played tile dice with Han Huang and Li Gen. Sitting straight, he vowed, "They say the dice have a spirit—is it true? If fortune is near, let me throw three lu in a row." He threw three times and got lu each time. Bao bowed in thanks—hence the omen of the five tablets.
7
堅至澠池，垂請至鄴展拜陵墓，因張國威刑，以安戎狄。 堅許之，權翼諫曰：「垂爪牙名將，所謂今之韓、白，世豪東夏，志不為人用。 頃以避禍歸誠，非慕德而至，列土幹城未可以滿其志，冠軍之號豈足以稱其心！ 且垂猶鷹也，饑則附人，飽便高颺，遇風塵之會，必有陵霄之志。 惟宜急其羈靽，不可任其所欲。 」堅不從，遣其將李蠻、閔亮、尹國率眾三千送垂，又遣石越戍鄴，張蠔戍并州。
At Mianchi Chui asked leave to visit the tombs at Ye and show Qin's majesty to the frontier peoples. Jian agreed. Quan Yi warned, "Chui is a tiger among generals—the Han Xin or Bai Qi of our day, a lord of the eastern lands who serves no master willingly. He came only to escape ruin, not from love of our virtue. Lands and titles cannot fill his ambition; the rank of champion general cannot satisfy him. He is like a hawk: hungry, he perches on your wrist; fed, he flies away. Give him turmoil and he will rise above the clouds. Bind him quickly. Do not let him have his way." Jian refused. He sent Li Man, Min Liang, and Yin Guo with three thousand men to escort Chui, posted Shi Yue at Ye and Zhang Mang at Bingzhou.
8
時堅子丕先在鄴，及垂至，丕館之於鄴西，垂具說淮南敗狀。 會堅將苻暉告丁零翟斌聚眾謀逼洛陽，歪謂垂曰：「惟斌兄弟因王師小失，敢肆凶勃，子母之軍，殆難為敵，非冠軍英略，莫可以滅也。 欲相煩一行可乎？ 」垂曰：「下官殿下之鷹犬，敢不惟命是聽。 」於是大賜金帛，一無所受，惟請舊田園。 丕許之，配垂兵二千，遣其將苻飛龍率氐騎一千為垂之副。 丕戒飛龍曰：「卿王室肺腑，年秩雖卑，其實帥也。 垂為三軍之統，卿為謀垂之主，用兵制勝之權，防微杜貳之略，委之於卿，卿其勉之。 」垂請入鄴城拜廟，丕不許。 乃潛服而入，亭吏禁之，垂怒，斬吏燒亭而去。 石越言於丕曰：「垂之在燕，破國亂家，及投命聖朝，蒙超常之遇，忽敢輕侮方鎮，殺吏焚亭，反形已露，終為亂階。 將老兵疲，可襲而取之矣。 」歪曰：「淮南之敗，眾散親離，而垂侍衛聖躬，誠不可忘。 」越曰：「垂既不忠於燕，其肯盡忠於我乎！ 且其亡虜也，主上寵同功舊，不能銘澤誓忠，而首謀為亂，今不擊之，必為後害。 」丕不從。 越退而告人曰：「公父子好存小仁，不顧天下大計，吾屬終當為鮮卑虜矣。」
Jian's son Fu Pi was already at Ye. Pi lodged Chui west of the city and heard his account of the Huai defeat. Fu Hui reported that Zhai Bin of the Dingling had raised troops against Luoyang. Pi told Chui, "The Bins exploit our setback. Their host will be hard to break—only your genius can crush them. Will you go? Will you take the field? Chui said, "I am Your Highness's hound. Command me. Pi offered gold and silk in profusion; Chui took none, asking only for his old estates. Pi agreed, gave him two thousand men, and sent Fu Feilong with a thousand Di horse as his lieutenant. Pi told Feilong, "You are of the royal blood. Young as you are, you are the true commander. Chui leads the army—you watch Chui. All power to command, all plans to check rebellion are yours. Be vigilant." Chui asked to enter Ye and bow at the ancestral shrine. Pi refused. He slipped in disguised. A clerk stopped him. Chui killed the clerk, burned the post, and left. Shi Yue told Pi, "Chui wrecked Yan and fled here. You favored him beyond measure, yet he kills your men and burns your posts. His rebellion is plain. His men are tired. Strike now and take him." Pi said, "At the Huai he stood by my father when all else fled. That debt stands." Yue said, "He betrayed Yan. Why trust him with Qin? The emperor treated this fugitive like an old comrade, and he repays us with revolt. If we do not strike, he will destroy us." Pi would not listen. Yue left muttering, "Father and son cherish petty kindness and forget the realm. We will end as Xianbei captives."
9
垂至河內，殺飛龍，悉誅氐兵，召募遠近，眾至三萬，濟河焚橋，令曰：「吾本外假秦聲，內規興復。 亂法者軍有常刑，奉命者賞不逾日，天下既定，封爵有差，不相負也。」
At Henei Chui killed Feilong and slaughtered the Di escort. Recruits swelled his ranks to thirty thousand. He crossed the river, burned the bridge, and proclaimed, "Outwardly I served Qin; inwardly I meant to restore Yan. Break discipline and face military law; obey and be rewarded within the day. When the realm is won, ranks and fiefs will be justly given. I will not cheat you."
10
翟斌聞垂之將濟河也，遣使推垂為盟主。 垂距之曰：「吾父子寄命秦朝，危而獲濟，荷主上不世之恩，蒙更生之惠，雖曰君臣，義深父子，豈可因其小隙，便懷二三。 吾本救豫州，不赴君等，何為斯議而及於我！ 」垂進欲襲據洛陽，故見苻暉以臣節，退又未審斌之誠款，故以此言距之。 垂至洛陽，暉閉門距守，不與垂通。 斌又遣長史河南郭通說垂，乃許之。 斌率眾會垂，勸稱尊號，垂曰：「新興侯，國之正統，孤之君也。 若以諸君之力，得平關東，當以大義喻秦，奉迎反正。 無上自尊，非孤心也。 」謀於眾曰：「洛陽四面受敵，北阻大河，至於控馭燕、趙，非形勝之便，不如北取鄴都，據之而制天下。 」眾咸以為然。 乃引師而東，遣建威將軍王騰起浮橋于石門。
Learning Chui meant to cross the river, Zhai Bin sent envoys offering him leadership of the league. Chui declined: "My father and I found refuge in Qin. In danger we were spared; the emperor gave us life anew. Though we are called subject and lord, our bond is like father and son. How could I turn on him for a slight? I came to save Yuzhou, not to join your revolt. Why drag me into this? Chui meant to take Luoyang by surprise, so he played the loyal subject before Fu Hui; uncertain of Bin's loyalty, he answered with this refusal. At Luoyang, Hui barred the gates and would not receive Chui. Bin sent his clerk Guo Tong to win Chui over, and Chui agreed. Bin joined Chui and urged him to take the throne. Chui said, "Fu Pi is the rightful heir. He is my sovereign. If we pacify the east with your help, I will persuade Qin by righteousness and restore the throne. To seize the crown for myself is not my wish. He told his officers, "Luoyang is beset on every side, with the Yellow River to the north. It is no strongpoint for ruling Yan and Zhao. Better march north, seize Ye, and command the realm from there." All agreed. He marched east and sent Wang Teng to throw a pontoon bridge across at Shimen.
11
初，垂之發鄴中，子農及兄子楷、紹，北子宙，為苻丕所留。 及誅飛龍，遣田生密告農等，使起兵趙、魏以相應。 於是農、宙奔列人，楷、紹奔辟陽，眾咸應之。 農西招庫辱官偉於上黨，東引乞特歸於東阿，各率眾數萬赴之，眾至十餘萬。 丕遣石越討農，為農所敗，斬越于陳。
When Chui left Ye, Pi had held back his son Nong, his nephews Kai and Shao, and another son Zhou. After killing Feilong he sent Tian Sheng secretly to tell Nong and the others to rise in Zhao and Wei. Nong and Zhou fled to Lieren, Kai and Shao to Piyang, and the people rallied to them. Nong called Guan Wei of the Kuru in the west and Qi Te Gui in the east; each brought tens of thousands until their force passed a hundred thousand. Pi sent Shi Yue against Nong. Nong routed him and killed Yue at Chen.
12
垂引兵至滎陽，乙太元八年自稱大將軍、大都督、燕王，承制行事，建元曰燕元。 令稱統府，府置四佐，王公已下稱臣，凡所封拜，一如王者，以翟斌為建義大將軍，封河南王； 翟檀為柱國大將軍、弘農王； 弟德為車騎大將軍、范陽王； 兄子楷征西大將軍、太原王。 眾至二十餘萬，濟自石門，長驅攻鄴。 農、楷、紹、宙等率眾會垂。 立子寶為燕王太子，封功臣為公侯伯子男者百餘人。
At Xingyang, in the eighth year of the Taiyuan era, Chui declared himself grand general, grand commander, and king of Yan, ruling by provisional mandate under the reign title Yan Yuan. His headquarters was styled the supreme command; four aides were appointed; princes and ministers called him lord. Enfeoffments followed royal forms. Zhai Bin was made general who establishes righteousness and king of Henan; Zhai Tan became pillar-of-state general and king of Hongnong; his brother De was general of chariots and cavalry and king of Fanyang; his nephew Kai was general who campaigns west and king of Taiyuan. His army swelled past two hundred thousand. He crossed at Shimen and marched hard on Ye. Nong, Kai, Shao, Zhou, and the rest joined Chui with their hosts. He named Bao crown prince and enfeoffed over a hundred ministers as dukes, marquises, earls, viscounts, and barons.
13

Fu Pi sent Jiang Rang to Chui: "When the emperor fled disaster, you guarded him and served with surpassing loyalty. Return to that path and keep faith to the end. Why throw away great merit for this rebellion? The sages praised those who amend their errors. Think on it. It is not too late. Chui told Rang, "I owe the emperor boundless grace. I meant to see Fu Pi safely to the capital with all his troops, then restore Yan and remain Qin's friend. Why will you not read the times and yield Ye? When righteousness demands it, kinship gives way—what of mere favor? If you will not turn back, I will use force. The matter is settled. Do not hope to beg your life alone on horseback. Rang rebuked him: "You had no place in Yan and came to Qin. What right have you to Yan's soil? The emperor and you are not of one kind, yet he honored you at first sight, bound you as kin, favored you above old friends, and gave you rank equal to the greatest princes. Never was trust like this! He would have entrusted his son and the realm to you. Why, after one defeat, plot treason? Armies without just cause fail. What heaven casts off, men cannot uphold. You raise a lawless host to revive a doomed house. I see no hope in it. Fu Pi is the heir, famed like the princes of Zhou, the court's shield in the east. He will not meekly yield you a hundred cities. Ministers die for their lord; rulers die for the altars. If you mean to rebel, use your armies. I have said enough. You are seventy. Think of your head on a pole—loyalty turned to treason. I grieve for you. Chui said nothing. His men urged death. Chui said, "When armies meet, envoys pass between them. Dogs bark at strangers. 垂上表於苻堅曰：「臣才非古人，致禍起蕭牆，身嬰時難，歸命聖朝。 陛下恩深周、漢，猥叨微顧之遇，位為列將，爵忝通侯，誓在戮力輸誠，常懼不及。 去夏桓沖送死，一擬雲消，回討鄖城，俘馘萬計，斯誠陛下神算之奇，頗亦愚臣忘死之效。 方將飲馬桂州，懸旌閩會，不圖天助亂德，大駕班師。 陛下單馬奔臣，臣奉衛匪貳，豈陛下聖明鑒臣單心，皇天后土實亦知之。 臣奉詔北巡，受制長樂。 然丕外失眾心，內多猜忌，今臣野次外庭，不聽謁廟。 丁零逆豎寇逼豫州，丕迫臣單赴，限以師程，惟給弊卒二千，盡無兵杖，復令飛龍潛為刺客。 及至洛陽，平原公暉復不信納。 臣竊惟進無淮陰功高之慮，退無李廣失利之愆，懼有青蠅，交亂白黑，丁零夷夏以臣忠而見疑，乃推臣為盟主。 臣受託善始，不遂令終，泣望西京，揮涕即邁。 軍次石門，所在雲赴，雖復周武之會于孟津，漢祖之集於垓下，不期之眾，實有甚焉。 欲令長樂公盡眾赴難，以禮發遣，而丕固守匹夫之志，不達變通之理。 臣息農收集故營，以備不虞，而石越傾鄴城之眾，輕相掩襲，兵陣未交，越已隕首。 臣既單車懸軫，歸者如雲，斯實天符，非臣之力。 且鄴者臣國舊都，應即惠及，然後西面受制，永守東籓，上成陛下遇臣之意，下全愚臣感報之誠。 今進師圍鄴，並喻丕以天時人事。 而丕不察機運，杜門自守，時出挑戰，鋒戈屢交，恆恐飛矢誤中，以傷陛下天性之念。 臣之此誠，未簡神聽，輒遏兵止銳，不敢竊攻。 夫運有推移，去來常事，惟陛下察之。」
Chui wrote to Fu Jian: "I am no sage of old. Trouble rose within my house. I came to you in my need. Your grace equals Zhou and Han. You made me a general and a marquis. I swore to serve with all my strength. Last summer we shattered Huan Chong. At Yuncheng we took tens of thousands. That was your divine strategy—and my willingness to die for you. I meant to drive to Guilin and plant banners in Fujian. Then heaven aided Qin's enemies and the great host retreated. You fled to me alone on horseback. I guarded you without wavering. Heaven and earth know my heart. I was ordered north under Fu Pi's command. Pi lost the people's hearts and trusts no one. I camp outside the walls and may not enter the shrine. The Dingling pressed Yuzhou. Pi sent me with two thousand broken men, no arms, and Feilong to murder me in secret. At Luoyang, Fu Hui would not admit me. I had neither Han Xin's fear of too great merit nor Li Guang's disgrace in retreat. Yet slander might confuse truth. Barbarian and Chinese alike, seeing my loyalty doubted, made me their leader. Trusted at the start, I cannot end well. Weeping I turn from the western capital and march away. At Shimen men gathered like clouds—a host greater than King Wu at Mengjin or Liu Bang at Gaixia. I asked Fu Pi to march out with all his men. He clung to obstinacy and would not bend. My son Nong rallied old camps for defense. Shi Yue led Ye's host against him and was killed before battle was joined. I came alone, and men flocked to me like clouds. That was heaven's sign, not my power. Ye is my old capital. Grant it to me, and I will serve you from the east forever—fulfilling your grace and my debt. Now I besiege Ye and have told Pi what heaven and duty demand. Pi will not yield. He sallies to fight again and again. I fear a stray shaft may wound your fatherly heart. Heaven has not heard me. I hold my troops and dare not strike in secret. Fortune turns. Coming and going are the way of things. May Your Majesty see it."

堅報曰：「朕以不德，忝承靈命，君臨萬邦，三十年矣。 遐方幽裔，莫不來庭，惟東南一隅，敢違王命。 朕爰奮六師，恭行天罰，而玄機不吊，王師敗績。 賴卿忠誠之至，輔翼朕躬，社稷之不隕，卿之力也。 《詩》云：『中心藏之，何日忘之。 』方任卿以元相，爵卿以郡侯，庶弘濟艱難，敬酬勳烈，何圖伯夷忽毀冰操，柳惠倏為淫夫！ 覽表惋然，有慚朝士。 卿既不容於本朝，匹馬而投命，朕則寵卿以將位，禮卿以上賓，任同舊臣，爵齊勳輔，歃血斷金，披心相付。 謂卿食椹懷音，保之偕老。 豈意畜水覆舟，養獸反害，悔之噬臍，將何所及！ 誕言駭眾，誇擬非常，周武之事，豈卿庸人所可論哉！ 失籠之鳥，非羅所羈； 脫網之鯨，豈罟所制！ 翹陸任懷，何須聞也。 念卿垂老，老而為賊，生為叛臣，死為逆鬼，侏張幽顯，布毒存亡，中原士女，何痛如之！ 朕之曆運興喪，豈復由卿！ 但長樂、平原以未立之年，遇卿於兩都，慮其經略未稱朕心，所恨者此焉而已。」
Jian answered: "Lacking virtue, I have ruled thirty years by heaven's mandate. All the world has submitted save the southeast, which still defies me. I marched the six hosts to punish them, but heaven did not aid me and my army was broken. You saved me at Feishui. The altars stand because of you. The Odes say, "I hide it in my heart—when shall I forget?" I meant to make you chief minister and a marquis, to share hardship and honor your deeds. Who thought Bo Yi would break his vow, or Liu Xia Hui turn lecher? Your letter grieves and shames me before the court. Cast out from Yan, you came to me on one horse. I made you a great general and honored guest, equal to my oldest ministers. We swore blood brotherhood and opened our hearts. I thought you would keep faith to the end, like the man who, eating mulberries, remembered home. Who thought the boat you filled would sink you, the beast you fed would bite you? Regret comes too late. Your boasts terrify men and ape King Wu. Such things are not for the likes of you. A bird out of the cage escapes every net; a whale free of the seine breaks every trap. Go where you will on land or water. I need not hear more. Old man, you choose to be a rebel in your age—traitor in life, outcast in death, poisoning land and people. What pain for the heartland! Whether my house stands or falls does not depend on you. Only Fu Pi and Fu Hui, still young when they knew you in the capitals, may lack judgment. That alone I regret."

Lou Hui memorialized: "Three years' mourning is the universal norm. War and famine killed that rite, and office was filled by any means. Men scramble for rank, some even wearing mourning garb to take post—not from loyalty but from greed. The sages did not let hardship break the rites or chaos change the teaching. Thus they closed the door to reckless ambition. Your Majesty restores the dynasty amid ruin. The realm grows calm. It is time to cleanse abuses and restore the old rules. Seeing Chui at the western ford, he marched west to meet him. Chui secretly sent Murong Zhen and Murong Guo to cross at Liyang by night and fortify the south bank. Zhao turned back exhausted. The seven commanderies, thirty-eight thousand households, were left undisturbed. More than seven thousand refugee families from Xuzhou were resettled at Liyang.
32
於是議征長子。 諸將咸諫，以慕容永未有釁，連歲征役，士卒疲怠，請俟他年。 垂將從之，及聞慕容德之策，笑曰：「吾計決矣。 且吾投老，扣囊底智，足以克之，不復留逆賊以累子孫也。 」乃發步騎七萬，遣其丹陽王慕容贊、龍驤張崇攻永弟支于晉陽。 永遣其將刁雲、慕容鐘率眾五萬屯潞川。 垂遣慕容楷出自滏口，慕容農入自壺關，垂頓於鄴之西南，月餘不進。 永謂垂詭道伐之，乃攝諸軍還杜太行軹關。 垂進師入自天井關，至於壺壁。 永率精卒五萬來距，阻河曲以自固，馳使請戰。 垂列陣於壺避之南，農、楷分為二翼，慕容國伏千兵於深澗，與永大戰。 垂引軍偽退，永追奔數里，國發伏兵馳斷其後，楷、農夾擊之，永師大敗，斬首八千餘級，永奔還長子。 慕容贊攻克晉陽。 垂進圍長子，永將賈韜潛為內應。 垂進軍入城，永奔北門，為前驅所獲，於是數而戮之，並其所署公卿刁雲等三十餘人。 永所統新舊八郡戶七萬六千八百及乘輿、服御、伎樂、珍寶悉獲之，於是品物具矣。
They debated an expedition against Changzi. His officers urged delay. Murong Yong had given no opening; the army was weary after years of war. Chui was about to agree when he heard Murong De's counsel and laughed, "My mind is made. I am old, but the last wit in my bag is enough to crush him. I will not leave this rebel for my sons to fight." He marched seventy thousand foot and horse, sending Murong Zan and Zhang Chong against Yong's brother Zhi at Jinyang. Yong sent Diao Yun and Murong Zhong with fifty thousand men to Luchuan. Kai marched from Fukou, Nong from Huguan. Chui camped southwest of Ye and did not move for a month. Yong thought it a feint and pulled his armies back to guard Zhihang Pass in the Taihang. Chui marched through Tianjing Pass to Hubi. Yong came with fifty thousand elite troops, blocked the river bend, and challenged him to fight. Chui formed south of Hubi with Nong and Kai on the wings. Murong Guo hid a thousand men in a ravine and joined battle. Chui feigned retreat. Yong pursued. Guo struck from behind; Kai and Nong closed on the flanks. Yong lost eight thousand men and fled to Changzi. Murong Zan took Jinyang. Chui besieged Changzi. Yong's officer Jia Tao plotted treason within the walls. Chui entered the city. Yong was seized at the north gate, condemned, and executed with more than thirty of his ministers including Diao Yun. He took eight commanderies, seventy-six thousand eight hundred households, the imperial regalia, musicians, and treasure. The royal store was complete.
33
使慕容農略地河南，攻廩丘、陽城，皆克之，太山、琅邪諸郡皆委城奔潰，農進師臨海，置守宰而還。 垂告捷于龍城之廟。
Murong Nong swept Henan, took Linqiu and Yangcheng, and drove the commanderies of Taishan and Langye to flight. He reached the sea, posted officials, and returned. Chui reported victory at the Longcheng shrine.
34
遣其太子寶及農與慕容麟等率眾八萬伐魏，慕容德、慕容紹以步騎一萬八千為寶後繼。 魏聞寶將至，徙往河西。 寶進師臨河，懼不敢濟。 還次參合，忽有大風黑氣，狀若堤防，或高或下，臨覆軍上。 沙門支曇猛言於寶曰：「風氣暴迅，魏軍將至之候，宜遣兵禦之。 」寶笑而不納。 曇猛固以為言，乃遣麟率騎三萬為後殿，以禦非常。 麟以曇猛言為虛，縱騎遊獵。 俄而黃霧四塞，日月晦冥，是夜魏師大至，三軍奔潰，寶與德等數千騎奔免，士眾還者十一二，紹死之。 初，寶至幽州，所乘車軸無故自折。 術士靳安以為大凶，固勸寶還，寶怒不從，故及於敗。
He sent Bao and Nong with eighty thousand men against Wei. Murong De and Murong Shao followed with eighteen thousand as reserve. Hearing Bao approached, the Wei moved west of the river. Bao reached the river but dared not cross. Marching back to Canhe, a black storm rose like a wall over the camp. The monk Zhi Tanmeng warned Bao, "This wind means Wei is near. Send troops to meet them." Bao laughed and ignored him. Tanmeng insisted. Bao sent Murong Lin with thirty thousand horse as rearguard. Lin thought the warning nonsense and let his men hunt. Yellow fog blotted out the sky. That night Wei fell on them. The army broke. Bao and De escaped with a few thousand horse. Nine in ten perished. Shao was killed. When Bao reached Youzhou, his carriage axle snapped without cause. The diviner Jin An called it dire and begged Bao to turn back. Bao refused in anger—and met disaster.
35
寶恨參合之敗，屢言魏有可乘之機。 慕容德亦曰：「魏人狃於參合之役，有陵太子之心，宜及聖略，摧其銳志。 」垂從之，留德守中山，自率大眾出參合，鑿山開道，次於獵嶺。 遣寶與農出天門，征北慕容隆、征西慕容盛逾青山，襲魏陳留公泥于平城，陷之，收其眾三萬餘人而還。
Smarting from Canhe, Bao kept saying Wei could be beaten. Murong De said, "Wei thinks itself invincible after Canhe. Strike now and break their pride." Chui agreed. He left De at Zhongshan, led a great host from Canhe, cut a road through the mountains, and camped at Lie Ridge. He sent Bao and Nong through Tianmen, Long and Sheng over Green Mountain to storm Ni at Pingcheng, and returned with thirty thousand captives.
36
垂至參合，見往年戰處積骸如山，設弔祭之禮，死者父兄一時號哭，軍中皆慟。 垂慚憤歐血，因而寢疾，乘馬輿而進。 過平城北三十里，疾篤，築燕昌城而還。 寶等至雲中，聞垂疾，皆引歸。 及垂至於平城，或有叛者奔告魏曰：「垂病已亡，輿屍在軍。 」魏又聞參合大哭，以為信然，乃進兵追之，知平城已陷而退，還館陰山。 垂至上谷之沮陽，乙太元二十一年死，時年七十一，凡在位十三年。 遺令曰：「方今禍難尚殷，喪禮一從簡易，朝終夕殯，事訖成服，三日之後，釋服從政。 強寇伺隙，秘勿發喪，至京然後舉哀行服。 」寶等遵行之。 偽諡成武皇帝，廟號世祖，墓曰宣平陵。
At Canhe the bones of the dead lay heaped like a hill. Chui mourned them. Fathers and brothers wailed, and the whole army wept. Shame and rage made him vomit blood. He fell ill and rode in a litter. Thirty li north of Pingcheng his illness worsened. He built Yanchang city and turned back. Bao and the others, at Yunzhong, heard Chui was ill and turned back. At Pingcheng deserters told Wei, "Chui is dead. His corpse travels in the litter." Wei heard the mourning at Canhe and believed Chui dead. They pursued until they learned Pingcheng had fallen, then withdrew to Yin Mountain. At Juyang in Shanggu, in the twenty-first year of Taiyuan, Chui died at seventy-one, having ruled thirteen years. His testament read: "Disaster still surrounds us. Keep mourning simple: bury at once, mourn three days, then return to rule. Enemies are near. Hide my death until you reach the capital, then mourn openly." Bao and the others obeyed. He was posthumously titled Emperor Chengwu, temple name Shizu, tomb Xuanping Mausoleum.
