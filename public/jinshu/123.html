1
慕容垂
Murong Chui
2
慕容垂，字道明，皝之第五子也。 少岐嶷有器度，身長七尺七寸，手垂過膝。 皝甚寵之，常目而謂諸弟曰：「此兒闊達好奇，終能破人家，或能成人家。 」故名霸，字道業，恩遇逾於世子俊，故俊不能平之。 以滅宇文之功，封都鄉侯。 石季龍來伐，既還，猶有兼併之志，遣將鄧恆率眾數萬屯于樂安，營攻取之備。 垂戍徒河，與恆相持，恆憚而不敢侵。 垂少好畋遊，因獵墜馬折齒，慕容俊僭即王位，改名𡙇，外以慕郤𡙇為名，內實惡而改之。 尋以讖記之文，乃去「夬」，以「垂」為名焉。
Murong Chui, whose courtesy name was Daoming, was the fifth son of Murong Huang. As a youth he was unusually sharp and showed great promise; he stood seven chi and seven cun tall, with hands that reached below his knees. Huang doted on him and would often look him in the eye and tell his younger brothers, “This son is open-handed and curious by nature; in the end he will either ruin a house or raise one up. ” He was therefore given the name Ba, with the courtesy name Daoye, and was favored even more than the heir apparent Jun, who could never quite accept it. For his role in the destruction of the Yuwen, he was enfeoffed as Marquis of Duxiang. After Shi Hu’s invasion had been repelled, Murong Huang still planned further conquests and sent the general Deng Heng with tens of thousands of men to camp at Le’an, readying an assault on the place. Chui held Tuhe and locked horns with Deng Heng, who was too wary to press the attack. In his youth Chui loved the chase; he once fell from his horse while hunting and broke his teeth. When Murong Jun assumed the royal title, he changed his name to 𡙇, outwardly in homage to Xi 𡙇 but in truth because he loathed the association and wished to cast it off. Before long, acting on a passage in the prophecy books, he dropped the element guai from his name and adopted Chui as his personal name.
3
石季龍之死也，趙魏亂，垂謂俊曰：「時來易失，赴機在速，兼弱攻昧，今其時矣。 」俊以新遭大喪，不許。 慕輿根言於俊曰：「王子之言，千載一時，不可失也。 」俊乃從之，以垂為前鋒都督。 俊既克幽州，將坑降卒，垂諫曰：「吊伐之義，先代常典。 今方平中原，宜綏懷以德，坑戮之刑不可為王師之先聲。 」俊從之。 及俊僭稱尊號，封垂吳王，徙鎮信都，以侍中、右禁將軍錄留台事，大收東北之利。 又為征南將軍、荊、兗二州牧，有聲于梁、楚之南。 再為司隸，偽王公已下莫不累跡。 時莫容暐嗣偽位，慕容恪為太宰。 恪甚重垂，常謂暐曰：「吳王將相之才十倍於臣，先帝以長幼之次，以臣先之，臣死之後，願陛下委政吳王，可謂親賢兼舉。 」及敗桓溫於枋頭，威名大振。 慕容評深忌惡之，乃謀誅垂。 垂懼禍及己，與世子全奔於苻堅。
At Shi Hu’s death the north fell into chaos. Chui urged Jun, “Opportunity slips away quickly, and one must move fast to seize it. To overwhelm the weak while they are still blind to danger—this is the moment. ” Jun, still in mourning for his father, would not agree. Murong Gen told Jun, “What the prince proposes comes once in a thousand years and must not be let slip. ” Jun took his counsel and made Chui commander of the vanguard. When Jun had conquered You Province, he planned to massacre the surrendered troops. Chui objected: “To comfort the suffering while chastising the guilty is the ancient way of a punitive expedition. As we are on the verge of pacifying the Central Plains, we should win hearts with kindness; mass executions must not be the opening chord of our campaign. ” Jun accepted his advice. After Jun assumed the imperial title, he created Chui Prince of Wu and posted him to Xindu as palace attendant and right forbidden general with authority over the rear court, drawing great revenue from the northeast. He later served as general who pacifies the south and governor of Jing and Yan, winning renown throughout the lands south of Liang and Chu. He was again made director of the masters of writing, and from the puppet kings and dukes downward, none failed to court his favor. At that time Murong Wei held the puppet throne, with Murong Ke as grand tutor. Ke held Chui in the highest regard and often told Wei, “The Prince of Wu’s gifts as statesman and commander surpass mine tenfold. Because I was the elder, the late emperor set me above him; when I am gone, I beg Your Majesty to place the realm in the Prince of Wu’s hands—truly to honor the worthy and share power wisely. ” After the victory over Huan Wen at Fangtou, his military fame soared. Murong Ping came to hate and fear him and plotted his death. Fearing for his life, Chui fled with the heir apparent Quan to Fu Jian.
4
自恪卒後，堅密有圖暐之謀，憚垂威名而未發。 及聞其至，堅大悅，郊迎執手，禮之甚重。 堅相王猛惡垂雄略，勸堅殺之。 堅不從，以為冠軍將軍，封賓都侯，食華陰之五百戶。 王猛伐洛，引全為參軍。 猛乃令人詭傳垂語于全曰：「吾已東還，汝可為計也。 」全信之，乃奔暐。 猛表全叛狀，垂懼而東奔，及藍田，為追騎所獲。 堅引見東堂，慰勉之曰：「卿家國失和，委身投朕。 賢子志不忘本，猶懷首丘。 《書》不云乎：「父父子子，無相及也。 」卿何為過懼而狼狽若斯也！ 」於是復垂爵位，恩待如初。
After Ke’s death Fu Jian quietly planned an attack on Wei but held back, dreading Chui’s reputation. Learning that Chui had come, Fu Jian was overjoyed, went out to meet him beyond the city, clasped his hand, and honored him lavishly. Wang Meng, Fu Jian’s chief minister, distrusted Chui’s bold designs and urged the emperor to put him to death. Fu Jian refused, instead appointing him general of the champions, enfeoffing him as Marquis of Bindu, and granting him an income of five hundred households at Huayin. When Wang Meng marched on Luoyang, he took Quan onto his staff. Meng then arranged for someone to forge a message to Quan in Chui’s name: “I have already gone back east; you must decide for yourself. ” Quan believed the tale and fled to Wei. Meng reported Quan’s treason; terrified, Chui fled eastward and was overtaken at Lantian by pursuit troops. Fu Jian received him in the Eastern Hall and reassured him: “Discord drove you from your own house; you came and placed yourself in Our hands. Your fine son has not forgotten his origins and still longs for his native hills. Does not the Book say, ‘Let the father be father and the son son, without implicating one another’? Why then such terror, why such panic! ” He then restored Chui’s rank and treated him as kindly as ever.
5
及堅擒暐，垂隨堅入鄴，收集諸子，對之悲慟，見其故吏，有不悅之色。 前郎中令高弼私於垂曰：「大王以命世之姿，遭無妄之運，迍邅棲伏，艱亦至矣。 天啟嘉會，靈命暫遷，此乃鴻漸之始，龍變之初，深願仁慈有以慰之。 且夫高世之略必懷遺俗之規，方當網漏吞舟，以弘苞養之義； 收納舊臣之胄，以成為山之功，奈何以一怒捐之？ 竊為大王不取。 」垂深納之。 垂在堅朝，歷位京兆尹，進封泉州侯，所在征伐，皆在大功。
When Fu Jian took Wei, Chui entered Ye at his side, gathered his sons, and wept before them; some of his old retainers he met wore faces of displeasure. His former director of the masters of writing, Gao Bi, took him aside and said, “Your Highness, born to shake the age, has suffered undeserved ruin; to wander in exile and live in hiding is hardship enough. Heaven now offers a rare conjunction, and the mandate has briefly changed hands—this is the start of your rise, the first stirrings of the dragon’s change. I pray you will meet it with a generous heart. A man who aims at greatness must hold to counsels that transcend the crowd; now is the time to cast a wide net and spare the great, to show mercy on a grand scale; to gather the sons of your former officers and build a mountain of loyalty—how can you lose them in one burst of wrath? I humbly submit that this is no course for Your Highness. ” Chui took the advice to heart. At Fu Jian’s court he rose to metropolitan magistrate of Jingzhao, was promoted to Marquis of Quanzhou, and in every campaign earned distinguished merit.
6
堅之敗於淮南也，垂軍獨全，堅以千餘騎奔垂。 垂世子寶言於垂曰：「家國傾喪，皇綱廢馳，至尊明命著之圖籙，當隆中興之業，建少康之功。 但時來之運未至，故韜光俟奮耳。 今天厭亂德，凶眾土崩，可謂乾啟神機，授之於我。 千載一時，今其會也，宜恭承皇天之意，因而取之。 且夫立大功者不顧小節，行大仁者不念小惠。 秦既蕩覆二京，空辱神器，仇恥之深，莫甚於此，願不以意氣微恩而忘社稷之重。 五木之祥，今其至矣。 」垂曰：「汝言是也。 然彼以赤心投命，若何害之！ 苟天所棄，圖之多便。 且縱令北還，更待其釁，既不負宿心，可以義取天下。 」垂弟德進曰：「夫鄰國相吞，有自來矣。 秦強而並燕，秦弱而圖之，此為報仇雪辱，豈所謂負宿心也！ 昔鄧祁侯不納三甥之言，終為楚所滅； 吳王夫差違子胥之諫，取禍句踐。 前事之不忘，後事之師表也。 願不棄湯、武之成蹤，追韓信之敗跡，乘彼土崩，恭行天罰，斬逆氐，復宗祀，建中興，繼洪烈，天下大機，弗宜失也。 若釋數萬之眾，授幹將之柄，是郤天時而待後害，非至計也。 語曰：'當斷不斷，反受其亂。 '願兄無疑。 」垂曰：「吾昔為太傅所不容，投身于秦主，又為王猛所譖，復見昭亮，國士之禮每深，報德之分未一。 如使秦運必窮，歷數歸我者，授首之便，何慮無之。 關西之地，會非吾有，自當有擾之者，吾可端拱而定關東。 君子不怙亂，不為禍先，且可觀之。 」乃以兵屬堅。 初，寶在長安，與韓黃、李根等因讌摴蒱，寶危坐整容，誓之曰：「世云摴蒱有神，豈虛也哉！ 若富貴可期，頻得三盧。 」於是三擲盡盧，寶拜而受賜，故云五木之祥。
When Fu Jian was routed at Huainan, only Chui’s force came through unscathed; Fu Jian rode to him with a little over a thousand cavalry. His heir Bao urged him: “Our house and state are shattered and the imperial order broken; Heaven’s mandate for Your Highness is written in the prognostications—you should restore the dynasty as Shaokang did. Only the hour had not yet come, so you hid your brilliance and bided your time. Now Heaven rejects Qin’s corruption and the enemy host collapses—the moment is ours, as if Qian itself had opened the way. Such a chance comes once in a thousand years, and it is here; you should accept Heaven’s will and seize it. Those who accomplish great deeds do not fuss over minor scruples; those who practice great kindness do not cling to small debts of gratitude. Qin has already sacked the two capitals and defiled the imperial regalia; no hatred runs deeper—do not let a private debt of thanks blind you to the fate of the realm. The omen of the dice—the hour foretold has come. ” Chui said, “You speak truly. Yet he came to me with an open heart—how can I do him harm! If Heaven has cast him off, opportunity to strike will not be lacking. And if we let him go north and wait until he gives cause, we keep faith with our old pledge and may still win the empire by righteous means. His younger brother Murong De pressed him: “Neighboring states devour one another—that is the way of the world. When Qin was strong it swallowed Yan; now that Qin is weak we turn on it—this is vengeance, not ingratitude! Of old, Marquis Ai of Deng ignored his three nephews’ counsel and was destroyed by Chu; King Fuchai of Wu spurned Wu Zixu’s advice and brought ruin on himself at Goujian’s hands. To remember the past is to instruct the future. Do not spurn the example of Tang and Wu, nor repeat Han Xin’s mistake; while their realm collapses, strike in Heaven’s name, cut down the Di usurpers, restore the ancestral rites, rebuild our house, and renew the great mandate—this chance must not slip away. To dismiss an army of thousands and put the sword back in his hand is to reject Heaven’s moment and invite future disaster—no wise course. As the proverb says, “He who hesitates when he should strike will be struck in turn.” Brother, put your doubts aside. ” Chui replied, “Once the grand tutor would not keep me, and I cast my lot with the Lord of Qin; Wang Meng slandered me, yet he cleared my name and honored me as a peer of the realm. His kindness ran deep, and I have not yet repaid it. If Qin’s fate is truly spent and the mandate is mine, how hard will it be to take his head when the time comes? The west is not mine to hold; others will tear it apart. I can wait at ease and secure the east. A gentleman does not profit from chaos or strike the first blow; for now let us watch and wait. ” He then turned his army over to Fu Jian. Earlier at Chang’an, Bao had played liubo at a feast with Han Huang, Li Gen, and others; sitting rigidly upright, he vowed: “They say the dice have a god—surely it is so! If fortune is near, grant me three casts of lu in a row. ” He threw three times and each time cast all lu; Bao bowed to accept the sign—hence “the omen of the dice.”
7
堅至澠池，垂請至鄴展拜陵墓，因張國威刑，以安戎狄。 堅許之，權翼諫曰：「垂爪牙名將，所謂今之韓、白，世豪東夏，志不為人用。 頃以避禍歸誠，非慕德而至，列土幹城未可以滿其志，冠軍之號豈足以稱其心！ 且垂猶鷹也，饑則附人，飽便高颺，遇風塵之會，必有陵霄之志。 惟宜急其羈靽，不可任其所欲。 」堅不從，遣其將李蠻、閔亮、尹國率眾三千送垂，又遣石越戍鄴，張蠔戍并州。
At Mianchi, Chui asked leave to visit Ye and pay homage at the tombs, and to awe the frontier peoples by a show of Qin’s authority. Fu Jian agreed. Quan Yi objected: “Chui is a general of fang and claw, a Han Xin or Bai Qi of our day, the greatest warrior of the eastern lands, and his heart is not for service. He came only to flee disaster, not from love of your virtue; a fief and a garrison cannot fill his ambition, nor can the title general of the champions satisfy him! Chui is like a hawk: hungry, he perches on your hand; fed, he flies away; give him a season of turmoil and he will aim for the sky. Bind him quickly; do not let him have his way. ” Fu Jian refused and sent Li Man, Min Liang, and Yin Guo with three thousand men to escort Chui, while posting Shi Yue at Ye and Zhang Hao in Bing Province.
8
時堅子丕先在鄴，及垂至，丕館之於鄴西，垂具說淮南敗狀。 會堅將苻暉告丁零翟斌聚眾謀逼洛陽，歪謂垂曰：「惟斌兄弟因王師小失，敢肆凶勃，子母之軍，殆難為敵，非冠軍英略，莫可以滅也。 欲相煩一行可乎？ 」垂曰：「下官殿下之鷹犬，敢不惟命是聽。 」於是大賜金帛，一無所受，惟請舊田園。 丕許之，配垂兵二千，遣其將苻飛龍率氐騎一千為垂之副。 丕戒飛龍曰：「卿王室肺腑，年秩雖卑，其實帥也。 垂為三軍之統，卿為謀垂之主，用兵制勝之權，防微杜貳之略，委之於卿，卿其勉之。 」垂請入鄴城拜廟，丕不許。 乃潛服而入，亭吏禁之，垂怒，斬吏燒亭而去。 石越言於丕曰：「垂之在燕，破國亂家，及投命聖朝，蒙超常之遇，忽敢輕侮方鎮，殺吏焚亭，反形已露，終為亂階。 將老兵疲，可襲而取之矣。 」歪曰：「淮南之敗，眾散親離，而垂侍衛聖躬，誠不可忘。 」越曰：「垂既不忠於燕，其肯盡忠於我乎！ 且其亡虜也，主上寵同功舊，不能銘澤誓忠，而首謀為亂，今不擊之，必為後害。 」丕不從。 越退而告人曰：「公父子好存小仁，不顧天下大計，吾屬終當為鮮卑虜矣。」
Fu Jian’s son Pi was already at Ye; when Chui arrived, Pi quartered him west of the city, and Chui gave a full account of the disaster at Huainan. Word came that Fu Hui reported the Dingling chief Zhai Bin had risen against Luoyang. Pi told Chui, “The Bin brothers exploit our setback and grow bold; mother and son together will be a hard foe—only your genius can break them. Will you undertake the campaign for us? ” Chui answered, “I am Your Highness’s hound—your servant dares not refuse. ” Pi offered rich gifts of gold and silk, but Chui took nothing, asking only for his old estates. Pi agreed, gave him two thousand men, and attached Fu Feilong with a thousand Di horsemen as his lieutenant. Pi cautioned Feilong: “You are of the imperial clan; young as you are, you are in truth the commander. Chui leads the army, but you are to watch him; all power to command and all counsel to prevent treachery are yours—do your utmost. Chui asked to enter Ye and bow at the ancestral shrine; Pi refused. He slipped in disguised; the gate officer barred him. Chui in a rage killed the man, burned the post, and left. Shi Yue warned Pi: “In Yan, Chui ruined kingdoms; Qin favored him beyond measure, yet he insults your authority, kills your officer, and burns his post—his rebellion is plain and he will bring trouble. His men are tired—we can strike now and seize him. ” Pi replied, “After Huainan he alone guarded the emperor—such loyalty must not be forgotten. ” Yue answered, “If he betrayed Yan, how will he be true to us! He is a fugitive whom our lord treated like an old companion; instead of gratitude he plots revolt—if we do not strike now, we will regret it later. ” Pi would not listen. Yue left muttering, “Father and son cherish petty kindness and ignore the realm—we shall end as captives of the Xianbei.”
9
垂至河內，殺飛龍，悉誅氐兵，召募遠近，眾至三萬，濟河焚橋，令曰：「吾本外假秦聲，內規興復。 亂法者軍有常刑，奉命者賞不逾日，天下既定，封爵有差，不相負也。」
At Henei Chui killed Feilong, slaughtered the Di detachment, rallied men until he had thirty thousand, crossed the river and burned the bridge, and declared, “Outwardly I served Qin; inwardly I sought to restore our house. Break discipline and the army will punish you; obey orders and reward comes within the day. When the empire is won, ranks will be granted fairly—we shall not betray one another.”
10
翟斌聞垂之將濟河也，遣使推垂為盟主。 垂距之曰：「吾父子寄命秦朝，危而獲濟，荷主上不世之恩，蒙更生之惠，雖曰君臣，義深父子，豈可因其小隙，便懷二三。 吾本救豫州，不赴君等，何為斯議而及於我！ 」垂進欲襲據洛陽，故見苻暉以臣節，退又未審斌之誠款，故以此言距之。 垂至洛陽，暉閉門距守，不與垂通。 斌又遣長史河南郭通說垂，乃許之。 斌率眾會垂，勸稱尊號，垂曰：「新興侯，國之正統，孤之君也。 若以諸君之力，得平關東，當以大義喻秦，奉迎反正。 無上自尊，非孤心也。 」謀於眾曰：「洛陽四面受敵，北阻大河，至於控馭燕、趙，非形勝之便，不如北取鄴都，據之而制天下。 」眾咸以為然。 乃引師而東，遣建威將軍王騰起浮橋于石門。
Learning that Chui was crossing the river, Zhai Bin sent envoys offering to make him leader of the alliance. Chui declined: "My father and I cast our lot with Qin; in danger we were rescued and owed the emperor an unexampled debt. Though called subject and sovereign, our tie was as close as father and son—how could I turn traitor over a petty slight? I came to save Yuzhou, not to join your rebellion—why drag me into this? Chui still meant to take Luoyang, so he played the loyal subject before Fu Hui; uncertain of Bin's sincerity, he spoke thus to put him off. At Luoyang, Hui shut the gates and would not receive him. Bin sent his chief clerk Guo Tong to win him over, and Chui at last agreed. Bin joined forces with Chui and urged him to take the throne. Chui replied, "The Marquis of Xinxing is the rightful heir of Qin and my lord. If we pacify the east with your aid, we should restore Qin by righteous means and welcome the emperor home. To set myself above him without cause is not my wish. He told his officers, "Luoyang is exposed on every side and cut off by the Yellow River; it is no base from which to hold Yan and Zhao. Better to seize Ye in the north and rule the realm from there. All agreed. He marched east and sent Wang Teng to throw a pontoon bridge across at Shimen.
11
初，垂之發鄴中，子農及兄子楷、紹，北子宙，為苻丕所留。 及誅飛龍，遣田生密告農等，使起兵趙、魏以相應。 於是農、宙奔列人，楷、紹奔辟陽，眾咸應之。 農西招庫辱官偉於上黨，東引乞特歸於東阿，各率眾數萬赴之，眾至十餘萬。 丕遣石越討農，為農所敗，斬越于陳。
When Chui left Ye, his son Nong, his nephews Kai and Shao, and his son Zhou had been held hostage by Fu Pi. After killing Feilong he sent Tian Sheng secretly to tell Nong and the others to rise in Zhao and Wei. Nong and Zhou fled to Lieren, Kai and Shao to Piyang, and the people rallied to them. Nong called Guan Wei of the Kulumu from Shangdang in the west and Qi Te Gui from Dong'e in the east; each brought tens of thousands, until their force passed a hundred thousand. Pi sent Shi Yue against Nong, who routed him and killed him at Chen.
12
垂引兵至滎陽，乙太元八年自稱大將軍、大都督、燕王，承制行事，建元曰燕元。 令稱統府，府置四佐，王公已下稱臣，凡所封拜，一如王者，以翟斌為建義大將軍，封河南王； 翟檀為柱國大將軍、弘農王； 弟德為車騎大將軍、范陽王； 兄子楷征西大將軍、太原王。 眾至二十餘萬，濟自石門，長驅攻鄴。 農、楷、紹、宙等率眾會垂。 立子寶為燕王太子，封功臣為公侯伯子男者百餘人。
At Xingyang, in the eighth year of the Taiyuan era, Chui proclaimed himself grand general, grand commander, and Prince of Yan, acting with imperial authority under the era name Yan Yuan. His headquarters was styled the supreme command; four aides were appointed; from puppet kings downward all styled him sovereign; enfeoffments followed royal precedent—Zhai Bin was made general who establishes righteousness and Prince of Henan; Zhai Tan became grand general of the state and Prince of Hongnong; his brother De, general of chariots and cavalry and Prince of Fanyang; his nephew Kai, general who pacifies the west and Prince of Taiyuan. His army swelled past two hundred thousand; crossing at Shimen, he bore down on Ye. Nong, Kai, Shao, Zhou, and the rest joined him with their forces. He named Bao crown prince of Yan and enfeoffed over a hundred followers as dukes, marquises, earls, viscounts, and barons.
13
苻丕乃遣侍郎姜讓謂垂曰：「往歲大駕失據，君保衛鑾輿，勤王誠義，邁蹤前烈。 宜述修前規，終忠貞之節，奈何棄崇山之功，為此過舉！ 過貴能改，先賢之嘉事也。 深宜詳思，悟猶未晚。 」垂謂讓曰：「孤受主上不世之恩，故欲安全長樂公，使盡眾赴京師，然後修復家國之業，與秦永為鄰好。 何故暗於機運，不以鄴見歸也？ 大義滅親，況于意氣之顧！ 公若迷而不返者，孤亦欲竊兵勢耳。 今事已然，恐單馬乞命不可得也。 」讓厲色責垂曰：「將軍不容于家國，投命於聖朝，燕之尺土，將軍豈有分乎！ 主上與將軍風殊類別，臭味不同，奇將軍于一見，托將軍以斷金，寵逾宗舊，任齊懿籓，自古君臣冥契之重，豈甚此邪！ 方付將軍以六尺之孤，萬里之命，奈何王師小敗，便有二圖！ 夫師起無名，終則弗成，天之所廢，人不能支。 將軍起無名之師，而欲興天所廢，竊未見其可。 長樂公主上之元子，聲德邁于唐、衛，居陝東之任，為朝廷維城，其可束手輸將軍以百城之地！ 大夫死王事，國君死社稷，將軍欲裂冠毀冕，拔本塞源者，自可任將軍兵勢，何復多雲。 但念將軍以七十之年，懸首白旗，高世之忠，忽為逆鬼，竊為將軍痛之。 」垂默然。 左右勸垂殺之，垂曰：「古者兵交，使在其間，犬各吠非其主，何所問也！ 」乃遣讓歸。
Fu Pi sent the attendant Jiang Rang to tell Chui, "When the emperor was driven from the capital, you alone guarded him; your loyalty on that day outshines the ancients. Return to your old faith and finish what you began—why throw away your glory for this reckless course! The wise praise a man who mends his ways. Consider well—it is not too late to turn back. Chui told Rang, "I owed the emperor an unexampled debt and meant to see the Duke of Changle safe, send him with all his men to the capital, and then rebuild our house while keeping peace with Qin. Why refuse to read the times and hand over Ye? When duty demands it, kinship counts for nothing—still less a private grudge! If you will not yield, I will take what I need by force. Matters stand as they do; do not expect mercy on a lone horse. Rang rebuked him harshly: "You had no place in Yan and came begging shelter to Qin—what claim have you to a foot of Yan's soil! The emperor and you are not of one blood, yet he honored you at first sight, bound you with oath, raised you above his oldest companions, and gave you power equal to a great prince—never was trust between sovereign and subject heavier! He would have left you his orphan and the realm—why, after one setback, plot treason! Armies without just cause never prevail; what Heaven casts off, no man can uphold. You march without righteous cause and would revive what Heaven has doomed—I see no hope in it. The Duke of Changle is the emperor's eldest son, famed beyond Tang and Wei, guardian of the east—he will never surrender a hundred cities to you! Ministers die for their prince, princes for their altars—if you mean to tear down the throne, use your soldiers and spare us the sermon. But think: at seventy, your head on a stake—the loyalty of ages turned rebel—I grieve for you. Chui said nothing. His attendants urged him to kill Rang. Chui said, "When armies meet, envoys pass between them; every dog barks for his own master—why blame him? He sent Rang away unharmed.
14
垂上表於苻堅曰：「臣才非古人，致禍起蕭牆，身嬰時難，歸命聖朝。 陛下恩深周、漢，猥叨微顧之遇，位為列將，爵忝通侯，誓在戮力輸誠，常懼不及。 去夏桓沖送死，一擬雲消，回討鄖城，俘馘萬計，斯誠陛下神算之奇，頗亦愚臣忘死之效。 方將飲馬桂州，懸旌閩會，不圖天助亂德，大駕班師。 陛下單馬奔臣，臣奉衛匪貳，豈陛下聖明鑒臣單心，皇天后土實亦知之。 臣奉詔北巡，受制長樂。 然丕外失眾心，內多猜忌，今臣野次外庭，不聽謁廟。 丁零逆豎寇逼豫州，丕迫臣單赴，限以師程，惟給弊卒二千，盡無兵杖，復令飛龍潛為刺客。 及至洛陽，平原公暉復不信納。 臣竊惟進無淮陰功高之慮，退無李廣失利之愆，懼有青蠅，交亂白黑，丁零夷夏以臣忠而見疑，乃推臣為盟主。 臣受託善始，不遂令終，泣望西京，揮涕即邁。 軍次石門，所在雲赴，雖復周武之會于孟津，漢祖之集於垓下，不期之眾，實有甚焉。 欲令長樂公盡眾赴難，以禮發遣，而丕固守匹夫之志，不達變通之理。 臣息農收集故營，以備不虞，而石越傾鄴城之眾，輕相掩襲，兵陣未交，越已隕首。 臣既單車懸軫，歸者如雲，斯實天符，非臣之力。 且鄴者臣國舊都，應即惠及，然後西面受制，永守東籓，上成陛下遇臣之意，下全愚臣感報之誠。 今進師圍鄴，並喻丕以天時人事。 而丕不察機運，杜門自守，時出挑戰，鋒戈屢交，恆恐飛矢誤中，以傷陛下天性之念。 臣之此誠，未簡神聽，輒遏兵止銳，不敢竊攻。 夫運有推移，去來常事，惟陛下察之。」
Chui wrote to Fu Jian: "My gifts do not match the ancients; trouble rose within the palace; in hardship I came to serve Your Majesty. Your grace exceeds Zhou and Han; you raised me from exile to general and marquis, and I swore to serve you with all my strength. Last summer Huan Chong was destroyed at a stroke; at Yuncheng we took tens of thousands of prisoners—this was your divine strategy and my small share of the victory. We were about to march on the south when Heaven aided Qin's ruin and the emperor withdrew his host. You fled to me on a lone horse and I guarded you faithfully—Heaven and earth know my heart, as you must. I was ordered north under the Duke of Changle's command. Pi lost the people's trust and grew suspicious; now I camp outside the walls and may not enter the shrine. The Dingling pressed Yuzhou; Pi sent me with two thousand broken men and no weapons, set a deadline, and planted Feilong to murder me. At Luoyang the Duke of Pingyuan would not admit me. I had neither Han Xin's fear of towering merit nor Li Guang's disgrace in defeat, yet I feared slander; because I was loyal, the Dingling made me their leader. I began in good faith but could not finish; weeping toward the western capital, I marched away. At Shimen men flocked to me in numbers greater than at Mengjin or Gaixia. I asked the Duke of Changle to march to the capital with all his men, but Pi clung to obstinacy and would not bend. My son Nong rallied our old camps for safety, but Shi Yue struck lightly from Ye and was slain before battle was joined. Though I rode alone, men returned to me like clouds—Heaven's sign, not my power. Ye is my ancestral seat and should be restored to me; then I would serve you from the east forever, fulfilling both your kindness and my debt. I now besiege Ye and have told Pi what Heaven and circumstance demand. Pi shuts his gates and fights on, and I fear a stray shaft may wound your fatherly heart. This is my sincerity; not yet heard in Heaven, I hold my army back and dare not strike in secret. Fortune turns; coming and going are the way of things—may Your Majesty understand."
15
堅報曰：「朕以不德，忝承靈命，君臨萬邦，三十年矣。 遐方幽裔，莫不來庭，惟東南一隅，敢違王命。 朕爰奮六師，恭行天罰，而玄機不吊，王師敗績。 賴卿忠誠之至，輔翼朕躬，社稷之不隕，卿之力也。 《詩》云：『中心藏之，何日忘之。 』方任卿以元相，爵卿以郡侯，庶弘濟艱難，敬酬勳烈，何圖伯夷忽毀冰操，柳惠倏為淫夫！ 覽表惋然，有慚朝士。 卿既不容於本朝，匹馬而投命，朕則寵卿以將位，禮卿以上賓，任同舊臣，爵齊勳輔，歃血斷金，披心相付。 謂卿食椹懷音，保之偕老。 豈意畜水覆舟，養獸反害，悔之噬臍，將何所及！ 誕言駭眾，誇擬非常，周武之事，豈卿庸人所可論哉！ 失籠之鳥，非羅所羈； 脫網之鯨，豈罟所制！ 翹陸任懷，何須聞也。 念卿垂老，老而為賊，生為叛臣，死為逆鬼，侏張幽顯，布毒存亡，中原士女，何痛如之！ 朕之曆運興喪，豈復由卿！ 但長樂、平原以未立之年，遇卿於兩都，慮其經略未稱朕心，所恨者此焉而已。」
Fu Jian answered: "With little virtue I have held the throne thirty years. All the world came to court save the southeast, which alone defied me. I raised the six armies to punish them, but Heaven did not favor us and my host was broken. Only your loyalty kept the throne standing—that is your doing. The Odes say, "Stored in the heart—when shall we forget?" I meant to make you chief minister and marquis, to reward your service—who thought Bo Yi would break his vow and Liu Xia Hui turn debauched! Your letter grieves and shames me before the court. You had no home in Yan and came to me alone; I made you a general and honored you as a guest, equal to my oldest servants, bound by oath and trust. I thought you would remember kindness and stand with me to the end. Who thought the water you store would sink the boat, the beast you feed would turn and bite—regret comes too late! You frighten the crowd with boasts and compare yourself to King Wu—such talk from a man like you! A bird escaped from the cage is caught by no net; a whale that has broken the seine—who can hold it! Go where you will—I need not hear more. Old as you are, you turn bandit—living a traitor, dead a rebel—you poison land and underworld; what pain for the people of the heartland! Whether my house stands or falls is no longer in your hands! Only for Changle and Pingyuan, still young when you knew them at the two capitals, I fear their plans were not to my liking—that is my sole regret."
16
垂攻拔鄴郛，丕固守中城，垂塹而圍之，分遣老弱于魏郡、肥鄉，築新興城以置輜重，擁漳水以灌之。
Chui took the outer city of Ye while Pi held the inner citadel; he trenched, flooded it with the Zhang River, sent the weak to Feixiang, and built Xinxing to store supplies.
17

Bin is a barbarian who rose by chance; his brothers became kings—since Huan Dou none had risen so high. Drunk with success, he asks for more—his wits are gone; he will not live out the year. Chui still bore with him and announced, "Prince Zhai deserves the highest post, but the government is not yet established—this office must wait. When the realm is pacified, we shall decide. 19
晉龍驤將軍劉牢之率眾救苻丕，至鄴，垂逆戰，敗績，遂撤鄴圍，退屯新城。 垂自新城北走，牢之追垂，連戰皆敗。 又戰於五橋澤，王師敗績，德及隆引兵要之於五丈橋，牢之馳馬跳五丈澗，會苻丕救至而免。

Chui retreated north from Xincheng; Laizhi pursued and defeated him again and again. 20

21

22

翟成長史鮮于得斬成而降，垂入行唐，悉坑其眾。
Cheng's chief clerk Xianyu De killed him and surrendered; Chui entered Xingtang and massacred the garrison.

Fu Pi abandoned Ye and fled to Bing Province.
25
慕容農攻克令支，斬徐岩兄弟。 時伐高句驪，復遼東、玄菟二郡，還屯龍城。
Murong Nong took Lingzhi and executed Xu Yan and his brothers. They campaigned against Koguryo, recovered Liaodong and Xuantu, and returned to Longcheng.
26
遣其征西慕容楷、衛軍慕容麟、鎮南慕容紹、征虜慕容宙等攻苻堅冀州牧苻定、鎮東苻紹、幽州牧苻謨、鎮北苻亮。 楷與定等書，喻以禍福，定等悉降。

27
垂留其太子寶守中山，率諸將南攻翟遼，以楷為前鋒都督。 遼之部眾皆燕、趙人也，咸曰：「太原王之子，吾之父母。 」相率歸附。 遼懼，遣使請降。 垂至黎陽，遼肉袒謝罪，垂厚撫之。
Chui left Bao at Zhongshan and marched south against Zhai Liao with Kai as vanguard. Liao's men were Yan and Zhao folk who said, "The Prince of Taiyuan's son is our own kin. They went over to Kai in droves. Terrified, Liao sent envoys to surrender. At Liyang, Liao came bare-chested to beg pardon, and Chui treated him kindly.
28
為其太子寶起承華觀，以寶錄尚書政事，巨細皆委之，重總大綱而已。 立其夫人段氏為皇后。 又以寶領侍中、大單于、驃騎大將軍、幽州牧。 建留台于龍城，以高陽王慕容隆錄留台尚書事。 時慕容暐及諸宗室為苻堅所害者，並招魂葬之。
He built the Chenghua Tower for Bao, who took charge of the masters of writing; great matters and small were left to him while Chui kept only the broad reins. He made his consort Lady Duan empress. Bao was made palace attendant, grand chanyu, general of agile cavalry, and governor of You Province. He set up a rear court at Longcheng and put Murong Long in charge of its records. Murong Wei and the royal kin slain by Fu Jian were given memorial burials with empty coffins.
29
清河太守賀耕聚眾定陵以叛，南應翟遼，慕容農討斬之，毀定陵城。 進師入鄴，以鄴城廣難固，築鳳陽門大道之東為隔城。
He Geng, administrator of Qinghe, rebelled at Dingling and joined Zhai Liao; Murong Nong killed him and razed the city. He entered Ye, found the city too vast to hold, and built a new walled quarter east of the Fengyang Gate avenue.
30
其尚書郎婁會上疏曰：「三年之喪，天下之達制，兵荒殺禮，遂以一切取士。 人心奔兢，苟求榮進，至乃身冒縗絰，以赴時役，豈必殉忠於國家，亦昧利於其間也。 聖王設教，不以顛沛而虧其道，不以喪亂而變其化，故能杜豪兢之門，塞奔波之路。 陛下鐘百王之季，廓中興之業，天下漸平，兵革方偃，誠宜蠲蕩瑕穢，率由舊章。 吏遭大喪，聽終三年之禮，則四方知化，人斯服禮。 」垂不從。
Lou Hui of the masters of writing memorialized: "Three years' mourning is the universal rule, but war and famine have killed ritual, and offices are filled without regard to decency. Men scramble for promotion, even wearing mourning garb while taking office—not from loyalty, but from greed. The sages held to their teaching in good times and bad; so they closed the door to reckless ambition and the path of frantic striving. Your Majesty stands at the end of an age of kings and builds restoration; the realm grows calm and war subsides—it is time to cleanse abuses and restore the old rites. Let officials observe three years' mourning for their parents, and the realm will learn ritual again. Chui refused.
31
翟遼死，子釗代立，攻逼鄴城，慕容農擊走之。 垂引師伐釗於滑台，次於黎陽津，釗于南岸距守，諸將惡其兵精，咸諫不宜濟河。 垂笑曰：「堅子何能為，吾今為鯽等殺之。 」遂徙營就西津，為牛皮船百餘艘，載疑兵列杖，溯流而上。 釗先以大眾備黎陽，見垂向西津，乃棄營西距。 垂潛遣其桂林王慕容鎮、驃騎慕容國于黎陽津夜濟，壁于河南。 釗聞而奔還，士眾疲渴，走歸滑台，釗攜妻子率數百騎北趣白鹿山。 農追擊，盡擒其眾，釗單騎奔長子。 釗所統七郡戶三萬八千皆安堵如故。 徙徐州流人七千餘戶于黎陽。
When Zhai Liao died, his son Zhao pressed Ye; Murong Nong drove him back. Chui marched on Zhao at Huatai and camped at Liyang Ford; Zhao held the south bank; his officers feared Zhao's seasoned troops and urged him not to cross. Chui laughed: "What can that boy do? I will kill him for you today. He shifted camp to the western ford, built a hundred boats of oxhide loaded with dummy troops, and sent them upstream. Zhao massed his army at Liyang; seeing Chui move west, he abandoned his camp and marched west to meet him. Chui secretly sent Murong Zhen and Murong Guo to cross at Liyang by night and fortify the south bank. Zhao turned back, but his men were spent; he fled to Huatai, then rode north toward Bailu Mountain with his family and a few hundred horsemen. Nong pursued and took the whole army; Zhao alone escaped to Changzi. The seven commanderies under Zhao, thirty-eight thousand households, were left undisturbed. He resettled more than seven thousand refugees from Xuzhou at Liyang.
32
於是議征長子。 諸將咸諫，以慕容永未有釁，連歲征役，士卒疲怠，請俟他年。 垂將從之，及聞慕容德之策，笑曰：「吾計決矣。 且吾投老，扣囊底智，足以克之，不復留逆賊以累子孫也。 」乃發步騎七萬，遣其丹陽王慕容贊、龍驤張崇攻永弟支于晉陽。 永遣其將刁雲、慕容鐘率眾五萬屯潞川。 垂遣慕容楷出自滏口，慕容農入自壺關，垂頓於鄴之西南，月餘不進。 永謂垂詭道伐之，乃攝諸軍還杜太行軹關。 垂進師入自天井關，至於壺壁。 永率精卒五萬來距，阻河曲以自固，馳使請戰。 垂列陣於壺避之南，農、楷分為二翼，慕容國伏千兵於深澗，與永大戰。 垂引軍偽退，永追奔數里，國發伏兵馳斷其後，楷、農夾擊之，永師大敗，斬首八千餘級，永奔還長子。 慕容贊攻克晉陽。 垂進圍長子，永將賈韜潛為內應。 垂進軍入城，永奔北門，為前驅所獲，於是數而戮之，並其所署公卿刁雲等三十餘人。 永所統新舊八郡戶七萬六千八百及乘輿、服御、伎樂、珍寶悉獲之，於是品物具矣。
They then debated an expedition against Changzi. His officers objected that Murong Yong had given no pretext, that years of war had exhausted the troops, and urged delay. Chui was inclined to agree until he heard Murong De's plan; then he laughed and said, "My mind is made up. I am old, but the wit left in my bag is enough to crush him—I will not leave this rebel to plague my sons. He raised seventy thousand foot and horse, and sent Murong Zan and Zhang Chong against Yong's brother Zhi at Jinyang. Yong sent Diao Yun and Murong Zhong with fifty thousand men to Luchuan. Chui sent Kai through Fukou and Nong through Huguan, while he himself camped southwest of Ye for more than a month without advancing. Yong thought Chui was feinting and withdrew his armies to guard Zhifuguan on the Taihang. Chui marched through Tianjing Pass to Hubi. Yong came with fifty thousand picked troops, blocked the river bend, and challenged him to fight. Chui formed line south of Hubi; Nong and Kai took the wings; Murong Guo hid a thousand men in a ravine and joined battle with Yong. Chui feigned retreat; Yong pursued for miles until Guo's ambush struck his rear and Kai and Nong closed from the flanks; Yong was routed, eight thousand were slain, and he fled to Changzi. Murong Zan took Jinyang. Chui besieged Changzi; Yong's general Jia Tao plotted treason within the walls. Chui entered the city; Yong ran for the north gate and was seized; Chui had him condemned and executed, with Diao Yun and more than thirty of his officials. He took Yong's eight commanderies with seventy-six thousand eight hundred households, the imperial carriage, regalia, musicians, and treasure—his house was complete.
33
使慕容農略地河南，攻廩丘、陽城，皆克之，太山、琅邪諸郡皆委城奔潰，農進師臨海，置守宰而還。 垂告捷于龍城之廟。
Murong Nong pacified Henan, took Linqiu and Yangcheng, and swept Taishan and Langye; he marched to the sea, installed officials, and returned. Chui reported victory at the Longcheng ancestral temple.
34
遣其太子寶及農與慕容麟等率眾八萬伐魏，慕容德、慕容紹以步騎一萬八千為寶後繼。 魏聞寶將至，徙往河西。 寶進師臨河，懼不敢濟。 還次參合，忽有大風黑氣，狀若堤防，或高或下，臨覆軍上。 沙門支曇猛言於寶曰：「風氣暴迅，魏軍將至之候，宜遣兵禦之。 」寶笑而不納。 曇猛固以為言，乃遣麟率騎三萬為後殿，以禦非常。 麟以曇猛言為虛，縱騎遊獵。 俄而黃霧四塞，日月晦冥，是夜魏師大至，三軍奔潰，寶與德等數千騎奔免，士眾還者十一二，紹死之。 初，寶至幽州，所乘車軸無故自折。 術士靳安以為大凶，固勸寶還，寶怒不從，故及於敗。
He sent Bao and Nong with Murong Lin and eighty thousand men against Wei; Murong De and Murong Shao followed with eighteen thousand. Hearing of Bao's approach, Wei withdrew west of the river. Bao reached the river but dared not cross. He withdrew to Canhe; suddenly a black wind rose like a wall over the camp. The monk Zhi Tanmeng warned Bao: "The wind is violent—a sign the Wei army is near; send troops to guard against it. Bao laughed and paid no heed. Tanmeng insisted; Bao sent Lin with thirty thousand horse as rear guard. Lin thought the warning empty and let his horsemen hunt at will. Yellow fog blotted out the sky; that night Wei fell upon them; the army collapsed; Bao and De escaped with a few thousand horse; barely one in ten returned; Shao was killed. When Bao reached Youzhou, the axle of his carriage snapped without cause. The diviner Jin An called it a dire omen and urged retreat; Bao in anger refused, and so met disaster.
35
寶恨參合之敗，屢言魏有可乘之機。 慕容德亦曰：「魏人狃於參合之役，有陵太子之心，宜及聖略，摧其銳志。 」垂從之，留德守中山，自率大眾出參合，鑿山開道，次於獵嶺。 遣寶與農出天門，征北慕容隆、征西慕容盛逾青山，襲魏陳留公泥于平城，陷之，收其眾三萬餘人而還。
Smarting from Canhe, Bao kept saying Wei could be struck again. Murong De added, "Wei, emboldened by Canhe, despises the crown prince—you should break their pride while you can. Chui agreed, left De at Zhongshan, led his main force from Canhe, cut a road through the mountains, and camped at Lie Ridge. He sent Bao and Nong through Tianmen, while Long and Sheng crossed Green Mountain, took Pingcheng from Wei's Duke Ni, and returned with thirty thousand men.
36
垂至參合，見往年戰處積骸如山，設弔祭之禮，死者父兄一時號哭，軍中皆慟。 垂慚憤歐血，因而寢疾，乘馬輿而進。 過平城北三十里，疾篤，築燕昌城而還。 寶等至雲中，聞垂疾，皆引歸。 及垂至於平城，或有叛者奔告魏曰：「垂病已亡，輿屍在軍。 」魏又聞參合大哭，以為信然，乃進兵追之，知平城已陷而退，還館陰山。 垂至上谷之沮陽，乙太元二十一年死，時年七十一，凡在位十三年。 遺令曰：「方今禍難尚殷，喪禮一從簡易，朝終夕殯，事訖成服，三日之後，釋服從政。 強寇伺隙，秘勿發喪，至京然後舉哀行服。 」寶等遵行之。 偽諡成武皇帝，廟號世祖，墓曰宣平陵。
At Canhe he found last year's field heaped with bones; he held mourning rites, and fathers and brothers of the dead wailed until the whole army wept. Shame and rage made him spit blood; he fell ill and was carried forward in a litter. Thirty li north of Pingcheng his illness worsened; he built Yanchang city and turned back. Bao and the others, reaching Yunzhong, heard Chui was ill and turned back. At Pingcheng deserters told Wei, "Chui is dead; his corpse rides in the army. Wei also heard the mourning at Canhe and believed it; they pursued until they learned Pingcheng had fallen, then withdrew to Yin Mountain. Chui reached Juyang in Shanggu and died in the twenty-first year of the Taiyuan era, aged seventy-one, having reigned thirteen years. His testament read: "Calamity still surrounds us; keep mourning simple—bury by evening, finish the rites, and after three days return to affairs of state. Enemies are near; conceal my death until you reach the capital, then mourn openly. Bao and the others obeyed. He was given the posthumous title Emperor Chengwu, temple name Shizu, and was buried at Xuanping Mausoleum.
