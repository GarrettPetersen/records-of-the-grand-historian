Luo Xian.
2
羅憲，字令則，襄陽人也。 父蒙，蜀廣漢太守。 憲年十三，能屬文，早知名。 師事譙周，周門人稱為子貢。 性方亮嚴整，待士無倦，輕財好施，不營產業。 仕蜀為太子舍人、宣信校尉。 再使于吳，吳人稱焉。 時黃皓預政，眾多附之，憲獨介然。 皓恚之，左遷巴東太守。 時大將軍閻宇都督巴東，拜憲領軍，為宇副貳。 魏之伐蜀，召宇西還，憲守永安城。 及成都敗，城中擾動，邊江長吏皆棄城走，憲斬亂者一人，百姓乃安。 知劉禪降，乃率所統臨於都亭三日。 吳聞蜀敗，遣將軍盛憲西上，外托救援，內欲襲憲。 憲曰：「本朝傾覆，吳為脣齒，不恤我難，而邀其利，吾寧當為降虜乎！ 」乃歸順。 於是繕甲完聚，厲以節義，士皆用命。 及鐘會、鄧艾死，百城無主，吳又使步協西征，憲大破其軍。 孫休怒，又遣陸抗助協。 憲距守經年，救援不至，城中疾疫太半。 或勸南出牂柯，北奔上庸，可以保全。 憲曰：「夫為人主，百姓所仰，既不能存，急而棄之，君子不為也。 畢命於此矣。 」會荊州刺史胡烈等救之，抗退。 加陵江將軍、監巴東軍事、使持節，領武陵太守。 泰始初入朝，詔曰：「憲忠烈果毅，有才策器幹，可給鼓吹。 」又賜山玄玉佩劍。 卒，贈使持節、安南將軍、武陵太守，追封西鄂侯，諡曰烈。
Luo Xian, whose courtesy name was Lingze, came from Xiangyang. His father, Luo Meng, had served as Administrator of Guanghan in Shu. At thirteen he could already write polished prose and had begun to make a name for himself. He studied under Qiao Zhou, whose students nicknamed him Zigong after Confucius's disciple. He was upright, principled, and disciplined; he treated scholars with inexhaustible courtesy; he cared little for money and gave freely, and he never bothered to build up landed property. In Shu he served as a gentleman attendant to the heir apparent and as Colonel Who Makes Trust Manifest. Sent twice as envoy to Wu, he earned high praise there. When the eunuch Huang Hao dominated the court and most officials curried his favor, Xian alone held himself aloof. Huang Hao, furious at him, had him relegated to Administrator of Badong. Grand General Yan Yu was overall commander in Badong; Xian was named army commander and served as his second. When Wei invaded Shu, Yan Yu was recalled westward, leaving Xian to hold Yong'an. After Chengdu fell the town was in uproar; magistrates along the river threw away their posts and ran. Xian executed one ringleader and the people settled down. When he learned Liu Shan had surrendered, he led his troops to the roadside pavilion and mourned there for three days. When Wu heard of Shu's collapse it sent General Sheng Xian upriver from the east—ostensibly to help, in truth to seize Xian's position. “Our court has fallen,” Xian said. “Wu should be our ally; instead of pitying our plight they mean to profit from it—do they think I will simply surrender?” So he pledged allegiance to Jin. He repaired arms, gathered supplies, and rallied his men with appeals to duty; every soldier obeyed. After Zhong Hui and Deng Ai were gone and no one held the region, Wu sent Bu Xie west; Xian smashed his army. Sun Xiu was furious and sent Lu Kang to reinforce Bu Xie. Xian held out for a year without reinforcements while plague carried off more than half the garrison. Some urged retreat south through Zangke or north toward Shangyong to save what they could. “A commander is what the people depend on,” Xian said. “If you cannot save them, running off at the first panic is not what a gentleman does.” I will die where I stand. Then Hu Lie, Inspector of Jingzhou, arrived with relief and Lu Kang pulled back. He was promoted to General Who Splashes the River, given supervisory command in Badong with credentials, and named Administrator of Wuling. Early in the Taishi era he came to court; the emperor decreed that Xian had shown loyalty, courage, and resolve and possessed real ability—he was awarded an honorary band. He was also given a sword mounted with dark mountain-pattern jade. At his death he was honored posthumously as Bearer of Credentials, General Who Pacifies the South, and Administrator of Wuling, enfeoffed posthumously as Marquis of Xi'e, with the posthumous epithet Lie.

Luo Shang, courtesy name Jingzhi, also known as Zhong. His father, Luo Shi, had been Administrator of Zangke. Orphaned young, Shang was raised by his uncle Xian. He wrote well. Wang Rong, Inspector of Jingzhou, named both Shang and Liu Qiao military aides and gave them real responsibility. Late in the Taikang era he was appointed Inspector of Liangzhou. When Zhao Xin rose in Shu, Shang submitted a memorial: “Xin is no commander of genius—his rebellion will fail; we need only wait him out.” He was then given provisional credentials as General Who Pacifies the West, Inspector of Yizhou, and Colonel of the Western Rong. Greedy and indecisive, he drew a bitter proverb from the Shu people: “Those Shang favors are crooks or flatterers; those he hates are the loyal and the frank.” His fortune rivaled small kingdoms; his mansion turned into a bazaar; his greed knew no limit, like wolves run wild. Another couplet ran: “Bandits from Shu we might survive—Luo Shang is what destroys us.” “The ‘General Who Pacifies the West’ only brings worse disaster.” Meanwhile Li Te had risen in Shu, struck at the governor, and killed Zhao Xin. Li Te then drove Shang from Chengdu into Jiangyang. Shang had earlier appealed to the provincial authorities; Zong Dai, Inspector of Jingzhou, marched Sun Fu of Jianping to his aid and halted at Jiangzhou. Their forces were strong, and everyone trapped by the rebels took heart. Shang sent his aide Ren Rui to pretend defection, slip word to allies outside, and coordinate a timed assault; they shattered Li Te's host, beheaded Li Te, and sent his head to Luoyang. Li Te's son Li Xiong declared himself ruler and set his capital at Pi. Shang sent General Wei Bo against him but could not break him. Shang died soon after, and Li Xiong took control of Shu.

滕修，字顯先，南陽西鄂人也。 仕吳為將帥，封西鄂侯。 孫皓時，代熊睦為廣州刺史，甚有威惠。 徵為執金吾。 廣州部曲督郭馬等為亂，皓以修宿有威惠，為嶺表所伏，以為使持節、都督廣州軍事、鎮南將軍、廣州牧以討之。 未克而王師伐吳，修率眾赴難。 至巴丘而皓已降，乃縞素流涕而還，與廣州刺史閭豐、蒼梧太守王毅各送印綬，詔以修為安南將軍，廣州牧、持節、都督如故，封武當侯，加鼓吹，委以南方事。 修在南積年，為邊夷所附。
Teng Xiu, courtesy name Xianxian, came from Xi'e in Nanyang. He commanded armies for Wu and held the marquisate of Xi'e. Under Sun Hao he succeeded Xiong Mu as Inspector of Guangzhou, ruling there with firm authority and genuine benevolence. He was recalled to court as Bearer of the Golden Mace. When troop captain Guo Ma and others mutinied in Guangzhou, Sun Hao sent Xiu—who had long commanded respect beyond the mountains—as Bearer of Credentials, commander-in-chief in Guangzhou, General Who Guards the South, and Governor of Guangzhou to crush the revolt. Before he could finish, Jin invaded Wu; Xiu marched his men east to defend his sovereign. He reached Baqiu only to learn Sun Hao had surrendered; wearing mourning white he turned back in tears. With Inspector Lu Feng of Guangzhou and Wang Yi of Cangwu he forwarded his seals to Jin. The court confirmed him as General Who Pacifies the South and Governor of Guangzhou with full authority, raised him to Marquis of Wudang, awarded him ceremonial bands, and left the south in his hands. He spent many years in the south and won the allegiance of the frontier peoples.

卒，請葬京師，帝嘉其意，賜墓田一頃，諡曰聲。 修之子並上表曰：「亡父修羈絏吳壤，為所驅馳； 幸逢開通，沐浴至化，得從俘虜握戎馬之要； 未覲聖顏，委南籓之重，實由勳勞少聞天聽故也。 年衰疾篤，屢乞骸骨，未蒙垂哀，奄至薨隕。 臣承遺意，輿櫬還都，瞻望雲闕，實懷痛裂。 竊聞博士諡修曰聲，直彰流播，不稱行績，不勝愚情，冒昧聞訴。 」帝乃賜諡曰忠。
When he died he asked to be buried near the capital; the emperor approved and granted a hectare of burial ground; his first posthumous title was Sheng. His sons Bing submitted a memorial: “Our late father served Wu under constraint— when heaven opened and civilization reached him, even from defeat he rose to military command— never having met Your Majesty, yet burdened with the southern frontier—only because his deeds seldom reached the throne.” Age and illness wore him down; he begged to retire but received no mercy, then suddenly passed away. We follow his last wish and bring his coffin to the capital; gazing toward the palace gates we are torn with grief. We hear the Erudites gave him the epithet Sheng—it rings wrong and sells him short; we humbly beg you to reconsider. The emperor then changed his posthumous epithet to Zhong, “Loyal.”

Bing's son Han began as aide to Yu Bing; he distinguished himself against Su Jun and was made founding marquis of Xiayang with sixteen hundred households, then General Who Pacifies the South and Inspector of Guangzhou. He governed Guangzhou for years with authority and kindness and was posthumously titled Dai. Han had a nephew, Dun, who became Inspector of Jiaozhou.

14
馬隆，字孝興，東平平陸人。 少而智勇，好立名節。 魏兗州刺史令狐愚坐事伏誅，舉州無敢收者。 隆以武吏托稱愚客，以私財殯葬，服喪三年，列植松柏，禮畢乃還，一州以為美談。 署武猛從事。 泰始中，將興伐吳之役，下詔曰：「吳會未平，宜得猛士以濟武功。 雖舊有薦舉之法，未足以盡殊才。 其普告州郡，有壯勇秀異才力傑出者，皆以名聞，將簡其尤異，擢而用之。 苟有其人，勿限所取。 」兗州舉隆才堪良將。 稍遷司馬督。
Ma Long, courtesy name Xiaoxing, came from Pinglu in Dongping. Even young he was clever and courageous and cared deeply for honor. When Linghu Yu, Inspector of Yanzhou, was executed for conspiracy, no one in the province dared bury him. Ma Long, a minor officer, claimed to be Linghu's retainer and buried him at his own expense, mourning three years and planting pines on the grave—throughout Yanzhou people praised the deed. He was named aide for martial affairs. Mid-Taishi, as Jin prepared to conquer Wu, an edict ran: “The southeast is still unruly—we need bold fighters for the campaign.” The usual recruitment channels miss extraordinary men. Let every province recommend anyone of unusual courage or strength; we will pick the best and promote them. If such men exist, send them forward without restriction. Yanzhou nominated Ma Long as qualified to command. He rose to serve as Sima supervisor.
15
初，涼州刺史楊欣失羌戎之和，隆陳其必敗。 俄而欣為虜所沒，河西斷絕，帝每有西顧之憂，臨朝而歎曰：「誰能為我討此虜通涼州者乎？ 」朝臣莫對。 隆進曰：「陛下若能任臣，臣能平之。 」帝曰：「必能滅賊，何為不任，顧卿方略何如耳。 隆曰：「陛下若能任臣，當聽臣自任。 」帝曰：「云何？ 隆曰：「臣請募勇士三千人，無問所從來，率之鼓行而西，稟陛下威德，醜虜何足滅哉！ 」帝許之，乃以隆為武威太守。 公卿僉曰：「六軍既眾，州郡兵多，但當用之，不宜橫設賞募以亂常典。 隆小將妄說，不可從也。 」帝弗納。 隆募限腰引弩三十六鈞、弓四鈞，立標簡試。 自旦至中，得三千五百人，隆曰：「足矣。 」因請自至武庫選杖。 武庫令與隆忿爭，御史中丞奏劾隆，隆曰：「臣當亡命戰場，以報所受，武庫令乃以魏時朽杖見給，不可復用，非陛下使臣滅賊意也。 」帝從之，又給其三年軍資。 隆於是西渡溫水。 虜樹機能等以眾萬計，或乘險以遏隆前，或設伏以截隆後。 隆依八陣圖作偏箱車，地廣則鹿角車營，路狹則為木屋施于車上，且戰且前，弓矢所及，應弦而倒。 奇謀間發，出敵不意。 或夾道累磁石，賊負鐵鎧，行不得前，隆卒悉被犀甲，無所留礙，賊咸以為神。 轉戰千里，殺傷以千數。 自隆之西，音問斷絕，朝廷憂之，或謂已沒。 後隆使夜到，帝撫掌歡笑。 詰朝，召群臣謂曰：「若從諸卿言，是無秦、涼也。 」乃詔曰：「隆以偏師寡眾，奮不顧難，冒險能濟。 其假節、宣威將軍，加赤幢、曲蓋、鼓吹。 」隆到武威，虜大人猝跋韓、且萬能等率萬餘落歸降，前後誅殺及降附者以萬計。 又率善戎沒骨能等與樹機能大戰，斬之，涼州遂平。 朝議將加隆將士勳賞，有司奏隆將士皆先加顯爵，不應更授，衛將軍楊珧駁曰：「前精募將士，少加爵命者，此適所以為誘引。 今隆全軍獨克，西土獲安，不得便以前授塞此後功，宜皆聽許，以明要信。 」乃從珧議，賜爵加秩各有差。
Long warned that Yang Xin, Inspector of Liangzhou, had alienated the Qiang and would surely fall. Xin was soon overrun and the Hexi corridor closed; at audiences the emperor kept sighing, “Who will reopen the road to Liangzhou?” No minister answered. Ma Long stepped forward: “Give me the mission, Your Majesty, and I will settle it.” The emperor said, “If you can do it, you shall—show me your plan.” Long replied, “Then give me a free hand to raise and lead the army myself.” “How do you mean?” “Let me recruit three thousand fighters—origin no object—and march west at drumbeat under your majesty’s awe; the rebels will not stand.” The emperor agreed and named him Administrator of Wuwei. The high ministers protested: “We already have massive armies—use them; ad hoc bounties only disrupt precedent.” Ma Long is a junior officer talking nonsense.” The emperor ignored them. He recruited men who could draw a thirty-six-jun crossbow at the waist and a four-jun bow, testing them against posted standards. From dawn to noon he signed up thirty-five hundred men. “Enough,” said Ma Long. He asked to choose his own staffs from the imperial arsenal. The armory chief argued with Ma Long and denounced him at court; Long replied: “I owe my life to defeating these rebels, but he handed me rotted Wei-era staves I cannot fight with—that cannot be what Your Majesty meant.” The emperor agreed and granted three more years of supplies. Ma Long crossed the Wen River and marched west. Shu Jinen commanded myriads—some held rugged ground ahead of Ma Long, others ambushed his rear. He built fighting carts after the Eight Formations—deer-antler lines on open ground, timber towers on carts in narrows—and rolled forward, shooting down anyone within bow range. He struck repeatedly from unexpected angles. He laid magnets along the trail: the rebels' iron mail locked them in place, while his men in rhino-hide armor walked through untouched—until the enemy thought them demons. His campaign ranged a thousand li and piled enemy casualties by the thousand. Once Ma Long marched west, silence fell—court officials feared he was lost. When his courier reached Luoyang by night, the emperor clapped and laughed with relief. Next morning he told his ministers: “Had I listened to you, Qin and Liang would be gone.” An edict praised Ma Long for winning with a scratch force against overwhelming odds. He received acting credentials as General Who Spreads Might, with crimson staff, curved canopy, and full honor guard. At Wuwei, chieftains Tufa Han and Qiewanneng brought ten thousand camps to surrender; kills and defections soon topped ten thousand. With allies such as Moguneng he crushed Shu Jinen in battle and killed him, ending the Liangzhou crisis. Officials balked at new honors because Ma Long's men already held titles; Yang Yao argued those earlier ranks had merely been recruiting incentives. Yang Yao argued that Ma Long's fresh triumph deserved full rewards without hiding behind old titles. The emperor agreed and distributed promotions and pay raises accordingly.

太康初，朝廷以西平荒毀，宜時興復，以隆為平虜護軍、西平太守，將所領精兵，又給牙門一軍，屯據西平。 時南虜成奚每為邊患，隆至，帥軍討之。 虜據險距守，隆令軍士皆負農器，將若田者。 虜以隆無征討意，禦眾稍怠。 隆因其無備，進兵擊破之。 畢隆之政，不敢為寇。 太熙初，封奉高縣侯，加授東羌校尉。 積十餘年，威信震於隴右。 時略陽太守馮翊嚴舒與楊駿通親，蜜圖代隆，毀隆年老謬耄，不宜服戎，於是征隆，以舒代鎮。 氏、羌聚結，百姓驚懼。 朝廷恐關隴復擾，乃免舒，遣隆復職，竟卒於官。
At the start of Taikang he was sent to rebuild shattered Xiping as Protector Who Pacifies the Barbarians and its administrator, with elite troops and an extra yamen corps. When Cheng Xi harassed the border, Ma Long marched out and attacked. The enemy dug in on rough ground, so Ma Long had his men sling hoes and rakes like farmers. The tribes assumed he would not fight and slackened their watch. He struck while they were unprepared and shattered them. They never raided again while Ma Long held command. In Taixi he became Marquis of Fenggao and Colonel of the Eastern Qiang. More than a decade on the Longyou frontier made his name feared and respected. Yan Shu of Lüeyang, tied by marriage to Yang Jun, smeared Ma Long as senile and maneuvered him out of office. Di and Qiang bands rose in alarm across the region. The court reversed itself, removed Yan Shu, restored Ma Long, and Ma Long died still serving.

19
胡奮，字玄威，安定臨涇人也，魏車騎將軍陰密侯遵之子也。 奮性開朗，有籌略，少好武事。 宣帝之伐遼東也，以白衣侍從左右，甚見接待。 還為校尉，稍遷徐州刺史，封夏陽子。 匈奴中部帥劉猛叛，使驍騎路蕃討之，以奮為監軍、假節，頓軍硜北，為蕃後繼。 擊猛，破之，猛帳下將李恪斬猛而降。 以功累遷征南將軍、假節、都督荊州諸軍事，遷護軍，加散騎常侍。 奮家世將門，晚乃好學，有刀筆之用，所在有聲績，居邊特有威惠。
Hu Fen, courtesy name Xuanwei, came from Linjing in Anding; his father was Hu Zun, Wei General of Chariots and Cavalry and Marquis of Yinmi. Tao Huang, courtesy name Shiying, came from Moling in Danyang. His father Tao Ji had governed Jiaozhou for Wu. He climbed Wu's bureaucracy to high office. Under Sun Hao, Jiaozhi's governor Sun Xu ruled with greed and cruelty. When Deng Xun came to levy troops he seized three thousand peacocks for shipment to Moling; exhausted locals plotted rebellion. County clerk Lu Xing murdered Sun Xu and Deng Xun and surrendered the territory to Jin. Sima Yan named Lu Xing General Who Pacifies the South and Administrator of Jiaozhi. Lu Xing soon fell to his aide Li Tong; the court tried Cuan Gu of Jianning, then Ma Rong of Brazil—each replacement died in turn. After Ma Rong died, Huo Yi dispatched Yang Ji with Mao Jiong, Dong Yuan, and yamen officers from Shu; they crushed Wu at Gucheng, killing Xiu Ze and Liu Jun. Wu countered with Yu Si supervising, Xue Xu commanding, and Tao Huang holding Cangwu—battle joined at Fenshui. Tao Huang lost, pulled back to Hepu, and sacrificed two generals. Xue Xu demanded why Tao Huang had volunteered yet lost two leaders. Tao Huang blamed disjointed commands—his allies would not follow his orders. Furious, Xue Xu prepared to withdraw. Tao Huang night-raided Dong Yuan, seized loot, sailed back, placated Xue Xu, and won command of Jiaozhou as vanguard. He took the sea route by surprise and struck straight at Jiaozhi against Dong Yuan. Suspecting an ambush beyond the ruined wall, he planted halberdiers to guard his rear. Dong Yuan feigned flight; Tao Huang chased into an ambush but his halberd line reversed the trap and broke Yuan. He used silk seized earlier to buy Liang Qi of Fuyan, who brought ten thousand warriors to his side. Dong Yuan's champion Xie Xi stayed in the city; Tao Huang turned Xie's brother Xiang—riding Tao Huang's carriage with full fanfare—to lure Xi. Defenders concluded Xiang's parade meant Xie Xi meant to defect. They murdered Xie Xi. Xue Xu and Tao Huang seized Jiaozhi. Wu named Tao Huang Inspector of Jiaozhou.

He warned that mocking Wu Yan's wording would isolate the Lu brothers from southern scholars. The Lu brothers relented and the gossip stopped.
39
初，陶璜之死也，九真戍兵作亂，逐其太守，九真賊帥趙祉圍郡城，彥悉討平之。 在鎮二十餘年，威恩宣著，南州寧靖。 自表求代，徵為大長秋。 卒於官。
When Tao Huang died, Jiuzhen mutinied; Wu Yan crushed Zhao Zhi's siege. Twenty years of firm yet generous rule pacified the south. He begged relief and was recalled as Grand Coachman. He died in that post.
40
張光
Zhang Guang.
41
張光，字景武，江夏鐘武人也。 身長八尺，明眉目，美音聲。 少為郡吏，家世有部曲，以牙門將伐吳有功，遷江夏西部都尉，轉北地都尉。
Zhang Guang, courtesy name Jingwu, came from Zhongwu in Jiangxia. Eight chi tall, handsome featured, with a rich voice. A county clerk from a gentry-with-retainers background, he earned promotions fighting Wu—from yamen general to Jiangxia west commandant and Beidi commandant.
42
初，趙王倫為關中都督，氐、羌反叛，太守張損戰沒，郡縣吏士少有全者。 光以百餘人戍馬蘭山北，賊圍之百餘日。 光撫厲將士，屢出奇兵擊賊，破之。 光以兵少路遠，自分敗沒。 會梁王肜遣司馬索靖將兵迎光，舉軍悲泣，遂還長安。 肜表光「處絕圍之地，有耿恭之忠，宜加甄賞，以明獎勸」。 於是擢授新平太守，加鼓吹。
When Sima Lun governed Guanzhong, Di and Qiang revolted and killed Administrator Zhang Sun. Zhang Guang held Malan Mountain north with a hundred men for over one hundred days under siege. He rallied his tiny garrison and raided until the besiegers broke. He assumed he would die far from aid. Prince Sima Yong sent Suo Jing to relieve him; his men wept and marched back to Chang'an. Sima Yong cited Zhang Guang's Geng Gong-style loyalty in proposing honors. The court raised him to Administrator of Xinping with ceremonial bands.
43
屬雍州刺史劉沈被密詔討河間王顒，光起兵助沈。 沈時委任秦州刺史皇甫重，重自以關西大族，心每輕光，謀多不用。 及二州軍潰，為顒所擒，顒謂光曰：「前起兵欲作何策？ 」光正色答曰：「但劉雍州不用鄙計，故令大王得有今日也。 」顒壯之，引與歡宴彌日，表為右衛司馬。
When Liu Shen moved secretly against Prince Sima Yong, Zhang Guang mobilized to support Liu Shen. Liu Shen had left Qinzhou to Huangfu Zhong, who dismissed Zhang Guang as a lesser western family and ignored his counsel. After both armies broke and Liu Shen fell to Sima Yong, Yong asked Zhang Guang what plan he had meant by rising. Zhang Guang answered coolly that Liu Shen had rejected his advice—hence Sima Yong's success. Sima Yong admired his nerve, feasted him all day, and nominated him Right Guard adjutant.
44
陳敏作亂，除光順陽太守，加陵江將軍，率步騎五千詣荊州討之。 刺史劉弘雅敬重光，稱為南楚之秀。 時江夏太守陶侃與敏大將錢端相距於長岐，將戰，襄陽太守皮初為步軍，使光設伏以待之，武陵太守苗光為水軍，藏舟艦于沔水。 皮初等與賊交戰，光發伏兵應之，水陸同奮，賊眾大敗。 弘表光有殊勳，遷材官將軍，梁州刺史。 先是，秦州人鄧定等二千餘家，饑餓流入漢中，保于成固，漸為抄盜，梁州刺史張殷遣巴西太守張燕討之。 定窘急，偽乞降于燕，並饋燕金銀，燕喜，為之緩師。 定密結李雄，雄遣眾救定，燕退，定遂進逼漢中。 太守杜正沖東奔魏興，殷亦棄官而遁。 光不得赴州，止于魏興，乃結諸郡守共謀進取。 燕唱言曰：「漢中荒敗，迫近大賊，克復之事，當俟英雄。 」正沖曰：「張燕受賊金銀，不時進討，阻兵緩寇，致喪漢中，實燕之罪也。 」光於是發怒，呵燕令出，斬之以徇。 綏撫荒殘，百姓悅服。 光於是卻鎮漢中。
Chen Min's revolt earned Zhang Guang the Shunyang post and river generalship with five thousand men to march on Jingzhou. Liu Hong hailed him as southern Chu's brightest star. As Tao Kan stalled Qian Duan at Changqi, Pi Chu took infantry, Zhang Guang set ambushes, and Miao Guang hid warships on the Han. Zhang Guang sprung his ambush while Pi Chu attacked; combined arms shattered Chen Min's army. Liu Hong cited his exceptional service and raised him to Specialist Crafts general and Inspector of Liangzhou. Qin refugees under Deng Ding had seized Chenggu and raided Hanzhong until Zhang Yin sent Zhang Yan against them. Deng Ding bribed Zhang Yan into slackening the siege with a fake surrender. Deng Ding called Li Xiong; Zhang Yan retreated; Deng Ding pressed toward Hanzhong. Du Zhengchong fled to Weixing and Zhang Yin deserted. Unable to enter Liangzhou, Zhang Guang rallied local governors at Weixing to retake Hanzhong. Zhang Yan insisted ruined Hanzhong could wait for heroes before reconquest. Du Zhengchong blamed Zhang Yan's bribery and delays for losing Hanzhong. Zhang Guang executed Zhang Yan for treason and exposed his corpse. He rebuilt trust among the ravaged populace. He reoccupied Hanzhong.
45
時逆賊王如餘黨李運、楊武等，自襄陽將三千餘家入漢中，光遣參軍晉邈率眾于黃金距之。 邈受運重賂，勸光納運。 光從邈言，使居成固。 既而邈以運多珍貨，又欲奪之，復言於光曰：「運之徒屬不事佃農，但營器杖，意在難測，可掩而取之。 」光又信焉。 遣邈眾討運，不克。 光乞師于氐王楊茂搜，茂搜遣子難敵助之。 難敵求貨於光，光不與。 楊武乃厚賂難敵，謂之曰「流人寶物悉在光處，今伐我，不如伐光。 」難敵大喜，聲言助光，內與運同，光弗之知也，遣息援率眾助邈。 運與難敵夾攻邈等，援為流矢所中死，賊遂大盛。 光嬰城固守，自夏迄冬，憤激成疾。 佐吏及百姓咸勸光退據魏興，光按劍曰：「吾受國厚恩，不能翦除寇賊，今得自死，便如登仙，何得退還也！ 」聲絕而卒，時年五十五。 百姓悲泣，遠近傷惜之。 有二子炅、邁。
Wang Ru's followers Li Yun and Yang Wu marched three thousand households into Hanzhong; Zhang Guang sent Jin Miao to block Huangjin pass. Jin Miao took Li Yun's bribes and lobbied to let him in. Zhang Guang let Li Yun settle at Chenggu. Greedy Jin Miao then urged a raid on Li Yun's treasure-laden camp. Zhang Guang believed him again. Jin Miao's assault on Li Yun failed. Zhang Guang begged Di king Yang Maosou for help; Yang Nan Di arrived. Yang Nan Di demanded payment; Zhang Guang declined. Yang Wu bribed Yang Nan Di to strike Zhang Guang instead of himself. Yang Nan Di feigned alliance while backing Li Yun; Zhang Guang unknowingly sent his son Zhang Yuan to help Jin Miao. Li Yun and Yang Nan Di crushed Jin Miao; Zhang Yuan fell to a stray shaft and the rebels swelled. Zhang Guang held the walls from summer to winter until rage and grief broke his health. Advisors begged him to fall back to Weixing; sword in hand he swore to die in place rather than retreat. He died mid-speech at fifty-five. People across the region mourned him. He left sons Zhang Jiong and Zhang Mai.
46
炅少辟太宰掾。 邁多才略，有父風。 州人推邁權領州事，與賊戰沒。 別駕範曠及督護王喬奉光妻息，率其遺眾，還據魏興。 其後義陽太守任愔為梁州，光妻子歸本郡。 南平太守應詹白都督王敦，稱「光在梁州能興微繼絕，威振巴漢。 值中原傾覆，征鎮失守，外無救助，內闕資儲，以寡敵眾，經年抗禦，厲節不撓，宜應追論顯贈，以慰存亡」。 敦不能從。
Zhang Jiong briefly served the Grand Chancellor's bureau. Zhang Mai inherited his father's tactical gifts. Locals made Zhang Mai interim governor until he fell in battle. Fan Kuang and Wang Qiao shepherded Zhang Guang's family and remnant forces to Weixing. Ren Yin succeeded to Liangzhou and Zhang Guang's kin went home. Ying Zhan told Wang Dun that Zhang Guang had restored lost Hanzhong and awed Ba–Han. The memorial praised Zhang Guang's hopeless defense and urged posthumous honors. Wang Dun refused.
47
趙誘
Zhao You.
48
趙誘，字元孫，淮南人也。 世以將顯。 州辟主簿。 值刺史郗隆被齊王冏檄，使起兵討趙王倫，隆欲承檄舉義，而諸子侄並在洛陽； 欲坐觀成敗，恐為冏所討，進退有疑，會群吏計議。 誘說隆曰：「趙王篡逆，海內所病。 今義兵飆起，其敗必矣。 今為明使君計，莫若自將精兵，徑赴許昌，上策也。 不然，且可留後，遣猛將將兵會盟，亦中策也。 若遣小軍隨形助勝。 下策耳。 」隆曰：「我受二帝恩，無所偏助，正欲保州而已。 」誘與治中留寶、主簿張褒等諫隆：「若無所助，變難將生，州亦不可保也。 」隆猶豫不決，遂為其下所害。 誘還家，杜門不出。 左將軍王敦以為參軍，加廣武將軍，與甘卓、周訪共討華軼，破之。 又擊杜弢於西湘，太興初，復與卓攻弢，滅之。 累功賜爵平阿縣侯，代陶侃為武昌太守。 時杜曾迎第五猗於荊州作亂，敦遣誘與襄陽太守朱軌共距之。 猗既愍帝所遣，加有時望，為荊楚所歸。 誘等苦戰皆沒，敦甚悼惜之，表贈征虜將軍、秦州刺史，諡曰敬。
Zhao You, courtesy name Yuansun, came from Huainan. His clan had long served as generals. He became provincial chief clerk. Xi Long was ordered to march against Sima Lun while his kin remained hostage in Luoyang. Torn between joining Sima Jiong and staying neutral, he asked his staff. Zhao You argued Sima Lun's usurpation alienated the empire. Righteous hosts would surely crush him. Best plan: Xi Long should march elite troops to Xuchang. Middle plan: hold the province but send a strong general to the coalition. Worst: commit a token force to whoever wins. That was the weakest option. Xi Long refused to pick sides, claiming duty only to defend the province. Zhao You and aides warned neutrality would invite disaster. Xi Long hesitated until his own men murdered him. Zhao You shut himself indoors. Wang Dun named Zhao You adjutant and secondary general; with Gan Zhuo and Zhou Fang he crushed Hua Yi. He fought Du Tao on the western Xiang and later helped wipe Du Tao out. Merit brought the Ping'e marquisate and Tao Kan's old Wuchang post. Du Zeng's revolt with Di Yi sent Zhao You and Zhu Gui to intercept. Di Yi bore Emperor Min's mandate and commanded respect across Jing-Chu. Zhao You and Zhu Gui died fighting; Wang Dun mourned them and sought posthumous General Who Conquers the Barbarians and Inspector of Qinzhou.
49
子龔，與誘俱死。 元帝為晉王，下令贈新昌太守。 龔弟胤，字伯舒。 王敦使周訪擊杜曾，胤請從行。 訪憚曾之強，欲先以胤餌曾，使其眾疲而後擊之。 胤多梟首級。 王導引為從事中郎。 南頓王宗反，胤殺宗。 於是王導、庾亮並倚仗之。 轉冠軍將軍，遷西豫州刺史，卒于官。
His son Zhao Gong fell beside him. Emperor Yuan later honored Zhao Gong as Administrator of Xinchang. Zhao Gong's brother Zhao Yin, courtesy name Boshi. Zhao Yin rode with Zhou Fang against Du Zeng. Zhou Fang meant to use Zhao Yin as bait to tire Du Zeng. Zhao Yin harvested many enemy heads. Wang Dao named him secretarial aide. Zhao Yin executed rebel Prince Sima Zong of Nandun. Wang Dao and Yu Liang both leaned on Zhao Yin. He rose to General Who Champions the Army and Inspector of West Yuzhou and died serving.
50
史評
Historians' appraisal.
51
史臣曰：忠為令德，貞曰事君，徇國家而竭身，曆夷險而一節。 羅憲、滕修，濯纓入仕，指巴東而受脤，出嶺嶠而揚麾。 屬鼎命淪胥，本朝失守，郕巴丘而流涕，集都亭而大臨。 古之忠烈，罕輩子茲！ 孝興之智勇，玄威之武藝，滅醜虜於河西，制凶酋於硜北，審楊欣之必敗，譏楊駿之速禍。 陶璜、吾彥，逸足齊驅，毛炅屈其深謀，陸抗奇其茂略。 薪楢之任，清規自遠； 鼙鼓之臣，厥聲彌劭。 景武，南楚秀士； 元孫，累葉將門，赴死喻於登仙。 效誠陳於上策，竟而俱斃，貞則斯存。
The historians praise loyalty through peril and ease. Luo Xian and Teng Xiu took office and defended Ba–Chu through dynastic collapse. They mourned Shu at Baqiu and Yong'an like loyal mourners. Few ancient exemplars matched their steadfast grief. Ma Long and Hu Fen shattered barbarians west of the river; Ma Long foresaw Yang Xin's fall and Hu Fen warned Yang Jun. Tao Huang and Wu Yan rivaled each other; Mao Jiong succumbed to Tao Huang's plots and Lu Kang prized Wu Yan. Their governance drew pure standards from afar. Their battlefield fame grew brighter still. Zhang Guang stood among southern Chu's finest. Zhao You's warrior house faced death as gladly as transcendence. They spoke truth and died—yet integrity survives.
52
贊曰：憲居玉疊，才博流譽。 修赴石門，惠政攸著。 孝興、玄威，操履無違。 愚墳畢禮，楊門致譏。 璜謀超絕，彥材雄傑。 潛師襲董，觀兵歎薛。 惟趙與張，神略多方。 作尉北地，立功西湘。
Encomium: Luo Xian atop Yudie—talent renowned. Teng Xiu at Shimen—his benign rule remembered. Ma Long and Hu Fen lived irreproachable lives. Yet folly at tombs and Yang Jun's clan drew mockery. Tao Huang towered in strategy; Wu Yan in valor. Stealth against Dong Yuan; Xue Xu reviewed armies with awe. Zhao You and Zhang Guang wielded versatile genius. Captains of Beidi, glory won on West Xiang.
