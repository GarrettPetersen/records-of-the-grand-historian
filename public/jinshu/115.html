苻丕苻登
Fu Pi; Fu Deng.
2
苻丕，字永叔，堅之長庶子也。 少而聰彗好學，博綜經史。 堅與言將略，嘉之，命鄧羌教以兵法。 文武才幹亞於苻融，為將善收士卒情，出鎮於鄴，東夏安之。 堅敗歸長安，丕為慕容垂所逼，自鄴奔枋頭。 堅之死也，丕復入鄴城，將收兵趙、魏，西赴長安。 會幽州刺史王永、平州刺史苻沖頻為垂將平規等所敗，乃遣昌黎太守宋敞焚燒和龍、薊城宮室，率眾三萬進屯壺關，遣使招丕。 丕乃去鄴，率男女六萬餘口進如潞川。 驃騎張蠔、并州刺史王騰迎之，入據晉陽，始知堅死問，舉哀于晉陽，三軍縞素。 王永留苻沖守壺關，率騎一萬會丕，勸稱尊號，丕從之，乃乙太元十年僭即皇帝位於晉陽南。 立堅行廟，太赦境內，改元曰太安。 置百官，以張蠔為侍中、司空，封上黨郡公； 王永為使持節、侍中、都督中外諸軍事、車騎大將軍、尚書令，進封清河公； 王騰為散騎常侍、中軍大將軍、司隸校尉、陽平郡公； 苻沖為左光祿大夫、尚書左僕射、西平王； 俱石子為衛將軍、濮陽公； 楊輔為尚書右僕射、濟陽公； 王亮為護軍將軍、彭城公； 強益耳、梁暢為侍中，徐義為吏部尚書，並封縣公。 自余封授各有差。
Fu Pi, whose courtesy name was Yongshu, was Fu Jian’s eldest son by a concubine. Even as a boy he was bright and bookish, with a wide command of the classical corpus and historical records. Fu Jian discussed campaigns and strategy with him, was impressed, and had Deng Qiang instruct him in the art of war. His gifts in civil and military affairs stood just below Fu Rong’s; as a commander he knew how to win his men’s loyalty, and when he was posted to Ye the eastern heartland stayed calm. After Fu Jian’s defeat and retreat to Chang’an, Fu Pi was driven from Ye by Murong Chui and fled to Biantou. When Fu Jian died, Fu Pi re-entered Ye, planning to raise forces in Zhao and Wei and march west toward Chang’an. Meanwhile Youzhou governor Wang Yong and Pingzhou governor Fu Chong had suffered repeated defeats at the hands of Murong Chui’s generals such as Ping Gui, so Yong sent Changli prefect Song Chang to torch the palaces at Helong and Ji, moved thirty thousand men up to Huguan, and sent envoys urging Fu Pi to join him. Fu Pi then quit Ye, shepherding well over sixty thousand civilians and soldiers toward the Lu River basin. General of Agile Cavalry Zhang Hao and Bingzhou inspector Wang Teng received him; once they had taken Jinyang they learned for certain that Fu Jian was dead, and the army mourned there in full white. Wang Yong left Fu Chong to hold Huguan, rode in with ten thousand horse to meet Fu Pi, and pressed him to take the throne; Fu Pi agreed, and in the tenth year of Taiyuan (385) he unlawfully declared himself emperor south of Jinyang. He set up a mobile shrine to Fu Jian, proclaimed a general amnesty within his domain, and adopted the era name Tai’an. He installed his bureaucracy, naming Zhang Hao Palace Attendant and Minister of Works with the ducal title of Shangdang. Wang Yong received the staff of authority, the palace post, supreme military command, the chariots-and-cavalry generalship, and the chief ministership, and was promoted to Duke of Qinghe. Wang Teng was made cavalier attendant-in-ordinary, general of the central army, colonel director of retainers, and Duke of Yangping. Fu Chong became grand master of the left, senior vice minister of the secretariat, and Prince of Xiping. Ju Shizi was appointed general of the guard and Duke of Puyang. Yang Fu was named right vice director of the secretariat and Duke of Jiyang. Wang Liang became general who guards the army and Duke of Pengcheng. Qiang Yier and Liang Chang were made palace attendants, Xu Yi minister of personnel, and each was ennobled as a county-level duke. The remaining appointments and noble ranks were distributed according to merit and station.
3
是時安西呂光自西域還師，至於宜禾，堅涼州刺史梁熙謀閉境距之。 高昌太守楊翰言於熙曰：「「呂光新定西國，兵強氣銳，其鋒不可當也。 度其事意，必有異圖。 且今關中擾亂，京師存亡未知，自河已西迄於流沙，地方萬里，帶甲十萬，鼎峙之勢實在今日。 若光出流沙，其勢難測。 高梧穀口，水險之要，宜先守之而奪其水。 彼既窮渴，自然投戈。 如其以遠不守，伊吾之關亦可距也。 若度此二要，雖有子房之策，難為計矣。 地有所必爭，真此機也。 」熙弗從。 美水令犍為張統說熙曰：「主上傾國南討，覆敗而還。 慕容垂擅兵河北，泓、沖寇逼京師，丁零雜虜，跋扈關、洛，州郡奸豪，所在風扇，王綱弛絕，人懷利己。 今呂光回師，將軍何以抗也？ 」熙曰：「誠深憂之，未知計之所出。 」統曰：「光雄果勇毅，明略絕人，今以蕩西域之威，擁歸師之銳，鋒若猛火之盛于原，弗可敵也。 將軍世受殊恩，忠誠夙著，立勳王室，宜在於今。 行唐公洛，上之從弟，勇冠一時。 為將軍計者，莫若奉為盟主，以攝眾望，推忠義以總率群豪，則光無異心也。 資其精銳，東兼毛興，連王統、楊璧，集四州之眾，掃凶逆于諸夏，甯帝室於關中，此桓文之舉也。 」熙又不從。 殺洛於西海，以子胤為鷹揚將軍，率眾五萬距光於酒泉。 敦煌太守姚靜、晉昌太守李純以郡降光。 胤及光戰于安彌，為光所敗。 武威太守彭濟執熙迎光，光殺之。 建威、西郡太守索泮，奮威、督洪池已南諸軍事、酒泉太守宋皓等，並為光所殺。
Meanwhile General Who Pacifies the West Lü Guang was marching home from the Western Regions and reached Yihe, where Fu Jian’s Liangzhou governor Liang Xi planned to shut the frontier and block him. Gaochang prefect Yang Han warned Liang Xi: “Lü Guang has just subdued the west; his troops are strong and their morale keen—no one should meet that spearhead head-on.” Judging his intentions, he must be nursing ambitions of his own. Moreover Guanzhong is in turmoil and the fate of the capital uncertain; west of the Yellow River all the way to the desert lies a realm ten thousand li across and a hundred thousand soldiers under arms—the moment for a three-way standoff is now. Once Lü Guang emerges from the desert, his next move will be impossible to predict. The Gaowu defile is a waterless choke point; you should seize it first and cut off their supply of water. Left parched and desperate, they will throw down their weapons of their own accord. If he will not hold a distant position, the Yiwu pass can still serve as a barrier. If he slips past both choke points, even a strategist like Zhang Liang could not salvage the situation. Some ground simply must be fought for, and this is that moment. ” Liang Xi refused to listen. Zhang Tong of Qianwei, magistrate of Meishui, urged Liang Xi: “Our sovereign staked the realm on a southern expedition and came home in ruinous defeat. Murong Chui holds Hebei at sword point, Fu Hong and Fu Chong press the capital, Dingling and other tribes swagger through the Guanzhong and Luoyang heartland, local strongmen rise wherever the wind blows, imperial authority has collapsed, and every man is out for himself. Now Lü Guang is marching back—how do you propose to stop him?” ” Liang Xi replied, “I share that fear, but I see no clear course.” ” Zhang Tong said, “Lü Guang is bold, resolute, and abler than ordinary men. Fresh from conquering the west and leading a seasoned army home, his attack will spread like wildfire across dry grass—no force can stand against it. Your house has long enjoyed the dynasty’s favor and a reputation for loyalty—now is the time to earn your place in its service. Duke of Xingtang Fu Luo, the emperor’s cousin, is the bravest man of the hour. My counsel is to acknowledge him as leader of the league, win the gentry’s trust, and rally the magnates under the banner of loyalty—then Lü Guang will have no cause to turn on you. Use his elite soldiers, join forces eastward with Mao Xing, link up with Wang Tong and Yang Bi, concentrate the four provinces’ hosts, drive the traitors from the Central Plain, and restore the court in Guanzhong—an achievement worthy of Duke Huan or Duke Wen of Jin.” ” Again Liang Xi would not heed him. He executed Fu Luo at Xihai, named his son Yin general who spreads might, and sent fifty thousand men to oppose Lü Guang at Jiuquan. Dunhuang prefect Yao Jing and Jinchang prefect Li Chun handed over their commanderies to Lü Guang. Yin met Lü Guang at Anmi and was routed. Wuwei prefect Peng Ji seized Liang Xi to welcome Lü Guang, who then put Liang Xi to death. Suo Pan, general who establishes might and western commandery prefect, and Song Hao, general who rouses might, military overseer south of Hongchi, and Jiuquan prefect, among others, were all executed by Lü Guang.
4
堅尚書令、魏昌公苻纂自關中來奔，拜太尉，進封東海王。 以中山太守王兗為平東將軍、平州刺史、阜城侯，苻定為征東將軍、冀州牧、高城侯，苻紹為鎮東將軍、督冀州諸軍事、重合侯，苻謨為征西將軍、幽州牧、高邑侯，苻亮為鎮北大將軍、督幽、并二州諸軍事，並進爵郡公。 定、紹據信都，謨、亮先據常山，慕容垂之圍鄴城也，並降于垂，聞丕稱尊號，遣使謝罪。 王兗固守博陵，與垂相持。 左將軍竇沖、秦州刺史王統、河州刺史毛興、益州刺史王廣、南秦州刺史楊璧、衛將軍楊定，並據隴右，遣使招丕，請討姚萇。 丕大悅，以定為驃騎大將軍、雍州牧，沖為征西大將軍、梁州牧，統鎮西大將軍，興車騎大將軍，璧征南大將軍，並開府儀同三司，加散騎常侍，廣安西將軍，皆進位州牧。
Fu Zuan, Fu Jian’s chief minister and Duke of Weichang, fled from Guanzhong to join Fu Pi and was named grand commandant and Prince of Donghai. Zhongshan prefect Wang Yan became general who pacifies the east, Pingzhou inspector, and Marquis of Fucheng; Fu Ding was named general who conquers the east and Ji governor as Marquis of Gaocheng; Fu Shao became general who guards the east and Ji theater commander as Marquis of Chonghe; Fu Mo took general who conquers the west and You governor as Marquis of Gaoyi; Fu Liang became supreme northern commander for You and Bing; each was promoted to commandery duke. Fu Ding and Fu Shao held Xindu while Fu Mo and Fu Liang had occupied Changshan; during Murong Chui’s siege of Ye they had all submitted to him, but when they learned that Fu Pi had taken the throne they sent envoys to apologize. Wang Yan held Boling in a stubborn standoff with Murong Chui. Left general Dou Chong, Qinzhou inspector Wang Tong, Hezhou inspector Mao Xing, Yizhou inspector Wang Guang, southern Qinzhou inspector Yang Bi, and guard general Yang Ding all controlled Longyou; they sent envoys urging Fu Pi to join them in a campaign against Yao Chang. Fu Pi was delighted: Yang Ding became grand general of agile cavalry and Yongzhou governor; Dou Chong general who conquers the west and Liangzhou governor; Wang Tong general who guards the west; Mao Xing general of chariots and cavalry; Yang Bi general who conquers the south—each received an independent command equal to the three dukes, the cavalier attendant title, Wang Guang the general who pacifies the west, and all were elevated to provincial governor rank.
5
於是王永宣檄州郡曰：「大行皇帝棄背萬國，四海無主。 征東大將軍、長樂公，先帝元子，聖武自天，受命荊南，威振衡海，分陝東都，道被夷夏，仁澤光於宇宙，德聽侔于《下武》。 永與司空蠔等謹順天人之望，以季秋吉辰奉公紹承大統，銜哀即事，棲穀總戎，枕戈待旦，志雪大恥。 慕容垂為封豕於關東，泓、沖繼凶於京邑，致乘輿播越，宗社淪傾。 羌賊姚萇，我之牧士，乘釁滔天，親行大逆，有生之巨賊也。 永累葉受恩，世荷將相，不與驪山之戎、滎澤之狄共戴皇天，同履厚土。 諸牧伯公侯或宛沛宗臣，或四七勳舊，豈忍舍破國之醜豎，縱殺君之逆賊乎！ 主上飛龍九五，實協天心，靈祥休瑞，史不輟書，投戈效義之士三十餘萬，少康、光武之功可旬朔而成。 今以衛將軍俱石子為前軍師，司空張蠔為中軍都督。 武將猛士，風烈雷震，志殄元凶，義無他顧。 永謹奉乘輿，恭行天罰。 君臣終始之義，在三忘軀之誠，戮力同之，以建晉、鄭之美。」
Wang Yong then issued a manifesto to every province and commandery: “The late emperor has left the realm, and no sovereign holds the allegiance of the land.” The general who conquers the east and Duke of Changle—the late emperor’s eldest son—is heaven-endowed with martial sagehood; invested south of the Jing, his majesty rolls from the Heng region to the sea; like the kings who split the realm east of Shan, his influence spans barbarian and Chinese; his kindness fills the cosmos, and his virtue rivals the ode ‘Lower Martial.’” Wang Yong and Minister of Works Zhang Hao, deferring to heaven and popular sentiment, on an auspicious autumn day invested the duke as heir to the throne; heartsick yet resolute, they tightened their belts, took command of the armies, slept with weapons at hand, and swore to erase this national humiliation.” Murong Chui rampages through the lands east of the passes like a monstrous boar; Fu Hong and Fu Chong have carried slaughter into the capital until the throne is driven into exile and the dynastic shrines lie in ruins.” The Qiang rebel Yao Chang—once a herdsman on our payroll—has exploited turmoil to tower above heaven itself and has personally committed the gravest treason; he is the greatest villain alive.” My house has enjoyed imperial favor for generations and worn ministerial ribbons age after age—I will not share heaven and earth with the savages of Mount Li or the barbarians of Ying marsh.” You governors and nobles—some kin to the imperial clan at Wan and Pei, some veterans of four generations of service—can you truly stomach sheltering the wretches who shattered the realm or indulging the traitors who murdered their sovereign?” Our sovereign has mounted the throne as the true dragon; omens crowd the chronicles without cease; more than three hundred thousand warriors have thrown down their arms to rally to justice—the restoration won by Shaokang or Guangwu can be ours within weeks.” General of the guard Ju Shizi is appointed mentor of the vanguard; Minister of Works Zhang Hao commands the central army.” Our fierce hosts move like wind and thunder; they mean to crush the arch-traitor and will look to nothing else until duty is done.” Wang Yong reverently attends the imperial carriage and carries out heaven’s judgment upon the rebels.” Let lord and ministers uphold their covenant unto death; let every loyal breast forget self as the three bonds demand; together we shall strive as Duke Wen of Jin and Duke Zhuang of Zheng once did.”
6
先是，慕容驎攻王兗於博陵，至是糧竭矢盡，郡功曹張猗逾城聚眾應驎。 袞臨城數之曰：「卿，秦之人也。 吾，卿之君也。 起眾應賊，號稱義兵，何名實相違之甚！ 卿兄往合鄉宗，親逐城主，天地不容，為世大戮。 身滅未幾，卿復續之。 卿見為吾吏，親尋干戈，競為戎首，為爾君者，不亦難乎！ 今人何取卿一切之功，寧能忘卿不忠不孝之事！ 古人有雲，求忠臣必出孝子之門，卿母在城，不能顧之，何忠義之可望！ 惡不絕世，卿之謂也。 不圖中州禮義之邦邦，而卿門風若斯。 卿去老母如脫屣，吾復何論哉！ 」既而城陷，兗及固安侯苻鑒，並為驎所殺。
Murong Lin had long besieged Wang Yan at Boling; supplies and arrows were gone when merit clerk Zhang Yi slipped over the walls and rallied townsmen to open the gates for Lin. Wang Yan leaned from the battlements and rebuked him: “You are a subject of Qin. I am your lord. Yet you raise militia for rebels and call it righteousness—how twisted your words and deeds have become!” Your elder brother once rallied the countryside, drove out the prefect, and earned heaven’s wrath—he was executed as a public criminal. His corpse is barely cold and you repeat his crime. You serve under my banner yet take up arms and lead the mutiny—what lord could endure such a vassal?” Do you think anyone will praise your petty deeds and forget disloyalty to your ruler and cruelty to your kin?” The elders said loyal ministers come only from filial homes—your mother remains inside these walls and you abandon her; where is your loyalty or duty?” Villainy runs in families, and yours proves the proverb.” I never imagined such conduct could come from a house in the heartland of ritual.” You cast off your aged mother like a worn shoe—why waste words on you?” ” Soon the city fell; Wang Yan and Marquis of Gu’an Fu Jian were both put to death by Murong Lin.
7
丕復以王永為司徒、錄尚書事，徐義為尚書令，加右光祿大夫。
Fu Pi again named Wang Yong minister of education with supervisory power over the secretariat; Xu Yi became chief minister of the secretariat with the additional title of right grand master of imperial entertainments.
8
初，王廣還自成都也，奔其兄秦州刺史統。 及長安不守，廣攻河州牧毛興於枹罕。 興遣建節將軍、臨清柏衛平率其宗人千七百夜擊廣軍，大敗之。 王統復遣兵助廣，興於是嬰城固守。 既而襲王廣，敗之，廣亡奔秦州，為隴西鮮卑匹蘭所執，送詣姚萇。 興既敗王廣，謀伐王統，平上邽。 袍罕諸氐皆窘於兵革而疲不堪命，乃殺興，推衛平為使持節、安西將軍、河州刺史，遣使請命。
Earlier, when Wang Guang withdrew from Chengdu, he threw himself on his brother Wang Tong, governor of Qinzhou. After Chang’an fell, Wang Guang attacked Hezhou governor Mao Xing at Fuhan. Mao Xing sent general who establishes integrity Bai Weiping of Linqing with seventeen hundred clansmen in a night attack that shattered Wang Guang’s army. Wang Tong sent reinforcements, so Mao Xing drew up his bridges and held Fuhan behind closed gates. He soon ambushed Wang Guang, routed him, and drove him toward Qinzhou, where Longxi Xianbei chief Pilan seized him and handed him over to Yao Chang. After beating Wang Guang, Mao Xing planned to march on Wang Tong and secure Shanggui. The Di clans of Fuhan, exhausted by endless war, slew Mao Xing and elevated Wei Ping to staff-bearing general who pacifies the west and Hezhou inspector, then sought Fu Pi’s confirmation.
9
刁雲殺慕容忠，乃推慕容永為使持節、大都督中外諸軍事、大將軍、大單于、雍、秦、梁、涼四州牧、錄尚書事、河東王、稱籓於垂。 征東苻定、鎮東苻紹、征北苻謨、鎮北苻亮皆降于慕容垂。
Diao Yun murdered Murong Zhong, then raised Murong Yong with staff authority as supreme commander, grand general and great chanyu, governor of Yong, Qin, Liang province (upper Han basin), and Hexi Liangzhou, chief minister, Prince of Hedong, nominally subordinate to Murong Chui. Fu Ding, Fu Shao, Fu Mo, and Fu Liang—the eastern and northern commanders—each capitulated to Murong Chui.
10
丕又進王永為左丞相，苻纂為大司馬，張蠔為太尉，王騰為驃騎大將軍、儀同三司，徐義為司空，苻沖為車騎大將軍、尚書令、儀同三司，俱石子為衛大將軍、尚書左僕射，領官皆如故。 永又檄州郡曰：「昔夏有窮夷之難，少康起焉； 王莽毒殺平帝，世祖重光漢道； 百六之運，何代無之！ 天降喪亂，羌胡猾夏，先帝晏駕賊庭，京師鞠為戎穴，神州蕭條，生靈塗炭。 天未亡秦，社稷有奉。 主上聖德恢弘，道侔光武，所在宅心，天人歸屬，必當隆中興之功，復配天之美。 姚萇殘虐，慕容垂凶暴，所過滅戶夷煙，毀發丘墓，毒遍存亡，痛纏幽顯，雖黃巾之害於九州，赤眉之暴于四海，方之未為甚也。 今素秋將及，行師令辰，公侯牧守，壘主鄉豪，或戮力國家，乃心王室，各率所統，以孟冬上旬會大駕于臨晉。 」於是天水姜延、馮翊寇明、河東王昭、新平張晏、京兆杜敏、扶風馬郎、建忠高平牧官都尉王敏等咸承檄起兵，各有眾數萬，遣使應丕。 皆就拜將軍、郡守，封列侯。 冠軍鄧景擁眾五千據彭池，與竇沖為首尾，擊萇平涼太守金熙。 安定北部都尉鮮卑沒奕於率鄯善王胡員吒、護羌中郎將梁苟奴等，與萇左將軍姚方成、鎮遠強京戰于孫丘谷，大敗之。
Fu Pi promoted Wang Yong to left chancellor, Fu Zuan to grand marshal, Zhang Hao to grand commandant, Wang Teng to grand general of agile cavalry with three-duke privileges, Xu Yi to minister of works, Fu Chong to chief minister and general of chariots and cavalry with three-duke privileges, Ju Shizi to guard general and senior vice minister—each kept his other posts. Wang Yong issued another circular: “When the Xia court faced the Youqiong crisis, Shaokang restored the line; Wang Mang murdered Emperor Ping, yet Emperor Guangwu relit the Han flame; Every dynasty knows its share of catastrophic cycles. Heaven has sent calamity: Qiang and Hu trample the Chinese heartland, our late sovereign died in rebel hands, the capital has become a barbarian warren, and the Central Plain lies in ashes. Heaven is not finished with Great Qin—the altars of state still have rightful heirs. Yao Chang struck Dajie by night with thirty thousand horse, slew Fu Deng’s empress Lady Mao and princes Fu Bian and Fu Shang, bagged dozens of noted commanders, and marched away more than fifty thousand captives.

Fu Deng’s general Wei Hefei attacked Yao Dangcheng at Xingcheng and was slain by Yao Chang’s troops.

Guo Zhi sent subordinates against Gou Yao and suffered a crushing defeat. Guo Zhi called Yang Kai east for support, fought Gou Yao east of Zheng, lost, defected to Yao Chang, and was given a generalship. Guo Zhi’s army melted away.

Gou Yao held ten thousand men at Nifang fort in secret league with Fu Deng; Fu Deng moved from Qulao through Fanchuan and camped at Matouyuan. Yao Chang rode out to oppose him, shattered Fu Deng’s army, slew minister Wu Zhong, and pressed toward Xinping. Yao Chang relieved Xinping; Fu Deng withdrew, struck Anding, lost, and fell back to Lucheng fort.

By then Yao Chang lay sick, haunted by visions of Fu Jian. Fu Deng fed his horses and massed the army, telling Fu Jian’s tablet, “Your great-grandson has borne spear and shield almost twelve years; heaven has favored every battle and shattered Yao’s ranks like ice.” Now your spirit sends plague upon the rebel Qiang—surely they must soon collapse.” I will seize their dying breath to execute heaven’s sentence, recover your coffin, and offer apology in the bright shrine.” ” He declared a general amnesty and promoted every official two ranks. He sparred with Yao Chong over the wheat fields along the Qingshui and suffered repeated defeats. He closed to within ninety li of Anding. As Yao Chang rallied he blocked Fu Deng; Fu Deng left camp to meet him, but Yao Xilong struck Fu Deng’s rear and forced him to flee. That night Yao Chang slipped past Fu Deng’s camp and seized his rear line thirty li away. At dawn scouts reported, “The enemy camps stand empty—none know where they went.” ” Fu Deng cried, “What manner of foe slips away unseen and strikes unwarned? I thought him dying, yet he returns—I share an age with this Qiang and fate is cruel!” ” He broke off the campaign and withdrew to Yong.

Fu Deng besieged him at Wild Man fort; Dou Chong begged Yao Chang for aid, and Yao Chang sent crown prince Yao Xing against Hu Kong fort to relieve him. Fu Deng raced back to Hu Kong fort, whereupon Dou Chong allied with Yao Chang.
29

When Yao Chang died Fu Deng crowed, “Yao Xing is a mere boy—I will thrash him with a stick.” ” He declared amnesty, threw every man eastward, stormed the Tuge forts of Yao Nu and Bo Pu, and marched from Ganquan toward Guanzhong. Yao Xing pursued within a few dozen li; Fu Deng raced by Liumo toward the ruined bridge where Yin Wei waited. Denied the river, his men died of thirst by the thousands. He fought Yin Wei and lost; that night his army collapsed and Fu Deng galloped alone to Yong.

When Fu Deng marched east he had left his brother Fu Guang as minister of education at Yong and crown prince Fu Chong at Hu Kong fort. When Fu Guang and Fu Chong heard of the defeat they fled and their troops scattered. Fu Deng found no haven at Yong, fled to Pingliang, and rallied survivors on Maomao Mountain. Yao Xing pressed the mountain; Fu Deng sent his son Prince of Ru’yin Fu Zong as hostage to the Longxi Xianbei Qifu Qiangui, sealed the pact by marriage, and Qifu Qiangui rode to his aid with twenty thousand horse. Fu Deng came down to meet them, fought Yao Xing south of the mountain, lost, and died in battle. He had reigned nine years and was fifty-two when he fell. Fu Chong fled to the Huang River basin, claimed the throne, and adopted the era name Yanxi. They honored Fu Deng posthumously as Emperor Gao with temple name Taizong. Qifu Qiangui drove out Fu Chong; both Fu Chong and Yang Ding perished.

Suo Pan, courtesy name Delin, came from Dunhuang. His clan ranked among the great houses for generations. In youth Suo Pan lived by the sword; grown, he reformed, turned scholar, and showed gifts fit for governing the age. While Zhang Tianxi directed the government he named Suo Pan champion general and secretary-adjunct. When Zhang Tianxi took the throne Suo Pan became director of military affairs and rose through palace recorder posts. As judicial inspector he struck awe through the provincial seat and transformed conduct in every county. Promoted to commandant of the left feathered forest, he earned renown for tireless efficiency. He left court as central rampart general, prefect of Wuwei in the western commandery, and colonel of military affairs. His rule was mild and fair; tribesmen and Chinese alike cherished his kindness, and Zhang Tianxi held him in high regard. Fu Jian exclaimed on meeting him, “Liangzhou breeds true gentlemen!” ” Soon Suo Pan’s reputation west of the Yellow River won him appointment as provincial attendant clerk. Recommended as worthy under Fu Jian, he served as gentleman of the palace, then magistrate of Chang’an, brought every lawbreaking noble scion to justice, and shut the door to influence peddling. The historians say: After the twin capitals fell and the realm shattered, the Chinese heartland became a lair of serpents and swine and the throne a frog’s burrow; arms clashed daily like hunters chasing the deer and crows seeking an uncertain perch. Fu Hong seized his frontier with savage cunning, exploited the Jie collapse, courted the eastern court while eyeing the northwest; poison gathered before his wolfish ambition could run free. Fu Jiàn inherited that legacy, led homesick hosts through the Shanxi gap, seized its towering passes and Qin’s fiercest blades, dared covet the great treasure and steal a majestic title—among pretenders he merits comparison. Fu Sheng’s cruelty flowed from wanton caprice. Seeing omens in the stars, he imagined the law planet drunk by night; he toyed with lives as if guessing when hungry beasts would next feed. Fu Jian, styled Yonggu, bore majestic bearing; he turned tribes into subjects and fulfilled the fish-dragon prophecies and the “grass fu” omens, cut down villains, seized the illicit throne, honored royal virtue, spread Confucian teaching, nurtured the people, and toiled at every duty. Wang Meng ordered state and army with vast talent; Fu Rong, imperial kin, guided policy; Quan and Xue offered blunt counsel; Deng and Zhang expanded power with loyalty and valor—worthy men thronged his court like prized timber, civil and military arms worked together, virtue and punishment both found their place. He crushed Yan, subdued Shu, seized Dai, annexed Liang, held two-thirds of the realm and seven of nine provinces; frontier peoples pledged loyalty, remote fastnesses bowed their hearts; poets halted his horse with songs and praised phoenix roosts—his feats rival antiquity’s heroes, far more than momentary peace. Even at the height of the Five Hu none matched him.
37
既而足己誇世，愎諫違謀，輕敵怒鄰，窮兵黷武。 懟三正之未葉，恥五運之猶乖，傾率土之師，起滔天之寇，負其犬羊之力，肆其吞噬之能。 自謂戰必勝，攻必取，便欲鳴鸞禹穴，駐蹕疑山，疏爵以侯楚材，築館以須歸命。 曾弗知人道助順，神理害盈，雖矜涿野之強，終致昆陽之敗。 遂使凶渠候隙，狡寇伺間，步搖啟其禍先，燒當乘其亂極，宗社遷於他族，身首罄於賊臣，貽戒將來，取笑天下，豈不哀哉！ 豈不謬哉！
Then he grew complacent, spurned advice, slighted foes and provoked neighbors, and exhausted the realm in wars. Resenting heaven’s seasons and ashamed the dynastic cycle still misfit, he hurled the whole army into rebel tides, trusting barbarian strength and devouring ambition. Certain every battle would bring victory, he dreamed of halting his chariot at Yu’s shrine on Mount Yi, ennobling Jin subjects and building lodges for surrendered emperors. He ignored that heaven favors the just and punishes pride; though he boasted like King Wu at Zhuolu, he ended like Wang Mang at Kunyang. Thus jackals watched for openings: Murong struck first, Shaodang Qiang rode the turmoil, the altars passed to strangers, his body fell to traitors—a warning and a jest to the world—how pitiful! What folly!
38
苻丕承亂僭竊，尋及傾敗，斯可謂天之所廢，人不能支。 苻登集離散之兵，厲死休之志，雖眾寡不敵，難以立功，而義烈慷慨，有足稱矣。
Fu Pi seized power amid ruin and quickly collapsed—truly heaven withdrew its mandate and no man could save him. Fu Deng rallied broken armies with banners sworn to fight to the death; though outnumbered and unable to restore the realm, his fervor deserved praise.
39
贊曰：洪惟壯勇，威棱氐種。 健藉世資，遂雄關、隴。 長生昏虐，敗不旋踵。 永固禎祥，肇自龍驤。 垂旒負扆，竊帝圖王。 患生縱敵，難起矜強。 丕、登僭假，淪胥以亡。
The encomium reads: Fu Hong towered in courage and struck awe among the Di. Fu Jiàn rode his inheritance to mastery of the passes and Longxi. Fu Sheng’s blind cruelty brought ruin before he could turn round. Fu Jian’s fortune rose from the Dragon-Soaring command. Veiled king though he was, he stole the mantle of emperor. Disaster came from sparing foes and pride in naked force. Fu Pi and Fu Deng ruled as pretenders and sank together into oblivion.
