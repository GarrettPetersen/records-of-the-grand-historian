苻丕苻登
Fu Pi and Fu Deng (biographies)
2
苻丕，字永叔，堅之長庶子也。 少而聰彗好學，博綜經史。 堅與言將略，嘉之，命鄧羌教以兵法。 文武才幹亞於苻融，為將善收士卒情，出鎮於鄴，東夏安之。 堅敗歸長安，丕為慕容垂所逼，自鄴奔枋頭。 堅之死也，丕復入鄴城，將收兵趙、魏，西赴長安。 會幽州刺史王永、平州刺史苻沖頻為垂將平規等所敗，乃遣昌黎太守宋敞焚燒和龍、薊城宮室，率眾三萬進屯壺關，遣使招丕。 丕乃去鄴，率男女六萬餘口進如潞川。 驃騎張蠔、并州刺史王騰迎之，入據晉陽，始知堅死問，舉哀于晉陽，三軍縞素。 王永留苻沖守壺關，率騎一萬會丕，勸稱尊號，丕從之，乃乙太元十年僭即皇帝位於晉陽南。 立堅行廟，太赦境內，改元曰太安。 置百官，以張蠔為侍中、司空，封上黨郡公； 王永為使持節、侍中、都督中外諸軍事、車騎大將軍、尚書令，進封清河公； 王騰為散騎常侍、中軍大將軍、司隸校尉、陽平郡公； 苻沖為左光祿大夫、尚書左僕射、西平王； 俱石子為衛將軍、濮陽公； 楊輔為尚書右僕射、濟陽公； 王亮為護軍將軍、彭城公； 強益耳、梁暢為侍中，徐義為吏部尚書，並封縣公。 自余封授各有差。
Fu Pi, whose courtesy name was Yongshu, was the eldest of Emperor Xuanzhao of Qin's sons born to a concubine. Even as a boy he was quick-witted and bookish, with wide learning in the classical canon and historical records. Fu Jian discussed strategy with him, was impressed, and assigned Deng Qiang to school him in the art of war. His gifts in civil and military affairs ranked just below Fu Rong's; as a commander he knew how to win the loyalty of the ranks. Posted to Ye, he brought stability to the eastern heartland. After Fu Jian's defeat at Fei River, Fu Pi was driven by Murong Chui to abandon Ye and flee to Fangtou. When news of Fu Jian's death reached him, Fu Pi reoccupied Ye, planning to raise forces in Zhao and Wei and march west toward Chang'an. Meanwhile Wang Yong of Youzhou and Fu Chong of Pingzhou had suffered repeated defeats at the hands of Murong Chui's officer Ping Gui. They dispatched Song Chang, Administrator of Changli, to torch the palaces at Helong and Ji, moved thirty thousand troops up to Huguan, and sent messengers urging Fu Pi to join them. Fu Pi accordingly quit Ye and marched more than sixty thousand people toward the Lu River basin. Zhang Hao, General of Agile Cavalry, and Wang Teng, Inspector of Bingzhou, received him; together they took Jinyang, where Fu Pi first received certain word of Fu Jian's death. The court mourned at Jinyang, and the entire host went into white mourning garb. Wang Yong left Fu Chong to hold Huguan, brought ten thousand cavalry to Fu Pi's camp, and urged him to take the throne; Fu Pi agreed. In the tenth year of the Eastern Jin Taiyuan era he presumptuously assumed the imperial title south of Jinyang. He set up a provisional ancestral shrine for Fu Jian, proclaimed a general amnesty throughout his domain, and adopted the reign title Taian. He installed a full bureaucracy, naming Zhang Hao Palace Attendant and Minister of Works with ducal rank in Shangdang; Wang Yong received the staff of authority and the posts of Palace Attendant, commander over all armies, General of Chariots and Cavalry, and Director of the Masters of Writing, and was promoted to Duke of Qinghe; Wang Teng became Supernumerary Cavalier Attendant, General of the Central Army, Colonel Director of Retainers, and Duke of Yangping; Fu Chong was named Grand Master of the Left, Left Vice Director of the Masters of Writing, and Prince of Xiping; Ju Shizi was appointed General Who Guards the Palace and Duke of Puyang; Yang Fu became Right Vice Director of the Masters of Writing and Duke of Jiyang; Wang Liang was named General Who Protects the Army and Duke of Pengcheng; Qiang Yier and Liang Chang were made Palace Attendants, Xu Yi Minister of Personnel, each receiving county-level ducal rank. All other titles and fiefs were distributed according to merit and station.
3
是時安西呂光自西域還師，至於宜禾，堅涼州刺史梁熙謀閉境距之。 高昌太守楊翰言於熙曰：「「呂光新定西國，兵強氣銳，其鋒不可當也。 度其事意，必有異圖。 且今關中擾亂，京師存亡未知，自河已西迄於流沙，地方萬里，帶甲十萬，鼎峙之勢實在今日。 若光出流沙，其勢難測。 高梧穀口，水險之要，宜先守之而奪其水。 彼既窮渴，自然投戈。 如其以遠不守，伊吾之關亦可距也。 若度此二要，雖有子房之策，難為計矣。 地有所必爭，真此機也。 」熙弗從。 美水令犍為張統說熙曰：「主上傾國南討，覆敗而還。 慕容垂擅兵河北，泓、沖寇逼京師，丁零雜虜，跋扈關、洛，州郡奸豪，所在風扇，王綱弛絕，人懷利己。 今呂光回師，將軍何以抗也？ 」熙曰：「誠深憂之，未知計之所出。 」統曰：「光雄果勇毅，明略絕人，今以蕩西域之威，擁歸師之銳，鋒若猛火之盛于原，弗可敵也。 將軍世受殊恩，忠誠夙著，立勳王室，宜在於今。 行唐公洛，上之從弟，勇冠一時。 為將軍計者，莫若奉為盟主，以攝眾望，推忠義以總率群豪，則光無異心也。 資其精銳，東兼毛興，連王統、楊璧，集四州之眾，掃凶逆于諸夏，甯帝室於關中，此桓文之舉也。 」熙又不從。 殺洛於西海，以子胤為鷹揚將軍，率眾五萬距光於酒泉。 敦煌太守姚靜、晉昌太守李純以郡降光。 胤及光戰于安彌，為光所敗。 武威太守彭濟執熙迎光，光殺之。 建威、西郡太守索泮，奮威、督洪池已南諸軍事、酒泉太守宋皓等，並為光所殺。
Meanwhile Lü Guang, General Who Pacifies the West, was marching home from the Tarim basin and reached Yihe. Liang Xi, whom Fu Jian had left as Inspector of Liangzhou, planned to shut the frontier and bar his passage. Yang Han, magistrate of Gaochang, warned Liang Xi: "Lü Guang has just subdued the Western Regions; his army is strong and his morale keen. You cannot meet that spearhead head-on. Judging his intentions, he must be planning something beyond a simple homecoming. Moreover Guanzhong is in turmoil and the fate of the capital uncertain. From the Yellow River west to the desert stretches ten thousand li of territory and a hundred thousand armored men. The moment for a three-way standoff of powers is now. Once Lü Guang emerges from the desert, his next move will be impossible to predict. The mouth of Gaowu Gorge is a choke point where water is the decisive terrain; seize it first and cut off their supply of water. Once they are parched and at their wits' end, they will lay down their arms of their own accord. If you decline to hold a distant line, you can still block him at the Yiwu frontier pass. Let him past those two barriers and even a strategist like Zhang Liang could not save you. Some ground history itself demands you fight for; this is that moment. Liang Xi rejected the counsel. Zhang Tong of Qianwei, magistrate of Meishui, urged Liang Xi: "Our sovereign staked the realm on the southern campaign and came home in ruinous defeat. Murong Chui dominates Hebei; Murong Hong and Murong Chong threaten the capital; Dingling and other tribes rampage through the Guanzhong and Luoyang regions; opportunists and strongmen stir insurrection wherever they can. Imperial authority has collapsed and every man looks to his own advantage. With Lü Guang's army marching home, how do you propose to stop him? Liang Xi answered: "I share that fear, yet I see no clear course. Zhang Tong replied: "Lü Guang is bold, decisive, and a born leader; his tactical sense is extraordinary. Fresh from conquering the west, his veterans will hit you like wildfire sweeping dry grass. They cannot be faced in open battle. Your family has long enjoyed the dynasty's exceptional favor and a reputation for loyalty. If ever there was a time to serve the house of Fu, it is now. Fu Luo, Duke of Xingtang, is a cousin of the late emperor and the foremost warrior of his generation. The soundest plan is to acclaim Fu Luo as leader of the alliance, win the gentry's confidence, and rally the regional lords under the banner of loyalty. Lü Guang will then have no excuse to turn on you. Use his elite soldiers to absorb Mao Xing in the east, link arms with Wang Tong and Yang Bi, concentrate the armies of four provinces, purge the traitors from the heartland, and restore order in Guanzhong—that would be a deed worthy of Duke Huan or Duke Wen of Qi and Jin. Again Liang Xi refused. He executed Fu Luo at Xihai, appointed his son Liang Yin General Who Displays Might, and sent fifty thousand men to block Lü Guang at Jiuquan. Yao Jing of Dunhuang and Li Chun of Jinchang yielded their prefectures to Lü Guang. Liang Yin met Lü Guang at Anmi and was routed. Peng Ji of Wuwei seized Liang Xi and handed him to Lü Guang, who put Liang Xi to death. Suo Pan, who held the titles of General Who Establishes Might and Administrator of Xijun, and Song Hao, General Who Displays Might, commander south of Hongchi Pool, and Administrator of Jiuquan, among others, were all executed by Lü Guang.
4
堅尚書令、魏昌公苻纂自關中來奔，拜太尉，進封東海王。 以中山太守王兗為平東將軍、平州刺史、阜城侯，苻定為征東將軍、冀州牧、高城侯，苻紹為鎮東將軍、督冀州諸軍事、重合侯，苻謨為征西將軍、幽州牧、高邑侯，苻亮為鎮北大將軍、督幽、并二州諸軍事，並進爵郡公。 定、紹據信都，謨、亮先據常山，慕容垂之圍鄴城也，並降于垂，聞丕稱尊號，遣使謝罪。 王兗固守博陵，與垂相持。 左將軍竇沖、秦州刺史王統、河州刺史毛興、益州刺史王廣、南秦州刺史楊璧、衛將軍楊定，並據隴右，遣使招丕，請討姚萇。 丕大悅，以定為驃騎大將軍、雍州牧，沖為征西大將軍、梁州牧，統鎮西大將軍，興車騎大將軍，璧征南大將軍，並開府儀同三司，加散騎常侍，廣安西將軍，皆進位州牧。
Fu Zuan, Fu Jian's former Director of the Masters of Writing and Duke of Weichang, escaped from Guanzhong to join Fu Pi, who named him Grand Commandant and elevated him to Prince of the Eastern Sea. Wang Gun, Administrator of Zhongshan, became General Who Pacifies the East, Inspector of Pingzhou, and Marquis of Fucheng. Fu Ding was named General Who Campaigns East, Governor of Jizhou, and Marquis of Gaocheng. Fu Shao received General Who Garrisons the East, command over all Jizhou forces, and the Marquisate of Chonghe. Fu Mo was appointed General Who Campaigns West, Governor of Youzhou, and Marquis of Gaoyi. Fu Liang became Grand General Who Garrisons the North with authority over Youzhou and Bingzhou. Each was then promoted to ducal rank at the commandery level. Fu Ding and Fu Shao held Xindu while Fu Mo and Fu Liang had earlier occupied Changshan; during Murong Chui's siege of Ye they had all submitted to him. Learning that Fu Pi had declared himself emperor, they dispatched messengers to beg forgiveness. Wang Gun held out in Boling in a prolonged stalemate with Murong Chui. Dou Chong, Wang Tong of Qinzhou, Mao Xing of Hezhou, Wang Guang of Yizhou, Yang Bi of Southern Qinzhou, and Yang Ding, General Who Guards the Palace, all controlled the Longyou plateau. They sent envoys inviting Fu Pi to join them in a campaign against Yao Chang. Fu Pi was delighted. He named Yang Ding Grand General of Agile Cavalry and Governor of Yongzhou; Dou Chong Grand General Who Campaigns West and Governor of Liangzhou; Wang Tong Grand General Who Garrisons the West; Mao Xing Grand General of Chariots and Cavalry; and Yang Bi Grand General Who Campaigns South—each with independent headquarters and honors matching the Three Excellencies—while adding the title Supernumerary Cavalier Attendant and appointing Wang Guang General Who Pacifies the West. All were promoted to provincial governor rank.
5
於是王永宣檄州郡曰：「大行皇帝棄背萬國，四海無主。 征東大將軍、長樂公，先帝元子，聖武自天，受命荊南，威振衡海，分陝東都，道被夷夏，仁澤光於宇宙，德聽侔于《下武》。 永與司空蠔等謹順天人之望，以季秋吉辰奉公紹承大統，銜哀即事，棲穀總戎，枕戈待旦，志雪大恥。 慕容垂為封豕於關東，泓、沖繼凶於京邑，致乘輿播越，宗社淪傾。 羌賊姚萇，我之牧士，乘釁滔天，親行大逆，有生之巨賊也。 永累葉受恩，世荷將相，不與驪山之戎、滎澤之狄共戴皇天，同履厚土。 諸牧伯公侯或宛沛宗臣，或四七勳舊，豈忍舍破國之醜豎，縱殺君之逆賊乎！ 主上飛龍九五，實協天心，靈祥休瑞，史不輟書，投戈效義之士三十餘萬，少康、光武之功可旬朔而成。 今以衛將軍俱石子為前軍師，司空張蠔為中軍都督。 武將猛士，風烈雷震，志殄元凶，義無他顧。 永謹奉乘輿，恭行天罰。 君臣終始之義，在三忘軀之誠，戮力同之，以建晉、鄭之美。」
Wang Yong then issued a manifesto to the provinces: "The late emperor has left the realm, and the empire has no sovereign. The Grand General Who Campaigns East, Duke of Changle, is the late emperor's firstborn: heaven-endowed in arms, entrusted with authority south of the Jing, his might rolls from the Heng range to the sea. He has shared the burden of the eastern capital, and his virtue embraces both barbarian and Chinese. His humane rule illuminates the cosmos, and his good name rivals the praise sung in the 'Lower Martial' ode of the Classic of Poetry. I, Wang Yong, together with Minister of Works Zhang Hao and others, have bowed to the will of Heaven and the people and, on an auspicious day in late autumn, installed our lord to carry on the imperial line. Though we mourn, we take up the work of state, endure hardship, and keep our weapons at hand, resolved to avenge this national disgrace. Murong Chui rampages east of the mountains like a monstrous boar; Murong Hong and Murong Chong have brought ruin to the capital, forcing the throne into exile and casting down the altars of state. The Qiang rebel Yao Chang, once a mere herdsman in our service, has exploited chaos to commit towering crimes and has personally murdered his sovereign—the greatest traitor alive. My house has enjoyed the dynasty's favor for generations and has borne the responsibilities of general and minister. I refuse to stand under the same sky or walk the same earth as the Rong of Mount Li or the Di of the Xingze marshes—those who would betray their sovereign. You governors and nobles are either kinsmen who stood with the house at Nanyang and Pei or veterans who earned your honors in the founding wars. Can you truly cast in your lot with upstarts who would shatter the realm or with regicides who stalk free? Our sovereign has ascended the throne as the true dragon; Heaven itself approves. Auspicious signs crowd the chronicles, and more than three hundred thousand men have already laid down private feuds to serve the righteous cause. The restoration achieved by Shaokang or Emperor Guangwu of Han can be repeated within weeks. Ju Shizi, General Who Guards the Palace, is named strategist of the vanguard; Minister of Works Zhang Hao commands the central army. Our warriors move like wind and thunder, intent on destroying the arch-traitor, with no thought but duty. I, Wang Yong, will escort the imperial regalia and execute Heaven's judgment upon the rebels. Let every minister remember the bond that ties liege and vassal from first to last, and the duty that demands we risk our lives without hesitation. Join your strength to ours and help build a restoration as glorious as those of Jin and Zheng in antiquity."
6
先是，慕容驎攻王兗於博陵，至是糧竭矢盡，郡功曹張猗逾城聚眾應驎。 袞臨城數之曰：「卿，秦之人也。 吾，卿之君也。 起眾應賊，號稱義兵，何名實相違之甚！ 卿兄往合鄉宗，親逐城主，天地不容，為世大戮。 身滅未幾，卿復續之。 卿見為吾吏，親尋干戈，競為戎首，為爾君者，不亦難乎！ 今人何取卿一切之功，寧能忘卿不忠不孝之事！ 古人有雲，求忠臣必出孝子之門，卿母在城，不能顧之，何忠義之可望！ 惡不絕世，卿之謂也。 不圖中州禮義之邦邦，而卿門風若斯。 卿去老母如脫屣，吾復何論哉！ 」既而城陷，兗及固安侯苻鑒，並為驎所殺。
Murong Lin had long besieged Wang Gun in Boling; supplies were now exhausted. Zhang Yi, a clerical officer of the commandery, slipped over the walls and raised a party that opened the city to Murong Lin. Wang Gun stood on the wall and rebuked him: "You were born a subject of Qin. I am your lawful superior. You have rallied men for a rebel host yet call it a righteous militia—never was name further from truth! Your brother once rallied the local clans and personally drove out his rightful lord. Heaven and earth condemned him, and he died a traitor's death. His corpse is barely cold, and you take up his crime. You serve under my command, yet you seize weapons and make yourself captain of mutiny. What lord could endure such subjects? Do you imagine anyone will honor your petty coup while forgetting treason and impiety? The ancients said loyal ministers come only from filial homes. Your own mother remains inside these walls, yet you abandon her—what loyalty do you claim? Villainy runs in families, as you prove. I did not think to see such conduct in the heartland where ritual is law, yet your house sinks to this. You cast off your aged mother as you would a worn shoe—I waste breath on you. The city soon fell. Wang Gun and Fu Jian, Marquis of Gu'an, were both put to death by Murong Lin.
7
丕復以王永為司徒、錄尚書事，徐義為尚書令，加右光祿大夫。
Fu Pi again named Wang Yong Minister of Education with oversight of the Masters of Writing, made Xu Yi Director of the Masters of Writing, and granted him the additional title Grand Master of the Right.
8
初，王廣還自成都也，奔其兄秦州刺史統。 及長安不守，廣攻河州牧毛興於枹罕。 興遣建節將軍、臨清柏衛平率其宗人千七百夜擊廣軍，大敗之。 王統復遣兵助廣，興於是嬰城固守。 既而襲王廣，敗之，廣亡奔秦州，為隴西鮮卑匹蘭所執，送詣姚萇。 興既敗王廣，謀伐王統，平上邽。 袍罕諸氐皆窘於兵革而疲不堪命，乃殺興，推衛平為使持節、安西將軍、河州刺史，遣使請命。
When Wang Guang withdrew from Chengdu, he fled to his brother Wang Tong, Inspector of Qinzhou. After Chang'an fell, Wang Guang besieged Mao Xing, Governor of Hezhou, at Fuhan. Mao Xing dispatched Weiping, General Who Establishes Integrity and Baron of Linqing, with seventeen hundred clansmen in a night attack that routed Wang Guang's force. Wang Tong sent reinforcements to his brother, and Mao Xing withdrew behind the walls of Fuhan to endure a siege. Mao Xing then sortied, crushed Wang Guang, and drove him toward Qinzhou, where Xianbei tribesmen of Longxi under Pilan captured him and delivered him to Yao Chang. After defeating Wang Guang, Mao Xing planned a campaign against Wang Tong to secure Shanggui. The Di clans of Fuhan were exhausted by endless war and could bear their orders no longer. They assassinated Mao Xing, acclaimed Wei Ping as Bearer of the Staff, General Who Pacifies the West, and Inspector of Hezhou, and sent envoys to seek recognition from the court.
9
刁雲殺慕容忠，乃推慕容永為使持節、大都督中外諸軍事、大將軍、大單于、雍、秦、梁、涼四州牧、錄尚書事、河東王、稱籓於垂。 征東苻定、鎮東苻紹、征北苻謨、鎮北苻亮皆降于慕容垂。
Diao Yun murdered Murong Zhong and raised Murong Yong with the full panoply of titles—Bearer of the Staff, supreme commander, Grand General, Great Chanyu, governor of Yong, Qin, Liang, and Liang provinces, overseer of the Masters of Writing, and Prince of Hedong—while acknowledging Murong Chui as overlord. Fu Ding, Fu Shao, Fu Mo, and Fu Liang—the generals holding the east and north—all submitted to Murong Chui.

登進所胡空堡，戎夏歸之者十有餘萬。 姚萇遣其將軍姚方成攻陷徐嵩堡，嵩被殺，悉坑戎士。 登率眾下隴入朝那，姚萇據武都相持，累戰互有勝負。 登軍中大饑，收葚以供兵士。 立其子崇為皇太子，弁為南安王，尚為北海王。 姚萇退還安定。 登就食新平，留其大軍于胡空堡，率騎萬餘圍萇營，四面大哭，哀聲動人。 萇惡之，乃命三軍哭以應登，登乃引退。
Fu Deng advanced on Hukongbao, and more than a hundred thousand tribesmen and Chinese flocked to him. Yao Chang sent Yao Fangcheng to storm Xu Song's fort; Xu Song died fighting and the garrison was buried alive. Fu Deng descended from the Long plateau into Chaona while Yao Chang held Wudu; they clashed repeatedly with mixed results. Famine gripped Fu Deng's camp, and the men fed on wild mulberries. He named his son Fu Chong crown prince, Fu Bian Prince of Nan'an, and Fu Shang Prince of Beihai. Yao Chang pulled back to Anding. Fu Deng foraged in Xinping, left his main force at Hukongbao, and rode out with ten thousand horsemen to ring Yao Chang's camp, where his men wailed on every side until the sound of grief shook the enemy. Yao Chang, unnerved, ordered his own army to wail in answer, and Fu Deng withdrew.
22

Because Fu Deng won battle after battle, Yao Chang concluded that Fu Jian's spirit was aiding him and set up a tablet to Fu Jian in his own camp, pleading, "The slaughter at Xinping years ago was not my doing. My brother Yao Xiang crossed north of the Shan defile, begged passage to march west, and—like the fox that turns toward its native hill—only wanted a glimpse of his homeland. You and Fu Mei barred his path in battle, yet could not stop him and fell in the field. Yao Xiang commanded me to strike the blow; the fault is not mine. Fu Deng is only a distant twig of your house, yet he burns to avenge you. I avenge my brother—where is the wrong in that? Once you bestowed on me the title General Dragon Soarer and said, 'It was as Dragon Soarer that I founded this realm—spare no effort!' That clear decree still rings in my ears. Even as a spirit, would you truly work through Fu Deng to destroy me and forget what you said when we campaigned together? I have raised this effigy so your spirit may rest here. Pardon my faults and heed my heartfelt plea. Fu Deng pressed his attack on Yao Chang, then mounted a tower and shouted, "Since time began, has any regicide set up an idol to beg blessings and expected it to help? He roared, "Yao Chang, regicide, show yourself and face me in single combat! Why slaughter the innocent? Yao Chang, unnerved, held his tongue. Yao Chang's own effigy of Fu Jian brought no victory, and his camp suffered nightly panic until he had the image's head struck off with full fanfare and sent to Fu Deng.
23

Fu Deng's generals Dou Luo, Dou Yu, and others were caught plotting treason and fled to Yao Chang. Fu Deng failed to take Pengchi but stormed the Mijie camp and every stronghold along Fanchuan, reducing them all. After a string of defeats Yao Chang sent Yao Chong to strike Dajie; Fu Deng intercepted him at Anqiu, annihilated twenty-five thousand men, then took Pingliang from Wu Zhong and Tang Kuang. He named Fu Shuoyuan of the Masters of Writing Forward Guard General and Colonel Who Extinguishes the Qiang to hold the city. Fu Deng seized Goutouyuan and pressed against Anding. Yao Chang led thirty thousand horsemen in a night assault on Dajie, slaughtered Fu Deng's wife Lady Mao and his sons Fu Bian and Fu Shang, captured dozens of noted commanders, and marched away more than fifty thousand captives.
24

Fu Deng rallied his survivors at Hukongbao and sent orders elevating Dou Chong to Grand Marshal, General of Agile Cavalry, grand vanguard commander, and governor of Longdong; Yang Ding to Left Chancellor, supreme commander, and director of all military affairs; and Yang Bi to Grand General with authority over Longyou. Dou Chong was to lead the available forces as vanguard from Fanchuan toward Chang'an. Fu Deng marched from Xinping straight to the Qianhugu redoubt at Xinfeng. Yang Ding would bring up the Longshang armies as rear guard while Yang Bi stayed to defend Qiuchi. He also instructed Yang Zheng of Bingzhou and Yang Kai of Jizhou to march their commands to a general rendezvous at Chang'an. Yao Chang dispatched Wang Polu to raid Qinzhou; Yang Ding met him at Genu Slope on the Qingshui River and shattered his army. Fu Deng besieged Zhang Longshi at Yangquanbao until Yao Chang relieved the place, forcing Fu Deng to withdraw. Yao Chang secretly ordered Ren Kan and Zong Du to pose as defectors, sent messengers inviting Fu Deng in, and promised to open the gates. Fu Deng believed them. Lei Edi rode up to warn Fu Deng: "Yao Chang is cunning and commands men's loyalty; this will prove a trap. Think twice. Fu Deng canceled the advance. When Yao Chang learned that Lei Edi had visited Fu Deng he told his officers, "That Qiang is full of guile; now that he has warned Fu Deng, the ruse is ruined. Learning that Yao Chang had raised the portcullis to trap him, Fu Deng exclaimed to his staff, "General Lei Who Campaigns East must be almost a prophet! Without him I would have walked into that boy's snare. Yao Chang then stormed Xinluo fortress. Qi Yinan, Administrator of Fufeng under Yao Chang, defected to Fu Deng. Fu Deng's generals Lu Chai, Qiang Wu, and others brought their troops over to Yao Chang. Fu Deng attacked Zhang Yesheng in Longdong; Yao Chang tried to save him but failed and pulled back. Wei Hefei, Fu Deng's general, besieged Yao Dangcheng at Xingcheng and was slain by Yao Chang's forces.
25
馮翊郭質起兵廣鄉以應登，宣檄三輔曰：「義感君子，利動小人。 吾等生逢先帝堯、舜之化，累世受恩，非常伯納言之子，即卿校牧守之胤，而可坐視豺狼忍害君父！ 裸屍薦棘，痛結幽泉，山陵無松隧之兆，靈主無清廟之頌，賊臣莫大之甚，自古所未聞。 雖茹荼之苦，銜蓼之辛，何以諭之！ 姚萇窮凶肆害，毒被人神，於圖讖歷數萬無一分，而敢妄竊重名，厚顏瞬息，日月固所不照，二儀實亦不育。 皇天雖欲絕之，亦將假手於忠節。 凡百君子，皆夙漸神化，有懷義方，含恥而存，孰若蹈道而沒乎！ 」眾咸然之。 唯鄭縣人苟曜不從，聚眾數千應姚萇。 登以質為平東將軍、馮翊太守。 質遣部將伐曜，大敗而歸。 質乃東引楊楷，以為聲援，又與曜戰于鄭東，為曜所敗，遂歸於萇，萇以為將軍。 質眾皆潰散。
Guo Zhi of Fengyi rose at Guangxiang in support of Fu Deng and issued a manifesto to the capital region: "Honor moves the noble; gain moves the base. We have lived under a sovereign as humane as Yao or Shun and received generations of grace. We are all sons of high ministers or heirs of governors—can we sit idle while jackals butcher our sovereign? His body was left naked among brambles; grief sinks to the yellow springs. No imperial mausoleum receives him, no hymn sounds in the clean temple. No traitor in history has outdone this—not in any age have we heard the like. Bitter herbs and biting wormwood cannot describe our pain! Yao Chang's cruelty poisons heaven and earth, yet prophecy and celestial mandate grant him not the slightest legitimacy. Still he steals the imperial title without shame. The luminaries turn away; Heaven and Earth disown him. Though High Heaven would destroy him, it will use the hands of the loyal to do it. Every man of honor has imbibed the transforming power of the sages and keeps righteousness in his breast. Better to die for the right path than live shamed! The crowd roared assent. Only Gou Yao of Zheng county refused, raising several thousand men for Yao Chang. Fu Deng named Guo Zhi General Who Pacifies the East and Administrator of Fengyi. Guo Zhi sent a deputy against Gou Yao and suffered a crushing defeat. Guo Zhi then called on Yang Kai in the east for support, fought Gou Yao again east of Zheng, lost, and submitted to Yao Chang, who made him a general. Guo Zhi's army melted away.
26
登自雍攻萇將金溫于范氏堡，克之，遂渡渭水，攻萇京兆太守韋范于段氏堡，不克，進據曲牢。 苟曜有眾一萬，據逆方堡，密應登，登去曲牢繁川，次於馬頭願。 萇率騎來距，大戰敗之，斬其尚書吳忠，進攻新平。 萇率眾救之，登引退，復攻安定，為萇所敗，據路承堡。
From Yong Fu Deng took Jin Wen at Fanshi fortress, crossed the Wei, failed against Wei Fan at Duanshi fortress, and occupied Qulao. Gou Yao commanded ten thousand men at Nifang fortress and secretly aided Fu Deng, who moved from Qulao toward Fanchuan and camped at Matouyuan. Yao Chang rode out to meet him, won a major victory, slew Minister Wu Zhong, and pressed on toward Xinping. Yao Chang relieved the city; Fu Deng withdrew, struck Anding again, lost to Yao Chang, and fell back to Lucheng fortress.
27
是時萇疾病，見苻堅為崇。 登聞之，秣馬萬兵，告堅神主曰：「曾孫登自受任執戈，幾將一紀，未嘗不上天錫祐，皇鑒垂矜，所在必克，賊旅冰摧。 今太皇帝之靈降災疢於逆羌，以形類推之，醜虜必將不振。 登當因其隕斃，順行天誅，拯復梓宮，謝罪清廟。 」於是大赦境內，百僚進位二等。 與萇將姚崇爭麥于清水，累為崇所敗。 進逼安定，去城九十餘里。 萇疾小瘳，率眾距登，登去營逆萇，萇遣其將姚熙隆別攻登營，登懼，退還。 萇夜引軍過登營三十餘里以躡登後。 旦而候人告曰：「賊諸營已空，不知所向。 」登驚曰：「此為何人，去令我不知，來令我不覺，謂其將死，忽然復來，朕與此羌同世，何其厄哉！ 」遂罷師還雍。
Yao Chang fell ill and was haunted by visions of Fu Jian. Hearing this, Fu Deng readied horse and men and addressed Fu Jian's tablet: "Your great-grandson has borne arms almost twelve years. Heaven has always blessed us; your spirit has shown pity. Every field is ours; rebel armies shatter like ice. Now your spirit has struck the traitor Qiang with illness; by every sign the wretch must fail. I shall seize his dying moment to execute Heaven's judgment, recover your catafalque, and make amends before the ancestral shrine. He proclaimed a general amnesty and promoted every official by two ranks. He fought Yao Chong for the wheat fields along the Qingshui and suffered repeated defeats. He advanced to within ninety li of Anding. As Yao Chang rallied, he marched against Fu Deng, who left camp to engage him. Yao Chang sent Yao Xilong against Fu Deng's rear; Fu Deng panicked and withdrew. By night Yao Chang slipped past Fu Deng's camp and swung more than thirty li onto his rear. At dawn scouts reported, "The enemy camps stand empty; we cannot tell where they went. Fu Deng cried, "What sort of foe vanishes without trace, returns unseen, plays at death yet strikes again? To share an age with this Qiang—what curse is this? He broke off the campaign and withdrew to Yong.
28
以竇沖為右丞相。 尋而沖叛，自稱秦王，建年號。 登攻之於野人堡，沖請救于姚萇，萇遣其太子興攻胡空堡以救之。 登引兵還赴胡空堡，沖遂與萇連和。
Dou Chong was named Right Chancellor. Soon Dou Chong rebelled, declared himself King of Qin, and adopted a reign title. Fu Deng besieged him at Yereng fortress. Dou Chong begged Yao Chang for help, and Yao Chang sent the crown prince Yao Xing against Hukongbao to lift the siege. Fu Deng wheeled back toward Hukongbao, and Dou Chong made common cause with Yao Chang.
29
至是萇死，登聞之喜曰：「姚興小兒，吾將折杖以笞之。 」於是大赦，盡眾而東，攻屠各姚奴、帛蒲二堡，克之，自甘泉向關中。 興追登不及數十里，登從六陌趣廢橋，興將尹緯據橋以待之。 登爭水不得，眾渴死者十二三。 與緯大戰，為緯所敗，其夜眾潰，登單馬奔雍。
When Yao Chang died, Fu Deng exulted, "Yao Xing is a mere boy; I will break a switch to whip him with. He proclaimed amnesty, marched the whole army east, stormed the Tuge strongholds of Yao Nu and Bopu, and advanced from Ganquan into Guanzhong. Yao Xing pursued but remained tens of li behind as Fu Deng hurried from Liumo toward Feiqiao, where Yin Wei held the bridge against him. Fu Deng could not win the watering places, and two or three men in ten died of thirst. He fought Yin Wei and lost; that night his army collapsed, and Fu Deng fled alone on horseback to Yong.
30
初，登之東也，留其弟司徒廣守雍，太子崇守胡空堡。 廣、崇聞登敗，出奔，眾散。 登至，無所歸，遂奔平涼，收集遺眾入馬毛山。 興率眾攻之，登遣子汝陰王宗質於隴西鮮卑乞伏乾歸，結婚請援，乾歸遣騎二萬救登。 登引軍出迎，與興戰于山南，為興所敗，登被殺。 在位九年，時年五十二。 崇奔於湟中，僭稱尊號，改元延初。 偽諡登曰高皇帝，廟號太宗。 崇為乾歸所逐，崇、定皆死。
When Fu Deng marched east he had left his brother Fu Guang, Minister of Education, at Yong and the crown prince Fu Chong at Hukongbao. When they heard of Fu Deng's defeat, Fu Guang and Fu Chong fled and their troops dispersed. Fu Deng found no refuge and raced to Pingliang, where he gathered survivors and withdrew into the Maomao Hills. Yao Xing attacked him there. Fu Deng sent his son Fu Zong, Prince of Ruyin, as hostage to the Xianbei chief Qifu Gangui of Longxi, sealed the tie with a marriage alliance, and begged rescue. Qifu Gangui sent twenty thousand horsemen. Fu Deng marched out to meet them, fought Yao Xing south of the mountains, was defeated, and was slain. He had reigned nine years and was fifty-two when he died. Fu Chong fled to the Huangshui valley, declared himself emperor, and adopted the reign title Yanchu. His court posthumously named him Emperor Gao with temple name Taizong. Qifu Gangui drove out Fu Chong; both Fu Chong and Yang Ding perished.
31
始，健以穆帝永和七年僭立，至登五世，凡四十有四歲，以孝武帝太元十九年滅。
From Fu Jian's usurpation in the seventh year of Yonghe under Emperor Mu of Jin through five generations to Fu Deng, the state lasted forty-four years and fell in the nineteenth year of Taiyuan under Emperor Xiaowu.
32

Suo Pan, courtesy name Delin, was a native of Dunhuang. His family had been eminent for generations. In youth he roamed as a knight-errant; as an adult he reformed, took to scholarship, and showed the makings of a statesman. While Zhang Tianxi directed affairs he named Suo Pan General Who Crowned the Army and aide in the secretariat. When Zhang Tianxi took the throne Suo Pan became Director of Military Affairs and served as recorder within the palace. As judicial supervisor he brought order to the provincial yamen and reformed conduct in every county. Promoted to left commandant of the imperial guard, he earned a name for diligence and efficiency. He was posted as general of the central bastion, Administrator of Wuwei in Xijun, and colonel in charge of tribal troops. His rule was mild and just; tribesmen and Chinese alike cherished his kindness, and Zhang Tianxi held him in high regard. Fu Jian met him and marveled, "Liangzhou truly breeds gentlemen! For his moral stature on the western frontier Suo Pan was named chief administrator of the province. When Lü Guang seized Guzang, Suo Pan held his commandery until Lü Guang stormed it and took him prisoner. Lü Guang said, "I have pacified the west and mean to rescue the capital. Liang Xi blocked my road home like a traitor to the court. Why do you cling to your district like him and rank yourself among the arch-villains? Suo Pan answered sharply, "You were ordered to chastise rebel Hu—were you ordered to ravage Liangzhou? What crime had our prince committed that you should destroy him? I regret only my weakness, which kept me from holding long enough to avenge my sovereign. Am I to be compared with Peng Ji of the Di, who betrayed his lord at the first rumor? When the sovereign falls, his minister dies with him—that is the constant teaching of propriety. He went to the public scaffold unflinching.
33
弟菱，有俊才，仕張天錫為執法中郎、冗從右監。 苻堅世至伏波將軍、典農都尉，與泮俱被害。
His younger brother Suo Ling was a brilliant man who served Zhang Tianxi as director of law enforcement and supernumerary overseer of the right. Under Fu Jian he rose to General Who Calms the Waves and commandant of agriculture; he perished together with Suo Pan.
34

Xu Song, courtesy name Yuangao, was the son of Xu Sheng. Even as a youth he was known for spotless integrity. Under Fu Jian he was nominated as a worthy candidate, served as a court gentleman, and rose to magistrate of Chang'an. He prosecuted every lawbreaking kinsman of the great houses without fear, and influence-peddling at his yamen ceased. Fu Jian was deeply impressed and told Xu Song's uncle Xu Cheng, "Most men take high office merely to go through the motions. This young man stands apart; he has the makings of a chief minister. He was transferred to Shiping, where he ruled with both authority and kindness. When the fortress fell Yao Fangcheng seized him for interrogation. Xu Song thundered, "Yao Chang deserved death a thousand times over, yet our sovereign stayed the execution his captors demanded and spared him. He rose to general's rank yet showed none of a hound's loyalty to its master and became the first to rebel. How can one expect human decency from you Qiang? Kill me now that I may join the late emperor and drag Yao Chang down to justice beneath the earth. Enraged, Yao Fangcheng had him cut in three and lacquered his skull into a chamber pot. Fu Deng mourned him bitterly and posthumously named him Grand General of Chariots and Cavalry with honors matching the Three Excellencies, with the posthumous epithet Loyal and Martial.
35
史臣曰：自兩京殄覆，九土分崩，赤縣成蛇豕之墟，紫宸遷蛙黽之穴，干戈日用，戰爭方興，猶逐鹿之並驅，若瞻烏之靡定。 苻洪擅蠻陬之桀黠，乘羯虜之危亡，乃附款江東而志圖關右，禍生蠆毒，未逞狼心。 健既承家，克隆凶緒，率思歸之眾，投山西之隙，據億丈之岩險，總三秦之果銳，敢窺大寶，遂竊鴻名，校數奸雄，有可言矣。 長生慘虐，稟自率由。 睹辰象之災，謂法星之夜飲； 忍生靈之命，疑猛獸之朝饑。 但肆毒于刑殘，曾無心於戒懼。 招亂速禍，不亦宜乎！
The historians write: After the twin capitals fell and the realm shattered, the heartland became a wasteland of serpents and swine, the throne a burrow for frogs and newts. Arms clashed daily as war spread—all pursued the deer together, none knew where the crow would next alight. Fu Hong wielded the fierce cunning of the southern tribes and exploited the Jie in their death throes. He pledged fealty to the court east of the Yangzi even as he schemed to take the northwest. Poison struck before his wolfish ambition could fully show. Fu Jian inherited that legacy, magnified its violent course, led homesick armies through the opening west of the mountains, seized precipitous strongholds, and concentrated the fiercest fighters of the three Qins. He dared covet the throne and usurp a grand title—among the age's usurpers he merits mention. Fu Sheng, styled Changsheng, was wantonly cruel by innate disposition. Seeing portents in the stars, he called them the planet of law carousing at night; he toyed with the lives of the people as if wondering whether a beast were hungry at dawn. He reveled in torture and mutilation and never thought of restraint. Small wonder he brought ruin upon himself.
36
永固雅量瑰姿，變夷眾夏，葉魚龍之謠詠，挺草付之休征，克翦奸回，纂承偽曆，遵明王之德教，闡先聖之儒風，撫育黎元，憂勤庶政。 王猛以宏材緯軍國，苻融以懿戚贊經綸，權薛以諒直進規謨，鄧、張以忠勇恢威略，俊賢效足，杞梓呈才，文武兼施，德刑具舉。 乃平燕定蜀，擒代吞涼，跨三分之二，居九州之七，遐荒慕義，幽險宅心，因止馬而獻歌，托棲鸞以成頌，因以功侔曩烈，豈直化洽當年！ 雖五胡之盛，莫之比也。
Fu Jian, styled Yonggu, combined dignity and presence, won over barbarian and Chinese alike, fulfilled the fish-into-dragon prophecies and the omen of the character Fu in the reed, purged traitors, seized the usurped mandate, promoted humane government, revived Confucian learning, cherished the common people, and toiled at every duty of state. Wang Meng shaped army and realm with grand ability; Fu Rong, as kinsman, helped steer policy; Quan and Xue offered blunt counsel; Deng and Zhang widened the empire through loyalty and valor. Talents flocked like fine timber in a forest; civil and military measures worked in tandem, kindness and law alike enforced. He subdued Yan, secured Shu, seized Dai, absorbed Liang, held two-thirds of the realm and seven of the nine provinces. Far lands offered allegiance; wild regions bent the knee. Hymns hailed him beside halted steeds; odes compared him to nesting phoenixes. His achievements rivaled the ancient conquerors—his sway was not merely a moment's peace. Even among the mightiest of the Five Hu, none matched him.
37
既而足己誇世，愎諫違謀，輕敵怒鄰，窮兵黷武。 懟三正之未葉，恥五運之猶乖，傾率土之師，起滔天之寇，負其犬羊之力，肆其吞噬之能。 自謂戰必勝，攻必取，便欲鳴鸞禹穴，駐蹕疑山，疏爵以侯楚材，築館以須歸命。 曾弗知人道助順，神理害盈，雖矜涿野之強，終致昆陽之敗。 遂使凶渠候隙，狡寇伺間，步搖啟其禍先，燒當乘其亂極，宗社遷於他族，身首罄於賊臣，貽戒將來，取笑天下，豈不哀哉！ 豈不謬哉！
Then he grew complacent, spurned good counsel, despised his foes and provoked his neighbors, and exhausted the realm in ceaseless war. Resentful that heaven's mandate had not settled in his favor, ashamed that the five phases had not turned his way, he hurled every soldier of the empire against a foe that blotted out the sky, trusting brute force and the appetite of a wolf. Certain every battle would be victory, he dreamed of halting his chariot at Yu's shrine and the Marchmount of the Nine Doubts, enfeoffing Jin's worthies and building guest halls for a conquered court's surrender. He did not see that heaven favors the righteous and cuts down the proud: however mighty he thought himself at Zhuolu, he met the shame of Kunyang. Villains watched for weakness; rebels seized their chance. The Buyao struck first; the Shao Dang tribes finished the ruin. The altars passed to strangers; his body was broken by traitors—a warning to posterity and a jest to the world. What grief! How profound his folly!
38
苻丕承亂僭竊，尋及傾敗，斯可謂天之所廢，人不能支。 苻登集離散之兵，厲死休之志，雖眾寡不敵，難以立功，而義烈慷慨，有足稱矣。
Fu Pi seized a throne amid chaos and soon collapsed—truly what Heaven abandons no man can uphold. Fu Deng rallied broken armies and forged them with a vow to fight to the death. Though outnumbered and unable to win lasting success, his fervor and honor deserve remembrance.
39
贊曰：洪惟壯勇，威棱氐種。 健藉世資，遂雄關、隴。 長生昏虐，敗不旋踵。 永固禎祥，肇自龍驤。 垂旒負扆，竊帝圖王。 患生縱敵，難起矜強。 丕、登僭假，淪胥以亡。
The encomium reads: Fu Hong was bold and fierce; his might awed the Di. Fu Jian drew on that inheritance and made himself master of the pass and Longyou. Fu Sheng ruled in brutish darkness and fell before he could turn around. Fu Jian's good fortune began when he bore the title Dragon Soarer. He took the imperial veil and screen and usurped the throne. Disaster came of sparing a foe; ruin came of boasting his might. Fu Pi and Fu Deng clung to false titles and sank together into oblivion.
