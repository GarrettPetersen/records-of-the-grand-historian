Murong Bao; Murong Sheng; Murong Xi; Murong Yun
2
慕容寶，字道祐，垂之第四子也。 少輕果無志操，好人佞己。 苻堅時為太子洗馬、萬年令。 堅淮肥之役，以寶為陵江將軍。 及為太子，砥礪自修，敦崇儒學，工談論，善屬文，曲事垂左右小臣，以求美譽。 垂之朝士翕然稱之，垂亦以為克保家業，甚賢之。
Murong Bao, style Daoyou, was Murong Chui's fourth son. As a young man he was impetuous and unprincipled, and he surrounded himself with flatterers. Under Fu Jian he held the posts of Crown Prince's attendant and magistrate of Wannian. During Fu Jian's battle at Fei on the Huai, Bao was appointed General Who Crosses the River. Once named heir apparent, he cultivated himself diligently, championed Confucian studies, excelled at debate and prose, and ingratiated himself with the lesser attendants around Murong Chui to win praise. Chui's courtiers united in acclaiming him, and Chui himself believed the house could be preserved in his hands and held him in high esteem.
3
垂死，其年寶嗣偽位，大赦境內，改元為永康。 以其太尉庫辱官偉為太師、左光祿大夫，段崇為太保，其餘拜授各有差。 遵垂遺令，校閱戶口，罷諸軍營分屬郡縣，定士族舊籍，明其官儀，而法峻政嚴，上下離德，百姓思亂者十室而九焉。
The year Chui died, Bao took the throne; he proclaimed a general amnesty and adopted the era name Yongkang. He named Grand Commandant Kulü Guanwei Grand Preceptor and Left Household Counsellor of the First Rank, made Duan Chong Grand Tutor, and distributed the remaining offices as rank dictated. He carried out Chui's last orders—auditing household registers, disbanding military camps into county administration, fixing gentry genealogies, and regulating ceremony—but his laws were harsh and his rule severe; court and people fell out, and rebellion was on nine out of ten minds.
4
初，垂以寶塚嗣未建，每憂之。 寶庶子清河公會多材藝，有雄略，垂深奇之。 及寶之北伐，使會代攝宮事，總錄、禮遇一同太子，所以見定旨也。 垂之伐魏，以龍城舊都，宗廟所在，復使會鎮幽州，委以東北之重，高選僚屬以崇威望。 臨死顧命，以會為寶嗣，而寶寵愛少子濮陽公策，意不在會。 寶庶長子長樂公盛自以同生年長，恥會先之，乃盛稱策宜為儲貳，而非毀會焉。 寶大悅，乃訪其趙王麟、高陽王隆，麟等咸希旨贊成之。 寶遂與麟等定計，立策母段氏為皇后，策為皇太子，盛、會進爵為王。 策字道符，年十一，美姿貌，而蠢弱不慧。
Earlier, because Bao had not yet secured a proper burial site for the heir, Chui had worried constantly. Bao's son by a concubine, Hui, Duke of Qinghe, was gifted and bold in strategy, and Chui admired him greatly. On Bao's northern campaign he left Hui to manage the palace with full prerogatives equal to the heir apparent—a clear sign of intended succession. Chui's campaign against Wei sent Hui back to guard Youzhou, the old capital and home of the ancestral shrines, with the northeast in his charge and elite staff to bolster his authority. Chui's deathbed charge named Hui as Bao's successor, but Bao doted on his youngest son Ce, Duke of Puyang, and had no heart for Hui. Bao's eldest son by a concubine, Sheng, Duke of Changle, born the same year but claiming seniority, resented Hui's precedence; he loudly urged Ce as heir and blackened Hui's name. Delighted, Bao consulted Princes Lin of Zhao and Long of Gaoyang; they read his mood and backed the plan. With Lin and the others Bao fixed the succession: Ce's mother the Duan became empress, Ce crown prince, and Sheng and Hui were raised to princes. Ce, style Daofu, was eleven—handsome of face but slow and witless.
5
魏伐并州，驃騎農逆戰，敗績，還于晉陽，司馬慕輿嵩閉門距之。 農率騎數千奔歸中山，行及潞川，為魏追軍所及，餘騎盡沒，單馬遁還。 寶引群臣於東堂議之。 中山尹苻謨曰：「魏軍強盛，千里轉鬥，乘勝而來，勇氣兼倍，若逸騎平原，形勢彌盛，殆難為敵，宜度險距之。 」中書令晆邃曰：「魏軍多騎，師行剽銳，馬上齎糧，不過旬日。 宜令郡縣聚千家為一堡，深溝高壘，清野待之。 至無所掠，資食無出，不過六旬，自然窮退。 」尚書封懿曰：「今魏師十萬，天下之勍敵也。 百姓雖欲營聚，不足自固，是則聚糧集兵以資強寇，且動眾心，示之以弱，阻關距戰，計之上也。 」慕容麟曰：「魏今乘勝氣銳，其鋒不可當，宜自完守設備，待其弊而乘之。 」於是修城積粟，為持久之備。
Wei struck Bingzhou; Flying Cavalry General Nong was beaten and fled to Jinyang, where Chief Commandant Murong Song barred the gates against him. Nong rode off with a few thousand horsemen toward Zhongshan; at the Lu River Wei pursuers caught him, his escort was destroyed, and he fled alone. Bao gathered his ministers in the eastern hall for counsel. Zhongshan governor Fu Mo argued: "Wei is strong, riding a thousand li of victory; on open ground their light horse will only grow more dangerous. We should fight from defensible ground." Palace Secretary Hui Sui said: "Wei relies on horse—swift columns, grain on the saddle, supplies for barely ten days." "Order every county to mass a thousand households per fort, dig moats, raise walls, and strip the countryside bare." "With nothing to seize and no food to draw on, within sixty days they must withdraw, spent." Minister Feng Yi said: "A hundred thousand Wei soldiers are the empire's hardest enemy." "The people cannot hold improvised forts; clustering them only feeds the enemy, panics the realm, and shows weakness. Better block the passes and fight—that is the superior plan." Murong Lin urged: "Wei's edge is unstoppable; fortify, wait for their decline, then strike." They repaired walls, stockpiled grain, and prepared for a long siege.
6
魏攻中山不克，進據博陵魯口，諸將望風奔退，郡縣悉降于魏，寶聞魏有內難，乃盡眾出距，步卒十二萬，騎三萬七千，次於曲陽柏肆。 魏軍進至新梁。 寶憚魏師之銳，乃遣征北隆夜襲魏軍，敗績而還。 魏軍方軌而至，對營相持，上下兇懼，三軍奪氣。 農、麟勸寶還中山，乃引歸。 魏軍追擊之，寶、農等棄大軍，率騎二萬奔還。 時大風雪，凍死者相枕於道。 寶恐為魏軍所及，命去袍杖戎器，寸刃無返。
Wei failed to take Zhongshan, seized Boling and Lukou, and commanderies fell as generals fled; hearing of turmoil in Wei, Bao marched out with 120,000 foot and 37,000 horse to Quyang and Baixi. The Wei army reached Xinliang. Fearing Wei's edge, Bao sent Long on a night raid; Long was beaten back. Wei advanced in tight order; the camps faced each other; dread spread through the ranks and morale collapsed. Nong and Lin persuaded Bao to retreat to Zhongshan, and he withdrew. Wei pursued; Bao and Nong abandoned the main army and galloped back with twenty thousand horse. A blizzard struck; corpses lay heaped along the road. Fearing capture, Bao ordered cloaks, staffs, and arms discarded—not a blade returned.
7
魏軍進攻中山，屯于芳林園。 其夜尚書慕容皓謀殺寶，立慕容麟。 皓妻兄蘇泥告之，寶使慕容隆收皓，皓與同謀數十人斬關奔魏。 麟懼不自安，以兵劫左衛將軍、北地王精，謀率禁旅弑寶。 精以義距之，麟怒，殺精，出奔丁零。
Wei pressed Zhongshan and camped at Fanglin Garden. That night Minister Murong Hao plotted to kill Bao and enthrone Murong Lin. Su Ni, brother of Hao's wife, betrayed the plot; Long was sent to seize Hao; Hao and dozens of conspirators broke out and fled to Wei. Lin, terrified, seized Left Guard General Jing, Prince of Beidi, intending to lead the palace guard against Bao. Jing resisted on principle; Lin killed him in rage and fled to the Dingling.
8
初，寶聞魏之來伐也，使慕容會率幽、并之眾赴中山，麟既叛，寶恐其逆奪會軍，將遣兵迎之。 麟侍郎段平子自丁零奔還，說麟招集丁零，軍眾甚盛，謀襲會軍，東據龍城。 寶與其太子策及農、隆等萬餘騎迎會於薊，以開封公慕容詳守中山。 會傾身誘納，繕甲厲兵，步騎二萬，列陣而進，迎寶薊南。 寶分其兵給農，隆，遣西河公庫辱官驥率眾三千助守中山。 會以策為太子，有恨色。 寶以告農、隆，俱曰：「會一年少，專任方事，習驕所致，豈有他也。 臣當以禮責之。 」幽平之士皆懷會威德，不樂去之，咸請曰：「清河王天資神武，權略過人，臣等與之誓同生死，感王恩澤，皆勇氣自倍。 願陛下與皇太子、諸王止駕薊宮，使王統臣等進解京師之圍，然後奉迎車駕。 」寶左右皆害其勇略，譖而不許，眾咸有怨言。 左右勸寶殺會，侍御史仇尼歸聞而告會曰：「左右密謀如是，主上將從之。 大王所恃唯父母也，父已異圖； 所杖者兵也，兵已去手，進退路窮，恐無自全之理。 盍誅二王，廢太子，大王自處東宮，兼領將相，以匡社稷。 」會不從。 寶謂農、隆曰：「觀會為變，事當必然，宜早殺之。 不爾，恐成大禍。 」農曰：「寇賊內侮，中州紛亂，會鎮撫舊都，安眾寧境，及京師有難，萬里星赴，威名之重，可以振服戎狄。 又逆跡未彰，宜且隱忍。 今社稷之危若綴旒然，復內相誅戮，有損威望。 」寶曰：「會逆心已成，而王等仁慈，不欲去之，恐一旦釁發，必先害諸父，然後及吾。 事敗之後，當思朕言。 」農等固諫，乃止。 會聞之彌懼，奔于廣都黃榆穀。 會遣仇尼歸等率壯士二千餘人分襲農、隆，隆是夜見殺，農中重創。 既而會歸於寶，寶意在誅會，誘而安之，潛使左衛慕輿騰斬會，不能傷。 會復奔其眾，於是勒兵攻寶。 寶率數百騎馳如龍城，會率眾追之，遣使請誅左右佞臣，並求太子，寶弗許。 會圍龍城，侍御郎高雲夜率敢死士百餘人襲會，敗之，眾悉逃散，單馬奔還中山，乃逾圍而入，為慕容詳所殺。
When Wei invaded, Bao had summoned Hui from You and Bing; after Lin's revolt he feared Hui would be overpowered and prepared to ride out to meet him. Lin's attendant Duan Pingzi escaped the Dingling and reported that Lin was rallying Dingling troops to ambush Hui and seize Longcheng. Bao rode out with Ce, Nong, Long, and ten thousand horse to meet Hui at Ji, leaving Murong Xiang, Duke of Kaifeng, to guard Zhongshan. Hui won men to his side, readied arms, and marched twenty thousand troops south of Ji in battle order to receive Bao. Bao split his force between Nong and Long and sent Kulü Guanji, Duke of Xihe, with three thousand men to reinforce Zhongshan. Learning Ce was crown prince, Hui showed open resentment. Bao confided in Nong and Long; both said Hui was young, sole commander on the frontier, and merely proud from habit—nothing more. "We shall rebuke him by ritual." The soldiers of You and Ping revered Hui and refused to part from him; they petitioned: "Prince Qinghe is heaven-sent in war and stratagem; we have sworn our lives to him and fight with redoubled courage under his grace." "Let Your Majesty, the crown prince, and the princes remain at Ji while the prince leads us to lift the capital's siege; then we shall escort the imperial carriage." Bao's courtiers feared Hui's prowess, slandered him, and refused; the army murmured in discontent. Attendants urged Bao to kill Hui; attendant censor Chou Nigui warned Hui: "The court is plotting your death; the sovereign will consent." "You rely only on your parents; your father already plots against you;" "and on your troops, which are slipping from your grasp. With no road forward or back, you cannot save yourself." "Slay the two princes, depose the heir, take the eastern palace yourself, and hold command—thus you save the realm." Hui refused. Bao told Nong and Long: "Hui's revolt is certain; kill him now." "Or disaster will follow." Nong said: "Enemies press within while the heartland churns; Hui holds the old capital, calms the borders, and when the capital falls he races a thousand li—his name alone can awe the barbarians." "His treason is not yet open; endure awhile." "The state hangs by a thread; fratricide now would shatter our authority." Bao said: "Hui's heart is set, yet you are too merciful to act—I fear when he strikes he will kill your fathers first, then me." "When ruin comes, remember what I said." Nong and the others pressed him hard, and he desisted. Hui, hearing this, fled in terror to Huangyu Valley at Guangdu. Hui sent Chou Nigui with two thousand men against Nong and Long; Long died that night and Nong was badly wounded. Hui then returned to Bao, who meant to kill him, soothed him with false kindness, and secretly ordered Murong Teng of the Left Guard to strike him—but Teng could not wound him. Hui fled back to his troops and led them against Bao. Bao galloped toward Longcheng with a few hundred horse; Hui pursued and demanded the sycophants' heads and the crown prince; Bao refused. Hui besieged Longcheng; Gentleman Gao Yun led a hundred dare-to-die men in a night attack, routed him, and his army scattered; alone he fled to Zhongshan, slipped through the lines, and Murong Xiang killed him.
9
詳僭稱尊號，置百官，改年號。 荒酒奢淫，殺戮無度，誅其王公以下五百餘人，內外震局，莫敢忤視。 城中大饑，公卿餓死者數十人。 麟率丁零之眾入中山，斬詳及其親党三百餘人，復僭稱尊號。 中山饑甚，麟出據新市，與魏師戰於義台，麟軍敗績。 魏師遂人中山，麟乃奔鄴。
Xiang usurped the imperial title, appointed officials, and changed the era name. He drank without restraint, killed without measure, and executed more than five hundred princes and officials; the court and city shook, and none dared meet his gaze. Famine ravaged the city; dozens of nobles and ministers starved. Lin entered Zhongshan with the Dingling, slew Xiang and three hundred kin and partisans, and usurped the throne himself. Zhongshan starved; Lin held Xinshi and fought Wei at Yitai; he was beaten. Wei entered Zhongshan; Lin fled to Ye.
10
慕容德遣侍郎李延勸寶南伐，寶大悅，慕容盛切諫，以為兵疲師老，魏新平中原，宜養兵觀釁，更俟他年。 寶將從之。 撫軍慕輿騰進曰：「今眾旅已集，宜乘新定之機以成進取之功。 人可使由之，而難與圖始，惟當獨決聖慮，不足廣采異同，以沮亂軍議也。 」寶曰：「吾計決矣，敢諫者斬！ 」寶發龍城，以慕輿騰為前軍大司馬，慕容農為中軍，寶為後軍，步騎三萬，次於乙連。 長上段速骨、宋赤眉因眾軍之憚役也，殺司空、樂浪王宙，逼立高陽王崇。 寶單騎奔農，仍引軍討速骨。 眾咸憚征幸亂，投杖奔之。 騰眾亦潰，寶、農馳還龍城。 蘭汗潛與速骨通謀，速骨進師攻城，農為蘭汗所譎，潛出赴賊，為速骨所殺。 眾皆奔散，寶與慕容盛、慕輿騰等南奔。 蘭汗奉太子策承制，遣使迎寶，及於薊城。 寶欲還北，盛等咸以汗之忠款虛實未明，今單馬而還，汗有貳志者，悔之無及。 寶從之，乃自薊而南。 至黎陽，聞慕容德稱制，懼而退。 遣慕輿騰招集散兵于钜鹿，慕容盛結豪桀于冀州，段儀、段溫收部曲于內黃，眾皆響會，克期將集。 會蘭汗遣左將軍蘇超迎寶，寶以汗垂之季舅，盛又汗之壻也，必謂忠款無貳，乃還至龍城。 汗引寶入于外邸，弑之，時年四十四，在位三年，即隆安三年也。 汗又殺其太子策及王公卿士百餘人。 汗自稱大都督、大將軍、大單于、昌黎王。 盛僭位，偽諡寶惠湣皇帝，廟號烈宗。
Murong De's envoy Li Yan urged a southern campaign; Bao was delighted; Murong Sheng urgently argued that the army was spent, Wei had just pacified the north, and they should rest, watch, and wait. Bao was about to agree. Pacifying General Murong Teng pressed him: "The hosts are assembled; seize this moment and strike." "Soldiers will follow once you move, but they cannot plan the start with you; decide alone and do not let debate disorder the council." Bao said: "My mind is made; whoever dissents dies!" Bao marched from Longcheng: Murong Teng led the van as Grand Marshal, Murong Nong the center, Bao the rear—thirty thousand troops—encamping at Yilian. Senior officer Duan Sugu and Song Chimei, the army loathing forced labor, killed Minister of Works Zhou, Prince of Lelang, and forced Prince Chong of Gaoyang onto the throne. Bao rode alone to Nong and turned to punish Sugu. The troops feared the march and court chaos; they threw down arms and joined the mutineers. Teng's force dissolved; Bao and Nong raced back to Longcheng. Lan Han plotted with Sugu; Sugu besieged the city; Nong, tricked by Lan Han, slipped out to the rebels and was killed. The army scattered; Bao fled south with Murong Sheng and Murong Teng. Lan Han installed Ce as regent, sent envoys to welcome Bao, and met him at Ji. Bao meant to return north; Sheng warned that Han's loyalty was unproved—if he rode back alone and Han turned, regret would come too late. Bao agreed and marched south from Ji. At Liyang he learned Murong De held power and retreated in fear. He sent Teng to rally stragglers at Julu, Sheng to win the bold in Jizhou, Duan Yi and Duan Wen to gather bands at Neihuang; men answered from every side and a day was set to rise. Lan Han sent Left General Su Chao to welcome him; Bao, reckoning Han Chui's maternal uncle and Sheng his son-in-law, trusted him and returned to Longcheng. Han brought Bao to the outer lodge and killed him; he was forty-four, had reigned three years—the third year of Longan. Han also slew crown prince Ce and more than a hundred princes and ministers. Han styled himself Grand Commander, Grand General, Grand Chanyu, and King of Changli. Sheng seized the throne and posthumously styled Bao Emperor Hui Min, temple name Liezong.
11
皝之遷于龍城也，植松為社主。 及秦滅燕，大風吹拔之。 後數年，社處忽有桑二根生焉。 先是，遼川無桑，及廆通于晉，求種江南，平州桑悉由吳來。 廆終而垂以吳王中興，寶之將敗，大風又拔其一。
When Murong Huang moved the capital to Longcheng, he planted pines as the spirit of the soil altar. When Qin destroyed Yan, a great wind tore them up. Years later, two mulberry shoots sprang up at the altar site. Liaochuan had had no mulberry until Huang sought seed from the Jiangnan through Jin; Pingzhou's mulberries all came from Wu. Huang died and Chui styled himself Prince of Wu in restoration; as Bao neared ruin, the great wind uprooted one mulberry again.
12
盛字道運，寶之庶長子也。 少沈敏，多謀略。 苻堅誅慕容氏，盛潛奔於沖。 及沖稱尊號，有自得之志，賞罰不均，政令不明。 盛年十二，謂叔父柔曰：「今中山王智不先眾，才不出下，恩未施人，先自驕大，以盛觀之，鮮不覆敗。 」俄而沖為段木延所殺，盛隨慕容永東如長子，謂柔曰：「今崎嶇於鋒刃之間，在疑忌之際，愚則為人所猜，智則危甚巢幕，當如鴻鵠高飛，一舉萬里，不可坐待罟網也。 」於是與柔及弟會間行東歸於慕容垂。 遇盜陝中，盛曰：「我六尺之軀，入水不溺，在火不焦，汝欲當吾鋒乎！ 試豎爾手中箭百步，我若中之，宜慎爾命，如其不中，當束身相授。 」盜用豎箭，盛一發中之。 盜曰：「郎貴人之子，故相試耳。 」資而遣之。 歲餘，永誅俊、垂之子孫，男女無遺。 盛既至，垂問以西事，畫地成圖。 垂笑曰：「昔魏武撫明帝之首，遂乃侯之，祖之愛孫，有自來矣。 」於是封長樂公。 驍勇剛毅，有伯父全之風烈。
Sheng, style Daoyun, was Bao's eldest son by a concubine. As a youth he was reserved and sharp, full of stratagems. When Fu Jian slaughtered the Murong house, Sheng fled secretly to Murong Chong. When Chong took the throne he grew arrogant; rewards and punishments were arbitrary and his orders muddled. Sheng laughed: "Minister Ding is young—how does he speak like an elder?" Sheng ruled with stern awe, kept few intimates, and suspected much—hence Xin's words.
24

Left General Murong Guo, Palace Guard Qin Yu, Duan Zan, and others plotted to strike Sheng with the guard; the plot was found; more than five hundred were executed. Forward General Duan Ji, Marquis of Reflecting Repentance, with Xing and Tai, stirred the guard by night with drums and shouts. Sheng heard, led his guard out to fight; the mob scattered. A rebel struck from the dark and wounded him; he was carried to the front hall, tightened the guard, and summoned his uncle Xi, Duke of Hejian, to entrust affairs. Xi had not arrived when Sheng died; he was twenty-nine and had reigned three years. He received the usurped posthumous title Emperor Zhaowu, tomb Xingping Mausoleum, temple name Zhongzong.
25

Sheng in youth was adrift in hardship; grown, he met endless calamity; he tasted every turn of fortune. Correcting Bao's darkness and indecision, he sharpened mechanisms and punishments; the slightest suspicion was cut in the bud. Court and realm trembled; even loyal kin turned away; old ministers were destroyed; his cruelty without kin brought unavoidable ruin. That year was the fifth year of Longan.

Yuan, style Daoguang, was Bao's fourth son. He amnestied below capital crime, changed the era to Guangshi, renamed the Northern Yan Terrace the Grand Chanyu Terrace, and set Left and Right Assistants below the Ministers.

30

Xi hated it, felled the tree, and a serpent more than a zhang long crawled out.

37

Army Commandant Du Jing brought a coffin to court and memorialized with utmost remonstrance. Xi in rage beheaded him. Lady Fu wanted chilled fish in summer and fresh rehmannia in winter; offices that failed faced death—such was her cruelty. When Lady Fu died, Xi wailed like a son for parents, embraced the corpse and said: "The body is cold; life is cut off!" He stiffened and ceased breathing; after long he revived. After the great encoffining he opened the coffin again and coupled with her. He wore deepest mourning hemp and ate gruel. He ordered the hundred officials to mourn in the palace and monks to wear plain dress. Offices inspected mourners: tears meant loyalty; none meant punishment; ministers trembled and squeezed out bitter tears. Murong Long's wife Zhang, Xi's sister-in-law, was beautiful and clever. Xi meant her to follow Lady Fu in death; he ruined her funeral boots, found worn felt inside, and had her executed. Her three daughters begged; Xi refused. He ordered every household from nobles to commoners to build tombs, draining the treasury. He sank three springs, encircled several li, and painted the eight Masters of Writing within. Xi said: "Do it well—I shall enter this tomb after." Observers called it ill-omened. Right Counsellor Wei Qiu and others feared sacrifice, bathed, and awaited death. He named Lady Fu's tomb Zhengping Mausoleum. Xi walked the funeral with loose hair and bare feet. The bier carriage was huge; they broke the north gate to pass it. Elders whispered: "The Murong destroy their own gate—it will not be long."
38

Zhao Luosheng reported; Xi said: "Rat bandits—I shall return and kill them." He gathered armor and galloped back. By night he reached Longcheng, failed at the north gate, fled to Dragon Soaring Park, hid in plain dress, was seized; Yun killed him and buried him with his sons north of the city. He was twenty-three and had reigned six years. Yun buried him in Lady Fu's tomb with the usurped posthumous title Emperor Zhaowen.
39

40

Though Wey forgot ruin yet passed eastward, and Duke Mu had surplus yet shamed the western neighbor—truly Fu's rebels, not Jin's whale.

寶以浮譽獲升，峻文禦俗，蕭牆內憤，勍敵外陵，雖毒不被物而惡足自剿。 盛則孝友冥符，文武不墜，韜光而夷仇賊，罪己而遜高危，翩翩然濁世之佳虜矣。 熙乃地非奧主，舉因淫德。 驪戎之態，取悅于匡床； 玄妻之姿，見奇於鬒發。 蕩輕舟于曲光之海，望朝涉于景雲之山，飾土木於驕心，窮怨嗟於蕞壤，宗祀夷滅，為馮氏之驅除焉。
Bao rose on empty fame, ruled with harsh laws; fury within, foes without—though poison did not touch all, evil destroyed itself. Sheng had filial piety and brotherhood, civil and martial unbroken; he hid light, humbled foes, blamed himself and yielded peril—a graceful captive of a turbid age. Xi was no lord of deep land; he rose entirely through dissolute virtue. Li Rong's manner pleased on the couch; Xuan Qi's beauty was marvelled at in black hair. He floated on the Curving Light Sea, gazed from Jingyun Mountain, adorned earth and wood for pride, exhausted the realm in lament—ancestral rites perished and he became Feng's cleanser.

贊曰：戎狄憑陵，山川沸騰。 天未悔禍，人非與能。 疾走而捷，先鳴則興。 道明烈烈，鞭笞豪傑。 掃燕夷魏，釗屠永滅。 大盜潛移，鴻名遂竊。 寶心生亂，盛清家難。 熙極驕淫，人懷憤惋。 孽貽身咎，災無以逭。
The encomium says: Barbarians relied on force; mountains and rivers boiled. Heaven had not yet repented; men were not fit to succeed. Run fast and strike quick; he who calls first rises. Daoming blazing, whipping heroes. Sweeping Yan, leveling Wei, cutting down forever. The great robber moved in secret; the great name was stolen. Bao's heart bred disorder; Sheng cleared the house's peril. Xi reached utmost pride and lust; men harbored resentment. Crime bequeathed bodily blame; disaster could not be escaped.
