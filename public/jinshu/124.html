Murong Bao; Murong Sheng; Murong Xi; Murong Yun
2
慕容寶，字道祐，垂之第四子也。 少輕果無志操，好人佞己。 苻堅時為太子洗馬、萬年令。 堅淮肥之役，以寶為陵江將軍。 及為太子，砥礪自修，敦崇儒學，工談論，善屬文，曲事垂左右小臣，以求美譽。 垂之朝士翕然稱之，垂亦以為克保家業，甚賢之。
Murong Bao, whose courtesy name was Daoyou, was the fourth son of Murong Chui. As a youth he was reckless and impulsive, lacking firm purpose, and he delighted in sycophants who played to his vanity. Under Fu Jian he held the posts of Groom of the Heir Apparent's Household and Magistrate of Wannian. During Fu Jian's campaign on the Huai and Fei, Bao was appointed General Who Crosses the River. Once made heir apparent, he applied himself to self-improvement, earnestly promoted Confucian learning, was adept at debate and polished at composition, and assiduously courted the minor officials around Murong Chui in hope of a glowing reputation. Chui's courtiers united in praising him, and Chui himself believed he would safeguard the family legacy and held him in high regard.
3
垂死，其年寶嗣偽位，大赦境內，改元為永康。 以其太尉庫辱官偉為太師、左光祿大夫，段崇為太保，其餘拜授各有差。 遵垂遺令，校閱戶口，罷諸軍營分屬郡縣，定士族舊籍，明其官儀，而法峻政嚴，上下離德，百姓思亂者十室而九焉。
The year Murong Chui died, Bao took the throne in his stead, declared a general amnesty throughout the realm, and adopted the reign title Yongkang. He appointed his Grand Commandant Kurou Guan Wei Grand Preceptor and Left Household Counselor of the Imperial Clan, made Duan Chong Grand Mentor, and assigned the remaining offices at various ranks. In accordance with Chui's last testament he audited household registers, dissolved the military encampments and reassigned their troops to local counties, restored the old gentry registers and clarified official protocol — yet his laws were harsh and his rule severe; court and people alike fell out of accord, and rebellion was on the minds of nine households in ten.
4
初，垂以寶塚嗣未建，每憂之。 寶庶子清河公會多材藝，有雄略，垂深奇之。 及寶之北伐，使會代攝宮事，總錄、禮遇一同太子，所以見定旨也。 垂之伐魏，以龍城舊都，宗廟所在，復使會鎮幽州，委以東北之重，高選僚屬以崇威望。 臨死顧命，以會為寶嗣，而寶寵愛少子濮陽公策，意不在會。 寶庶長子長樂公盛自以同生年長，恥會先之，乃盛稱策宜為儲貳，而非毀會焉。 寶大悅，乃訪其趙王麟、高陽王隆，麟等咸希旨贊成之。 寶遂與麟等定計，立策母段氏為皇后，策為皇太子，盛、會進爵為王。 策字道符，年十一，美姿貌，而蠢弱不慧。
Earlier, Murong Chui had been deeply troubled because Bao had not yet named an heir of the main line. Bao's son by a concubine, the Duke of Qinghe Murong Hui, was accomplished in many fields and possessed bold strategic vision; Chui regarded him with exceptional admiration. When Bao led a northern campaign, he had Hui govern the palace in his absence; Hui's authority and ceremonial standing matched those of the heir apparent, making plain that he was the intended successor. When Chui campaigned against Wei, he again stationed Hui in You Province to guard Longcheng, the old capital and seat of the ancestral shrines, entrusting him with the security of the northeast and appointing eminent officials to bolster his authority. On his deathbed Chui named Hui as Bao's successor, but Bao favored his youngest son, the Duke of Puyang Murong Ce, and had no intention of following that wish. Bao's eldest son by a concubine, the Duke of Changle Murong Sheng, resented that Hui, though younger, had been placed ahead of him; he loudly advocated making Ce heir and spoke ill of Hui at every turn. Bao was delighted and consulted the Prince of Zhao Murong Lin and the Prince of Gaoyang Murong Long; Lin and the others all read his wishes and endorsed the plan. Bao then settled the matter with Lin and the others: he made Ce's mother, Lady Duan, empress, named Ce crown prince, and raised Sheng and Hui to princely rank. Ce, whose courtesy name was Daofu, was eleven years old and handsome in bearing, but slow-witted and without discernment.
5
魏伐并州，驃騎農逆戰，敗績，還于晉陽，司馬慕輿嵩閉門距之。 農率騎數千奔歸中山，行及潞川，為魏追軍所及，餘騎盡沒，單馬遁還。 寶引群臣於東堂議之。 中山尹苻謨曰：「魏軍強盛，千里轉鬥，乘勝而來，勇氣兼倍，若逸騎平原，形勢彌盛，殆難為敵，宜度險距之。 」中書令晆邃曰：「魏軍多騎，師行剽銳，馬上齎糧，不過旬日。 宜令郡縣聚千家為一堡，深溝高壘，清野待之。 至無所掠，資食無出，不過六旬，自然窮退。 」尚書封懿曰：「今魏師十萬，天下之勍敵也。 百姓雖欲營聚，不足自固，是則聚糧集兵以資強寇，且動眾心，示之以弱，阻關距戰，計之上也。 」慕容麟曰：「魏今乘勝氣銳，其鋒不可當，宜自完守設備，待其弊而乘之。 」於是修城積粟，為持久之備。
When Wei invaded Bing Province, the Cavalry General Murong Nong met them in battle, suffered defeat, and withdrew to Jinyang, where the Major Murong Song shut the gates against him. Nong fled with several thousand horsemen toward Zhongshan; at the Lu River he was overtaken by Wei pursuers, his remaining cavalry were wiped out, and he escaped alone on horseback. Bao gathered his ministers in the Eastern Hall to deliberate. The Governor of Zhongshan, Fu Mo, said: "The Wei forces are formidable. Having fought a thousand li from home, they arrive flushed with victory and doubly bold. If we let their cavalry loose on open ground their advantage will only grow, and they will be nearly impossible to defeat. We should take up a defensible position in difficult country and hold them there." " The Director of the Secretariat, Fei Sui, said: "The Wei army is mostly cavalry; their columns move with swift ferocity and carry provisions on horseback — they cannot sustain themselves for more than ten days." We should order the commanderies and counties to gather a thousand households into each fortified settlement, dig deep ditches and raise high ramparts, and clear the countryside to await them. Once there is nothing left to plunder and no supplies to be had, within sixty days they will withdraw of their own accord, exhausted. " The Master of Writing Feng Yi said: "The Wei host now numbers a hundred thousand men — the fiercest foe in the realm." Even if the people wish to gather in fortified settlements, they cannot hold them; doing so would only stockpile grain and troops for a powerful enemy, unsettle the populace, and advertise weakness. Blocking the passes and meeting them in battle at the frontier is the better plan." " Murong Lin said: "Wei now rides the momentum of victory with keen morale; their edge cannot be met head-on. We should fortify our defenses and wait until they weaken, then strike." " They then repaired the walls, stockpiled grain, and made ready for a prolonged defense.
6
魏攻中山不克，進據博陵魯口，諸將望風奔退，郡縣悉降于魏，寶聞魏有內難，乃盡眾出距，步卒十二萬，騎三萬七千，次於曲陽柏肆。 魏軍進至新梁。 寶憚魏師之銳，乃遣征北隆夜襲魏軍，敗績而還。 魏軍方軌而至，對營相持，上下兇懼，三軍奪氣。 農、麟勸寶還中山，乃引歸。 魏軍追擊之，寶、農等棄大軍，率騎二萬奔還。 時大風雪，凍死者相枕於道。 寶恐為魏軍所及，命去袍杖戎器，寸刃無返。
Wei besieged Zhongshan without success, then advanced to occupy Boling Lukou; commanders fled at the mere rumor of their approach, and commanderies and counties surrendered to Wei in succession. Hearing that Wei faced internal troubles, Bao marched out with his full strength to resist: a hundred and twenty thousand infantry and thirty-seven thousand cavalry, encamped at Qufu and Bosi. The Wei army advanced to Xinliang. Fearing the Wei army's edge, Bao sent the Northern Expeditionary Murong Long to raid the Wei camp by night; Long was defeated and driven back. The Wei army advanced in tight formation; the two camps faced each other. Dread spread through every rank, and the army's fighting spirit collapsed. Nong and Lin urged Bao to withdraw to Zhongshan, and he turned the army homeward. The Wei army gave chase; Bao, Nong, and the others abandoned the main body and fled homeward with twenty thousand horsemen. A blizzard struck; the frozen dead lay heaped along the road. Fearing capture by the Wei pursuers, Bao ordered robes, staffs, and weapons thrown away — not a blade was to be carried back.
7
魏軍進攻中山，屯于芳林園。 其夜尚書慕容皓謀殺寶，立慕容麟。 皓妻兄蘇泥告之，寶使慕容隆收皓，皓與同謀數十人斬關奔魏。 麟懼不自安，以兵劫左衛將軍、北地王精，謀率禁旅弑寶。 精以義距之，麟怒，殺精，出奔丁零。
The Wei army advanced against Zhongshan and encamped at Fanglin Garden. That night the Master of Writing Murong Hao plotted to kill Bao and install Murong Lin on the throne. Hao's brother-in-law Su Ni exposed the plot; Bao sent Murong Long to arrest Hao, but Hao and several dozen conspirators cut through the gate and fled to Wei. Lin, fearing for his own safety, seized the Left Guard General, Prince of Beidi Murong Jing, and plotted to lead the palace guard in assassinating Bao. Jing resisted on grounds of loyalty; Lin in fury killed him and fled to the Dingling.
8
初，寶聞魏之來伐也，使慕容會率幽、并之眾赴中山，麟既叛，寶恐其逆奪會軍，將遣兵迎之。 麟侍郎段平子自丁零奔還，說麟招集丁零，軍眾甚盛，謀襲會軍，東據龍城。 寶與其太子策及農、隆等萬餘騎迎會於薊，以開封公慕容詳守中山。 會傾身誘納，繕甲厲兵，步騎二萬，列陣而進，迎寶薊南。 寶分其兵給農，隆，遣西河公庫辱官驥率眾三千助守中山。 會以策為太子，有恨色。 寶以告農、隆，俱曰：「會一年少，專任方事，習驕所致，豈有他也。 臣當以禮責之。 」幽平之士皆懷會威德，不樂去之，咸請曰：「清河王天資神武，權略過人，臣等與之誓同生死，感王恩澤，皆勇氣自倍。 願陛下與皇太子、諸王止駕薊宮，使王統臣等進解京師之圍，然後奉迎車駕。 」寶左右皆害其勇略，譖而不許，眾咸有怨言。 左右勸寶殺會，侍御史仇尼歸聞而告會曰：「左右密謀如是，主上將從之。 大王所恃唯父母也，父已異圖； 所杖者兵也，兵已去手，進退路窮，恐無自全之理。 盍誅二王，廢太子，大王自處東宮，兼領將相，以匡社稷。 」會不從。 寶謂農、隆曰：「觀會為變，事當必然，宜早殺之。 不爾，恐成大禍。 」農曰：「寇賊內侮，中州紛亂，會鎮撫舊都，安眾寧境，及京師有難，萬里星赴，威名之重，可以振服戎狄。 又逆跡未彰，宜且隱忍。 今社稷之危若綴旒然，復內相誅戮，有損威望。 」寶曰：「會逆心已成，而王等仁慈，不欲去之，恐一旦釁發，必先害諸父，然後及吾。 事敗之後，當思朕言。 」農等固諫，乃止。 會聞之彌懼，奔于廣都黃榆穀。 會遣仇尼歸等率壯士二千餘人分襲農、隆，隆是夜見殺，農中重創。 既而會歸於寶，寶意在誅會，誘而安之，潛使左衛慕輿騰斬會，不能傷。 會復奔其眾，於是勒兵攻寶。 寶率數百騎馳如龍城，會率眾追之，遣使請誅左右佞臣，並求太子，寶弗許。 會圍龍城，侍御郎高雲夜率敢死士百餘人襲會，敗之，眾悉逃散，單馬奔還中山，乃逾圍而入，為慕容詳所殺。
When Bao first learned of Wei's invasion, he had ordered Murong Hui to lead the armies of You and Bing to Zhongshan. After Lin's rebellion, Bao feared Hui might seize those troops in revolt and prepared to send forces to intercept him. Lin's attendant Duan Pingzi fled back from the Dingling and reported that Lin was rallying Dingling forces in great numbers, intending to strike Hui's army and seize Longcheng in the east. Bao rode out with more than ten thousand horsemen — the crown prince Ce, Nong, Long, and others — to meet Hui at Ji, leaving the Duke of Kaifeng Murong Xiang to defend Zhongshan. Hui worked tirelessly to win loyalty, repaired armor and sharpened weapons, and marched south of Ji with twenty thousand infantry and cavalry in battle order to receive Bao. Bao divided his forces between Nong and Long and sent the Duke of Xihe Kurou Guan Ji with three thousand men to reinforce the defense of Zhongshan. When Hui learned that Ce had been made crown prince, resentment showed plainly on his face. Bao told Nong and Long; both said: "Hui is still young and holds sole command on the frontier; this is merely the arrogance of office — there is surely nothing more to it. We shall take him to task in the proper ceremonial manner." " The officers of You and Ping all revered Hui's authority and virtue and were loath to part from him; they petitioned as one: "The Prince of Qinghe possesses Heaven-given martial genius and strategic gifts beyond ordinary men; we have sworn to live and die with him, and stirred by Your Majesty's gracious favor our courage is redoubled." We beg Your Majesty, together with the crown prince and the princes, to remain at the Ji palace while the prince leads us to raise the siege of the capital; afterward we shall escort the imperial carriage home." " Bao's attendants feared Hui's courage and strategic gifts; they slandered him until the request was denied, and discontent spread through the ranks. Bao's attendants urged him to kill Hui; the Attending Censor Chou Nigui heard of it and warned Hui: "The attendants are secretly plotting along these lines, and the sovereign is about to agree." What you rely on, great prince, is only your parents — and your father already harbors other designs; what you lean on is your army, and that army has already slipped from your grasp. With advance and retreat alike cut off, I fear there is no path to safety. Why not kill the two princes, depose the crown prince, and install yourself in the Eastern Palace while also taking command as general and minister — thereby setting the state aright?" Hui refused. Bao told Nong and Long: "Judging by Hui's conduct, rebellion is inevitable; he should be killed at once." Otherwise I fear a catastrophe." " Nong said: "Enemies press us from within while the heartland is in turmoil. Hui pacifies the old capital, settles the people, and secures the borders; when the capital faces peril he hurries from afar like a shooting star — the weight of his renown alone can awe the northern tribes into submission." Moreover his treason is not yet open; for now we should endure in silence." The peril to the altars of state now hangs by a thread; to turn on one another within would only sap our authority." Bao said: "Hui's rebellious intent is already fixed, yet you are too merciful to remove him. I fear that once trouble breaks out he will strike my uncles first and come for me afterward." When disaster comes, you will remember what I said today." " Nong and the others pressed their remonstrance until he relented. When Hui learned of this he grew still more fearful and fled to Huangyu Valley in Guangdu. Hui sent Chou Nigui and others with more than two thousand stalwarts to strike Nong and Long in separate attacks; Long was killed that night and Nong was gravely wounded. Hui then returned to Bao. Bao meant to kill him, lulled him with reassurances, and secretly ordered the Left Guard Murong Teng to strike him down — but failed to wound him. Hui fled again to his troops and thereupon marshaled his forces to attack Bao. Bao fled to Longcheng with several hundred horsemen; Hui pursued with his host and sent envoys demanding the execution of the sycophantic attendants and the surrender of the crown prince — Bao refused both. Hui besieged Longcheng; the Attendant Gentleman Gao Yun led more than a hundred dare-to-die men in a night attack, routed him, and his army scattered; Hui alone fled on horseback to Zhongshan, slipped through the siege lines, and was killed by Murong Xiang.
9
詳僭稱尊號，置百官，改年號。 荒酒奢淫，殺戮無度，誅其王公以下五百餘人，內外震局，莫敢忤視。 城中大饑，公卿餓死者數十人。 麟率丁零之眾入中山，斬詳及其親党三百餘人，復僭稱尊號。 中山饑甚，麟出據新市，與魏師戰於義台，麟軍敗績。 魏師遂人中山，麟乃奔鄴。
Xiang presumptuously took the imperial title, established the full bureaucracy, and changed the reign title. He drowned himself in wine and debauchery, killing without restraint; he executed more than five hundred princes and officials down to the common ranks, and the court and realm alike trembled — none dared meet his gaze. Famine gripped the city; dozens of nobles and high officials starved to death. Lin led the Dingling into Zhongshan, executed Xiang and more than three hundred of his kin and partisans, and again presumptuously took the imperial title. Famine in Zhongshan was extreme; Lin marched out to occupy Xinshi and fought the Wei army at Yitai, where his forces were routed. The Wei army then entered Zhongshan; Lin fled to Ye.
10
慕容德遣侍郎李延勸寶南伐，寶大悅，慕容盛切諫，以為兵疲師老，魏新平中原，宜養兵觀釁，更俟他年。 寶將從之。 撫軍慕輿騰進曰：「今眾旅已集，宜乘新定之機以成進取之功。 人可使由之，而難與圖始，惟當獨決聖慮，不足廣采異同，以沮亂軍議也。 」寶曰：「吾計決矣，敢諫者斬！ 」寶發龍城，以慕輿騰為前軍大司馬，慕容農為中軍，寶為後軍，步騎三萬，次於乙連。 長上段速骨、宋赤眉因眾軍之憚役也，殺司空、樂浪王宙，逼立高陽王崇。 寶單騎奔農，仍引軍討速骨。 眾咸憚征幸亂，投杖奔之。 騰眾亦潰，寶、農馳還龍城。 蘭汗潛與速骨通謀，速骨進師攻城，農為蘭汗所譎，潛出赴賊，為速骨所殺。 眾皆奔散，寶與慕容盛、慕輿騰等南奔。 蘭汗奉太子策承制，遣使迎寶，及於薊城。 寶欲還北，盛等咸以汗之忠款虛實未明，今單馬而還，汗有貳志者，悔之無及。 寶從之，乃自薊而南。 至黎陽，聞慕容德稱制，懼而退。 遣慕輿騰招集散兵于钜鹿，慕容盛結豪桀于冀州，段儀、段溫收部曲于內黃，眾皆響會，克期將集。 會蘭汗遣左將軍蘇超迎寶，寶以汗垂之季舅，盛又汗之壻也，必謂忠款無貳，乃還至龍城。 汗引寶入于外邸，弑之，時年四十四，在位三年，即隆安三年也。 汗又殺其太子策及王公卿士百餘人。 汗自稱大都督、大將軍、大單于、昌黎王。 盛僭位，偽諡寶惠湣皇帝，廟號烈宗。
Murong De sent the Attendant Gentleman Li Yan to urge Bao to march south; Bao was delighted. Murong Sheng remonstrated earnestly, arguing that the army was exhausted, Wei had newly pacified the central plain, and they should rest their forces, watch for an opening, and wait for another year. Bao was about to heed him. The Adjutant Murong Teng stepped forward and said: "The army is already assembled; we should seize this moment of fresh consolidation to win the merit of advance." Men may be led along afterward, but it is hard to take them into counsel at the outset; Your Majesty should decide alone and not gather too many dissenting views, lest the army's deliberations be thrown into confusion." Bao said: "My mind is made up; whoever dares remonstrate will be beheaded!" Bao marched from Longcheng with Murong Teng as Grand Marshal of the vanguard, Murong Nong commanding the center, and himself in the rear — thirty thousand infantry and cavalry — encamped at Yilian. The senior officer Duan Sugu and Song Chimei, exploiting the troops' fear of forced labor, killed the Minister of Works, Prince of Lelang Murong Zhou, and compelled the Prince of Gaoyang Murong Chong to accept the throne. Bao fled alone to Nong and then led troops against Sugu. The troops feared both the campaign and the turmoil; they threw down their weapons and fled to the rebels. Teng's force also collapsed; Bao and Nong galloped back to Longcheng. Lan Han secretly conspired with Sugu; Sugu advanced to besiege the city. Nong was deceived by Lan Han, slipped out secretly to join the rebels, and was killed by Sugu. The army scattered; Bao fled south with Murong Sheng, Murong Teng, and others. Lan Han, acting in the crown prince's name, sent envoys to welcome Bao; they met him at Ji. Bao wished to return north; Sheng and the others argued that Han's loyalty was still unproved — if Bao rode back alone and Han harbored treachery, the mistake could not be undone. Bao took their counsel and turned south from Ji. At Liyang he learned that Murong De had assumed independent authority and, alarmed, withdrew. He sent Murong Teng to rally scattered troops at Julu; Murong Sheng gathered bold men in Ji Province; Duan Yi and Duan Wen mustered their followings at Neihuang — all answered the call and fixed a date to assemble. Then Lan Han sent the Left General Su Chao to welcome Bao; Bao, considering Han Chui's youngest maternal uncle and Sheng Han's son-in-law, judged his loyalty secure and returned to Longcheng. Han led Bao into the outer residence and assassinated him; he was forty-four and had reigned three years — the third year of the Long'an era. Han also killed the crown prince Ce and more than a hundred princes, dukes, and ministers. Han styled himself Grand Commander, Grand General, Grand Chanyu, and King of Changli. Sheng took the throne and posthumously titled Bao Emperor Hui Min, with the temple name Lie Zong.

皝之遷于龍城也，植松為社主。 及秦滅燕，大風吹拔之。 後數年，社處忽有桑二根生焉。 先是，遼川無桑，及廆通于晉，求種江南，平州桑悉由吳來。 廆終而垂以吳王中興，寶之將敗，大風又拔其一。

Sheng, whose courtesy name was Daoyun, was Bao's eldest son by a concubine. As a youth he was reserved and sharp, possessed of many stratagems. When Fu Jian executed the Murong clan, Sheng secretly fled to Murong Chong. When Chong took the imperial title he grew complacent; rewards and punishments were uneven and his orders unclear. At twelve Sheng told his uncle Rou: "The Prince of Zhongshan is neither wiser than the crowd nor more talented than his subordinates, yet he has shown no kindness to others and already thinks himself grand — in my view he will hardly escape ruin." " Soon Chong was killed by Duan Muyan; Sheng followed Murong Yong east to Changzi and told Rou: "We now walk a knife-edge amid suspicion — play the fool and men will suspect us, play the wise and we are birds in a burning nest. We must rise like wild swans and fly ten thousand li at a stroke, not sit waiting for the net." " He then traveled secretly east with Rou and his younger brother Hui to join Murong Chui. They met bandits in the Shaan gorge; Sheng said: "This body of mine does not drown in water or burn in fire — do you wish to test my blade?" Plant your arrow at a hundred paces; if I hit it, spare your life; if I miss, I will bind myself and surrender. " The bandit raised his arrow; Sheng struck it with a single shot. The bandit said: "Young lord, you are a nobleman's son — I was only testing you." " He gave them provisions and sent them on their way. More than a year later Yong executed Murong Jun and all of Chui's descendants, men and women alike, without exception. When Sheng arrived, Chui questioned him about affairs in the west and he traced a map on the ground. Chui smiled and said: "Long ago Cao Cao stroked Emperor Ming's head and thereafter enfeoffed him — a grandfather's love for a grandson is an old story." " He thereupon enfeoffed him as Duke of Changle. Fierce, resolute, and bold, he bore the martial spirit of his uncle Murong Quan.

寶即偽位，進爵為王。 寶自龍城南伐，盛留統後事，及段速骨作亂，馳出迎衛。 寶幾為速骨所獲，賴盛以免。 盛屢進奇策於寶，寶不能從，是以屢敗。 寶既如龍城，盛留在後。 寶為蘭汗所殺，盛馳進赴哀，將軍張真固諫以為不可，盛曰：「我今投命，告以哀窮。 汗性愚近，必顧念婚姻，不忍害我。 旬月之間，足展吾志。 」遂人赴喪。 汗妻乙氏泣涕請盛，汗亦哀之，遣其子穆迎盛，舍之宮內，親敬如舊。 汗兄提、弟難勸汗殺盛，汗不從。 慕容奇，汗之外孫也，汗亦宥之。 奇入見盛，遂相與謀。 盛遣奇起兵於外，眾至數千。 汗遣蘭提討奇。 提驕很淫荒，事汗無禮，盛因間之於汗曰：「奇，小兒也，未能辦此，必內有應之者。 提素驕，不可委以大眾。 」汗因發怒，收提誅之，遣其撫軍仇尼慕率眾討奇。 汗兄弟見提之誅，莫不危懼，皆阻兵背汗，襲敗慕軍。 汗大懼，遣其子穆率眾討之。 穆謂汗曰：「慕容盛，我之仇也。 奇今起逆，盛必應之。 兼內有蕭牆之難，不宜養心腹之疾。 」汗將誅盛，引見察之。 盛妻以告，於是偽稱疾篤，不復出入，汗乃止。 有李旱、衛雙、劉志、張豪、張真者，皆盛之舊昵，蘭穆引為腹心。 旱等屢入見盛，潛結大謀。 會穆討蘭難等斬之，大饗將士，汗、穆皆醉。 盛夜因如廁，袒而逾牆，入於東宮，與李旱等誅穆，眾皆踴呼，進攻汗，斬之。 汗二子魯公和、陳公楊分屯令支、白狼，遣李旱、張真襲誅之。 於是內外怗然，士女咸悅，盛謙揖自卑，不稱尊號。 其年，以長樂王稱制，赦其境內，改元曰建平。 諸王降爵為公，文武各復舊位。
When Bao took the throne, Sheng was raised to princely rank. When Bao marched south from Longcheng, Sheng remained to manage affairs at home; when Duan Sugu rebelled, he galloped out to protect him. Bao was nearly captured by Sugu and escaped only thanks to Sheng. Sheng repeatedly offered bold stratagems to Bao, but Bao would not heed them and so suffered defeat after defeat. After Bao reached Longcheng, Sheng stayed behind. When Bao was killed by Lan Han, Sheng hurried forward to mourn; the general Zhang Zhengu urged him not to go, but Sheng said: "I now throw myself on their mercy and plead our grief and destitution." Han is dull and shortsighted; he will surely remember the marriage tie and be unwilling to harm me. Within a month I shall have room enough to carry out my design. " He then went in to attend the funeral. Han's wife Lady Yi wept and pleaded for Sheng; Han pitied him too, sent his son Mu to welcome him, lodged him in the palace, and treated him with the same intimate respect as before. Han's elder brother Ti and younger brother Nan urged Han to kill Sheng, but Han refused. Murong Qi was Han's grandson by a daughter; Han spared him as well. Qi came to see Sheng, and together they laid their plans. Sheng sent Qi to raise troops abroad; his force grew to several thousand. Han sent Lan Ti to suppress Qi. Ti was arrogant, cruel, and dissolute and served Han without proper respect; Sheng seized the chance to tell Han: "Qi is only a boy and cannot have done this alone — someone within must be backing him." Ti has always been arrogant and cannot be trusted with a large army. " Han flew into a rage, arrested Ti and executed him, then sent his Adjutant Chou Nimu with an army against Qi. When Han's brothers saw Ti executed, all were terrified; each raised troops against Han and routed Mu's army. Han was greatly alarmed and sent his son Mu to lead troops against them. Mu told Han: "Murong Sheng is my enemy." Qi now rebels; Sheng is sure to answer him. Moreover trouble stirs within the palace — we must not harbor a disease at the heart. " Han was about to kill Sheng and summoned him for a look. Sheng's wife warned him; he feigned grave illness and no longer went abroad, and Han desisted. Li Han, Wei Shuang, Liu Zhi, Zhang Hao, and Zhang Zhen were all Sheng's old intimates whom Lan Mu drew into his inner circle. They repeatedly visited Sheng and secretly formed a great conspiracy. When Mu marched out to punish Lan Nan and the others and beheaded them, he held a great feast for the officers; Han and Mu both grew drunk. Sheng slipped out at night as if to the privy, stripped, climbed the wall, entered the Eastern Palace, and with Li Han and the others killed Mu; the troops shouted and stormed Han's quarters and cut him down. Han's two sons, the Duke of Lu He and the Duke of Chen Yang, held Lingzhi and Bailang; Sheng sent Li Han and Zhang Zhen to strike and kill them. Within and without the realm grew calm; men and women rejoiced alike; Sheng with humble modesty refused to take the imperial title. That year, as Prince of Changle he assumed regency, declared an amnesty throughout the realm, and changed the reign title to Jianping. Princes were reduced to dukes and civil and military officers resumed their former posts.

盛聽詩歌及周公之事，顧謂群臣曰：「周公之輔成王，不能以至誠感上下，誅兄弟以杜流言，猶擅美于經傳，歌德於管弦。 至如我之太宰桓王，承百王之季，主在可奪之年，二寇窺窬，難過往日，臨朝輔政，群情緝穆，經略外敷，辟境千里，以禮讓維宗親，德刑制群後，敦睦雍熙，時無二論。 勳道之茂，豈可與周公同日而言乎！ 而燕詠闕而不論，盛德掩而不述，非所謂也。 」乃命中書更為《燕頌》以述恪之功焉。 又引中書令常忠、尚書陽璆、秘書監郎敷於東堂，問曰：「古來君子皆謂周公忠聖，豈不謬哉！ 」璆曰：「周公居攝政之重，而能達群臣之名，及流言之謗，致烈風以悟主，道契神靈，義光萬代，故累葉稱其高，後王無以奪其美。 」盛曰：「常令以為何如？ 」忠曰：「昔武王疾篤，周公有請令之誠，流言之際，義感天地，楚撻伯禽以訓就王德。 周公為臣之忠，聖達之美，《詩》《書》已來未之有也。 」盛曰：「異哉二君之言！ 朕見周公之詐，未見其忠聖也。 昔武王得九齡之夢，白文王，文王曰：「我百，爾九十，吾與爾三焉。 」及文王之終，已驗武王之壽矣。 武王之算未盡而求代其死，是非詐乎！ 若惑於天命，是不聖也。 據攝天位而丹誠不見，致兄弟之間有干戈之事。 夫文王之化，自近及遠，故曰刑于寡妻，至於兄弟。 周公親違聖父之典而蹈嫌疑之蹤，戮罰同氣以逞私忿，何忠之有乎！ 但時無直筆之史，後儒承其謬談故也。 」忠曰：「啟金縢而返風，亦足以明其不詐。 遭二叔流言之變，而能大義滅親，終安宗國，復子明辟，輔成大業，以致太平，制禮作樂，流慶無窮，亦不可謂非至德也。 」盛曰：「卿徒因成文而未原大理，朕今相為論之。 昔周自後稷積德累仁，至於文、武。 文、武以大聖應期，遂有天下。 生靈仰其德，四海歸其仁。 成王雖幼統洪業，而卜世修長，加呂、召、毛、畢為之師傅。 若無周公攝政，王道足以成也。 周公無故以安危為己任，專臨朝之權，闕北面之禮。 管、蔡忠存王室，以為周公代主非人臣之道，故言公將不利於孺子。 周公當明大順之節，陳誠義以曉群疑，而乃阻兵都邑，擅行誅戮。 不臣之罪彰于海內，方貽王《鴟鴞》之詩，歸非於主，是何謂乎！ 又周公舉事，稱告二公，二公足明周公之無罪而坐觀成王之疑，此則二公之心亦有猜于周公也。 但以疏不間親，故寄言于管、蔡，可謂忠不見於當時，仁不及于兄弟。 知群望之有歸，天命之不在己，然後返政成王，以為忠耳。 大風拔木之征，乃皇天祐存周道，不忘文、武之德，是以赦周公之始愆，欲成周室之大美。 考周公之心，原周公之行，乃天下之罪人，何至德之謂也！ 周公復位，二公所以杜口不言其本心者，以明管、蔡之忠也。」
Listening to songs about the Duke of Zhou, Sheng turned to his ministers and said: "The Duke of Zhou's service to King Cheng — he could not win the hearts of high and low by sincerity alone, killed his brothers to silence rumor, yet still monopolizes praise in the classics and sings his virtue to music." But my Grand Preceptor Prince Huan inherited an age after a hundred kings, when the throne stood in years that might be seized; two enemies pressed in with difficulties surpassing any former day; he governed at court, harmonized all hearts, extended the borders a thousand li abroad, bound the clan by ritual yielding and ruled the host by virtue and law, fostering harmony until none dissented. Can such merit be mentioned in the same breath as the Duke of Zhou's! Yet Yan songs pass him over in silence and great virtue goes unrecorded — this will not do." " He ordered the Masters of Writing to compose anew the "Song of Yan" to recount Murong Ke's achievements. He also summoned the Director of the Secretariat Chang Zhong, the Master of Writing Yang Qiu, and the Secretariat Director Lang Fu to the Eastern Hall and asked: "Men of old all call the Duke of Zhou loyal and sagely — are they not mistaken?" " Qiu said: "The Duke of Zhou bore the weight of regency yet could win the ministers' names to light; when slander spread he moved Heaven with a fierce wind to awaken the king — his Way matched the spirits and his righteousness lights ten thousand generations; age after age praises his height and later kings cannot displace his glory." Sheng said: "Director Chang, what is your view?" " Zhong said: "When King Wu lay gravely ill, the Duke of Zhou prayed to yield his own life; at the moment of slander his righteousness moved Heaven and Earth; he flogged the Duke of Bo to complete the king's virtue." The Duke of Zhou's loyalty as minister and sagely excellence — since the Odes and Documents there has been nothing like it." Sheng said: "How strange are your words!" I see the Duke of Zhou's deceit, not his loyalty and sageliness. Long ago King Wu dreamed the nine-year span and told King Wen; Wen said: "I have a hundred years, you ninety — I give you three of mine." " When Wen died, King Wu's allotted span had already been confirmed. King Wu's years were not yet spent yet the Duke sought to die in his place — is this not deceit! If he was deluded by Heaven's mandate, he was not sagely. He seized regency of the throne yet showed no true loyalty, and between brothers war arose. King Wen's teaching spread from near to far — thus it is said: begin with the wife, reach to brothers. The Duke personally violated his sage father's example and walked a path of suspicion, punished his own kin to vent private wrath — where is the loyalty! But there was no historian with an upright brush in that age; later Confucians inherited their false tales — that is all." " Zhong said: "Opening the golden coffer and the returning wind is proof enough that he was not deceitful." He faced the two uncles' slander yet could sacrifice kin for the greater good, secured the state, restored the son and clarified the regency, assisted the great enterprise, brought peace, established rites and music, and his blessing flows without end — one cannot call this less than utmost virtue." Sheng said: "You only follow finished texts and have not traced the great principle; I shall debate this with you now." Long ago Zhou from Hou Ji onward accumulated virtue and benevolence down to Wen and Wu. Wen and Wu with great sageliness answered the age and took All under Heaven. The people looked up to their virtue; the four seas returned to their benevolence. King Cheng though young inherited the great enterprise, yet the house was long in fortune; Lü, Shao, Mao, and Bi served as his tutors. Without the Duke of Zhou as regent, the kingly Way would have been sufficient to prevail. The Duke without cause made the realm's safety his private charge, monopolized court authority, and neglected the ritual of facing north. Guan and Cai loyally upheld the royal house and held that the Duke's replacing the sovereign was not a minister's Way; therefore they said the Duke would harm the young king. The Duke should have proclaimed the great principle of obedience and set forth sincere righteousness to clear doubts — yet he blocked troops at the capital and executed at will. His disloyalty was manifest throughout the realm; he presented the king the Ode of the Owl, turning blame upon his lord — what is this! When the Duke acted he reported to the Two Dukes; they could have proved his innocence yet sat watching the king's doubts — their hearts too suspected the Duke. Because the distant may not come between kin, they lodged their words in Guan and Cai — loyalty was unseen in that age and benevolence never reached his brothers. Knowing the people's allegiance had found its object and Heaven's mandate was not his, only then did he return power to King Cheng — and this they call loyalty. The great wind that uprooted trees was Heaven blessing the Zhou Way and remembering the virtue of Wen and Wu; it pardoned the Duke of Zhou's first fault to complete the Zhou house's glory. Weigh the Duke of Zhou's heart and trace his deeds — he was a criminal under Heaven; how can one call this utmost virtue! When the Duke returned to power, the Two Dukes kept silent about their true thoughts to show that Guan and Cai had been loyal."

Bao adopted him, granted the Murong surname, and enfeoffed him Duke of Xiyang.
41
熙之葬苻氏也，馮跋詣雲，告之以謀。 雲懼曰：「吾嬰疾歷年，卿等所知，願更圖之。 」跋逼曰：「慕容氏世衰，河間虐暴，惑妖淫之女而逆亂天常，百姓不堪其害，思亂者十室九焉，此天亡之時也。 公自高氏名家，何能為他養子！ 機運難邀，千歲一時，公焉得辭也！ 」扶之而出。 雲曰：「吾疾苦日久，廢絕世務。 卿今興建大事，謬見推逼。 所以徘徊，非為身也，實惟否德不足以濟元元故耳。 」跋等強之，雲遂即天王位，復姓高氏，大赦境內殊死以下，改元曰正始，國號大燕。 署馮跋侍中、都督中外諸軍事、征北大將軍、開府儀同三司、錄尚書事、武邑公，封伯、子、男，鄉、亭侯者五十餘人，士卒賜穀帛有差。 熙之群官，復其爵位。 立妻李氏為天王後，子彭為太子。 越騎校尉慕輿良謀叛，雲誅之。
When Xi buried Lady Fu, Feng Ba came to Yun with the plot. Yun in fear said: "I have been ill for years, as you know — choose another leader." " Ba pressed: "The Murongs decline; the Prince of Hejian is cruel, led astray by a witch and perverting Heaven's way — the people cannot endure it; nine in ten think of rebellion — Heaven's hour has come." You are of the great Gao house — how can you remain another's adopted son! Fortune comes once in a thousand years — how can you refuse! " They raised him up and led him out. Yun said: "My illness is long and I have left worldly affairs." You raise a great affair and wrongly force me. I hesitate not for myself — my virtue is too slight to save the people. " They pressed him; Yun took the title Heavenly King, resumed the surname Gao, declared amnesty short of capital crimes, adopted the reign title Zhengshi, and named the state Great Yan. He appointed Feng Ba Attendant-in-Ordinary, Commander-in-Chief, Grand General Who Campaigns North, Grand Marshal equal to the Three Excellencies, Director of the Masters of Writing, and Duke of Wuyi; he enfeoffed more than fifty marquises and earls; soldiers received grain and cloth by rank. Xi's officials had their ranks restored. He made his wife Lady Li Heavenly Queen and his son Peng crown prince. The Rapid Cavalry Commandant Murong Liang plotted rebellion; Yun executed him.
42
雲臨東堂，幸臣離班、桃仁懷劍執紙而入，稱有所啟，拔劍擊雲，雲以幾距班，桃仁進而弑之。 馮跋遷雲屍於東宮，偽諡惠懿皇帝。 雲自以無功德而為豪桀所推，常內懷懼，故寵養壯士以為腹心。 離班、桃仁等並專典禁衛，委之以爪牙之任，賞賜月至數千萬，衣食臥起皆與之同，終以此致敗雲。
At the Eastern Hall, favored minister Li Ban and Taoren entered with swords hidden in papers, struck Yun — he blocked Ban with a table — and Taoren killed him. Feng Ba moved Yun's body to the Eastern Palace with the posthumous title Emperor Huiyi. Yun, knowing he lacked merit yet was pushed forward by the bold, lived in constant fear and kept stalwarts as his inner guard. Li Ban, Taoren, and others held the palace guard alone, trusted as his claws and teeth, rewarded by the tens of millions monthly, sharing his food and bed — and through this ruined him.
43
史臣曰：四星東聚，金陵之氣已分； 五馬南浮，玉塞之雄方擾。 市朝屢改，艱虞靡息。 慕容垂天資英傑，威震本朝，以雄略見猜而庇身寬政，永固受之而以禮，道明事之而畢力。 然而隼質難羈，狼心自野。 淮南失律，三甥之謀已構； 河朔分麾，五木之祥雲啟。 斬飛龍而遐舉，逾石門而長邁，遂使翟氏景從，鄴師宵逸，收羅趙、魏，驅駕英雄。 叩囊餘奇，摧五萬于河曲； 浮船秘策，招七郡于黎陽。 返遼陰之舊物，創中山之新社，類帝禋宗，僭擬斯備。 夫以重耳歸晉，賴五臣之功； 句踐紿吳，資五千之卒。 惡有業殊二霸，眾微一旅，掎拔而傾山嶽，騰嘯而禦風雲！ 雖衛人忘亡復傳于東國，任好余裕伊愧於西鄰，信苻氏之奸回，非晉室之鯨鯢矣。
The historiographer says: Four stars gathered in the east — Jinling's destiny was already divided; five horses drifted south — the north's strength was in turmoil. Courts and markets shifted endlessly; hardship never ceased. Murong Chui was Heaven-touched, heroic, and awe-inspiring; suspected for his bold strategy he sheltered under a lenient master; Fu Jian received him with ritual; he served Fu Jian with all his strength. Yet a falcon's nature cannot be tethered; a wolf's heart is wild from birth. At Huainan discipline failed — the three nephews' plot took shape; north of the Yellow River banners divided — the omen of the Five Mulberries rose. He struck down the flying dragon and soared away, crossed Shimen and marched on — the Di followed, the Ye army fled by night; he gathered Zhao and Wei and drove the heroes. From the bag's last wonders he crushed fifty thousand at the river bend; with secret plans on floating boats he drew seven commanderies at Liyang. He recovered Liaoyin's old lands and founded Zhongshan anew; imitating imperial rites, usurpation was complete. Chong'er returned to Jin on the merit of five ministers; Goujian deceived Wu with five thousand men. How could achievement rival the two hegemons come from a single slight brigade, prying mountains aside and roaring at wind and cloud! Though Wey forgot ruin yet lived on in the east; Duke Mu's surplus shamed Yi in the west — truly the Fu clan's treachery, not the Jin house's ruin.
44
寶以浮譽獲升，峻文禦俗，蕭牆內憤，勍敵外陵，雖毒不被物而惡足自剿。 盛則孝友冥符，文武不墜，韜光而夷仇賊，罪己而遜高危，翩翩然濁世之佳虜矣。 熙乃地非奧主，舉因淫德。 驪戎之態，取悅于匡床； 玄妻之姿，見奇於鬒發。 蕩輕舟于曲光之海，望朝涉于景雲之山，飾土木於驕心，窮怨嗟於蕞壤，宗祀夷滅，為馮氏之驅除焉。
Bao rose on empty reputation, ruled by harsh law; fury within, fierce foes without — though his poison did not touch all, wickedness enough to destroy himself. Sheng matched filial piety and friendship in secret, kept civil and martial from falling; he hid his light, pacified foes, blamed himself and yielded peril — a fine spirit in a turbid age. Xi was no hidden lord; his rise came entirely through licentious favor. Li Rong's charms pleased on the couch; Xuan Qi's beauty dazzled in dark hair. He floated light boats on Quguang's sea, watched dawn crossings from Jingyun Mountain, built earth and wood to feed pride, exhausted grief on a mote of soil — the ancestral rites perished and he became prey for the Feng clan.
45
贊曰：戎狄憑陵，山川沸騰。 天未悔禍，人非與能。 疾走而捷，先鳴則興。 道明烈烈，鞭笞豪傑。 掃燕夷魏，釗屠永滅。 大盜潛移，鴻名遂竊。 寶心生亂，盛清家難。 熙極驕淫，人懷憤惋。 孽貽身咎，災無以逭。
The eulogy says: Barbarians relied on force; mountains and rivers seethed. Heaven had not yet repented the calamity; no man could succeed. Run fast and you are swift; cry first and you rise. Daoming blazing, lashing the bold heroes. He swept Yan, leveled Wei, struck Ju and Yong to extinction. The great thief moved in secret; the great name was stolen. Bao's heart bred disorder; Sheng cleared the family peril. Xi reached arrogant debauchery's extreme; men nursed wrath. Sin passed to the body — disaster could not be escaped.
