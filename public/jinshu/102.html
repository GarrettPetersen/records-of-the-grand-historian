Liu Cong.
2
劉聰，字玄明，一名載，元海第四子也。 母曰張夫人。 初，聰之在孕也，張氏夢日入懷，寤而以告，元海曰：「此吉徵也，慎勿言。 」十五月而生聰焉，夜有白光之異，形體非常。 左耳有一白毫，長二尺餘，甚光澤。 幼而聰悟好學，博士朱紀大奇之。 年十四，究通經史，兼綜百家之言，《孫吳兵法》靡不誦之。 工草隸，善屬文，著述懷詩百餘篇、賦頌五十餘篇。 十五習擊刺，猿臂善射，彎弓三百斤，膂力驍捷，冠絕一時。 太原王渾見而悅之，謂元海曰：「此兒吾所不能測也。」
Liu Cong, whose courtesy name was Xuanming and who was also known as Zai, was the fourth son of Liu Yuan (Yuanhai). His mother was known as Lady Zhang. While Liu Cong was still in the womb, Lady Zhang dreamed that the sun entered her breast; when she awoke she told Liu Yuan, who said, "That is a favorable portent—say nothing of it." " After fifteen months she bore him, and by night an unnatural white light appeared; his physique was extraordinary. A white hair more than two chi long grew from his left ear, bright and glossy. As a boy he was bright, quick to learn, and eager for study; the academician Zhu Ji considered him remarkable. By fourteen he had mastered the canonical texts and histories and surveyed the hundred schools; he knew every line of the Sun and Wu military treatises by heart. He excelled at cursive and clerical calligraphy and at literary composition, leaving more than a hundred reflective poems and over fifty fu pieces and encomia. At fifteen he trained in swordsmanship; long-armed like an ape, he drew a three-hundred-jin bow and moved with fierce strength and speed—none of his contemporaries could match him. Wang Hun of Taiyuan took to him at once and told Liu Yuan, "This youth is beyond my powers to measure."
3
弱冠游于京師，名士莫不交結，樂廣、張華尤異之也。 新興太守郭頤辟為主簿，舉良將，入為驍騎別部司馬，累遷右部都尉，善於撫接，五部豪右無不歸之。 河間王顒表為赤沙中郎將。 聰以元海在鄴，懼為成都王穎所害，乃亡奔成都王，拜右積弩將軍，參前鋒戰事。 元海為北單于，立為右賢王，隨還右部。 及即大單于位，更拜鹿蠡王。 既殺其兄和，群臣勸即尊位。 聰初讓其弟北海王乂，乂與公卿泣涕固請，聰久而許之，曰：「乂及群公正以四海未定，禍難尚殷，貪孤年長故耳。 此國家之事，孤敢不祗從。 今便欲遠遵魯隱，待乂年長，復子明辟。 」於是以僭即皇帝位，大赦境內，改元光興。 尊元海妻單氏曰皇太后，其母張氏為帝太后，乂為皇太弟，領大單于、大司徒，立其妻呼延氏為皇后，封其子粲為河內王，署使持節、撫軍大將軍、都督中外諸軍事，易河間王，翼彭城王，悝高平王。 遣粲及其征東王彌、龍驤劉曜等率眾四萬，長驅入洛川，遂出轘轅，周旋梁、陳、汝、潁之間，陷壘壁百餘。 以其司空劉景為大司馬，左光祿劉殷為大司徒，右光祿王育為大司空。 偽太后單氏姿色絕麗，聰蒸焉。 單即乂之母也，乂屢以為言，單氏慚恚而死，聰悲悼無已。 -{后}-知其故，乂之寵因此漸衰，然猶追念單氏，未便黜廢。 又尊母為皇太后。
When he came of age he frequented the capital, befriended every notable in town, and won particular regard from Yue Guang and Zhang Hua. Guo Yi, governor of Xinping, appointed him chief clerk and nominated him as an outstanding candidate for military office; he became acting-major of an independent fierce-cavalry unit and eventually colonel of the right division. Skilled at winning people over, he drew every powerful clan of the five divisions to his side. Prince Sima Yong of Hejian recommended him for appointment as general of the Red Sands guards. Because Liu Yuan was at Ye, Liu Cong feared Prince Sima Ying of Chengdu would kill him, so he fled to Sima Ying's camp and was made general of the right strong crossbow corps, serving on the forward staff. When Liu Yuan became chanyu of the northern Xiongnu, Liu Cong was named right virtuous king and followed him back to the right division. When Liu Yuan took the great chanyu title, Liu Cong was promoted to prince of Luli. Once he had killed his elder brother Liu He, his officials pressed him to mount the throne. Liu Cong first offered the throne to his younger brother Liu Yi, prince of Beihai; Yi and the senior officials wept and insisted until Liu Cong at length agreed. "Yi and you ministers," he said, "want me only because the realm is unsettled, disaster still rife, and I am the elder." This concerns the fate of the state—how could I refuse what reverence demands?" For now I mean to follow the example of Duke Yin of Lu: when Yi comes of age, I shall yield the throne and restore him to full authority." " With that he seized the imperial title, proclaimed an amnesty throughout his realm, and adopted the reign title Guangxing. He honored Liu Yuan's widow Lady Shan as empress dowager and his own mother Lady Zhang as dowager empress; he named Liu Yi imperial younger brother and heir apparent, with concurrent titles of grand chanyu and grand tutor; he took Lady Huyan as empress and enfeoffed his son Liu Can as prince of Henan with full credentials as grand marshal who comforts the army and commander of all forces at court and in the field; his sons Yi was prince of Hejian, Yi prince of Pengcheng, and Kui prince of Gaoping. He sent Liu Can with Wang Mi, east-conquest commander, Liu Yao, dragon-general, and forty thousand men deep into the Luoyang plain; they passed Huanyuan pass and ranged across Liang, Chen, Ru, and Ying, reducing more than a hundred fortified camps. He named Minister of Works Liu Jing grand marshal, Household Physician of the Left Liu Yin grand tutor, and Household Physician of the Right Wang Yu minister of works. The sham empress dowager Lady Shan was extraordinarily beautiful; Liu Cong forced an illicit liaison with her. Lady Shan was Liu Yi's mother; Yi protested again and again until she died of shame and rage. Liu Cong grieved for her without end. When the empress learned why, Liu Yi's standing slipped—but Liu Cong still cherished Lady Shan's memory and did not cast Yi aside. He then elevated his own mother to empress dowager.
4
署其衛尉呼延晏為使持節、前鋒大都督、前軍大將軍。 配禁兵二萬七千，自宜陽入洛川，命王彌、劉曜及鎮軍石勒進師會之。 晏比及河南，王師前後十二敗，死者三萬餘人。 彌等未至，晏留輜重于張方故壘，遂寇洛陽，攻陷平昌門，焚東陽、宣陽諸門及諸府寺。 懷帝遣河南尹劉默距之，王師敗於社門。 晏以外繼不至，出自東陽門，掠王公已下子女二百餘人而去。 時帝將濟河東遁，具船于洛水，晏盡焚之，還于張方故壘。 王彌、劉曜至，復與晏會圍洛陽。 時城內饑甚，人皆相食，百官分散，莫有固志。 宣陽門陷，彌、晏入於南宮，升太極前殿，縱兵大掠，悉收宮人、珍寶。 曜於是害諸王公及百官已下三萬餘人，于洛水北築為京觀。 遷帝及惠帝羊-{后}-、傳國六璽於平陽。 聰大赦，改年嘉平，以帝為特進、左光祿大夫、平阿公。
He named his captain of the guard Huyan Yan commander-in-chief of the van with full credentials and grand general of the forward army. He gave him twenty-seven thousand household troops to strike the Luoyang basin from Yiyang and told Wang Mi, Liu Yao, and stabilizing-general Shi Le to march and link up with him. By the time Huyan Yan reached Henan, Jin armies had lost twelve battles in a row and more than thirty thousand men lay dead. Wang Mi had not arrived when Huyan Yan parked his baggage at Zhang Fang's old camp, struck Luoyang, broke the Pingchang Gate, and set fire to the Dongyang and Xuanyang gates and government compounds. Emperor Huai ordered Henan governor Liu Mo to resist; Jin forces were routed at the She Gate. When relief failed to come, Huyan Yan rode out the Dongyang Gate, seized more than two hundred sons and daughters of the nobility, and withdrew. The emperor was preparing to slip east across the river; boats waited on the Luo until Huyan Yan burned every hull and fell back to Zhang Fang's old fort. Wang Mi and Liu Yao arrived and joined Huyan Yan to tighten the siege of Luoyang. Starvation inside the walls turned men cannibal; officials fled; no one held a steady resolve. When the Xuanyang Gate fell, Wang Mi and Huyan Yan burst into the southern palace, mounted the hall fronting the Taiji sanctuary, and let their troops sack it—every woman of the harem and every treasure was taken. Liu Yao then slaughtered more than thirty thousand princes, nobles, and officials, heaping the bodies north of the Luo into a victory mound. He moved the emperor, Empress Yang of Emperor Hui, and the six dynastic jade seals to Pingyang. Liu Cong declared a general amnesty, adopted the year-name Jiaping, and gave the Jin emperor the titular rank of supernumerary grandee, left household grandee, and duke of Ping'e.
5
遣其平西趙染、安西劉雅率騎二萬攻南陽王模于長安，粲、曜率大眾繼之。 染敗王師于潼關，將軍呂毅死之。 軍至於下邽，模乃降染。 染送模於粲，粲害模及其子范陽王黎，送衛將軍梁芬、模長史魯繇、兼散騎常侍杜驁、辛謐及北宮純等於平陽。 聰以粲之害模也，大怒。 粲曰：「臣殺模本不以其晚識天命之故，但以其晉氏肺腑，洛陽之難不能死節，天下之惡一也，故誅之。 」聰曰：「雖然，吾恐汝不免誅降之殃也。 夫天道至神，理無不報。」
He sent pacification-generals of the west Zhao Ran and Liu Ya with twenty thousand cavalry against Sima Mo, prince of Nanyang, at Chang'an; Liu Can and Liu Yao brought the main army behind them. Zhao Ran beat the Jin host at Tong Pass, where general Lu Yi fell in action. When the army reached Xiagui, Sima Mo capitulated to Zhao Ran. Zhao Ran forwarded Sima Mo to Liu Can, who executed Mo and his son Li, prince of Fanyang, then sent Liang Fen, guard-general, Lu Yao, chief clerk, concurrent cavaliers Du Ao and Xin Mi, Bei Gongchun, and others to Pingyang. Liu Cong was furious that Liu Can had put Sima Mo to death. Liu Can replied: "I executed Mo not because he suddenly recognized Heaven's will but because, bone of the Jin imperial clan, he failed to die for honor during Luoyang's catastrophe—one of the worst villains alive—so I removed him." " Liu Cong said: "Even so, I fear you invite the curse that falls on those who kill men who yield." Heaven's way is subtle beyond telling; cause never fails of consequence."
6
署劉曜為車騎大將軍、開府儀同三司、雍州牧，改封中山王，鎮長安，王彌為大將軍，封齊公。 尋而石勒等殺彌於己吾而並其眾，表彌叛狀。 聰大怒，遣使讓勒專害公輔，有無上之心，又恐勒之有二志也，以彌部眾配之。 劉曜既據長安，安定太守賈疋及諸氐羌皆送質任，唯雍州刺史麹特、新平太守竺恢固守不降。 護軍麹允、頻陽令梁肅自京兆南山將奔安定，遇疋任子于陰密，擁還臨涇，推疋為平南將軍，率眾五萬，攻曜于長安，扶風太守梁綜及麹特、竺恢等亦率眾十萬會之。 曜遣劉雅、趙染來距，敗績而還。 曜又盡長安銳卒與諸軍戰于黃丘，曜眾大敗，中流矢，退保甘渠。 杜人王禿、紀特等攻劉粲于新豐，粲還平陽。 曜攻陷池陽，掠萬餘人歸於長安。 時閻鼎等奉秦王為皇太子，入于雍城，關中戎晉莫不回應。
He named Liu Yao grand chariot-and-cavalry general with privilege equal to the Three Excellencies and shepherd of Yongzhou, transferring his title to prince of Zhongshan to garrison Chang'an; Wang Mi became grand general and duke of Qi. Soon Shi Le killed Wang Mi at Yiwu, swallowed his army, and memorialized Wang Mi's rebellion. Liu Cong raged: envoys rebuked Shi Le for murdering a pillar minister without orders—treason in intent—and fearing divided loyalty handed Wang Mi's troops to him anyway. Once Liu Yao held Chang'an, Jia Yu of Anding and the Di and Qiang tribes all sent hostages; only Yongzhou's Qu Te and Xinping's Zhu Hui held their walls and refused submission. Protector Qu Yun and Pinyang magistrate Liang Su fled south from Jingzhao toward Anding, picked up Jia Yu's hostage son at Yinmi, turned toward Linjing, and proclaimed Jia Yu pacifier-general of the south—fifty thousand men marched on Liu Yao at Chang'an while Liang Zong of Fufeng, Qu Te, Zhu Hui, and others brought another hundred thousand to the rendezvous. Liu Yao sent Liu Ya and Zhao Ran to intercept them but suffered defeat and pulled back. Liu Yao committed every elite regiment from Chang'an to Huangqiu, suffered a crushing rout, took an arrow wound himself, and withdrew to Ganqu. Wang Tu and Ji Te of Du county attacked Liu Can at Xinfeng, forcing him back to Pingyang. Liu Yao stormed Chiyang, dragged off more than ten thousand captives, and retired to Chang'an. Meanwhile Yan Ding enthroned the prince of Qin as heir apparent at Yong city; every Jin and tribal leader in Guanzhong rallied to him.
7
聰-{后}-呼延氏死，將納其太保劉殷女，其弟乂固諫。 聰更訪之于太宰劉延年、大傅劉景，景等皆曰：「臣常聞太保自云周劉康公之後，與聖氏本源既殊，納之為允。 」聰大悅，使其兼大鴻臚李弘拜殷二女為左右貴嬪，位在昭儀上。 又納殷女孫四人為貴人，位次貴嬪。 謂弘曰：「此女輩皆姿色超世，女德冠時，且太保於朕實自不同，卿意安乎？ 」弘曰：「太保胤自有周，與聖源實別，陛下正以姓同為恨耳。 且魏司空東萊王基當世大儒，豈不達禮乎！ 為子納司空太原王沈女，以其姓同而源異故也。 」聰大悅，賜弘黃金六十斤，曰：「卿當以此意諭吾子弟輩。 」於是六劉之寵傾于後宮，聰稀復出外，事皆中黃門納奏，左貴嬪決之。
After Empress Huyan died, Liu Cong planned to take daughters of grand guardian Liu Yin; his brother Liu Yi vehemently opposed it. Liu Cong asked grand preceptor Liu Yannian and grand tutor Liu Jing; Liu Jing said, "The grand guardian claims descent from Duke Kang of Zhou—his bloodline diverges from yours; the marriage is permissible." " Delighted, Liu Cong ordered Li Hong, concurrent grand herald of guests, to install two of Liu Yin's daughters as senior noble concorts outranking the Brilliant Companion. He added four of Liu Yin's granddaughters as noble ladies just below those consorts. He told Li Hong, "Each of these women is a paragon of beauty and virtue, and the grand guardian's lineage is hardly the same as mine—does that settle your conscience?" " Li Hong replied, "His house stems from Zhou itself—quite apart from yours—Your Majesty only bridles at sharing the surname Liu." When Wei dynast's minister of works Wang Ji of Donglai, the age's great classicist, took a bride with the same surname, did he flout ritual? He married his son to a daughter of minister of works Wang Shen of Taiyuan precisely because the clan name matched yet the descent differed." " Liu Cong was delighted, gave Li Hong sixty jin of gold, and said, "Explain this reasoning to my sons and brothers." " The six Liu ladies now dominated the harem; Liu Cong seldom emerged; eunuchs filed memorials and the senior left consort ruled on them.
8
聰假懷帝儀同三司，封會稽郡公，庾瑉等以次加秩。 聰引帝入宴，謂帝曰：「卿為豫章王時，朕嘗與王武子相造，武子示朕於卿，卿言聞其名久矣。 以卿所制樂府歌示朕，謂朕曰：'聞君善為辭賦，試為看之。 '朕時與武子俱為《盛德頌》，卿稱善者久之。 又引朕射於皇堂，朕得十二籌，卿與武子俱得九籌，卿贈朕柘弓、銀研，卿頗憶否？ 」帝曰：「臣安敢忘之，但恨爾日不早識龍顏。 」聰曰：「卿家骨肉相殘，何其甚也？ 」帝曰：「此殆非人事，皇天之意也。 大漢將應乾受曆，故為陛下自相驅除。 且臣家若能奉武皇之業，九族敦睦，陛下何由得之！ 」至日夕乃出，以小劉貴人賜帝，謂帝曰：「此名公之孫，今特以相妻，卿宜善遇之。 」拜劉為會稽國夫人。
Liu Cong gave Emperor Huai honorary parity with the Three Excellencies and the ducal title of Kuaiji; Yu Min and others received stepped promotions. Liu Cong led Emperor Huai into a banquet and said, "When you were prince of Yuzhang, I once called on Wang Yan with Wang Wu zi; he introduced me, and you said you had wanted to meet me for years." You showed me the Music Bureau verses you had composed and said to me, 'They say you excel at lyrics and rhapsodies—write something for me to see.' Then you and Wang Yan each presented odes to sublime virtue, and you praised the verses at length. You took me to the imperial hall for archery: I scored twelve counters while you and Wang Yan each scored nine, and you gave me a mulberry bow and silver inkstone. Do you remember? " Emperor Huai replied, "How could I forget—I only regret that I failed then to recognize Your Majesty's dragon bearing." " Liu Cong asked, "Why did your own kin tear one another apart so viciously?" " The emperor answered, "That was scarcely human malice—it was Heaven's design." The great Han is destined to answer Heaven's cycle; that is why my kinsmen destroyed one another to clear the way for Your Majesty. Had my clan sustained Emperor Wu's enterprise and kept the nine branches at peace among themselves, how could Your Majesty ever have reached the throne? " At dusk they released him and Liu Cong bestowed the young Lady Liu on him as wife, saying, "She is descended from a great house; I give her to you in marriage—treat her kindly." " She was invested as duchess consort of Kuaiji.
9
遣其鎮北靳沖寇太原，平北卜珝率眾繼之。 沖攻太原不克，而歸罪於珝，輒斬之。 聰聞之，大怒曰：「此人朕所不得加刑，沖何人哉！ 」遣其御史中丞浩衍持節斬沖。 左都水使者襄陵王攄坐魚蟹不供，將作大匠望都公靳陵坐溫明、徽光二殿不成，皆斬於東市。 聰游獵無度，常晨出暮歸，觀漁于汾水，以燭繼晝。 中軍王彰諫曰：「今大難未夷，余晉假息，陛下不懼白龍魚服之禍，而昏夜忘歸。 陛下當思先帝創業之艱難，嗣承之不易，鴻業已爾，四海屬情，何可墜之于垂成，隳之於將就！ 比竊觀陛下所為，臣實痛心疾首有日矣。 且愚人系漢之心未專，而思晉之懷猶盛，劉琨去此咫尺之間，狂狷刺客息頃而至。 帝王輕出，一夫敵耳。 願陛下改往修來，則憶兆幸甚。 」聰大怒，命斬之。 上夫人王氏叩頭乞哀，乃囚之詔獄。 聰母以聰刑怒過差，三日不食，弟乂、子粲並與切諫。 聰怒曰：「吾豈桀、紂、幽、厲乎，而汝等生來哭人！ 」其太宰劉延年及諸公卿列侯百有餘人，皆免冠涕泣固諫曰：「光文皇帝以聖武膺期，創建鴻祚，而六合未一，夙世升遐。 陛下睿德自天，龍飛紹統，東平洛邑，南定長安，真可謂功高周成，德超夏啟。 往也唐虞，今則陛下，曆觀書記，未有此比。 而頃頻以小務不供而斬王公，直言忤旨，便囚大將，游獵無度，機管不修，臣等竊所未解，臣等所以破肝糜胃忘寢與食者也。 」聰乃赦彰。
He dispatched pacification-general of the north Jin Chong against Taiyuan while pacification-general of the north Bu Xu followed with reinforcements. Jin Chong failed to take Taiyuan, blamed Bu Xu, and executed him on his own authority. When Liu Cong learned of it he thundered, "Even I could not put that man to death—who is Jin Chong to kill him!" " He sent chief palace censor Hao Yan with imperial credentials to execute Jin Chong. Prince Sima Yun of Xiangling, left superintendent of waterways, died because fish and crabs were short for the kitchens; Duke Jin Ling of Wangdu, chief builder, died because the Bright Warmth and Radiant Light halls remained unfinished—both were beheaded at the eastern market. Liu Cong hunted without measure, sometimes riding out at dawn and returning after dark; he watched fishermen on the Fen by torchlight long after sunset. Central Army commander Wang Zhang urged him: "The great catastrophe is not ended—surviving Jin still draws breath—yet Your Majesty ignores the peril of the dragon disguised as a fish and wanders until midnight without returning." Remember the toil of the founding fathers and the fragility of the line you now hold: the realm already leans on you—how can you shatter it on the very verge of success or waste it at the moment of consummation! I have watched what Your Majesty does, and for many days my heart has burned with anguish. The common folk are not yet devoted solely to Han; their hunger for the old Jin is still keen. Liu Kun is a short ride away, and a desperate blade can reach you in a moment. When an emperor wanders lightly abroad, any lone assassin may strike him down. If Your Majesty amends past faults and builds anew, the myriad subjects will count themselves blessed beyond measure. " Liu Cong flew into a rage and ordered Wang Zhang executed. Senior lady Wang kowtowed until her forehead bled; Liu Cong relented enough to jail Wang Zhang in the imperial prison instead. Liu Cong's mother fasted three days to protest his cruel judgments; his brother Liu Yi and son Liu Can joined in urgent pleas. Liu Cong snarled, "Do I look like Jie, Zhou, King You, or King Li—that you were born only to weep at me!" " Grand preceptor Liu Yannian and more than a hundred dukes and ministers shed their caps and wept as they pressed him: "Emperor Guangwen answered Heaven's call in sacred might and laid the foundations of our fortune, yet the realm was still divided when he passed away untimely." Your Majesty drew sagely virtue from Heaven, ascended the throne as heir to the mandate, pacified Luoyi in the east and Chang'an in the south—one might truly say your merit rivals the age of King Cheng of Zhou and your virtue surpasses Lord Qi of Xia. The age of Tang and Yao lies in the past; today we have Your Majesty. Nowhere in the annals is there a ruler who matches this achievement. Yet of late you have put princes and dukes to death for trifling shortages, jailed a commander for frank counsel, hunted without restraint, and neglected the machinery of rule—we cannot fathom it, and it is why we ruin our health and forget to eat or sleep. " Liu Cong then pardoned Wang Zhang.

The Jin court was blind and cruel; it treated the people as straw—so Heaven cut their mandate short. Ghosts keened along the corridors of Guangji Hall—then anew in Founding Inception. Blood rained upon Pingyang for ten li—a crimson cloak on tile and field. His son Liu Yue had long been ashes—yet now walked broad daylight halls again. Liu Cong shuddered and told Liu Can: “Sickness shackles my bed—I rot by inches—and strangeness pours in thicker each dusk.” I once called Yue’s tales sorcery—now I meet him day on day. The boy comes to fetch me.” Who knew the dead keep such fire? If so—death holds no terror for me.” The age still writhes—no season for long veiled mourning: coffin me at dusk if I fail at dawn; let ten days find me in earth.”” They summoned Liu Yao as Chancellor and Keeper of Documents to steady the reins—yet he refused until the summons broke against his stubbornness. Liu Jing stayed Grand Steward; Ji, Commander-in-Chief; Liu Yi as Grand Mentor; Zhu Ji Mentor; Huyan Yan Protector—all sharing the Keeper’s ledger; Fan Long Acting Director ranked peer to Three Dukes; Jin Zhun Grand Provisioner commanding the capital police—between them rotating judgment on petitions.

Soon after, Liu Cong died. Nine years sat he on that borrowed throne—the court styled him Emperor Zhaowu despite the lie, temple name Mighty Ancestor.

劉粲
Liu Can.
51
粲字士光。 少而俊傑，才兼文武。 自為宰相，威福任情，疏遠忠賢，昵近奸佞，任性嚴刻無恩惠，距諫飾非。 好興造宮室，相國之府仿像紫宮，在位無幾，作兼晝夜，饑困窮叛，死亡相繼，粲弗之恤也。 既嗣偽位，尊聰-{后}-靳氏為皇太后，樊氏號弘道皇后，宣氏號弘德皇后，王氏號弘孝皇后。 靳等年皆未滿二十，並國色也，粲晨夜蒸淫於內，志不在哀。 立其妻靳氏為皇后，子元公為太子，大赦境內，改元漢昌。 雨血於平陽。
Liu Can, courtesy name Shiguang. In youth he blazed heroic—steel and silk both bent to his will. Once crowned chancellor he traded favor and thunder on whim—cast the loyal abroad, curled close to parasites; harsh whim wore no cloak of mercy, and loyal words died at the gate while fault wore gold. He loved scaffolding—raised a chancellor mansion aping the Purple Palace—and though his tenure snapped short he drove labor without nightfall while hunger bred mutiny stacked corpses like cordwood—with never a shrug from Liu Can. When he took the throne he raised Liu Cong’s widow Lady Jin to Grand Dowager, invested Lady Fan as Empress Grand Course, Lady Xuan as Empress Grand Virtue, and Lady Wang as Empress Grand Filial Piety. None yet twenty—each ravishing as stormlight—and Can steamed day and night through inner chambers heedless whether the court mourned. His own Jin consort wore the phoenix crown; Prince Yuangong the heir’s seal; empire-wide trumpets heralded Han Flourish and amnesty beneath new bells. Blood again sheeted Pingyang’s skies.
52
靳準將有異謀，私於粲曰：「如聞諸公將欲行伊尹、霍光之事，謀先誅太保及臣，以大司馬統萬機。 陛下若不先之，臣恐禍之來也不晨則夕。 」粲弗納。 准懼其言之不從，謂聰二靳氏曰：「今諸公侯欲廢帝，立濟南王，恐吾家無復種矣。 盍言之於帝。 」二靳承間言之。 粲誅其太宰、上洛王劉景，太師、昌國公劉顗，大司馬、濟南王劉驥，大司徒、齊王劉勱等。 太傅朱紀、太尉范隆出奔長安。 又誅其車騎大將軍、吳王劉逞，驥母弟也。 粲大閱上林，謀討石勒。 以靳准為大將軍、錄尚書事。 粲荒耽酒色，遊宴後庭，軍國之事一決於准。 准矯粲命，以從弟明為車騎將軍，康為衛將軍。
Jin Zhun nursed treason and murmured to Can: “Word says the great lords mean to play Yi Yin and Huo Guang—murder the Protector and myself first, then hand the realm to the Commander-in-Chief.” If Your Majesty does not strike before dawn, I fear disaster will own dusk.” Can shut his ears. Zhun, seeing his seed fall on stone, cornered Liu Cong’s two Jin consorts: “The lords mean to unseat the throne for the Prince of Jinan—our kin will leave no ash.” Whisper it in the emperor’s ear.” The two Ladies slithered rumor into pillow talk. Can turned steel on Grand Steward Liu Jing Prince of Shangluo, Grand Mentor Liu Yi Duke of Changguo, Commander Liu Ji Prince of Jinan, Chancellor Liu Mai Prince of Qi—and all their train. Mentor Zhu Ji and Grand Guardian Fan Long bolted west toward Chang’an. He cut down Chariot-General Liu Cheng Prince of Wu—Ji’s own milk-brother. Can paraded the imperial hunt-park mustering host—designs on Shi Le’s throat. Jin Zhun became Commander-in-Chief and Keeper of Documents. Can drowned in cups and couches while rear gardens rang with revel—soldier’s wage and dynasty’s whim both signed by Jin Zhun’s chop. Zhun forged edicts naming cousins Ming commander of chariotry, Kang Guards captain.
53
準將作亂，以金紫光祿大夫王延耆德時望，謀之於延。 延弗從，馳將告之，遇靳康，劫延以歸。 准勒兵入宮，升其光極前殿，下使甲士執粲，數而殺之。 劉氏男女無少長皆斬於東市。 發掘元海、聰墓，焚燒其宗廟。 鬼大哭，聲聞百里。
When Jin Zhun readied rebellion he courted Counsellor Wang Yan—gray pillar of esteem—and drew him into counsel. Yan refused and spurred toward the throne to expose the plot—but Jin Kang intercepted him mid-gallop and dragged him back in chains. Zhun drove soldiery through the gates, mounted the dais of Guangji’s forehall, and had armored fists haul Liu Can forward—each crime tallied aloud before the blade fell. Every member of the Liu family—children and elders alike—was put to death at the Eastern Market. They desecrated the tombs of Liu Yuan and Liu Cong and burned down the ancestral shrine. Spirits were said to howl in grief, the uproar rumored to carry a hundred li.
54
准自號大將軍、漢天王，置百官，遣使稱籓于晉。 左光祿劉雅出奔西平。 尚書北宮純、胡崧等招集晉人，保于東宮，靳康攻滅之。 準將以王延為左光祿，延罵曰：「屠各逆奴，何不速殺我，以吾左目置西陽門，觀相國之入也，右目置建春門，觀大將軍之入也。 」准怒，殺之。
Jin Zhun proclaimed himself grand general and king of Han, constituted a full court, and dispatched envoys to offer vassalage to Jin. Liu Ya, Left Household Grandee, fled to Xiping. Minister Beigong Chun, Hu Song, and others rallied Han Chinese, entrenched themselves in the Eastern Palace, and were overrun and destroyed by Jin Kang. Jin Zhun meant to appoint Wang Yan Left Household Grandee. Wang Yan swore at him: "You Tuge traitor—kill me at once. Hang my left eye on the Xiyang Gate to see the grand chancellor enter, and my right on the Jianchun Gate to see the grand general ride in." Furious, Jin Zhun had him executed.

Chen Yuanda
56
陳元達，字長宏，後部人也。 本姓高，以生月妨父，故改云陳。 少面孤貧，常躬耕兼誦書，樂道行詠，忻忻如也。 至年四十，不與人交通。 元海之為左賢王，聞而招之，元達不答。 及元海僭號，人謂元達曰：「往劉公相屈，君蔑而不顧，今稱號龍飛，君其懼乎？ 」元達笑曰：「是何言邪？ 彼人姿度卓犖，有籠羅宇宙之志，吾固知之久矣。 然往日所以不往者，以期運未至，不能無事喧喧，彼自有以亮吾矣。 卿但識之，吾恐不過二三日，驛書必至。 」其暮，元海果徵元達為黃門郎。 人曰：「君殆聖乎！ 」既至，引見，元海曰：「卿若早來，豈為郎官而已。 」元達曰：「臣惟性之有分，盈分者顛。 臣若早叩天門者，恐大王賜處於九卿、納言之間，此則非臣之分，臣將何以堪之！ 是以抑情盤桓，待分而至，大王無過授之謗，小臣免招寇之禍，不亦可乎！ 」元海大悅。 在位忠謇，屢進讜言，退而削草，雖子弟莫得而知也。 聰每謂元達曰：「卿當畏朕，反使朕畏卿乎？ 」元達叩頭謝曰：「臣聞師臣者王，友臣者霸。 臣誠愚暗無可采也，幸邀陛下垂齊桓納九九之義，故使微臣得盡愚忠。 昔世宗遙可汲黯之奏，故能恢隆漢道； 桀紂誅諫，幽厲弭謗，是以三代之亡也忽焉。 陛下以大聖應期，挺不世之量，能遠捐商周覆國之弊，近模孝武光漢之美，則天下幸甚，群臣知免。 」及其死也，人盡冤之。
Chen Yuanda, courtesy name Changhong, came from the Rear Division of the confederation. His birth surname was Gao; the month of his birth was deemed inauspicious for his father, so the family took the name Chen instead. Orphaned and poor from an early age, he farmed his own fields while studying, took delight in the Way, and composed verse as he went, always quietly content. He reached forty without entering public life or cultivating contacts. While Liu Yuan still held the title of Left Virtuous King, he heard of Chen Yuanda and invited him, but Chen did not answer. After Liu Yuan declared himself emperor, people said to Chen Yuanda, "Lord Liu once humbled himself to invite you, and you refused him coldly. Now that he has taken the throne, aren't you afraid?" Chen Yuanda laughed. "What kind of talk is that?" His bearing is remarkable, and he means to bring the world to heel—I have known that for a long time. I stayed away because the time had not yet come; I was not about to stir up a fuss without reason. He is sure to take my measure in due course. Mark my words: his summons will reach you by mounted courier within two or three days. That same evening Liu Yuan appointed him Gentlemen of the Yellow Gate. People exclaimed, "You must be nearly a sage!" When he presented himself, Liu Yuan said, "If you had joined me sooner, you would not be offered a mere gentleman rank." Chen Yuanda replied, "Each man has his proper measure; to exceed it is to court disaster." Had I answered your first summons, Your Majesty might have placed me among the Nine Ministers or among the highest counselors—offices beyond my station. How could I have sustained that burden? So I held back until my proper station opened up—sparing you talk of reckless promotion and sparing myself the jealousy high office breeds. Surely that suited us both. Liu Yuan was immensely pleased. In service he was loyal and outspoken, often submitting frank counsel; afterward he destroyed his drafts so that not even family members knew what he had written. Liu Cong would say to him, "You should stand in awe of me—is it rather I who stands in awe of you?" Chen Yuanda kowtowed and said, "The king who honors his advisor as a teacher will rule as true sovereign; he who befriends him will lead the hegemons." I am dull and hardly worth heeding, yet I beg you to show me Duke Huan of Qi's willingness to hear the humblest counsel, so that I may offer the loyalty I have. Emperor Wu endorsed Ji An's blunt memorials even from a distance, and so restored the greatness of Han; Jie and Zhou slew their critics; Kings You and Li silenced dissent—and the Three Dynasties collapsed overnight. You are the sage ordained for this age, with stature rarely matched in any generation. Reject from afar the ways that doomed Shang and Zhou, and take as your near model Emperor Wu's restoration of Han, and the realm will rejoice while your servants are spared ruin. When he died, everyone felt he had been cruelly wronged.
