傅玄
Fu Xuan.
2
傅玄，字休奕，北地泥陽人也。 祖燮，漢漢陽太守。 父幹，魏扶風太守。 玄少孤貧，博學善屬文，解鐘律。 性剛勁亮直，不能容人之短。 郡上計吏再舉孝廉，太尉辟，皆不就。 州舉秀才，除郎中，與東海繆施俱以時譽選入著作，撰集《魏書》。 後參安東、衛軍軍事，轉溫令，再遷弘農太守，領典農校尉。 所居稱職，數上書陳便宜，多所匡正。 五等建，封鶉觚男。 武帝為晉王，以玄為散騎常侍。 及受禪，進爵為子，加附馬都尉。
Fu Xuan, whose courtesy name was Xiuyi, came from Niyang in Beidi commandery. His grandfather Xie had served as Governor of Hanyang in Han times. His father, Fu Gan, was Governor of Fufeng under Wei. Orphaned while still young and living in want, Fu Xuan nevertheless mastered a wide range of learning, wrote well, and understood bells and pitch pipes. He was by nature rigid, outspoken, and blunt—unable to indulge other people's faults. The commandery clerk who submitted the annual accounts nominated him twice as Filial and Incorrupt, and the Grand Commandant summoned him to serve; he declined every offer. The province put him forward as a Flourishing Talent, and he was made a Gentleman of the Palace. Together with Miao Shi of Donghai he was picked—on the strength of contemporary repute—for the Editorial Service to compile the Book of Wei. He later served as staff officer on the Anton and Guards armies, then became magistrate of Wen, rose twice more to Governor of Hongnong, and held the concurrent post of Colonel Director of Agriculture. Wherever he served he earned a reputation for competence; he sent up memorial after memorial on practical reforms, and many of his points were adopted. When the five noble ranks were instituted, he received the barony of Chungu. While Emperor Wu was still Prince of Jin, he named Fu Xuan Regular Cavalry Attendant-in-Ordinary. After the abdication his title was raised to viscount, and he was also given the concurrent appointment of Chief Commandant of Attendant Cavalry.
3
帝初即位，廣納直言，開不諱之路，玄及散騎常侍皇甫陶共掌諫職。 玄上疏曰：「臣聞先王之臨天下也，明其大教，長其義節。 道化隆于上，清議行於下，上下相奉，人懷義心。 亡秦蕩滅先王之制，以法術相禦，而義心亡矣。 近者魏武好法術，而天下貴刑名； 魏文慕通達，而天下賤守節。 其後綱維不攝，而虛無放誕之論盈於朝野，使天下無復清議，而亡秦之病復發於今。 陛下聖德，龍興受禪，弘堯、舜之化，開正直之路，體夏禹之至儉，綜殷周之典文，臣詠歎而已，將又奚言！ 惟未舉清遠有禮之臣，以敦風節； 未退虛鄙，以懲不恪，臣是以猶敢有言。 」詔報曰：「舉清遠有禮之臣者，此尤今之要也。 」乃使玄草詔進之。 玄復上疏曰：
As soon as the emperor ascended the throne, he encouraged blunt advice and cleared a path where nothing need be unsaid; Fu Xuan and Regular Cavalry Attendant Huangfu Tao jointly supervised remonstrance. Fu Xuan presented a memorial: "I have heard that when the sage-kings faced the realm, they made clear the great teachings and cultivated moral duty. The transforming influence of the Way flourished at court, while honest opinion held sway in the countryside; ruler and ruled reinforced each other, and every heart held fast to what was right. The lost house of Qin tore down the institutions of the ancient kings, ruled by legal technique alone, and the moral sense of the people collapsed. In Wei times Emperor Wu doted on legalist methods, and the empire learned to prize harsh statutory doctrine; Emperor Wen admired fashionable cleverness and loose liberality, and the empire came to scorn steadfast integrity. After that the bonds of moral order slackened; idle talk of voidness and reckless license flooded court and capital until honest public judgment vanished—the malady of Qin has returned in our own age. Your Majesty's virtue, like a dragon's rise at the acceptance of the mandate, carries forward the reforming example of Yao and Shun, opens the way of honest counsel, embraces the deep austerity of Yu, and gathers the classical heritage of Yin and Zhou—I can only stand back in admiration; what is left for me to say? Only this remains: you have not yet elevated officials who are principled, aloof, and ceremonious, so as to shore up moral bearing; nor have you yet cleared away the shallow and mean to chastise slack conduct—which is why I still presume to speak." The reply edict said: "Raising up principled, courteous men is precisely what the times require most. " The emperor then told Fu Xuan to draft the edict and advance it. Fu Xuan submitted another long memorial:
4
書奏，帝下詔曰：「二常侍懇懇於所論，可謂乃心欲佐益時事者也。 而主者率以常制裁之，豈得不使發憤耶！ 二常侍所論，或舉其大較而未備其條目，亦可便令作之，然後主者八坐廣共研精。 凡關言於人主，人臣之所至難。 而人主若不能虛心聽納，自古忠臣直士之所慷慨，至使杜口結舌。 每念於此，未嘗不歎息也。 故前詔敢有直言，勿有所距，庶幾得以發懞補過，獲保高位。 苟言有偏善，情在忠益，雖文辭有謬誤，言語有失得，皆當曠然恕之。 古人猶不拒誹謗，況皆善意在可採錄乎！ 近者孔晁、綦毋龢皆案以輕慢之罪，所以皆原，欲使四海知區區之朝無諱言之忌也。 」俄遷侍中。
When the paper reached the throne, the emperor issued an edict: "The two Regular Attendants have argued their case with real earnestness; plainly they mean to help set the age to rights. Yet the responsible offices keep trapping them in routine precedent—small wonder they boil with frustration! Their memorial may give the broad outline without every detail; have them expand it into a full draft, then let the relevant departments and the Eight Seats thrash it out together. Speaking truth to a sovereign is about the hardest duty a subject can bear. If the ruler will not listen with an open mind, loyal men through the ages have pressed their case until, despairing, they clammed up entirely. Whenever I think of it, I cannot help sighing. That is why the earlier edict invited blunt counsel with nothing turned away—so honest mistakes could surface, faults be repaired, and high office kept secure. If a statement has something worthwhile in it and the motive is loyal service, then even clumsy wording or a careless phrase ought to be met with generous forgiveness. The ancients did not shut their ears even to bitter criticism—how much less should we shut out advice offered in good faith! Not long ago Kong Chao and Qi Wu He were charged with disrespect; both were forgiven on purpose, so the empire would see that this court does not fear plain speech." Soon afterward Fu Xuan was promoted to Palace Attendant.
5
初，玄進皇甫陶，及入而抵，玄以事與陶爭，言喧嘩，為有司所奏，二人竟坐免官。 俄復為御史中丞。 時頗有水旱之災，玄復上疏曰：
Earlier Fu Xuan had recommended Huangfu Tao; after Tao took office the two were at odds. They quarreled aloud over policy until the authorities impeached them, and both men lost their posts. Before long he was reappointed Palace Assistant Clerk. Floods and droughts had become frequent; Fu Xuan once more laid a memorial before the throne:
6
詔曰：「得所陳便宜，言農事得失及水官興廢，又安邊禦胡政事寬猛之宜，申省周備，一二具之，此誠為國大本，當今急務也。 如所論皆善，深知乃心，廣思諸宜，動靜以聞也。」
An edict answered: "The practical measures you cite—agriculture's strengths and failures, which waterworks to keep or abolish, how to secure the frontier against the Hu and calibrate strict versus lenient rule—you have spelled out item by item. These are indeed the state's foundation and the business that cannot wait. If your proposals hold up, we credit your sincerity; think through every fitting remedy and keep us informed as circumstances shift."
7
五年，遷太僕。 時比年不登，羌胡擾邊，詔公卿會議。 玄應對所問，陳事切直，雖不盡施行，而常見優容。 轉司隸校尉。
In the fifth year he was promoted to Grand Coachman. Harvests had failed year after year and Qiang and Hu raiders harried the frontier; an edict summoned the highest ministers to council. Fu Xuan replied to each question in blunt, uncompromising terms. Not every suggestion was adopted, yet the throne generally indulged him. He was transferred to Metropolitan Governor.
8
獻皇后崩于弘訓宮，設喪位。 舊制，司隸於端門外坐，在諸卿上，絕席。 其入殿，按本品秩在諸卿下，以次坐，不絕席。 而謁者以弘訓宮為殿內，制玄位在卿下。 玄恚怒，厲聲色而責謁者。 謁者妄稱尚書所處，玄對百僚而罵尚書以下。 御史中丞庾純奏玄不敬，玄又自表不以實，坐免官。 然玄天性峻急，不能有所容； 每有奏劾，或值日暮，捧白簡，整簪帶，竦踴不寐，坐而待旦。 於是貴遊懾伏，臺閣生風。 尋卒于家，時年六十二，諡曰剛。
When Empress Dowager Xian died in Hongxun Palace, mourning stations were arranged. By longstanding rule the Metropolitan Governor sat outside the Duan Gate above the chief ministers, on his own mat. Inside the hall, according to rank he took his place below the ministers in sequence and shared the common seating. The court herald treated Hongxun Palace as "within the hall precinct" and assigned Fu Xuan a seat beneath the ministers. Fu Xuan flushed with rage and dressed down the herald in a thundering voice. The herald falsely said the Secretariat had fixed the seating; Fu Xuan then berated everyone from the Secretariat downward in front of the full assembly. Palace Assistant Clerk Yu Chun impeached Fu Xuan for lèse-majesté; Fu Xuan's own statement did not match the facts, and he was stripped of office. Yet Fu Xuan's temperament was harsh and impatient—he suffered no opposition. Whenever he framed an impeachment—even if night was falling—he would clutch his white accusation slip, straighten cap and sash, sit bolt upright without sleeping, and wait for dawn. The great families quailed; a chill wind seemed to blow through the ministries. He died at home not long after, aged sixty-two, with the posthumous epithet Gang, "Relentless."
9
玄少時避難於河內，專心誦學，後雖顯貴，而著述不廢。 撰論經國九流及三史故事，評斷得失，各為區例，名為《傅子》，為內、外、中篇，凡有四部、六錄，合百四十首，數十萬言，並文集百餘卷行於世。 玄初作內篇成，子咸以示司空王沈。 沈與玄書曰：「省足下所著書，言富理濟，經綸政體，存重儒教，足以塞楊、墨之流遁，齊孫、孟于往代。 每開卷，未嘗不歎息也。 『不見賈生，自以過之，乃今不及』，信矣！」
In his youth Fu Xuan had taken refuge in Henei and immersed himself in scholarship; though he rose to high rank, he never stopped writing. He wrote on ordering the realm, the nine schools of thought, and episodes drawn from the three histories, weighing right and wrong and sorting each topic—compiled as the Master Fu in inner, outer, and middle books: four parts, six registers, one hundred forty chapters and several hundred thousand characters, plus another hundred-odd fascicles of collected prose that circulated widely. When the inner books were complete, his son Fu Xian showed them to Wang Shen, the Minister of Works. Wang Shen wrote to him: "I have read your work: it is ample in argument and sound on policy; it maps the machinery of government and honors Confucian teaching—enough to shut down the evasions of Yang Zhu and Mozi and to set Sunzi and Mencius beside the worthies of old. Every time I open the manuscript I cannot help sighing. "'Without meeting Jia Yi I fancied myself his better—now I see I am not.' That verdict was just!"

子咸
Fu Xian.
13
咸字長虞，剛簡有大節。 風格峻整，識性明悟，疾惡如仇，推賢樂善，常慕季文子、仲山甫之志。 好屬文論，雖綺麗不足，而言成規鑒。 潁川庾純常歎曰：「長虞之文近乎詩人之作矣！」
His courtesy name was Changyu; he was austere, upright, and possessed of strong moral principle. His deportment was stern and disciplined, his judgment lucid; he hated wickedness as he would a personal foe, lifted up the worthy, rejoiced in goodness, and modeled himself on Ji Wenzi and Zhong Shanfu. He loved to write essays; they were never ornate, yet every line carried the force of a standard and a mirror. Yu Chun of Yingchuan used to exclaim, "Changyu's prose stands almost with the poets!"
14
咸甯初，襲父爵，拜太子洗馬，累遷尚書右丞。 出為冀州刺史，繼母杜氏不肯隨咸之官，自表解職。 三旬之間，遷司徒左長史。 時帝留心政事，詔訪朝臣政之損益。 咸上言曰：「陛下處至尊之位，而修布衣之事，親覽萬機，勞心日昃。 在昔帝王，躬自菲薄，以利天下，未有逾陛下也。 然泰始開元以暨於今，十有五年矣。 而軍國未豐，百姓不贍，一歲不登便有菜色者，誠由官眾事殷，復除猥濫，蠶食者多而親農者少也。 臣以頑疏，謬忝近職，每見聖詔以百姓饑饉為慮，無能雲補，伏用慚恧，敢不自竭，以對天問。 舊都督有四，今並監軍，乃盈於十。 夏禹敷土，分為九州，今之刺史，幾向一倍。 戶口比漢十分之一，而置郡縣更多。 空校牙門，無益宿衛，而虛立軍府，動有百數。 五等諸侯，復坐置官屬。 諸所寵給，皆生於百姓。 一夫不農，有受其饑，今之不農，不可勝計。 縱使五稼普收，僅足相接； 暫有災患，便不繼贍。 以為當今之急，先並官省事，靜事息役，上下用心，惟農是務也。」
Early in the Xianning era he inherited his father's noble rank, became Horsewasher to the heir apparent, and rose through several posts to Right Assistant Director of the Masters of Writing. When he went out as Inspector of Ji Province, his stepmother Lady Du refused to travel with him; he therefore asked to resign. Within thirty days he was promoted to Senior Clerk on the left of the Minister of Education. The emperor was then actively engaged with governance and ordered his ministers to say what helped or hurt statecraft. Fu Xian replied: "You hold the highest throne yet busy yourself like a common clerk, personally tending every thread of government from dawn past midday. Among past emperors who tightened their belts for the realm's sake, none has outdone you. Yet from the founding Taishi era to the present, fifteen years have passed. Still the army and treasury run thin and the common people have no margin; one bad harvest and faces turn hungry—because offices multiply and paperwork swells, tax favors are handed out indiscriminately, parasites abound, and few tend the soil themselves. I am thick-witted and unworthy of the place near you; whenever an edict voices concern over famine I have nothing to offer and hang my head in shame—yet I dare answer your inquiry with all I have. There used to be four regional commanders-in-chief; now, counting army supervisors, there are more than ten. Yu smeared the flood-soaked earth and carved nine provinces; today's inspectors are nearly twice as many. Registered households are barely one-tenth of Han levels, yet we maintain even more commanderies and counties. Idle detachments at the Ya gates do nothing for palace security, yet phantom army offices sprawl by the hundreds. The ranked nobles again maintain full administrative retinues. Every stipend they enjoy is wrung from common households. When one man leaves the plough someone goes hungry; today the idle cannot even be counted. Even if every crop ripened well it would only barely cover consumption; the instant calamity strikes there is nothing in reserve. I believe the pressing task is to consolidate offices, cut redundant duties, still disturbances, and ease corvée so that everyone from the throne down fixes mind and muscle on farming alone."
15
咸在位多所執正。 豫州大中正夏侯駿上言，魯國小中正、司空司馬孔毓，四移病所，不能接賓，求以尚書郎曹馥代毓，旬日復上毓為中正。 司徒三卻，駿故據正。 咸以駿與奪惟意，乃奏免駿大中正。 司徒魏舒，駿之姻屬，屢卻不署，咸據正甚苦。 舒終不從，咸遂獨上。 舒奏咸激訕不直，詔轉咸為車騎司馬。
In office Fu Xian repeatedly stood his ground on what was right. The Greater Provincial Evaluator for Yu, Xiahou Jun, reported that Kong Yu—junior evaluator for Lu and secretary to the Minister of Works—had changed his sick-leave residence four times and could no longer receive visitors; Jun asked to replace him with Cao Fu of the Masters of Writing, then ten days later nominated Kong Yu again. The Minister of Education rejected the paperwork three times running; Jun dug in on what he saw as proper procedure. Fu Xian argued that Jun was handing out posts on whim and memorialized to strip him of the Greater Evaluator title. Minister Wei Shu—Jun's kinsman by marriage—kept refusing to countersign; Fu Xian hammered away at procedural justice. Wei Shu still would not yield, so Fu Xian forwarded the memorial on his own. Wei Shu accused Fu Xian of inflammatory disrespect; an edict reassigned him as Chief Clerk to the Chief Commandant of Cavalry.
16
咸以世俗奢侈，又上書曰：「臣以為穀帛難生，而用之不節，無緣不匱。 故先王之化天下，食肉衣帛，皆有其制。 竊謂奢侈之費，甚於天災。 古者堯有茅茨，今之百姓競豐其屋。 古者臣無玉食，今之賈豎皆厭粱肉。 古者后妃乃有殊飾，今之婢妾被服綾羅。 古者大夫乃不徒行，今之賤隸乘輕驅肥。 古者人稠地狹而有儲蓄，由於節也； 今者土廣人稀而患不足，由於奢也。 欲時之儉，當詰其奢； 奢不見詰，轉相高尚。 昔毛玠為吏部尚書，時無敢好衣美食者。 魏武帝歎曰：『孤之法不如毛尚書。 』令使諸部用心，各如毛玠，風俗之移，在不難矣。 」又議移縣獄於郡及二社應立，朝廷從之。 遷尚書左丞。
Seeing society sunk in extravagance, Fu Xian submitted again: "Grain and cloth do not come easily; spend without restraint and shortage follows inevitably. The ancient kings who civilized the realm set strict rules even for eating meat and wearing silk. I would argue that waste costs more than flood or drought. Yao's hall was thatched straw; the common people now outdo one another in grand houses. Once, only the great ate off jade; today even peddlers turn up their noses at fine millet and meat. In old days only palace ladies wore rare finery; now every chambermaid swathes herself in silk. Once, ministers did not go on foot; now the lowliest grooms trot out in light chars and plump ponies. Antiquity was crowded and cramped, yet granaries held surplus—because people practiced restraint. Now land lies wide and population thin, yet want persists—because extravagance drains everything. If we want an age of thrift, we must confront waste head-on; left unchecked, ostentation becomes a contest of one-upmanship. When Mao Jie ran the Ministry of Personnel, no one dared dress richly or dine sumptuously. Cao Cao once sighed, "My statutes cannot compare with Minister Mao's discipline." "If every bureau applied Mao Jie's zeal, reforming morals would be easy." He further urged relocating county jails to commandery seats and erecting the twin altars to soil and grain; the court agreed. He rose to Left Assistant Director of the Masters of Writing.
17
惠帝即位，楊駿輔政。 咸言於駿曰：「事與世變，禮隨時宜，諒暗之不行尚矣。 由世道彌薄，權不可假，故雖斬焉在疚，而躬覽萬機也。 逮至漢文，以天下體大，服重難久，遂制既葬而除。 世祖武皇帝雖大孝蒸蒸，亦從時釋服，制心喪三年，至於萬機之事，則有不遑。 今聖上欲委政於公，諒暗自居，此雖謙讓之心，而天下未以為善。 天下未以為善者，以億兆顒顒，戴仰宸極，聽於冢宰，懼天光有蔽。 人心既已若此，而明公處之固未為易也。 竊謂山陵之事既畢，明公當思隆替之宜。 周公聖人，猶不免謗。 以此推之，周公之任既未易而處，況聖上春秋非成王之年乎！ 得意忘言，言未易盡。 苟明公有以察其悾款，言豈在多。 」時司隸荀愷從兄喪，自表赴哀，詔聽之而未下，愷乃造駿。 咸因奏曰：「死喪之戚，兄弟孔懷。 同堂亡隕，方在信宿，聖恩矜憫，聽使臨喪。 詔未下而便以行造，急諂媚之敬，無友于之情。 宜加顯貶，以隆風教。 」帝以駿管朝政，有詔不問，駿甚憚之。 咸復與駿箋諷切之，駿意稍折，漸以不平。 由是欲出為京兆、弘農太守，駿甥李斌說駿，不宜斥出正人，乃止。 駿弟濟素與咸善，與咸書曰：「江海之流混混，故能成其深廣也。 天下大器，非可稍了，而相觀每事欲了。 生子癡，了官事，官事未易了也。 了事正作癡，復為快耳！ 左丞總司天臺，維正八坐，此未易居。 以君盡性而處未易居之任，益不易也。 想慮破頭，故具有白。 」咸答曰：「衛公云酒色之殺人，此甚於作直。 坐酒色死，人不為悔。 逆畏以直致禍，此由心不直正，欲以苟且為明哲耳！ 自古以直致禍者，當自矯枉過直，或不忠允，欲以亢厲為聲，故致忿耳。 安有空空為忠益，而當見疾乎！ 」居無何，駿誅。 咸轉為太子中庶子，遷御史中丞。
Emperor Hui took the throne with Yang Jun steering policy. Fu Xian told Yang Jun: "Times change, and ritual must keep pace—full palace seclusion during mourning has long since ceased to be workable. As public morality thins, power cannot be delegated; even wrapped in mourning one must still grasp every lever of rule oneself. Emperor Wen of Han faced an empire too vast for endless sackcloth; he settled on shedding mourning garments once burial ended. Emperor Wu showed towering filial piety, yet he too shortened formal mourning to suit the age—three years of restrained grief—because government business would not wait. Now the sovereign proposes to hand you the reins while he retires into mourning withdrawal—it sounds humble, but the realm reads it as wrongheaded. They crane their necks toward the throne; they dread hearing policy filter only through a chief minister while the emperor's light stays hidden. With opinion running this way, Your Honor walks an impossible tightrope. Once the late emperor rests in his tomb, you must weigh what consolidation—or collapse—requires. Even the Duke of Zhou, a sage, faced backbiters. If holding the Duke of Zhou's mandate was treacherous even then, think how much harder now—the emperor is no child-king Cheng. Prince Zhao Lun made him Palace Secretariat Director to steady nerves. He pleaded illness—Sima Lun sent censors to haul him to his desk. Wang Rong and Chen Zhun said, "With Fu Zhi on duty we sleep soundly. The realm leaned on him that heavily.
31
倫篡，又為右光祿、開府，加侍中。 惠帝還宮，祗以經受偽職請退，不許。 初，倫之篡也，孫秀與義陽王威等十餘人預撰儀式禪文。 及倫敗，齊王冏收侍中劉逵、常侍騶捷、杜育、黃門郎陸機、右丞周導、王尊等付廷尉。 以禪文出中書，復議處祗罪，會赦得原。 後以禪文草本非祗所撰，於是詔復光祿大夫。 子宣，尚弘農公主。
When Sima Lun seized the throne Fu Zhi became Right Grand Counselor with independent headquarters. Emperor Hui's return left Fu Zhi asking to resign for serving the usurper—the court refused. During Sima Lun's coup Sun Xiu and Prince Sima Wei pre-wrote the abdication liturgy. When Sima Lun fell, Prince Sima Min jailed Liu Kui, Lu Ji, and others. Because the abdication edict came from the Secretariat they debated Fu Zhi's guilt—an amnesty spared him. Later they learned Fu Zhi never penned the draft—his Grand Counselor title returned. His son Fu Xuan married the Princess of Hongnong.
32
尋遷太子少傅，上章遜位還第。 及成都王穎為太傅，復以祗為少傅，加侍中。 懷帝即位，遷光祿大夫、侍中，未拜，加右僕射、中書監。 時太傅東海王越輔政，祗既居端右，每宣君臣謙光之道，由此上下雍穆。 祗明達國體，朝廷制度多所經綜。 曆左光祿、開府，行太子太傅，侍中如故。 疾篤遜位，不許。 遷司徒，以足疾，詔版輿上殿，不拜。
Soon Junior Tutor—he begged to retire home. When Prince Chengdu Ying became Grand Tutor he recalled Fu Zhi as Junior Tutor with Palace Attendant. Emperor Huai promoted him toward Grand Counselor and Secretariat Director before the seals arrived. While Prince Donghai Yue ruled, Fu Zhi held the chief seat and preached humility between sovereign and ministers. Fu Zhi mastered state ritual and shaped institutions. He rotated through Left Grand Counselor with headquarters, acting heir's tutor, keeping Palace Attendant. Deathly ill he offered to resign—the throne refused. Made Minister of Education on a litter—excused from bows.
33
大將軍苟晞表請遷都，使祗出詣河陰，修理舟楫，為水行之備。 及洛陽陷沒，遂共建行台，推祗為盟主，以司徒、持節、大都督諸軍事傳檄四方。 遣子宣將公主與尚書令和郁赴告方伯征義兵，祗自屯盟津小城，宣弟暢行河陰令，以待宣。 祗以暴疾薨，時年六十九。 祗自以義誠不終，力疾手筆敕厲其二子宣、暢，辭旨深切，覽者莫不感激慷慨。 祗著文章駁論十餘萬言。
General Gou Xi urged relocating the court and sent Fu Zhi to Heyin to ready boats. After Luoyang fell allies built a mobile command and named Fu Zhi covenant leader as Minister of Education and supreme commander. He sent Fu Xuan and He Yu to rally governors while he held Meng ford; younger son Chang governed Heyin pending reinforcements. Fu Zhi died suddenly at sixty-nine. Feeling his loyal mission unfinished he scribbled dying orders to Fu Xuan and Fu Chang—every reader wept. He left more than a hundred thousand characters of essays and rebuttals.
34
宣字世弘。 年六歲喪繼母，哭泣如成人，中表異之。 及長，好學，趙王倫以為相國掾、尚書郎、太子中舍人，遷司徒西曹掾。 去職，累遷為秘書丞、驃騎從事中郎。 惠帝至自長安，以宣為左丞，不就，遷黃門郎。 懷帝即位，轉吏部郎，又為御史中丞。 卒年四十九，無子，以暢子沖為嗣。
Fu Xuan bore the courtesy name Shihong. At six he mourned his stepmother like a grown man—kin marveled. Adult, studious, Sima Lun named him to staff posts until Senior Clerk on the Minister of Education's western bureau. Leaving office he rose to secretary director and adjutant to the chief cavalry command. When Emperor Hui returned from Chang'an he offered Fu Xuan Left Assistant Director—Fu Xuan declined—then named Yellow Gate Gentleman. Under Emperor Huai he rotated through the Masters of Writing and again became Palace Assistant Clerk. He died at forty-nine without sons; his nephew Fu Chong, Fu Chang's boy, inherited his line.
35
暢字世道。 年五歲，父友見而戲之，解暢衣，取其金環與侍者，暢不之惜，以此賞之。 年未弱冠，甚有重名。 以選入侍講東宮，為秘書丞。 尋沒于石勒，勒以為大將軍右司馬。 諳識朝儀，恆居機密，勒甚重之。 作《晉諸公敘贊》二十二卷，又為《公卿故事》九卷。 卒。 子詠，過江為交州刺史、太子右率。
Fu Chang bore the courtesy name Shidao. At five a friend of his father teased him: stripping his jacket and pocketing his gold ring for a servant—Fu Chang shrugged it off, and the man praised his aplomb. Before his capping ceremony he was already celebrated. Chosen as lecturer in the heir's palace, he rose to Secretary Manager. Captured soon afterward by Shi Le, he was named senior clerk on Shi Le's staff. He mastered court ritual and Shi Le's inner councils; the warlord prized him. He wrote twenty-two fascicles of Narrative Encomia for Jin grandees and nine fascicles of Ducal Ministers' Precedents. He died. His son Fu Yong crossed south to serve as Inspector of Jiaozhou and Right Guard to the heir apparent.

Historians' Commentary
37
史臣曰：武帝覽觀四方，平章百姓，永言啟沃，任切爭臣。 傅玄體強直之姿，懷匪躬之操，抗辭正色，補闕弼違，諤諤當朝，不忝其職者矣。 及乎位居三獨，彈擊是司，遂能使台閣生風，貴戚斂手。 雖前代鮑、葛，何以加之！ 然而惟此褊心，乏弘雅之度，驟聞競爽，為物議所譏，惜哉！ 古人取戒于韋弦，良有以也。 長虞風格凝峻，弗墜家聲。 及其納諫汝南，獻書臨晉，居諒直之地，有先見之明矣。 傅祗名父之子，早樹風猷，崎嶇危亂之朝，匡救君臣之際，卒能保全祿位，可謂有道存焉。
The historians write: Emperor Wu scanned the realm and steadied the common people; he hungered for honest counsel and leaned hard on his remonstrators. Fu Xuan carried himself with iron integrity, risking everything to speak plain truth and plug policy gaps—he was every inch the remonstrator his post demanded. Once he joined the Three Independents and turned impeachment into his métier, the ministries trembled and great clans flinched. Not even Han's famed scourges Bao Xuan and Ge Gong outdid him. Yet his temper ran narrow and lacked magnanimity; his readiness to clash drew ridicule—what a pity! The ancients prized both taut bow and yielding strap as reminders—his story proves why. Fu Xian's stern dignity upheld the Fu name. When he pressed Prince Runan with counsel and laid strategy at Linjin, his blunt station proved prophetic. Fu Zhi, heir to a celebrated father, charted his own course through chaos, steadying sovereign and ministers alike and keeping honor and office—a man in whom the Way clearly rested.
