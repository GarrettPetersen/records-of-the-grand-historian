1
高瓊，家世燕人。 祖霸，父乾。 五代時，李景據江南，潛結契丹，歲遣單使往復。 霸將契丹之命，以乾從行使景。 方至江左，諜間北使與中夏構隙，以紓疆場之難，遂殺霸，居乾濠州，聲言為汴人所殺。 乾在濠州生三子，以江左蹙弱，尋挈族歸中朝，給田亳州之蒙城，因土著焉。
Gao Qiong came from a family of Yan men. His grandfather was Ba and his father Qian. During the Five Dynasties, Li Jing controlled the lands south of the Yangzi and maintained a clandestine tie with the Khitan, dispatching sole envoys to and fro each year. Ba bore the Khitan commission, with Qian accompanying him as envoy to Li Jing. When they reached the Jiangnan region, agents provoked a breach between the northern envoy and the Song court to relieve pressure on the frontier. They killed Ba, detained Qian at Haozhou, and spread word that Bian men had slain him. While at Haozhou Qian fathered three sons. Finding the southland increasingly precarious, he soon returned with his kin to the Song court, received farmland at Mengcheng in Bozhou, and made his home there.
2
瓊少勇鷙無賴，為盜，事敗，將磔於市，暑雨創潰，伺守者稍怠，即掣釘而遁。 事王審琦，太宗尹京邑，知其材勇，召置帳下。 太宗嘗侍宴禁中，甚醉，及退，太祖送至苑門。 時瓊與戴興、王超、李斌、桑讚從，瓊左手執靮，右手執鐙，太宗乃能乘馬。 太祖顧瓊等壯之，因賜以控鶴官衣帶及器帛，且勖令盡心焉。
As a youth Qiong was violent and unruly. Caught as a robber and condemned to public dismemberment, he had the good fortune of a summer downpour that reopened his wounds. When his jailers relaxed their watch, he tore loose the nails and fled. He entered the service of Wang Shenqi. When Prince Taizong governed the capital district and learned of his ability and valor, he took Qiong into his personal guard. Once, after drinking heavily at a palace feast, Taizong withdrew so intoxicated that Taizu escorted him as far as the imperial garden gate. Qiong was among those attending—Dai Xing, Wang Chao, Li Bin, and Sang Zan—and held the bridle in his left hand and the stirrup in his right so that Taizong could mount. Taizu glanced back, impressed by their sturdy presence, and rewarded them with the insignia, robes, and gifts of the Crane-Control Guard, exhorting them to give their utmost in service.
3
會有龍騎亡命卒數十人，因知州臧丙出郊，謀劫其導從以叛。 瓊聞即白丙，趣還城，因自率從卒數十人，挾弓矢單騎追捕，至榆林村，及之。 賊入村後舍，登牆以拒。 賊首青腳狼者注弩將射瓊，瓊引弓一發斃之，遂悉擒送於州。 丙上其事。 會將北伐，召歸。 授馬步軍都軍頭、領薊州刺史、樓船戰棹都指揮使，步船千艘赴雄州。 又城易州。 師還，為天武右廂都指揮使、領本州團練使。
Some thirty-odd deserters from the Dragon Cavalry learned that Prefect Zang Bing was leaving the city and planned to ambush his escort and rise in revolt. Qiong at once informed Bing and urged him back to the city, then set out with some forty men, bow in hand, riding alone in pursuit until he overtook the fugitives at Yulin Village. The rebels barricaded themselves in a cottage at the rear of the village and fought from atop its wall. Their leader, known as Green-Foot Wolf, aimed a crossbow at Qiong; Qiong answered with a single arrow and brought him down, then took the entire band prisoner and delivered them to the prefectural authorities. Bing reported the exploit to the throne. When preparations began for a northern expedition, he was summoned back to court. He received appointment as chief of the combined horse-and-foot forces, concurrent prefect of Jizhou, and commander of tower ships, and sailed a thousand craft to Xiongzhou. He further built up the defenses of Yizhou. After the campaign he was made commander of the right wing of the Heavenly Martial Guard and regimental commissioner of his home prefecture.
4
端拱初，遷左廂，改領富州團練使。 是秋，出為單州防禦使，改貝州部署。 其出守也，與范廷召、王超、孔守正並命焉。 數月，廷召等皆復補兵職，瓊頗悒悒。 時王承衍鎮貝丘，公主每入禁中，頗知上於瓊厚，承衍每寬慰之。 二年，召還。 故事，廉察以上入朝，始有茶藥之賜，至是特賜瓊焉。 三月，遷朔、易帥臣，制授瓊侍衛步軍都指揮使、領歸義軍節度，廷召輩始加觀察使，不得與瓊比。 出為幷州馬步軍都部署，時潘美亦在太原，舊制，節度使領軍職者居上，瓊以美舊臣，表請居其下，從之。 戍兵有以廩食陳腐嘩言者，瓊知之，一日，出巡諸營，士卒方聚食，因取其飯自啖之，謂眾曰：「今邊鄙無警，爾等坐飽甘豐，宜知幸也。 」眾言遂息。 改鎮州都部署。 至道中，就改保大軍節度，典軍如故。
Early in the Duangong reign he moved to command of the left wing and took the regimental commission at Fuzhou. That autumn he was posted out as military defender of Danzhou, then reassigned as deployment commissioner at Beizhou. Fan Tingzhao, Wang Chao, and Kong Shouzheng received provincial postings at the same time he did. Within months his peers were all back in active military command, while Qiong brooded over his seeming demotion. Wang Chengyan was then posted at Beiqu. The princess, on her visits to court, knew how highly the emperor esteemed Qiong, and Chengyan repeatedly reassured him. In the second year of the reign he was recalled to the capital. By custom only provincial surveillance commissioners and higher ranks received the imperial gifts of tea and medicine on entering court; Qiong was exceptionally granted them on this occasion. In the third month, as frontier commanders were reshuffled, Qiong was appointed commander of the palace guard infantry and military governor of the Guiyi Army—ranks that Tingzhao and the others, newly promoted only to surveillance commissioner, could not match. Posted as deployment commissioner of Bingzhou's horse and foot forces, he found Pan Mei already at Taiyuan. Though military governors normally outranked such posts, Qiong petitioned to serve beneath the senior Mei, and the request was approved. When garrison troops grumbled about spoiled rations, Qiong rode out to inspect the camps. Finding soldiers at their meal, he took a bowl and ate with them, telling the men: "The border is quiet and your bellies are full—you ought to count yourselves fortunate." The muttering subsided at once. He was reassigned as deployment commissioner at Zhenzhou. During the Zhidao reign he was further appointed military governor of the Baoda Army while retaining command of the troops.
5
真宗即位，加彰信軍節度，充太宗山陵部署，復為幷代都部署。 咸平中，契丹犯塞，其母車帳至狼山大夏。 上親巡河朔，遣楊允恭馳往，召瓊率所部出土門，與石保吉會鎮、定。 既而傅潛以逗留得罪，即召瓊代之。 兵罷，復還本任。 轉運使言其政績，詔褒之。
Upon Zhenzong's accession, Qiong received the additional post of military governor of the Zhangxin Army, oversaw construction of Taizong's tomb, and was again placed in command of the Bing and Dai deployment. During Xianping the Khitan breached the border, and the empress dowager's camp advanced as far as Langshan and Daxia. The emperor toured Hebei in person and sent Yang Yonggong racing ahead to summon Qiong to march his troops through Tumen Pass and rendezvous with Shi Baoji at Zhenzhou and Dingzhou. Fu Qian was soon convicted of dallying on campaign, and Qiong was called up to take his place. After the fighting he resumed his original command. The transport commissioner commended his record of governance, and the throne issued a decree of praise.
6
咸平三年，代還，以手創不任持笏，詔執梃入謁，授殿前都指揮使。 先是，范廷召、桑讚所將邊兵臨敵退衄，言者請罪之。 以問瓊，瓊對曰：「兵違將令，於法當誅。 然陛下去歲已釋其罪，今復行之，又方屯諸路，非時代易，臣恐眾心疑懼。 」乃止。
In Xianping 3, returning from a tour of duty, he could not grip the court tablet because of an old wound to his hand; the emperor permitted him to attend audience with a staff and appointed him commander of the palace front guard. Earlier, Fan Tingzhao and Sang Zan had withdrawn their border forces in the face of the enemy, and memorialists demanded they be punished. Asked for his view, Qiong answered: "By military law, troops who defy their generals deserve death." Yet Your Majesty pardoned them only last year. To punish them again now, with armies still stationed on every frontier and no change of command imminent, would only breed fear and distrust among the ranks. The proposal was abandoned.
7
景德中，車駕北巡。 時前軍已與敵接戰，上欲親臨營壘，或勸南還，瓊曰：「敵師已老，陛下宜親往，以督其成。 」上悅，即日進幸澶淵。 明年，以罷兵，料簡兵卒諸班直十年者出補軍校，年老者退為本班剩員。 瓊進曰：「此非激勸之道，宿衛豈不勞乎？ 」自是八年者皆得敘補焉。
During Jingde the emperor marched north in person. The vanguard was already fighting when the emperor proposed visiting the front. Some counseled retreat to the south, but Qiong said: "The enemy is exhausted. Your Majesty should go yourself and drive the victory home." Delighted, the emperor pressed on to Chanyuan that very day. The following year, with peace restored, guardsmen of ten years' service were sifted to fill junior officer posts, while older men were retired as surplus members of their original units. Qiong objected: "That is no way to reward loyalty. Is palace duty not labor enough in itself?" Thereafter guardsmen with eight years of service were also eligible for promotion.
8
馬軍都校葛霸權步軍司，會以疾在告，令瓊兼領二司。 瓊從容上言曰：「臣衰老，儻又有犬馬之疾，則須一將總此二職。 臣事先朝時，侍衛都虞候以上常至十員，職位相亞，易於遷改，且使軍伍熟其名望，邊藩緩急，亦可選用。 」上深然之。 未幾，以久疾求解兵柄，授檢校太尉、忠武軍節度。 三年冬，疾甚，上欲親臨問之，宰相不可，乃止。 卒，年七十二，贈侍中。
Ge Ba of the horse guard was acting chief of the foot guard bureau; when illness kept him away, Qiong was ordered to command both services. Qiong submitted a measured memorial: "I am aging. Should illness strike me again, one general ought to be ready to hold both commands." In the previous reign there were often as many as ten officers from chief commandant of the palace guard upward. Their ranks were nearly equal, promotion was straightforward, the troops knew their names, and in a frontier crisis they could be drawn upon at once. The emperor strongly agreed. Soon, citing prolonged illness, he sought release from military command and was made honorary grand preceptor and military governor of the Zhongwu Army. That winter his illness worsened. The emperor wished to call on him personally, but the chief ministers dissuaded him. He died at seventy-two and was posthumously granted the title of palace attendant.
9
瓊不識字，曉達軍政，然頗自任，罕與副將參議。 善訓諸子：繼勳、繼宣、繼忠、繼密、繼和、繼隆、繼元。 繼勳、繼宣最知名。
Though illiterate, Qiong had a thorough grasp of military affairs; yet he was headstrong and seldom took counsel from his deputies. He raised his sons with discipline: Jixun, Jixuan, Jizhong, Jimi, Jihe, Jilong, and Jiyuan. Of these, Jixun and Jixuan were the best known.
10
子繼勳
His son Jixun
11
繼勳，字紹先，初補右班殿直。 儀狀頎偉，太宗見而異之，召問其家世，以瓊子對。 擢寄班祗候，累遷內殿崇班。
Jixun, courtesy name Shaoxian, first entered service as a right-company palace guard. Tall and striking in appearance, he caught Taizong's eye. Summoned and questioned about his lineage, he answered that he was Qiong's son. He was promoted to attendant of the imperial guard company and eventually rose to inner-hall honored company.
12
咸平初，王均據益州。 以崇儀副使為益州兵馬都監、提舉西川諸州軍巡檢公事。 招安使雷有終以兵五百授繼勳，守東郭二門，會賊攻彌牟砦，繼勳引兵轉鬥至嘉州，敗之，獲黃繖、金塗鎗以還。 有終益以勁兵復進攻二門，克之，乃建幟城上。 諸將知城拔，有終乃引軍薄天長門，賊復來拒戰。 會日暮，有終欲少休，繼勳曰：「賊窘矣，急擊之，無失也。 」率十數騎鏖戰，身被數創，血濡甲； 馬死，更馬以進。 會入內都知秦翰來援，賊退保子城，不敢出。 繼勳潛知賊欲夜遁，開圍使得潰去，均卒敗滅。 以功遷崇儀使。 賊餘黨保山藪中，時出剽劫，乃徙綿漢劍門路都巡檢使。 繼勳募惡少年偵賊動靜，窮躡岩穴，掩其不備，悉擒殺之。
Early in Xianping, Wang Jun rebelled and held Yizhou. As deputy commissioner of honored ceremonial he was made chief military supervisor of Yizhou and commissioner overseeing inspection of the armies of the western Sichuan prefectures. Pacification commissioner Lei Youzhong assigned Jixun five hundred men to hold the eastern outer gates. When rebels assaulted Mimu Fort, Jixun fought through to Jiazhou, routed them, and returned bearing a yellow parasol and gilded spears as trophies. Youzhong reinforced him with crack troops and again stormed the two gates. Once they fell, banners were raised on the walls. Learning the city had fallen, Youzhong pressed the army toward the Tianchang Gate, where the rebels sallied out to fight again. At dusk Youzhong wanted to pause, but Jixun urged: "The enemy is cornered. Press the attack now—do not let them escape." At the head of a dozen horsemen he plunged into the melee, taking several wounds until blood soaked his armor; when his horse was killed he mounted another and kept fighting. Reinforcements arrived under inner-palace director Qin Han. The rebels fell back into the inner citadel and dared not sally forth. Learning secretly that the rebels meant to flee by night, Jixun opened the ring and let them break out in confusion; Wang Jun was eventually crushed. For this service he was promoted to commissioner of honored ceremonial. Rebel remnants lurked in the hills and raided intermittently, so he was transferred to chief inspector of the Mian-Han-Jianmen route. Jixun enlisted hard cases among local youths to track the rebels, chased them into their mountain lairs, and catching them off guard killed them to the last man.
13
又徙峽路鈐轄，還朝，遷洛苑使、幷代州鈐轄。 徙屯岢嵐軍。 契丹聚兵五萬屯草城川，繼勳登高望之，謂軍使賈宗曰：「彼眾而陣不整，將不才也。 我兵雖少，可以奇取勝。 先伏兵山下，敵見我弱，必急攻我。 我誘之南走，爾起乘之，當大潰。 」轉戰至寒光嶺，伏發，契丹果敗，相蹂躪死者萬餘人，獲馬、牛、橐駝甚眾。 遷弓箭庫使，賜金帶、錦袍，領榮州刺史，徙麟、府州鈐轄。
He was next made controller of the gorges route, returned to court, and was promoted to director of the Luoyang park and controller of Bing and Dai. He was posted to garrison Keilan Army. When the Khitan concentrated fifty thousand men at Caocheng River, Jixun surveyed them from a height and told army commissioner Jia Zong: "They are numerous but their lines are ragged—their commander is no soldier." Though we are fewer, we can win by a surprise stroke. Hide men below the hill first. Seeing us weak, they will rush to attack. I will feign retreat to the south. You rise and strike from ambush, and they should break completely. Fighting through to Hanguang Ridge, the ambush was sprung and the Khitan routed. More than ten thousand perished in the stampede, and vast numbers of horses, cattle, and camels were taken. He was promoted to director of the bow and arrow store, given a gold belt and brocade robe, made concurrent prefect of Rongzhou, and transferred to controller of Lin and Fu.
14
時屯兵河外，饋運不屬。 繼勳扼兔毛川，援送軍食，師乃濟。 徙知環州，又徙瀛州。 時歲饑，募富人出粟以給貧者。 明年大稔，木生連理者四，郡人上治狀請留。 遷內藏庫使，以宮苑使奉使契丹。 還，知定州，遷西上閤門使、昭州團練使，徙鄜延路鈐轄，坐市馬虧價失官。 已而復為西上閤門使、榮州刺史、知冀州、領果州團練使。 徙貝州，復知瀛州。
Troops were then encamped west of the Yellow River with no steady supply line. Jixun seized control of Tumaocuan, convoyed grain to the army, and only then could the force cross. He was transferred to govern Huanzhou, then Yingzhou. In a year of famine he persuaded wealthy households to release grain for the poor. The next year brought a bumper harvest and four trees with fused trunks—a sign of good rule for which the people petitioned to keep him in office. Promoted to director of the inner treasury, he was sent to the Khitan as palace park commissioner. Returning, he governed Dingzhou, rose to western upper gate commissioner and regimental commissioner of Zhaozhou, then controller of the Fuyan route, but lost his post for underpaying in horse purchases. He was soon restored as western upper gate commissioner, prefect of Rongzhou, governor of Jizhou, and regimental commissioner of Guozhou. He was posted to Beizhou before returning to govern Yingzhou.
15
恭謝禮成，徙昭信軍節度使，為莊獻明肅太后山陵、莊懿太后園陵都總管，以老病乞骸骨。 召見便殿，許一子扶掖，俾勿拜，聽辭管軍。 授建雄軍節度使、知滑州。 河水暴溢，齧堤岸，繼勳雖老，躬自督役，露坐河上，暮夜猶不輟，水乃殺怒，滑人德之。 卒，年七十八，輟視朝一日，贈太尉。 繼勳性謙，有機略，善撫禦士卒，臨戰輒勝。 在蜀有威名，號「神將」。
After the thanksgiving rites he was made military governor of the Zhaoxin army and chief superintendent of the tombs of Empresses Zhuangxian Mingsu and Zhuangyi, then petitioned to retire on grounds of age and illness. Summoned to the side hall, he was permitted one son to steady him, excused from bowing, and allowed to relinquish command of the army. He was appointed military governor of the Jianxiong army and governor of Huazhou. When the Yellow River flooded and ate at the dikes, Jixun though aged supervised the repairs in person, sitting exposed on the bank day and night until the waters subsided. The people of Huazhou honored him for it. He died at seventy-eight. Court audiences were suspended for a day, and he was posthumously made grand preceptor. Jixun was modest, resourceful, and adept at winning soldiers' loyalty; in battle he was seldom defeated. In Sichuan he enjoyed such renown that men called him the Divine General.

子繼宣
His son Jixuan
17
繼宣，字舜舉。 幼善騎射，頗工筆劄，知讀書。 以恩補西頭供奉官、惠民河巡督漕船。 會歲饑多盜，兼沿河巡檢捉賊，遷閤門祗候、邠州兵馬都監。 曹瑋守邠，數與言兵，薦其可用。
Jixuan, courtesy name Shunju. From boyhood he excelled at riding and archery, wrote a fair hand, and had some learning. By privilege of birth he was made western attendant palace officer and inspector of grain barges on the Huimin Canal. In a famine year rife with banditry he was also made riverine inspector to hunt thieves, then promoted to gate attendant and chief military supervisor of Binzhou. Under Cao Wei's command at Bin he often talked strategy with him, and Wei recommended him as capable.
18
乾興初，以內殿崇班為益州都監。 蜀人富侈，元夕大張燈，知府薛奎戒以備盜，繼宣籍惡少年飲犒之，使夜中潛誌盜背，明日皆獲。 歷磁、相、邢、洺都巡檢使，知安肅軍，徙保州。 累遷禮賓使、益州路兵馬鈐轄。 還，為西上閤門使、涇原路鈐轄兼安撫使、知渭州，遷四方館使、昭州刺史、知雄州。
Early in Qianxing he was made inner-hall honored company and chief supervisor of Yizhou. The people of Shu were lavish, lighting the city on the Lantern Festival. Prefect Xue Kui warned of thieves. Jixuan enlisted local rowdies, wined and rewarded them, had them secretly mark thieves' backs in the night, and by morning had them all. He served as chief inspector of Ci, Xiang, Xing, and Ming, governed Ansu Army, and was transferred to Baozhou. He rose to commissioner of ceremonial reception and military controller of the Yizhou route. Returning to court he became western upper gate commissioner, military controller and pacification commissioner of the Jingyuan route and governor of Weizhou, then commissioner of the four directions hall, prefect of Zhaozhou, and governor of Xiongzhou.
19
初，元昊反，聲言侵關隴。 繼宣請備麟府。 未幾，羌兵果入寇河外，陷豐州。 擢捧日天武四廂都指揮使、恩州團練使、知幷州。 俄寇麟府，繼宣帥兵營陵井，抵天門關。 是夕大雨，及河，師半濟，黑凌暴合，舟不得進，乃具牲酒為文以禱。 已而凌解，師濟，進屯府谷，間遣勇士夜亂賊營。 又募黥配廂軍，得二千餘人，號清邊軍，命偏將王凱主之。 軍次三松嶺，賊數萬眾圍之，清邊軍奮起，斬首千餘級。 其相躪藉死者不可勝計。 築寧遠砦，相視地脈，鑿石出泉。 已而城五砦，遷眉州防禦使，卒。
When Yuan Hao first rebelled, he announced an invasion of Guan and Long. Jixuan petitioned to ready the defenses of Lin and Fu. Soon Tangut forces did cross the Yellow River and take Fengzhou. He was promoted to commander of the four wings of the Sun-Held Heavenly Martial Guard, regimental commissioner of Enzhou, and governor of Bingzhou. When the enemy soon raided Lin and Fu, Jixuan marched to encamp at Lingjing and reached Tianmen Pass. That night a downpour struck. At the river, with half the army across, black ice suddenly closed the channel and boats could not move. He offered sacrificial wine and composed a prayer. The ice soon parted and the army crossed. He advanced to Fugu and sent raiders by night to sow chaos in the enemy camp. He also recruited more than two thousand tattooed convicts from the wing armies into a force called the Clear-Border Army under deputy commander Wang Kai. At Sansong Ridge tens of thousands of enemy troops surrounded them. The Clear-Border Army fought back and took more than a thousand heads. Uncounted more died in the crush. He built Ningyuan Fort, read the terrain, and drilled rock until a spring appeared. He soon walled five forts, was transferred to defender of Meizhou, and died.
20
范廷召
Fan Tingzhao.
21
宋初，從平李筠、李重進，轉本班都知。 又從征太原，再轉散都頭、都虞候、領費州刺史。 太平興國中，以日騎軍都指揮使從平太原，征范陽。 秦王廷美嘗遣親吏閻懷忠、趙瓊犒禁軍列校，廷召預焉，坐出為唐州馬步軍都指揮使。
In the founding years of Song he helped suppress Li Jun and Li Chongjin and rose to chief of his original company. He campaigned again at Taiyuan and was twice promoted—to chief of the loose company, chief commandant, and concurrent prefect of Feizhou. During Taiping Xingguo, as commander of the sun-cavalry army he helped pacify Taiyuan and marched against Fanyang. When Prince of Qin Tingmei sent personal clerks Yan Huaizhong and Zhao Qiong to reward palace guard officers, Tingzhao was implicated and was banished to combined horse-and-foot command at Tangzhou.
22
雍熙三年，議北征，召入為馬步軍都軍頭、領平州刺史、幽州道前軍先鋒都指揮使。 與賊遇固安南，破其眾三千，斬首千餘級，克固安、新城二縣，乘勝下涿州。 廷召復與賊戰，中流矢，血漬甲縷，神色自若，督戰益急，詔褒之。 師還，遷日騎右廂都指揮使、領本州圍練使，又遷左廂，移領高州。 端拱初，出為齊州防禦使，數月，授捧日天武四廂都指揮使、領澄州防禦使。 二年，轉殿前都虞候、領涼州觀察使、鎮州副都部署。 大破契丹三萬眾於徐河，斬首數千級。
In Yongxi 3, as a northern campaign was planned, he was recalled as chief of combined horse and foot armies, concurrent prefect of Pingzhou, and vanguard commander on the Youzhou route. South of Gu'an he routed three thousand enemy troops, took more than a thousand heads, captured Gu'an and Xincheng, and pressed on to Zhuozhou. Tingzhao fought again, took an arrow through his armor, yet kept an unruffled face and drove the battle harder still. The throne issued praise. After the campaign he commanded the right wing of the sun-cavalry army and held the regimental commission of his prefecture, then shifted to the left wing and the regimental commission at Gaozhou. Early in Duangong he was posted as defender of Qizhou; within months he was made commander of the four wings of the Sun-Held Heavenly Martial Guard and defender of Chengzhou. In the second year he became chief commandant of the palace front guard, surveillance commissioner of Liangzhou, and deputy deployment commissioner at Zhenzhou. At Xu River he shattered thirty thousand Khitan and took several thousand heads.
23
淳化二年，為平虜橋砦都部署，歷幷代、環慶兩路副部署。 至道中，遣將從五路討李繼遷，命廷召副李繼隆為環慶靈都部署。 廷召出延州路，與賊遇白池，獲米募軍主吃囉等兵器、鎧甲數萬。 是役也，諸將失期，獨廷召與王超大小數十戰，屢克捷，上嘉之。 俄又為幷代兩路都部署。 三年，遷侍衛馬軍都指揮使、領河西軍節度，為定州行營都部署。
In Chunhua 2 he commanded Pingluqiao Fort and served as deputy deployment commissioner on the Bing-Dai and Huanqing routes. During Zhidao, as five columns marched against Li Jiqian, Tingzhao was made deputy to Li Jilong as Huanqing-Ling deployment commissioner. Tingzhao marched out on the Yanzhou route, met the enemy at Baichi, and captured tens of thousands of weapons and suits of armor, including the Mi-recruited chieftain Chiluo. In that campaign other generals missed their deadlines; only Tingzhao and Wang Chao fought dozens of actions and won repeatedly. The emperor commended them. He was soon made combined deployment commissioner of the Bing and Dai routes. In the third year he was promoted to commander of the palace guard cavalry, military governor of the Hexi army, and field headquarters deployment commissioner at Dingzhou.
24
咸平二年，契丹入塞，車駕北巡。 廷召與戰瀛州西，斬首二萬級，逐北至莫州東三十里，又斬首萬餘，奪其所掠老幼數萬口，契丹遁去。 師還，錄功加檢校太傅，益賦邑，又改殿前都指揮使。 四年正月被疾，車駕臨問，卒，年七十五，贈侍中。
In Xianping 2 the Khitan crossed the border and the emperor marched north. West of Yingzhou Tingzhao took twenty thousand heads, pursued to thirty li east of Mozhou, took another ten thousand, recovered tens of thousands of captives, and drove the Khitan off. On return his service was rewarded with honorary grand mentor, an enlarged fief, and appointment as commander of the palace front guard. In the first month of the fourth year he fell ill. The emperor visited him at his bedside. He died at seventy-five and was posthumously made palace attendant.
25
廷召在軍四十餘年，由顯德以來，凡親征，未嘗不從。 善騎射，嘗出獵，有群鳥飛過，廷召發矢，並貫其三，觀者駭異。 性惡飛禽，所至處彈射殆絕。 尤不喜驢鳴，聞必擊殺之。
Tingzhao served more than forty years in the army. From the Xiande reign onward he never missed an imperial campaign. A fine horseman and archer, he once on a hunt shot a single arrow through three birds in flight, to the astonishment of all who watched. He detested birds and hunted them nearly to extinction wherever he went. He loathed the bray of donkeys and always ordered any braying beast killed.
26
子守均，至散員都虞候、演州刺史； 守信，內殿承製、閤門祗候； 守宣，內殿崇班； 守慶更名珪，後為西京作坊副使、淮南江浙荊湖制置發運副使。
His son Shoujun rose to chief commandant of the loose company and prefect of Yanzhou; Shouxin became inner-hall artisan and gate attendant; Shouxuan, inner-hall honored company; Shouqing, who changed his name to Gui, later served as deputy director of the western capital workshops and deputy transport commissioner for Huainan, Jiang-Zhe, and Jing-Hu.
27
葛霸，真定人。 姿表雄毅，善擊刺騎射。 始事太宗於藩邸； 踐阼，補殿前指揮使，稍遷本班都知，三遷至散員都虞候。 雍熙中，幽州之師失律，大補軍校，以霸為驍騎軍都指揮使、領檀州刺史，戍定州。 嘗遇敵唐河，與戰，敗走之，斬獲甚眾。 俄召為御前忠佐馬步軍都軍頭。 端拱初，出為博州團練使，歷潞、代二州部署。 淳化元年，擢殿前都虞候、領潘州觀察使，為高陽關副都部署，進都部署。 凡七戰。 召還，制授保順軍節度，典軍如故。 出為鎮州都部署，徙天雄軍。
Ge Ba was a native of Zhending. Bold and imposing in bearing, he excelled at lance work, riding, and archery. He entered Taizong's service while Taizong was still a prince; on Taizong's accession he was made palace front guard commander, rose gradually to chief of the original company, and after three promotions reached chief commandant of the loose company. After the Youzhou debacle in Yongxi, with ranks of officers refilled, Ba was made commander of the valiant cavalry army, concurrent prefect of Tanzhou, and posted to Dingzhou. At Tang River he met the enemy, routed them in pursuit, and killed or captured a great host. He was soon recalled as chief of the imperial front loyal-assistant combined horse and foot armies. Early in Duangong he was posted as regimental commissioner of Bozhou and served as deployment commissioner at Lu and Dai. In Chunhua 1 he was promoted to chief commandant of the palace front guard and surveillance commissioner of Panzhou, made deputy then full deployment commissioner at Gaoyang Pass. He fought seven battles in all. Recalled, he was by decree made military governor of the Baoshun army while retaining troop command. He was posted as deployment commissioner at Zhenzhou, then transferred to the Tianxiong army.
28
咸平三年，車駕勞師於大名，霸與石保吉同來覲。 時康保裔沒於河間，即日以霸為貝、冀、高陽關前軍行營都部署。 二月，就遷副都指揮使。 未幾，改邠寧、涇原、環慶三路都部署。 四年，遷侍衛馬軍都指揮使，領感德軍節度。
In Xianping 3 the emperor reviewed troops at Daming; Ba came with Shi Baoji to audience. When Kang Baoyi fell at Hejian, Ba was that same day made vanguard field headquarters deployment commissioner for Beizhou, Jizhou, and Gaoyang Pass. In the second month he was also promoted to deputy commander. Soon he was made combined deployment commissioner of the Binning, Jingyuan, and Huanqing routes. In the fourth year he was promoted to commander of the palace guard cavalry and military governor of the Gande army.
29
景德元年，河決澶州橫壟埽，命為修河都部署。 未行，屬北邊有警，真宗議親征，以霸為駕前西面邢洺路都部署，又副李繼隆為駕前東面排陣使，駐澶州。 明年召還，以功特加封邑。 上言朝廷居明德心喪，尚遏音樂，請停迎授之制，奏可。 是年冬，以霸久典兵，年且老，罷軍職，授昭德軍節度、幷代都部署。 時廷臣有隸麾下者，頗擾軍民，霸昏耄，為所罔，真宗知之，故有是召。
In Jingde 1 the Yellow River burst at Henglong Bank in Cizhou and he was ordered to direct river repairs. Before he could depart, alarms rose on the northern frontier. As Zhenzong contemplated marching in person, Ba was made western-route deployment commissioner before the imperial carriage on the Xing-Ming route and eastern-route battle-array deputy under Li Jilong, stationed at Cizhou. Recalled the next year, he received a special enlargement of his fief for his service. He memorialized that with the court still in mourning for Empress Mingde and music curtailed, the ceremony of welcoming and investing officials should be suspended. The request was granted. That winter, because Ba had held command too long and was aging, he was relieved of military office and made military governor of the Zhaode army and combined Bing-Dai deployment commissioner. Some court officials under his command were harassing soldiers and civilians; senile and misled, Ba was recalled for that reason.
30
四年夏，徙知耀州。 霸雖懦，然能謹直自持。 會東封，表求扈蹕。 既以疾不能從，車駕還次衛南，疾少間，迎謁行在。 上嘉其意，勞問久之。 未幾卒，年七十五，贈太尉。
That summer in the fourth year he was made governor of Yaozhou. Timid though he was, Ba still held himself to a strict standard of integrity. At the eastern tour of Mount Tai he petitioned to join the imperial procession. Illness kept him from the tour, but when the emperor halted at Weinan on the return, he recovered enough to come and greet the traveling palace. The emperor praised his loyalty and questioned and consoled him at length. He soon died at seventy-five and was posthumously made grand preceptor.
31
子懷信、懷正、懷敏、懷煦。 懷信至如京副使； 懷煦，內殿承制； 懷正，博州團練使、知滄、莫二州。
He had four sons: Huaixin, Huaizheng, Huaimin, and Huaixu. Huaixin rose to deputy commissioner of the capital grain office; Huaixu, inner-hall artisan; Huaizheng, regimental commissioner of Bozhou and governor of Cang and Mo.
32
〈子〉 懷敏
Son Huaimin.
33
懷敏以蔭授西頭供奉官，加閤門祗候。 歷同提點益州路刑獄、襄鄧都巡檢。 使契丹，知隰、莫、保三州，累遷東染院使、康州刺史、知雄州，就遷西上閤門使。 上《平燕策》。 會歲旱，塘水涸，懷敏慮契丹使至測知其廣深，乃擁界河水注之，塘復如故。 召對邊事，復還雄州，改萊州團練使。 濁流砦兵叛，殺官吏潰去，懷敏發兵掩襲，盡誅其黨。 在雄州五年，徙滄州。
Huaimin entered service by privilege as western attendant palace officer and gate attendant. He served as co-commissioner for criminal affairs on the Yizhou route and chief inspector of Xiang and Deng. After an embassy to the Khitan he governed Xi, Mo, and Bao, rose to director of the eastern dyeing bureau, prefect of Kangzhou, and governor of Xiongzhou, and was promoted to western upper gate commissioner. He submitted a Plan for Pacifying Yan. In a drought year the moat ran dry. Fearing Khitan envoys would measure its depth, Huaimin diverted border-river water to refill it. Called to discuss frontier affairs, he returned to Xiongzhou and was made regimental commissioner of Laizhou. When troops at Zhuoliu Fort mutinied, killed officials, and fled, Huaimin sent a raiding force and executed the ringleaders. After five years governing Xiongzhou he was posted to Cangzhou.
34
懷敏為王德用妹婿，德用貶，亦絳知滁州。 陝西用兵，起為涇原路馬步軍副總管兼涇原秦鳳兩路經略、安撫副使。 既入對，以曹瑋嘗所被介胄賜之，令制置鄜延、環慶兩路存廢砦柵。 擢龍神衛四廂都指揮、眉州防禦使、本路副都總管、知涇原。 遷捧日天武四廂都指揮使、鄜延路副都總管。 進殿前都虞候、知延州。 范仲淹言其猾懦不知兵，復徙涇原路兼招討、經略、安撫副使。
Huaimin was Wang Deyong's brother-in-law; when Deyong was demoted, Huaimin was reduced to governor of Chuzhou. When war broke out in Shaanxi he was recalled as deputy chief of Jingyuan horse and foot and deputy commissioner for strategy and pacification of Jingyuan and Qin-Feng. On audience he was given armor once worn by Cao Wei and ordered to decide which forts on the Fuyan and Huanqing routes to keep or abandon. He was promoted to commander of the four wings of the dragon spirit guard, defender of Meizhou, deputy chief commander of the route, and governor of Jingyuan. He was transferred to commander of the four wings of the Sun-Held Heavenly Martial Guard and deputy chief commander of the Fuyan route. He advanced to chief commandant of the palace front guard and governor of Yanzhou. Fan Zhongyan called him cunning, timid, and unfit for command; he was shifted back to the Jingyuan route as deputy commissioner for punitive campaigns, strategy, and pacification.
35
慶曆二年，元昊寇鎮戎軍，懷敏出瓦亭砦，督砦主都監許思純、環慶路都監劉賀、天聖砦主張貴，及緣邊都巡檢使向進、劉湛、趙瑜等禦敵。 軍次安邊砦，給芻秣未絕，懷敏輒離軍，夜至開遠堡北一里而舍。 既而自鎮戎軍西南，又先引從騎百餘以前，承受趙正曰：「敵近，不可輕進。 」懷敏乃少止。 日暮趨養馬城，與知鎮戎軍曹英及涇原路都監李知和王保王文、鎮戎軍都監李嶽、西路都巡檢使趙璘等會兵。 聞元昊徙軍新壕外，懷敏議質明襲之，乃命諸命將分四路趣定川砦：劉湛、向進出西水口，涇原路都監趙珣出蓮華堡，曹英、李知和出劉璠堡，懷敏出定西堡。 知和與英督軍夜發。 翌日，湛、進行次趙福堡，遇敵，戰不勝，保向家峽，懷敏使珣、英並鎮戎軍西路巡檢李良臣、孟淵援之。
In Qingli 2, when Yuan Hao attacked Zhenrong Army, Huaimin marched from Wating Fort and directed fort commander Xu Sichun, Huanqing supervisor Liu He, Tiansheng fort commander Zhang Gui, and frontier inspectors Xiang Jin, Liu Zhan, and Zhao Yu against the enemy. Halting at Anbian Fort before fodder issue was even complete, Huaimin abruptly left the army and camped a li north of Kaiyuan Fort that night. Then from southwest of Zhenrong Army he again rode ahead with a hundred-odd followers. Staff officer Zhao Zheng warned: "The enemy is close—we must not advance rashly." Huaimin paused briefly. At dusk he rushed to Yangma City and joined Cao Ying, governor of Zhenrong Army, Jingyuan supervisor Li Zhihe, Wang Bao, Wang Wen, Zhenrong supervisor Li Yue, and western-route chief inspector Zhao Lin. Learning Yuan Hao had shifted outside the new moat, Huaimin planned a dawn strike and divided his commanders toward Dingchuan Fort: Liu Zhan and Xiang Jin by West Water Mouth, Zhao Xun by Lianhua Fort, Cao Ying and Li Zhihe by Liu Fan Fort, and Huaimin by Dingxi Fort. Li Zhihe and Cao Ying led their columns out by night. Next day Liu Zhan and Xiang Jin halted at Zhaofu Fort, met the enemy, failed to win, and fell back to Xiangjia Gorge. Huaimin sent Zhao Xun and Cao Ying with western-route inspectors Li Liangchen and Meng Yuan to their aid.
36
俄報敵已拔柵逾邊壕，懷敏入保定川砦，敵毀板橋，斷其歸路，別為二十四道以過軍，環圍之。 又絕定川水泉上流，以饑渴其眾。 劉賀率蕃兵門於河西，不勝，餘眾潰去。 懷敏為中軍屯塞門東偏，英等陣東北隅。 敵自褊江三、葉燮會出，四面環之。 先以銳兵衝中軍，不動，回擊英軍。 會黑風起東北，部伍相失，陣遂擾。 士卒攀城堞爭入，英面被流矢，仆壕中，懷敏部兵見之亦奔駭。 懷敏為眾蹂躪幾死，輿致甕城，久之乃蘇。 復選士據門橋，揮手刃以拒入城者。 趙珣等以騎軍四合禦敵，敵眾稍卻，然大軍無鬥志。 珣馳入，勸懷敏還軍中。
Word soon came that the enemy had torn up palisades and crossed the border ditch. Huaimin entered Baodingchuan Fort. The enemy destroyed the pontoon bridge, cut off retreat, opened twenty-four crossing paths, and surrounded them. They also dammed the upper reaches of the Dingchuan springs to thirst the army. Liu He led Tangut auxiliaries to hold the west-bank gate, was beaten, and the rest fled. Huaimin encamped the center on the east side of Saimen; Cao Ying and the others formed on the northeast. The enemy sallied from Bianjiang and Yexie and closed in on four sides. Elite troops first struck the center without breaking it, then wheeled to hit Cao Ying's wing. A black gale rose from the northeast; units lost contact and the formation collapsed. Soldiers scrambled over the ramparts to get inside. Cao Ying took an arrow in the face and fell into the ditch. Huaimin's men panicked at the sight and fled. Huaimin was nearly trampled to death, was carried into Weng City, and long afterward came to. He then posted picked men on the gate bridge, slashing at anyone trying to force entry. Zhao Xun and others rallied cavalry on four sides and the enemy drew back slightly, but the main army had lost its will to fight. Zhao Xun rode in and urged Huaimin back to the army.
37
是夕，敵聚火圍城四隅，臨西北呼曰：「爾得非總管廳點陣圖者邪？ 爾固能軍，乃入我圍中，今復何往！ 」夜四鼓，懷敏召曹英、趙珣、李知和、王保、王文、許思純、劉賀、李良臣、趙瑜計議，莫知所出，遂謀結陣走鎮戎軍。 雞鳴，懷敏自諭：「親軍左右及在後者皆毋得動，平明，從吾往安西堡。 以英、珣為先鋒，賀、思純為左右翼，知和為殿，聽中軍鼓乃得行。 」至卯，鼓未作，懷敏先上馬，而大軍按堵未動。 懷敏周麾者再，將徑去，有執鞚者勸不可，懷敏不得已而還。 使參謀郭京等取芻城中，未至，懷敏復上馬，叱執轡者使去，不聽，拔劍且擊之，士遂散。 懷敏驅馬東南馳二百里，至長城壕，路已斷，敵周圍之，遂與諸將皆遇害。 餘軍九千四百餘人，馬六百餘匹，為敵所斷。 其子宗晟與趙正、郭京、承受王昭明等還保定川。
That night the enemy lit fires on all four sides of the walls and shouted from the northwest: "Are you not the man who plotted battle arrays in the commander's hall?" You fancy yourself a commander—yet you walked into our trap. Where will you run now! At the fourth watch Huaimin summoned Cao Ying, Zhao Xun, Li Zhihe, Wang Bao, Wang Wen, Xu Sichun, Liu He, Li Liangchen, and Zhao Yu. With no plan in sight, they resolved to break out toward Zhenrong Army. At cockcrow Huaimin announced: "Personal guards and the rear must not stir. At dawn follow me to Anxi Fort." Cao Ying and Zhao Xun would lead, Liu He and Xu Sichun would cover the flanks, Li Zhihe the rear, and none were to move until the center drum sounded. By dawn the drum had not sounded, yet Huaimin mounted first while the main army still stood in place. Huaimin rode the line twice waving his command, about to bolt; a man holding his reins begged him to stay, and he unwillingly turned back. He sent staff officer Guo Jing to fetch fodder inside the walls. Before they returned Huaimin mounted again, ordered the reins let go, and when they refused slashed at them with his sword. The troops broke ranks. Huaimin galloped southeast two hundred li to the Great Wall ditch. The road was cut; the enemy closed in; he and his generals were killed. More than nine thousand four hundred troops and six hundred horses were lost to the enemy. His son Zongsheng, with Zhao Zheng, Guo Jing, staff officer Wang Zhaoming, and others, made it back to Baodingchuan.
38
初，懷敏令軍中步兵毋得動，及前陣已去，後軍多不知者，故皆得存。 時韓質、郝從政、胡息以兵六千保蓮華堡，劉湛、向進兵一千保向家峽，皆不赴援。 於是敵長驅抵渭州，幅員六七百里，焚蕩廬舍，屠掠民畜而去。 奏至，帝嗟悼久之，贈懷敏鎮戎軍節度使兼太尉，英、知和、珣、保、文、質、嶽、貴、璘、思純、良臣及同時戰沒者，及涇原巡檢楊遵、籠竿城巡檢姚奭、涇原都巡檢司監押董謙、同巡檢唐斌、指使霍達，皆贈官有差。 復降向進等官，落郝從政、趙瑜職。
Huaimin had ordered the infantry not to move. When the vanguard fled, the rear ranks mostly did not know and so survived. Han Zhi, Hao Congzheng, and Hu Xi held Lianhua Fort with six thousand men; Liu Zhan and Xiang Jin held Xiangjia Gorge with one thousand. Neither column came to help. The enemy then swept six or seven hundred li to Weizhou, burning settlements and slaughtering people and livestock as they went. When the report reached court the emperor grieved long. Huaimin was posthumously made military governor of Zhenrong Army and grand preceptor. Cao Ying, Li Zhihe, Zhao Xun, Wang Bao, Wang Wen, Han Zhi, Li Yue, Zhang Gui, Zhao Lin, Xu Sichun, Li Liangchen, and others killed in the battle, with Jingyuan inspector Yang Zun, Longgan fort inspector Yao Shang, inspector superintendent Dong Qian, co-inspector Tang Bin, and commanding officer Huo Da, all received posthumous honors in varying degrees. Xiang Jin and others were demoted; Hao Congzheng and Zhao Yu were dismissed.
39
懷敏通時事，善候人情，故多以才薦之。 及用為將，而輕率昧於應變，遂至覆軍。 帝念之，賜諡忠隱。 子宗晟、宗壽、宗禮、宗師，皆遷官。
Huaimin was politically astute and good at reading men, which is why so many praised his talents. Yet as a field commander he was reckless and slow to adapt, and so destroyed his army. The emperor still held him in regard and granted the posthumous name Loyal and Reserved. His sons Zongsheng, Zongshou, Zongli, and Zongshi were all promoted.
40
論曰：真宗澶淵之役，高瓊之功亦盛矣。 范廷召年十八，能手刃父仇； 瓊將磔於市，幸以逃免； 葛霸善擊刺馬射，給事藩邸，皆非素習韜略者也，及其出身戎行，迭居節鎮，而卓有可觀，由所遇之得其時也。 或謂瓊頗自用，謀議不及參佐，而洞曉軍政； 霸雖失於巽懦，而能謹直自持； 廷召性雖癖，在軍中四十年，累從征討，所至有功：皆不害其為驍果也。 廷召諸子，珪為最賢，霸子懷敏以戰死，固皆足稱。 若繼宣、繼勳之將業，則過其父遠甚，此「克勤敏功鍾慶之碑」所由以立歟！ 夫以三子之自樹如此，而不得與狄青、郭逵同日而論者，豈非拳勇之有餘，而器識之不足也歟！
The historians comment: In Zhenzong's Chanyuan campaign, Gao Qiong's service was likewise great. Fan Tingzhao at eighteen avenged his father with his own hand; Gao Qiong escaped dismemberment in the marketplace by a hair's breadth; Ge Ba excelled at arms and served in the princely household—none had trained in strategy. Yet once they took to the field and rose to provincial command, each achieved much, for fortune favored their hour. Some say Qiong was willful and seldom consulted his staff, yet understood war administration thoroughly; Ba, though timid and yielding, still kept himself honest; Tingzhao, for all his eccentricities, served forty years in the field with merit wherever he fought—eccentricity did not diminish his valor. Of Tingzhao's sons Gui was the finest; Ba's son Huaimin died in battle—each in his way deserves praise. Jixuan and Jixun as generals far outshone their father—is that not why the stele "He Who Toils Diligently and Wins Merit Gathers Blessings" was raised! Men who built careers such as these yet cannot stand beside Di Qing and Guo Kui—does this not show courage in abundance but judgment too small?
