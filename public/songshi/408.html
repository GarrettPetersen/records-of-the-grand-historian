1
吳昌裔汪綱陳宓王霆
Wu Changyi, Wang Gang, Chen Mi, and Wang Ting.
2
吳昌裔，字季永，中江人。 蚤孤，與兄泳痛自植立，不肯逐時好，得程頤、張載、朱熹諸書，輒研繹不倦。 嘉定七年舉進士，聞漢陽守黃幹得熹之學，往從之。
Wu Changyi, whose style was Jiyong, came from Zhongjiang. Orphaned in childhood, he and his elder brother Yong strove hard to stand on their own and would not chase the tastes of the day. Once he obtained the writings of Cheng Yi, Zhang Zai, Zhu Xi, and the like, he studied and explicated them tirelessly. In the seventh year of the Jiading reign he passed the jinshi examination. Hearing that Huang Gan, prefect of Hanyang, had inherited Zhu Xi's learning, he went to study under him.
3
調閩中尉。 利路轉運使曹彥約聞其賢，俾司糴場。 時歲饑，議糴上流，昌裔請發本倉所儲數萬而徐糴以償，從之。 調眉州教授。 眉士故尚蘇軾學，昌裔取諸經為之講說，祠周惇頤及顥、頤、載、熹，揭《白鹿洞學規》，仿潭州釋奠儀，簿正祭器，士習丕變。 製置使崔與之薦之，改知華陽縣。 修學宮，來四方士，斥羨錢二十萬緡，買良田備旱。 通判眉州，著《苦言》十篇，慮蜀甚悉。 攝郡事，禦軍有紀律。 尋權漢州，故事比攝官，奉饋皆如真，昌裔命削其半。 核兵籍，興社倉，郡政畢舉。 興元帥趙彥呐議東納武仙，西結秦、鞏，人莫敢言，昌裔獨奮筆力辨其非。 未幾，武仙敗，二州之民果叛。
He was posted as assistant magistrate in Minzhong. Cao Yanyue, transport commissioner of the Lizhou circuit, heard of his talent and put him in charge of the grain-purchase depot. Famine struck that year, and officials proposed buying grain upstream. Changyi asked to release the tens of thousands of bushels held in the local granary and replenish the stock gradually through purchases; his plan was adopted. He was transferred to serve as instructor at Meizhou. Mei's scholars had long favored Su Shi's school of thought. Changyi lectured on the classics instead, enshrined Zhou Dunyi together with the Cheng brothers, Zhang Zai, and Zhu Xi, posted the regulations of Bailudong Academy, modeled the Tanzhou libation rite, inventoried the sacrificial vessels, and scholarly habits changed profoundly. Pacification commissioner Cui Yuizhi recommended him, and he was appointed magistrate of Huayang County. He restored the county school and attracted scholars from all quarters. He spent two hundred thousand strings of surplus funds to buy fertile fields as a reserve against drought. As vice-prefect of Meizhou, he wrote ten essays entitled Bitter Words, setting out his concerns for Shu in meticulous detail. While acting prefect, he kept the garrison troops under strict discipline. He soon held acting authority over Han Prefecture. By custom, acting officials received presentation gifts on the same scale as regular appointees; Changyi ordered those gifts cut in half. He audited the military registers, established community granaries, and brought every item of prefectural administration to completion. Commander Zhao Yan'na of Xingyuan proposed accepting Wu Xian in the east and allying with Qin and Gong in the west. No one else dared object, but Changyi alone took up his brush and argued forcefully that the plan was wrong. Before long Wu Xian was defeated, and the people of the two prefectures did indeed rebel.
4
台臣故事，季詣獄點檢。 時有爭常州田萬四千畝，平江亦數百畝，株逮百餘人，視其牘，乃趙善湘之子汝櫄、汝榟也，州縣不敢決，昌裔連疏劾罷之。 冬洊雷，春大雨雪，昌裔居齋宮秉燭草疏，凡上躬缺失，宮庭嬖私，廟堂除授，皆以為言。 又言：“將帥方命，女寵私謁，舊黨之用，邊疆之禍，皆此陰類。 ”且曰：“今大昕坐朝，間有時不視事之文； 私第謁假，或有時不入堂之報。 上有耽樂慆逸之漸，下無協恭和衷之風。 內則嬖禦懷私，為君心之蠹； 外則子弟寡謹，為朝政之累。 遊言噂遝，寵賂章聞，欲《簫》、《勺》大和，得乎？ ”
By censorial custom, each season the censors inspected the prisons. At the time a dispute involved fourteen thousand mu of fields in Changzhou and several hundred mu in Pingjiang, with more than a hundred people implicated. The papers showed that the claimants were Rukai and Ruzi, sons of Zhao Shanxiang. The local authorities dared not decide the case, so Changyi submitted a series of memorials impeaching them until they were removed from office. Repeated thunder in winter and heavy rain and snow in spring moved Changyi, while residing in the fasting palace, to draft memorials by candlelight. He spoke out on every matter touching the emperor's personal failings, palace favorites, and court appointments. He also wrote: "Generals who defy orders, palace women who seek private favors, the revival of old factional allies, and disasters on the frontiers all spring from this same corrupting influence." He went on: "The emperor now holds court at dawn, yet from time to time edicts appear saying he is not attending to affairs; from his private residence he takes leave, and there are even reports that at times he does not enter the audience hall." Above, the court shows a growing tendency toward indulgence and idleness; below, there is no spirit of united respect and shared purpose. Within the palace, favored attendants pursue private ends and eat away at the ruler's judgment; outside it, imperial kinsmen lack restraint and weigh down the conduct of government. Loose talk swarms everywhere, and favoritism and bribes are openly reported. How can one hope for the great harmony of the Xiao and Shao? "
5
又念蜀事阽危，條四事以進：實規橅，審功賞，訪軍實，儲帥才。 時有果、閬州守臣逃遁而進職，有知遂寧李煒父子足跡不至邊庭而受賞，僨軍之趙楷、棄城之朱揚祖皆不加罰； 又帥臣趙彥呐年老智衰，其子淫刑黷貨，士卒不用命，安癸仲恥遭抨彈，經營復用，欲起謫籍以代帥垣，昌裔皆抗疏彈擊。
Mindful that affairs in Shu stood on the brink of collapse, he submitted four proposals: give strategic plans real substance, scrutinize rewards for merit, investigate true military strength, and cultivate commanders' talent. At the time prefectural officials of Guo and Lang who had fled were nevertheless promoted, Li Wei and his son of Suining received rewards though they never set foot on the frontier, and neither Zhao Kai of the mutinous army nor Zhu Yangzu, who had abandoned his city, was punished; moreover Commander Zhao Yan'na had grown old and feeble in judgment, his son inflicted cruel punishments and amassed illicit wealth, and the troops would not obey orders. An Guizhong, shamed by impeachment, maneuvered for reinstatement and sought to recall banished officials to replace the command structure. Changyi submitted defiant memorials attacking every one of these abuses.
6
又曆言三邊之事曰：“今朝廷之上，百辟晏然，言論多於施行，浮文妨於實務。 後族王宮之冗費，列曹坐局之常程，群工閑慢之差除，諸道非泛之申請，以至土木經營，時節宴遊，神霄祈禳，大禮錫賚，藻飾治具，無異平時。 至於治兵足食之方，脩車備馬之事，乃缺略不講。 ”且援靖康之敝，痛哭言之。
He also spoke in turn on the affairs of the three frontiers: "At court today the hundred officials sit at ease. Talk outruns action, and empty documents obstruct real work." The redundant expenses of the empress's kin and royal palaces, the routine paperwork of the bureaus, leisurely transfers among clerks, extraordinary requests from the circuits, construction projects, seasonal banquets and excursions, prayers at the Shenxiao Shrine, gifts for great ceremonies, and all the other trappings of governance continued as in peacetime. Yet methods for training troops and securing provisions, for repairing chariots and preparing horses, were left out entirely. He cited the disasters of the Jingkang era and spoke through tears.
7
出為大理少卿，屢疏引去，不許。 會杜範再入台，擊參政李鳴復，謂昌裔與範善，必相為謀者，數讒之，以權工部侍郎出參讚四川宣撫司軍事。 人曰：“此李綱救太原也。 太原不可救，特以綱主戰，故出之耳。 ”昌裔曰：“君命也，不可不亟行。 ”慷慨襆被出關，忽得疾，中道病甚，帝聞之，授秘閣修撰，改嘉興府。 昌裔曰：“吾以疾不能歸救父母，上負聖恩，下負此心，若舍遠就近，舍危就安，人其謂我何？ ”辭至四五，而言者以避事論矣。
He was appointed vice minister of the Court of Judicial Review. He repeatedly memorialized to resign, but permission was denied. When Du Fan returned to the Censorate and attacked Councilor Li Mingfu, enemies said Changyi was close to Fan and must be conspiring with him. They slandered him repeatedly until he was sent out as acting vice minister of Works to participate in the military affairs of the Sichuan Pacification Commission. People said, "This is like Li Gang being sent to save Taiyuan." Taiyuan could not be saved; Li Gang was sent away only because he favored war. Changyi replied, "It is the ruler's command. I must set out at once." He wrapped his bedding with high spirit and left the pass, but suddenly fell ill and grew gravely sick on the road. When the emperor heard of it, he appointed Changyi Secretariat Drafter and transferred him to Jiaxing Prefecture. Changyi said, "Illness keeps me from returning to save my parents. I fail the emperor above and my own conscience below. If I abandon the distant post for a nearby one and exchange danger for safety, what will people say of me?" He declined four or five times, yet critics accused him of shirking duty.
8
改贛州，辭，以右文殿修撰主管鴻慶宮。 遷浙東提刑，辭，改知婺州。 婺告旱，民日夜望之，乃不忍終辭，減騶從供帳，遣僚佐召邑令周行阡陌，蠲粟八萬一千石、錢二十五萬緡有奇。 加集英殿修撰，卒，以寶章閣待制致仕。
He was transferred to Ganzhou, declined again, and was made Right Culture Hall Drafter with charge of the Hongqing Palace. He was promoted to judicial intendant of eastern Zhejiang, declined, and was appointed prefect of Wuzhou instead. Wuzhou reported drought, and the people watched for him day and night. He could not bring himself to decline the post entirely. He cut back his escort and official expenses, sent aides to summon county magistrates for tours of the fields, and remitted eighty-one thousand shi of grain and more than two hundred fifty thousand strings of cash. He was promoted to Hall for Assembling Excellence Drafter, then died and was granted retirement as Baozhang Pavilion Attendant-in-Ordinary.
9
昌裔剛正莊重，遇事敢言，典章多所閑習。 嚐輯至和、紹興諸臣奏議本末。 名《儲鑒》。 又會粹周、漢以至宋蜀道得失，興師取財之所，名《蜀鑒》。 有奏議、《四書講義》、《鄉約口義》、《諸老記聞》、《容台議禮》、文集行於世。
Changyi was upright, stern, and grave, spoke boldly when matters arose, and was well versed in statutes and ritual. He once compiled the origins and outcomes of memorial proposals by ministers of the Zhihe and Shaoxing eras. He titled it Stored Mirror. He also compiled the gains and losses of the Shu routes from Zhou and Han through Song, together with the places where armies were raised and wealth exacted, and titled the work Shu Mirror. His Memorials, Expositions on the Four Books, Oral Explications of the Village Compact, Records Heard from the Elders, Rites Discussed at the Guest Terrace, and collected writings circulated in his day.
10
初，昌裔與徐清叟、杜範一日並入台，皆天下正士，四方想聞風采，人至和《三諫詩》以侈之。 然才七閱月以遷，故莫不惋惜雲。 後諡忠肅。
When Changyi entered the Censorate on the same day as Xu Qingsou and Du Fan, all three were regarded as upright men of the realm. People throughout the empire looked to their example, and some even wrote Three Remonstrators poems in praise of them. Yet after only seven months he was transferred, and everyone regretted it. He was later given the posthumous title Loyal and Stern.
11
汪綱，字仲舉，黟縣人，簽書樞密院勃之曾孫也。 以祖任入官，淳熙十四年中銓試，調鎮江府司戶參軍。
Wang Gang, whose style was Zhongju, came from Yi County. He was the great-grandson of Bo, who had served as Secretariat Drafter of the Bureau of Military Affairs. He entered office through his grandfather's yin privilege. In the fourteenth year of the Chunxi reign he passed the selection examination and was posted as revenue clerk of Zhenjiang Prefecture.
12
馬大同鎮京口，強毅自任，綱言論獨不詭隨。 議者欲以兩淮鐵錢交子行於沿江，廷議令大同倡率行之，綱貽書曰：“邊麵行鐵錢，慮銅寶泄於外耳。 私鑄盛行，故錢輕而物重。 今若場務出納不以鐵錢取息，堅守四色請買舊制，冶鑄定額不求餘羨，重禁以戢私鑄，支散邊戍與在軍中半者無異，不以鐵錢準折，則淮民將自便之，何至以敝內郡邪？ ”大同始悟。 試湖南轉運司，又中，綱笑曰：“此豈足以用世澤物耶？ ”乃刻意問學，博通古今，精究義理； 覃思本原。
When Ma Datong governed Jingkou, he was forceful and self-willed. Gang alone refused to bend his views to suit him. Councilors wished to circulate the Huai iron-cash exchange notes along the Yangzi coast, and the court ordered Datong to take the lead. Gang wrote to him: "On the frontier they use iron cash only because they fear copper currency leaking abroad." Private coining is rampant, so money is debased and goods dear. If market offices no longer take interest in iron cash, if we firmly uphold the old four-color purchase system, fix smelting quotas without seeking surplus profit, strictly forbid private coining, and pay frontier garrisons and troops in the field on the same terms without converting through iron cash, the Huai people will manage on their own. Why should we harm the inner commanderies?" Only then did Datong understand. He passed the Hunan transport office examination as well. Gang laughed and said, "Can examinations like this really serve the age or benefit the people?" He then devoted himself to learning, mastered antiquity and the present, and investigated principle and meaning deeply; pondering deeply the root of things.
13
調桂陽軍平陽縣令，縣連溪峒，蠻蜒與居，綱一遇以恩信。 科罰之害既三十年，綱下車，首白諸台，罷之。 桂陽歲貢銀二萬九千餘兩，而平陽當其三分之二。 綱謂向者銀礦坌發價輕，故可勉以應，今地寶已竭，市於他郡，其價倍蓰，願力請痛蠲損之。 歲饑，旁邑有曹伍者，群聚惡少入境，強貸發廩，眾至千餘，挾界頭、牛橋二砦兵為援，地盤踞萬山間，前後令未嚐一涉其境，不虞綱之至也，相率出迎。 綱已夙具酒食，令之曰：“汝何敢亂，順者得食，亂者就誅。 ”夜宿砦中，呼砦官詰責不能防守狀，皆皇恐伏地請死，杖其首惡者八人，發粟振糶，民賴以安。
He was posted magistrate of Pingyang County in Guiyang Commandery, a county bordering stream gorges where Man and Yan peoples lived. From the first encounter he treated them with kindness and trust. Penal levies had harmed the county for thirty years. As soon as Gang took office, he reported to the censorial offices and had them abolished. Guiyang annually presented more than twenty-nine thousand liang of silver in tribute, of which Pingyang bore two-thirds. Gang said that formerly silver mines had flourished and prices were low, so the county could barely meet its quota. Now the local deposits were exhausted, and silver bought in other commanderies cost many times more. He pressed earnestly for a sharp reduction. During a famine year, a man named Cao Wu in a neighboring county gathered more than a thousand ruffians who crossed the border, forced loans, and broke open granaries, backed by troops from the Jietou and Niuqiao forts in mountainous terrain no previous magistrate had ever entered. They did not expect Gang to come and went out together to welcome him. Gang had long since prepared food and drink. He ordered them: "How dare you make trouble? Those who submit will be fed; those who rebel will be executed." That night he lodged in the fort, summoned the fort officers, and reproached them for failing to guard their posts. They prostrated themselves in terror and begged for death. He caned the eight ringleaders, issued grain, and sold relief grain, and the people were thereby saved.
14
改知金壇縣，親嫌，更弋陽縣。 父義和為侍御史主管佑神觀。 尋丁父喪，服除，知蘭溪縣，決擿如神。 歲旱，郡倚辦勸分，綱謂勸分所以助義倉，一切行之，非所謂安富恤貧也，願假常平錢為糶本，使得循環迭濟。 又躬勸富民浚築塘堰，大興水利，餓者得食其力，全活甚眾。 郡守張抑及部使者列綱為一道荒政之冠。 以言去，邑人相率投軌直其事，綱力止之。
He was appointed magistrate of Jintan County, but because of a kinship taboo was transferred to Yiyang County. His father Yihe served as investigating censor with charge of the You Shen Shrine. He soon mourned his father's death. When mourning ended, he governed Lanxi County and decided cases with uncanny insight. In a drought year the prefecture relied on wholesale exhortation to share grain. Gang said exhortation to share was meant to support the charity granary, not to enrich the secure and relieve the poor by forcing everyone at once. He asked to borrow Ever-Normal funds as capital for relief sales so grain could circulate in rotation. He also personally exhorted wealthy households to dredge ponds and build weirs, greatly expanding irrigation works so the hungry could earn food by labor and very many lives were saved. Prefect Zhang Yi and the circuit envoy ranked him the finest famine administrator in the circuit. He left office after a memorial attack. The townspeople in succession cast themselves before his carriage to plead his case, but Gang firmly stopped them.
15
繼知太平縣，主管兩浙轉運司文字，未赴，罹內艱，擢監行在左藏西庫。 屬金人殺其主允濟自立，遣使來告襲位，議者即欲遣幣，綱言：‘使名不遜，當止之境上，姑命左帑視例計辦，或且留京口總司，令盱眙諭之曰：‘紀年名節，皆犯先朝避忌，歲幣乃爾前主所增，今既易代，當復隆興、大定之舊。 ’俟此議定，而後正旦、生辰之使可遣。 遲以歲月，吾擇邊將葺城堡，簡軍實，儲峙糗糧，使沿邊屹然有不可犯之勢，聽其自相攻擊，然後以全力製其後。 ”廟堂韙之。
He next governed Taiping County and was appointed to supervise documents for the Two-Zhe transport office, but had not yet taken up the post when he suffered the death of a close relative. He was then promoted to supervise the Western Storehouse of the Left Treasury in the capital. When the Jurchens killed their ruler Yunji and set up a new sovereign, they sent envoys to announce the succession and councilors at once wished to send tribute. Gang said: "The envoy's title is not deferential. He should be stopped at the border. For the moment order the Left Treasury to calculate provisions by precedent, or detain him temporarily at the Jingkou headquarters and have Xuyi instruct him: 'Your reign title and tabooed names all violate our former dynasty's avoidances, and the annual tribute was increased by your former ruler. Now that the dynasty has changed, it should return to the Longxing and Dading precedents.'" Wait until this matter is settled, and only then may envoys for New Year's Day and the birthday be dispatched." Delay for months and years while we choose frontier generals to repair castles, streamline military strength, and stock provisions so the border stands in an inviolable posture. Let them attack one another, and then bring full force to bear from behind. The court approved his plan.
16
提轄東西庫，又幹辦諸司審計司。 以選知高郵軍，陛辭，言：“揚、楚二州當各屯二萬人，壯其聲勢，而以高郵為家計砦。 高郵三面阻水，湖澤奧阻，戎馬所不能騁，獨西南一路直距天長，無險可守，乃去城六十里隨地經畫，或浚溝塹，或備設伏，以扼其衝。 ”又慮湖可以入淮，招水卒五千人造百艘列三砦以戒非常。 興化民田濱海，昔範仲淹築堰以障寫鹵，守毛澤民置石達函管以疏運河水勢，歲久皆壞，綱乃增修之。 部使者聞於朝，增一秩，提舉淮東常平。 淮米越江有禁，綱念“淮民有警則室廬莫保，歲凶則轉徙無歸，豐年可以少蘇，重以苛禁，自分畛域，豈為民父母意哉！ 請下金陵糴三十萬以通淮西之運，京口糴五十萬以通淮東之運。 ”又言：“兩淮之積不可多，昇、潤之積不可少。 平江積米數百萬，陳陳相因，久而紅腐，宜視其收貯近久，取餉輦下百司、諸軍。 江上歲餫當至京者，貯之京口、金陵轉漕。 兩淮、中都諸倉，亦當廣糴以補其數。 ”
He supervised the eastern and western storehouses and also handled affairs for the Various Offices Audit Office. Selected as prefect of Gaoyou Commandery, he said at his farewell audience: "Yang and Chu prefectures should each station twenty thousand troops to strengthen their posture, with Gaoyou serving as the home-base fort." Gaoyou is blocked by water on three sides, and its lakes and marshes are too tangled for cavalry to charge freely. Only the southwest route runs straight toward Tianchang with no natural defense. He therefore went sixty li from the city to plan defenses according to the terrain, digging ditches and moats or setting ambushes to block the approach. He also feared that the lake could give access into the Huai, so he recruited five thousand water soldiers, built a hundred vessels, and arrayed three forts to guard against emergencies. The farmland of Xinghua bordered the sea. Fan Zhongyan had once built dikes to block salt intrusion, and Prefect Mao Zemin had installed stone sluice pipes to drain the transport canal. After many years both works had fallen into ruin, and Gang enlarged and repaired them. The circuit envoy reported to court, he was given one additional rank, and he was made intendant of Huaidong Ever-Normal granaries. Transport of Huai rice across the Yangzi was forbidden. Gang reflected: "When alarm strikes, Huai people cannot protect their homes; in famine years they wander with nowhere to return. In good years they recover only a little, yet harsh prohibitions divide their own region. Is this how a parent to the people should act?" He requested that Jinling purchase three hundred thousand bushels to open transport for western Huai and Jingkou purchase five hundred thousand to open transport for eastern Huai. He also said: "Stores in the Two Huai cannot be too large, while stores in Sheng and Run cannot be too small." Pingjiang's stored rice numbered in the millions, piled year upon year until it turned red and rotten. He proposed examining how long each store had been held and using the oldest grain to supply the capital offices and armies. Annual grain transport on the Yangzi bound for the capital should be stored at Jingkou and Jinling for relay shipment. Granaries in the Two Huai and the central capital region should also purchase grain broadly to replenish their stocks. "
17

The pacification commissioner asked which defensive preparations should come first. Gang said: "The Huai region has long been called a deep treasury of revenue. Ironworks lie to the west and fish and rice abound to the east, enough for self-sufficiency." Western Huai has many mountains and eastern Huai much water, enough for self-defense. If the Two Huai could truly be united as one, with troops and wealth flowing together and their combined posture made one, they could suffice even without relying on Jiang and Zhe. In the dynasty's flourishing age, border commanderies stored enough grain to last ten years; During the Qingli era, a single garrison in Zhongshan still held 1.8 million shi. The court should now follow the former dynasty's precedent, allow merchants to deliver grain near the frontier passes, and issue capital vouchers redeemable in the capital. Those who delivered grain received ennoblement, and if the promise were kept faithfully, deliveries would multiply and border stores would never lack for grain. Prefectural forbidden troops were never meant for corvée labor but to draw rations in outer commanderies. Now they are not used for battle but forced into labor service. In emergencies the frontier relies solely on the main army, whose men count the days until rotation and never settle into local conditions. How can that compare with local troops who grew up on the border, with graves and families there, and guard the land as their own? The court should carefully select strong men, expand their registers, attach them all to Imperial Front Army rolls, and issue ration certificates to help prefectures supply clothing and grain, generally following the Shanyang Martial Vanguard system. Then the frontier need not draw garrisons from the Yangzi, and the Yangzi need not send palace troops. With living certificates and rotating shifts, both labor and expense would cease. "

Someone then proposed that the Pacification Commission buy waste land broadly for military farms. Gang argued: "Waste land is easy to acquire, but labor and waterworks cannot be completed quickly. People abandon property, deceive officials, and good fields never materialize. Public funds are wasted and cultivation rarely succeeds." Far better to urge the people to cultivate idle fields fully, have the government assist when streams and canals are blocked, turn barren soil fertile, and let the people build surplus stores. Chao Cuo's proposal for grain deliveries and this dynasty's convenient-purchase system already embody this approach. The Pacification Commission saw that the plan was useless and dropped it.

Huaidong's salt-boiling profits had once comprised half the empire's revenue, but over the years decay spread, salt capital eroded daily, and treasury stores emptied. The office owed the two general commissions more than five hundred thousand strings, owed saltern households two hundred eighty thousand, and had borrowed five hundred thousand from court. When the ration office restored salt notes, the old rule forbade merchants to advance subsidy payments, leaving the salt office unable to support itself. Gang exposed hidden abuses: empty quotas, false disbursements, and shifting accounts. Once these practices were blocked, revenue actually increased. After repaying all debts, he still gained three hundred thousand strings, established a reserve treasury, and prepared against shortfalls in salt capital. He added fifty new salterns, required all depots to meet the Qiandao quota of 3.9 million shi totaling thirteen million strings in revenue, and ranked officials by performance. Gang restrained himself and led subordinates by example. He declined mutual gifts between censorial and prefectural offices and alone increased depot officials' salaries to support their integrity.

Monthly rations for Shandong submissions rose by three hundred thirty thousand strings and sixty thousand shi of grain. Meanwhile western Zhe salt profits ran a deficit of more than seven hundred thousand strings, and transport grain from various prefectures failed to arrive on schedule. Gang verified accounts against reality, warned against delays in audit, planned and disposed affairs, and thereby kept rations from running short.

He declined repeatedly but was not permitted to withdraw. While reviewing prisoners in Wuzhou, he found a slave who had seized a blade to kill his master, failed to find him, and killed the master's son instead, then falsely implicated others. Gang ordered him executed at once. He released prisoners wrongly held in Quzhou. In Taizhou a thief named Zhong Baiyi was not a co-conspirator, but the assistant magistrate, coveting a reward, rushed the case to the Pacification Commission. Gang said, "Thieves must be punished severely, but how can charges be forged to complete a case?" The sentence was therefore reduced from death. While praying for rain at Longrui Palace, a vermilion creature wound about the altar terrace for three days. Gang said, "I want only rain. Do not make wonders to confuse the people." Before he finished speaking, thunder and heavy rain arrived, and the year brought a great harvest.

He was promoted direct attendant of the Hall of Glorious Culture, appointed prefect of Shaoxing, and made supervisor of eastern Zhejiang Pacification Commission affairs while also serving as judicial intendant. Inquiring into the people's hardships, he abolished abusive practices with especial urgency. Xiaoshan had an ancient transport canal running west to Qiantang and east to Tai and Ming. Sand had built up for more than thirty li, trapping boats in place. He dredged more than eight thousand zhang, restored the sluice at the river mouth to keep silt out and control the current, and paved the towpath with brick all the way to the city. Every ten li he built a rest lodge. He named them "Bestowing Water" and placed them under Daoist clergy. Thereafter travelers by boat and cart, on water and land, passed day or night, in heat or cold, with easy passage and rejoicing that forgot fatigue. Subordinate counties bordered the sea, while sixteen townships of Zhuji bordered the lake, where marsh irrigation brought broad benefit. Powerful families privately built embankments and enclosed lake margins as fields. Once the lake flow was choked, water could not drain away, and even moderate rain flooded towns and farmland. Coastal fields relied on ponds for protection, but dikes easily collapsed and brine ruined crops. Losses often reached hundreds of thousands of mu yearly, with rent remissions numbering in the tens of thousands. On Gang's recommendation, an edict ordered the Ever-Normal intendant to expose encroached estates. Strange pleas and clever requests were all sternly rejected, and lake fields began to be restored; the prefecture set aside thirty thousand strings specifically for repairs, and coastal fields began to be secured. Gang said, "This commandery commands the coastal route and closely guards the capital region, yet its military registers are thin and weak." He therefore recruited water troops, branded fork-handlers, trained them intensively, and assigned them no other duties. He built more than a thousand barracks, broad, orderly, and solid, added armor and weapons, and his martial renown blazed forth. He concurrently served as acting Minister of Agriculture, soon became direct attendant of the Dragon Diagram Hall, and continued in office.

When Emperor Lizong took the throne, Gang was appointed Right Culture Hall Drafter, then Hall for Assembling Excellence Drafter, continued in office, and was further made Baomo Pavilion Attendant-in-Ordinary. In the third year of Baoqing a great flood struck. Gang issued more than thirty-eight thousand shi of grain and fifty thousand strings for relief, remitted more than sixty thousand shi of rent, and the starving revived as if it were a normal year. Yue had general-fund account entries totaling four hundred ten thousand strings, of which two hundred fifty thousand were empty quotas since the Shaoxing era. Successive commanders, fearing shortfalls, had falsely inflated them with funds earmarked for repairing the imperial tombs. Gang said, "Failing a quota is a small fault; deceiving one's superiors is a great crime." He gathered the facts and reported them to court. An edict remitted ninety-five thousand strings, and long-standing abuses thereby became clearly exposed.

In the first year of Shaoding he was summoned to the traveling court. At audience Gang said, "Officials now put profit before righteousness and personal plans before the state. They are compliant, lazy, and shrinking, rush in competition, grow greedy and corrupt, and deceive one another. The court should find means to shift this." The emperor said, "I have heard your governance is excellent. How are the people's resources in central Yue?" He replied, "Last year's floods hit Zhuji hardest. This year we are fortunate to have a fair harvest. Within ten years a thousand li have been peaceful, all through the court's august virtue. What strength have I?" He was made acting vice minister of Revenue. After several months he memorialized to retire. He was specially granted two ranks, retained as vice minister of Revenue, and bestowed a gold belt. When he died, many people of Yue shed tears, and some went together to weep at temples and shrines.

Gang's learning had deep roots. He was widely informed and remembered much. He investigated books on military affairs, agriculture, medicine, divination, yin-yang, pitch pipes, and calendars; his mind was sharp and spirit-like, and he decided affairs at once. In Yue he bore four seals and faced documents piled like mountains, yet he held to brevity while mastering detail, finished business in no more than twenty quarters of an hour, and kept the public hall as calm as still water. If a low official or clerk spoke one word that hit the principle, he would follow it wholeheartedly. In writing he excelled at discussing affairs, citing antiquity and the present in arguments that were broad, forceful, and vigorous. He disliked luxury in dress and furnishings. Provision accounts and carriage fittings, though worn, he would not replace. His writings included Collected Works of the Forgiving Studio, Records of the Left Treasury, and Casual Preserved Notes.

Through his father's yin privilege he served as Nan'an salt tax officer in Quanzhou, supervised the Southern Outer Muzong Court and then the Western Outer Court, and governed Anxi County.

The ruler's virtue values clarity above all, great ministers' hearts value fairness above all, and censors' words value directness above all. Your Majesty governs diligently yet achievement is not realized, lives frugally yet finances are not abundant, and loves the people benevolently yet real benefit does not reach them widely. This truly comes from above and below deceiving one another and devoted to concealment. Box memorials and sealed pouches let those with grievances speak fully, and Your Majesty entrusts nearby ministers to select among them, showing intent to act on their words. Other deceptions may likewise be imagined. I therefore say the ruler's virtue values clarity above all.

Great ministers' conduct gradually diverges from their beginnings. Whoever proposed seeking candid speech is driven off on other grounds; remonstrance officials who speak even slightly directly are transferred to other posts. The loyal and indignant are called unrestful, the cutting and direct are eyed as fishing for fame, those whom popular resentment gathers are promoted in succession, and those whom public opinion favors are relegated outward in turn. A certain man's transfer was of one who once aggravated others' crimes to satisfy colleagues' private resentment; a certain man's promotion was of one who once cited ancient affairs to ornament recent heaven-sent omens. Upright men of heavy reputation are long cast aside over private dislike, while old villains are drawn back through clever pleas. If great ministers truly blocked the gate of favor and stopped the path of evil, promotion and demotion would be fitting and popular hearts would submit. I therefore say great ministers' hearts value fairness above all.

Censors in ordinary times never stand apart; when affairs arise they dare not speak to the full. When the Jurchens reopened relations, a matter most vital to the state, attendants and students alike strove in contention hoping to aid court strategy, yet those charged with speech uttered not one word. Beneath the capital, embezzlement reached vast sums and no one could stop it; while in prefectures and counties guilt of a hair's breadth was gathered to fill quotas. What great ministers wished to do was carried through; those they did not favor were pushed aside. In Emperor Renzong's time people mocked a chief councilor for following censors' wind and intent. Now people mock censors for daring not disobey the Secretariat. How can this be the ancestors' intent in establishing these offices? I therefore say censors' words value directness above all.

In pointing out defective affairs, this memorial was even more incisive than his former one.
32
宓遂請罷，歸。 在告日，擢太府丞，不拜，出知南康軍。 詣史彌遠別，彌遠曰：“子言甚切當，第愚昧不能行，殊有愧耳。 ”至官，歲大侵，奏蠲其賦十之九。 會流民群集，宓就役之，築江堤，而給其食。 時造白鹿洞，與諸生討論。 改知南劍州。 時大旱疫，蠲逋賦十數萬，且弛新輸三之一，躬率僚吏持錢粟藥餌戶給之。 創延平書院，悉仿白鹿洞之規。
Mi thereupon requested dismissal and returned home. While on leave he was promoted to vice director of the Court of the Imperial Treasury but declined the post and was appointed prefect of Nankang Commandery. When he went to bid Shi Miyuan farewell, Miyuan said, "Your words are very incisive and fitting, but I am too foolish to carry them out, and I am deeply ashamed." Upon reaching his post, a severe famine struck, and he memorialized to remit nine-tenths of the tax levy. When displaced people gathered in crowds, Mi put them to work building river dikes and provided them with food. At the time he restored Bailudong Academy and discussed learning with his students. He was transferred to serve as prefect of Nanjian Prefecture. A great drought and pestilence struck. He remitted overdue taxes numbering in the hundreds of thousands, relaxed new payments by one-third, and personally led officials door to door with money, grain, and medicine. He founded Yanping Academy, entirely following the regulations of Bailudong.
33
知漳州，未行，聞寧宗崩，嗚咽累日。 亡何，請致仕。 寶慶二年，提點廣東刑獄，章復三上，迄不就。 直秘閣，主管崇禧觀，宓拜祠命而辭職名。 卒，進職一等致仕。 三學諸生以起宓為請，而沒已閱月矣。
Appointed prefect of Zhangzhou, he had not yet departed when he heard that Emperor Ningzong had died and sobbed for many days. Before long he requested retirement. In the second year of Baoqing he was appointed judicial intendant of Guangdong, but submitted memorials three times and never took the post. He was made direct attendant of the Secretariat with charge of the Chongxi Shrine. Mi accepted the shrine appointment but declined the official title. He died and was granted one additional rank in retirement. Students of the Three Academies petitioned for Mi's recall, but he had already been dead for more than a month.
34
初，宓之在朝也，寺丞丁焴往使金，宓歎曰：“世仇未復，何以好為？ ”餞詩有“百年中國豈無人”之句。 後數年，聞關外不靖，以書抵焴曰：“蜀口去關外雖遠，實如一身。 近事可寒心，皆士大夫之罪，豈非賄道不絕之故耶？ ”焴服其言。
When Mi was still at court, Vice Director Ding Huang was sent as envoy to Jin. Mi sighed and said, "Our hereditary enemy is not yet restored. How can we act as if all is well?" His farewell poem contained the line, "For a hundred years has China truly lacked men?" Several years later, hearing that the region beyond the passes was unsettled, he wrote to Huang: "Shukou is far from beyond the passes, yet in truth they are one body." Recent affairs are chilling, and all are the fault of scholar-officials. Is this not because the path of bribery never ceases?" Huang accepted his argument.
35
宓天性剛毅，信道尤篤，嚐為《朱墨銘》，謂朱屬陽，墨屬陰，以驗理欲分寸之多寡。 自言居官必如顏真卿，居家必如陶潛，而深愛諸葛亮身死家無餘財，庫無餘帛。 庶乎能蹈其語者，端平初，殿中侍御史王遂首言：“宓事先帝有論諫之直，而不及俟聖化之更，宜褒其身後，以勸天下之為臣者。 ”帝為感動，詔贈直龍圖閣。 所著書有《論語注義問答》、《春秋三傳抄》、《讀通鑒綱目》、《唐史贅疣》之稿數十卷，藏於家。
Mi was firm and resolute by nature and especially devoted to the Way. He once wrote Inscription on Vermilion and Ink, saying vermilion belongs to yang and ink to yin, using them to measure the balance of principle and desire. He said that in office one must be like Yan Zhenqing and at home like Tao Qian, and he deeply admired Zhuge Liang, who at death left his family no surplus wealth and the treasury no surplus cloth. Whether one could truly live by his words is another matter. At the beginning of the Duanping reign, Palace Attendant Censor Wang Sui first said, "Mi served the late emperor with bold remonstrance but did not live to see the sage transformation renewed. He should be honored after death to encourage subjects throughout the realm." The emperor was moved and ordered him posthumously granted direct attendant of the Dragon Diagram Hall. His writings included Questions and Answers on the Meaning of the Analects Commentary, Extracts of the Three Commentaries on the Spring and Autumn, Reading the Comprehensive Mirror Outlines, and drafts of Excrescences on Tang History in several tens of scrolls, kept in his family.
36
王霆，字定叟，東陽人。 高大父豪，帥眾誅方臘，以功補官。 霆少有奇氣，試有司不偶，去就武舉，嘉定四年，中絕倫異等。 喬行簡考藝別頭，喜曰：“吾為朝廷得一帥才矣。 ”
Wang Ting, whose style was Dingsou, came from Dongyang. His great-grandfather Gao led troops to suppress Fang La and received office by merit. Ting in youth had unusual spirit. He failed the civil examinations, turned to the military examination, and in the fourth year of Jiading passed in the peerless extraordinary grade. Qiao Xingjian examined his arts as top candidate and said with joy, "I have found a commander for the court." "
37
授承節郎，從軍於鄂，帥鍾興嗣戍邊，請於樞密院，以霆為隨軍都錢糧官。 總領綦奎委霆專一教閱總效軍，尋委帥師守禦黃州。 沿江製置副使李辟置幕下，淮右兵叛，遣霆招諭之。 霆於軍事知無不言，謂：“招募良家子，不可以寅緣關節冒濫其間，防守江麵，全藉正軍，若義勇、民兵，特可為聲援耳。 而所謂大軍，羸病者多，兵械損舊，豈不敗事。 調兵防江，當於江岸創屋居之，使之專心守禦。 諸軍伍法既廢，平居則無以稽其虛籍冒請之敝，無以糾其竄逸生事之人，緩急則無以稽其並力向敵之誌，無以連其逃陳不進之心。 此尉繚子所以著束部伍之令，太公謂伍法為要者謂此也。 用兵不以人數多寡為勝負，惟教習之精否，則勝負之形可見矣。 ”
He was appointed Bearer of Credentials and joined the army at E. Commander Zhong Xingsi, garrisoning the frontier, requested from the Bureau of Military Affairs that Ting serve as the army's overall funds-and-grain officer. Overall director Qi Kui entrusted Ting exclusively to train the General Effectiveness Army, and soon entrusted him to command troops defending Huangzhou. Vice Pacification Commissioner Li along the Yangzi recruited him to his staff. When Huai West troops rebelled, he sent Ting to summon and instruct them. On military affairs Ting spoke without reserve. He said, "When recruiting sons of good families, one cannot let unqualified men slip in through connections. Defending the Yangzi relies entirely on regular troops; volunteers and militia can serve only as vocal support." Yet the so-called main army has many weak and sick men and damaged, outdated weapons. How can that not ruin affairs? Troops transferred to guard the river should be housed on the riverbank so they can concentrate on defense. Since company organization in the armies has been abandoned, in peacetime there is no way to audit empty registers and false claims or correct deserters who stir trouble. In emergencies there is no way to test their will to fight together or bind men who flee the line. This is why Master Wei Liao wrote orders binding company organization, and why the Grand Duke said company method is essential. Victory does not depend on numbers but on whether training is refined. Then the outcome can be foreseen. "
38
大帥薦之，召試為閣門舍人。 入對言：“恢復之說有二：曰規模，曰機會。 顧今日之規模安在哉？ 守令所以牧民，而惠養之未加； 將帥所以禦軍，而拊循之未至。 邦財未裕，而楮券之敝浸深； 軍儲未豐，而和糴之害徒慘。 官有土地而荒蕪，民因賦役而破蕩，獄訟類成冤抑，銓曹率多淹留。 薦舉無反坐，貪徒得以引類而通班； 按刺不徇公，微官易以迕意而連譴。 以言郡計，則紛耗於囊橐包苴； 以言戰功，則多私於親昵故舊。 至如降卒中處，養虎遺患，輕敵開邊，以肉喂虎。 夫以規模之切要者而不滿人意如此，臣敢輕進恢復之說以誤上聽哉？ 凡臣之所陳者，誠播告中外之臣，悉懲其舊而圖其新。 規模既立，然後義旗一麾，諸道並進，臣力尚壯，願效前驅。 惟陛下堅定而勉圖之。 ”帝稱其言可采。 升武功大夫，出知濠州，賜金帶。 至州，節浮費，糴粟買馬，以備不虞。 尋差知安豐軍，臣僚上言：“王霆在濠，人甚安之，不宜輕易。 ”詔再任濠，職事修舉，特轉橫班。 諸使交薦之。
The commander recommended him, and he was summoned and tested as Gate Attendant. At audience he said, "The theory of restoration has two parts: scale and opportunity." But where is today's scale? Prefects and magistrates exist to govern the people, yet fostering care is not added; Generals exist to command armies, yet comforting and guiding has not arrived. State finances are not ample, yet the evil of paper notes deepens daily; Military stores are not abundant, yet the harm of harmonized purchase grows only crueler. Government land lies waste, the people are broken by tax and corvée, prison cases mostly become wrongful suppression, and the selection office mostly detains candidates. Recommendations carry no counter-penalty, so greedy men draw their kind into court ranks; investigations do not follow fairness, and petty officials are easily punished in chains for offending superiors. Speaking of prefectural accounts, they are wasted on purses and bundles of gifts; speaking of battle merit, it mostly favors kin, intimates, and old friends. Surrendered soldiers are placed in the interior, nurturing tigers and leaving calamity behind; the enemy is treated lightly and the border opened—feeding tigers with flesh. If what is essential to scale fails to satisfy intent like this, how dare I lightly advance the theory of restoration and mislead Your Majesty? All that I have presented should be proclaimed to subjects within and without, that all may punish the old and plot the new. Once scale is established, then raise the righteous banner once and advance on every route together. My strength is still robust, and I wish to serve as vanguard. Only may Your Majesty be firm and strive to achieve it. The emperor said his words could be adopted. He was promoted to Martial Merit Grand Master, appointed prefect of Haozhou, and bestowed a gold belt. Upon reaching the prefecture, he cut unnecessary expenses, purchased grain and horses, and prepared against emergencies. Soon he was assigned to govern Anfeng Commandery. Court officials memorialized, "Wang Ting is at Hao and the people are very settled there. He should not be lightly moved." An edict ordered him to serve again at Hao. His duties were well carried out, and he was specially transferred to the lateral rank. Various commissioners repeatedly recommended him.
39
北兵至浮光，其民奔遁，相屬於道，朝論以為霆可守之，乃知光州兼沿邊都巡檢使。 冒雪夜行，倍道疾馳至州，分遣間探，整飭戰守之具，大戰於謝令橋，光人遂安。 督府魏了翁以書來慰安之，以緡錢十萬勞其軍。 霆以召，尋為吉州刺史，仍知光州。 霆固辭，丞相鄭清之、製置使史嵩之皆數以書留霆，霆不從，且曰：“士大夫當以世從道，不可以道從世也。 ”
Northern troops reached Fuguang and its people fled in panic along the roads. Court opinion held that Ting could defend the region, and he was made prefect of Guang Prefecture while also serving as frontier overall patrol commissioner. Braving snow he traveled by night and raced to the prefecture at double speed. He sent out scouts, put battle equipment in order, fought a great battle at Xieling Bridge, and the people of Guang were secured. Supervisor Wei Liaoweng sent a letter to comfort him and gave one hundred thousand strings to reward his army. Ting was summoned to court and soon made prefect of Ji Prefecture while still governing Guang Prefecture. Ting firmly declined. Chief Councilor Zheng Qingzhi and Pacification Commissioner Shi Songzhi repeatedly wrote to detain him, but Ting would not comply and said, "Scholar-officials should let the age follow the Way, not let the Way follow the age." "
40
再授閣門舍人，尋為達州刺史、右屯衛大將軍兼知蘄州，不赴。 尋遷淮西馬步軍副總管兼淮西遊擊軍副都統製。 論遊擊軍十事，不報。 提舉崇禧觀。 知高郵軍，流民邦傑聚眾三千人為盜，霆剿其渠魁，餘黨悉散。 時議出師，和者甚多，霆以為：“莫若遣間探覘敵情，如不得已然後行之； 否則無故自蕩其根本，是外兵未至而內兵先慘烈也。 ”諸軍畢行，惟高郵遲之，境內賴以安全。 由是與時迕，而讒者益眾。
He was again appointed Gate Attendant, then made prefect of Da Prefecture and Grand General of the Right Garrison Guard while also governing Qizhou, but did not go. Soon he was made vice overall commander of Huai West horse and foot armies and vice overall controller of Huai West mobile armies. He discussed ten matters concerning the mobile army, but received no response. He was made intendant of the Chongxi Shrine. As prefect of Gaoyou Commandery, he suppressed a band of three thousand displaced people led by Bang Jie; the ringleaders were killed and the rest scattered. At the time expedition was debated and many favored it. Ting argued, "Better to send scouts to observe enemy conditions, and act only if there is no other way; otherwise the court will shake its own foundation without cause, and inner troops will suffer bitter slaughter before outer enemies even arrive." All armies marched, but Gaoyou delayed, and the region relied on that delay for safety. Because of this he went against the times, and slanderers grew more numerous.
41
提舉雲台觀。 執政期論邊事，且謂朝廷即有齊安之命。 霆曰：“秋防已急，邊守不宜臨時更易，盍少需之。 ”乃授帶行左領軍衛大將軍，充沿江製置副使司計議官，霆乃撰《沿江等邊志》一編上之。 製置使董槐、鄧泳交薦之，差知壽昌軍，改蘄州，建學舍，祠忠臣。 嚐歎曰：“兩淮藩籬也，大江門戶也，三輔堂奧也。 藩籬不固則門戶且危，門戶既危則堂奧豈能久安乎？ ”於是貽書丞相杜範，乞瞰江審察形勢，置三新城：蘄春置於龍眼磯，安慶置於孟城，滁陽置於宣化。 不報。 卒。
He was made intendant of the Yuntai Shrine. The councilors scheduled discussion of frontier affairs and said the court would soon appoint him to Qian'an. Ting said, "Autumn defense is already urgent. Frontier defenders should not be changed at the last moment. Why not wait a little?" He was then appointed Grand General of the Left Army Guard with belt and planning officer of the Vice Pacification Commission along the Yangzi. Ting compiled Records of Frontier Regions along the River and presented it. Pacification Commissioners Dong Huai and Deng Yong repeatedly recommended him. He was assigned to govern Shouchang Commandery, transferred to Qizhou, built a school hall, and enshrined loyal ministers. He once sighed and said, "The Two Huai are the fence, the Great River is the gate, and the Three Metropolises are the inner hall." If the fence is not secure, the gate will soon be in peril. If the gate is in peril, how can the inner hall long remain secure? He then wrote to Chief Councilor Du Fan, asking to survey the Yangzi and examine the strategic situation, and establish three new cities: Qichun at Longyan Jetty, Anqing at Mengcheng, and Chuyang at Xuanhua. No response was given. He died.
42
初，其父析業，霆獨以讓其兄。 處宗族有恩意，嚐訓其子弟曰：“窮理盡性，學之本也。 ”有《玉溪集》行於世。
When his father divided the family estate, Ting alone yielded his share to his elder brother. He treated his clan with kindness and once instructed his sons and nephews, "Exhausting principle and fulfilling nature is the root of learning." His Jade Stream Collected Works circulated in his day.
43
論曰：吳昌裔訪道東南，一何勤哉！ 故其造深醇，見諸事功者，足以知其學無雜也。 汪綱之遺愛在越，先民所謂擇賢久任者，固不我欺矣。 陳宓以宰相子，論諫之直，於今有光。 王霆通兵家言，而謂不可以道從世，此古人謀帥貴乎“說《禮》、《樂》而敦《詩》、《書》”也。
The historians comment: Wu Changyi sought the Way in the southeast—how diligent he was! His achievement was deep and pure, and what appears in his practical accomplishments shows that his learning had no admixture. Wang Gang's lingering benevolence was in Yue. What the ancients called choosing the worthy and employing them long—the ancients did not deceive us. Chen Mi, though a chief councilor's son, had a remonstrance so direct that it still shines today. Wang Ting mastered military theory yet said one cannot let the Way follow the age. This is what the ancients meant in seeking commanders who "discuss Rites and Music and cherish the Odes and Documents."
