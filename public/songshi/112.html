1
禮十五 〈 （嘉禮三）〉 ○聖節諸慶節
Rites 15 ( Auspicious Rites 3) Imperial Birthday and Other Celebratory Festivals
2
聖節。 建隆元年，群臣請以二月十六日為長春節。 正月十七日，於大相國寺建道場以祝壽，至日，上壽退，百僚詣寺行香。 尋詔：“今後長春節及諸慶節，常參官、致仕官、僧道、百姓等毋得進奉。 ”
Imperial Birthday In Jianlong 1 (960), the ministers asked that the emperor's birthday on the sixteenth day of the second month be established as the Changchun Festival. On the seventeenth day of the first month, a longevity ritual was opened at the Great Xiangguo Monastery; when the day came, the emperor withdrew after receiving birthday felicitations, and all officials went to the monastery to burn incense. An edict soon followed: "From now on, at the Changchun Festival and other celebratory festivals, officials on regular attendance, retired officials, clergy, and the common people must not present tribute offerings. "
3
太宗以十月七日為乾明節，復改為壽寧節。
Taizong's birthday was first observed as the Qianming Festival on the seventh day of the tenth month, then renamed the Shouning Festival.
4
真宗以十二月二日為承天節。 其儀：帝御長春殿，諸王上壽，次樞密使副、宣徽、三司使，次使相，次管軍節度使、兩使留後、觀察使，次節度使至觀察使，次皇親任觀察使以下，各上壽，仍以金酒器、銀香合、馬、袖表為獻。 既畢，鹹赴崇德殿敘班，宰相率百官上壽，賜酒三行，皆用教坊樂，賜衣一襲，文武群臣、方鎮州軍皆有貢禮。 前一月，百官、內職、牧伯各就佛寺修齋祝壽，罷日以香賜之，仍各設會，賜上尊酒及諸果，百官兼賜教坊樂。
Zhenzong's birthday was established as the Chengtian Festival on the second day of the twelfth month. The procedure was as follows: the emperor presided in the Changchun Hall. Princes offered birthday felicitations first, followed in order by the vice commissioners of the Bureau of Military Affairs, the commissioners of the Palace and Inner Service, and the Three Departments; then commissioners with ministerial rank; then army commanders, acting commissioners, and observation commissioners; then military commissioners through observation commissioners; then imperial clansmen of observation commissioner rank and below. Each group offered felicitations and presented gold wine vessels, silver incense boxes, horses, and sleeve memorials. Afterward everyone assembled in the Chongde Hall in proper order. The chief minister led the officials in offering birthday felicitations. The court granted three rounds of wine with Court Entertainment Office music and one suit of clothing; civil and military officials and the regional commands all presented tribute. A month in advance, officials, inner-service staff, and regional governors observed vegetarian fasts at Buddhist temples to pray for the emperor's long life. When the fast ended, they received incense as a gift and held banquets at which fine wine and fruit were served; officials also received music from the Court Entertainment Office.
5
景德二年，始令樞密三司使副、學士復赴百官齋會，少卿、監、刺史以上及近職一子賜恩，僧道則賜紫衣、師號，禁屠，輟刑。
In Jingde 2 (1005), vice commissioners of the Bureau of Military Affairs and the Three Departments and Hanlin academicians were again required to attend the officials' fasting banquets. Junior ministers, supervisors, and prefects and above, plus one son of each close attendant, received imperial favor. Monks and Daoists received purple robes and honorific titles, slaughter was banned, and executions were suspended.
6
仁宗以四月十四日為乾元節，正月八日皇太后為長寧節。 詔定長寧節上壽儀：太后垂簾崇政殿，百官及契丹使班庭下，宰臣以下進奉上壽，閣門使於殿上簾外立侍，百官再拜，宰臣升殿，跪進酒簾外，內臣跪承以入。 宰臣奏曰：“長寧節，臣等不勝歡抃，謹上千萬歲壽。 ”復降，再拜，三稱萬歲。 內臣承旨宣曰：“得公等壽酒，與公等同喜。 ”鹹再拜。 宰臣升殿，內侍出簾外跪授虛盞，宰臣跪受，降，再拜舞蹈，三稱萬歲。 內侍承旨宣群臣升殿，再拜，升，陳進奉物當殿庭，通事舍人稱“宰臣以下進奉”，客省使殿上喝“進奉出”。 內謁者監進第二盞，賜酒三行，侍中奏禮畢，皆再拜舞蹈。 太后還內，百官詣內東門拜表稱賀。 其外命婦舊入內者即入內上壽，不入內者進表。 內侍引內命婦上壽，次引外命婦，如百官儀。 次日大宴。
Renzong's birthday was the Qianyuan Festival on the fourteenth day of the fourth month; the Empress Dowager's was the Changning Festival on the eighth day of the first month. An edict prescribed the Changning birthday ceremony: the Empress Dowager held audience behind a curtain in the Chongzheng Hall while officials and the Khitan envoy stood in the courtyard. Chief ministers and below presented birthday felicitations. Gate commissioners waited outside the curtain on the dais. Officials bowed twice; the chief minister ascended, knelt, and offered wine at the curtain; inner attendants knelt to receive it and carry it inside. The chief minister reported: "On the Changning Festival, we cannot contain our joy and respectfully wish Your Majesty ten thousand myriad years of life. " He withdrew, bowed twice, and thrice shouted "Long live the Empress!" An inner attendant, conveying the imperial response, announced: "Having received your birthday wine, I share this joy with you. " Everyone bowed again. The chief minister ascended again. An inner attendant knelt outside the curtain and handed over an empty cup; the chief minister knelt to receive it, withdrew, bowed twice, performed the dance obeisance, and thrice shouted "Long live the Empress!" An inner attendant announced that the ministers should ascend. They bowed twice, went up, and displayed tribute in the central courtyard. The relay secretary called "Tribute from the chief ministers and below"; the guest-reception commissioner on the dais called "Withdraw the tribute." The inner usher director presented the second cup. Three rounds of wine were granted. When the attendant-in-chief reported that the rites were finished, everyone bowed twice and performed the dance obeisance. The Empress Dowager withdrew to the inner quarters; officials went to the inner east gate to present congratulatory memorials. Outer consorts who had previously been admitted to the palace entered to offer birthday felicitations; those not admitted submitted memorials instead. Inner attendants led inner consorts to offer birthday felicitations, then outer consorts, following the officials' procedure. A grand banquet was held the next day.
7
英宗以正月三日為壽聖節。 禮官奏：“故事，聖節上壽，親王、樞密於長春殿，宰臣、百官於崇德殿，天聖諒沍皆於崇政殿。 ”於是紫宸上壽，群臣升殿間，飲獻一觴而退。 又一日，賜宴於錫慶院。
Yingzong's birthday was established as the Shousheng Festival on the third day of the first month. The ritual officials reported: "By precedent, birthday felicitations for the emperor had princes and the Bureau of Military Affairs in the Changchun Hall and chief ministers and officials in the Chongde Hall; during the Tiansheng mourning period everyone used the Chongzheng Hall. " Birthday felicitations were therefore held in the Zichen Hall; ministers ascended in turn, drank, presented one cup, and withdrew. On another day a banquet was granted at the Xiqing Court.
8
神宗以熙寧元年四月十日為同天節，以宅憂罷上壽，惟拜表稱賀。 明年，親王、樞密使、管軍、駙馬、諸司使副詣垂拱殿，宰臣、百官、大國使詣紫宸殿上壽，命坐，賜酒三行，不舉樂。 明年，以大旱，罷同天節上壽，群臣赴東上閣門表賀。
In Xining 1 (1068), Shenzong's birthday on the tenth day of the fourth month became the Tongtian Festival. While he was in mourning for his father, birthday felicitations were suspended and officials offered congratulatory memorials only. The following year princes, the Bureau of Military Affairs commissioner, army commanders, imperial sons-in-law, and vice commissioners of the various offices went to the Chui'gong Hall, while chief ministers, officials, and envoys of great states offered birthday felicitations in the Zichen Hall. They were seated and granted three rounds of wine without music. The next year, because of severe drought, Tongtian Festival birthday felicitations were canceled; ministers presented congratulatory memorials at the eastern upper Gate Office.
9
中書門下言：“同天節上壽班，自今樞密使副、宣徽、三司使、殿前馬步軍副都指揮使以上共作一班，進酒一盞； 親王、宗室、使相至觀察、駙馬、管軍觀察使以上，皆赴紫宸殿，依本班序立上壽，更不赴垂拱殿。 ”蓋以管軍觀察使以上及親王、駙馬並於垂拱殿以官序高下各班進酒畢而日晏，外朝有班者仍詣紫宸殿，議者以為近瀆，改焉。 而詔袒免以上宗婦聽班賀於禁中。
The Secretariat and Chancellery reported: "For Tongtian Festival birthday formations, henceforth the vice commissioners of the Bureau of Military Affairs, the commissioners of the Palace and Inner Service and the Three Departments, and the vice commanders-in-chief of the Palace and Horse Armies and above shall form a single formation and present one cup of wine together. Princes, imperial clansmen, commissioners with ministerial rank through observation commissioners, imperial sons-in-law, and army observation commissioners and above shall all go to the Zichen Hall, stand in birthday formation by their usual ranks, and no longer attend the Chui'gong Hall. " The change was made because army observation commissioners and above, together with princes and imperial sons-in-law, had each presented wine in separate rank-ordered formations at the Chui'gong Hall until evening, after which outer-court officials still had to proceed to the Zichen Hall—critics considered this nearly irreverent. An edict also allowed imperial clanswomen of the fifth mourning degree and above to join formation and offer congratulations within the palace.
10
哲宗即位，詔以太皇太后七月十六日為坤成節。 宰臣請以十二月八日為興龍節。 哲宗本七日生，以避僖祖忌，故後一日。
When Zhezong ascended the throne, an edict established the Grand Empress Dowager's birthday as the Kuncheng Festival on the sixteenth day of the seventh month. The chief ministers asked that the eighth day of the twelfth month be established as the Xinglong Festival. Zhezong was actually born on the seventh day, but the observance was moved one day later to avoid the taboo anniversary of Emperor Xizu.
11
徽宗以十月十日為天寧節，定上壽儀：皇帝御垂拱殿，群臣通班起居畢，分班，從義郎以下醫官、待詔等先退。 知引進司官一員讀奏目，知東上閣門官一員奏進壽酒，由東階升，舍人通教坊使以下讚再拜，奏聖躬萬福，又再拜，復位。 次看盞人稍前，舍人讚再拜，讚上殿祗候，分東西兩階立，俟進酒升殿。 次舍人引親王入殿庭，北向立，讚再拜，班首奏萬福。 舍人引進奉西入，列於親王後，酒器簷床置馬前，揖天武躬奏萬福，進奉馬先出。 內侍進禦茶床，殿中監酹酒訖，知東上閣門官殿上躬奏：“親王某以下進壽酒。 ”舍人揖親王以下躬讚再拜，乃引親王二員升殿，知東上閣門官引詣御坐前，舍人東階下西向立， 〈 （後準此。）〉 尚醞典禦奉槃、盞授班首，搢笏受盤、盞，西向立，奉禦啟盞，親王一員搢笏注酒，班首奉詣御坐東進訖，少退，虛跪，興，以槃授典禦，退，閣門引降階。 舍人引當殿北向立，東上，讚拜，興，搢笏跪奉表，舍人接表，一員在東，餘詣親王西，置表笏上，授引進。 知引進司官殿上讀奏目，退，親王以下俯伏，興，躬，舍人讚再拜，引班首升東階，餘殿下分立。 閣門引詣御坐東，北向搢笏，尚醞典禦如前奉盤立，樂作，皇帝飲訖，受盞，復位，再拜如上儀。 知引進司官詣折檻東，西向宣曰“進奉收”。 讚拜，舞蹈，又再拜，西出。 親王以下赴紫宸殿立班。 引進官宣“進奉出”，天武奉進奉以出。 閣門復立殿上，教坊使讚送禦酒，又再拜，教坊致語訖，讚再拜，退。 次樞密官上壽，次管軍觀察以上上壽、進奉並如儀。 內侍舉禦茶床，舍人讚教坊使以下謝祗應，再拜訖，閣門側奏無公事。
Huizong's birthday became the Tianning Festival on the tenth day of the tenth month. The birthday ritual was fixed as follows: the emperor presided in the Chui'gong Hall. After the assembled ministers completed their general salutation and formed ranks, medical officers, awaiting-edict officials, and those below gentleman for meritorious service withdrew first. An official of the Reception Directorate read the memorial list. An eastern upper Gate Office official reported the presentation of birthday wine and ascended by the eastern steps. The usher had those below the Court Entertainment Office commissioner bow twice, report the emperor's ten thousand blessings of body, bow twice again, and return to position. Next the cup-inspector advanced slightly. The usher called for two bows, then for attendance on the hall. Attendants divided between the eastern and western steps and waited for the wine presentation to ascend. Next the usher led the princes into the hall courtyard to stand facing north, called for two bows, and the formation leader reported ten thousand blessings. The usher led tribute offerings in from the west and arrayed them behind the princes. Wine vessels on the eave couch were set before the horses. The Tianwu guards bowed and reported ten thousand blessings, and the tribute horses were led out first. Inner attendants brought forward the imperial tea couch. The director of the palace service libated the wine. The eastern upper Gate Office official on the dais bowed and reported: "Prince So-and-so and below present birthday wine. " The usher had the princes and below bow and called for two bows, then led two princes up the hall. The eastern upper Gate Office official led them before the imperial seat while the usher stood on the eastern steps below facing west. ( (Hereafter follow this precedent.) The imperial brew master presented tray and cup to the formation leader, who inserted his tablet, received them, and stood facing west. The imperial attendant opened the cup; one prince inserted his tablet and poured the wine. The formation leader bore it east of the imperial seat and presented it, then withdrew slightly, performed an empty kneeling obeisance, rose, handed the tray to the brew master, withdrew, and the Gate Office led him down the steps. The usher led those on duty in the hall to stand facing north and advance east. He called for bowing, rising, inserting the tablet, and kneeling to present the memorial. The usher received the memorial; one stood on the east while the rest went west of the princes, placed the memorial on the tablet, and handed it to the reception official. The Reception Directorate official read the memorial list on the dais and withdrew. The princes and below prostrated themselves, rose, and bowed. The usher called for two bows and led the formation leader up the eastern steps while the rest stood divided below. The Gate Office led them east of the imperial seat. Facing north they inserted their tablets while the imperial brew master presented the tray as before. Music sounded. When the emperor finished drinking, he received the cup, returned to position, and bowed twice as above. The Reception Directorate official went east of the balustrade, faced west, and announced "Tribute received." He called for bowing and the dance obeisance, then two more bows, and they exited to the west. The princes and below went to the Zichen Hall to form ranks. The reception commissioner announced "Tribute withdrawn," and the Tianwu guards bore the tribute out. The Gate Office again stood on the dais. The Court Entertainment Office commissioner called for presenting the imperial wine; they bowed twice again. When the Court Entertainment address was finished, he called for two bows and they withdrew. Next Bureau of Military Affairs officials offered birthday felicitations; next army observation commissioners and above offered felicitations and tribute, all following the same ritual. Inner attendants raised the imperial tea couch. The usher had those below the Court Entertainment Office commissioner thank the court for attending. When the bows were finished, the Gate Office reported from the side that there was no official business.
12
皇帝赴紫宸殿後閣受群臣上壽。 質明，三公以下百僚並於殿門外就次，東上閣門、御史台、太常寺分引入詣殿庭東西立。 閣門附內侍進班齊牌，皇帝出閣，禁衛諸班親從迎駕，自讚常起居。 皇帝升坐，鳴鞭，禮直官、通事舍人引三公至執政官，御史台、東上閣門分引百官，並橫行北向立，典儀讚再拜舞蹈，班首奏萬福，又再拜訖，分東西立。 禮直官引殿中監、少監升東階，詣酒尊所稍西，南向西上立，舍人揖教坊使以下通班大起居，次看盞人謝升殿，讚再拜。 內侍進禦茶床，殿侍酹酒訖，禮直官、通事舍人分引三公至執政官，御史台、東上閣門分引百僚，並橫行北向立，典儀讚再拜，讚者承傳，在位官皆再拜。 禮直官、通事舍人引上公升東階，東上閣門官接引升殿，授盞、啟盞如上儀。 上公詣御坐，俯伏跪奏：“文武百僚、上公具官臣某等稽首言：天寧令節，臣等不勝大慶，謹上千萬歲壽。 ”俯伏，興，退，降階，舍人接引復位，典儀讚再拜訖，禮直官引知樞密院官詣御坐前承旨，退詣折檻稍東，西向宣曰：“得公等壽酒，與公等內外同慶。 ”典儀讚拜如儀，百官分東西立。 禮直官、通事舍人引上公升東階，東上閣門官接引詣御坐東，搢笏，殿中監授盤，上公奉進御坐東，北向，樂作，皇帝飲訖，閣門引接盞，降，復位典儀讚拜如上儀。 宗室遙郡以下先退。 禮直官引樞密院官詣御坐前承旨，退詣折檻稍東，宣曰：“宣群臣升殿。 ”典儀讚拜訖，禮直官、通事舍人分引三公以下升東階，親王、使相以下升西階； 御史台、東上閣門分引秘書監以下升兩朵殿，並東西廊席後立。 尚醞典禦以盞授殿中監，奉禦啟盞，殿中監西向立，殿中少監以酒注於盞， 〈 （第二、第三準此。）〉 奉詣御坐前，躬進訖，少退，奉盤西向立。 樂作，皇帝飲訖，殿中監接盞退，授奉禦，出笏復位。 通事舍人分引殿上官橫行北向，舍人讚再拜，典儀曰“再拜”，讚者承傳，皆再拜。 舍人讚就坐，各立席後，復讚就坐，群官皆坐。 酒初行，先宰臣，次百官，皆作樂。 尚食典禦、奉禦進食，太官設群官食，皇帝再舉酒，群官興，立席後，樂作，飲訖，舍人讚就坐，再行群官酒，皇帝三舉酒，並如第二之儀。 酒三行，舍人曰“可起”，群官興，立席後。 若宣示盞，即隨東上閣門官以下揖，稱“宣示盞”，躬，讚就坐。 若宣勸，即立席後，躬，飲訖，讚再拜。 內侍舉禦茶床，禮直官引左輔詣御坐前北向俯伏跪奏：“左輔具官臣某言禮畢。 ”俯伏，興，退，復位。 禮直官、通事舍人分引三公以下文武百僚降階橫行北向立，樞密院官在親王後。 典儀讚再拜，皆舞蹈再拜退。
The emperor went to the rear chamber of the Zichen Hall to receive birthday felicitations from the assembled ministers. At daybreak the three dukes and all officials below took their places outside the hall gates. The eastern upper Gate Office, the Censorate, and the Court of Imperial Sacrifices led them in separately to stand east and west in the hall courtyard. The Gate Office had inner attendants present the formation tally-board. The emperor came out of the chamber. Guard formations and close attendants welcomed the procession and called the regular salutation themselves. The emperor ascended and took his seat; the whip sounded. Ritual officers and relay secretaries led the three dukes through the chief administrators; the Censorate and eastern upper Gate Office led the hundred officials separately. All stood in horizontal formation facing north. The master of ceremonies called for two bows and the dance obeisance. The formation leader reported ten thousand blessings, bowed twice again, then divided east and west. Ritual officers led the director and vice-director of the palace service up the eastern steps to the wine altar slightly west, standing south-facing and advancing west. The usher had those below the Court Entertainment Office commissioner perform the great salutation in full formation. Next the cup-inspector thanked for ascending the hall and called for two bows. Inner attendants brought forward the imperial tea couch. The palace attendant libated the wine. Ritual officers and relay secretaries again led the three dukes through the chief administrators while the Censorate and eastern upper Gate Office led the hundred officials separately. All stood in horizontal formation facing north. The master of ceremonies called for two bows; the caller transmitted the call; every official in attendance bowed twice. Ritual officers and relay secretaries led the senior duke up the eastern steps. The eastern upper Gate Office official led him onto the hall. Presenting and opening the cup followed the ritual above. The senior duke went before the imperial seat, prostrated himself kneeling, and reported: "The civil and military officials, the senior duke with full title, your servant and others bow our heads and say: On the Tianning command festival we cannot contain our great rejoicing and respectfully wish Your Majesty ten thousand myriad years of life. " He prostrated himself, rose, withdrew, and descended the steps while the usher led him back to position. When the master of ceremonies called for two bows, ritual officers led the Bureau of Military Affairs official before the imperial seat to receive the order, withdrew to slightly east of the balustrade, faced west, and announced: "Having received your birthday wine, I share this joy with you within and without. " The master of ceremonies called for bowing as in the ritual, and officials divided east and west. Ritual officers and relay secretaries led the senior duke up the eastern steps. The eastern upper Gate Office official led him east of the imperial seat. He inserted his tablet; the director of the palace service presented the tray; the senior duke bore it east of the imperial seat facing north. Music sounded. When the emperor finished drinking, the Gate Office led him to receive the cup, descend, and return to position; the master of ceremonies called for bowing as above. Imperial clansmen of remote commanderies and below withdrew first. Ritual officers led the Bureau of Military Affairs official before the imperial seat to receive the order, withdrew to slightly east of the balustrade, and announced: "It is ordered that the assembled ministers ascend the hall. " When the master of ceremonies finished calling for bows, ritual officers and relay secretaries led the three dukes and below up the eastern steps and princes and commissioners with ministerial rank and below up the western steps. The Censorate and eastern upper Gate Office led those from the Directorate of the Palace Library and below up the two flower halls, all standing behind the east and west corridor seats. The imperial brew master presented the cup to the director of the palace service. The imperial attendant opened the cup. The director stood facing west while the vice-director poured wine into the cup. ( (The second and third rounds follow this procedure.) He bore it before the imperial seat, bowed and presented it, withdrew slightly, and stood facing west holding the tray. Music sounded. When the emperor finished drinking, the director received the cup and withdrew, handing it to the imperial attendant; they removed their tablets and returned to position. Relay secretaries led officials on the hall into horizontal formation facing north. The usher called for two bows. The master of ceremonies said "Bow again"; the caller transmitted; everyone bowed again. The usher called to be seated; each stood behind his seat; called again to be seated; and all officials sat. When wine was first served, chief ministers came first, then the hundred officials, with music throughout. The imperial food master and attendant presented food. The Imperial Kitchen set out food for the assembled officials. The emperor raised his cup a second time; officials rose and stood behind their seats while music played. When they finished drinking, the usher called them to sit. Wine was served again to the assembled officials. The emperor raised his cup a third time, all following the second-round procedure. After three rounds of wine the usher said "You may rise"; the assembled officials rose and stood behind their seats. If an imperial cup was displayed, they followed the eastern upper Gate Office officials in bowing, saying "Imperial cup displayed," bowing, and sitting when the usher called. If the emperor encouraged drinking, they stood behind their seats, bowed, drank, and the usher called for two bows. Inner attendants raised the imperial tea couch. Ritual officers led the left chief counselor before the imperial seat facing north. He prostrated himself kneeling and reported: "The left chief counselor with full title, your servant, reports that the rites are complete. " He prostrated himself, rose, withdrew, and returned to position. Ritual officers and relay secretaries led the three dukes and civil and military officials down the steps to stand in horizontal formation facing north, with Bureau of Military Affairs officials behind the princes. The master of ceremonies called for two bows. All performed the dance obeisance, bowed twice again, and withdrew.
13
靖康元年四月十三日，太宰徐處仁等表請為乾龍節。 至日，皇帝帥百官詣龍德宮上壽畢，即本宮賜侍從官以上宴。
On the thirteenth day of the fourth month in Jingkang 1 (1126), Grand Counselor Xu Churen and others memorialized asking that the emperor's birthday be established as the Qianlong Festival. On the day itself the emperor led the hundred officials to the Longde Palace to offer birthday felicitations. When that was finished, a banquet was granted at the palace to close attendants and above.
14
建炎元年五月，宰臣等上言，請以五月二十一日為天申節。 詔曰：“朕承祖宗遺澤，獲托士民之上，求所以扶危持顛之道，未知攸濟。 念二聖鑾輿在遠，萬民失業，將士暴露，夙夜痛悼，寢食幾廢，況以眇躬之故，聞樂飲酒，以自為樂乎？ 非惟深拂朕誌，實增感於朕心。 所有將來天申節百官上壽常禮，可令寢罷。 ”至是止就佛寺啟散祝壽道場，詣閣門或後殿拜表稱賀。
In the fifth month of Jianyan 1 (1127), the chief ministers memorialized asking that the twenty-first day of the fifth month be established as the Tianshen Festival. The edict read: "We have received our ancestors' legacy and been placed above the people. We seek how to steady what is tottering and save what is falling, and do not yet know the way. The two emperors remain captive far away, the people have lost their livelihoods, and soldiers lie exposed in the field. We grieve day and night and can scarcely eat or sleep. How could we, for our slight person alone, hear music and drink wine for our own pleasure? That would not only deeply violate our intent; it would truly add to the grief in our heart. The regular ritual by which officials offer birthday felicitations at the coming Tianshen Festival is therefore suspended. " From then on only Buddhist monasteries opened and closed the longevity ritual arenas; officials offered congratulatory memorials at the Gate Office or the rear hall.
15
紹興十三年二月，臣僚奏：“臣聞孝理天下者，帝王之盛德，歸美報上者，臣子之至誠。 是皆因性自然，發於觀感，必各盡其至，然後為稱。 恭惟陛下撫艱難之運，憂勤在禦，兢兢業業，圖濟中興，孝德通於神明，皇天為之悔禍，長樂還闕，適當誕節之前，陛下以天下養，獲伸宮闈上壽之儀，臣民得於觀聽，天下無不欣慶，所以崇大養而成孝理之功者，既以盡善盡美矣。 陛下誕聖佳辰，乃臣子所願奉觴上壽，以盡歸美之意，其可不舉而行之乎？ 臣愚，欲望將來天申節許令有司舉行舊典，至日，百官得以奉萬年之觴，仰祝聖壽，天下幸甚。 ”太常、禮部討論：每遇聖節，樞密院以下先詣垂拱殿上壽畢，宰臣率百僚於紫宸殿上壽。 前一月，分日啟建道場，至前一日，樞密院官滿散依例作齋筵。 至日，三省官上壽立班訖，次赴滿散作齋筵。 後二日，大宴於集英殿。 時命御史台、太常寺修立儀注。
In the second month of Shaoxing 13 (1143), officials memorialized: "We have heard that ruling the realm through filial piety is the supreme virtue of emperors and kings, and repaying one's superiors with praise is the utmost sincerity of subjects. Such things arise naturally from character, spring from what one observes and feels, and each must be carried to its fullest before it can be called fitting. We respectfully consider that Your Majesty has embraced a time of hardship, remains anxious and diligent on the throne, and strives tirelessly for restoration. Filial virtue has moved Heaven itself: Changle has returned to the palace just before the birthday season. Nourished by the realm, Your Majesty could at last perform the palace birthday ritual for all to witness, and none under Heaven failed to rejoice. The work of exalting great nourishment and completing filial governance has already reached its fullest measure. Your Majesty's sacred birthday is when subjects wish to raise cups and offer long life to fulfill their duty of praise—how can we not carry this out? We humbly ask that at the coming Tianshen Festival the responsible offices be allowed to restore the old precedents so that on the day officials may present cups of ten thousand years and wish Your Majesty long life—a great blessing for the realm. " The Court of Imperial Sacrifices and the Ministry of Rites deliberated: on each imperial birthday, officials from the Bureau of Military Affairs and below first offered felicitations in the Chui'gong Hall; when that was finished, chief ministers led the hundred officials to offer felicitations in the Zichen Hall. A month in advance ritual arenas were opened on separate days. On the eve of the festival, Bureau of Military Affairs officials held the concluding feast as usual. On the day itself, officials of the Three Departments completed their birthday formation, then attended the concluding feast. Two days later a grand banquet was held in the Jiying Hall. At that time the Censorate and the Court of Imperial Sacrifices were ordered to draft ritual regulations.
16
孝宗隆興元年，太上皇帝天申節，皇帝及宰臣、文武百僚詣德壽宮上壽。 是日，以欽宗大祥，前一日，皇帝起居如宮中儀，百僚拜表稱賀。
In Longxing 1 (1163), on the Retired Emperor's Tianshen Festival, the emperor led chief ministers and civil and military officials to the Deshou Palace to offer birthday felicitations. Because it was the great mourning for Emperor Qinzong, on the day before the emperor performed palace salutations as usual while officials presented congratulatory memorials.
17
乾道八年，立皇太子，皇帝率皇太子及文武百僚詣德壽宮上壽。 前期，儀鸞司陳設德壽宮殿門之內外，設御坐於殿上當中南向，設大次於德壽宮門內南向，小次於殿東廊西向，設皇帝褥位二：一於御坐東南，西向； 一於御坐之南，北向。 尚醞設禦酒尊、酒器於御坐之東，有司又設禦茶床於御坐之西，俱稍北。 其日，文武百僚內不係從駕者，並先赴德壽宮門外以俟迎駕起居。 質明，皇帝服靴袍出即御坐，從駕臣僚、禁衛起居如常儀。 皇帝降坐，乘輦將至德壽宮，文武百僚迎駕再拜起居訖，前導官、太常卿、閣門官、太常博士、禮直官先入，詣大次前分左右立定。 皇帝降輦入次，御史台、閣門、太常寺分引皇太子並文武百僚入詣殿廷，東西相向立定，前導官導皇帝入小次，簾降。 皇太子並文武百僚並橫行北向立。 太上皇帝出宮升御坐，鳴鞭，小次簾卷。 前導官導皇帝升殿東階，詣殿折檻前北向褥位，再拜，躬奏聖躬萬福，再拜，皇帝詣太上皇帝御坐之東褥位西向立，前導官於殿上隨地之宜立。 次舍人揖皇太子並文武百僚躬，典儀曰“再拜”，讚者承傳，在位官皆再拜，搢笏舞蹈，又再拜，皇太子不離位，奏聖躬萬福，各再拜，直身，分東西相向立。 禮直官引奉盤盞官、受盤盞官、承旨宣答官、奏禮畢官、殿中監、少監升殿。 內侍進禦茶床，尚醞典禦以盤盞、酒注授殿中監、少監，次禮直官引奉盤盞官詣酒尊所北向，殿中監啟盞，殿中少監注酒，奉盤盞官奉酒詣皇帝前北向，禮直官引受盤盞官詣太上皇帝御坐前，西向立，皇太子並文武百僚橫行北向立。 奉盤盞官躬進皇帝，皇帝奉酒，前導官導皇帝詣太上皇帝御坐前躬進訖，少後，以盤授受盤盞官。 前導官導皇帝詣太上御坐前褥位北向俯伏跪，殿下皇太子並百僚皆躬身。 皇帝奏：“臣某謹率文武百僚稽首言：天申令節，臣某與百僚等不勝大慶，謹上千萬歲壽。 ”奏訖，伏，興，再拜，在位官皆再拜。 承旨宣答官宣曰：“得皇帝壽酒，與皇帝並百僚內外同慶。 ”皇帝再拜，在位官皆再拜訖，分東西相向立。 皇帝詣御坐東，西向立，奉盤盞官以盤北向恭進，皇帝奉盤，樂作，俟太上皇帝飲酒，皇帝躬接盞訖，皇帝少後，以盤盞授受盤盞官，以授殿中監，各復位立。 皇太子並文武百僚橫行北向，皇帝詣褥位北向再拜，在位官皆再拜。 皇帝詣太上御坐東褥位西向立，皇太子、文武百僚再拜，搢笏舞蹈，又再拜訖，內侍舉茶床，奏禮畢官北向俯伏跪奏：“具官臣某言禮畢。 ”在位官再拜。 太上皇帝駕興，皇帝從入，文武百僚以次退。
In Qiandao 8 (1172), when the heir apparent was established, the emperor led the heir apparent and civil and military officials to the Deshou Palace to offer birthday felicitations. In advance the Directorate of Imperial Insignia arranged settings inside and outside the Deshou Palace gates: an imperial seat at the center of the hall facing south, a great secondary seat inside the gate facing south, and a small secondary seat in the eastern corridor facing west. Two cushioned seats for the emperor were placed—one southeast of the imperial seat facing west, and one south of the imperial seat facing north. The imperial brew office placed wine vessels east of the imperial seat while other offices set the imperial tea couch slightly north of the west side. On that day civil and military officials not in the imperial procession first gathered outside the Deshou Palace gate to await the emperor's arrival and salutation. At daybreak the emperor appeared in boots and robe and took the imperial seat. Attendants in the procession and the guard performed salutations as usual. The emperor descended and mounted his palanquin. As he approached the Deshou Palace, officials welcomed the procession, bowed twice, and completed their salutations. Advance guides, the director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, gate commissioners, ritual doctors, and ritual officers entered first and took their places left and right before the great secondary seat. The emperor alighted and entered the secondary seat. The Censorate, Gate Office, and Court of Imperial Sacrifices led the heir apparent and officials into the courtyard to stand east and west facing one another. Advance guides led the emperor into the small secondary seat and lowered the curtain. The heir apparent and civil and military officials all stood in horizontal formation facing north. The Retired Emperor emerged, ascended, and took the imperial seat. The whip sounded and the curtain of the small secondary seat was raised. Advance guides led the emperor up the eastern steps to the cushioned place before the balustrade facing north. He bowed twice, bowed and reported the Retired Emperor's ten thousand blessings of body, and bowed twice again. The emperor then stood west-facing east of the Retired Emperor's seat while advance guides took suitable positions on the dais. Next the usher had the heir apparent and officials bow. The master of ceremonies called "Bow twice," the caller transmitted, and all officials bowed twice, inserted their tablets, performed the dance obeisance, and bowed twice again. The heir apparent remained in place, reported ten thousand blessings of body, everyone bowed twice again, straightened, and divided east and west. Ritual officers led the tray bearers, cup receivers, order announcers, rite-completion reporters, and the director and vice-director of the palace service onto the hall. Inner attendants brought forward the imperial tea couch. The imperial brew master gave tray, cup, and pitcher to the director and vice-director of the palace service. Ritual officers then led the tray bearer north-facing to the wine altar; the director opened the cup and the vice-director poured. The tray bearer bore wine before the emperor facing north while ritual officers led the cup receiver to stand west-facing before the Retired Emperor. The heir apparent and officials stood in horizontal formation facing north. The tray bearer bowed and presented wine to the emperor, who bore it forward. Advance guides led him before the Retired Emperor to bow and present it, then withdraw slightly and hand the tray to the cup receiver. Advance guides led the emperor to the cushioned place before the Retired Emperor's seat, where he prostrated himself kneeling. Below the hall the heir apparent and all officials bowed. The emperor reported: "Your servant So-and-so respectfully leads the civil and military officials in bowing our heads and saying: On the Tianshen command festival your servant and the hundred officials cannot contain our great rejoicing and respectfully wish ten thousand myriad years of life. " When the report was finished he prostrated himself, rose, and bowed twice; all officials in attendance bowed twice. The order announcer proclaimed: "Having received the emperor's birthday wine, I share this joy with the emperor and the hundred officials within and without. " The emperor bowed twice and all officials bowed twice, then divided east and west. The emperor went east of the imperial seat and stood facing west. The tray bearer respectfully presented the tray facing north; the emperor received it while music played. When the Retired Emperor finished drinking, the emperor bowed and received the cup, withdrew slightly, and handed tray and cup to the cup receiver and then to the director of the palace service; each returned to position. The heir apparent and officials stood in horizontal formation facing north. The emperor went to the cushioned place facing north and bowed twice; all officials bowed twice. The emperor went to the cushioned place west-facing east of the Retired Emperor. The heir apparent and officials bowed twice, inserted their tablets, performed the dance obeisance, and bowed twice again. Inner attendants raised the tea couch. The rite-completion reporter prostrated himself facing north and reported: "Your servant with full title reports that the rites are complete. " All officials in attendance bowed twice. The Retired Emperor's carriage rose; the emperor followed him in; civil and military officials withdrew in order.
18
淳熙二年十一月，詔：“太上皇帝聖壽無疆，新歲七十，以十一日冬至加上尊號冊寶，十二月十七日立春行慶壽禮。 ”是日早，文武百僚並簪花赴文德殿立班，聽宣慶壽赦。 宣赦訖，從駕至德壽宮行慶壽禮，致詞曰：“皇帝臣某言：天祐君親，錫茲難老，維春之吉，年德加新。 臣某與群臣等不勝大慶，謹上千萬歲壽。 ”餘與前上壽儀注同。 禮畢，從駕官、應奉官、禁衛等並簪花從駕還內，文武百僚文德殿拜表稱賀。
In the eleventh month of Chunxi 2 (1175) an edict stated: "The Retired Emperor's sacred life knows no limit; in the new year he reaches seventy. On the eleventh day, the winter solstice, superior honorific titles and investiture seals will be added; on the seventeenth day of the twelfth month, the beginning of spring, the longevity celebration ritual will be performed. " Early that day civil and military officials wore floral pins, went to the Wende Hall to form ranks, and heard the longevity celebration amnesty proclaimed. When the amnesty was finished they followed the procession to the Deshou Palace for the longevity celebration, with the address: "The emperor your servant So-and-so says: Heaven protects ruler and parent and grants long life hard to age; in the auspiciousness of spring the year's virtue is renewed. Your servant So-and-so together with the assembled ministers cannot contain our great rejoicing and respectfully wish ten thousand myriad years of life. " The remainder followed the previous birthday ritual regulations. When the rites were finished, officials in the imperial procession, attendants, and guards wore floral pins and followed the emperor back within while civil and military officials presented congratulatory memorials at the Wende Hall.
19
十年十二月，以太上皇后新年七十，詔以立春日行慶賀之禮。 十三年春正月朔，以太上皇帝聖壽八十，帝率群臣詣德壽宮行禮，其儀注、恩赦並如淳熙二年典故。
In the twelfth month of the tenth year, because the Retired Empress would reach seventy in the new year, an edict ordered congratulatory rites on the day the spring season began. On the first day of the first month in the spring of the thirteenth year, when the Retired Emperor's sacred life reached eighty, the emperor led the assembled ministers to the Deshou Palace. The ritual regulations and amnesty followed the Chunxi 2 precedents.
20
孝宗以十月二十二日為會慶節，光宗以九月四日為重明節，寧宗以十月十九日為天祐節、尋改為瑞慶節，理宗以正月五日為天基節，度宗以四月九日為乾會節，瀛國公以九月二十八日為天瑞節。 其上壽稱賀之禮，大略皆如天申節儀。
Xiaozong's birthday was the Huiqing Festival on the twenty-second day of the tenth month; Guangzong's the Chongming Festival on the fourth day of the ninth month; Ningzong's first the Tianyou Festival on the nineteenth day of the tenth month, later renamed the Ruiqing Festival; Lizong's the Tianji Festival on the fifth day of the first month; Duzong's the Qianhui Festival on the ninth day of the fourth month; and the Lord of Ying's the Tianrui Festival on the twenty-eighth day of the ninth month. Their birthday felicitations and congratulatory rites largely followed the Tianshen Festival procedure.
21
諸慶節，古無是也，真宗以後始有之。 大中祥符元年，詔以正月三日天書降日為天慶節，休假五日，兩京諸路州、府、軍、監前七日建道場設醮，斷屠宰； 節日，士庶特令宴樂，京師然燈。 又以六月六日為天貺節，京師斷屠宰，百官行香上清宮。 又以七月一日聖祖降日為先天節，十月二十四日降延恩殿日為降聖節，休假、宴樂並如天慶節。 中書、親王、節度、樞密、三司以下至駙馬都尉，詣長春殿進金縷延壽帶、金絲續命縷，上保生壽酒。 改禦崇德殿，賜百官飲，如聖節儀。 前一日，以金縷延壽帶、金塗銀結續命縷、緋彩羅延壽帶、彩絲續命縷分賜百官，節日戴以入。 禮畢，宴百官於錫慶院。 天禧初，詔以大中祥符元年四月一日天書再降內中功德閣為天禎節，一如天貺節。 尋以仁宗嫌名，改為天祺節。
The various celebratory festivals did not exist in antiquity; they began only after Zhenzong. In Dazhong Xiangfu 1 (1008) an edict established the Tianqing Festival on the third day of the first month, when the celestial writings descended, with five days of leave. In the two capitals and all circuits, prefectures, armies, and supervisory posts, ritual arenas were opened and libations performed seven days in advance, and slaughter was forbidden. On the festival day commoners were specially permitted feasting and music, and lamps were lit in the capital. The sixth day of the sixth month was also made the Tiankuang Festival. In the capital slaughter was forbidden and officials burned incense at the Shangqing Palace. The first day of the seventh month, when the sacred ancestor descended, became the Xiantian Festival, and the twenty-fourth day of the tenth month, the day of descent at the Yan'en Hall, became the Jiangsheng Festival. Leave, feasting, and music all followed the Tianqing Festival. The Secretariat, princes, military commissioners, Bureau of Military Affairs, Three Departments, and down to imperial sons-in-law went to the Changchun Hall to present gold-thread longevity belts and life-prolonging cords and offer life-preserving birthday wine. The setting was moved to the Chongde Hall, where officials were granted drink as at an imperial birthday. On the day before, gold-thread longevity belts, gold-plated silver knotted life-prolonging cords, crimson silk longevity belts, and colored silk life-prolonging cords were distributed to officials, who wore them when entering on the festival day. When the rites were finished, officials were banqueted at the Xiqing Court. At the beginning of Tianxi an edict established the Tianzhen Festival on the first day of the fourth month in Dazhong Xiangfu 1, when the celestial writings descended again within the Merit Hall, following the Tiankuang Festival exactly. Shortly thereafter, because of Renzong's taboo name, it was renamed the Tianqi Festival.
22
政和三年十一月五日，以修祀事，天真示見，詔為天應節。 又以五月十二日祭方丘日為寧貺節，既又以二月十五日太上混元上德皇帝降聖日為真元節，八月九日青華帝君生辰為元成節，正月四日有太祖神禦之州府宮殿行香為開基節，十月二十五日為天符節，皆如天慶節，著為令。
On the fifth day of the eleventh month in Zhenghe 3 (1113), while sacrificial repairs were under way the Heavenly Truth manifested and the day was edicted as the Tianying Festival. The twelfth day of the fifth month, the day of sacrifice at the Square Mound, became the Ningkuang Festival. The fifteenth day of the second month, the descent day of the Supreme Lord of Mixed Origin and Upper Virtue, became the Zhenyuan Festival; the ninth day of the eighth month, the birthday of the Blue Flower Sovereign Lord, the Yuancheng Festival; the fourth day of the first month, when incense was burned at palaces holding Taizu's spirit tablet, the Kaiji Festival; and the twenty-fifth day of the tenth month the Tianfu Festival—all following the Tianqing Festival and entered into statute.
23
高宗建炎元年十一月五日，詔：“政和以來添置諸節，除開基節外，餘並依祖宗法。 ”
On the fifth day of the eleventh month in Jianyan 1 (1127), Gaozong edicted: "The various festivals added since the Zhenghe period, except the Kaiji Festival, shall all follow ancestral law. "
