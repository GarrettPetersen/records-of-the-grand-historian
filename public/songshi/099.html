1
南郊壇制。 梁及後唐郊壇皆在洛陽。 宋初始作壇于東都南薰門外，四成、十二陛、三壝。 設燎壇於內壇之外丙地，高一丈二尺。 設皇帝更衣大次於東壝東門之內道北，南向。 仁宗天聖六年，始築外壝，周以短垣，置靈星門。 親郊則立表於青城，表三壝。 神宗熙寧七年，詔中書、門下參定青城殿宇門名。 先是，每郊撰進，至是始定名，前門曰泰禋，東偏門曰迎禧，正東門曰祥曦，正西門曰景曜，後三門曰拱極，內東側門曰夤明，西側門曰肅成，殿曰端誠，殿前東、西門曰左右嘉德，便殿曰熙成，後園門曰寶華，著為定式。 元豐元年二月，詔內壝之外，眾星位周環，每二步植一杙，繚以青繩，以為限域。 既而詳定奉祀禮文所言：「《周官》外祀皆有兆域，後世因之，稍增其制。 國朝郊壇率循唐舊，雖儀注具載圜丘三壝，每壝二十五步，而有司乃以青繩代內壝，誠不足以等神位、序祀事、嚴內外之限也。 伏請除去青繩，為三壝之制。 」從之。
Regulations governing the Southern Suburban Altar. Under both Liang and Later Tang, the suburban sacrifice altars were located at Luoyang. When the Song dynasty was first established, an altar was erected outside the Nanxun Gate of the Eastern Capital, built in four tiers with twelve stairways and three surrounding enclosures. A fire altar for burning offerings was placed in the bing sector outside the inner enclosure, twelve feet high. The emperor's grand robing pavilion was established north of the inner passage inside the eastern gate of the east enclosure, facing south. In the sixth year of the Tiansheng era (1028), Emperor Renzong had the outer enclosure built for the first time, ringed with a low wall and furnished with a Spirit Star Gate. When the emperor conducted the suburban rite in person, ritual markers were set up at the Azure City to delineate the three enclosures. In the seventh year of the Xining era (1074), Emperor Shenzong ordered the Secretariat and Chancellery to determine the official names for the halls and gates of the Azure City. Until then, new names had been proposed afresh before each suburban sacrifice; only now were they fixed for good. The front gate was named Great Offering; the east side gate, Welcoming Blessings; the main east gate, Auspicious Dawn; the main west gate, Radiant Splendor; the three rear gates, Arching the Pole; the inner east side gate, Dawn Brightness; the west side gate, Solemn Completion; the main hall, Sincere Integrity; the east and west gates before it, Left and Right Virtue; the side hall, Flourishing Completion; and the rear garden gate, Treasured Splendor. These were codified as the permanent standard. In the second month of the first year of Yuanfeng (1078), an edict directed that beyond the inner enclosure, where the star deities were arrayed in a ring, a stake be planted every two paces and laced with blue cord to mark the boundary. Later, the Deliberated Rites for Suburban and Temple Sacrifices stated: "According to the Offices of Zhou, all outer sacrifices had demarcated precincts; later dynasties followed this practice and gradually expanded the regulations. The suburban altars of our dynasty largely followed Tang practice. Although the ritual manuals clearly prescribe three enclosures for the Round Mound, each twenty-five paces wide, the responsible officials had substituted blue cord for the inner enclosure — which is plainly inadequate to distinguish ranks among the deities, sequence the rites, and enforce the separation of inner and outer precincts. We respectfully ask that the blue cord be abolished and the proper three-enclosure arrangement restored. " The court approved the proposal.
2
徽宗政和三年，詔有司討論壇壝之制。 十月，禮制局言：「壇舊制四成，一成二十丈，再成十五丈，三成十丈，四成五丈，成高八尺一寸； 十有二陛，陛十有二級； 三壝，二十五步。 古所謂地上圜丘、澤中方丘，皆因地形之自然。 王者建國，或無自然之丘，則于郊澤吉土以兆壇位。 為壇之制，當用陽數，今定為壇三成，一成用九九之數，廣八十一丈，再成用六九之數，廣五十四丈，三成用三九之數，廣二十七丈。 每成高二十七尺，三成總二百七十有六，《乾》之策也。 為三壝，壝三十六步，亦《乾》之策也。 成與壝地之數也。 」詔行之。
In the third year of the Zhenghe era (1113), Emperor Huizong ordered the relevant offices to deliberate on the design of the altar platform and its enclosures. In the tenth month the Ritual Regulations Bureau submitted: "Under the old regulations the altar had four tiers: the first tier measured twenty zhang across, the second fifteen, the third ten, and the fourth five, with each tier eight feet and one inch high; there were twelve stairways, each with twelve steps; and three enclosures, each twenty-five paces wide. The ancient Round Mound on elevated ground and Square Mound in a marshland both conformed to natural topography. When a ruler founded his state and no natural mound was available, he selected auspicious ground in the suburban marsh to establish the altar. Altar construction should follow yang numerology. It is now decreed that the altar shall have three tiers: the first, based on nine times nine, shall be eighty-one zhang wide; the second, on six times nine, fifty-four zhang; and the third, on three times nine, twenty-seven zhang. Each tier shall stand twenty-seven feet high, the three tiers together totaling two hundred seventy-six feet — the number derived from the Qian hexagram. There shall be three enclosures, each thirty-six paces wide — likewise derived from the Qian hexagram. Such are the numerological proportions governing the tiers and enclosures. " The emperor ordered these regulations put into effect.
3
建炎二年，高宗至揚州，庶事草創，築壇于州南門內江都縣之東南，詔東京所屬官吏奉祭器、大樂、儀仗、法物赴行在所。 紹興十三年，太常寺言：「國朝圓壇在國之東南，壇側建青城齋宮，以備郊宿。 今宜於臨安府行宮東南修建。 」於是，遂詔臨安府及殿前司修建圓壇，第一成縱廣七丈，第二成縱廣一十二丈，第三成縱廣一十七丈，第四成縱廣二十二丈。 一十二陛，每陛七十二級，每成一十二綴。 三壝，第一壝去壇二十五步，中壝去內壝、外壝去中壝各半之。 燎壇方一丈，高一丈二尺，開上南出戶，方六尺，三出陛，在壇南二十步丙地。 其青城及望祭殿與行事陪祠官宿齋幕次，並令絞縛，更不修蓋。 先是，張杓為京尹，議築齋宮，可一勞永逸，宇文價曰：「陛下方經略河南，今築青城，是無中原也。 」遂罷役。
In the second year of Jianyan (1128), after Emperor Gaozong reached Yangzhou with government still in its infancy, an altar was erected southeast of Jiangdu County within the prefecture's south gate. An edict directed officials from the former Eastern Capital to bring sacrificial vessels, court music, insignia, and ritual implements to the traveling court. In the thirteenth year of Shaoxing (1143), the Court of Imperial Sacrifices reported: "The dynasty's Round Altar stands in the southeast of the realm, with an Azure City fasting palace built beside it to house the emperor during the pre-sacrifice vigil. It is now appropriate to construct the same southeast of the imperial residence in Lin'an. " Accordingly, Lin'an Prefecture and the Palace Command were ordered to build the Round Altar: the first tier seven zhang square, the second twelve, the third seventeen, and the fourth twenty-two. It had twelve stairways of seventy-two steps each, with twelve courses per tier. Three enclosures were laid out: the innermost twenty-five paces from the altar, with the middle and outer enclosures each half again as far from the one inside it. The fire altar was one zhang square and twelve feet high, open at the top with a six-foot square door facing south and three outward stairways, situated in the bing sector twenty paces south of the main altar. The Azure City, the Hall for Gazing Sacrifice, and the fasting tent quarters for officiating and attendant officials were all to be rope-rigged temporary structures, without permanent roofing. Earlier, when Zhang Biao served as metropolitan magistrate, he had proposed building a permanent fasting palace as a lasting solution. Yuwen Jia objected: "Your Majesty is still striving to recover Henan; to build the Azure City now would be to abandon hope of restoring the Central Plains. " Construction was accordingly suspended.
4
神位。 元豐元年十一月，詳定郊廟奉祀禮文所言：「按東漢壇位，天神從祀者至千五百一十四，故外設重營，以為等限。 日月在中營內南道，而北斗在北道之西，至於五星中宮宿之屬，則其位皆中營，二十八宿外宮星之屬，則其位皆外營。 然則為重營者，所以等神位也。 唐因隋制，設為三壝，天神列位不出內壝，而御位特設於壇下之東南。 若夫公卿分獻、文武從祀，與夫樂架饌幔，則皆在中壝之內，而大次之設乃在外壝。 然則為三壝者，所以序祀事也。」
Spirit Positions. In the eleventh month of the first year of Yuanfeng (1078), the Deliberated Rites for Suburban and Temple Sacrifices explained: "Under Eastern Han altar arrangements, as many as 1,514 celestial deities received attendant sacrifice, so double outer encampments were established to demarcate ranks. The sun and moon stood on the southern avenue within the inner encampment, and the Northern Dipper west of the northern avenue. The Five Stars and inner-palace constellations were all placed in the inner encampment, while the Twenty-eight Lunar Mansions and outer-palace stars occupied the outer encampment. The double encampments, then, served to distinguish ranks among the deities. Tang adopted Sui practice and instituted three enclosures: celestial deities were ranked no farther out than the inner enclosure, while the emperor's position was specially placed southeast below the altar platform. The separate offerings presented by dukes and ministers, the attendant sacrifices of civil and military officials, and the music stands and offering curtains all lay within the middle enclosure, whereas the grand pavilions were placed in the outer enclosure. The three enclosures, then, served to sequence the ritual proceedings."
5
景德三年，鹵簿使王欽若言：「漢以五帝為天神之佐，今在第一龕； 天皇大帝在第二龕，與六甲、嶽瀆之類接席； 帝座，天市之尊，今與二十八宿、積薪、騰蛇、杵臼之類同在第三龕。 卑主尊臣，甚未便也。 若以北極、帝坐本非天帝，蓋是天帝所居，則北極在第二，帝坐在第三，亦高下未等。 又太微之次少左右執法，子星之次少孫星，望令司天監參驗。 」乃詔禮儀使、太常禮院、司天監檢定之。
In the third year of Jingde (1006), Imperial Escort Commissioner Wang Qinruo reported: "The Han dynasty regarded the Five Emperors as assistants to the Celestial Thearch, yet they now occupy the first niche; the Great Celestial Thearch stands in the second niche alongside the Six Jia spirits and the mountain and river deities; and the Imperial Seat — sovereign of the Celestial Market Enclosure — now shares the third niche with the Twenty-eight Lunar Mansions, Piled Firewood, Soaring Serpent, Pestle and Mortar, and similar lesser asterisms. To have the humble ranked above the exalted is most improper. If the Northern Pole and Imperial Seat are not the Celestial Thearch himself but merely his dwelling, then placing the Northern Pole in the second rank and the Imperial Seat in the third still leaves their relative precedence unequal. Furthermore, the Taiwei sequence lacks the Left and Right Law-Enforcers, and the Son Star sequence lacks the Grandson Star. We ask that the Directorate of Astronomy investigate and verify these matters. " An edict thereupon directed the Commissioner of Ritual, the Court of Imperial Sacrifices Ritual Office, and the Directorate of Astronomy to review and determine the correct arrangement.
6
禮儀使趙安仁言：「按《開寶通禮》，元氣廣大則稱昊天，據遠視之蒼然，則稱蒼天。 人之所尊，莫過於帝，托之於天，故稱上帝。 天皇大帝即北辰耀魄寶也，自是星中之尊。 《易》曰：'日月麗乎天，百穀草木麗乎土。 '又曰：'在天成象，在地成形。 '蓋明辰象非天，草木非地，是則天以蒼昊為體，不入星辰之列。 又《郊祀錄》：'壇第二等祀天皇大帝、北斗、天一、太一、紫微、五帝坐，差在行位前，餘內官諸位及五星、十二辰、河漢，都四十九坐齊列，俱在十二陛之間。 '唐建中間，司天冬官正郭獻之奏：'天皇、北極、天一、太一，准《天寶敕》併合升第一等。 '貞元二年親郊，乙太常議，詔復從《開元禮》，仍為定制。 《郊祀錄》又云：'壇第三等有中宮、天市垣、帝坐等十七坐，並在前。 '《開元禮義羅》云：'帝有五坐，一在紫微宮，一在大角，一在太微宮，一在心，一在天市垣。 '即帝坐者非直指天帝也。 又得判司天監史序狀：'天皇大帝一星在紫微勾陳中，其神曰耀魄寶，即天皇是星，五帝乃天帝也。 北極五星在紫微垣內，居中一星曰北辰，第一主月為太子，第二主日為帝王，第三為庶子，第四為嫡子，第五為天子之樞，蓋北辰所主非一，又非帝坐之比。 太微垣十星有左右執法、上將、次將之名，不可備陳，故總名太微垣。 《星經》舊載孫星，而《壇圖》止有子星，辨其尊卑，不可同位。 '竊惟《壇圖》舊制，悉有明據，天神定位，難以躋升，望依《星經》，悉以舊禮為定。」
Commissioner of Ritual Zhao Anren argued: "According to the Comprehensive Rites of Kaibao, when primordial qi is vast it is called Vast Heaven; when viewed from afar in its azure expanse, it is called Azure Heaven. What humans revere above all is the sovereign; entrusting this reverence to Heaven, he is therefore called the Supreme Thearch. The Great Celestial Thearch is the North Star known as Radiant Soul Treasure — by nature the most exalted among the stars. The Book of Changes states: "The sun and moon cling to heaven; the hundred grains and plants cling to earth." It further states: "In heaven they form images; on earth they take shape." This shows that stellar images are not heaven itself and plants are not earth itself; heaven takes azure vastness as its substance and does not belong among the stars. The Record of Suburban Sacrifice further records: "The altar's second rank receives sacrifice to the Great Celestial Thearch, the Northern Dipper, Celestial One, Supreme One, Purple Forbidden Enclosure, and the Five Thearch Seats, placed slightly ahead of the procession line. The remaining inner-palace positions together with the Five Stars, Twelve Chronograms, and Milky Way — forty-nine seats in all — are aligned between the twelve stairways." During the Jianzhong era of Tang, Guo Xianzhi, director of the Winter Office in the Directorate of Astronomy, memorialized that the Great Celestial Thearch, Northern Pole, Celestial One, and Supreme One should, per the Tianbao edict, all be promoted to the first rank. When the emperor conducted the suburban rite in person in the second year of Zhenyuan (786), the Court of Imperial Sacrifices deliberated, and an edict restored the Kaiyuan Rites as the permanent standard. The Record of Suburban Sacrifice also states: "The altar's third rank includes the Central Palace, Celestial Market Enclosure, Imperial Seat, and seventeen positions in all, each placed in the forward row." The Exegesis of Kaiyuan Rites explains: "The Thearch has five seats: one in the Purple Forbidden Palace, one at Great Horn, one in the Supreme Palace Enclosure, one at Heart, and one in the Celestial Market Enclosure." The Imperial Seat, then, does not refer directly to the Celestial Thearch himself. Acting Director of Astronomy Shi Xu also submitted a memorial stating: "The Great Celestial Thearch is a single star within the Purple Forbidden Hooked Array; its spirit is called Radiant Soul Treasure. The Great Celestial Thearch is a star; the Five Emperors are the Celestial Thearch." The five stars of the Northern Pole lie within the Purple Forbidden Enclosure. The central star is called the North Star: the first governs the moon and signifies the heir apparent; the second governs the sun and signifies the emperor; the third signifies the common son; the fourth the legitimate son; and the fifth the pivot of the Son of Heaven. The North Star governs many things and is not comparable to the Imperial Seat. The ten stars of the Supreme Palace Enclosure include the Left and Right Law-Enforcers, Senior General, and Junior General — too numerous to list individually — and are therefore collectively designated the Supreme Palace Enclosure. The Classic of Stars records the Grandson Star, but the Altar Diagram includes only the Son Star. Given their difference in rank, they cannot occupy the same position. I respectfully observe that the established Altar Diagram rests on clear precedent, and the ranks of celestial deities should not be casually elevated. I ask that the Classic of Stars be followed and the existing ritual arrangement be maintained as the standard."
7
欽若復言：「舊史《天文志》並云：北極，北辰最尊者。 又勾陳口中一星曰天皇大帝，鄭玄注《周禮》謂：'禮天者，冬至祭天皇於北極也。 '後魏孝文禋六宗，亦升天皇五帝上。 按晉《天文志》：'帝坐光而潤，則天子吉，威令行。 '既名帝坐，則為天子所占，列於下位，未見其可。 又安仁議，以子、孫二星不可同位。 陛下方洽高禖之慶，以廣維城之基，苟因前代闕文，便為得禮，實恐聖朝茂典，尤未適中。 」詔天皇、北極特升第一龕，又設孫星于子星位次，帝坐如故。
Qinruo rejoined: "The Astronomical Treatises of earlier histories all declare that the Northern Pole — the North Star — is the most exalted. One star within the mouth of the Hooked Array is called the Great Celestial Thearch. Zheng Xuan's commentary on the Rites of Zhou states: "Those who worship Heaven sacrifice to the Celestial Thearch at the Northern Pole at the winter solstice." Emperor Xiaowen of Northern Wei performed the yin sacrifice to the Six Ancestors and likewise ranked the Great Celestial Thearch above the Five Emperors. The Jin Astronomical Treatise records: "When the Imperial Seat shines bright and lustrous, the Son of Heaven is fortunate and his commands prevail." Since it is called the Imperial Seat, it signifies the Son of Heaven himself; to rank it in a lower position seems plainly wrong. Anren's proposal that the Son and Grandson stars cannot share a position is well taken. Your Majesty is presently celebrating the blessings of the High Matron to strengthen the imperial house. To treat gaps in earlier texts as sufficient grounds for ritual propriety would, I fear, leave our dynasty's splendid ceremonial code far short of what is fitting. " An edict specially elevated the Great Celestial Thearch and Northern Pole to the first niche, established the Grandson Star in the position after the Son Star, and left the Imperial Seat unchanged.
8
欽若又言：「帝坐止三，紫微、太微者已列第二等，唯天市一坐在第三等。 按《晉志》，大角及心中星但雲天王坐，實與帝坐不類。 」詔特升第二龕。
Qinruo added: "There are only three true Imperial Seats. Those of the Purple Forbidden and Supreme Palace Enclosures already occupy the second rank; only the Celestial Market seat remains in the third. According to the Jin Treatise, Great Horn and the star at Heart are merely called Heavenly King Seats and are not truly comparable to the Imperial Seat. " An edict specially elevated it to the second niche.
9
舊郊丘，神位板皆有司題署，命欽若改造之。 至是，欽若奉板便殿，壇上四位，塗以朱漆金字，余皆黑漆，第一等金字，第二等黃字，第三等以降朱字，悉貯漆匣，覆以黃縑帊。 帝降階觀之，即付有司。 又以新定《壇圖》，五帝、五嶽、中鎮、河漢合在第三等。
At the suburban altars, spirit-position tablets had previously been inscribed by responsible officials; Qinruo was ordered to remake them. Qinruo then presented the tablets in the side hall. The four positions on the altar platform were finished in cinnabar lacquer with gold lettering; the rest in black lacquer — first rank in gold, second in yellow, third and below in cinnabar — each stored in a lacquered case covered with yellow silk. The emperor descended to inspect them and immediately entrusted them to the responsible officials. Under the newly fixed Altar Diagram, the Five Emperors, Five Sacred Mountains, Central Peak, and Milky Way were all grouped in the third rank.
10
四年，判太常禮院孫奭言：「准禮，冬至祀圜丘，有司攝事，以天神六百九十位從祀。 今惟有五方上帝及五人神十七位，天皇大帝以下並不設位。 且太昊、勾芒，惟孟夏雩祀、季秋大享及之，今乃祀於冬至，恐未協宜。 」翰林學士晁迥等言：「按《開寶通禮》：圜丘，有司攝事，祀昊天、配帝、五方帝、日月、五星、中官、外官、眾星總六百八十七位； 雩祀、大享，昊天、配帝、五天帝、五人帝、五官總十七位； 方丘，祭皇地祇、配帝、神州、嶽鎮、海瀆七十一位。 今司天監所設圜丘、雩祀、明堂、方丘並七十位，即是方丘有嶽、瀆從祀，圜丘無星辰，而反以人帝從祀。 望如奭請，以《通禮》及神位為定，其有增益者如後敕。 」從之。
In the fourth year, Sun Shi of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices Ritual Office reported: "According to ritual precedent, when officials conduct the winter solstice sacrifice at the Round Mound by proxy, six hundred ninety celestial deities receive attendant sacrifice. At present only the Five Directional Supreme Thearchs and seventeen human-spirit deities are honored; from the Great Celestial Thearch downward no positions are established at all. Moreover, Taihao and Goumang are honored only at the midsummer rain sacrifice and the autumn great offering; to include them in the winter solstice rite may be inappropriate. Hanlin Academician Chao Jiong and others replied: "According to the Comprehensive Rites of Kaibao, when officials conduct the Round Mound sacrifice by proxy, they honor Vast Heaven, the associated Thearch, the Five Directional Thearchs, the sun and moon, the Five Stars, inner-palace stars, outer-palace stars, and the host of stars — six hundred eighty-seven positions in all; at the rain sacrifice and great offering, Vast Heaven, the associated Thearch, the Five Heavenly Thearchs, Five Human Thearchs, and Five Officials — seventeen positions in all; and at the Square Mound, Imperial Earth, the associated Thearch, the Divine Land, mountains, peaks, seas, and rivers — seventy-one positions. The Directorate of Astronomy now assigns only seventy positions each to the Round Mound, rain sacrifice, Bright Hall, and Square Mound — so that mountains and rivers attend at the Square Mound while the Round Mound has no stars, yet human thearchs are given attendant sacrifice instead. We ask that Sun Shi's request be granted and the Comprehensive Rites and established spirit positions be taken as the standard, with any additions to follow subsequent edicts. " The court approved.
11
政和三年，議禮局上《五禮新儀》：皇帝祀昊天上帝，太史設神位版，昊天上帝位於壇上北方南向，席以稿秸； 太祖位於壇上東方西向，席以蒲越； 天皇大帝、五帝、大明、夜明、北極九位於第一龕； 北斗、太一、帝坐、五帝內坐、五星、十二辰、河漢等內官神位五十有四於第二龕； 二十八宿等中官神位百五十有九於第三龕； 外官神位一百有六於內壝之內； 眾星三百有六十於內壝之外。 第一龕席以稿秸，余以莞席，皆內向配位。
In the third year of Zhenghe (1113), the Deliberative Ritual Bureau submitted the New Rites of the Five Sacrifices: when the emperor sacrifices to the Supreme Thearch of Vast Heaven, the Grand Astrologer sets out the spirit-position tablets. The Supreme Thearch of Vast Heaven stands on the northern side of the altar facing south, upon a mat of straw stalks; the Founding Ancestor stands on the eastern side of the altar facing west, upon a woven rush mat; the Great Celestial Thearch, Five Emperors, Great Brightness, Night Brightness, and Northern Pole — nine deities in the first niche; the Northern Dipper, Supreme One, Imperial Seat, Five Thearch Inner Seats, Five Stars, Twelve Chronograms, Milky Way, and other inner-palace deities — fifty-four in the second niche; the Twenty-eight Lunar Mansions and other central-palace deities — one hundred fifty-nine in the third niche; outer-palace deities — one hundred six within the inner enclosure; and the host of stars — three hundred sixty outside the inner enclosure. The first niche uses straw-stalk mats; the others use sedge mats. All associated positions face inward.
12
九年正月，詔以四月幸西京，有事於南郊。 自國初以來，南郊四祭及感生帝、皇地祇、神州凡七祭，並以四祖迭配。 太祖親郊者四，並以宣祖配。 太宗即位，其七祭但以宣祖、太祖更配。 是歲親享天地，始奉太祖升侑。 雍熙元年冬至親郊，從禮儀使扈蒙之議，復以宣祖配。 四年正月，禮儀使蘇易簡言：「親祀圜丘，以宣祖配，此則符聖人大孝之道，成嚴父配天之儀。 太祖皇帝光啟丕圖，恭臨大寶，以聖授聖，傳於無窮。 按唐永徽中，以高祖、太宗同配上帝。 欲望將來親祀郊丘，奉宣祖、太祖同配； 其常祀祈穀、神州、明堂，以宣祖崇配； 圜丘、北郊、雩祀，以太祖崇配。 」奏可。
In the first month of the ninth year, an edict announced that in the fourth month the emperor would visit the Western Capital to conduct the suburban sacrifice. Since the founding of the dynasty, the four suburban sacrifices together with those to the Life-Generating Thearch, Imperial Earth, and the Divine Land — seven rites in all — have assigned the four imperial ancestors in rotation as associated spirits. The Founding Ancestor conducted the suburban sacrifice in person four times, each time with the Proclamation Ancestor as the associated spirit. When Emperor Taizong ascended the throne, the seven sacrifices alternated only between the Proclamation Ancestor and the Founding Ancestor as associates. That year, when he personally sacrificed to Heaven and Earth, he first elevated the Founding Ancestor to share the offering. At the winter solstice suburban sacrifice in the first year of Yongxi (984), following Commissioner of Ritual Hu Meng's proposal, the Proclamation Ancestor was again made the associated spirit. In the first month of the fourth year, Commissioner of Ritual Su Yijian argued: "To sacrifice personally at the Round Mound with the Proclamation Ancestor as associate accords with the sage's supreme path of filial piety and fulfills the rite of the revered father sharing Heaven's altar. The Founding Ancestor brilliantly inaugurated the great enterprise, reverently assumed the imperial throne, and passed the sage's mandate from sage to sage for all eternity. Under Tang, during the Yonghui period, Gaozu and Taizong were jointly associated with the Supreme Thearch. We ask that in future personal suburban sacrifices, the Proclamation Ancestor and Founding Ancestor serve jointly as associates; for the regular sacrifices of Prayer for Grain, Divine Land, and Bright Hall, the Proclamation Ancestor be honored as associate; and for the Round Mound, Northern Suburb, and rain sacrifice, the Founding Ancestor be honored as associate. " The memorial was approved.
13
真宗至道三年十一月，有司言：「冬至圜丘、孟夏雩祀、夏至方丘，請奉太宗配； 上辛祈谷、季秋明堂，奉太祖配； 上辛祀感生帝、孟冬祭神州地祇，奉宣祖配； 其親郊，奉太祖、太宗並配。 」詔可。 乾興元年，真宗崩，詔禮官定遷郊祀配帝，乃請：「祈穀及祭神州地祇，以太祖配； 雩祀及昊天上帝及皇地祇，乙太宗配； 感生帝，以宣祖配； 明堂，以真宗配； 親祀郊丘，以太祖、太宗配。 」奏可。
In the eleventh month of the third year of Zhidao (997), the responsible offices reported: "For the winter solstice Round Mound, midsummer rain sacrifice, and summer solstice Square Mound, we request that Emperor Taizong serve as associate; for the first xin day Prayer for Grain and the autumn Bright Hall sacrifice, the Founding Ancestor; for the first xin day sacrifice to the Life-Generating Thearch and the winter sacrifice to the Divine Land of Earth, the Proclamation Ancestor; and for personal suburban sacrifice, the Founding Ancestor and Taizong jointly. " An edict approved. In the first year of Qianxing (1022), after Emperor Zhenzong's death, ritual officials were ordered to determine the associated spirits for suburban sacrifice. They proposed: "For Prayer for Grain and sacrifice to the Divine Land of Earth, the Founding Ancestor; for the rain sacrifice, Supreme Thearch of Vast Heaven, and Imperial Earth, Taizong on the yi day; for the Life-Generating Thearch, the Proclamation Ancestor; for the Bright Hall, Emperor Zhenzong; and for personal suburban sacrifice, the Founding Ancestor and Taizong." The memorial was approved.
14
景祐二年郊，詔以太祖、太宗、真宗三廟萬世不遷。 南郊以太祖定配，二宗迭配，親祀皆侑。 常祀圜丘、皇地祇配以太祖，祈谷、雩祀、神州配乙太宗，感生帝、明堂以宣祖、真宗配如舊。 慶曆元年，判太常寺呂公綽言：「歷代郊祀，配位無側向，真宗示輔臣《封禪圖》曰：'嘗見郊祀昊天上帝，不以正坐，蓋皇地祇次之。 今修登封，上帝宜當子位，太祖、太宗配位，宜比郊祀而斜置之。 '其後，有司不諭先帝以告成報功、酌宜從變之意，每郊儀範，既引祥符側置之文，又載西向北上之禮，臨時擇一，未嘗考定。 」乃詔南郊祖宗之配，並以東方西向為定。 皇祐五年郊，詔自今圜丘，三聖並侑。 嘉祐六年，諫官楊畋論水災繇郊廟未順。 禮院亦言：「對越天地，神無二主。 唐始用三祖同配，後遂罷之。 皇祐初，詔三聖並侑，後復迭配，未幾復並侑，以為定制。 雖出孝思，然頗違經典，當時有司失於講求。 」下兩制議，翰林學士王珪等曰：「推尊以享帝，義之至也。 然尊尊不可以瀆，故郊無二主。 今三後並侑，欲以致孝也，而適所以瀆乎享帝，非無以寧神也，請如禮官議。 」七年正月，詔南郊以太祖定配。
At the suburban sacrifice in the second year of Jingyou (1035), an edict declared that the temples of the Founding Ancestor, Taizong, and Zhenzong would remain permanently fixed. At the Southern Suburb the Founding Ancestor was the fixed associate, with the two later emperors rotating; at personal sacrifice all shared the offering. At regular sacrifices to the Round Mound and Imperial Earth, the Founding Ancestor served as associate; for Prayer for Grain, rain sacrifice, and Divine Land, Taizong on the yi day; for the Life-Generating Thearch and Bright Hall, the Proclamation Ancestor and Zhenzong as before. In the first year of Qingli (1041), Lü Gongchuo of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices reported: "In past dynasties' suburban sacrifices, associate positions had no fixed sideways orientation. Emperor Zhenzong once showed his ministers the Diagram of the Feng and Shan Sacrifices, observing: "I once noticed that at suburban sacrifice to the Supreme Thearch of Vast Heaven, the deity was not seated directly forward — Imperial Earth comes next. In performing the Mount Tai ascent, the Supreme Thearch should occupy the zi position, and the associate positions of the Founding Ancestor and Taizong should, as at suburban sacrifice, be placed obliquely." Thereafter the responsible offices failed to grasp the late emperor's intent to report completion, repay merit, and adapt ritual appropriately. Each suburban manual cited both the Xiangfu text on oblique placement and the rite of facing west and proceeding north, choosing between them ad hoc without ever settling the matter. An edict then fixed that ancestral associates at the Southern Suburb should all stand on the east facing west. At the suburban sacrifice in the fifth year of Huangyou (1053), an edict ordered that henceforth at the Round Mound all three sage emperors share the offering together. In the sixth year of Jiayou (1061), remonstrance official Yang Tian argued that the floods arose because suburban altars and ancestral temples were not properly aligned. The Ritual Office also argued: "In worshipping Heaven and Earth, there can be no dual sovereign. Tang initially associated three ancestors jointly, but later abolished the practice. Early in Huangyou, an edict ordered all three sage emperors to share the offering; later they rotated again; soon all three shared again — and this became the fixed practice. Though born of filial devotion, it largely violated classical precedent, and the responsible offices of the time failed to investigate properly. The matter was referred to the Hanlin and academies for deliberation. Wang Gui and others argued: "To elevate and honor in sacrificing to the Thearch is the highest expression of righteousness. Yet the exalted must not be profaned; therefore suburban sacrifice admits no dual sovereign. To have all three emperors share the offering, though intended as filial piety, in fact profanes the sacrifice to the Thearch and fails to bring peace to the spirits. We ask that the ritual officials' proposal be adopted. In the first month of the seventh year (1062), an edict fixed the Founding Ancestor as the permanent associate at the Southern Suburb.
15
高宗建炎二年，車駕至揚州，築壇于江都縣之東南。 是歲冬至，祀昊天上帝，以太祖配。 度宗鹹淳二年，將舉郊祀，時復議以高宗參配。 吏部侍郎兼中書門下省檢正洪燾等議，以為：「物無二本，事無二初，舜之郊嚳，商之郊契，周郊後稷，皆所以推原其始也。 禮者，所以別等差，視儀則，遠而尊者配於郊，近而親者配於明堂，明有等也。 臣等謂宜如紹興故事，奉太宗配，將來明堂遵用先皇帝彝典，以高宗參侑，庶於報本之禮、奉先之孝，為兩盡其至。 」詔恭依。
In the second year of Jianyan (1128), after the court reached Yangzhou, an altar was built southeast of Jiangdu County. That winter solstice, the court sacrificed to the Supreme Thearch of Vast Heaven with the Founding Ancestor as associate. In the second year of Xianchun (1266), as a suburban sacrifice was planned, debate resumed over including Emperor Gaozong as an associate. Hong Tao, Vice Minister of Personnel and Secretariat Reviewer, and others argued: "Things have no dual roots and affairs no dual beginnings. Shun's suburban sacrifice to Ku, Shang's to Qi, and Zhou's to Hou Ji all trace reverence to its origin. Rites distinguish ranks and govern ceremonial propriety: the distant and exalted are associated at the suburb, the near and intimate at the Bright Hall — the hierarchy is explicit. We propose following the Shaoxing precedent with Taizong as associate at the suburb, and at the future Bright Hall following the late emperor's established regulation with Gaozong sharing the offering — thereby fulfilling both the rite of repaying one's origins and the filial piety of honoring ancestors to the fullest. " The edict respectfully approved.

儀注。 乾德元年八月，禮儀使陶穀言：「饗廟、郊天，兩日行禮，從祀官前七日皆合於尚書省受誓戒，自來一日之內受兩處誓戒，有虧虔潔。 今擬十一月十六日行郊禮，望依禮文於八日先受從享太廟誓戒，九日別受郊天誓戒，其日請放朝參。 」從之。 自後百官受誓戒於朝堂，宗室受於太廟。
Ritual Procedures. In the eighth month of the first year of Qiande (963), Commissioner of Ritual Tao Gu reported: "The temple feast and Heaven sacrifice fall on consecutive days, and attendant officials should receive oath-abstention at the Secretariat seven days in advance. Receiving oath-abstention at two places in a single day impairs reverent purity. The suburban rite is scheduled for the sixteenth of the eleventh month. We ask that on the eighth, officials first receive oath-abstention for attending the Grand Temple, and on the ninth receive separate oath-abstention for the Heaven sacrifice, with court attendance suspended on that day. " The proposal was adopted. Thereafter civil officials received oath-abstention in the court hall, and the imperial clan at the Grand Temple.
17
祭之日均用丑時，秋夏以一刻，春冬以七刻，前二日遣官奏告。 配帝之室，儀鸞司設大次、小次及文武侍臣、蕃客之次，太常設樂位、神位、版位等事。 前一日司尊彝帥其屬以法陳祭器於堂東，僕射、禮部尚書視滌濯告潔，禮部尚書、侍郎省牲，光祿卿奉牲，告充、告備，禮部尚書視鼎鑊，禮部侍郎視腥熟之節。 祭之旦，光祿卿率其屬取籩、豆、簠、簋實之。 及薦腥，禮部尚書帥其屬薦籩、豆、簠、簋，戶部、兵部、工部尚書薦三牲之腥熟俎。 禮畢，各徹，而有司受之以出。 晡後，郊社令帥其屬掃除，御史按視之。 奏中嚴外辦以禮部侍郎，請解嚴以禮部郎中。 贊者設亞、終獻位於小次之南，宗室位於其後； 設公卿位於亞、終獻之南，分獻官位於公卿之後，執事者又在其後，俱重行，西向北上。 其致福也，太牢以牛左肩、臂、臑折九個，少牢以羊左肩七個、犆豕以左肩五個。 有司攝事、進胙皆如禮。 太尉展視以授使者，再拜稽首。 既享，大宴，號曰飲福，自宰臣而下至應執事及樂工、馭車馬人等，並均給有差，以為定式。 是歲十一月日至，皇帝服袞冕，執圭，合祭天地於圜丘，還禦明德門樓，肆赦。
Sacrifices were timed to the chou hour: one quarter-watch earlier in autumn and summer, seven quarter-watches earlier in spring and winter. Two days beforehand, officials were dispatched to announce the rite. In the associated Thearch's chamber, the Imperial Insignia Office arranged the grand and minor pavilions and seating for civil and military attendants and foreign guests, while the Court of Imperial Sacrifices arranged music stands, spirit positions, and tablet placements. On the eve of sacrifice, the Director of Ritual Vessels arranged the vessels east of the hall. The Grand Counselor and Minister of Rites inspected the washing and declared purity. The Minister and Vice Minister of Rites inspected the sacrificial animals; the Director of the Imperial Commissariat presented them and announced sufficiency and readiness. The Minister of Rites inspected the cauldrons, and the Vice Minister the stages of preparation from raw to cooked. On the morning of sacrifice, the Director of the Imperial Commissariat had his staff fill the platters, bowls, and grain vessels. When presenting the raw offering, the Minister of Rites presented the platters and vessels, while the Ministers of Revenue, War, and Works presented the raw and cooked trays of the three sacrificial animals. When the rite concluded, the offerings were withdrawn and received by the responsible offices. After mid-afternoon, the Director of Suburban and Altar Affairs led his staff in sweeping the site, which the censor inspected. The Vice Minister of Rites announced mid-solemnity and outer readiness; the Director of the Ministry of Rites requested release from solemnity. The invoker placed the secondary and final offering positions south of the minor pavilion, with the imperial clan behind; dukes and ministers south of those, separate offering officials behind them, and officiants behind again — all in double rows, facing west and aligned northward. For the blessing offering, the great victim required nine cuts from the ox's left shoulder, arm, and shank; the lesser victim seven from the sheep's left shoulder; and the single victim five from the pig's left shoulder. Proxy officiants and the presentation of sacrificial meat all followed ritual precedent. The Grand Commandant inspected the offering and handed it to the envoy, who bowed twice and touched his forehead to the ground. After the sacrifice came the great feast called Drinking Blessing. From the chief minister down through all officiants, musicians, and charioteers, shares were distributed in graded amounts as the fixed practice. That year on the winter solstice of the eleventh month, the emperor wore dark ceremonial robes and coronet, held the jade tablet, and jointly sacrificed to Heaven and Earth at the Round Mound. He then returned to the Bright Virtue Gate tower and proclaimed a general amnesty.
18
仁宗天聖二年，詔加真宗諡，上謂輔臣曰：「郊祀重事，朕欲就禁中習儀，其令禮官草具以聞。 」先郊三日，奉諡冊寶於太廟。 次日，薦享玉清昭應、景靈宮，宿太廟。 既享，赴青城，至大次，就更衣壇改服袞冕行事。 五年，郊後擇日恭謝，大禮使王曾請節廟樂，帝曰：「三年一享，不敢憚勞也。 」三獻終，增禮生七人，各引本室太祝升殿，徹豆。 三日，又齋長春殿，謝玉清昭應宮。 禮畢，賀皇太后，比籍田，勞酒儀，略如元會。 其恭謝云：「臣某虔遵舊典，郊祀禮成，中外協心，不勝歡抃。 」宣答曰：「皇帝德備孝恭，禮成嚴配，萬國稱頌，歡豫增深。 」帝再拜還內。 樞密使以下稱賀，閣門使宣答，樞密副使升殿侍立，百官稱賀。 酒三行，還內殿，受命婦賀，司賓自殿側幕次引內命婦於殿庭，北向立，尚儀奏：「請皇太后即御坐。 」司賓贊：「再拜。 」引班首升自西階，稱封號妾某氏等言：「郊祀再舉，福祚鹹均，凡在照臨，不勝忻抃。 」降，再拜。 尚宮承旨，降自東階，稱「皇太后聖旨」，又再拜。 司賓宣答曰「已成钜禮，歡豫良深。 」皆再拜。 次外命婦賀，如內命婦儀，退，皆赴別殿賀皇帝，惟不致詞，不宣答。
In the second year of Tiansheng (1024), as an edict added a posthumous title for Emperor Zhenzong, the emperor told his ministers: "The suburban sacrifice is a weighty affair. I wish to rehearse the rites within the palace. Have the ritual officials draft the procedures and report." Three days before the suburban sacrifice, the posthumous title registers and seals were presented at the Grand Temple. The next day, offerings were presented at the Jade Clarity Manifest Response and Splendid Spirit palaces, and the emperor lodged at the Grand Temple. After the offering, he proceeded to the Azure City, reached the grand pavilion, changed into dark ceremonial robes at the robing altar, and performed the sacrifice. In the fifth year, a day was chosen after the suburban sacrifice for reverent thanksgiving. Grand Ritual Commissioner Wang Zeng asked to reduce temple music, but the emperor replied: "We sacrifice only once every three years — I dare not shrink from the effort." When the three offerings were complete, seven additional ritual students were added, each leading his chamber's Grand Invoker up the hall to withdraw the bean vessels. On the third day, he fasted again in the Everlasting Spring Hall and gave thanks at the Jade Clarity Manifest Response Palace. When the rites concluded, he congratulated the Empress Dowager. The reward-wine ceremony resembled the New Year's audience, much as at the plowing ceremony. The thanksgiving address read: "Your subject reverently follows the established regulations. The suburban sacrifice is complete, and all within and without the court are united in joy beyond measure." The response announced: "The emperor's virtue is complete in filial piety and respect. The rites are accomplished in solemn matching. The ten thousand states offer praise, and joy increases ever deeper." The emperor bowed twice and returned within. From the Bureau of Military Affairs commissioner downward, officials offered congratulations. The Gatekeeper announced the responses, the vice commissioner ascended to attend, and all officials congratulated the emperor. After three rounds of wine, the emperor returned to the inner hall for congratulations from titled consorts. The Master of Guests led the inner consorts from the side curtain into the courtyard, where they stood facing north. The Director of Ceremonies announced: "We request that the Empress Dowager take her seat." The Master of Guests intoned: "Bow twice." He led the senior consort up the western steps. She announced her title and said: "The suburban sacrifice has again been performed. Blessings are equally shared, and all within your radiance rejoice beyond measure." She descended and bowed twice. The Palace Superintendent received the command, descended the eastern steps, announced the Empress Dowager's sacred decree, and all bowed twice again. The Master of Guests announced the response: "The great rite is accomplished, and joy is truly profound." All bowed twice. Outer titled consorts then congratulated following the same rites, withdrew, and proceeded to a separate hall to congratulate the emperor — but without formal addresses or announced responses.

神宗元豐六年十一月二日，帝將親郊，奉仁宗、英宗徽號冊寶於太廟。 是日晚，齋於大慶殿。 三日，薦享于景靈宮，齋於太廟。 四日，朝享七室，齋於南郊之青城。 五日冬至，祀昊天上帝於圜丘，以太祖配。 是日，帝服靴袍，乘輦至大次。 有司請行禮。 服大裘，被袞冕以出，至壇中壝門外，殿中監進大圭，帝執以入，宮架樂作，至午階下版位，西向立，樂止。 禮儀使贊曰：「有司謹具，請行事。 」宮架奏《景安》之樂，文舞作六成，止，帝再拜，詣罍洗，宮架樂作，至洗南北向，樂止。 帝搢圭，盥帨訖，樂作，至壇下，樂止。 升午階，登歌樂作，至壇上，樂止。 殿中監進鎮圭，《嘉安》樂作，詣上帝神坐前，北向跪，奠鎮圭於繅藉，執大圭，俯伏，興，搢圭跪，三上香，奠玉幣，執圭，俯伏，興，再拜。 內侍舉鎮圭授殿中監，樂止。 《廣安》樂作，詣太祖神坐前，東向，奠圭幣如上帝儀。 登歌樂作，帝降壇，樂止。 宮架樂作，還位，西向立，樂止。 禮部尚書、戶部尚書以下奉饌俎，宮架《豐安》樂作，奉奠訖，樂止。 再詣罍洗，帝搢大圭，盥帨，洗爵拭爵訖，執大圭，宮架樂作，至壇下，樂止。 升自午階，登歌樂作，至壇上，樂止。 登歌《禧安》樂作，詣上帝神坐前，搢圭跪，執爵祭酒，三奠訖，執圭，俯伏，興，樂止。 太祝讀冊，帝再拜訖，樂作。 次詣太祖神坐前，如前儀。 登歌樂作，帝降自午階，樂止。 宮架樂作，還位，西向立，樂止。 文舞退，武舞進，宮架《正安》之樂作，樂止。 亞獻盥帨訖，《正安》樂作，禮畢，樂止。 終獻行禮並如上儀，獻畢，宮架樂作，帝升自午階，樂止。 登歌樂作，至飲福位，樂止。 《禧安》樂作，帝再拜，搢圭跪，受爵，祭酒三，啐酒，奠爵，受俎，奠俎，受摶黍豆，再受爵，飲福訖，奠爵，執圭，俯伏，興，再拜，樂作。 帝降，還位如前儀。 禮部、戶部尚書徹俎豆，禮直官曰：「賜胙行事。 」陪祀官再拜，宮架《宴安》樂作，一成止。 宮架樂作，帝詣望燎位，南向立，樂止。 禮直官曰：「可燎。 」俟火燎半柴，禮儀使跪奏：「禮畢。 」宮架樂作，帝出中壝門，殿中監受大圭，歸大次，樂止。 有司奏解嚴。
On the second day of the eleventh month of the sixth year of Yuanfeng (1083), as Emperor Shenzong prepared to conduct the suburban sacrifice in person, the honorific registers and seals of Renzong and Yingzong were presented at the Grand Temple. That evening he fasted in the Great Celebration Hall. On the third day, offerings were presented at the Splendid Spirit Palace, and he fasted at the Grand Temple. On the fourth day, he attended morning sacrifice in the seven chambers and fasted at the Southern Suburb's Azure City. On the fifth day, the winter solstice, he sacrificed to the Supreme Thearch of Vast Heaven at the Round Mound with the Founding Ancestor as associate. That day the emperor wore everyday boots and robe and rode the imperial carriage to the grand pavilion. The responsible officials requested that the rites begin. He donned the great fur robe and dark ceremonial coronet, emerged to the gate of the middle enclosure, received the great jade tablet from the Palace Superintendent, and entered as palace music sounded. He reached the tablet position below the noon stairway, stood facing west, and the music ceased. The Commissioner of Ritual intoned: "The responsible offices have duly prepared. We request that the rites proceed." Palace music performed the hymn Jing'an. The civil dance ran six sections and stopped. The emperor bowed twice and proceeded to the washing urn as music sounded again. He reached the basin, stood facing north, and the music ceased. The emperor inserted the tablet, washed his hands, and dried them. Music sounded as he reached the foot of the altar, then ceased. He ascended the noon stairway as the ascending hymn sounded, reached the altar summit, and the music ceased. The Palace Superintendent presented the securing jade tablet as the hymn Jia'an sounded. The emperor proceeded before the Supreme Thearch's spirit seat, knelt facing north, placed the securing tablet on the silk mat, held the great tablet, prostrated himself, rose, inserted the tablet and knelt, offered incense three times, presented jade and silk, held the tablet, prostrated himself again, rose, and bowed twice. The inner attendant raised the securing tablet and handed it to the Palace Superintendent, and the music ceased. The hymn Guang'an sounded as he proceeded before the Founding Ancestor's spirit seat, facing east, and presented jade and silk following the same rite as for the Supreme Thearch. The ascending hymn sounded. The emperor descended the altar, and the music ceased. Palace music sounded as he returned to his position and stood facing west, then ceased. The Minister of Rites, Minister of Revenue, and officials below presented the food trays as palace music performed Feng'an. When the presentation was complete, the music ceased. He again proceeded to the washing urn. The emperor inserted the great tablet, washed his hands, dried them, and washed and wiped the goblet. Holding the great tablet, he descended to the foot of the altar as palace music sounded, then ceased. He ascended the noon stairway as the ascending hymn sounded, reached the altar summit, and the music ceased. The ascending hymn Xi'an sounded. He proceeded before the Supreme Thearch's spirit seat, inserted the tablet and knelt, libated wine three times, held the tablet, prostrated himself, and rose as the music ceased. The Grand Invoker read the prayer text. The emperor bowed twice, and music sounded. Next he proceeded before the Founding Ancestor's spirit seat following the same procedure. The ascending hymn sounded as the emperor descended the noon stairway, and the music ceased. Palace music sounded as he returned to his position and stood facing west, then ceased. The civil dance withdrew and the military dance advanced as palace music performed Zheng'an, then ceased. After the secondary offering officiant finished washing his hands, Zheng'an sounded, the rite concluded, and the music ceased. The final offering followed the same procedure. When the offerings were complete, palace music sounded as the emperor ascended the noon stairway, then ceased. The ascending hymn sounded as he reached the Drinking Blessing position, and the music ceased. The hymn Xi'an sounded. The emperor bowed twice, inserted the tablet and knelt, received the goblet, libated three times, sipped the wine, and set down the goblet. He received the meat tray and set it down, received the millet and beans, received the goblet again, and finished drinking the blessing. He set down the goblet, held the tablet, prostrated himself, rose, bowed twice, and music sounded. The emperor descended and returned to his position as before. The Ministers of Rites and Revenue withdrew the trays and vessels. The ritual officer announced: "Bestow the sacrificial meat and proceed with the rites." Attendant sacrifice officials bowed twice as palace music performed Yan'an for one section and stopped. Palace music sounded as the emperor proceeded to the burning-watching position and stood facing south, then ceased. The ritual officer announced: "The burning may proceed." When the fire had burned halfway through the firewood, the Commissioner of Ritual knelt and reported: "The rites are complete." Palace music sounded as the emperor exited the middle enclosure gate. The Palace Superintendent received the great tablet, and the emperor returned to the grand pavilion as the music ceased. The responsible offices announced release from solemnity.
20
帝乘輿還青城，百官稱賀於端誠殿。 有司轉仗衛，奏中嚴外辦。 帝服通天冠、絳紗袍，乘輿以出。 至玉輅所，侍中跪請降輿升輅。 帝升輅，門下侍郎奏請進行，又奏請少駐，侍臣乘馬，將至宣德門，奏《采薺》一曲，入門，樂止。 侍中請降輅赴幄次，有司奏解嚴。 帝常服，乘輿禦宣德門，肆赦，群臣稱賀如常儀。
The emperor rode back to the Azure City, and all officials offered congratulations in the Sincere Integrity Hall. The responsible offices shifted the guard of honor and announced mid-solemnity and outer readiness. The emperor wore the heaven-piercing cap and crimson gauze robe and rode out in the imperial carriage. At the jade carriage, the Palace Attendant knelt and asked the emperor to descend the palanquin and board the carriage. The emperor boarded the carriage. The Vice Director of the Secretariat requested departure, then a brief halt. Attendant ministers rode on horseback. Approaching the Xuande Gate, Gathering Shepherd's Purse was performed once before they entered the gate and the music ceased. The Palace Attendant asked the emperor to descend and proceed to the tent quarters, and the responsible offices announced release from solemnity. In everyday dress the emperor rode to the Xuande Gate, proclaimed a general amnesty, and the ministers congratulated him according to regular ceremony.
21
神宗之嗣位也，英宗之喪未除。 是歲當郊，帝以為疑，以問講讀官王珪、司馬光、王安石，皆對以不當廢。 珪又謂：「'喪三年不祭，惟祭天地、社稷，為越紼而行事。 '《傳》謂：'不敢以卑廢尊也。 '景德二年，真宗居明德太后之喪，即易月而服除。 明年遂享太廟，而合祀天地於圜丘。 請冬至行郊廟之禮，其服冕、車輅、儀物、音樂緣神事者皆不可廢。 」詔用景德故事，惟郊廟及景靈宮禮神用樂，鹵簿鼓吹及樓前宮架、諸軍音樂，皆備而不作，警場止鳴金鉦、鼓角，仍罷諸軍呈閱騎隊。 故事，齋宿必禦樓警嚴，幸後苑觀花，作水戲，至是悉罷之。 有司言：「故事，當謁謝於祖宗神禦殿，獻享月吉禮，以禮官攝。 」詔遣輔臣仍罷詣佛寺。 是後國有故，皆遣輔臣。
When Shenzong succeeded to the throne, mourning for Yingzong had not yet ended. A suburban sacrifice was due that year. The emperor was uncertain and consulted lecture officials Wang Gui, Sima Guang, and Wang Anshi. All replied that it should not be canceled. Wang Gui added: "During the three-year mourning period one does not sacrifice except to Heaven and Earth and the altars of soil and grain — one performs the rites by stepping over the mourning cord." The Commentary states: "One dare not let the humble abolish the exalted." In the second year of Jingde, when Zhenzong was mourning Empress Dowager Mingde, he shortened the mourning period and completed it. The following year he sacrificed at the Grand Temple and jointly honored Heaven and Earth at the Round Mound. We ask that the suburban and temple rites be performed at the winter solstice. The coronet and robes, carriage, ritual objects, and music pertaining to the divine rites must not be abandoned. An edict adopted the Jingde precedent: music was used only at the suburban altars, temples, and Splendid Spirit Palace to honor the spirits. Imperial escort drums, tower palace music, and military music were prepared but not performed. The alert ground sounded only gongs and horns, and the military cavalry review was suspended. By precedent, during the fasting vigil the emperor mounted the tower for the alert, visited the rear garden to view flowers, and staged water performances — all of which were now suspended. The responsible offices reported: "By precedent, one should visit the ancestral spirit halls to give thanks, and the monthly auspicious offering should be performed by ritual officials acting as proxy." An edict dispatched chief ministers but still suspended visits to Buddhist temples. Thereafter, whenever the state was in mourning, chief ministers were always dispatched in the emperor's place.
22
高宗紹興十二年，臣僚言：「自南巡以來，三歲之祀，獨於明堂，而郊天之禮未舉，來歲乞行大禮。 」詔建圜壇于臨安府行宮東城之外，自是凡六郊焉。
In the twelfth year of Shaoxing (1142), officials reported: "Since the court moved south, triennial sacrifices have been held only at the Bright Hall, while the Heaven sacrifice has not been performed. We ask that the great rite be held next year." An edict ordered a round altar built outside the eastern wall of the imperial residence in Lin'an. Six suburban sacrifices were performed thereafter.
23
孝宗隆興二年，詔曰：「聯恭覽國史，太祖乾德詔書有云：'務從省約，無至勞煩。 '仰見事天之誠，愛民之仁，所以垂萬世之統者在是。 今歲郊見，可令有司，除禮物、軍賞，其餘並從省約。 。 初降詔以十一月行事，以冬至適在晦日，以至道典故，改用獻歲上辛，遂改來年元為乾道。 乃以正月一日有事南郊，禮成，進胙于德壽宮，以牛腥體肩三、臂上臑二。 導駕官自端誠殿簪花從駕至德壽宮上壽，飲福稱賀，陳設儀注，並同上壽禮。 皇帝致詞曰：「皇帝臣某言：享帝合宮，受天純嘏，臣某與百僚不勝大慶，謹上千萬歲壽。 」自後郊祀、明堂進胙飲福，並如上儀。
In the second year of Longxing (1164), an edict stated: "We have reverently reviewed the dynastic history. An edict of the Founding Ancestor in Qiande reads: 'Strive for economy and simplicity; do not cause undue toil and trouble.'" In this one sees the sincerity of serving Heaven and the benevolence of loving the people — the foundation on which the imperial succession is transmitted for ten thousand generations. For this year's suburban audience with Heaven, the responsible offices are directed that apart from ritual gifts and military rewards, all else should follow economy and simplicity. . An edict initially scheduled the rite for the eleventh month, but because the winter solstice fell on the month's last day, following the Zhidao precedent the first xin day of the offering year was chosen instead, and the next year's era name was changed to Qiandao. The Southern Suburb sacrifice was performed on the first day of the first month. When the rite concluded, sacrificial meat was presented at the Virtue and Longevity Palace: three shoulder cuts and two upper-arm cuts from the raw ox. Escort officials wearing flowers in their caps followed the imperial carriage from the Sincere Integrity Hall to the Virtue and Longevity Palace to offer longevity congratulations, drink the blessing wine, and celebrate — all following the standard longevity ceremony. The emperor's address read: "Your subject the emperor reports: Having sacrificed to the Thearch at the Bright Hall and received Heaven's pure blessing, your subject and the hundred officials rejoice beyond measure and respectfully offer ten thousand years of life." Thereafter, presenting sacrificial meat and drinking the blessing wine at suburban sacrifice and Bright Hall ceremonies all followed this procedure.
24
光宗紹熙二年十一月郊，以值雨，行禮於望祭殿。 帝遂感疾。 理宗四十一年，一郊而已。 度宗鹹淳二年，權工部尚書趙汝暨等奏：「今歲大禮，正在先帝大祥之後，臣等竊惟帝王受命，郊見天地，不可緩也。 古者有改元即郊，不用前郊三年為計。 況今適在當郊之歲，既逾大祥之期，圜丘之祀，豈容不舉？ 」於是降禮，以十一月十七日款謁南郊，適太史院言：「十六日太陰交蝕。 」遂改來年正月一日南郊行禮，太常寺言：「皇帝既已從吉，請依儀用樂。 其十二月二十九日朝獻景靈宮，三十日朝享太廟，尚在禫制之內，所有迎神、奠幣、酌獻、送神作樂外，其盥洗升降行步等樂，備而不作。」
At the suburban sacrifice in the eleventh month of the second year of Shaoxi (1191), rain forced the rites to be performed in the Hall for Gazing Sacrifice. The emperor then fell ill. During Lizong's forty-one-year reign, only one suburban sacrifice was performed. In the second year of Xianchun (1266), Acting Minister of Works Zhao Ruji and others memorialized: "This year's great rite falls just after the late emperor's da xiang mourning. We respectfully observe that when an emperor receives the mandate, audience with Heaven and Earth at the suburb cannot be delayed." In antiquity some performed the suburban sacrifice upon changing the era name, without calculating from the previous suburb three years earlier. Moreover, this is precisely the year when the suburb is due, and the da xiang period has passed. How can the Round Mound sacrifice be omitted? Rites were promulgated for a reverent visit to the Southern Suburb on the seventeenth of the eleventh month, but the Astronomical Bureau reported: "A lunar eclipse will occur on the sixteenth." The rite was moved to the first day of the first month of the following year. The Court of Imperial Sacrifices reported: "Since the emperor has completed mourning, we ask that music be used according to ritual precedent." The court presentation at the Splendid Spirit Palace on the twenty-ninth of the twelfth month and the morning offering at the Grand Temple on the thirtieth still fell within chan mourning. Apart from music for welcoming the spirits, presenting silks, pouring offerings, and sending them off, music for washing, ascending, descending, and procession was to be prepared but not performed."
