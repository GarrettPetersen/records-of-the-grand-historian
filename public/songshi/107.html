1
宗廟之禮。 每歲以四孟月及季冬，凡五享，朔、望則上食、薦新。 三年一祫，以孟冬； 五年一禘，以孟夏，唯親郊、封祀。 又有朝享、告謝及新主祔謁，皆大祀也。 二薦，則行一獻禮。 其祔祭，春祀司命及戶，夏祀灶，季夏祀中霤，秋祀門及厲，冬祀行，惟臘享、禘祫則遍祀焉。
Rites of the Ancestral Temple. Each year the court holds five seasonal offerings—in the four opening months of the seasons and the last month of winter—and on the new and full moons presents food and offers the new harvest. Every three years a xia rite was held in early winter; every five years a di rite in early summer, except when the emperor personally performed the suburban sacrifice or the feng and shan rites. There were also court offerings, thanksgiving rites, and audiences when new spirit tablets were enshrined—all counted as great sacrifices. For the two seasonal offerings, only the single-presentation rite was used. Collateral sacrifices were distributed through the year: in spring to the Director of Fate and the Household; in summer to the Stove; in the last summer month to the Central Court; in autumn to the Gate and the Plague Spirit; in winter to the Pathway—only at the La offering and the di and xia rites were all of them worshipped together.
2
禘祫之禮。 真宗咸平二年八月，太常禮院言：「今年冬祭畫日，以十月六日薦享太廟。 按《禮》，三年一祫，以孟冬。 又《疑義》云：三年喪畢，遭禘則禘，遭袷則袷。 宜改孟冬薦享為祫享。 」仁宗天聖元年，禮官言：「真宗神主祔廟，已行吉祭，三年之制，又從易月之文，自天禧二年四月禘享，至今已及五年，合行禘禮。 」遂以孟夏薦享為禘享。 八年九月，太常禮院言：「自天聖六年夏行禘享之禮，至此年十月，請以孟冬薦享為祫享。 」詔恭依。
The Di and Xia Rites. In the eighth month of Xianping 2 under Emperor Zhenzong, the Court of Imperial Rites reported: "The winter sacrifice for this year has been scheduled for the sixth day of the tenth month, when offerings will be presented at the imperial ancestral temple. According to the canonical Rites, a xia sacrifice comes every three years, in early winter. The Doubts on the Rites likewise states that once the three-year mourning is finished, whichever great rite falls due—di or xia—should be performed. The early-winter seasonal offering should therefore be conducted as a xia rite. " In Renzong's first year of Tiansheng, ritual officials argued: "Now that Emperor Zhenzong's tablet has been enshrined and the auspicious rites completed, and with the three-year mourning period shortened as permitted, it has been five years since the di offering in the fourth month of Tianxi 2—the di rite is due. " The court accordingly held the early-summer seasonal offering as a di rite. In the ninth month of year 8, the Court of Imperial Rites reported: "Since the di rite was performed in the summer of Tiansheng 6, the early-winter offering this tenth month should be conducted as a xia rite. " The emperor ordered that this be followed.
3
嘉祐四年十月，仁宗親詣太廟行祫享禮，以宰臣富弼為祫享大禮使，韓琦為禮儀使，樞密使宋庠為儀仗使，參知政事曾公亮為橋道頓遞使，樞密副使程戡為鹵簿使。 同判宗正寺趙良規請正太祖東向位，禮官不敢決。 觀文殿學士王舉正等議曰：「大祫之禮所以合昭穆，辨尊卑，必以受命之祖居東向之位。 本朝以太祖為受命之君，然僖祖以降，四廟在上，故每遇大袷，止列昭穆而虛東向。 魏、晉以來，亦用此禮。 今親享之盛，宜如舊便。」
In the tenth month of Jiayou 4, Emperor Renzong went in person to the imperial ancestral temple for the xia rite. Chief minister Fu Bi served as grand commissioner of the xia ceremony, Han Qi as ritual commissioner, Privy Councilor Song Qi as equipage commissioner, Vice Grand Councilor Zeng Gongliang as bridge-and-escort commissioner, and Vice Privy Councilor Cheng Kan as guard-of-honor commissioner. Zhao Lianggui, associate director of the Court of the Imperial Clan, petitioned to restore Emperor Taizu to the east-facing seat, but the ritual officials would not settle the question. Academician Wang Juzheng and others argued: "The great xia rite joins the zhao and mu lines and fixes rank and precedence; the ancestor who received the Mandate must sit in the east-facing place. Our dynasty regards Emperor Taizu as the Mandate-receiving sovereign, yet from Emperor Xi downward four ancestral shrines stand above him; at every great xia only the zhao and mu tablets are set out and the east-facing seat is left empty. Wei and Jin had followed the same practice. For so solemn an offering in the emperor's own person, the established arrangement should stand."
4
禮官張洞、韓維言：「國朝每遇禘祫，奉別廟四後之主合食太廟。 唐《郊祀志》載禘祫祝文，自獻祖至肅宗所配皆一後，惟睿宗二後，蓋昭成，明皇母也。 《續曲台禮》有別廟皇后合食之文，蓋未有本室，遇祫享即祔祖姑下。 所以大順中，三太后配列禘祭，議者議其非禮。 臣謂每室既有定配，則餘後不當參列，義當革正。」
Ritual officials Zhang Dong and Han Wei argued: "At every di and xia our dynasty brings the tablets of the four empresses from their separate shrines to share the communal feast in the imperial ancestral temple. Tang's Record of Suburban Sacrifices shows that from Emperor Xianzu through Suzong each emperor had one consort paired in the rites; only Ruizong had two—Zhaocheng, mother of Emperor Xuanzong. The Continued Qu Terrace Rites preserves language on empresses from separate shrines sharing the feast—when they lacked a main chamber, a xia rite placed them below the founding ancestress. That is why in the Dazhun era three empress dowagers were ranked in the di sacrifice—a step critics condemned as uncanonical. We hold that once each chamber has its fixed consort, the other empresses should not be included—the practice ought to be corrected."
5
學士孫抃等議：「《春秋傳》曰：『大祫者何，合祭也。 未毀廟之主皆升合食於太祖。 』是以國朝事宗廟百有餘年，至祫之日，別廟後主皆升合食，非無典據。 大中祥符中已曾定議，禮官著酌中之論，先帝有『恭依』之詔。 他年有司攝事，四後皆預。 今甫欲親祫而四後見黜，不亦疑於以禮之煩故邪？ 宗廟之禮，至尊至重，苟未能盡祖宗之意，則莫若守舊禮。 臣等愚以謂如故便。」
Academician Sun Bian and others argued: "The Spring and Autumn commentary asks, 'What is the great xia?'—a joint sacrifice. Every tablet from shrines not yet moved to the distant hall ascends to feast with the founding ancestor.' " Our dynasty has tended the ancestral temple for more than a century; on xia day the empresses' tablets from separate shrines have always joined the communal feast—hardly without precedent. In the Dazhong Xiangfu period the question was already settled; ritual officials drafted a balanced opinion, and the late emperor ordered it followed. Whenever officials conducted the rite in the emperor's stead, all four empresses took part. Now, on the eve of a xia the emperor will perform in person, the four empresses are to be excluded—does this not look like trimming ritual burden rather than honoring the ancestors? Ancestral rites are the most solemn of all; if we cannot fully satisfy our forebears' intent, we should keep the established practice. We humbly advise leaving matters as they have been."
6
學士歐陽修等曰：「古者宗廟之制，皆一帝一後。 後世有以子貴者，始著並祔之文，其不當祔者，則有別廟之祭。 本朝禘祫，乃以別廟之後列於配後之下，非惟於古無文，於今又四不可。 淑德，太宗之元配，列於元德之下； 章懷，真宗之元配，列於章懿之下，一也。 升祔之後，統以帝樂； 別廟之後，則以本室樂章自隨，二也。 升祔之後，同牢而祭，牲器祝冊亦統於帝； 別廟諸後，乃從專享，三也。 升祔之後，聯席而坐； 別廟之後，位乃相絕，四也。 章獻、章懿在奉慈廟，每遇禘祫，本廟致享，最為得禮。 若四後各祭於廟，則其尊自申，是於禮無失。 以為行之已久，重於改作，則是失禮之舉，無復是正也。 請從禮官。」
Academician Ouyang Xiu and others argued: "In antiquity each emperor had one consort in the ancestral temple. Later ages, when a mother was honored through her son, joint enshrinement was codified; consorts not so enshrined received rites at separate shrines. Our di and xia rites rank empresses from separate shrines below the principal consort—without ancient precedent and, in the present case, with four further objections. Empress Shude, Taizong's first consort, is placed below Empress Yuande; Empress Zhanghuai, Zhenzong's first consort, stands below Empress Zhangyi—the first objection. Enshrined consorts use the emperor's hymn; empresses from separate shrines keep their own shrine hymns—the second objection. Enshrined consorts share one victim and one set of vessels and prayer texts with the emperor; empresses from separate shrines receive separate offerings—the third objection. Enshrined consorts sit on adjoining mats; empresses from separate shrines are set far apart—the fourth objection. Empresses Zhangxian and Zhangyi are housed at the Fengci Shrine; at each di and xia their own shrine is served—most correct by ritual. If all four empresses are worshipped in their own shrines, their rank is properly displayed without breach of ritual. To refuse reform because a wrong practice has lasted long is to treat a breach of ritual as beyond correction. We ask that the ritual officials' proposal be adopted."
7
詔：「四後祫享依舊，須大禮畢，別加討論。 」仍詔：「祫享前一日，皇帝詣景靈宮，如南郊禮，衛士毋得迎駕呼萬歲。 」有司言：「諸司奉禮，攝廩犧令省牲，依《通禮》改正祀儀。 散齋四日於別殿，致齋二日於大慶殿，一日於太廟。 尚舍直殿下，設小次，御坐不設黃道褥位。 七室各用一太牢，每坐簠簋二，鉶三，籩豆為後，無黼扆、席幾。 出三閣瑞石、篆書玉璽印、青玉環、金山陳於庭。 別廟四後合食，牲樂奠拜無異儀。 故事，七祀、功臣無牲，止於廟牲肉分割，知廟卿行事。 請依《續曲台禮》，共料一羊，而獻官三員，功臣單席，如大中祥符加褥。」
An edict ordered: "The four empresses shall continue to share the xia feast as before; after the great ceremony, the question will be reconsidered separately. " It was further ordered that on the eve of the xia rite the emperor should visit Jingling Palace as for the suburban sacrifice, and that guards must not hail the procession with cries of "Long live!" " The responsible offices reported that all departments should observe ritual form, the acting director of imperial herds should inspect the victims, and the sacrificial protocol should be revised per the Comprehensive Rites. Dispersed fasting for four days in a side hall, concentrated fasting for two days in the Hall of Great Celebration and one day in the imperial ancestral temple. The Palace Supplies Office was to attend below the hall steps, set up a minor resting pavilion, and omit the yellow-path mat for the imperial seat. Each of the seven chambers was to receive one great victim; at each seat two grain vessels, three meat stands, and platters and cups behind—with no embroidered screen, mat, or side table. The auspicious stone from the Three Pavilions, the seal-script jade imperial seal, the green jade ring, and the golden mountain were displayed in the courtyard. The four empresses from separate shrines shared the feast, with victims, music, offerings, and obeisances performed by the same protocol. By precedent the seven collateral spirits and meritorious ministers received no separate victims—only portions of the temple victim's meat—while the temple director conducted their rites. We ask that per the Continued Qu Terrace Rites one sheep be shared in common, with three presenting officials and meritorious ministers on separate mats, as in the Dazhong Xiangfu addition of cushions."
8
十月二日，命樞密副使張望告昊天上帝、皇地祇。 帝齋大慶殿。 十一日，服通天冠、絳紗袍，執圭、乘輿，至大慶殿門外降輿，乘大輦，至天興殿，薦享畢，齋於太廟。 明日，帝常服至大次，改袞冕，行禮畢，質明，乘大輦還宮，更服靴袍，禦紫宸殿，宰臣、百官賀，升宣德門肆赦。 二十一日，詣諸觀寺行恭謝禮。 二十六日，御集英殿為飲福宴。
On the second day of the tenth month, Vice Privy Councilor Zhang Wang was dispatched to announce the rite to the Supreme Lord of Heaven and Imperial Earth. The emperor kept ritual abstention in the Hall of Great Celebration. On the eleventh day he wore the Tongtian crown and crimson gauze robe, held the jade tablet, and rode the imperial carriage; outside the Hall of Great Celebration he transferred to the great palanquin, proceeded to the Hall of Heavenly Prosperity for the offering, then kept abstention at the imperial ancestral temple. The next day he came in ordinary dress to the great pavilion, changed into full ceremonial robes, and completed the rites; at dawn he returned by great palanquin, changed into court boots and robe, took the throne at the Hall of Purple Brightness to receive congratulations from ministers and officials, then ascended Xuande Gate to proclaim a general amnesty. On the twenty-first day he visited the Daoist abbeys and Buddhist temples to perform the thanksgiving rites. On the twenty-sixth day he held the blessed-draught banquet at the Hall of Assembled Excellence.

In the first year of Zhiping, the responsible offices reported: "Per the scheduled calendar, the early-winter seasonal offering is to be conducted as a xia sacrifice. The Spring and Autumn Annals records that while Duke Min's mourning was still incomplete an auspicious

禘，《三傳》譏之。 真宗以咸平二年六月喪除，至十月乃祫祭。 天聖元年在諒陰，有司誤通天禧舊禘之數，在再期內按行禘祭。 以理推之，是二年冬應祫，而誤禘於元年夏，故四十九年間九禘八祫，例皆太速。 事失於始，則歲月相乘，不得而正。 今在大祥內，禮未應袷，明年未禫，亦未應禘，至六月即吉，二月合行祫祭，乞依舊時享，庶合典禮。」
di rite was held, and all three commentaries condemned it. Emperor Zhenzong ended mourning in the sixth month of Xianping 2 but did not hold the xia rite until the tenth month. In Tiansheng 1, still in mourning seclusion, officials mistakenly applied the old Tianxi di schedule and held a di rite within the second mourning period. By rights a xia was due in the winter of year 2, but a di was wrongly held in the summer of year 1; over the next forty-nine years there were nine di and eight xia rites—each cycle too short. Once the initial step was wrong, the error compounded year by year and could no longer be set right. We are still within the great auspicious mourning period, so a xia is not yet due; next year, before mourning ends, a di is likewise premature. Auspicious rites resume only in the sixth month, and a xia would be proper in the second month—we ask that the regular seasonal offering be held instead, in keeping with canonical ritual."
11
二年二月，翰林學士王珪等上議曰：「同知太常禮院呂夏卿狀：古者新君踐阼之三年，先君之喪二十七月為禫祭，然後新主祔廟，特行禘祭，謂之始禘。 是冬十月行袷祭，明年又行禘祭，自此五年，再為禘祫。 喪除必有禘祫者，為再大祭之本也。 今當袷祭，緣陛下未終三年之制，納有司之說，十月依舊時享。 然享廟、袷祭，其禮不同。 故事，郊享之年遇祫未嘗權罷，唯罷臘祭。 是則孟享與享廟嘗並行於季冬矣。 其禘祫年數，乞一依太常禮院請，今年十月行祫祭，明年四月行禘祭。 仍如夏卿議。 」權罷今年臘享。
In the second month of year 2, Hanlin Academician Wang Gui and others memorialized: "Associate Director Lü Xiaqing of the Court of Imperial Rites reported that in antiquity, in the new ruler's third year, when the twenty-seven-month mourning for the late sovereign ended with the end-of-mourning sacrifice, the new tablet was enshrined and a special di rite—the inaugural di—was performed. That winter's tenth month saw the xia rite; the following year the di rite; thereafter di and xia recurred every five years. The di and xia after mourning ends are the foundation of the two great succession rites. A xia is now due, but because Your Majesty has not yet completed the three-year mourning, accepting the offices' view, the regular seasonal offering will be held in the tenth month instead. Yet a seasonal temple offering and a xia rite are not the same ceremony. By precedent, in years when the suburban sacrifice coincided with a xia, the xia was never set aside—only the La offering was suspended. Thus the first-month offering and the temple offering have sometimes been held together in late winter. For the di and xia schedule, we ask that the Court of Imperial Rites' proposal be followed in full: a xia this tenth month and a di next fourth month. This accords with Xiaqing's proposal as well. " The La offering for that year was provisionally suspended.
12
熙寧八年，有司言：「已尊僖祖為太廟始祖，孟夏禘祭，當正東向之位。 」又言：「太廟禘祭神位，已尊始祖居東向之位，自順祖而下，昭、穆各以南北為序。 自今禘祫，著為定禮。」
In Xining 8 the responsible offices reported: "Emperor Xi has been honored as founding ancestor of the imperial ancestral temple; at the early-summer di rite he should take the corrected east-facing seat. " They added that at the imperial ancestral temple di rite the founding ancestor now occupied the east-facing place, and from Emperor Shun downward the zhao and mu lines were ordered north and south. Henceforth di and xia were to follow this as fixed ritual."
13
元豐四年，詳定郊廟禮文所言：「禘祫之義，存於《周禮》、《春秋》，而不著其名。 行禮之年，經皆無文，唯《公羊傳》曰：『五年而再盛祭。 』《禮緯》曰：『三年一祫，五年一禘。 』而又分為二說：鄭氏則曰：『前三後二』，謂禘後四十二月而祫，祫後十八月而禘。 徐邈則曰：『前二後三』，謂二祭相去各三十月。 以二說考之，惟鄭氏曰：『魯禮，三年喪畢，祫於太廟，明年禘於群廟，自後五年而再盛祭，一祫一禘。 』實為有據。 本朝慶曆初用徐邈說，每三十月一祭。 熙寧八年，既禘而祫，此有司之失也。 請今十八月而禘，禘四十二月而祫，庶幾舉禮不煩，事神不瀆。 」太常禮院言：「本朝自慶曆以來，皆三十月而一祭。 至熙寧五年後，始不通計，遂至八年禘祫並在一歲。 昨元豐三年四月已行禘禮，今年若依舊例，十月行祫享，即比年頻袷，復踵前失。 請依慶曆以來之制，通計年數，皆三十月而祭。 」詔如見行典禮。
In Yuanfeng 4 the commission revising suburban and temple ritual texts stated: "The di and xia rites are attested in the Rites of Zhou and the Spring and Autumn Annals, though the terms themselves are not named there. The years in which they fall are nowhere specified in the classics; only the Gongyang Commentary says, 'Every five years the great sacrifices recur. ' The Ritual Apocrypha states, 'A xia every three years, a di every five.' ' Two schools disagree: Zheng's followers hold 'three before, two after'—twelve months after a di comes a xia, and eighteen months after a xia comes a di. Xu Miao holds 'two before, three after,' meaning the two rites fall thirty months apart. Of the two views, only Zheng's school states: 'In Lu ritual, when three-year mourning ends, xia at the imperial ancestral temple; the next year di at the group shrines; thereafter every five years the great rites recur—one xia, one di.' ' This view has solid warrant. Our dynasty at the opening of Qingli followed Xu Miao and held a sacrifice every thirty months. In Xining 8 a di was followed immediately by a xia—an error by the responsible offices. We ask that henceforth a di follow eighteen months after a xia and a xia forty-two months after a di, so ritual is not excessive and the spirits not profaned. " The Court of Imperial Rites replied: "Since Qingli our dynasty has held a sacrifice every thirty months. After Xining 5 the schedule was no longer reckoned correctly, until in year 8 both di and xia fell in a single year. The di rite was already performed in the fourth month of Yuanfeng 3; if this year the old practice is followed with a xia in the tenth month, xia rites will come in quick succession and repeat the earlier mistake. We ask that the Qingli practice be restored: count the full interval and hold a sacrifice every thirty months. " The emperor ordered that the ritual then in force be followed.
14
詳定所又言：「古者稞獻、饋食，禴祠、烝、嘗，並為先王之享，未嘗廢一時之祭。 故孔氏《正義》以為：『天子夏為大祭之禘，不廢時祭之礿； 秋為大祭之祫，不廢時祭之嘗。 』則王禮三年一袷與禘享，更為時祭。 本朝沿襲故常，久未厘正，請每禘祫之月雖已大祭，仍行時享，以嚴天子備禮，所以丕崇祖宗之義。 其郊禮、親祠準此。」
The ritual commission added: "In antiquity libation presentations, feeding rites, and the spring, autumn, winter, and summer offerings were all part of the former kings' worship—no seasonal sacrifice was ever set aside. Kong Yingda's Correct Meaning therefore states: 'In summer the Son of Heaven holds the great di rite yet does not suspend the seasonal yue offering; in autumn he holds the great xia rite yet does not suspend the seasonal chang offering.' ' Thus in royal ritual the triennial xia and quinquennial di are added to, not substituted for, the seasonal offerings. Our dynasty has long followed old custom without correction; we ask that even in months when di or xia is performed, the seasonal temple offering still be held, so the Son of Heaven's full ritual is preserved and the ancestors greatly honored. Suburban sacrifice and personal temple worship should follow the same rule."
15
又言：「《禮》：不王不禘。 虞、夏、商、周四代所禘，皆以帝有天下，其世係所出者明，故追祭所及者遠也。 太祖受命，祭四親廟，推僖祖而上所自出者，譜失其傳，有司因仍舊說，禘祫皆合群廟之主，綴食於始祖，失禮莫甚。 今國家世係與四代不同，既求其祖之所自出而不得，則禘禮當闕，必推見祖係乃可以行。 」神宗謂輔臣曰：「禘者，本以審禘祖之所自出，故禮，不王不禘。 秦、漢以後，譜牒不明，莫知其祖之所自出，由禘禮可廢也。」
They also stated: "The Rites declare: only a true king may perform the di. The di rites of Yu, Xia, Shang, and Zhou were all performed because the sovereign held the realm and his line of descent was clear—so retrospective worship could reach far back. When Taizu received the Mandate he worshipped at the four close ancestral shrines; tracing above Emperor Xi to the dynastic founder, the genealogy was lost; officials followed the old story and at every di and xia combined all shrine tablets in feast at the founding ancestor—a grave breach of ritual. Our dynasty's lineage differs from those four ages; since the founding ancestor cannot be identified, the di rite should be omitted until the visible ancestral line is established. " Emperor Shenzong told his chief ministers: "The di rite exists to identify the ancestor from whom one sprang; hence the Rites: only a true king may perform the di. From Qin and Han onward genealogies were unclear and the founding ancestor unknown—so the di rite may be set aside."
16
已而詳定所言：「古者天子祭宗廟，有堂事焉，有室事焉。 按《禮》，祝延屍入奧，灌後乃出延牲，延屍主出於室，坐於堂上，始祖南面，昭在東，穆在西，乃行朝踐之禮，是堂事也。 設饌於堂，復延主入室，始祖東面，昭南穆北，徙常上之饌於室中，乃行饋食之禮，是室事也。 請每行大祫，堂上設南面之位，室中設東面之位。 」禮部言：「合食之禮，始祖東面、昭南穆北者，本室中之位也。 今設位戶外，祖宗昭、穆別為幄次，殆非合食之義。 請自今祫享，即前楹通設帳幕，以應室中之位。」
Later the ritual commission stated: "In antiquity when the Son of Heaven worshipped at the ancestral temple, there were hall rites and chamber rites. According to the Rites, the invoker led the representative corpse into the inner recess; after libation the victim was presented; the spirit tablet was brought from the chamber to the hall; the founding ancestor faced south with zhao east and mu west, and the court audience rite was performed—the hall rite. Food was set in the hall, then the tablet returned to the chamber; the founding ancestor faced east with zhao south and mu north; the hall offerings were moved inside, and the feeding rite was performed—the chamber rite. We ask that at each great xia a south-facing seat be set in the hall and an east-facing seat in the chamber. " The Ministry of Rites replied: "In the joint feast, the founding ancestor east-facing with zhao south and mu north is the chamber arrangement. Setting seats outside with ancestors, zhao, and mu in separate canopies scarcely matches a true joint feast. From the next xia onward, we ask that curtains be hung continuously before the front pillars to match the chamber seating."
17
大觀四年，議禮局請：「每大祫，堂上設南面之位，室中設東南之位，始祖南面則昭穆東西相向，始祖東面則昭穆南北相向，以應古義。 」又請：「陳瑞物及代國之寶與貢物可出而陳者，並令有司依嘉祐、元豐詔旨，凡親祠太廟準此。 」從之。
In Daguan 4 the Bureau for Deliberating Ritual proposed: "At each great xia, set a south-facing seat in the hall and a southeast-facing seat in the chamber—when the founding ancestor faces south, zhao and mu face east and west; when he faces east, zhao and mu face north and south, in accord with antiquity. " They also asked that auspicious objects, dynastic treasures, and displayable tribute items be set out by the relevant offices per the Jiayou and Yuanfeng edicts, with all personal worship at the imperial ancestral temple following the same rule." " The proposal was approved.
18
南渡之後，有祫而無禘。 高宗建炎二年，祫享於洪州。 紹興二年，祫享於溫州。 時儀文草創，奉遷祖宗及祧廟神主、別廟神主，各設幄合食於太廟。 始祖東向，昭、穆以次南北相向。
After the court moved south, xia rites were held but not di. In Jianyan 2 under Emperor Gaozong, a xia rite was held at Hong Prefecture. In Shaoxing 2, a xia rite was held at Wen Prefecture. Ritual forms were then newly devised: ancestral tablets from the main, distant, and separate shrines were brought, each under its own canopy, for a joint feast in the imperial ancestral temple. The founding ancestor faced east; zhao and mu in succession faced one another north and south.
19
五年，吏部員外郎董弅言：「臣聞戎、祀，國之大事，而宗廟之祭，又祀之大者也。 大祀，禘祫為重，祫大禘小，則袷為莫大焉。 今戎事方殷，祭祀之禮未暇遍舉，然事有違經戾古，上不當天地神祇之意，下未合億兆黎庶之心，特出於一時大臣好勝之臆說，而行之六十年未有知其非者。 顧雖治兵禦戎之際，正厥違誤，宜不可緩。 仰惟太祖受天明命，混一區宇，即其功德所起，宜祇享以正東向之尊。 逮至仁宗，親行祫享，嘗議太祖東向，用昭正統之緒。 當時在廷之臣，僉謂自古必以受命之祖乃居東向之位，本朝太祖乃受命之君，若論七廟之次，有僖祖以降四廟在上，當時大祫，止列昭穆而虛東向，蓋終不敢以非受命之祖而居之也。 暨熙寧之初，僖祖以世次當祧，禮官韓維等據經有請，適王安石用事，奮其臆說，乃俾章衡建議，尊僖祖為始祖，肇居東向。 馮京奏謂士大夫以太祖不得東向為恨，安石肆言以折之。 已而又欲罷太祖郊配，神宗以太祖開基受命，不許，安石終不以為然。 元祐之初，翼祖既祧，正合典禮。 至於崇寧，宣祖當祧，適蔡京用事，一遵安石之術，乃建言請立九廟，自我作古，其已祧翼祖、宣祖並即依舊。 循沿至今，太祖尚居第四室，遇大祫處昭穆之列。 今若正太祖東向之尊，委合《禮經》。」
In year 5, Vice Director of Personnel Dong Bian memorialized: "I have heard that war and sacrifice are the state's greatest affairs, and ancestral temple worship the greatest of sacrifices. Among great sacrifices di and xia weigh most heavily; xia is greater than di, so xia is supreme. War now presses and not every sacrifice can be performed, yet some practices violate the classics and antiquity—offending Heaven, Earth, and the spirits above and the people below—born solely from one minister's contentious private notion and followed for sixty years without recognition of error. Even amid military preparations, correcting these errors cannot wait. Taizu received Heaven's clear Mandate and unified the realm; as the source of dynastic merit, he should receive worship with the corrected east-facing honor. By Emperor Renzong's time, when he performed the xia in person, Taizu's east-facing seat was debated to display the legitimacy of the orthodox line. Ministers at court agreed that only the Mandate-receiving ancestor could take the east-facing seat; Taizu was our Mandate-receiving sovereign; yet with four shrines from Emperor Xi above him in the seven-temple order, the great xia arrayed only zhao and mu and left the east-facing seat empty—they dared not seat a non-Mandate ancestor there. At the opening of Xining, Emperor Xi should by generational order have moved to the distant shrine; ritual officials Han Wei and others petitioned on canonical grounds; Wang Anshi then held power, advanced his private theory, and had Zhang Heng propose honoring Emperor Xi as founding ancestor with the east-facing seat. Feng Jing reported that scholar-officials resented Taizu not facing east; Anshi spoke out sharply to refute them. He later also sought to end Taizu's suburban pairing; Shenzong, holding that Taizu founded the dynasty and received the Mandate, refused; Anshi never accepted the refusal. At the opening of Yuanyou, Emperor Yi had already moved to the distant shrine—fully in accord with ritual. By Chongning, Emperor Xuan should have moved to the distant shrine; Cai Jing then held power, followed Anshi's method, and proposed nine temples, making antiquity anew; the already removed Emperors Yi and Xuan were kept in place. Down to the present Taizu still occupies the fourth chamber; at great xia he stands among the zhao and mu. To restore Taizu's east-facing honor now would truly accord with the Ritual Canon."
20
太常寺丞王普又言：「弅所奏深得禮意，而其言尚有未盡。 臣竊以古者廟製異宮，則太祖居中，而群廟列其左右； 後世廟製同堂，則太祖居右，而諸室皆列其左。 古者祫享，朝踐於堂，則太祖南向，而昭穆位於東西； 饋食於室，則太祖東向，而昭穆位於南北。 後世祫享一於堂上，而用室中之位，故唯以東向為太祖之尊焉。 若夫群廟迭毀，而太祖不遷，則其禮尚矣。 臣故知太祖即廟之始祖，是為廟號，非諡號也。 惟我太宗嗣服之初，太祖廟號已定，雖更累朝，世次猶近，每於祫享，必虛東向之位，以其非太祖必不可居也。 迨至熙寧，又尊僖祖為廟之始祖，百世不遷，祫享東向，而太祖常居穆位，則名實舛矣。 倘以熙寧之禮為是，僖祖當稱太祖，而太祖當改廟號。 然則太祖之名不正，前日之失大矣。 今宜奉太祖神主居第一室，永為廟之始祖。 每歲五享、告朔、薦新，止於七廟。 三年一祫，則太祖正東向之位。 太宗、仁宗、神宗南向為昭，真宗、英宗、哲宗北向為穆。 五年一禘，則迎宣祖神主享於太廟，而以太祖配焉。 如是，則宗廟之事盡合《禮經》，無復前日之失矣。 」上曰：「太祖皇帝開基創業，始受天命，祫享宜居東向之位。 」宰相趙鼎等奏曰：「三昭三穆，與太祖之廟而七，載在《禮經》，無可疑者。」
Assistant Director Wang Pu of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices added: "Bian's memorial grasps ritual intent well, yet does not exhaust the matter. I hold that when temples were separate halls, Taizu stood at the center with the other shrines to left and right; when temples shared one hall, Taizu stood on the right and all chambers to his left. In antiquity at xia, the court audience rite was in the hall: Taizu faced south with zhao and mu east and west; feeding was in the chamber: Taizu faced east with zhao and mu north and south. Later ages held xia entirely in the hall but used chamber seating—hence only east-facing marks Taizu's honor. When subsidiary shrines are removed in turn while Taizu is never moved, that ritual principle still stands. I therefore know that Taizu is the temple's founding ancestor—a temple title, not a posthumous epithet. At the start of Taizong's reign Taizu's temple title was already fixed; though reigns passed, generations were still near; at each xia the east-facing seat was left empty—only Taizu could occupy it. By Xining, Emperor Xi was again honored as founding ancestor, never removed, facing east at xia while Taizu stood in the mu line—name and reality diverged. If Xining ritual were correct, Emperor Xi should be called Taizu and Taizu should change his temple title. Then Taizu's title would be wrong and the earlier error grave. Taizu's tablet should now occupy the first chamber as the temple's permanent founding ancestor. The five annual offerings, new-moon announcements, and new-harvest offerings should be limited to the seven temples. Every three years at xia, Taizu should take the corrected east-facing seat. Taizong, Renzong, and Shenzong south-facing as zhao; Zhenzong, Yingzong, and Zhezong north-facing as mu. Every five years at di, welcome Emperor Xuan's tablet to the imperial ancestral temple with Taizu as paired sovereign. Then ancestral affairs would fully accord with the Ritual Canon and past errors end. " The emperor said: "Emperor Taizu founded the dynasty and first received the Mandate; at xia he should occupy the east-facing seat. " Chief minister Zhao Ding and others replied: "Three zhao and three mu with Taizu's shrine make seven—recorded in the Ritual Canon, beyond doubt."
21
紹熙五年九月，太常少卿曾三復亦言：請祧宣祖，就正太祖東向之位，其言甚切。 既而吏部尚書鄭僑等亦乞因大行祔廟之際，定宗廟萬世之禮，慰太祖在天之靈，破熙寧不經之論。 今太祖為始祖，則太宗為昭，真宗為穆，自是而下以至孝宗，四昭四穆與太祖之廟而九。 上參古禮，而不廢崇寧九廟之制，於義為允。 又言：「治平四年，僖祖祧遷，藏在西夾室。 至熙寧五年，王安石以私意使章衡等議，乃復祔僖祖以為始祖，又將推以配天，欲罷太祖郊配。 韓維、司馬光等力爭，而安石主其說愈堅。 孫固慮其罷太祖配天，建議以僖祖權居東向之位。 既曰權居，則當厘正明矣。 」詔從之。
In the ninth month of Shaoxi 5, Vice Director Zeng Sanfu of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices likewise petitioned to remove Emperor Xuan to the distant shrine and restore Taizu's east-facing seat—his words were urgent. Then Minister of Personnel Zheng Qiao and others asked that when the late emperor was enshrined, the ancestral rites for all generations be settled, comforting Taizu's spirit and refuting Xining's uncanonical theory. With Taizu as founding ancestor, Taizong is zhao and Zhenzong mu; down to Xiaozong, four zhao and four mu with Taizu's shrine make nine. The emperor consulted ancient ritual without abolishing Chongning's nine-temple system—a reasonable compromise. They also noted that in Zhiping 4 Emperor Xi's tablet was moved to the distant shrine and stored in the west side chamber. By Xining 5, Wang Anshi from private intent had Zhang Heng and others deliberate, re-enshrining Emperor Xi as founding ancestor and proposing him for Heaven pairing while seeking to end Taizu's suburban pairing. Han Wei, Sima Guang, and others argued forcefully; Anshi held ever more firmly to his view. Sun Gu, fearing abolition of Taizu's Heaven pairing, proposed that Emperor Xi provisionally take the east-facing seat. Since it was provisional, it should now be clearly corrected. " The emperor ordered this followed.
22
閏十月，權禮部侍郎許及之言：「僖、順、翼、宣四祖，為太祖之祖考，所遷之主，恐不得藏於子孫之廟。 今順、翼二祖藏於西夾室，實居太廟太祖之右。 遇祫享，則於夾室之前，設位以昭穆焉。 」於是詔有司集議，吏部尚書兼侍讀鄭僑等言：「僖祖當用唐興聖之制，立為別廟，順祖、翼祖、宣祖之主皆祔藏焉。 如此，則僖祖自居別廟之尊，三祖不祔子孫之廟。 自漢、魏以來，太祖而上，毀廟之主皆不合食，今遇祫，則即廟而享，於禮尤稱。 」諸儒如樓鑰、陳傅良皆以為可，詔從之。
In the intercalary tenth month, Acting Vice Minister of Rites Xu Jiyan argued: "The four ancestors Xi, Shun, Yi, and Xuan were Taizu's forefathers; their distant-shrine tablets perhaps should not be stored in descendants' temple. Emperors Shun and Yi are now stored in the west side chamber—to the right of Taizu in the imperial ancestral temple. At xia, seats are set before the side chambers according to zhao and mu. " An edict ordered deliberation; Minister of Personnel and Palace Reader Zheng Qiao and others argued: "Emperor Xi should follow Tang's Xingsheng precedent as a separate shrine, with the tablets of Emperors Shun, Yi, and Xuan enshrined there. Thus Emperor Xi would hold separate-shrine honor and the three ancestors would not stand in descendants' temple. From Han and Wei onward, tablets above Taizu from removed shrines never shared the feast; at xia they would be worshipped at their own shrines—especially fitting. " Scholars such as Lou Yue and Chen Fuliang approved; the emperor ordered it followed.
23
時朱熹在講筵，獨入議狀，條其不可者四，大略云：「準尚書吏部牒，集議四祖祧主宜有所歸。 今詳群議雖多，而皆有可疑。 若曰藏之夾室，則是以祖宗之主下藏於子孫之夾室。 至於祫祭，設幄於夾室之前，則亦不得謂之祫。 欲別立一廟，則喪事即遠，有毀無立。 欲藏之天興殿，則宗廟、原廟不可相雜。 議者皆知其不安，特以其心欲尊奉太祖三年一袷時暫東向之故，其實無益於太祖之尊，而徒使僖祖、太祖兩朝威靈，相與校強弱於冥冥之中。 今但以太祖當日追尊帝號之令而默推之，則知今日太祖在天之靈，必有所不忍而不敢當矣。 又況僖祖祧主遷於治平，不過數年，神宗復奉以為始祖，已為得禮之正而合於人心，所謂『有其舉之，莫敢廢者』。 」又言：「當以僖祖為始祖，如周之後稷，太祖如周之文王，太宗如周之武王，與仁宗之廟，皆萬世不祧； 昭穆而次，以至高宗之廟亦萬世不祧。 」又言：「元祐大儒程頤以為王安石言『僖祖不當祧』，復立廟為得禮。 竊詳頤之議論與安石不同，至論此事則深服之，足以見義理人心之所同，固有不約而合者。 特以司馬光、韓維之徒皆是大賢，人所敬信，其議偶不出此，而安石乃以變亂穿鑿得罪於公議，故欲堅守二賢之說，並安石所當取者而盡廢之。 今以程頤之說考之，則是非可判矣。」
Zhu Xi, then at the lecture seat, alone submitted a deliberation listing four objections, in summary: "Per the Ministry of Personnel dispatch, deliberation is convened on where the four ancestors' distant-shrine tablets should go. Though many proposals have been offered, all are doubtful. To store them in side chambers is to place ancestors' tablets below in descendants' side chambers. Setting canopies before side chambers at xia cannot be called xia. To establish a separate shrine: mourning is long past—removal is allowed, establishment is not. To store them in the Hall of Heavenly Prosperity mixes ancestral and original shrines improperly. Deliberators know this is unsound, yet wish only to honor Taizu by having him face east temporarily at triennial xia—it does not truly honor Taizu but sets the numinous authority of Emperor Xi and Taizu contending in the unseen realm. Infer silently from Taizu's own day the edict posthumously honoring his title, and one knows Taizu's spirit in Heaven must find this unbearable and dare not accept it. Moreover, Emperor Xi's tablet was moved in Zhiping; within a few years Shenzong again honored him as founding ancestor—already correct ritual and popular assent: 'When one initiates it, none dare abolish it.' " He also argued: "Emperor Xi should be founding ancestor, like Zhou's Hou Ji; Taizu like King Wen; Taizong like King Wu; with Renzong's shrine—all never removed for ten thousand generations; zhao and mu in succession, down to Gaozong's shrine also never removed. " He also noted that the great scholar Cheng Yi of Yuanyou held Wang Anshi right that 'Emperor Xi should not be removed' and that re-establishing his shrine accorded with ritual. Yi's views differ from Anshi's on most matters, yet on this he deeply approved—showing that principle and popular sentiment can coincide without prior agreement. Only because Sima Guang, Han Wei, and their like are revered worthies whose views happened not to reach this conclusion, while Anshi offended public opinion by forced innovation—opponents wish to hold fast to the two worthies and reject even what Anshi got right. Examined by Cheng Yi's view, right and wrong are clear."
24
時宰臣趙汝愚既以安石之論為非，異議者懼其軋己，藉以求勝，事竟不行。 熹時以得罪，遺汝愚書曰：「相公以宗子入輔王室，而無故輕納妄議，拆祖宗之廟以快其私，欲望神靈降歆，垂休錫羨，以永國祚於無窮，其可得乎？ 」時太廟殿已為十二室，故孝宗升祔，而東室尚虛。 熹以為非所以祝延壽康之意，深不然之，因自劾不堪言語侍從之選，乞追奪待制，不許。 及光宗祔廟，遂復為九世十二室。 蓋自昌陵祔廟，逾二百年而後正太祖之位。 慶元二年四月，禮部太常寺言：「已於太廟之西，別建僖祖廟，及告遷僖、順、翼、宣帝後神主詣僖祖廟奉安。 所有今年孟冬祫享，先詣四祖廟室行禮，次詣太廟，逐幄次行禮。」
Chief minister Zhao Ruyu already rejected Anshi's theory; opponents feared being overruled and used the dispute to prevail—the matter was not carried out. Xi, then under censure, wrote Ruyu: "You entered as a clansman to assist the throne, yet without cause accepted reckless proposals and dismantled the ancestral temple for private ends—can you expect the spirits' favor and endless dynastic fortune?" " The ancestral hall already had twelve chambers; when Xiaozong was enshrined, the east chamber remained vacant. Xi held this contrary to prayers for the emperor's long life, deeply disapproved, and impeached himself as unfit for court discourse, begging revocation of his academician title—refused. When Guangzong was enshrined, the arrangement again became nine generations in twelve chambers. From the enshrinement of the mausoleum tablets in the temple, more than two centuries passed before Taizu's seat was finally corrected. In the fourth month of Qingyuan 2, the Ministry of Rites and Court of Imperial Sacrifices reported: "A separate shrine for Emperor Xi has been built west of the imperial ancestral temple, and the tablets of Emperors Xi, Shun, Yi, and Xuan and their consorts are to be moved there for installation. For this year's early-winter xia rite, ceremonies will first be held at the four ancestors' shrine chambers, then at the imperial ancestral temple, with rites at each canopy in turn."
25
理宗紹定四年九月丙戌，京師大火，延及太廟。 太常少卿度正言：「伏見近世大儒侍講朱熹詳考古禮，尚論宗廟之制，畫而為圖，其說甚備。 然其為製，務效於古而頗更本朝之制，故學士大夫皆有異論，遂不能行。 今天降災異，火發民家，延及宗廟，舉而行之，莫此時為宜。 臣於向來備聞其說，今備員禮寺，適當此變，若遂隱默，則為有負，謹為二說以獻。 其一，純用朱熹之說，謂本朝廟製未合於古，因畫為圖，謂僖祖如周後稷，當為本朝始祖。 夫尊僖祖以為始祖，是乃順太祖皇帝之孝心也。 始祖之廟居於中，左昭右穆各為一廟，門皆南向，位皆東向。 祧廟之主藏於始祖之廟夾室，昭常為昭，穆常為穆，自不相亂。 三年合食，則並出祧廟之主，合享於始祖之廟。 始祖東向，群昭之主皆位北而南向，群穆之主皆位南而北向。 昭穆既分，尊卑以定。 其說合於古而宜於今，盡美盡善。 舉而行之，祖宗在天之靈必歆享於此，而垂祐於無窮也。 其一說，則因本朝之制，而參以朱熹之說。 蓋本朝廟製，神宗嘗命禮官陸佃討論，欲復古制，未及施行。 渡江以來，稽古禮文之事，多所未暇。 今欲驟行更革，恐未足以成其事，而徒為紛紛。 或且仍遵本朝之制，自西徂東，並為一列。 惟於每室之後，量展一間，以藏祧廟之主。 每室之前，量展二間，遇三年袷享，則以帷幄幕之，通為一室，盡出諸廟主及祧廟主並為一列，合食其上。 前乎此廟為一室，凡遇袷享，合祭於其室，名為袷享，而實未嘗合。 今量展此三間，後有藏祧主之所，前有祖宗合食之地，於本朝之制，初無大段更革，而頗已得三年大袷之義。 今來朝廷若能舉行朱熹前議，固無以加； 如其不然，姑從後說，亦為允當，不失禮意。 然宗廟之禮，倘無其故，何敢妄議？ 今因大火之後，若加損益，亦惟其時，乞賜詳議。 」有旨，令侍從、禮部、太常集議，後竟不行。
On the bingxu day of the ninth month of Shaoding 4 under Emperor Lizong, a great fire broke out in the capital and spread to the imperial ancestral temple. Vice Director Du Zheng of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices memorialized: "The great scholar Lecturer Zhu Xi of recent times examined ancient ritual in detail, discussed ancestral temple regulation, drew diagrams, and his exposition is thorough. Yet his design strove to imitate antiquity and altered our dynasty's arrangements considerably; scholar-officials disagreed, and it was never implemented. Heaven has now sent a portent: fire spread from homes to the ancestral temple—to implement his plan, no moment is more fitting. I have long heard his views in full; now serving in the ritual office at this crisis, silence would be a failure of duty—I respectfully offer two proposals. The first is to adopt Zhu Xi's view entirely: our temple design does not accord with antiquity; he drew diagrams holding that Emperor Xi, like Zhou's Hou Ji, should be our founding ancestor. To honor Emperor Xi as founding ancestor follows Emperor Taizu's filial intent. The founding ancestor's shrine stands at the center; zhao left and mu right each have a separate shrine; all gates face south and all seats face east. Distant-shrine tablets are stored in the founding ancestor's side chambers; zhao remains zhao and mu mu, never confused. At the triennial joint feast, distant-shrine tablets are all brought out for joint worship at the founding ancestor's shrine. The founding ancestor faces east; all zhao tablets stand north facing south, all mu south facing north. With zhao and mu divided, rank and precedence are fixed. This view accords with antiquity and suits the present—altogether sound. Implemented, the ancestors' spirits in Heaven will surely delight in this and bestow endless blessing. The second proposal follows our dynasty's arrangements while incorporating Zhu Xi's views. Our temple design—Shenzong once ordered ritual official Lu Dian to deliberate on restoring antiquity, but it was never implemented. Since moving south across the Yangzi, examination of ancient ritual texts has largely been neglected. Sudden reform now may not succeed and will only create uproar. Perhaps for now still follow our dynasty's arrangement, from west to east in a single row. Only behind each chamber, extend one bay to store distant-shrine tablets. Before each chamber, extend two bays; at triennial xia, hang curtains to form one hall, bring out all shrine and distant-shrine tablets in one row, and hold the joint feast there. Previously the temple was one hall; at xia, sacrifice was called joint but was never truly combined. Extending these three bays provides storage for distant tablets behind and a true joint feast before—no great change to our arrangements, yet largely achieving the triennial great xia in spirit. If the court can implement Zhu Xi's earlier proposal, nothing could be better; if not, the latter proposal is also acceptable and preserves ritual intent. Yet on ancestral ritual, without such cause, who would dare propose changes? After the great fire, if changes are to be made, the moment is now—we beg thorough deliberation. " The emperor ordered attendants, the Ministry of Rites, and the Court of Imperial Sacrifices to deliberate; in the end nothing was implemented.
