1
韓令坤
Han Lingkun
2
韓令坤，磁州武安人。
Han Lingkun was a native of Wu'an in Cizhou.
3
父倫，少以勇敢隸成德軍兵籍，累遷徐州下邳鎮將兼守禦指揮使。 世宗以令坤貴，擢陳州行軍司馬，及令坤領陳州，徙倫許州。 罷職，復居宛丘，多以不法干郡政，私酤求市利，掊斂民財，公私患之。 項城民武郁詣闕訴其事，命殿中侍御史率汀按之。 倫詐報汀云被詔赴闕，汀奏之。 世宗怒，追劾具狀，法當棄市。 令坤泣請於世宗，遂免死流海島。 顯德六年，為左驍衛中郎將，遷左監門衛將軍。 宋初，拜磁州刺史，轉亳州團練使。 乾德四年，改本州防禦使，卒。
His father Lun had been known since youth for his bravery and served on the rolls of the Chengde Army, rising through repeated promotions to become commander of the Xiapi garrison in Xuzhou and concurrently head of the frontier defense command. After Lingkun rose to prominence, Emperor Shizong of Zhou promoted Lun to army aide of Chenzhou; when Lingkun was assigned to govern Chenzhou, Lun was transferred to Xuzhou. After leaving office he settled again in Wanqiu, where he repeatedly meddled in prefectural affairs by illegal means, ran a private distillery for profit, and squeezed wealth from the populace until officials and commoners alike were aggrieved. A resident of Xiangcheng named Wu Yu traveled to the capital to report these abuses, and the throne ordered Palace Attendant Censor Shuai Ting to conduct an inquiry. Lun deceived Ting by claiming he had been summoned to court by imperial edict, and Ting reported this to the throne. Emperor Shizong was enraged; investigators pursued the case to a complete finding of fact, and the statute called for public execution. Lingkun wept and begged the emperor for mercy, who then spared Lun's life and banished him to a distant island. In the sixth year of Xiande (959) he was made middle guard of the Left Valiant Cavalry Guards, then transferred to general of the Left Gate Guard. At the founding of the Song he was appointed prefect of Cizhou, then promoted to regimental commissioner of Bozhou. In the fourth year of Qiande (966) he was made defense commissioner of his native prefecture, where he died.
4
世宗命宰相李穀將兵征淮南，俾令坤等十二將以從。 穀退保正陽，為吳人所乘。 令坤與宣祖、李重進合兵擊之，大敗吳人。 世宗親征，聞揚州無備，遣令坤及宣祖、白延遇、趙晁等襲之。 令坤先令延遇以精騎數百遲明馳入，城中不之覺。 令坤繼至撫之，民皆按堵。 南唐東都副留守馮延魯為僧匿寺中，令坤求獲之，送行在，遂以令坤知州事。 由是泰州懼以城降。
Emperor Shizong ordered Chief Minister Li Gu to take command of an expedition against Huainan, with Lingkun and eleven other generals placed under him. Li Gu fell back to hold Zhengyang, where Wu troops pressed their advantage against him. Lingkun united with Zhao Kuangyin and Li Chongjin to attack them and routed the Wu forces decisively. When the emperor took the field in person and learned that Yangzhou lay unguarded, he sent Lingkun, Zhao Kuangyin, Bai Yanyu, Zhao Chao, and others to seize it by surprise. Lingkun first sent Yanyu ahead with several hundred elite cavalry to charge into the city at dawn before anyone inside knew they were coming. Lingkun followed to pacify the population, and the people remained calm and undisturbed in their homes. Feng Yanlu, the Southern Tang deputy military governor of the eastern capital, had disguised himself as a monk in a temple; Lingkun tracked him down and sent him to the emperor's camp, after which Lingkun was put in charge of the prefecture. Taizhou, shaken by this turn of events, then surrendered.
5
時錢俶受詔攻常、潤，圍毗陵，反為南唐所敗。 南唐乘勝遣將陸孟俊逼泰州，周師不能守，孟俊遂進軍蜀岡，逼揚州，令坤棄其城。 世宗怒，命太祖與張永德領兵趨六合援之。 令坤聞援至，復入城守，與孟俊兵戰，大敗之，擒孟俊，敗其將馬貴於楚州灣頭堰，擒漣州刺史秦進崇。 俄命向拱為緣江招討使，以令坤副之，下壽州。 歸朝，加檢校太尉、領鎮安軍節度使。 世宗乃復幸淮右，次楚州，遣令坤率兵先入揚州，命權知軍府事。 揚州城為吳人所毀，詔發丁壯別築新城，命令坤為修城都部署。
At that time Qian Chu of Wuyue, acting on imperial orders to attack Chang and Run prefectures, besieged Piling but was in turn defeated by Southern Tang. Southern Tang pressed its advantage by sending Lu Mengjun against Taizhou; the Zhou garrison could not hold, and Mengjun marched on to Shugang, threatening Yangzhou until Lingkun was forced to abandon the city. Emperor Shizong was furious and ordered Zhao Kuangyin and Zhang Yongde to hurry troops to Liuhe to relieve him. Hearing that reinforcements had arrived, Lingkun re-entered the city to hold it, engaged Mengjun's army, and won a crushing victory; he took Mengjun prisoner, routed Mengjun's general Ma Gui at the Wantou weir in Chuzhou, and captured Qin Jinchong, prefect of Lianzhou. Soon afterward Xiang Gong was made riverine pacification commissioner with Lingkun as his deputy, and they marched on Shouzhou. After returning to court he was promoted to honorary Grand Preceptor and appointed military governor of the Zhen'an Army. Emperor Shizong then returned in person to the Huai region, stopping at Chuzhou; he sent Lingkun ahead with troops into Yangzhou and appointed him acting military prefect. Because Wu forces had wrecked the old city of Yangzhou, the court ordered labor levies to erect a new town elsewhere and named Lingkun overall director of the construction.
6
宋初，移領天平軍，加侍衛馬步軍都指揮使、同平章事。 太祖親征李筠，詔令坤率兵屯河陽。 及澤、潞平，還京，錫宴令坤等於禮賢講武殿，賜襲衣、器幣、鞍勒馬有差，以功加兼侍中。 又從討李重進。 建隆二年，改成德軍節度，充北面緣邊兵馬都部署。 將赴鎮，上於別殿置酒餞之，因勖其為治。
At the founding of the Song he was transferred to command the Tianping Army and was also made commander-in-chief of the palace horse and foot guards with the rank of Associate Grand Councillor. When Emperor Taizu took the field in person against Li Yun, he ordered Lingkun to lead troops and encamp at Heyang. After Ze and Lu prefectures were pacified he returned to the capital, where the emperor feasted Lingkun and his fellows in the Hall for Honoring the Worthy and Reviewing Arms, bestowing court robes, ritual gifts, saddles, bridles, and horses in varying measure, and promoting him to Vice Grand Councillor for his achievements. He also took part in the campaign against Li Chongjin. In the second year of Jianlong (961) he was made military governor of the Chengde Army and overall commander of frontier forces on the northern border. Before he departed for his post, the emperor entertained him with wine in a side hall and urged him on the principles of good governance.
7
乾德六年，疽發背，卒，年四十六。 太祖素服發哀於講武殿，錄其子慶朝為閑廄使，慶雄為閑廄副使。 令坤有才略，識治道，與太祖同事周室，情好親密。 鎮常山凡七年，北邊以寧。 聞其卒，甚悼惜之。
In the sixth year of Qiande (968) a carbuncle on his back proved fatal; he was forty-six. Emperor Taizu appeared in plain mourning dress at the Hall for Reviewing Arms to lament him, appointing his son Qingchao commissioner of the imperial stables and Qingxiong deputy commissioner. Lingkun possessed ability and strategic insight, understood the principles of governance, and had served the Later Zhou together with Taizu; the two were on terms of close friendship. He held Changshan for seven years in all, and the northern frontier remained secure throughout. When he heard of Lingkun's death, the emperor grieved deeply.
8
初，南唐遣邊鎬破湖南，以馬希崇分司揚州，及令坤克取之，希崇以妓楊氏獻，令坤甚嬖之。 會擒陸孟俊，將械送行在所，楊氏於簾間窺見之，即拊膺慟哭。 令坤怪，問之，楊氏曰：「孟俊往年入潭州，殺我家二百口，惟妾為希崇所匿得免，願甘心焉。 」令坤以詰孟俊，孟俊具狀，令坤乃殺之。
Earlier, when Southern Tang had sent Bian Hao to conquer Hunan, Ma Xichong had been left nominally in charge at Yangzhou; after Lingkun took the city, Xichong presented the courtesan Lady Yang to him, and Lingkun became deeply infatuated with her. When Lu Mengjun was captured and was to be sent in chains to the imperial camp, Lady Yang caught sight of him from behind a curtain and immediately clutched her breast and burst into bitter weeping. Lingkun was astonished and asked her why. Lady Yang said, "Years ago Mengjun entered Tanzhou and slaughtered two hundred members of my family; only I was hidden away by Xichong and thus survived. I have long wished to see him pay with his life. " Lingkun confronted Mengjun with her accusation; Mengjun confessed the facts in full, and Lingkun then had him executed.
9
慕容延釗
Murong Yanzhao
10
慕容延釗，太原人。 父章，襄州馬步軍都校、領開州刺史。 延釗少以勇幹聞。 漢祖之興也，周祖為其佐命，以延釗隸帳下。 周廣順初，補西頭供奉官，歷尚食副使、鐵騎都虞候。
Murong Yanzhao was a native of Taiyuan. His father Zhang had been chief commander of horse and foot forces in Xiangzhou and concurrently prefect of Kaizhou. Yanzhao was known from youth for his courage and executive ability. When the Later Han founder rose to power, Guo Wei of Later Zhou served as his chief supporter, and Yanzhao was assigned to Wei's personal staff. At the start of the Guangshun era (951) he was made an attendant of the western bureau, later serving as deputy commissioner of the imperial pantry and colonel of the Iron Cavalry.
11
太祖即位，延釗方握重兵屯真定，帝遣使諭旨，許以便宜從事。 延釗與韓令坤率所部兵按治邊境，以鎮靜聞。 太祖嘉之，加殿前都點檢、同中書門下三品，避其父名故也。 李筠叛，初命與王全斌由東路會兵進討，俄為行營都部署、知潞州行府事； 及平，加兼侍中，詔還澶州。
When Taizu ascended the throne, Yanzhao was commanding substantial forces encamped at Zhending; the emperor sent envoys to convey his wishes and authorized him to act at his own discretion. Yanzhao and Han Lingkun led their troops to pacify the frontier, earning renown for keeping the borderlands quiet. Taizu praised their service and promoted Yanzhao to commander-in-chief of the palace guards with third-rank Secretariat status, using this title to avoid the taboo on his father's given name. When Li Yun rebelled, Yanzhao was first ordered to join Wang Quanbin on the eastern route for the punitive campaign; he was soon made overall commander of the field headquarters with acting authority over the Luzhou military prefecture. After the rebellion was crushed he was made Vice Grand Councillor as well and ordered back to Chanzhou.
12
建隆二年，長春節來朝，賜宅一區。 表解軍職，徙為山南東道節度、西南面兵馬都部署。 是冬大寒，遣中使賜貂裘、百子氈帳。 四年春，命師南征，以延釗為湖南道行營前軍都部署。 時延釗被病，詔令肩輿即戎事。 賊將汪端與眾數千擾朗州，延釗擒之，磔於市。 荊、湘既平，加檢校太尉。 是冬，卒，年五十一。
In the second year of Jianlong (961) he came to court for the Long Spring festival and received a residence as a gift. He petitioned to relinquish his military commission and was transferred to military governor of Shannan East Circuit and overall commander of frontier forces in the southwest. That winter was exceptionally severe, and the court sent palace envoys with gifts of sable coats and felt tents patterned with the hundred-children motif. In the spring of the fourth year (963) the court launched a southern campaign and appointed Yanzhao overall commander of the vanguard of the Hunan expeditionary headquarters. Yanzhao was ill at the time, but the emperor ordered him to take the field even if he had to be carried in a litter. The rebel leader Wang Duan was raiding Langzhou with several thousand men; Yanzhao captured him and had him torn apart in the marketplace. After Jing and Xiang were pacified he was promoted to honorary Grand Preceptor. He died that winter at the age of fifty-one.
13
初，延釗與太祖友善，顯德末，太祖任殿前都點檢，延釗為副，常兄事延釗； 及即位，每遣使勞問，猶以兄呼之。 洎寢疾，御封藥以賜，聞其卒，慟哭久之。 贈中書令，追封河南郡王，錄其子弟授官者四人。
Yanzhao and Taizu had been close friends from the outset; toward the end of Xiande, when Taizu was commander-in-chief of the palace guards with Yanzhao as his deputy, Taizu always treated Yanzhao as an elder brother. After Taizu took the throne he still sent envoys to inquire after him and continued to address him as elder brother. When Yanzhao fell gravely ill, the emperor sent medicine sealed with the imperial signet; on hearing of his death Taizu wept at length. He was posthumously made Secretariat Director, ennobled as Prince of Henan Commandery, and four of his sons and younger kinsmen were granted official posts.
14
子德業、德豐、德鈞。 德業至衛州刺史； 德鈞至尚食副使。 延釗弟延忠，歷內殿直、供奉西頭官都知，至磁州刺史； 延卿至虎捷軍都指揮使。 延卿子德琛。
His sons were Deye, Defeng, and Dejun. Deye rose to become prefect of Weizhou. Dejun rose to deputy commissioner of the imperial pantry. Yanzhao's younger brother Yanzhong served as inner palace attendant and director of the western bureau attendants before becoming prefect of Cizhou. Another brother, Yanqing, rose to commander-in-chief of the Tiger Swift Army. Yanqing's son was Dechen.
15
子德豐
Son: Defeng
16
德豐，字日新，幼聰悟，延釗愛之，嘗曰：「興吾門者必此子。 」八歲，補山南東道衙內指揮使。 延釗卒，授如京使。
Defeng, whose style name was Rixin, was clever and quick-witted as a boy; Yanzhao doted on him and once said, "This boy will be the one to bring honor to our house. " At the age of eight he was appointed commander of the inner guard of the Shannan East Circuit headquarters. After Yanzhao's death he was appointed envoy to the capital.
17
開寶中，從征太原，領禦砦南面巡檢。 又為揚州都監。 征南唐，為洞子都監。 城既下，命為昇州都監，市廛安靜，澤國富饒，使者多裒聚金帛，德豐獨以廉潔聞。 俄領蔚州刺史。
During the Kaibao era (968–976) he took part in the campaign against Taiyuan as inspector of the southern face of the imperial encampment. He later served as overall military supervisor of Yangzhou. During the campaign against Southern Tang he was placed in charge of the sapper corps. After the city fell he was made overall supervisor of Shengzhou; though the markets were orderly and the water country rich, and many officials amassed gold and silks, Defeng alone was known for his integrity. He was soon appointed prefect of Yuzhou.
18
太平興國二年，知慶州兼邠、寧都巡檢。 嘗破小遇族，奪名馬數十匹，詔書褒諭。 居任九年，以簡靜為治，邊鎮安之。
In the second year of Taiping Xingguo (977) he was made acting prefect of Qingzhou and concurrently frontier inspector of Bin and Ning circuits. He once defeated the Xiaoyu tribe and captured several dozen prized horses, for which the throne issued an edict of commendation. He held the post for nine years, governing with simplicity and restraint, and the frontier remained tranquil throughout.
19
雍熙四年，使登、萊閱強壯，及還，拜西上閣門使。 是冬，出為定遠軍鈐轄，命領後陣中隊，別將萬騎以禦邊害。
In the fourth year of Yongxi (987) he was sent to Deng and Lai to review able-bodied men for service; on his return he was made envoy of the western upper gate. That winter he was posted as military controller of the Dingyuan Army, charged with commanding the rear-array central corps and an additional ten thousand cavalry to guard against frontier raids.
20
淳化二年，進秩東上，知邢州。 三年，改判四方館事，出知延州。 時侯延廣知靈武，或言其得西夏情，倔強難制，命德豐代之，就賜白金三千兩。 會建使名，改為四方館使。 未幾，以所部不治，徙知慶州，俄又改靈州兼部署。 穀價湧貴，德豐出私廩賑饑民，全活者眾。 轉引進使。 賊入境，德豐率兵擊走，獲羊馬甚眾。
In the second year of Chunhua (991) he was promoted to envoy of the eastern upper gate and appointed acting prefect of Xingzhou. In the third year (992) he was put in charge of the Four Directions Hostel and sent out to govern Yanzhou. Hou Yanguang was then governing Lingwu; some reported that he had inside knowledge of Western Xia affairs yet was obstinate and difficult to control, so Defeng was ordered to replace him and was granted three thousand taels of white gold on the spot. When the formal title of hostel envoy was established, his post was retitled envoy of the Four Directions Hostel. Before long, because his command was poorly disciplined, he was transferred to Qingzhou; soon afterward he was reassigned to Lingzhou with concurrent frontier deployment authority. When grain prices soared, Defeng opened his private granaries to feed the hungry, saving a great many lives. He was then made reception commissioner. When raiders crossed the border, Defeng led troops to repulse them and seized a large number of sheep and horses.
21
咸平二年，遷客省使，知鎮州，召對便坐，撫慰甚至。 是冬，遼人南侵，德豐繕兵固守，餉饋不絕，詔獎之。 三年，改滄州。 德豐輕財好施，厚享將士。 在西邊時，母留京師，妻孥寓長安，貧甚，真宗憫之，特詔給團練使奉。 逾年，進潁州團練使，知貝、瀛二州。 五年，卒，年五十五。 家無餘財，談者善之。
In the second year of Xianping (999) he was promoted to commissioner of the guest bureau and made acting prefect of Zhenzhou; summoned to an informal audience, he received exceptional personal reassurance from the throne. That winter, when the Khitan invaded from the north, Defeng drilled his troops and held the line while keeping supplies flowing without interruption, for which the court issued a commendation. In the third year (1000) he was transferred to Cangzhou. Defeng was generous with his wealth and treated his officers and soldiers lavishly. While he served on the western frontier his mother remained in the capital and his wife and children lived in Chang'an in dire poverty; Emperor Zhenzong took pity on them and specially ordered that they receive a regimental commissioner's stipend. A year later he was promoted to regimental commissioner of Yingzhou with concurrent authority over Bei and Ying prefectures. In the fifth year he died at the age of fifty-five. He left no surplus wealth in his household, and commentators praised him for it.
22
子惟素，至殿內承制。
His son Weisu rose to become a palace drafter.
23
從子德琛
Nephew: Dechen
24
德琛以延釗蔭補供奉官，累遷內殿崇班、知夔州。 李順之亂，賊酋張餘領眾十萬餘、舟千艘來寇，與順戰龍山，斬首千餘級； 又與白繼贇擊賊，斬二萬餘，悉焚其舟。 賊剽開州，圍雲安，德琛往援之，又斬百餘級。 累詔褒諭。 歷西京作坊、左藏二副使。
Through Yanzhao's hereditary privilege Dechen entered service as an attendant and rose to honored officer of the inner palace and acting prefect of Kuizhou. During Li Shun's rebellion the bandit leader Zhang Yu led more than a hundred thousand men and a thousand boats against the government; Dechen fought Shun at Longshan and took more than a thousand heads. He then joined Bai Jiyun in attacking the rebels, killing more than twenty thousand and burning all their vessels. When rebels raided Kaizhou and besieged Yun'an, Dechen marched to their relief and took another hundred-odd heads. The throne repeatedly issued edicts praising his service. He later served as deputy commissioner of the western capital workshops and of the left treasury.
25
咸平二年，轉崇儀副使、荊湖北路鈐轄。 蠻擾澧、鼎境上，德琛戰於北氵義，奪耕牛、鎧甲，斬馘以歸。 徙峽路鈐轄，未至，復知夔州。 景德中，領梧州刺史，復任峽路，再遷莊宅使，又為並、代鈐轄，知憲州。 天禧初，改右監門衛大將軍。
In the second year of Xianping he was made deputy commissioner of honored ritual and military controller of Jinghu North Circuit. When tribal forces raided the borders of Li and Ding prefectures, Dechen fought them at Beiyi, capturing plow oxen and armor and returning with enemy heads. He was transferred to military controller of the Gorges route, but before he could take up the post he was again appointed acting prefect of Kuizhou. During the Jingde era he governed Wuzhou, returned to the Gorges command, was twice promoted to commissioner of crown estates, and later served as military controller of Bing and Dai circuits with acting authority over Xianzhou. At the start of the Tianxi era he was made great general of the Right Gate Guard.
26
符彥卿
Fu Yanqing
27
符彥卿，字冠侯，陳州宛丘人。 父存審，後唐宣武軍節度、蕃漢馬步軍都總管兼中書令。 彥卿年十三，能騎射。 事莊宗於太原，以謹愿稱，出入臥內，及長，以為親從指揮使。 入汴，遷散員指揮使。 郭從謙之亂，莊宗左右皆引去，惟彥卿力戰，射殺十數人，俄矢集乘輿，遂慟哭而去。 天成三年，以龍武都虞候、吉州刺史討王都於定州，大破契丹於嘉山。 明年克其城，授耀州團練使。 改慶州刺史。 奉詔築堡方渠北烏侖山口，以招党項。 清泰初，改易州，兼領北面騎軍，賜戎服、介胄、戰馬。 嘗射獵遂城鹽臺淀，一日射麞、彘、狼、狐、兔四十二，觀者神之。 晉天福初，授同州節度。 兄彥饒亦鎮滑臺。 俄而彥饒叛，彥卿上表待罪，乞田里，晉祖釋不問。 改左羽林統軍，俄兼領右羽林，改鎮鄜延。
Fu Yanqing, whose style name was Guanhou, was a native of Wanqiu in Chenzhou. His father Cunshen had been military governor of the Later Tang Xuanwu Army, overall commander of barbarian and Han forces, and concurrently Secretariat Director. Yanqing could ride and shoot by the age of thirteen. He served Emperor Zhuangzong at Taiyuan, where his cautious and dutiful character won trust enough to admit him to the imperial sleeping quarters; when he came of age he was made commander of the personal guard. After the court moved to Bian he was promoted to commander of the loose guard. During Guo Congqian's mutiny all of Zhuangzong's attendants fled, but Yanqing alone fought on, killing more than ten assailants with his bow; when arrows soon riddled the imperial carriage he wept and withdrew. In the third year of Tiancheng, as colonel of the Dragon Martial Guards and prefect of Jizhou, he campaigned against Wang Du at Dingzhou and routed the Khitan at Jiashan. The following year he took the city and was appointed regimental commissioner of Yaozhou. He was then made prefect of Qingzhou. On imperial orders he built a fort north of Fangqu at Wulun Pass to win over the Tangut tribes. At the start of Qingtai he was transferred to Yizhou with concurrent command of northern cavalry and was granted military dress, armor, and war horses. Once while hunting at Yantai Marsh near Suicheng he brought down forty-two deer, boar, wolves, foxes, and hares in a single day, astonishing all who watched. At the beginning of the Later Jin Tianfu era he was made military governor of Tongzhou. His elder brother Yanrao also held Huatai as his command. When Yanrao soon afterward rebelled, Yanqing submitted a memorial accepting blame and begging to retire to the countryside; the Jin founder pardoned him without further inquiry. He was made commander of the Left Feathered Forest Guards, soon took the Right Feathered Forest as well, and was transferred to govern Yan-Yan Circuit.
28
少帝幼與彥卿狎，即位，召還，出鎮河陽三城。 遼人南侵，詔彥卿率所部拒戰澶州。 契丹騎兵數萬圍高行周於鐵邱，諸將莫敢當其鋒，彥卿獨引數百騎擊之，遼人遁去，行周得免。 又副李守貞討平青州楊光遠，移鎮許州，封祁國公。
The young emperor had been close to Yanqing since childhood; on his accession he recalled him and then posted him to govern the three cities of Heyang. When the Khitan invaded from the north, he was ordered to lead his troops against them at Chanzhou. Tens of thousands of Khitan cavalry had Gao Xingzhou surrounded at Tieqiu, and none of the other generals dared meet their charge; Yanqing alone led several hundred horsemen into the attack, driving the Khitan off and saving Xingzhou. He next served under Li Shouzhen in suppressing Yang Guangyuan of Qingzhou, was transferred to Xuzhou, and was enfeoffed as Duke of Qi.
29
開運二年，與杜重威、李守貞經略北鄙。 契丹主率眾十餘萬圍晉師於陽城，軍中乏水，鑿井輒壞，爭絞泥吮之，人馬多渴死，時晉師居下風，將戰，弓弩莫施。 彥卿謂張彥澤、皇甫遇曰：「與其束手就擒，曷若死戰，然未必死。 」彥澤等然之。 遂潛兵尾其後，順風擊之，契丹大敗，其主乘橐駝以遁，獲其器甲、旗仗數萬以歸。 少帝嘉之，改武寧軍節度、同平章事。
In the second year of Kaiyun he joined Du Chongwei and Li Shouzhen in operations along the northern frontier. The Khitan ruler led more than a hundred thousand men to surround the Jin army at Yangcheng; water ran so short that every well they dug collapsed and soldiers fought over mud to suck moisture, until men and horses died of thirst in great numbers; the Jin force was downwind, and when battle was joined their bows could not reach the enemy. Yanqing told Zhang Yanze and Huangfu Yu, "Better to fight to the death than surrender with bound hands — and we may not die at all. " Yanze and the others agreed. They sent troops quietly to follow the Khitan rear and struck with the wind at their backs; the Khitan were routed, their ruler fled on a camel, and the Jin army returned with tens of thousands of captured weapons, armor, banners, and gear. The young emperor praised him and appointed him military governor of the Wuning Army with the rank of Associate Grand Councillor.
30
為左右所間，會再出師河朔，彥卿不預，易其行伍，配以羸師數千，戍荊州口。 及杜重威以大軍降於滹水，急詔彥卿與高行周領禁兵屯澶淵。 會彥澤引遼兵入汴，彥卿與行周遂歸遼。 遼主以陽城之敗詰彥卿，彥卿對曰：「臣事晉王，不敢愛死，今日之事，死生唯命。 」遼主笑而釋之。
Slandered at court, he was passed over when the army marched again into Hebei; his command was reorganized and he was left with several thousand weak troops to garrison Jingzhoukou. When Du Chongwei surrendered the main army at the Hutuo River, urgent orders went out for Yanqing and Gao Xingzhou to lead palace troops to Chanyuan. When Zhang Yanze led Khitan troops into Bian, Yanqing and Xingzhou submitted to the Liao. The Liao ruler questioned him about the defeat at Yangcheng; Yanqing replied, "I served the Prince of Jin and never shrank from death; as for today, my life is entirely in your hands. " The Liao ruler laughed and let him go.
31
會徐、宋寇盜蠭起，遼主即遣歸鎮。 行次甬橋，賊魁李仁恕擁眾數萬攻徐州。 彥卿領數十騎遽至城下，仁恕遣其徒執彥卿馬，請隨入城，俄頃，彥卿子昭序自城中遣軍校陳守習縋而出，大呼賊中曰：「相公當為國討賊，何故自入虎口，乃助賊攻城？ 我雖父子，今為讎敵，當死戰，城不可入！ 。 」賊惶愧，羅拜彥卿前，乞免罪，彥卿為設誓，乃解去。
When banditry flared in Xu and Song, the Liao ruler promptly sent him back to his post. At Yongqiao the bandit leader Li Renshu mustered tens of thousands of men to attack Xuzhou. Yanqing galloped to the walls with only a few dozen horsemen; Renshu's men seized his horse and urged him to enter the city with them; soon his son Zhaoxu sent the officer Chen Shouxi down on a rope from the battlements, who shouted to the rebels, "My father should be destroying bandits for the state — why throw himself into the tiger's maw and help rebels storm his own city? Father and son though we are, we are enemies now; I will fight you to the death — you shall not enter this city! . " The rebels, shamed and afraid, kowtowed before Yanqing and begged forgiveness; he bound them with an oath, and they withdrew.
32
漢祖入汴，彥卿自徐州來朝，改鎮兗州，加兼侍中。 乾祐中，加兼中書令，封魏國公，拜守太保，移鎮青州。 及殺楊邠輩，召促赴闕下。
When the Later Han founder entered Bian, Yanqing came from Xuzhou to court, was transferred to Yanzhou, and was made Vice Grand Councillor as well. During Qianyou he was made Secretariat Director as well, enfeoffed as Duke of Wei, appointed Defender Grand Preceptor, and transferred to Qingzhou. After Yang Bin and his associates were executed, he was urgently summoned to the capital.
33
世宗初，幷人擾潞州，潞兵敗，命彥卿領兵從磁州固鎮路壓其背。 及帝親征，命為行營一行都部署兼知太原行府事，領步騎二萬進討。
Early in Emperor Shizong's reign the forces of Bingzhou raided Luzhou and defeated its garrison; Yanqing was ordered to advance by the Guzhen road from Cizhou and strike their rear. When the emperor took the field in person, Yanqing was made overall commander of the first field headquarters with acting authority over the Taiyuan military prefecture, leading twenty thousand infantry and cavalry against the enemy.
34
初，彥卿之行也，世宗以幷人雖敗，朝廷饋運不繼，未議攻擊，且令觀兵城下，徐圖進取。 及周師入境，汾、晉吏民望風款接，皆以久罹虐政，願輸軍須以資兵力，世宗從之。 而連下數州，彥卿等皆以芻糧未備，欲旋軍。 世宗不之省，乃調山東近郡輓軍食濟之。 及世宗至城下，命與郭從義、向訓、白重贊、史彥超率萬騎屯忻口，以拒北援，又下盂縣。
When Yanqing first marched out, Shizong judged that although the Bingzhou forces had been beaten, court supply lines were still unreliable and ordered no immediate assault; Yanqing was told to observe the enemy below the walls and plan a gradual advance. Once Zhou troops crossed the border, officials and commoners throughout Fen and Jin submitted at the first approach; after years of harsh rule they offered supplies to sustain the army, and Shizong accepted. After several prefectures fell in succession, Yanqing and the other commanders argued that fodder was not yet ready and urged a withdrawal. Shizong would not hear of it and instead requisitioned grain from nearby commanderies east of the mountains to feed the army. When Shizong arrived below the walls he ordered Yanqing, Guo Congyi, Xiang Xun, Bai Chongzan, and Shi Yanchao to hold Xinkou with ten thousand cavalry against northern reinforcements, and Mengxian was also taken.
35
遼人駐忻北，遊騎及近郊，史彥超以二千騎當其鋒，左右馳擊，彥超死之； 敗遼眾二千餘，遼騎遁走。 先鋒為遼人所掩，重傷數百人，諸將論議矛盾，師故不振。 世宗乃班師，數賜彥卿繒綵、鞍勒馬，遣歸本鎮。 還京，拜彥卿太傅，改封魏王。 恭帝即位，加守太尉。
The Khitan encamped north of Xin and their scouts reached the suburbs; Shi Yanchao met their vanguard with two thousand horsemen and charged to either flank, but was killed in the fighting. They routed more than two thousand Khitan troops and drove their cavalry off. The vanguard was ambushed by the Khitan, several hundred men were badly wounded, and the generals could not agree on a course of action, so the army failed to regain momentum. Shizong then withdrew the army, repeatedly rewarded Yanqing with silks and saddled horses, and sent him back to his original post. Back in the capital he was made Grand Preceptor and ennobled as Prince of Wei. When Emperor Gong succeeded, he was made Defender Grand Marshal as well.
36
太祖即位，加守太師。 建隆四年春，來朝，賜襲衣、玉帶。 宴射於金鳳園，太祖七發皆中的，彥卿貢名馬稱賀。
When Taizu took the throne, he was made Defender Grand Mentor. In the spring of the fourth year of Jianlong he came to court and received court robes and a jade belt. At an archery feast in the Golden Phoenix Garden Taizu hit the mark with all seven shots, and Yanqing presented a famed horse in congratulation.
37
開寶二年六月，移鳳翔節度，被病肩輿赴鎮，至西京，上言疾亟，請就醫洛陽，從之。 假滿百日，猶請其奉，為御史所劾，下留司御史臺。 太祖以姻舊特免推鞫，止罷其節制。 八年六月，卒，年七十八。 喪事官給。
In the sixth month of the second year of Kaibao he was transferred to Fengxiang; ill, he traveled to his post in a litter; at the western capital he reported that his illness was grave and begged to seek treatment in Luoyang, which was granted. When his hundred-day leave expired he still claimed his stipend; censors impeached him and the case went to the retained censorate. Taizu, owing to their kinship by marriage and long friendship, specially spared him interrogation and merely stripped him of his military command. He died in the sixth month of the eighth year at the age of seventy-eight. Official funds covered his funeral.
38
彥卿將家子，勇略有謀，善用兵。 存審之第四子，軍中謂之『符第四』。 前後賞賜鉅萬，悉分給帳下，故士卒樂為效死。 遼人自陽城之敗，尤畏彥卿，或馬病不飲齕，必唾而咒曰：「此中豈有符王邪？ 」晉少主既陷契丹，德光之母問左右曰：「彥卿安在？ 」或對曰：「聞其已遣歸徐州矣。 」德光母曰：「留此人中原，何失策之甚！ 」其威名如此。
Yanqing was born into a military family, brave and resourceful, and skilled in the use of troops. As Cunshen's fourth son, the army knew him as "Fu the Fourth." Rewards to him over the years ran to vast sums, all of which he distributed among the men under his command, so his soldiers were glad to fight to the death for him. After the defeat at Yangcheng the Khitan especially feared Yanqing; if a horse fell sick and refused fodder, they would spit and curse, "Surely Prince Fu cannot be here? " After the young Jin emperor had fallen into Khitan hands, Empress Dowager Yelü, mother of Emperor Taizong, asked her attendants, "Where is Yanqing? " Someone answered, "I hear he has already been sent back to Xuzhou. " The empress dowager said, "To leave such a man in the Central Plains — what a blunder! " Such was the terror of his name.
39
鎮大名餘十年，政委牙校劉思遇。 思遇貪黠，怙勢斂貨財，公府之利多入其家，彥卿不之覺。 時藩鎮率遣親吏受民租，概量增溢，公取其餘羨，而魏郡尤甚。 太祖聞之，遣常參官主其事，由是斛量始平。 詔以羨餘粟賜彥卿，以愧其心。
He held Daming for more than ten years and left daily administration to his adjutant Liu Siyu. Siyu was greedy and cunning, used his power to amass wealth, and diverted most of the commandery's profits into his own household while Yanqing remained unaware. Military governors commonly sent trusted agents to collect rents, measuring grain generously and keeping the surplus for themselves, and Wei Commandery was among the worst offenders. When Taizu learned of this he sent a regular court official to take charge, and grain measures at last became fair. An edict granted Yanqing the surplus grain as a rebuke meant to stir his conscience.
40
彥卿酷好鷹犬，吏卒有過，求名鷹犬以獻，雖盛怒必貰之。 性不飲酒，頗謙恭下士，對賓客終日談笑，不及世務，不伐戰功。 居洛陽七八年，每春月，乘小駟從家僮一二遊僧寺名園，優遊自適。
Yanqing was passionately fond of falcons and hunting dogs; subordinates who erred could win pardon even from his fiercest rage by presenting prized birds or hounds. He did not drink, treated scholars with modest courtesy, spent whole days laughing and talking with guests without touching on public affairs, and never boasted of his battlefield achievements. During the seven or eight years he lived in Luoyang, each spring he would take a light carriage with one or two servants to wander among monasteries and famous gardens at his leisure.
41
周世宗宣懿皇后、太宗懿德皇后，皆彥卿女也。 自恭帝及太祖兩朝，賜詔書不名。 子昭信、昭愿、昭壽。
Empress Xuanyi of Emperor Shizong of Zhou and Empress Yide of Emperor Taizong of Song were both Yanqing's daughters. Throughout the reigns of Emperor Gong and Taizu, edicts addressed to him omitted his personal name. His sons were Zhaoxin, Zhaoyuan, and Zhaoshou.
42
昭信，天雄軍衙內都指揮使、領賀州刺史。 周顯德初，卒，贈檢校太保、閬州防禦使。
Zhaoxin was commander of the inner guard of the Tianxiong Army headquarters and concurrently prefect of Hezhou. He died early in the Xiande era and was posthumously made honorary Grand Guardian and defense commissioner of Langzhou.
43
子昭愿
Son: Zhaoyuan
44
昭愿，字致恭，謹厚謙約，頗讀書好士。 周廣順中，以蔭補天雄軍牙職，俄領興州刺史。
Zhaoyuan, whose style name was Zhigong, was cautious, sincere, and modest, devoted himself to study, and delighted in the company of scholars. During the Guangshun era he entered service through hereditary privilege as a staff officer of the Tianxiong Army and soon became prefect of Xingzhou.
45
開寶中，改領恩州。 彥卿養疾居洛，入補供奉官。 四年，改領羅州刺史。 七年，遷西京作坊副使。 俄授尚食使，出護陳、許、蔡、潁等州巡兵。 從征太原，為御營四面巡檢使。 及攻幽州，命與定國軍節度偓率兵萬餘，置砦城南。 師還，真拜蔡州刺史，知幷澶二州。 不逾月，復移幷門兼副部署。 丁內艱，起復，為本州團練使，連知永興軍、梓滑二州。
During the Kaibao era he was transferred to govern Enzhou. While Yanqing convalesced in Luoyang, Zhaoyuan went to the capital and was appointed a palace attendant. In the fourth year he was transferred to govern Luozhou. In the seventh year he was made deputy commissioner of the western capital workshops. He was soon made commissioner of the imperial pantry and sent out to oversee patrol troops in Chen, Xu, Cai, Ying, and neighboring prefectures. He took part in the campaign against Taiyuan as inspector of the imperial encampment on all four sides. During the assault on Youzhou he was ordered to join Wo, military governor of the Dingguo Army, in leading more than ten thousand men to build a stockade south of the city. After the army withdrew he was formally appointed prefect of Caizhou with concurrent authority over Bing and Chan prefectures. Within a month he was reassigned to Bing Gate with the additional post of deputy deployment commander. After his mother's death he was recalled from mourning to serve as regimental commissioner of his home prefecture and in succession governed Yongxing Army and Zi and Hua prefectures.
46
咸平初，又為天雄軍、邢州二鈐轄。 三年，以疾求歸京師，詔遣中使、尚醫馳傳診視。 既還，帝賜以名方御藥，拜本州防禦使。 四年，卒，年五十七。 車駕臨哭，贈鎮東軍節度。 子承煦，為左千牛衛將軍。
At the start of the Xianping era he again served as military controller of the Tianxiong Army and Xingzhou. In the third year he petitioned to return to the capital on account of illness, and the throne sent palace envoys and the imperial physician by express relay to examine him. Once he had returned, the emperor granted him renowned imperial prescriptions and made him defense commissioner of his home prefecture. He died in the fourth year at the age of fifty-seven. The emperor came in person to mourn him and posthumously made him military governor of the Zhendong Army. His son Chengxu became general of the Left Thousand-Ox Guard.
47
子昭壽
Son: Zhaoshou
48
昭壽，初補供奉官。 開寶七年，改西京作坊副使。 歷遷六宅副使、領蘭州刺史。 雍熙二年冬，命與劉知信護鎮州屯兵。 命遣將北征，又與知信為押隊都監，轉尚食使，真拜光州刺史。 端拱二年，知洪州。 淳化四年，改定州。 咸平初，遷鳳州團練使、益州鈐轄。
Zhaoshou first entered service as a palace attendant. In the seventh year of Kaibao he was made deputy commissioner of the western capital workshops. He rose through the posts of deputy commissioner of the Six Residences and prefect of Lanzhou. In the winter of the second year of Yongxi he was ordered to guard the encamped troops at Zhenzhou together with Liu Zhixin. When the court sent generals on the northern expedition, he again served with Zhixin as overall escort supervisor, was made commissioner of the imperial pantry, and was formally appointed prefect of Guangzhou. In the second year of Duangong he was made acting prefect of Hongzhou. In the fourth year of Chunhua he was transferred to Dingzhou. At the start of the Xianping era he was made regimental commissioner of Fengzhou and military controller of Yizhou.
49
昭壽以貴家子日事遊宴，簡倨自恣，常紗帽素氅衣，偃息後圃，不理戎務，有所裁決，即令家人傳道。 多集錦工就廨舍織纖麗綺帛，每有所須，取給於市，餘半歲方給其直，又令部曲私邀取之。 廣糴黍稻，未及成熟者亦取之，悉貯寺觀中，久之損敗，即勒道釋償之。 縱其下淩忽軍校。
As a scion of a great house Zhaoshou spent his days in feasting and diversion, aloof and self-indulgent; he commonly wore a gauze cap and plain cloak, lounged in his rear garden, neglected military affairs, and had family members relay whatever decisions he made. He gathered brocade weavers at his official residence to produce fine silks; whenever he needed goods he took them from the market and paid only after half a year, and also had his retainers seize supplies on the side. He bought up grain in large quantities, taking even crops before they ripened, and stored it all in temples and monasteries; when it eventually spoiled he forced monks and priests to pay compensation. He allowed his subordinates to bully and disrespect army officers.
50
劍南自李順平後，人心洶洶，知州牛冕緩馳無政，昭壽又不能御軍，人皆怨憤。 神衛卒趙延順等八人謀，欲害昭壽，未敢發。 三年正旦，中使自峨眉山還京，昭壽戒馭吏具鞍馬將送之，延順等悉解廄中馬韁，奔逸庭下，陽逐喧呼，登廳執昭壽殺之，並殺二僕，據甲仗庫，取兵器。 都監王澤聞之，急召本軍都虞候王均率兵擒捕。 延順左執昭壽首，右操劍，仿徨無所適，卒見均至，即與眾推均為帥，合驍猛、威武兵為亂。 牛冕洎轉運使張適奔漢州。 是秋，官兵討平之。 見《雷有終傳》。
After Li Shun's rebellion was crushed in the Sword South region popular unrest remained high; Acting Prefect Niu Mian was lax and ineffective, Zhaoshou could not control his troops, and resentment spread everywhere. Eight soldiers of the Divine Guard led by Zhao Yanshun plotted to kill Zhaoshou but did not yet dare strike. On New Year's Day of the third year a palace envoy was returning from Mount Emei; Zhaoshou ordered his staff to prepare horses to escort him. Yanshun and his men loosed every horse in the stable so they stampeded into the courtyard, then feigned pursuit with loud cries, rushed into the hall, seized Zhaoshou and killed him along with two servants, seized the armory, and took up weapons. Overall supervisor Wang Ze heard the commotion and urgently summoned the army colonel Wang Jun to lead troops against them. Yanshun held Zhaoshou's head in his left hand and a sword in his right, wandering in confusion; when Wang Jun arrived the mutineers at once proclaimed him their leader and rallied the Valiant Fierce and Martial Awesome regiments in open revolt. Niu Mian and transport commissioner Zhang Shi fled to Hanzhou. That autumn government forces suppressed the rebellion. See the "Biography of Lei Youzhong."
51
昭壽子承諒，娶齊王女嘉興縣主，至內殿承制。
Zhaoshou's son Chengliang married the Princess of Jiaxing, daughter of the Prince of Qi, and rose to become an inner-palace drafter.
52
論曰：五季之亂，內則權臣擅命，外則藩鎮握兵。 宋興，內外廓清，若天去其疾，或納節以備宿衛，或請老以奉朝請。 雖太祖善禦，諸臣知機，要亦否極而泰之象也。 彥卿一門二后，累朝襲寵，有謀善戰，聲振殊俗，與時進退，其名將之賢者歟？ 令坤、延釗素與太祖親善，平荊、湘則南服底定，鎮常山則北邊載寧，未嘗恃舊與功以啟嫌隙。 創業君臣有過人者，類如是夫。
The commentators observe: During the chaos of the Five Dynasties, powerful ministers seized authority within the court while military governors held the armies on the frontier. When the Song arose, the realm was cleared within and without as if Heaven had lifted a sickness; some surrendered their command seals to serve in the palace guards, others retired to attend regular court audiences. Though Taizu governed skillfully and his ministers knew when to yield, this was above all the turning from utter disorder to renewal. Yanqing's family produced two empresses and enjoyed favor through successive reigns; resourceful and formidable in battle, his fame reached foreign peoples, and he advanced and withdrew with the times — was he not among the greatest of renowned generals? Lingkun and Yanzhao had long been close to Taizu; one pacified Jing and Xiang and settled the south, the other held Changshan and kept the north secure, and neither ever traded on old friendship or merit to breed suspicion. Among the founding emperor and his ministers there were men who rose above the ordinary, and they were generally of this sort.
