3
入閣儀。 唐制：天子日御正衙以見群臣，必立仗。 朔望薦食陵寢，不能臨前殿，則御便殿，乃自正衙喚仗由宣政兩門而入，是謂東、西上閣門，群臣俟於正衙者因隨以入，故謂之入閣。 五代以來，正衙既廢，而入閣亦希闊不講，宋復行之。
The Entering-the-Palace-Hall Ceremony Under Tang practice, the emperor held daily audience in the main hall and always posted ceremonial guards. On new and full moon days, when the emperor made tomb offerings and could not attend the front hall, he held court in a side hall instead. Guards summoned from the main hall entered through the eastern and western upper gates of the Xuanzheng Palace, and officials waiting there followed them in—hence the name “entering the palace hall.” After the Five Dynasties the main audience hall fell out of use and the inner-hall ceremony was rarely performed; the Song revived it.

In the eleventh month of Chunhua 2, Taizong decreed an inner-hall audience at the Hall of Civil Virtue on the first day of the twelfth month and charged Yang Huizhi and Zhang Ji of the Historiography Institute to draft new protocol. The day before, the responsible offices set up the Hall of Civil Virtue ( called “Civilization” in early Song . At daybreak, civil and military officials formed ranks east and west in the courtyard, while other officials, military officers, and deputy expedition commanders lined up on the southern steps outside the screen before the main audience gate; next the vice censor-in-chief and censors of the three bureaus took position; the vice censor alone passed through the Golden Crow guard line, bowed once to each side, and returned to his place; next two investigating censors took up supervision of the hall, standing on the northern steps outside the screen, facing north; next Secretariat, Chancellery, Hanlin, Privy Council, and two-province officials formed separate lines; next the Bureau of Astronomy announced the hour; next the Palace Gate office reported that all formations were in place. The emperor, in boots and robe, rode the palanquin to the Hall of Everlasting Spring, where the privy councilor and others presented themselves; escorts then led him to the Hall of Civil Virtue. On the dais the edict recipient called for fans, and the curtains were raised. The emperor took the throne; fans were withdrawn and the ceremonial guard commissioner burned incense; next civil and military officials bowed; next the Bureau of Astronomy announced the hour with the cock call; Penal-law and awaiting-edict officials took the supervisory memorial post; Secretariat and Chancellery stood flanking the incense table; two-province, Censorate, Hanlin, War and Personnel vice ministers, Golden Crow general, and supervising censors filed out in turn to stand outside the gate ( academicians alone waited north of the gate for the chief councilor . The Secretariat and Chancellery advanced to the incense table and said, “Your servants have already reported Secretariat business in full.” When done, they withdrew, bowed toward the hall, and left. Next penal-law and awaiting-edict officials each presented business, the Palace Domestic commissioner replied, and they returned to formation. Next the impeachment officer and left and right recorders withdrew. Breach of decorum inside the hall was impeached according to regulation. If the impeachment officer erred, the attendance recorder corrected him; if the attendance recorder erred, the gate commissioner corrected him; if the gate commissioner erred, the Palace Domestic commissioner corrected him. Everyone leaving stamped his boots, hurried out, and bowed toward the hall. Next Secretariat, Chancellery, and academicians took position; the gate commissioner announced dismissal of the guard; officials bowed twice, received corridor refreshments, and bowed again. Next the gate commissioner reported no further business; civil and military officials withdrew; fans were called for, curtains lowered, and the emperor returned to the palace. For corridor refreshments, officials stood in the eastern and western corridors north of the Diligence-in-Government gates—civil east, military west, higher rank to the north; the vice censor reached his place, bowed once facing south, then sat to eat; at the censorate clerks’ turn the announcer called; they tucked their tablets and ate; when finished the announcer called again, and the meal ended. On the first day of the fifth month, he added 350 men to the yellow-banner guard, had civil and military officials follow Secretariat and Chancellery in crosswise attendance, placed Hanlin academicians after the vice grand councilors, and had them leave east and west with the military commissioners, bowing toward the hall. Zhenzong held it three times; after Jingde the ceremony fell out of use. Renzong accepted drafting edict officer Li Shu’s proposal to continue reading seasonal ordinances and ordered ritual officials to draft protocol, but critics said it violated canonical ritual and the plan was dropped.

熙寧三年，知制誥宋敏求等言：“奉詔重修定閣門儀制內文德殿殿入閣儀，按今文德殿，唐宣政殿也； 紫宸殿，唐紫宸殿也。 然祖宗視朝，皆嘗御文德入閣。 唐制，常設仗衛於宣政殿，或遇上坐紫宸，即喚仗入閣。 如此，則當御紫宸殿入閣，方合舊典。 ”翰林學士王珪等議：“按入閣者，乃唐舊日紫宸殿受常朝之儀也。 唐紫宸與今同，宣政殿即今文德殿。 唐制，天子坐朝，必立仗於正衙。 若止御紫宸，即喚正衙仗自宣政殿東西閣門入，故為入閣。 五代以來遂廢正衙立仗之制。 今閣門所載入閣儀者，止是唐常朝之儀，非盛禮也。 ”自是入閣之禮遂罷。
In Xining 3, drafting edict officer Song Minqiu and others said, “By edict we are revising the inner-hall protocol for the Hall of Civil Virtue in the palace gate regulations. The present Hall of Civil Virtue is the Tang Hall of Proclaiming Governance; the Hall of Purple Eminence is the Tang Hall of Purple Eminence. Yet our founding emperors had all held inner-hall audience at the Hall of Civil Virtue. Under Tang practice guards were normally posted at the Hall of Proclaiming Governance; when the emperor sat in the Hall of Purple Eminence, he summoned them into the inner hall. On that logic, inner-hall audience should be held at the Hall of Purple Eminence to match ancient practice. ” Hanlin academician Wang Gui and others argued, “Entering the palace hall was originally the Tang routine for regular audience at the Hall of Purple Eminence. The Tang Hall of Purple Eminence is our present hall; the Hall of Proclaiming Governance is our Hall of Civil Virtue. Under Tang practice, whenever the emperor held court, guards were posted in the main audience hall. If he sat only in the Hall of Purple Eminence, he summoned the main-hall guard through the eastern and western gates of the Hall of Proclaiming Governance—hence “entering the palace hall.” Since the Five Dynasties the main-hall guard posting had been abandoned. The inner-hall protocol in the gate regulations is only the Tang routine audience, not a major ceremony. ” Thereafter the inner-hall ceremony was discontinued.

On the first of the month, if not a holiday, five days earlier the gate office notified all departments to prepare; the day before, officials set up the Hall of Civil Virtue. That day the Golden Crow general in ordinary dress led his command’s guard, and the acting Palace Domestic director led the detailed guard; both entered the courtyard and faced off east and west; civil and military officials lined up east and west; army officers entered in groups and stood facing north; court announcers led two impeachment censors through the gate onto the ramp to stand below the hall, facing north; next civil and military ranks were ushered in to face each other east and west; army officers then formed their north-facing line in the courtyard. The emperor, in boots and robe, held court at the Hall of Hanging Bow; whips cracked; eunuchs, gate officers, and army controllers offered routine new- and full-moon attendance; next came the privy council, Palace Domestic, fiscal commissioners’ deputies, privy council academicians, Inner Reception commissioner and subordinates down to medical officers and awaiting-edict officials, the two attendance recorders, and the grand attendance. Bureau commissioners and below withdrew and formed ranks. When the palanquin reached the rear of the Hall of Civil Virtue, the gate office reported ranks complete; the emperor emerged; fans were called for on the dais; he mounted the couch; whips cracked; fans opened, curtains rose, the ceremonial guard commissioner burned incense, called officials to position, and they bowed four times in attendance; the hour-announcer called the time; west of the impeachment censors, a palace secretary called for the grand attendance. Censors passed behind the civil and military ranks to facing positions; then left and right Golden Crow generals combined south of the edict stone for grand attendance; the lead officer stepped out, bowed, and reported peace throughout the realm, bowed twice, and each returned to his east or west escort post. General-announcing secretaries stood north and south of the edict stone, facing each other. The secretary withdrew to the western steps, then bowed to the chief councilor, princes, and others, reported attendance in the names of the hundred officials and chief councilor, and led the chief councilor’s party across while army officers held position. The gate commissioner called grand attendance; the secretary led the chief councilor north of the ritual stone to kneel prostrate and offer the monthly congratulation: “The civil and military officials, Chief Councilor [full title] and your servant [name], on this auspicious day of mid-spring respectfully wish Your Majesty blessings without end as Heaven’s—your servants cannot contain our joy.” ” He returned to place and bowed five times. The gate commissioner ushered the Secretariat up the eastern steps; the privy councilor and grand councilors ascended the western steps to attend; one supervising secretary took the left-province post; the rotation-memorial official stood south of the supervising secretary ( (if the rotation-memorial post was vacant, the Censorate submitted a request slip in advance. Two civil officials followed the same rule and filed memorials at the gate first) ; the Personnel vice minister and penal-law officials stood south of the rotation-memorial official; the War vice minister south of the right-province line faced the Personnel vice minister east and west, tucked their tablets, stepped out, set the roster down, and placed their tablets on it ( (Personnel and War vice ministers served ex officio as directors of the eastern and western evaluation courts; penal-law officials served as directors of penal review and the Court of Judicial Review) ; imperial princes, commissioner-envoys, and those below filed out by formation; the rotation-memorial official was led south of the edict stone; the Palace Domestic commissioner on the dais received and proclaimed the imperial reply as prescribed; Next the Personnel and War vice ministers and penal-law officials exchanged paired bows and withdrew. Next the impeachment censor, if there was no impeachment to report, exchanged paired bows and withdrew ( (if there was impeachment, protocol was followed as usual) . The supervising secretary was led south of the edict stone to bow and report personally that there was no further business in the hall; was called to attend, bowed, and exited to the west; next came the attendance recorders, then attendant officials and below, who combined formations south of the edict stone and bowed; were called to attend and bow, then filed out by formation; Celestial Martial officers and others were called to wait outside the gate, then all withdrew. Fans were called for, curtains lowered, the emperor left the seat, and whips cracked; The palace secretary, on imperial order at the dais, dismissed the guard; four-color officers stamped their boots, hurried south of the edict stone, and announced dismissal of the guard by imperial command. The Golden Crow general and acting Palace Domestic director exchanged bows, then followed the guard out; princes, commissioner-envoys, military commissioners down to prefects, academicians, capital officials, and army officers lined up in the court hall to thank the emperor for tea and wine. The emperor returned to the Hall of Hanging Bow, where the Secretariat, privy council, and petitioners presented business; Formations for thanks and farewell audiences were not introduced. In the rear hall he took audience and issued orders as needed. If gracious edicts, proclamations, or imperial letters were issued that day, they waited until the emperor withdrew to the Hall of Hanging Bow, then brought the document box out. 詔從所定。 徽宗初建明堂，禮制局列上七議：
An edict adopted these provisions. When Huizong first built the Hall of Brightness, the Ritual Regulations Bureau submitted seven proposals:
12
一曰：古者朔必告廟，示不敢專。 請視朝聽朔必先奏告，以見繼述之意。
First: In antiquity the new moon was always reported at the ancestral temple, showing that the ruler did not act alone. We ask that court audience on the new moon be preceded by a memorial report to the temple, expressing continuity with past rulers.
13
二曰：古者天子負扆南向以朝諸侯，聽朔則各隨其方。 請自今御明堂正南向之位，布政則隨月而御堂，其閏月則居門焉。
Second: In antiquity the emperor, leaning on the screen facing south, received the feudal lords; at the new moon audience each stood according to his direction. We ask that hereafter he take the due-south seat at the Hall of Brightness, hold the monthly hall according to the month when promulgating policy, and occupy the gate position in intercalary months.
14
三曰：《禮記•月令》，天子居青陽、總章，每月異禮。 請稽《月令》十二堂之制，修定時令，使有司奉而行之。
Third: The Book of Rites’ “Monthly Ordinances” has the Son of Heaven dwell in Green Yang and Total Splendor, with different rites each month. We ask that the twelve-hall system in the Monthly Ordinances be studied, seasonal ordinances revised, and responsible offices ordered to enforce them.
15
四曰：《月令》以季秋之月為來歲受朔之日。 請以每歲十月於明堂受新曆，退而頒之郡國。
Fourth: The Monthly Ordinances designate late autumn as the day to receive the coming year’s new moon. We ask that each year in the tenth month at the Hall of Brightness he receive the new calendar and then promulgate it to the commanderies and states.
16
五曰：古者天子負扆，公、侯、伯、子、男、蠻夷戎狄四塞之國各以內外尊卑為位。 請自今元正、冬至及大朝會並御明堂，遼使依賓禮，蕃國各隨其方，立於四門之外。
Fifth: In antiquity the emperor leaned on the screen while dukes, marquises, earls, viscounts, barons, and frontier states of the four quarters took position by rank, inner and outer. We ask that New Year’s Day, the winter solstice, and major court assemblies hereafter be held at the Hall of Brightness, with Liao envoys under guest rites and tributary states standing outside the four gates by direction.
17
六曰：古者以明堂為布政之宮，自今若有御劄、手詔並請先於明堂宣示，然後榜之朝堂，頒之天下。
Sixth: In antiquity the Hall of Brightness was the seat of government; we ask that imperial letters and handwritten edicts hereafter be proclaimed there first, then posted in the court hall and issued throughout the realm.
18
七曰：赦書、德音，舊制宣於文德殿，自今非御樓肆赦，並於明堂宣讀。
Seventh: Amnesty documents and gracious edicts were formerly proclaimed at the Hall of Civil Virtue; we ask that hereafter, except when granting amnesty from the tower, all be read at the Hall of Brightness.

政和七年九月一日，詔頒朔、布政自十月為始。 是月一日，上御明堂平朔左個，頒天運、政治及八年戊戍歲運、曆數於天下。 自是每月朔御明堂布是月之政。 先是，群臣五上表請負扆聽朝，詔弗允，至是復再請，始從之。 十一月一日上御明堂，南面以朝百辟，退坐於平朔頒政。 其禮：百官常服立明堂下，乘輿自內殿出，負坐斧扆明堂。 大晟樂作，百官朝於堂下，大臣升階進呈所頒布時令，左右丞一員跪請付外施行，宰相承制可之，左右丞乃下授頒政官，頒政官受而讀之訖，出，閣門奏禮畢。 帝降坐，百官乃退。 自是以為常。 其歲運、曆數、天運、政治之辭，文多不載。 是後則各隨歲月星曆氣運推移沿改，而易其辭焉。
On the first day of the ninth month of Zhenghe 7, an edict ordered that promulgation of the new moon and government begin in the tenth month. On the first day of that month the emperor took the left bay of the Level New Moon hall at the Hall of Brightness and promulgated celestial cycles, government policy, and the wuxu eighth-year calendrical data throughout the realm. Thereafter on each new moon he held court at the Hall of Brightness to promulgate that month’s policy. Earlier the officials had five times memorialized asking him to lean on the screen and hold court; the throne had refused; now they asked again and he consented. On the first day of the eleventh month he held court at the Hall of Brightness facing south to receive the officials, then withdrew to Level New Moon to promulgate policy. The ritual: officials in ordinary dress stood below the hall; the imperial carriage emerged from the inner palace; leaning on the axe-screen, he sat in the Hall of Brightness. Great Splendor music played as officials attended below; senior ministers ascended to present the seasonal ordinances; one vice director knelt to request transmission for implementation; the chief councilor approved; the vice directors handed the text to the promulgation officer, who read it aloud; the gate office then reported the rite complete. The emperor left his seat and the officials withdrew. This became regular practice. The texts of the year-cycle, calendrical data, celestial cycles, and policy are mostly not recorded here. Thereafter the wording changed each year with the calendrical and celestial cycles.

When finished, ritual officers and secretaries led officials out in order; eunuchs led the crown prince upstairs, back to the side chamber; the curtain was lowered; attendants withdrew as usual.

少頃，禮直官、舍人引知樞密院官以下入，就位立定，內侍引皇太子降階，詣南向褥位，樞密以下參賀如上儀。 訖，退。 次引師、傅、保、賓客以下入，就位，參賀如上儀。 師、傅、保以下以次出。
Shortly afterward ritual officers led privy council officials in; eunuchs led the crown prince down to the south-facing mat; the privy council offered congratulations as above. When finished, they withdrew. Next tutors, mentors, protectors, and guest-friends entered, took position, and offered congratulations as above. Tutors, mentors, protectors, and those below withdrew in order.
27
內侍引皇太子升坐，禮直官引文武宮官入，就位，重行北向立，典儀曰“再拜”，在位官皆再拜。 左庶子少前，跪言：“具官某言：元正首祚 〈 （冬至云“天正長至”）〉 ，伏惟皇太子殿下，與時同休。 ”俯伏，興，復位。 典儀曰“再拜”，在位者皆再拜，分東西序立。 左庶子少前，跪言禮畢。 左右近侍降簾，皇太子降坐，宮官退，左右侍衛以次出。
Eunuchs led the crown prince to his seat; ritual officers led palace civil and military officials in to stand in double rows facing north; the master of ceremonies called for double bows and all bowed twice. The left aide stepped forward, knelt, and said, “Your servant [name] says: On the first fortune of the new year ( (on the winter solstice say “Heaven’s correct long reach”) , we respectfully wish Your Highness the Crown Prince peace in keeping with the season.” ” He prostrated himself, rose, and returned to position. The master of ceremonies called for double bows; all bowed twice and formed ranks east and west. The left aide stepped forward, knelt, and announced that the rite was complete. Close attendants lowered the curtain; the crown prince left his seat; palace officials withdrew; attendants filed out in order.
28
皇太子與百官相見。 至道元年，有司言：“百官見皇太子，自兩省五品、尚書省御史台四品、諸司三品以上皆答拜，餘悉受拜。 宮官自左右庶子以下，悉用參見之儀。 其宴會位在王公上。 ”
The Crown Prince Meets the Hundred Officials In Zhidao 1, officials reported, “When officials meet the crown prince, those of fifth rank and above in the two provinces, fourth rank and above in the Secretariat and Censorate, and third rank and above in other bureaus return his bow; all others receive his bow. Palace officials from the left and right aides down all used the audience-visit protocol. At banquets he sat above kings and dukes. ” (end of report).
29
與師、傅、保相見。 《政和新儀》：前一日，所司設師、傅、保以下次與宮門外道，西南向； 設軒架之樂於殿庭，近南，北向。 其日質明，諸衛率各勒所部屯門列仗，典謁設皇太子位於殿東階下西向，設師、傅、保位，於殿西階之西，三少位於傅、保之南稍卻，俱東向北上。 師、傅、保以下俱朝服至宮門，通事舍人引就次，左庶子請內嚴。 通事舍人引師、傅、保立於正殿門之西，三少在其南稍卻，俱東向北上。 左庶子言外備，諸侍奉之官各服其器服，俱詣閣奉迎。 皇太子朝服以出，左右侍衛如常儀，軒架作《翼安之樂》，至東階下西向立，樂止。 通事舍人引師、傅、保及三少入，就位，軒架作《正安之樂》，至位樂止。 皇太子再拜，師、傅、保以下答拜 〈 （若三少特見，則三少先拜）〉 。 通事舍人引師、傅、保以下出，軒架《正安之樂》作，出門，樂止。 左庶子前跪稱：“左庶子某言，禮畢。 ”皇太子入，左右侍衛及樂作如來儀。
Meeting the Tutor, Mentor, and Protector New Rites of Zhenghe: The day before, officials set positions for tutors, mentors, protectors, and those below outside the palace gate, facing southwest; and placed elevated-rack musicians in the courtyard near the south, facing north. At daybreak each guard commandant posted his command at the gate; the master of audiences placed the crown prince below the eastern steps facing west, tutors west of the western steps, and the three junior mentors south of them slightly back—all facing east with seniority to the north. Tutors, mentors, protectors, and those below arrived at the gate in court dress; the general-affairs secretary led them to position; the left aide requested inner readiness. The general-affairs secretary led tutors, mentors, and protectors to stand west of the main gate, with the three junior mentors south of them slightly back—all facing east with seniority to the north. The left aide announced the outer court was ready; attending officials donned their ritual regalia and went to the pavilion to receive the crown prince. The crown prince emerged in court dress with attendants as usual; musicians played “Assisting Peace”; he stood below the eastern steps facing west, and the music stopped. The general-affairs secretary led tutors, mentors, protectors, and the three junior mentors in; musicians played “Correct Peace” until they reached position. The crown prince bowed twice; tutors, mentors, protectors, and those below returned the bow ( (if the three junior mentors were received separately, they bowed first) . The general-affairs secretary led tutors, mentors, protectors, and those below out to “Correct Peace”; music stopped at the gate. The left aide advanced, knelt, and said, “Your servant [name], left aide, reports that the rite is complete. ” The crown prince entered; attendants and music followed the arrival protocol.
