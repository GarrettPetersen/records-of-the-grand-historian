1
吉禮一
Felicitous Rites (Part One).
2
太宗尚儒雅，勤於治政，修明典章，大抵曠廢舉矣。 真宗承重熙之後，契丹既通好，天下無事，於是封泰山，祀汾陰，天書、聖祖崇奉迭興，專置詳定所，命執政、翰林、禮官參領之。 尋改禮儀院，仍歲增修，纖微委曲，緣情稱宜，蓋一時彌文之制也。
Emperor Taizong favored scholarly refinement and threw himself into administration, clarifying institutions and ordinances and largely reviving practices that had fallen into disuse. Emperor Zhenzong came to the throne after the Chongxi era. With peace made with the Khitan and the empire untroubled, he mounted the feng rites on Mount Tai and sacrificed at Fenyin; cults to the Heavenly Writ and the Sacred Ancestor followed one upon another. A dedicated office for detailed review was set up, and chief ministers, Hanlin scholars, and ritual officers were charged to oversee it. It was soon retitled the Court of Ritual, revised annually with ever finer detail, shaped to circumstance as propriety required—the ornate ceremonial code of the times.
3
自《通禮》之後，其制度儀注傳於有司者，殆數百篇。 先是，天禧中，陳寬編次禮院所承新舊詔敕，不就。 天聖初，王皞始類成書，盡乾興，為《禮閣新編》，大率禮文，無著述體，而本末完具，有司便之。 景祐四年，賈昌朝撰《太常新禮》及《祀儀》，止于慶曆三年。 皇祐中，文彥博又撰《大享明堂記》二十卷。 至嘉祐中，歐陽修纂集散失，命官設局，主《通禮》而記其變，及《新禮》以類相從，為一百卷，賜名《太常因革禮》，異于舊者蓋十三四焉。
Following the Comprehensive Rites, the regulations and ritual protocols held by the responsible agencies ran to nearly three hundred separate works. Previously, during Tianxi, Chen Kuan had begun arranging old and new edicts held by the ritual office, but never finished. In early Tiansheng, Wang Hao first assembled a classified compilation through the Qianxing reign, called New Compilation of the Ritual Archive. It was mostly ritual text without the structure of a formal treatise, yet covered everything start to finish, which the agencies found practical. In Jingyou 4, Jia Changchao drafted the Court of Imperial Sacrifices New Rites and Sacrificial Protocols, carrying through Qingli 3. During Huangyou, Wen Yanbo likewise wrote the Record of the Grand Offering at the Bright Hall in twenty juan. In Jiayou, Ouyang Xiu collected and collated scattered material, had an office set up, used the Comprehensive Rites as the backbone while noting alterations, and grouped the New Rites by category into one hundred juan, entitled Rites of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices Through Reform—roughly thirteen or fourteen percent unlike the older version.
4
未幾，又命龍圖直學士宋敏求同御史台、閣門、禮院詳定《朝會儀注》，總四十六卷：曰《閣門儀》，曰《朝會禮文》，曰《儀注》，曰《徽號寶冊儀》。 《祭祀》總百九十一卷：曰《祀儀》，曰《南郊式》，曰《大禮式》，曰《郊廟奉祀禮文》，曰《明堂袷享令式》，曰《天興殿儀》，曰《四孟朝獻儀》，曰《景靈宮供奉敕令格式》，曰《儀禮敕令格式》。 《祈禳》總四十卷：曰《祀賽式》，曰《齋醮式》，曰《金籙儀》。 《蕃國》總七十一卷：曰《大遼令式》，曰《高麗入貢儀》，曰《女真排辦儀》，曰《諸蕃進貢令式》。 《喪葬》總百六十三卷：曰《葬式》，曰《宗室外臣葬敕令格式》，曰《孝贈式》。 其損益之制，視前多矣。
Soon after, Song Minqiu, Dragon Diagram Academician-in-Attendance, was joined with the Censorate, Gate Office, and ritual court to fix the Protocols for Court Assemblies—forty-six juan in all: Gate Office Protocols, Court Assembly Ritual Texts, Protocol Directions, and Protocols for Honorific Titles and Imperial Registers. Under Sacrifice came 191 juan: Sacrificial Protocols, Southern Suburb Forms, Great Rite Forms, Suburban and Temple Sacrificial Ritual Texts, Bright Hall Joint Offering Command Forms, Hall of Heavenly Prosperity Protocols, Four Seasonal Morning Offering Protocols, Hall of Numinous Splendor Supply Edicts and Formats, and Ritual Edicts and Formats. Prayer and Exorcism comprised forty juan: Sacrificial Competition Forms, Purification and Offering Forms, and Golden Register Protocols. Foreign States ran to seventy-one juan: Liao Command Forms, Koryŏ Tribute-Entry Protocols, Jurchen Arrangement Protocols, and Various Barbarian Tribute-Entry Command Forms. Mourning and Burial totaled 163 juan: Burial Forms, Imperial Clan and Outer Official Burial Edicts and Formats, and Filial Gift Forms. Provisions for what to add or cut were vastly more than in earlier compilations.
5
紹聖而後，累詔續編，起治平，訖政和，凡五十一年，為書三百卷，今皆不傳。 而大觀初，置議禮局於尚書省，命詳議、檢討官具禮制本末，議定請旨。 三年書成，為《吉禮》二百三十一卷、《祭服制度》十六卷，頒焉。 議禮局請分秩五禮，詔依《開寶通禮》之序。 政和元年，續修成四百七十七卷，且命仿是修定儀注。 三年，《五禮新儀》成，凡二百二十卷，增置禮直官，許士庶就問新儀，而詔開封尹王革編類通行者，刊本給天下，使悉知禮意，其不奉行者論罪。 宣和初，有言其煩擾者，遂罷之。
From Shaosheng onward, edicts repeatedly called for further compilation from Zhiping to Zhenghe—fifty-one years in all, yielding three hundred juan, all now lost. At the opening of Daguan, a Bureau for Discussion of Rites was placed in the Ministry of Revenue, with deliberation and collation officers to trace each rite from origin to end, settle it, and seek imperial confirmation. Three years later the work was finished: Auspicious Rites in 231 juan and Regulations for Sacrificial Garments in 16 juan, then issued. The bureau asked that the Five Rites be ranked and ordered; the throne directed that the sequence follow the Kaibao Comprehensive Rites. In Zhenghe 1 the continued edition reached 477 juan, with orders to use it as the model for revising ritual protocols. In year three the New Protocols of the Five Rites appeared—220 juan—with new ritual duty officers and leave for scholars and commoners to inquire about them. Wang Ge, Director of Kaifeng, was told to compile what could be put into practice, print copies for the empire, and make ritual meaning known; failure to comply was criminal. Early in Xuanhe, critics called it unwieldy, and the project was dropped.
6
初，議禮局之置也，詔求天下古器，更制尊、爵、鼎、彝之屬。 其後，又置禮制局於編類御筆所。 於是郊廟禋祀之器，多更其舊。 既有詔討論冠服，遂廢靴用履，其他無所改議，而禮制局亦罷。
When the bureau was first set up, the court solicited ancient vessels nationwide and had new sets of zun, jue, ding, and yi made. Later a Bureau for Ritual Systems was added to the office that compiled the emperor's draft writings. Many implements used in suburban and temple offerings were then altered from their former types. An edict then called for review of caps and dress; boots gave way to shoes, but little else was changed before that bureau too was shut down.
7
大抵累朝典禮，講議最詳。 祀禮修于元豐，而成於元祐，至崇寧復有所增損。 其存於有司者，惟《元豐郊廟禮文》及《政和五禮新儀》而已。 乃若圜丘之罷合祭天地； 明堂專以英宗配帝，悉罷從祀群神； 大蠟分四郊； 壽星改祀老人； 禧祖已祧而復，遂為始祖； 即景靈宮建諸神禦殿，以四孟薦享； 虛禘祭； 去牙盤食； 卻尊號； 罷入閣儀並常朝及正衙橫行。 此熙甯、元豐變禮之最大者也。
On the whole, successive courts debated canonical ceremony with unusual thoroughness. Sacrificial rites were reworked under Yuanfeng, finished under Yuanyou, and revised again in Chongning. What the agencies still held were the Yuanfeng Suburban and Temple Ritual Texts and the Zhenghe New Protocols of the Five Rites alone. Such as ending the combined sacrifice of Heaven and Earth at the Round Mound; at the Bright Hall pairing only Emperor Yingzong with the Thearch and ending all collateral spirit offerings; splitting the great la sacrifice among the four suburbs; replacing the Longevity Star rite with worship of the Old Man; restoring Ancestor Xi, who had been displaced, as founding ancestor; building spirit halls at the Hall of Numinous Splendor for seasonal offerings; hollowing out the di and xia sacrifices; doing away with tray-and-stand food; declining honorific titles; and canceling entry into the privy chamber, regular audiences, and cross-processions at the main court— these were the largest ritual reforms of the Xining and Yuanfeng periods.
8
元祐冊後，政和冠皇子，元符創景靈西宮，崇甯親祀方澤、作明堂、立九廟、鑄九鼎、祀熒惑，大觀受八寶、大祀皆前期十日而戒。 凡此蓋治平以前所未嘗行者。
Later came enfeoffing the empress in Yuanyou, crowning an imperial prince in Zhenghe, the western Hall of Numinous Splendor in Yuanfu, Chongning's personal sacrifices at the Square Mound, Bright Hall rites, Nine Temples, Nine Cauldrons, and rites to the Sparkling Deluder; Daguan's reception of the Eight Treasures; and ten-day warnings before every great sacrifice. None of this had been done before Zhiping.
9
欽宗即位，嘗詔春秋釋奠改從《元豐儀》，罷《新儀》不用而未暇也。 靖康之厄，蕩析無餘。
On accession, Emperor Qinzong ordered spring and autumn shidian to follow Yuanfeng forms and the New Protocols set aside, but the court never had time to implement it. The Jingkang disaster left nothing standing.
10
南渡中興，銳意修復，高宗嘗謂輔臣曰：「晉武平吳之後，上下不知有禮，旋致禍亂。 周禮不秉，其何能國？ 」孝宗繼志，典章文物，有可稱述。 治平日久，經學大明，諸儒如王普、董{分廾}等多以禮名家。 當時嘗續編《太常因革禮》矣，淳熙復有編輯之旨。 其後朱熹講明詳備，嘗欲取《儀禮》、《周官》、《二戴記》為本，編次朝廷公卿大夫士民之禮，盡取漢、晉而下及唐諸儒之說，考訂辨正，以為當代之典，未及成書而沒。
After crossing south and rebuilding, the court worked hard to restore rites. Emperor Gaozong told his ministers: "When Jin Wu pacified Wu, court and country forgot ceremony, and ruin followed quickly. Without the rites of Zhou, how can a state endure? Emperor Xiaozong carried on that aim; in statutes and ritual objects there was much worth noting. In a long age of peace, classical studies revived, and scholars like Wang Pu and Dong [fen gong] among others made names in ritual. The court then continued the Rites Through Reform; Chunxi brought another editorial mandate. Zhu Xi later lectured with great completeness and planned to base a code on the Ceremonies, Offices of Zhou, and the two Dai ritual records, ordering rites for court, nobles, officials, and people, drawing on Han through Tang commentators to correct and fix practice for the present—he died before finishing.
11
理宗四十年間，屢有意乎禮文之事，雖曰崇尚理學，所謂「禮雲禮雲，玉帛雲乎哉」，蓋可三歎。 鹹淳以降，無足言者。
Across Emperor Lizong's forty years the throne often turned to ritual texts; though Neo-Confucian learning was prized, one may thrice sigh at the question whether rites are only jade and silk. After Xianchun there is little to record.
12
今因前史之舊，芟其繁亂，匯為五禮，以備一代之制，使後之觀者有足征焉。
Here, following earlier histories, we prune the tangled and assemble the Five Rites so the institutions of the age remain on record for later readers.
13
五禮之序，以吉禮為首，主邦國神祇祭祀之事。 凡祀典皆領于太常。 歲之大祀三十：正月上辛祈谷，孟夏雩祀，季秋大享明堂，冬至圜丘祭昊天上帝，正月上辛又祀感生帝，四立及土王日祀五方帝，春分朝日，秋分夕月，東西太一，臘日大蠟祭百神，夏至祭皇地祇，孟冬祭神州地祇，四孟、季冬薦享太廟、後廟，春秋二仲及臘日祭太社、太稷，二仲九宮貴神。 中祀九：仲春祭五龍，立春後醜日祀風師、亥日享先農，季春巳日享先蠶，立夏後申日祀雨師，春秋二仲上丁釋奠文宣王、上戊釋奠武成王。 小祀九：仲春祀馬祖，仲夏享先牧，仲秋祭馬社，仲冬祭馬步，季夏土王日祀中霤，立秋後辰日祀靈星，秋分享壽星，立冬後亥日祠司中、司命、司人、司祿，孟冬祭司寒。
Among the Five Rites, felicitous rites stand first, governing sacrifice to state and land spirits. Every entry in the sacrificial canon falls under the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. Thirty great sacrifices fill the year: first-month shang day prayer for grain; midsummer rain rite; autumn's last month and winter solstice offerings to Heaven at the Round Mound; first-month shang day to the Life-Giving Spirit; four establishments and earth-king days to the Five Thearchs; equinox audience with sun and moon; eastern and western Grand Unity; la day great la to the hundred spirits; summer solstice to Earth; early winter to the Spirit of the Divine State's land; four seasonal months and winter's last month at Grand and Rear Temples; spring and autumn mid-months and la day to Grand Earth and Grain altars; mid-season rites to the nine-palace noble spirits. Nine medium sacrifices: mid-spring to the Five Dragons; post-spring-establishment chou day to the Wind Master and hai day to the Former Farmer; last spring si day to the Former Silkworm; post-summer-establishment shen day to the Rain Master; spring and autumn upper ding shidian to the Cultural Thearch and upper wu to the Martial Completion King. Nine minor sacrifices: mid-spring Horse Ancestor; midsummer Former Herdsman; mid-autumn Horse Altar; midwinter Horse Pace; last summer earth-king day Central Drain; post-autumn chen day Numinous Star; autumn Longevity Star; post-winter hai day to the four Directors; early winter Spirit of Cold.
14
其諸州奉祀，則五郊迎氣日祭嶽、鎮、海、瀆，春秋二仲享先代帝王及週六廟，並如中祀。 州縣祭社稷，奠文宣王，祀風雨，並如小祀。 凡有大赦，則令諸州祭嶽、瀆、名山、大川在境內者，及歷代帝王、忠臣、烈士載祀典者，仍禁近祠廟鹹加祭。 有不克定時日者，太卜署預擇一季祠祭之日，謂之「畫日」。 凡壇壝、牲器、玉帛、饌具、齋戒之制，皆具《通禮》。 後復有高禖、大小酺神之屬，增大祀為四十二焉。
Prefectural sacrifices follow the same ranks: five-suburb qi-reception days to mountains, seas, and streams; spring and autumn mid-months to former dynasties' rulers and Zhou's six temples—as medium rites. Counties sacrifice to local soil-and-grain altars, shidian to the Cultural Thearch, and wind and rain—as minor rites. On great amnesties, prefectures must sacrifice to local mountains, rivers, and canonized rulers and martyrs, but may not pile extra rites on nearby shrines. If no fixed date is possible, the Directorate of Divination chooses a seasonal sacrifice day in advance—the "painted day." Altars, victims, vessels, jade, offerings, and purification rules all appear in the Comprehensive Rites. Later high qi and great and small exorcism spirits were added, raising great sacrifices to forty-two.
15
其後，神宗詔改定大祀：太一，東以春，西以秋，中以夏冬； 增大蠟為四，東西蠟主日配月； 太廟月祭朔。 而中祀：四望，南北蠟。 小祀：以四立祭司命、戶、灶、中霤、門、厲、行，以藏冰、出冰祭司寒，及月薦新太廟。 歲通舊祀凡九十二，惟五享後廟焉。 政和中，定《五禮新儀》，以熒惑、陽德觀、帝鼐、坊州朝獻聖祖、應天府祀大火為大祀； 雷神、歷代帝王、寶鼎、牡鼎、蒼鼎、岡鼎、彤鼎、阜鼎、皛鼎、魁鼎、會應廟、慶厲軍祭後土為中祀； 山林川澤之屬，州縣祭社稷、祀風伯雨師雷神為小祀。 餘悉如故。
Emperor Shenzong then ordered great sacrifices revised: Grand Unity east in spring, west in autumn, center in summer and winter; great la expanded to four, eastern and western la pairing sun with moon; Grand Temple offering on each month's first day. Medium sacrifices added the Four Outlooks and northern and southern la. Minor sacrifices: at four establishments to fate, household, stove, central drain, gate, pestilence, and travel spirits; at ice storage and removal to Cold; monthly new grain at the Grand Temple. Annual rites under the old count totaled ninety-two, apart from five Rear Temple offerings. Zhenghe's New Protocols made the Sparkling Deluder, Yang Virtue Hall, Emperor Nai, Fengzhou Sacred Ancestor morning offering, and Yingtianfu Great Fire rites great sacrifices; Thunder Spirit, successive rulers, precious cauldrons and the nine tripods, Responsive Assembly Temple, and Qingli army earth rite as medium sacrifices; mountains, rivers, and marshes, plus local soil-and-grain and wind-rain-thunder rites, as minor sacrifices. Everything else stayed as before.
16
建炎四年十一月，權工部尚書韓肖胄言：「祖宗以來，每歲大、中、小祀百有餘所，罔敢廢闕。 自車駕巡幸，惟存宗廟之祭，至天地諸神之祀，則廢而不舉。 今國步尚艱，天未悔禍，正宜齋明恭肅，通於神明，而忽大事、棄重禮，恐非所以消弭天災，導迎景貺。 雖小祀未可遍舉，如天地、五帝、日月星辰、社稷，欲詔有司以時舉行。 所有器服並牲牢禮料，恐國用未充，難如舊制，乞下太常寺相度裁定，省繁就簡，庶幾神不乏祀，仰副陛下昭事懷柔、為民求福之意。 」尋命禮部太常裁定：每歲以立春上辛祀谷，孟夏雩祀，季秋及冬至日四祀天，夏至日一祀地，立春上辛日祀感生帝，立冬後祀神州地祇，春秋二社及臘前一日祭太社、太稷。 免牲、玉，權用酒酺，仍依方色奠幣。 以輔臣為初獻，禮官為亞、終獻。
In Jianyan 4.11, Acting Works Minister Han Xiaozhou memorialized: "Since the founders, the court has observed well over a hundred great, medium, and minor sacrifices yearly without fail. Since the flight south, only ancestral temple rites continue; Heaven, Earth, and the spirit hosts go unhonored. The realm is still in peril and Heaven has not turned from calamity—precisely when we should be pure and solemn before the spirits, yet we neglect the greatest duties and heaviest rites; that may not be how to quiet disaster and draw blessing. Not every minor rite can be restored, but for Heaven and Earth, the Five Thearchs, sun, moon, stars, and the soil-and-grain altars, let the agencies hold them on schedule. Vessels, dress, victims, and supplies may exceed present means; let the Court of Imperial Sacrifices simplify where the old scale is impossible, so spirits are not left hungry and Your Majesty's intent—to serve Heaven clearly and seek the people's good—may still be met. The Ministry of Rites and Court of Imperial Sacrifices then fixed the calendar: spring's first shang day for grain; midsummer rain; autumn's last month and winter solstice for Heaven (four times); summer solstice for Earth; spring's first shang for Life-Giving Spirit; after winter establishment for the Divine State's land spirit; spring and autumn she days and the eve of la for Grand Earth and Grain. Victims and jade were set aside; wine libations stood in temporarily, with silks still by directional color. Chief ministers made the primary offering; ritual officers the secondary and final.
17
紹興三年，復大火祀，配以閼伯，以辰、戌出納之月祀之。 二十七年，禮部太常寺言：「每歲大祀三十六，除天地、宗廟、社稷、感生帝、九宮貴神、高禖、文宣王等已行外，其餘並乞寓祠齋宮。 」自紹興以來，大祀所行二十有三而已，至是乃悉復之。
Shaoxing 3 restored the Great Fire rite with Quebo as associate, in the chen and xu months of its rising and setting. In year 27 they reported: "Thirty-six great sacrifices yearly remain; aside from those already held—to Heaven and Earth, temples, soil and grain, Life-Giving Spirit, nine-palace spirits, high qi, Cultural Thearch, and the like—let the rest be performed as lodged rites at the purification palace. Since Shaoxing only twenty-three great rites had been held; now the full set returned.
18
舊制，郊廟祝文稱嗣皇帝，諸祭稱皇帝。 著作局准《開元禮》全稱帝號。 真宗以兼秘書監李至請，改從舊制。 又諸祭祝辭皆臨事撰進，多違典禮，乃命至增撰舊辭八十四首，為《正辭錄》三卷。 既復命知制誥李宗諤、楊億、直史館陳彭年詳定之，以為永式。 祝版當進署者，並命秘閣吏書，上親署訖，禦寶封給之。 凡先代帝王，祝文止稱廟號。 凡親行大祀，則皇子弟為亞獻、終獻。
Formerly suburban and temple prayers named the heir emperor; other sacrifices said emperor. The Composition Office, following the Kaiyuan Rites, used the full imperial title. Emperor Zhenzong, on Li Zhi's request as concurrent Secretariat Director, reverted to the older form. Prayers for every sacrifice had been drafted on the spot, often breaking precedent; Li Zhi was told to add eighty-four standard prayers in three juan, the Correct Prayer Record. Drafting controllers Li Zong'e and Yang Yi and Academician Chen Pengnian were then assigned to fix them as permanent forms. Prayer boards needing signature were copied by Secretariat clerks; the emperor signed them, sealed them, and issued them. Prayers to former dynasties' rulers used temple names only. When the emperor led a great sacrifice in person, imperial clansmen were secondary and final offerers.
19
五代以來，宰相為大禮使，太常卿為禮儀使，御史中丞為儀仗使，兵部尚書為鹵簿使，京府尹為橋道頓遞使。 至是，大禮使或用親王，禮儀使專命翰林學士，儀仗、鹵簿使亦或以他官。 太平興國九年，始鑄五使印。 太宗將封泰山，以儀仗使兼判橋道頓遞事。 大中祥符後，凡有大禮，以中書、樞密分為五使，仍特鑄印。
Since the Five Dynasties, the chief minister headed the Grand Ritual Commission, the Court of Imperial Sacrifices director the Ritual Protocol Commission, the vice censor-in-chief the Protocol of Honor Commission, the war minister the Halberd Array Commission, and the capital intendant the Bridge and Relay Commission. By then the grand ritual role might fall to a prince; ritual protocol went to Hanlin scholars; honor and halberd posts might go to others. Taiping Xingguo 9 first cast seals for the five commissioners. As Emperor Taizong prepared the Mount Tai feng, the Protocol of Honor Commissioner also judged bridge and relay affairs. After Dazhong Xiangfu, every great rite split the Secretariat and Military Affairs into five commissions, each with its own seal.
20
景祐二年，詔有司：「皇地祇、神州，舊常參官攝事，非所以尊神，自今命兩省。 歲九大祠，宰臣攝事者，參知政事、尚書丞郎、學士奉祠。 」於是參知政事盛度，享太廟已受誓戒，除知樞密院，乃不奉祠。 又故事，三歲一親郊，不郊輒代以他禮，慶賞與郊同，而五使皆以輔臣，不以官之高下。 天聖中，乃以朝林學士領儀仗，御史中丞領鹵簿，始用官次。 又每歲大祀，皆遣台省近臣攝太尉，其後或委他官，太中祥符始復舊制。 又國朝沿唐制，以太尉掌誓戒。 今議太尉三公，非其所任，請以吏部尚書掌誓戒。 詔用左僕射，闕則用右僕射、刑部尚書一員蒞之。
In Jingyou 2 the throne told the agencies: "Earth and the Divine State's land spirit were long served by routine officials acting as proxies—not fit honor for the gods. Hereafter the two departments shall be charged. Each year at the nine great sacrifices, when chief ministers served as proxies, Vice Grand Councilors, Masters of Writing section chiefs and deputies, and Hanlin academicians carried out the offerings. ” Vice Grand Councilor Sheng Du had already taken the abstention vows for the Grand Ancestral Temple rite when he was transferred to head Military Affairs—so he never performed the sacrifice. By old custom the emperor went out to the suburbs every three years; if the suburban rite was skipped, another ceremony stood in, with rewards and enfeoffments on the suburban scale. The five commissioners were always grand councilors, rank notwithstanding. Under Tiansheng, a Hanlin academician first took the honor commission and the vice censor-in-chief the halberd array—rank order replacing ministerial standing. Each great annual sacrifice also sent a censorate or secretariat intimate to serve as Grand Marshal; later others sometimes took the post, until Taizong’s Xiangfu reign restored the old rule. The court kept the Tang arrangement—the Grand Marshal presided over the abstention vows. Critics said the Grand Marshal, a nominal Three Ducal title, was wrong for the role and asked that the Minister of Personnel take charge of the vows. The throne ordered the Left Vice Director to preside; if absent, a Right Vice Director or the Minister of Justice would fill in.
21
熙寧四年，參知政事王珪言：「南郊，乘輿所過，必勘箭然後出入，此師行之法，不可施於郊祀。 」禮院亦言。 於是，凡車駕出入門皆罷之。 六年，以詳定所請，又罷太廟及宣德、朱雀、南薰諸門勘契。 又皇帝自大次至版位，內臣二人執翟羽前導，號曰「拂翟」，失禮尤甚，請除之。
In Xining 4, Vice Grand Councilor Wang Gui argued: “On the southern suburban progress every gate required tally-arrows before the imperial carriage could pass—an army field drill, unfit for suburban worship. ” The Ritual Office agreed. Every gate check on the imperial progress was abolished. In year six the review office won abolition of tally checks at the Grand Ancestral Temple and at Xuande, Zhuque, and Nanxun gates as well. Two inner attendants also marched ahead with pheasant plumes from the rest pavilion to the ritual stance—the “brushing the pheasant” parade, a grave breach; they asked that it be dropped.
22
凡郊壇，值雨雪，即齋宮門望祭殿望拜，祭日不設登歌，祀官以公服行事，中祀以上皆給明衣。
Rain or snow at the suburban altar meant bowing from the fasting palace gate toward the sacrifice hall, no ascent hymn on the day itself, officials in court dress, and bright robes for medium rites and above.
23
開寶元年十一月郊，以燎壇稍遠，不聞告燎之聲，始用爟火，令光明遠照，通於祀所。
Kaibao 1’s eleventh-month suburban rite: the pyre stood too far to hear the blaze announcement, so they lit a beacon fire to carry light to the altar.
24
又太廟初獻，依開寶例，以玉斝、玉瓚，亞獻以金斝，終獻以瓢斝。 外壇器亦如之。 慶曆中，太常請皇帝獻天地、配帝以匏爵，亞獻以木爵。 親祠太廟，酌以玉斝，亞獻以金斝。 郊廟飲福，皇帝皆以玉斝。 詔飲福，唯用金斝。 亞、終獻，酌以銀斝。 至飲福，尚食奉禦酌上尊酒，投溫器以進。
Grand Ancestral Temple first offerings followed Kaibao: jade goblet and jade libation cup, gold for the second libation, gourd for the last. The outer altar used the same vessels. During Qingli the Court of Imperial Sacrifices asked that Heaven, Earth, and accompanying deities receive gourd cups from the emperor and wood cups at the second libation. At personal Grand Ancestral Temple rites the emperor poured with a jade goblet; the second libation used gold. For blessing wine at suburban and temple rites the emperor always drank from jade goblets. An edict limited blessing wine to gold goblets alone. Second and final libations were poured from silver goblets. For blessing wine the Imperial Kitchen had stewards ladle top-grade wine, warm it, and serve it up.
25
凡常祀，天地宗廟，皆內降禦封香，仍制漆匱，付光祿、司農寺。 每祠祭，命判寺官緘署禮料送祀所。 凡祈告，亦內出香。 遂為定制。 嘉祐中，裴煜請：「大祠悉降禦封香，中、小祠供太府香。 中祠減大祠之半，小祠減中祠之半。 東、西太一宮系大祠，歲太府供香，非時祈請，降禦封香准大祠例。 及皇地祇、五方帝、百神、文宣、武成從配神位，牲牢寡薄。 」呂公著亦論廟牲未備，悉加其數。 元符元年，左司員外郎曾旼言：「周人以氣臭事神，近世易之以香。 按何佟之議，以為南郊、明堂用沉香，本天之質，陽所宜也； 北效用上和香，以地於人親，宜加雜馥。 今令文北極天皇而下皆用濕香，至於眾星之位，香不復設，恐於義未盡。 」於是每陛各設香。 又言：「先儒以為實柴所祀者無玉，郐鍮燎所祀者無幣。 今太常令式，眾星皆不用幣，蓋出於此。 然考《典瑞》、《玉人》之官，皆曰'圭璧以祀日月星辰'。 則實柴所祀非無玉矣。 郐鍮燎無幣，恐或未然。 」至是遂命眾星隨其方色用幣。
Routine Heaven, Earth, and temple rites always received palace-sealed incense in lacquer cases, handled by the Court of Imperial Entertainments and the Ministry of Revenue. Before each rite the presiding director sealed the ritual stores and forwarded them to the altar. Prayer-announcements likewise drew incense from the palace. The practice became permanent law. In Jiayou, Pei Yu proposed: “Every great rite should get palace-sealed incense; medium and small rites should use Grand Storehouse incense. Medium rites would take half a great rite’s allotment; small rites half a medium rite’s. The Eastern and Western Great One Palaces ranked as great rites—the Grand Storehouse supplied their yearly incense, while ad hoc prayers received palace-sealed incense as at a great rite. Victims for Earth Supreme, the Five Directional Emperors, the Hundred Spirits, and Wen Xuan and Wu Cheng in accompanying seats were likewise too scant. ” Lü Gongzhu too said temple victims fell short, and every quota was raised. Yuanfu 1: Left Office outsider Zeng Min noted: “Zhou worship relied on smell; later ages switched to incense. He Tongzhi had argued that agarwood suits the southern suburb and Bright Hall—heaven’s own quality, fit for yang; the northern altar should use supreme-harmony incense—earth is kin to humanity and deserves mixed perfumes. Statutes today use moist incense down from the North Pole Heavenly Sovereign, yet star thrones go without incense—the symbolism may be incomplete. ” Incense was placed on every terrace step. He added: “Old commentators said bao chai worship used no jade and Kui/Tang blaze worship used no silk. Current Court of Imperial Sacrifices forms withhold silk from all stars—likely from that reading. Yet the Directors of Regalia and Jade Workers both read: ‘gui and bi for sun, moon, stars, and constellations.’ So bao chai was not jadeless after all. Whether Kui and Tang blaze truly lacked silk may be doubtful. ” Stars were then ordered to use silk matching their directional colors.
26
慶曆三年，禮官余靖言：「祈穀、祀感生帝同日，其禮當異，不可皆用四圭有邸，色尚赤。 」乃定祈穀、明堂蒼璧尺二寸，感生帝四圭有邸，朝日日圭、夕月月圭皆五寸，從祀神州無玉，報社稷兩圭有邸，祈不用玉。 明年，《祀儀》成，比《通禮》多所更定雲。 嘉祐中，集賢校理江休復言：「《六典》大祀養牲，在滌三月，袷享日近，已逾其期，而牲牢未供。 乞依漢、唐置廩犧局。 」下禮院議：歲大小祀幾百數，而牲盛之事，儲養無素，宜如休復言。 乃置廩犧局，設牢預養，籍田舊地，種植粢盛，納於神倉，以待祭祀之用。
Qingli 3: ritual officer Yu Jing said: “Prayer-for-grain and the Life-Giving Emperor share a day but not the same ritual—one cannot give every rite the four-gui-with-base in red. ” They set prayer-for-grain and Bright Hall at an azure bi of one foot two inches; Life-Giving Emperor at four gui with base; morning sun and evening moon gui at five inches each; Divine State accompaniment without jade; soil-and-grain reports with paired gui-with-base; prayers without jade. Next year the Sacrifice Regulament appeared, revising much against the Comprehensive Ritual, as noted. Jiayou: Hanlin collator Jiang Xiufu wrote: “The Six Offices require three months in the cleansing pen for great-sacrifice beasts—yet joint-honor day nears past deadline with victims still missing. He asked for a grain-and-victim bureau on Han and Tang models. ” The Ritual Office replied: hundreds of rites yearly, yet no standing herd—Xiufu’s plan should be adopted. They then founded the Grain-and-Victim Bureau, pre-raised stock in pens, planted offering grain on old register fields, and filled the god granary for rites to come.
27
元豐六年，詳定禮文所言：「本朝昊天上帝、皇地祇、太祖位各設三牲，非尚質貴誠之義。 請親祠圜丘、方澤正配位皆用犢，不設羊豕俎及鼎匕，有司攝事亦如之。 又簠、簋、尊、豆皆非陶器，及用龍杓。 請改用陶，以椫為杓。 又請南北郊先行升煙瘞血之禮，至薦奠畢，即如舊儀，於壇坎燔瘞牲幣。 又北郊皇地祇及神州地祇，當為坎瘞，今乃建壇燔燎，非是。 請今祭地祝版、牲幣並瘞於坎。 又《祀儀》：惟昊天上帝、皇地祇、高禖燔瘞犢首，自感生帝、神州地祇而下皆不燔瘞牲體，殊不應典禮。 請自今昊天上帝、感生帝皆燔牲首以報陽； 皇地祇、神州、太社、太稷，凡地之祭，皆瘞牲之左髀以報陰。 薦享太廟亦皆升首於室。」
Yuanfeng 6: the ritual review office said: “Three victims each at Supreme Heaven, Earth Supreme, and Taizu’s seats betray simplicity and sincerity. Personal rites at Round Altar and Square Mound, primary and accompanying, should use calves only—no sheep or pig trays or tripod ladles; proxies the same. Grain vessels, wine jars, and offering bowls were not pottery; dragon ladles were used. They asked for pottery ware and birch ladles. They also wanted southern and northern suburbs to open with smoke ascent and blood burial, then after offerings finish burn and bury victims and silk in the pits as before. Northern rites to Earth Supreme and the Divine State Earth Spirit should bury in pits, not burn on built altars—that was the error. Henceforth prayer boards, victims, and silk at earth rites should all go into the pit. The Sacrifice Regulament burned and buried calf heads only for Supreme Heaven, Earth Supreme, and High Consort; below Life-Giving Emperor and Divine State Earth Spirit no flesh was burned or buried—a wide miss against the classics. They asked that Supreme Heaven and Life-Giving Emperor hereafter burn victim heads to answer yang; Earth Supreme, Divine State, and the Grand Soil and Grain altars—all earth rites should bury the victim’s left shank for yin. Grand Ancestral Temple offerings should likewise raise heads to the inner chamber.”
28
又言：「古者祭祀用牲，有豚解，有體解，薦腥則解為十一體。 今親祠南郊，正配位之俎，不殊左右胖，不分貴賤，無豚解、體解之別。 請郊廟薦腥，解其牲兩髀、兩肩、兩肋並脊為七體，左右胖俱用。 其載於俎，以兩髀在端，兩肩、兩肋次之，脊居中，皆進末。 至薦熟，沉肉于湯，止用右胖。 髀不升俎，前後肱骨離為三，曰肩、臂、臑。 後髀股骨去體離為二，曰肫、胳。 前脊謂之正脊，次直謂之脡脊，闊於脡脊謂之橫脊，皆二骨。 肋骨最後二為短肋，旁中二為正肋，最前二為代肋。 若升俎，則肩、臂臑在上端，膊、胳在下端，脊、肋在中央。 其俎之序，則肩、臂、臑、正脊、脡脊、橫脊、代肋、長肋、短肋、膊、胳凡十一體，而骨體升俎，進神坐前如少牢禮，皆進下。 其牲體各預以半為腥俎，半為熟俎，腸胃膚俎亦然。」
He went on: “Ancient victims were cut whole-pig style or body style; raw offerings came in eleven pieces. Today’s personal southern suburb makes no left-right split on primary and accompanying trays, no rank distinction, no whole-pig versus body cut. Suburban and temple raw offerings should split two thighs, two shoulders, two ribs, and the spine into seven parts, using both sides. On the tray both thighs go to the ends, shoulders and ribs follow, spine in the middle, each piece presented end-first. Cooked offerings steep the meat in broth and use only the right side. Thighs never mount the tray; fore and hind arm bones divide into shoulder, arm, and shank. The hind quarter, thigh bone stripped from the carcass, yields shin and flank. The fore spine is the proper spine; the next straight strip the filet spine; broader still, the cross spine—each of two bones. Ribs count from the rear: last pair short ribs, middle pair proper ribs, front pair substitute ribs. On the tray shoulder, arm, and shank sit uppermost; flank and shin below; spine and ribs in the center. Order runs shoulder, arm, shank, proper spine, filet spine, cross spine, substitute rib, long ribs, short ribs, flank, shin—eleven pieces; like the lesser pen rite before the spirit seat, all presented bottom-first. Each carcass is split beforehand: half raw tray, half cooked; intestinal, stomach, and skin trays follow the same rule.”
29
又請：「親祠飲福酒訖，仿《儀禮》'佐食摶黍'之說，命太官令取忝於簋，摶以授祝，祝受以豆，以嘏乎皇帝而無嘏辭。 又本朝親祠南郊，習儀於壇所，明堂習儀於大慶殿，皆近於瀆。 伏請南郊習儀於青城，明堂習儀於尚書省，以遠神為恭。 又賜胙：三師，三公，侍中，中書令，門下、中書侍郎，尚書左、右丞，知樞密、同知院事，禮儀、儀仗、鹵簿、頓遞使，牛羊豕肩、臂、臑各五； 太子三師、三少，特進，觀文大學士、學士，御史大夫，六尚書，金紫、銀青光祿大夫，節度使，資政殿大學士，觀文翰林資政端明龍圖天章寶文承旨、侍講、侍讀，學士，左右散騎常侍，尚書列曹侍郎，龍圖、天章、寶文直學士，光祿、正議、通議大夫，御史中丞，太子賓客、詹事，給事中，中書舍人，節度觀察留後，左右諫議，龍圖、天章、寶文待制，太中、中大夫，秘書、殿中丞，太常、宗正卿，牛豕肩、臂、臑各三； 入內內侍省押班、副都知，光祿卿，監禮官，博士，牛羊脊、肋各三； 太祝，奉禮，司奠彝，郊社、太廟、宮闈令，監牲牢、供應祠事內官，羊髀、膊、胳三； 應執事、職掌、樂工、門幹、宰手、馭馬、馭車人，並均給脾、肫、胳、觳及腸、胃、膚之類。」
He also asked: “After the emperor’s blessing wine at a personal rite, echoing the Ceremonial Rites ‘associate kneads millet,’ the Grand Kitchen Director should take millet from the grain vessel, knead it, hand it to the invocator, who receives it in a bowl and blesses the emperor—without a spoken blessing. Personal southern-suburb rehearsals at the altar itself and Bright Hall drills in Great Celebration Hall both hug the spirits too closely—near profanation. They pleaded to rehearse southern rites at Qingcheng and Bright Hall rites at the Masters of Writing compound—distance as reverence. Sacrificial meat went to the Three Preceptors, Three Ducal ministers, Palace Attendants, Secretariat chiefs, Gate and Secretariat vice ministers, Masters of Writing deputies, Military Affairs heads, and the ritual, honor, halberd, and relay commissioners—five each of ox, sheep, and pig shoulder, arm, and shank; The Crown Prince’s tutors, Special Advancement holders, Exalted Culture and Hanlin academicians, censor-in-chief, six ministers, gold- and silver-purple grand masters, commissioners, Palatial Hall scholars, lecturers and readers, Regular Attendants, bureau vice ministers, direct academicians, Court of Imperial Entertainments and Correct and Universal grand masters, vice censor-in-chief, Crown Prince staff, edict recipients, drafters, observation commissioners, remonstrators, awaiting-appointment scholars, grand masters, director deputies, and Court of Imperial Sacrifices and Imperial Clan chiefs received three each of ox and pig shoulder, arm, and shank; Inner Service deputies, the Court of Imperial Entertainments director, ritual supervisors, and erudites received three each of ox and sheep spine and ribs; Grand invocators, ritual attendants, vessel officers, suburban and temple directors, and inner attendants overseeing victims and supplies received three of sheep thigh, flank, and shin; Every functionary, clerk, musician, gate guard, butcher, and driver shared alike in spleen, shin, flank, bones, intestines, stomach, skin, and the rest.”
