李賢呂原 〈 （子常）〉 嶽正彭時商輅劉定之
Li Xian, Lu Yuan [Sub-biography: style name Zichang.] Yue Zheng, Peng Shi, Shang Lu, Liu Dingzhi
2
李賢，字原德，鄧人。 舉鄉試第一，宣德八年成進士。 奉命察蝗災於河津，授驗封主事，少師楊士奇欲一見，賢竟不往。
Li Xian, whose style was Yuande, came from Deng. He topped the provincial examinations and passed the jinshi degree in the eighth year of Xuande. Commissioned to investigate locust plagues at Hejin, he was appointed registrar at Yifeng; Yang Shiqi, the junior preceptor, wished to meet him, but Xian never went.
3
正統初，言：「塞外降人居京師者盈萬，指揮使月俸三十五石，實支僅一石，降人反實支十七石五鬥，是一降人當京官十七員半矣。 宜漸出之外，省冗費，且消患未萌。 」帝不能用。 時詔文武臣誥敕，非九年不給。 賢言：「限以九年，或官不能滿秩，或親老不待，不得者十八九，無以勸臣下。 請仍三年便。 」從之。 遷考功郎中，改文選。 扈從北征，師覆脫還。
Early in the Zhengtong reign he memorialized: "More than ten thousand men who surrendered beyond the frontier now live in the capital. A commander is allotted thirty-five piculs of grain a month but actually receives only one, while a surrendered tribesman actually receives seventeen and a half—so that a single surrendered man costs as much as seventeen and a half metropolitan officials." They should gradually be resettled outside the capital to cut waste and to remove a danger before it takes root. " The Emperor did not act on the proposal. An edict then required that civil and military officials receive their patent letters of appointment only after nine years of service. Xian argued: "With a nine-year limit, some officials never complete a full term and others have aged parents who cannot wait; eight or nine out of ten never receive their patents, and there is no way to encourage loyal service." I ask that the former rule of every three years be restored." " The Emperor agreed. He was promoted to director in the Bureau of Evaluation and then transferred to the Bureau of Appointments. He accompanied the northern campaign; when the army was annihilated he escaped and made his way back.
4
景泰二年二月上正本十策，曰勤聖學，顧箴警，戒嗜欲，絕玩好，慎舉措，崇節儉，畏天變，勉貴近，振士風，結民心。 帝善之，命翰林寫置左右，備省覽。 尋又陳車戰火器之利，帝頗采納。 是冬，擢兵部右侍郎，轉戶部。 也先數貢馬，賢謂輦金帛以強寇自弊，非策。 因陳邊備廢馳狀，于謙請下其章厲諸將。 轉吏部，采古二十二君行事可法者。 曰《鑒古錄》，上之。
In the second month of the second year of Jingtai he presented ten fundamental policies: pursue sage learning diligently, heed admonitory maxims, guard against licentious desires, renounce frivolous pleasures, act with care in all matters, uphold frugality, fear heavenly warnings, encourage those of rank who are near the throne, revive the spirit of the scholar-official class, and bind the people's hearts. The Emperor approved them and ordered the Hanlin Academy to copy them and keep them at hand for his review. Soon afterward he also argued the advantages of chariot warfare and firearms, and the Emperor adopted much of what he said. That winter he was promoted to vice minister of war and then transferred to the Ministry of Revenue. Esan repeatedly sent tribute horses; Xian said that hauling gold and silks to strengthen a raider was to harm oneself deliberately—not a sound policy. He then described how frontier defenses had fallen into neglect; Yu Qian asked that his memorial be distributed to rouse the generals. Transferred to the Ministry of Personnel, he gathered the conduct of twenty-two ancient rulers that could serve as models. He titled it 《Records for Mirroring Antiquity》 and presented it to the throne.
5
英宗復位，命兼翰林學士，入直文淵閣，與徐有貞同預機務。 未幾，進尚書。 賢氣度端凝，奏對皆中機宜，帝深眷之。 山東饑，發帑振不足，召有貞及賢議，有貞謂頒振多中飽。 賢曰：「慮中飽而不貸，坐視民死，是因噎廢食也。 」遂命增銀。
When Emperor Yingzong was restored to the throne, Xian was appointed concurrently as Hanlin academician, entered the Wenyuan Pavilion on confidential duty, and shared state affairs with Xu Youzhen. Before long he was promoted to minister. Xian's bearing was dignified and steady; in audience his answers always struck the right point, and the Emperor held him in deep regard. When Shandong suffered famine, treasury funds released for relief proved insufficient; the Emperor summoned Youzhen and Xian to discuss the matter. Youzhen said that relief distributions were largely embezzled in transit. Xian said: "To fear embezzlement and withhold relief, watching the people starve, is to give up eating for fear of choking." " The Emperor then ordered additional silver for relief.
6
石亨、曹吉祥與有貞爭權，並忌賢。 諸御史論亨、吉祥，亨、吉祥疑出有貞、賢意，訴之帝，下二人獄。 會有風雷變，得釋，謫賢福建參政。 未行，王翺奏賢可大用，遂留為吏部左侍郎。 逾月，復尚書，直內閣如故。 亨知帝向賢，怒，然無可如何，乃佯與交歡。 賢亦深自匿，非宣召不入，而帝益親賢，顧問無虛日。
Shi Heng and Cao Jixiang were locked in a power struggle with Youzhen and equally resented Xian. When censors impeached Heng and Jixiang, the two men suspected Youzhen and Xian were behind it; they accused them to the Emperor, and both men were thrown into prison. When wind and thunder shifted as an omen they were released; Xian was demoted to assistant administrator of Fujian. Before he could depart, Wang Ao memorialized that Xian merited greater use; he was kept on as left vice minister of personnel. A month later he was restored as minister and resumed his seat in the inner cabinet as before. Heng knew the Emperor favored Xian and was furious, yet could do nothing about it; he therefore pretended friendship. Xian also kept himself deeply withdrawn, entering court only when summoned by imperial announcement; yet the Emperor drew still closer to him and consulted him without a day's lapse.
7
孛來近塞獵。 亨言傳國璽在彼，可掩而取，帝色動。 賢言釁不可啟，璽不足寶，事遂寢。 亨益惡賢。 時帝亦厭亨、吉祥驕橫，屏人語賢曰：「此輩幹政，四方奏事者先至其門，為之奈何？ 」賢曰：「陛下惟獨斷，則趨附自息。 」帝曰：「向嘗不用其言，乃怫然見辭色。 」賢曰：「願制之以漸。 」當亨、吉祥用事，賢顧忌不敢盡言，然每從容論對，所以裁抑之者甚至。 及亨得罪，帝復問賢「奪門」事。 賢曰：「『迎駕』則可，『奪門』豈可示後？ 天位乃陛下固有，『奪』即非順。 且爾時幸而成功，萬一事機先露，亨等不足惜，不審置陛下何地！ 」帝悟曰：「然。 」賢曰：「若郕王果不起，群臣表請陛下復位，安用擾攘為？ 此輩又安所得邀升賞，招權納賄安自起？ 老成耆舊依然在職，何至有殺戮降黜之事致幹天象？ 《易》曰『開國承家，小人勿用』，正謂此也。 」帝曰：「然。 」詔自今章奏勿用「奪門」字，並議革冒功者四千余人。 至成化初，諸被革者訴請。 復以賢言，並奪太平侯張瑾、興濟伯楊宗爵，時論益大快之。
Bolai came near the frontier to hunt. Heng said the imperial seal was with Bolai and could be taken in a surprise attack; the Emperor's expression changed. Xian said that provocation must not be invited and the seal was no treasure worth the risk; the plan was abandoned. Heng hated Xian all the more. The Emperor had also grown weary of Heng and Jixiang's arrogance; dismissing his attendants he said to Xian: "These men meddle in government affairs; memorials from every quarter reach their gates before mine. What can be done?" " Xian said: "If Your Majesty decides every matter on your own authority, the rush to court them will die away of itself." " The Emperor said: "Once when I did not follow their advice they showed displeasure and took their leave with angry faces." " Xian said: "I hope Your Majesty will restrain them step by step." While Heng and Jixiang held power, Xian, out of caution, did not dare speak his whole mind; yet whenever he answered at ease, the restraints he placed on them were very strong indeed. After Heng was punished, the Emperor again asked Xian about the "Seizing the Gate" coup. Xian said: "'Welcoming the imperial carriage' was permissible, but how could 'Seizing the Gate' be held up as an example to posterity?" The throne was inherently yours, Your Majesty; to 'seize' it was not in accord with right order. Moreover, even if it had fortunately succeeded, had the plot been exposed beforehand—Heng and the rest would not be worth pity, but where would that have left Your Majesty! " The Emperor understood and said: "You are right." " Xian said: "If the Prince of Cheng had in fact not recovered, the officials would have memorialized as a body begging Your Majesty's restoration—why need all that turmoil?" These men moreover would never have won promotions and rewards—whence would they have drawn power and taken bribes? The seasoned elders would still have held office—how could there have been killings, demotions, and dismissals that provoked heavenly signs?" The 《Book of Changes》 says, 'In founding a state and handing on the family, do not employ petty men'—it speaks precisely of this." " The Emperor said: "You are right." An edict forbade the phrase "Seizing the Gate" in memorials from that day forward, and they discussed stripping false merit from more than four thousand men. At the beginning of the Chenghua reign those who had been stripped appealed for restoration. Again on Xian's advice, the Peace Marquis Zhang Jin and the Baron of Promoting Relief Yang Zong were also stripped of their titles, to the even greater satisfaction of public opinion.
8
帝既任賢，所言皆見聽。 于謙嘗分遣降人南征，陳汝言希宦官指，盡召之還。 賢力言不可。 帝曰：「吾亦悔之。 今已就道，後當聽其願去者。 」帝憂軍官支俸多，歲入不給。 賢請汰老弱於外，則費省而人不覺。 帝深納焉。 時歲有邊警，天下大水，江南北尤甚。 賢外籌邊計，內請寬百姓，罷一切征求。 帝用其言，四方得蘇息。 七年二月，空中有聲，帝欲禳之，命賢撰青詞。 賢言君不恤民，天下怨叛，厥有鼓妖。 因請行寬恤之政，又請罷江南織造，清錦衣獄，止邊臣貢獻，停內外采買。 帝難之。 賢執爭數四，同列皆懼。 賢退曰：「大臣當知無不言，可卷舌偷位耶？ 」終天順之世，賢為首輔，呂原、彭時佐之，然賢委任最專。
Once the Emperor had entrusted affairs to Xian, his counsel was followed in every matter. Yu Qian had once dispatched surrendered tribesmen south on campaign; Chen Ruyan, courting the eunuchs' wishes, recalled them all. Xian argued forcefully that this must not be done. The Emperor said: "I regret it as well." "They are already on the march; hereafter let those who wish to leave do so." The Emperor worried that military officers' salaries were too numerous and annual revenue could not cover them. Xian asked that the aged and weak be weeded out in the provinces; expense would be saved and no one would notice. The Emperor adopted the proposal wholeheartedly. In those years frontier alarms came every year and great floods struck the empire, especially south of the Yangtze. Outwardly Xian planned frontier strategy; inwardly he asked leniency for the people and an end to every exaction. The Emperor followed his counsel and the realm obtained respite. In the second month of the seventh year a sound was heard in the sky; the Emperor wished to perform expiatory rites and ordered Xian to draft a green prayer. Xian said that when a ruler does not care for his people the realm resents and rebels—and that is why there are drum demons in the sky. He therefore asked for policies of leniency, also to halt Jiangnan weaving, clear the Jinyi prison, stop tribute from frontier officials, and end purchases inside and outside the court. The Emperor found it difficult to accept. Xian argued repeatedly on four points; his colleagues were all afraid. On withdrawing, Xian said: "A great minister should know there is nothing he may not say—can one bite one's tongue and cling to office?" Throughout the Tianshun reign Xian was chief minister, assisted by Lu Yuan and Peng Shi, yet Xian's commission was the most exclusive.
9
初，御史劉濬劾柳溥敗軍罪，觸帝怒。 賢言御史耳目官，不宜譴。 石亨譖賢曲護。 帝浸疏賢，尋悟，待之如初。 每獨對，良久方出。 遇事必召問可否，或遣中官就問。 賢務持大體，尤以惜人才、開言路為急。 所薦引年富、軒輗、耿九疇、王竑、李秉、程信、姚夔、崔恭、李紹等，皆為名臣。 時勸帝延見大臣，有所薦，必先與吏、兵二部論定之。 及入對，帝訪文臣，請問王翺； 武臣，請問馬昂。 兩人相左右，故言無不行，而人不病其專，惟群小與為難。
Earlier, Censor Liu Jun impeached Liu Pu for defeat in battle and provoked the Emperor's anger. Xian said that censors are the emperor's eyes and ears and ought not to be punished. Shi Heng slandered Xian for shielding the censor unfairly. The Emperor gradually grew cool toward Xian, but soon understood and treated him as before. Whenever he was received in private audience, he remained a long while before emerging. When affairs arose the Emperor was sure to summon him to ask whether a course was feasible, or sent palace eunuchs to inquire. Xian strove to uphold the larger pattern of governance, especially urgent to cherish talent and open the avenue of frank counsel. Those he recommended—Nian Fu, Xuan Yuan, Geng Jiuchou, Wang Hong, Li Bing, Cheng Xin, Yao Kui, Cui Gong, Li Shao, and others—were all renowned ministers. He often urged the Emperor to receive great ministers in audience; whatever he wished to recommend he first discussed and settled with the ministries of personnel and war. When he entered audience, if the Emperor inquired about civil officials, Xian would ask about Wang Ao; for military officials, about Ma Ang. The two men supported him from either side, so his counsel was always followed yet men did not resent his monopoly—only petty men made trouble for him.
10
曹欽之反也，擊賢東朝房，執將殺之，逼草奏釋己罪。 賴王翺救，乃免。 賢密疏請擒賊黨。 時方擾攘，不知賢所在。 得疏，帝大喜。 裹傷入見，慰勞之，特加太子太保。 賢因言，賊既誅，急宜詔天下停不急務，而求直言以通閉塞。 帝從之。
When Cao Qin rebelled, rebels struck Xian's eastern audience chamber, seized him intending to kill him, and forced him to draft a memorial absolving their crimes. Thanks to Wang Ao's rescue he was spared. Xian secretly memorialized asking that the rebel clique be arrested. At the time turmoil had just broken out and no one knew where Xian was. When the memorial arrived the Emperor was greatly pleased. Binding his wounds he entered audience; the Emperor comforted him and specially added the title Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent. Xian then said that once the rebels were executed the court should urgently proclaim throughout the realm a halt to non-urgent affairs and seek frank counsel to open what had been blocked. The Emperor agreed.
11
門達方用事，錦衣官校恣橫為劇患。 賢累請禁止，帝召達誡諭之。 達怙寵益驕，賢乘間復具陳達罪，帝復召戒達。 達銜次骨，因袁彬獄陷賢，賢幾不免，語載達傳。
Men Da was then in power, and Jinyi surveillance officers ran rampant as a severe scourge. Xian repeatedly asked that this be forbidden; the Emperor summoned Da and admonished him. Da, relying on imperial favor, grew all the more arrogant; Xian seized an opening and again set forth Da's crimes in full, and the Emperor again summoned and warned him. Da bore a lasting grudge; through the Yuan Bin prison case he trapped Xian, and Xian nearly perished—the full account appears in Da's biography.
12
帝不豫，臥文華殿。 會有間東宮於帝者，帝頗惑之，密告賢。 賢頓首伏地曰：「此大事，願陛下三思。 」帝曰：「然則必傳位太子乎？ 」賢又頓首曰：「宗社幸甚。 」帝起，立召太子至。 賢扶太子令謝。 太子謝，抱帝足泣，帝亦泣，讒竟不行。
The Emperor fell ill and lay in the Wenhua Hall. Someone slandered the crown prince to the Emperor; the Emperor was deeply troubled and confided in Xian in secret. Xian kowtowed and said prostrate on the ground: "This is a grave matter; I beg Your Majesty to think it over three times." The Emperor said: "Then must the throne be passed to the crown prince?" Xian kowtowed again and said: "Fortunate indeed for the altars of state." The Emperor rose and immediately summoned the crown prince. Xian supported the crown prince and had him give thanks. The crown prince gave thanks, embraced the Emperor's feet, and wept; the Emperor wept as well, and the slander in the end did not prevail.
13
門達既竄，其黨多投匿名書構賢。 賢乞罷，有詔慰留。 吳後廢，言官請誅牛玉，語侵賢，又有造蜚語構賢者。 帝命衛士宿賢家，護出入。 成化二年三月遭父喪，詔起復。 三辭不許，遣中官護行營葬。 還至京，又辭。 遣使宣意，遂視事。 其年冬卒，年五十九。 帝震悼，贈太師，謚文達。
After Men Da fled, most of his faction sent anonymous letters to frame Xian. Xian asked to resign; an edict comforted him and kept him at his post. After the deposed Empress Wu, remonstrating officials called for Niu Yu's execution in language that implicated Xian, and others manufactured slander to frame him. The Emperor ordered guards to lodge at Xian's home to protect him when he went out or returned. In the third month of the second year of Chenghua he suffered his father's death; an edict ordered him recalled from mourning service. He declined three times but was not permitted; palace eunuchs were sent to escort him and oversee the funeral. When he returned to the capital he declined again. An envoy was sent to convey the imperial intent, and he then resumed his duties. That winter he died, aged fifty-nine. The Emperor was shaken with grief, posthumously granted him Grand Preceptor, and gave him the posthumous name Wendate.
14
賢自以受知人主，所言無不盡。 景帝崩，將以汪後殉葬，用賢言而止。 惠帝少子幽禁已六十年，英宗憐欲赦之，以問賢。 賢頓首曰：「此堯、舜用心也！ 天地祖宗實式憑之。 」帝意乃決。 帝嘗祭山川壇，以夜出未便，欲遣官代祀。 賢引祖訓爭之，卒成禮而還。 嘗言內帑余財，不以恤荒濟軍，則人主必生侈心，而移之於土木禱祠聲色之用。 前後頻請發帑振貸恤邊，不可勝計。 故事，方面官敕三品京官保舉。 賢患其營競，令吏部每缺舉二人，請帝簡用。 並推之例始此。
Because Xian felt he had won his sovereign's trust, there was nothing he held back in counsel. When Emperor Jingdi died, the court was about to bury Empress Wang as a companion sacrifice; on Xian's advice this was stopped. The deposed young son of Emperor Hui had been confined for sixty years; Emperor Yingzong pitied him and wished to pardon him, and asked Xian. Xian kowtowed and said: "This is the mind of Yao and Shun!" Heaven and Earth and the imperial ancestors surely rely on this. The Emperor's mind was then made up. The Emperor once sacrificed at the mountain and river altar; going out at night was inconvenient, and he wished to send an official to substitute for him. Xian cited ancestral instruction and argued against it; in the end the rite was completed and he returned. He once said that surplus wealth in the inner treasury, if not used to relieve famine and support the army, would make the ruler grow extravagant and shift it to earthworks, prayers, and the pleasures of sound and color. Again and again he asked to open the treasury for relief, loans, and frontier support—too many times to count. By precedent, patents for provincial officials required guarantee by capital officials of third rank. Xian worried about their competition for patronage and ordered the Ministry of Personnel to nominate two men for each vacancy, asking the Emperor to choose. The precedent of joint nomination and promotion began here.
15
自三楊以來，得君無如賢者。 然自郎署結知景帝，超擢侍郎，而所著書顧謂景帝為荒淫。 其抑葉盛，擠嶽正，不救羅倫，尤為世所惜雲。
Since the Three Yangs, no one had won the ruler's trust as Xian did. Yet from his start in the secretariat, when he won Jingdi's trust and was abruptly promoted to vice minister, in his writings he nevertheless called Emperor Jing dissolute and profligate. His suppressing Ye Sheng, squeezing out Yue Zheng, and not saving Luo Lun were especially regretted by the world.
16
呂原，字逢原，秀水人。 父嗣芳，萬泉教諭。 兄本，景州訓導。 嗣芳老，就養景州，與本相繼卒。 貧不能歸葬，厝於景，原時至墓慟哭。 久之，奉母南歸，家益貧。 知府黃懋奇原文，補諸生，遣入學，舉鄉試第一。
Lu Yuan, whose style was Fengyuan, came from Xiushui. His father Sifang was instructor at Wanquan. His elder brother Ben was instructor at Jingzhou. When Sifang grew old he went to Jingzhou for support; he and Ben died one after another. Too poor to return them for burial, they were stored at Jing; Yuan would go to the tomb and weep bitterly. After a long while he escorted his mother south to return home; the family grew still poorer. Prefect Huang Maoqi admired his writing, made him a licensed student, sent him to the academy, and he ranked first in the provincial examination.
17
正統七年，進士及第，授編修。 十二年，與侍講裴綸等十人同選入東閣肄業，直經筵。 景泰初，進侍講，與同官倪謙授小內侍書於文華殿東廡。 帝至，命謙講《國風》。 原講《堯典》，皆稱旨。 問何官，並以中允兼侍講對。 帝曰：「品同耳，何相兼為？ 」進二人侍講學士，兼中允。 尋進左春坊大學士。
In the seventh year of Zhengtong he passed the jinshi examination with highest honors and was appointed compiler. In the twelfth year he and Lecturer Pei Lun and ten others were jointly selected to study in the Eastern Pavilion and attend the classic lecture. Early in Jingtai he was promoted to lecturer and, with his colleague Ni Qian, taught the young palace attendants writing in the eastern veranda of the Wenhua Hall. When the Emperor came he ordered Qian to lecture on the Odes. Yuan lectured on the Canon of Yao—both pleased the Emperor. Asked what offices they held, both answered that they were junior tutors concurrently serving as lecturers. The Emperor said: "The ranks are the same—why hold both offices at once?" Both were promoted to lecturer-academicians, concurrently junior tutors. Soon he was promoted to grand secretary of the left Eastern Palace.

Patent letters for officials whose parents reach seventy, caps and sashes for commoners at eighty—that is what it means to "honor one's own elders and extend that honor to others' elders." " Li Xian approved and had the policy memorialized and enacted.
33
帝愛時風度，選庶吉士。 命賢盡用北人，南人必若時者方可。 賢以語時。 俄中官牛玉宣旨，時謂玉曰：「南士出時上者不少，何可抑之？ 」已，選十五人，南六人與焉。
The Emperor admired Shi's bearing and was selecting new Hanlin fellows. He told Li Xian to take only northerners; southerners were acceptable only if they were Shi's equal. Li Xian relayed this to Shi. Soon the eunuch Niu Yu delivered the edict; Shi told him, "Many southern scholars have outranked me—how can we hold them back?" " In the end fifteen were chosen, six of them southerners.
34
門達構賢，帝惑之，曰：「去賢，行專用時矣。 」或傳其語，時矍然曰：「李公有經濟才，何可去？ 」因力直之。 且曰：「賢去，時不得獨留。 」語聞，帝意乃解。
Men Da slandered Li Xian; the Emperor was taken in and said, "Dismiss Xian, and I'll rely on Shi alone." " When word reached him, Shi exclaimed, "Lord Li has real administrative talent—he mustn't go!" He therefore argued strenuously on Li Xian's behalf. He added, "If Li Xian goes, I cannot stay on alone." " The Emperor heard this and changed his mind.
35
帝大漸，口占遺命，定後妃名分，勿以嬪禦殉葬，凡四事，付閣臣潤色。 時讀竟，涕下，悲愴不自勝。 中官復命，帝亦為隕涕。
As the Emperor lay near death, he dictated his final wishes: fixing the status of the consorts and forbidding palace women from being buried with him—four points in all, left to the grand secretaries to draft properly. When Shi finished reading he wept uncontrollably, overcome with grief. The eunuch reported back, and the Emperor wept as well.
36
憲宗即位，議上兩宮尊號。 中官夏時希周貴妃旨，言錢後久病，不當稱太后。 而貴妃，帝所生母，宜獨上尊號。 賢曰：「遺詔已定，何事多言。 」時曰：「李公言是也。 朝廷所以服天下，在正綱常。 若不爾，損聖德非小。 」頃之，中官復傳貴妃旨：「子為皇帝，母當為太后，豈有無子而稱太后者？ 宣德間有故事。 」賢色變，目時。 時曰：「今日事與宣德間不同。 胡後表讓位，退居別宮，故在正統初不加尊。 今名分固在，安得為比？ 」中官曰：「如是何不草讓表？ 」時曰：「先帝存日未嘗行，今誰敢草？ 若人臣阿意順從，是萬世罪人也。 」中官厲聲怵以危語。 時拱手向天曰：「太祖、太宗神靈在上，孰敢有二心！ 錢皇后無子，何所規利而為之爭？ 臣義不忍默者，欲全主上聖德耳。 若推大孝之心，則兩宮並尊為宜。 」賢亦極言之，議遂定。 及將上寶冊，時曰：「兩宮同稱則無別，錢太后宜加兩字，以便稱謂。 」乃尊為慈懿皇太后，貴妃為皇太后。 越數日，中官覃包至內閣曰：「上意固如是。 但迫於太后，不敢自主，非二公力急，幾誤大事。 」時閣臣陳文默無語，聞包言，甚愧。 禮成，進吏部右侍郎，兼學士，同知經筵。
When Xianzong succeeded, the court debated honorific titles for the two empress dowagers. The eunuch Xia Shi, currying favor with Noble Consort Zhou, argued that Empress Qian's long illness disqualified her from the title Empress Dowager. The noble consort, the Emperor's biological mother, alone deserved the honorific title. Li Xian said, "The dying edict has settled this—why reopen it?" " Shi agreed: "Lord Li is right." The throne wins obedience by upholding proper ritual and moral order. Otherwise the damage to the Emperor's moral standing would be grave. " Soon the eunuch returned with the consort's message: "An emperor's mother is empress dowager—how can a woman with no son claim that title?" There was a Xuande precedent for this. " Li Xian went pale and looked to Shi. Shi replied, "Today's case isn't the Xuande one." Empress Hu had formally abdicated and retired to a separate residence, so she received no honorific at the start of Zhengtong. Empress Qian's status stands firm—there is no comparison. " The eunuch asked, "Then why not draft an abdication memorial for her?" " Shi answered, "The late Emperor never permitted this in his lifetime—who would dare draft one now?" Any minister who toadying complied would be condemned for all time. " The eunuch raised his voice and tried to frighten them with threats. Shi bowed toward heaven and declared, "With the spirits of Taizu and Taizong watching, who would dare dissemble!" Empress Qian had no son—what could she possibly gain by fighting this? I speak because I cannot stay silent if I mean to preserve the Emperor's moral standing. In the spirit of true filial piety, both mothers should receive equal honor. " Li Xian argued just as hard, and the matter was settled. Before the patent was presented, Shi said, "If both bear the same title there'll be no distinction—give Empress Dowager Qian two extra characters for clarity." " Empress Qian was styled Empress Dowager Ciyi; the noble consort became Empress Dowager. A few days later the eunuch Tan Bao told the Grand Secretariat, "The Emperor had always meant it this way." But he bowed to pressure from the empress dowager and didn't dare decide alone—without your forceful stand, a grave mistake would nearly have been made. " Grand Secretary Chen Wen had said nothing throughout; hearing this, he was deeply ashamed. After the ceremony he was promoted to right vice minister of personnel, continuing as academician and sharing direction of the imperial lectures.
37
成化改元，進兵部尚書，兼官如故。 明年秋，乞歸省。 三年二月詔趣還朝，《英宗實錄》成，加太子少保。 兼文淵閣大學士。
With the Chenghua reign he became minister of war, keeping his other posts. The following autumn he asked to visit his family. In the second month of Chenghua 3 he was recalled; when the 《Veritable Records of Emperor Yingzong》 was finished, he was made junior guardian of the heir apparent. He also became grand secretary of the Wenyuan Pavilion.
38
四年，慈懿太后崩，詔議山陵。 時及商輅、劉定之言：「太后作配先帝，正位中宮，陛下尊為太后，詔示天下。 先帝全夫婦之倫，陛下盡母子之愛，於義俱得。 今梓宮當合葬裕陵，主當祔廟，此不易之禮。 比聞欲別卜葬地，臣等實懷疑懼。 竊謂皇上所以遲疑者，必以今皇太后萬壽後，當與先帝同尊，自嫌二後並配，非祖宗制。 考之於古，漢文帝尊所生母薄太后，而呂後仍祔長陵。 宋仁宗追尊生母李宸妃，而劉後仍祔太廟。 今若陵廟之制稍有未合，則有乖前美，貽譏來葉。 」於是諸大臣相繼言之。 帝猶重違太后意，時偕朝臣伏文華門泣請。 帝與太后皆感動，始從時議。
In Chenghua 4 Empress Dowager Ciyi died, and the court was ordered to plan her burial. Shi, Shang Lu, and Liu Dingzhi argued, "The empress dowager was the late Emperor's lawful consort and rightful empress; you honored her before all the realm." The late Emperor kept marital propriety; you have shown filial love—both obligations are met. Her coffin should be buried with the late Emperor at Yuling and her tablet placed in the ancestral temple—that is immovable custom. We hear you may choose a separate burial site—that fills us with alarm. We suspect your hesitation stems from concern that when the present empress dowager dies, two empresses would share the late Emperor's tomb—contrary to ancestral custom. History shows that Emperor Wen honored his birth mother Empress Dowager Bo, yet Empress Lü remained enshrined at Changling. Song Renzong elevated his birth mother Consort Li, yet Empress Liu kept her place in the ancestral temple. Any deviation from proper tomb and temple rites would betray past precedent and invite posterity's scorn. " Other senior ministers spoke in turn. The Emperor still shrank from defying the empress dowager; Shi and other officials knelt weeping at Wenhua Gate to plead. Emperor and empress dowager were both moved, and he accepted Shi's counsel.
39
彗見三臺，時等言：「外廷大政固所當先，宮中根本尤為至急。 諺雲『子出多母』。 今嬪嬙眾多，維熊無兆。 必陛下愛有所專，而專寵者已過生育之期故也。 望均恩愛，為宗社大計。 」時帝專寵萬貴妃，妃年已近四十，時故云然。 又言：「大臣黜陟，宜斷自宸衷，或集群臣僉議。 不可悉委臣下，使大權旁落。 」帝雖不能從，而心嘉其忠。
When a comet appeared at Santai, Shi and others warned, "Outer-court governance matters, but the imperial succession is even more urgent." As the saying goes, "Many mothers produce many sons." The harem is crowded, yet no pregnancy has been announced. Surely Your Majesty favors one woman exclusively—and she is past childbearing age. We urge you to spread your favor equally, for the sake of the dynasty. " The Emperor then doted on Consort Wan, who was nearly forty—hence Shi's remark. He also urged, "Ministerial appointments should be decided by Your Majesty alone—or by full deliberation with the court." Don't delegate everything to subordinates and let power slip away. " The Emperor couldn't act on it, but admired his loyalty.
40
都御史項忠討滿四不利。 朝議命撫寧侯朱永將京軍往赴。 永故難其行，多所邀請。 時惡其張大，且度軍可無行，第令整裝待。 會忠馳奏，已圍賊石城。 帝遣中官懷恩、黃賜偕兵部尚書白圭、程信等至閣議。 時曰：「賊四出攻剽，鋒誠不可當。 今入石城自保，我軍圍甚固，此困獸易擒耳。 」信曰：「安知忠不退師？ 」時曰：「彼部分已定，何故自退？ 且今出師，度何時到？ 」信曰：「來春。 」時曰：「如此，益緩不及事。 事成敗，冬月決矣。 」信忿，出危言曰：「忠若敗，必斬一二人，然後出師。 」眾危之，問時何見。 曰：「觀忠疏曲折，知其能。 若聞別遣禁軍，將退避不敢任，賊不可知矣。 」時惟商輅然其言。 至冬，賊果平，人乃大服。 改吏部尚書。
Censor-in-chief Xiang Zhong's campaign against Man Si was faring poorly. The court decided to send Marquis of Funing Zhu Yong with capital troops to reinforce him. Zhu Yong deliberately dragged his feet and made numerous demands. Shi resented his arrogance and judged the army wouldn't need to march—he ordered them merely to stand ready. Xiang Zhong then reported by express dispatch that he had already besieged the rebels at Shicheng. The Emperor sent eunuchs Huai'en and Huang Ci with Minister of War Bai Gui and Cheng Xin to the Grand Secretariat to decide. Shi said, "The rebels have been raiding everywhere—their momentum was truly hard to stop." Now they've holed up in Shicheng under a tight siege—a trapped beast, easily taken. " Cheng Xin asked, "How do we know Xiang Zhong won't pull back?" " Shi replied, "His positions are set—why would he withdraw on his own?" And if we deploy now, when would reinforcements even arrive? " Cheng Xin said, "Not until next spring." " Shi said, "By then it would be far too late to matter." Victory or defeat would be settled by winter. " Cheng Xin, furious, threw out a grim prediction: "If Xiang Zhong fails, someone's head will roll before any relief force is sent." " Everyone took this as an omen and asked Shi how he read the situation. He said, "Reading Xiang Zhong's detailed reports, I know he's capable. If he learns we're sending a separate imperial force, he'll pull back and refuse command—and the rebels' fate becomes unpredictable." Only Shang Lu agreed with him. By winter the rebels were indeed crushed, and everyone came to admire Shi's judgment. He was appointed Minister of Personnel.
41
五年得疾在告。 逾三月，帝趣赴閣視事，免朝參。 是冬，無雪。 疏言：「光祿寺采辦，各城門抽分，掊克不堪。 而獻珍珠寶石者，倍估增直，漁竭帑藏。 乞革其弊，以惠小民。 」帝優詔褒納。 畿輔、山東、河南旱，請免夏稅鹽鈔，及太仆寺賠課馬。 京師米貴，請發倉儲五十萬石平糶。 並從之。 時以舊臣見倚重，遇事爭執無所避。 而是時帝怠於政，大臣希得見。 萬安同在閣，結中貴戚畹，上下壅隔，時頗懷憂。
In the fifth year he fell ill and asked for sick leave. After three months the Emperor summoned him back to the Grand Secretariat, exempting him from attending court. That winter brought no snow. He memorialized: "The Guanglu Temple's procurement, with levies at every city gate, has become extortion beyond bearing. Those offering pearls and gems inflate valuations and bleed the treasury dry. I ask that these abuses be abolished for the people's sake. The Emperor responded with a gracious edict of praise and acceptance. When drought struck the capital region, Shandong, and Henan, he asked to waive summer taxes and salt certificates, and the Taipusi's compensatory horse quotas. With grain prices soaring in the capital, he asked to release five hundred thousand piculs from granaries for sale at fair price. All were approved. As a senior minister in whom the Emperor placed great trust, Shi held nothing back in debate when issues arose. But the Emperor had grown negligent of government affairs, and senior ministers rarely gained audience. Wan An sat in the Grand Secretariat with him, cultivating ties with powerful eunuchs and imperial in-laws—court and throne were cut off from each other, and Shi grew deeply concerned.
42
七年，疾復作，乞致仕。 帝慰留之，不得去。 冬，彗復見，時言政本七事：一，毋惑佛事，糜金錢； 二，傳旨專委司禮監，毋令他人，以防詐偽； 三，延見大臣議政事； 四，近幸賜予太多，工匠冒官無紀，而重囚死徙者，法不蔽罪。 宜戒淫刑僭賞； 五，虛懷受諫，勿惡切直； 六，戒廷臣毋依違，凡政令失當，直言論奏； 七，清理牧馬草地，減退勢要莊田。 皆切中時弊。
In the seventh year his illness returned and he asked to retire. The Emperor urged him to stay and would not let him go. When the comet reappeared that winter, Shi set out seven principles of good government: First, do not be seduced by Buddhist rites that squander gold and silver; Second, route imperial orders exclusively through the Directorate of Ceremonial, not through others, to prevent forgery; Third, receive senior ministers to deliberate on state affairs; Fourth, favorites receive too many gifts; artisans hold fake offices with no oversight, while convicted felons escape proper punishment; restrain excessive punishments and improper rewards; Fifth, welcome remonstrance with an open mind; do not resent blunt counsel; Sixth, warn court officials against vacillation—whenever policy goes wrong, speak up directly; Seventh, reclaim pasturelands and reduce the estates of the powerful. Every point struck at the day's ills.
43
寧晉伯劉聚為從父太監永誠請封謚，且乞祠額，禮部執故事卻之。 帝特賜額曰「褒功」，命內閣擬封謚。 時等言：「即予永誠，將來守邊內臣皆援此陳乞，是變祖宗法自今日始。 」或言宋童貫封王，時曰：「貫封王在徽宗末年，豈盛世事耶？ 」乃寢。
Marquis of Ningjin Liu Ju petitioned for enfeoffment and a posthumous title for his uncle, the eunuch Yong Cheng, and also requested a shrine inscription—the Ministry of Rites invoked precedent and refused. The Emperor personally granted the inscription "Commending Merit" and ordered the Grand Secretariat to draft enfeoffment and a posthumous title. Shi and his colleagues argued, "Grant this to Yong Cheng and every border eunuch will cite it as precedent—we would be changing ancestral law starting today." " Someone cited Tong Guan's enfeoffment as king in the Song. Shi replied, "That was in Huizong's last years—hardly a model for a golden age, is it?" The proposal was dropped.
44
時每因災變上言，或留中，或下所司，多阻隔，悒悒不得誌。 五年以後，凡七在告，帝輒命醫就視，數遣內臣賜賚。 十一年正月，以秩滿進少保。 逾月卒，年六十。 贈太師，謚文憲。
Shi often memorialized when disasters struck, but his submissions were held back or diverted—with little effect—and he grew depressed, unable to achieve what he sought. After the fifth year he took sick leave seven times, and each time the Emperor sent physicians and eunuchs bearing gifts. In the first month of the eleventh year, his tenure complete, he was promoted to Junior Mentor. A month later he died, aged sixty. Posthumously enfeoffed as Grand Preceptor with the posthumous name Wenxian.
45
時立朝三十年，孜孜奉國，持正存大體，公退未嘗以政語子弟。 有所論薦，不使其人知。 燕居無惰容，服禦儉約，無聲樂之奉，非其義不取，有古大臣風。
Shi served at court for thirty years, devoted to the state, upright and mindful of the larger good—after work he never discussed politics with his family. When he recommended someone, he never told them. Even at home he kept a disciplined bearing; he dressed plainly, kept no musicians, took nothing unearned—he had the bearing of the great ministers of old.
46
三年，錦衣指揮盧忠令校尉上變，告上皇與少監阮浪、內使王瑤圖復位。 帝震怒，捕二人下詔獄，窮治之。 忠筮於術者同寅，寅以大義折之，且曰：「此大兇兆，死不足贖。 」忠懼，佯狂以冀免。 輅及中官王誠言於帝曰：「忠病風，無足信，不宜聽妄言，傷大倫。 」帝意少解。 乃並下忠獄，坐以他罪，降為事官立功。 殺瑤，錮浪於獄，事得不竟。
In the third year, Brocade Guard commander Lu Zhong had an officer submit an urgent report accusing the Retired Emperor, Junior Supervisor Ruan Lang, and Inner Attendant Wang Yao of plotting restoration. The Emperor flew into a rage, arrested the two men, and had them tortured in the imperial prison. Zhong consulted the diviner Tong Yin, who rebuked him on moral grounds: "This is an evil omen—death wouldn't atone for it." Terrified, Zhong feigned madness hoping to escape punishment. Lu and eunuch Wang Cheng told the Emperor, "Zhong is mad with illness—he's not credible. Don't heed this slander and violate the bonds of kinship." The Emperor's anger subsided somewhat. Zhong was also imprisoned, convicted on other charges, and demoted to serve as a common soldier to earn redemption. Wang Yao was executed and Ruan Lang imprisoned—the plot was never fully pursued.
47
太子既易，進兵部左侍郎，兼左春坊大學士如故，賜第南薰裏。 塞上腴田率為勢豪侵據，輅請核還之軍。 開封、鳳陽諸府饑民流濟寧、臨清間，為有司驅逐。 輅憂其為變，請招墾畿內八府閑田，給糧種，民皆有所歸。 鐘同、章綸下獄，輅力救得無死。 《寰宇通志》成，加兼太常卿。
After the crown prince was replaced, he was promoted to Left Vice Minister of War while retaining his post as Left Chunfang Grand Academician, and given a residence in Nanxun Lane. Fertile frontier lands had been seized by the powerful; Lu requested they be surveyed and restored to military use. Starving refugees from Kaifeng and Fengyang drifted toward Jining and Linqing, only to be driven away by local officials. Fearing unrest, Lu proposed resettling them on idle land in eight prefectures near the capital with seed grain—giving the refugees a place to settle. When Zhong Tong and Zhang Lun were imprisoned, Lu fought to save them from execution. When the Comprehensive Gazetteer of the Realm was completed, he was additionally made Grand Minister of Ceremonial.
48
景帝不豫，群臣請建東宮，不許。 將繼奏，輅援筆曰：「陛下宣宗章皇帝之子，當立章皇帝子孫。 」聞者感動。 以日暮，奏未入，而是夜石亨輩已迎復上皇。 明日，王文、于謙等被收，召輅與高谷入便殿，溫旨諭之，命草復位詔。 亨密語輅，赦文毋別具條款。 輅曰：「舊制也，不敢易。 」亨輩不悅，諷言官劾輅朋奸，下之獄。 輅上書自訴《復儲疏》在禮部，可覆驗，不省。 中官興安稍解之，帝愈怒。 安曰：「向者此輩創議南遷，不審置陛下何地。 」帝意漸釋，乃斥為民。 然帝每獨念「輅，朕所取士，嘗與姚夔侍東宮」，不忍棄之。 以忌者，竟不復用。
When Emperor Jing fell ill, ministers petitioned to name a crown prince—the Emperor refused. As the memorial continued, Lu wrote: "Your Majesty is the son of Emperor Xuan and Emperor Zhang—you should name a descendant of Emperor Zhang as heir." All who heard it were moved. Dusk came before the memorial could be submitted—and that very night Shi Heng and his faction restored the Retired Emperor. The next day Wang Wen and Yu Qian were arrested. Lu and Gao Gu were summoned to the side hall, addressed with gentle words, and ordered to draft the restoration edict. Shi Heng privately told Lu that the amnesty should not list separate charges. Lu replied, "That is established practice—I dare not change it." Shi Heng's faction was displeased and had censors impeach Lu for complicity; he was thrown into prison. Lu petitioned in his defense, noting that his Memorial on Restoring the Heir was on file at the Ministry of Rites and could be verified—the court ignored him. Eunuch Xing An tried to intercede for him, but the Emperor only grew angrier. Xing An said, "These same men once proposed retreating south—did they ever consider where that would leave Your Majesty?" The Emperor's anger gradually eased, and Lu was banished to commoner status. Yet the Emperor often thought to himself, "Lu is a scholar I chose myself—he once served the Eastern Palace with Yao Kui," and could not bring himself to discard him entirely. But enemies blocked his recall, and he was never reappointed.
49
明年，彗星見，給事中董旻、御史胡深等劾不職大臣，並及輅。 御史林誠詆輅曾與易儲，不宜用，帝不聽。 輅因求罷。 帝怒，命廷鞫諸言者，欲加重譴。 輅曰：「臣嘗請優容言者，今論臣反責之，如公論何？ 」帝悅，旻等各予杖復職。 尋進兵部尚書。 久之，進戶部。 《宋元通鑒綱目》成，改兼文淵閣大學士。 皇太子立，加太子少保，進吏部尚書。 十三年進謹身殿大學士。
The next year a comet appeared, and supervising secretary Dong Min and censor Hu Shen impeached negligent ministers—including Lu. Censor Lin Cheng attacked Lu for his role in the succession crisis and argued he should not be used—the Emperor refused to listen. Lu then asked to resign. The Emperor was angered and ordered a public trial of the accusers, intending harsh punishment. Lu said, "I have pleaded for tolerance toward critics—yet now you would punish them for attacking me. What would people think?" The Emperor was pleased; Dong Min and the others were beaten but kept their posts. He was soon promoted to Minister of War. If the throne rejects indulgence and officials shed their fear of loss, good government may be expected within days.

向者御史建白，欲令大臣入內議政，疏寢不行。 夫人主當總攬威權，親決機務。 政事早朝未決者，日禦便殿，使大臣敷奏。 言官察其邪正而糾劾之，史官直書簡冊，以示懲勸。 此前代故事，祖宗成法也，願陛下遵而行之。 若僅封章入奏，中旨外傳，恐偏聽獨任，致生奸亂，欲治化之成難矣。
Earlier a censor proposed that senior ministers enter the inner court to deliberate on policy; the memorial was shelved and never carried out. The sovereign should hold all authority in his own hands and decide critical affairs personally. When morning audience leaves business unresolved, hold court daily in the side hall and have senior ministers present their views. Censors should scrutinize right and wrong and impeach where needed; historians should record the proceedings faithfully, so that reward and reproof are plain for all to see. These are precedents of earlier dynasties and established practice of our own forebears; I pray Your Majesty will follow them. If governance consists only of sealed memorials going in and private edicts going out, partial counsel and unchecked power will breed corruption and disorder, and good government will be hard to achieve.
72
人主之德，欲其明如日月以察直枉，仁如天地以覆群生，勇如雷霆以收威柄。 故司馬光之告君，以仁明武為言，即《中庸》所謂知仁勇也。 知仁勇非學而能之哉？ 夫經莫要於《尚書》、《春秋》，史莫正於《通鑒綱目》。 陛下留心垂覽。 其於君也，既知禹、湯、文、武之所以興，又知桀、紂、幽、厲之所以替，而趨避審矣。 於馭內臣也，既知有呂強、張承業之忠，又知有仇士良、陳弘誌之惡； 於馭廷臣也，既知有蕭、曹、房、杜之良，又知有李林甫、楊國忠之奸，而用舍當矣。 如是則於知仁勇之德，豈不大有助哉。 茍徒如向者儒臣進講，誦述其善，諱避其惡，是猶恐道路之有陷阱，閉目而過之，其不至於冥行顛仆者幾何。
A ruler's virtue should be bright as the sun and moon to distinguish right from wrong, broad as Heaven and Earth in caring for the people, and fierce as thunder in reclaiming authority. When Sima Guang counseled his ruler, he spoke of benevolence, clarity, and resolve—the wisdom, benevolence, and courage described in the Doctrine of the Mean. Can wisdom, benevolence, and courage be mastered without study? Among the classics, none matters more than the Documents and the Spring and Autumn Annals; among histories, none is more authoritative than the Comprehensive Mirror in Outline. Your Majesty should read them with close attention. As a ruler, one will learn both how Yu, Tang, Wen, and Wu rose to power and how Jie, Zhou, You, and Li lost it—and one's choices will be the wiser for it. In managing inner attendants, one will know the loyalty of men like Lü Qiang and Zhang Chengye and the wickedness of men like Qiu Shiliang and Chen Hongzhi; In managing court ministers, one will know the worth of men like Xiao He, Cao Can, Fang Xuanling, and Du Ruhui and the treachery of men like Li Linfu and Yang Guozhong—and appointments will be made wisely. In this way, would it not greatly strengthen the virtues of wisdom, benevolence, and courage? If lectures merely recite the good and avoid the evil, as court scholars have done, it is like shutting one's eyes on a road full of pits—how long before one stumbles and falls?
73
今天下雖遭大創，尚如金甌之未缺。 誠能本聖學以見之政治，臣見國勢可強，仇恥可雪，兄弟之恩可全，祖宗之制可復，亦何憚而不為此。 書奏，帝優詔答之。
Though the realm has suffered a grievous wound, it remains like a golden bowl not yet cracked. If Your Majesty truly grounds governance in the sage's teaching, I believe national strength can be restored, the humiliation of defeat avenged, fraternal bonds preserved, and ancestral institutions revived—why hesitate to act? When the memorial was submitted, the emperor replied with a gracious edict of praise.
74
三年遷洗馬。 也先使者乞遣報使，帝堅不許。 定之疏引故事以請，帝下廷議，竟不果遣。 久之，遷右庶子。 天順改元，調通政司左參議，仍兼侍講。 尋進翰林學士。 憲宗立，進太常少卿，兼侍讀學士，直經筵。
In the third year he was promoted to Palace Steward. Esen's envoy asked that a return mission be dispatched; the emperor firmly refused. Dingzhi memorialized citing precedent in support of the request; the emperor referred the matter to court debate, but no envoy was sent in the end. After some time he was promoted to Right Sub-Preceptor. When the Tianshun era began, he was transferred to Left Assistant Director of the Transmission Office while retaining his post as Lecturer-in-Waiting. He was soon promoted to Hanlin Academician. When Emperor Xianzong ascended the throne, he was promoted to Vice Minister of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, Reader-in-Waiting, and lecturer at the Classics Lectures.
75
成化二年十二月，以本官入直文淵閣，進工部右侍郎，兼翰林學士。 江西、湖廣災，有司方征民賦。 定之言國儲充積，倉庾至不能容。 而此張口待哺之氓，乃責其租課，非聖主恤下意。 帝感其言，即命停征。 四年進禮部左侍郎。 萬貴妃專寵，皇后希得見，儲嗣未兆。 郕王女及笄未下嫁。 定之因久旱，並論及之。 且請經筵兼講太祖禦制諸書，斥異端邪教，勿令害政耗財。 帝留其疏不下。 五年卒官。 贈禮部尚書，謚文安。
In the twelfth month of Chenghua 2, he entered the Wenyuan Pavilion in his existing capacity, was promoted to Right Vice Minister of Works, and continued as Hanlin Academician. When Jiangxi and Huguang were stricken by disaster, local officials were still collecting taxes from the people. Dingzhi argued that the state's granaries were overflowing and could scarcely hold any more. Yet these starving people were still being taxed—hardly the conduct of a sage ruler who cares for his subjects. Moved by his words, the emperor immediately ordered the collections stopped. In the fourth year he was promoted to Left Vice Minister of Rites. Consort Wan held the emperor's exclusive favor; the empress was rarely seen; and no heir had yet been designated. The Prince of Cheng's daughter had come of age but had not yet been married. Citing the prolonged drought, Dingzhi raised these matters together in a memorial. He also asked that the Classics Lectures include the Taizu Emperor's own writings, and that heterodox cults be suppressed before they could corrupt government and drain the treasury. The emperor withheld the memorial and took no action on it. He died in office in the fifth year. He was posthumously honored as Minister of Rites, with the posthumous name Wen'an, "Cultured and Tranquil."
76
定之謙恭質直，以文學名一時。 嘗有中旨命制元宵詩，內使卻立以俟。 據案伸紙，立成七言絕句百首。 又嘗一日草九制，筆不停書。 有質宋人名字者，就列其世次，若譜系然，人服其敏博。
Dingzhi was modest, plain-spoken, and upright, and was renowned in his day for his literary accomplishments. Once, on an imperial command, he was asked to compose Lantern Festival poems while a palace eunuch stood by waiting. He spread paper at his desk and instantly produced a hundred seven-character quatrains. On another occasion he drafted nine imperial edicts in a single day without pausing. When asked to identify Song dynasty figures by name, he could instantly arrange them by generation as if reading from a family register; all marveled at his quickness and erudition.
77
贊曰：英宗之復辟也，當師旅饑饉之余，民氣未復，權奸內訌，柱石傾移，朝野多故，時事亦孔棘矣。 李賢以一身搘拄其間，沛然若有余。 獎厲人材，振飭綱紀。 迨憲、孝之世，名臣相望，猶多賢所識拔。 偉哉宰相才也。 彭時、商輅侃侃守義，盡忠獻納，粹然一出於正。 其於慈懿典禮，非所謂善成君德者歟？ 輅科名與宋王曾、宋庠埒，德望亦無愧焉。 呂原、嶽正、劉定之雖相業未優，而原之行誼，正之氣概，定之之建白，鹹有可稱，故以時次，並列於篇。
The commentator writes: When Emperor Yingzong regained the throne, the realm was still reeling from war and famine. Public morale had not recovered, powerful factions were feuding within the court, the pillars of state had been shaken, and affairs at every level were desperately strained. Li Xian bore the weight of it almost single-handedly, with strength to spare. He encouraged talent and restored order to the institutions of state. By the reigns of the Xian and Xiao emperors, distinguished ministers filled the court—many of them men he had discovered and promoted. What grand ministerial talent he possessed. Peng Shi and Shang Lu spoke with principled forthrightness, serving with loyal devotion and offering candid counsel—all of it grounded in rectitude. In the rites honoring Empress Dowager Ci, did they not exemplify what it means to help complete a ruler's moral cultivation? Shang Lu's examination rank matched those of Song Wang Ceng and Song Bao, and his moral standing was equally beyond reproach. Lu Yuan, Yue Zheng, and Liu Dingzhi did not rank among the greatest ministers, yet Yuan's integrity, Zheng's force of character, and Dingzhi's memorial counsel all merit mention; they are therefore included here in chronological order.
