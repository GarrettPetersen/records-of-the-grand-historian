何鑒馬中錫陸完洪鐘 〈 （陳鎬蔣昇）〉 陳金俞諫周南 〈 （孫祿）〉 馬昊
He Jian, Ma Zhongxi, Lu Wan, and Hong Zhong Opening bracket for supplementary biographies. (Chen Hao, Jiang Sheng)〉 Chen Jin, Yu Jian, and Zhou Nan Opening bracket for supplementary biographies. (Sun Lu)〉 Ma Hao
2
何鑒，字世光，浙江新昌人。 成化五年進士。 授宜興知縣。 征拜御史，巡宣府、大同。 劾巡撫鄭寧以下數十人不職，按裨將孟璽等罪。 還巡太倉。 總督太監卒犯法，逮治之，為所構，下錦衣獄。 得釋，再按江北。 鳳陽皇陵所在，近境取寸木，法皆死，陵軍多倚禁虐民。 鑒請以山麓為限，他樵采勿禁，遂著為令。 出為河南知府。 振累歲饑，條行荒政十事。 歷四川左、右布政使。
He Jian, whose style name was Shiguang, was a native of Xinchang in Zhejiang. In the fifth year of the Chenghua reign he passed the jinshi examination. He was appointed magistrate of Yixing. Summoned to office as a censor, he toured Xuanfu and Datong. He impeached Grand Coordinator Zheng Ning and dozens of officials under him for neglect of duty, and prosecuted the offenses of deputy generals such as Meng Xi. On his return he inspected Taicang. When soldiers under the grand coordinator's eunuch broke the law, he arrested and tried them; they framed him, and he was thrown into the Brocade Guard prison. After he was released, he again conducted inspections in the Jiangbei region. Fengyang, where the imperial tombs lay, was nearby; under the law even taking a splinter of wood from the vicinity meant death, and the tomb guards often abused the people under cover of the prohibition. He Jian asked that the mountain foothills be set as the boundary and that gathering firewood elsewhere be permitted; this was then written into law. He left office to become prefect of Henan. After years of famine he drew up ten measures for famine relief. He served in turn as left and right administrative commissioner of Sichuan.
3
弘治六年以右副都御史巡撫江南，兼理杭、嘉、湖三府稅糧。 蘇、松水災，用便宜發漕米十五萬石振之。 與侍郎徐貫疏吳淞、白茆諸渠，泄水入海，水患以除。 復巡撫山東，遷刑部侍郎。 母憂去。
In the sixth year of Hongzhi he was appointed Right Vice Censor-in-Chief and grand coordinator of Jiangnan, with charge also of the grain tax of Hangzhou, Jiaxing, and Huzhou. When Suzhou and Songjiang suffered flooding, he used his discretionary authority to release 150,000 piculs of transported grain for relief. Together with Vice Minister Xu Guan he memorialized on dredging the Wusong, Baimao, and other channels so the water could drain to the sea, and the flood disasters were thereby ended. He again served as grand coordinator of Shandong and was promoted to Vice Minister of Punishments. He left office to observe mourning for his mother.
4
十八年還朝。 時承平久，生齒日繁。 孝宗覽天下戶籍數乃視國初反減，咎所司溺職，欲厘正之。 敕鑒以故官兼左僉都御史往河南、湖廣、陜西閱實戶口。 得戶二十三萬五千有奇，口七十三萬九千有奇，因疏善後十事及軍民利病以聞。 會孝宗已崩，武宗悉采納之。
In the eighteenth year he returned to court. The realm had long been at peace, and the population grew day by day. Emperor Xiaozong reviewed the empire's household registers and found the numbers had actually fallen since the founding of the dynasty; he blamed the responsible offices for negligence and wished to set the matter right. He ordered He Jian, retaining his former rank while also serving as Left Censor-in-Chief, to go to Henan, Huguang, and Shaanxi to verify household registers. He registered more than 235,000 households and more than 739,000 persons, and memorialized ten follow-up measures together with the advantages and harms affecting soldiers and civilians. By then Emperor Xiaozong had died, but Emperor Wuzong adopted all of his recommendations.
5
正德二年拜南京兵部尚書參贊機務。 鑒前撫江南，嘗按千戶張文冕罪，文冕亡去。 至是構於劉瑾，而瑾亦嗛鑒不與通，遂坐以事連罰米。 貧不能償，奏訴獲免。
In the second year of Zhengde he was appointed Minister of War at Nanjing with concurrent participation in state deliberations. When He Jian had earlier served as grand coordinator of Jiangnan, he had prosecuted the battalion commander Zhang Wenmian for an offense; Zhang fled. Now Zhang framed him before Liu Jin, and Jin also resented He Jian for refusing to deal with him; he was therefore punished by being ordered to provide rice as a fine. Too poor to pay, he submitted a petition and was excused.
6
六年正月，召為刑部尚書。 時大盜並起，劉寵、劉宸、楊虎、劉惠、齊彥名、朱諒等亂畿輔； 方四、曹甫、藍廷瑞、鄢本恕等躪四川； 汪澄二、羅光權、王浩八、王鈺五等擾江西，皆稱王。 四方告急無虛日。 兵部尚書王敞不能辦賊。 帝既命洪鐘、陳金、馬中錫督師分討。 其年五月，罷敞，以鑒代之。 鑒乃選將練兵，錄民間材武士，令鄉聚悉樹柵浚溝，團結相救。 河南、山西兵守黃河，斷太行。 京操班軍，留守所在城邑。 每漕艘運卒一人屯河濱，護運道，通行旅。 文武大吏軼賊，請敕峻責之，而褒縣令能擊賊者。 以中錫玩寇，奏遣陸完代還，調邊將從完討賊。 賊連為邊軍所破，奔迸四出。 會中官谷大用、伏羌伯毛銳率師駐臨清，賊遂謀以十二月朔伺帝省牲南郊，乘間犯駕，先一日趨霸州。 鑒立奏聞，夜設備。 厥明，帝召問鑒。 鑒請早出安人心，遂成禮而還。 賊知有備，西掠保定諸州縣以去。 河南巡撫鄧璋請濟師，鑒言：「山東賊不及萬，官軍奚啻十倍。 緣勢要私人營充頭目，撓律攘功，失將士心。 請盡遣若屬還。 都指揮以下失事，即軍前行戮。 益調邊軍助璋。 」帝悉從之。 尋以捷書屢聞，加鑒太子少保。
In the first month of the sixth year he was summoned to serve as Minister of Punishments. At that time great bandits rose everywhere: Liu Chong, Liu Chen, Yang Hu, Liu Hui, Qi Yanming, Zhu Liang, and others threw the capital region into turmoil; Fang Si, Cao Fu, Lan Tingrui, Yan Benshu, and others overran Sichuan; Wang Cheng'er, Luo Guangquan, Wang Haoba, Wang Yuwu, and others harassed Jiangxi—all proclaiming themselves kings. Distress reports from every quarter arrived day after day without letup. Minister of War Wang Chang could not deal with the bandits. The Emperor had already ordered Hong Zhong, Chen Jin, and Ma Zhongxi to take command of armies and pursue the bandits separately. In the fifth month of that year Chang was dismissed and He Jian replaced him. He Jian thereupon selected generals and trained troops, enrolled able-bodied martial men from among the people, and ordered every village to erect stockades, dig ditches, and unite for mutual defense. Troops from Henan and Shanxi guarded the Yellow River and blocked the Taihang passes. Capital drill troops and rotation troops remained to garrison the cities where they were stationed. For each grain transport vessel one transport soldier was stationed along the riverbank to guard the transport route and keep travelers safe. When civil and military officials of every rank let the bandits slip away, he asked that the throne issue stern reprimands, while rewarding magistrates who could defeat bandits. Because Ma Zhongxi had treated the bandits lightly, he memorialized to send Lu Wan to replace him and to transfer frontier generals to follow Wan in hunting the bandits. The bandits, repeatedly broken by frontier troops, fled in all directions. When the eunuch Gu Dayong and the hereditary noble Fuqiang Bo Mao Rui led troops stationed at Linqing, the bandits resolved that on the first day of the twelfth month, when the Emperor would inspect the sacrificial animals at the Southern Suburb, they would seize the opportunity to assault the imperial procession; they therefore advanced one day early toward Bazhou. He Jian immediately memorialized the Emperor and made preparations through the night. At dawn the Emperor summoned He Jian for questioning. He Jian asked that the Emperor set out early to reassure the people; the rites were completed and he returned. Learning that preparations had been made, the bandits raided west through Baoding and other prefectures and counties before departing. Grand Coordinator Deng Zhang of Henan requested reinforcements; He Jian said, "The bandits in Shandong number fewer than ten thousand, while government troops are more than ten times as many." "Because private dependents of the powerful have been installed as commanders, they obstruct discipline and seize credit, and the officers and soldiers have lost heart." "I ask that all such persons be sent back." "Any regional commander or below who fails in action shall be executed on the spot before the army." "Further dispatch frontier troops to aid Zhang." The Emperor adopted all of this. Soon reports of victory arrived repeatedly, and He Jian was made Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent.
7
明年正月，賊突霸州，京師戒嚴。 鑒令邊兵亟邀賊，賊遁去。 賊渠楊虎、朱諒死，其黨分擾山東、河南。 鑒以山東賊劉寵、劉宸、齊彥名等，責邊將許泰、郤永、劉暉、李鋐； 以河南賊劉惠、趙鐩、刑老虎等，責邊將馮禎、時源、神周、金輔。 未幾，毛銳敗績，與大用俱召還。 鑒乃請用彭澤，與仇鉞同辦河南賊，而以山東賊專委陸完。 五月，河南賊平。 七月，山東余賊亦平。 陳金、洪鐘亦以次平江西、四川諸賊。 帝喜，加鑒太子太保，蔭子錦衣世百戶。 鑒乃上言：「群盜蕩平，民罹兵久，乞量免田租，多方振贍。 黜貪殘長吏，停不急工役。 還民故業，貸以牛種，復其家三年。 有訐舊事及怙惡者，並置於理。 」帝悉報可。
In the first month of the following year the bandits suddenly attacked Bazhou, and the capital went on alert. He Jian ordered frontier troops to intercept the bandits at once, and they fled. The bandit chiefs Yang Hu and Zhu Liang died, and their followers dispersed to harass Shandong and Henan separately. For the Shandong bandits Liu Chong, Liu Chen, Qi Yanming, and others, he held frontier generals Xu Tai, Xi Yong, Liu Hui, and Li Zhen responsible; for the Henan bandits Liu Hui, Zhao Zan, Xing Laohu, and others, he held frontier generals Feng Zhen, Shi Yuan, Shen Zhou, and Jin Fu responsible. Before long Mao Rui suffered defeat, and he and Dayong were both recalled. He Jian then requested that Peng Ze be employed, who together with Qiu Yao would handle the Henan bandits, while the Shandong bandits were entrusted solely to Lu Wan. In the fifth month the Henan bandits were pacified. In the seventh month the remaining bandits in Shandong were also pacified. Chen Jin and Hong Zhong likewise pacified the bandits of Jiangxi and Sichuan in turn. The Emperor rejoiced, made He Jian Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent, and conferred hereditary enrollment in the Embroidered Guard on his son for one hundred households. He Jian then memorialized, "The massed bandits have been swept away; the people have long suffered from warfare—I beg that land tax be remitted in measure and relief be provided by every means." "Remove corrupt and cruel senior officials; halt unurgent labor corvée." "Restore the people to their former livelihoods, lend them oxen and seed, and exempt their households for three years." "Those who bring up old grievances or cling to evil shall all be brought to justice." The Emperor approved all of this in reply.
8
先是，七月中，鑒以群盜未盡，請留邊將劉暉戍山東，時源戍河南，郤永戍畿輔，李鋐戍淮、揚，各假總兵之職，俟事寧始罷。 仇鉞言，邊軍久勞，風土不習，人馬俱病。 今賊已漸平，請留三之一討賊，余悉遣還。 廷議，二人議俱是，請四將各千人鎮壓，他將許泰、神周、金輔、溫恭輩俱統所部還邊鎮。 帝許之，命延綏軍徑還，遼東、宣府，大同軍過闕勞賜。
Earlier, in the seventh month, because the massed bandits were not yet fully gone, He Jian had asked that frontier general Liu Hui garrison Shandong, Shi Yuan Henan, Xi Yong the capital region, and Li Zhen Huaiyang, each given acting powers of regional commander until affairs were settled. Qiu Yao said the frontier armies had labored long, were unaccustomed to the climate and terrain, and both men and horses were worn out. "Now that the bandits are gradually being pacified, I ask that one-third be kept to pursue bandits and the rest all sent back." At court it was debated; both views were held correct, and it was requested that each of the four generals keep one thousand men for suppression while the other generals—Xu Tai, Shen Zhou, Jin Fu, Wen Gong, and the rest—should each lead their commands back to frontier garrisons. The Emperor consented, ordering the Yan-sui army to return directly and the Liaodong, Xuanfu, and Datong armies to pass through the capital for commendation and rewards.
9
帝時好弄兵。 群小寵幸者言邊軍憨健過京軍遠甚，宜留之京營。 帝以為然。 至十一月，三鎮軍畢至，遂命留之，以京軍往代。 鑒力陳不可，廷臣集議，復極言其害，帝竟不從。 自是，邊軍於大內團操，號為「外四家軍」，而江彬進用矣。
The Emperor at this time delighted in military exercises. Favorites among his entourage said frontier troops were far sturdier and readier than capital troops and ought to be kept in the capital camps. The Emperor thought this correct. By the eleventh month the armies of the three garrisons had all arrived, and he ordered them kept while capital troops went to replace them on the frontier. He Jian urged forcefully that this could not be done; court officials assembled for deliberation and again spoke at length of the harm, yet the Emperor in the end would not agree. From this time frontier troops drilled in formation within the inner palace, styled the "Four Outer Garrison Armies," and Jiang Bin came into favor.
10
八年，宣府送迤北降人脫脫太等至京，命充禦馬監勇士。 鑒等上言：「漢、魏徙氐、羌於關中，郭欽、江統皆勸晉武早絕亂階。 苻堅處鮮卑於漢南，苻融亦慮其窺測虛實。 今使降人出入禁中，假寵逾分，且生慢侮。 萬一北寇聞之，潛使黠賊偽降，以為間諜，寧不為將來患哉？ 」帝不聽。
In the eighth year Xuanfu sent surrendered men from the northern frontier, Tuotuotai and others, to the capital, and the Emperor ordered them enrolled as warriors of the Imperial Stud Service. He Jian and others memorialized, "In Han and Wei the Di and Qiang were moved into the Guanzhong region; Guo Qin and Jiang Tong both urged Emperor Wu of Jin to cut off the source of disorder early." "Fu Jian settled the Xianbei south of the Han River, and Fu Rong also feared they would spy out the court's weaknesses." "Now to let surrendered men enter and leave the forbidden palace, granting them favor beyond their station, will moreover breed contempt and disrespect." "Should northern invaders hear of this and secretly send cunning men to feign surrender as spies, will this not become a calamity for the future?" The Emperor would not listen.
11
寧王宸濠謀復護衛，鑒力遏之。 都督白玉以失事罷，厚賄豹房諸幸臣求復，鑒執不從。 諸幸臣嗾诇事者發鑒家僮取將校金錢，言官遂交章劾鑒，致仕去。 閱九年卒，年八十。
The Prince of Ning, Zhu Chenhao, plotted to recover his guard forces; He Jian firmly barred the way. Regional Commander Bai Yu, dismissed for failure, lavished bribes on the favored men of the leopard quarters to seek reinstatement; He Jian held firm and would not agree. The favored men incited informers to expose He Jian's household servants taking money from commanders; censorial officials then submitted memorials in succession impeaching He Jian, and he left office and retired. Nine years later he died, aged eighty.
12
馬中錫，字天祿，故城人。 父偉，為唐府長史，以直諫忤王，械送京師，而盡縲其家人。 中錫以幼免，乃奔訴巡按御史。 御史言於王，釋其家。 復奉母走京師訴冤，父竟得白，終處州知府。
Ma Zhongxi, whose style name was Tianlu, was a native of Gucheng. His father Wei served as secretary of the Tang princely establishment; after a forthright remonstrance offended the prince, he was shackled and sent to the capital while the entire family was thrown into chains. Spared on account of his youth, Zhongxi fled to appeal to the touring censor. The censor appealed to the prince, and the family was freed. Taking his mother, he rushed to the capital to seek redress; his father was ultimately exonerated and ended his career as prefect of Chuzhou.
13
中錫舉成化十年鄉試第一，明年成進士，授刑科給事中。 萬貴妃弟通驕橫，再疏斥之，再被杖。 公主侵畿內田，勘還之民。 又嘗劾汪直違恣罪。 歷陜西督學副使。
Zhongxi topped the provincial examinations in Chenghua 10; the following year he became a metropolitan graduate and was appointed supervising secretary in the Bureau of Punishments. Tong, younger brother of Consort Wan, was insufferably arrogant; Zhongxi denounced him twice in memorials and twice was beaten with the staff. When a princess seized lands within the capital region, he surveyed the holdings and restored them to the commoners. He also impeached Wang Zhi on charges of lawless arrogance. He later served as vice commissioner for education in Shaanxi.
14
弘治五年，召為大理右少卿。 南京守備太監蔣琮與兵部郎中婁性、指揮石文通相訐，連數百人，遣官按，不服。 中錫偕司禮太監趙忠等往，一訊得實。 性除名，琮下獄抵罪。 擢右副都御史，巡撫宣府。 劾罷貪耄總兵官馬儀，革鎮守以下私役軍士，使隸尺籍。 寇嘗犯邊，督軍敗之。 引疾歸，中外交薦。
In Hongzhi 5 he was summoned to serve as Right Vice Minister of the Court of Judicial Review. The Nanjing garrison eunuch Jiang Cong, Bureau of War director Lou Xing, and commander Shi Wentong had lodged mutual accusations implicating several hundred people; when investigators were sent, they refused to yield. Zhongxi went with Zhao Zhong, Director of Ceremonials, and others; one round of questioning settled the matter. Xing was struck from the rolls, and Cong was cast into prison to answer for his crimes. Promoted to Right Vice Censor-in-Chief, he was appointed grand coordinator of Xuanfu. He impeached and dismissed the grasping, aged regional commander Ma Yi, ended the private impressment of troops by the garrison command downward, and restored soldiers to the official muster rolls. When raiders crossed the border, he directed the troops and repelled them. He retired citing illness, and recommendations poured in from within and beyond the court.
15
武宗即位，起撫遼東。 還屯田於軍，而劾鎮守太監朱秀置官店、擅馬市諸罪。 正德元年入歷兵部左右侍郎。 劉瑾初得誌，其黨朱瀛冒邊功至數百人。 尚書閻仲宇許之，中錫持不可。 瑾大恚，中旨改南京工部。 明年勒致仕。 其冬，逮系詔獄，械送遼東，責償所收腐粟。 逾年事竣，斥為民。 瑾誅，起撫大同。 中錫居官廉，所至革弊任怨，以故有名。
When Emperor Wuzong took the throne, Zhongxi was recalled to serve as grand coordinator of Liaodong. He restored colony lands to the troops and impeached the garrison eunuch Zhu Xiu for operating government shops, running private horse markets, and related offenses. In Zhengde 1 he advanced through the Ministry of War as Left and Right Vice Minister. Once Liu Jin held power, his follower Zhu Ying falsely claimed frontier merit for hundreds of men. Minister Yan Zhongyu agreed, but Zhongxi refused to allow it. Jin was furious; by secret edict Zhongxi was transferred to the Nanjing Ministry of Works. The following year he was forced into retirement. That winter he was seized and held in the Imperial Prison, then shackled and sent to Liaodong to repay the spoiled grain he had collected. When the matter closed a year later, he was demoted to commoner status. After Jin's execution he was restored as grand coordinator of Datong. A scrupulously honest official, he reformed abuses wherever he served and willingly bore ill will—which is why his name endured.
16
六年三月，賊劉六等起，吏部尚書楊一清建議遣大臣節制諸道兵。 乃薦中錫為右都御史提督軍務，與惠安伯張偉統禁兵南征。
In the third month of year six the bandits Liu Six and others rose; Minister of Personnel Yang Yiqing proposed sending a senior minister to coordinate forces on every circuit. He recommended Zhongxi as Right Censor-in-Chief to supervise military affairs, with Hui'an Earl Zhang Wei commanding the capital guard on a southern expedition.
17
劉六名寵，其弟七名宸，文安人也，並驍悍善騎射。 先是，有司患盜，召寵、宸及其黨楊虎、齊彥名等協捕，頻有功。 會劉瑾家人梁洪征賄於寵等不得，誣為盜。 遣寧杲、柳尚義繪形捕之，破其家。 寵等乃投大盜張茂。 茂家高樓重屋，復壁深窖，素招亡命為逋逃主。 宦官張忠與鄰，茂結為兄，夤緣馬永成、谷大用、於經輩得出入豹房，侍帝蹴鞠，而乘間為盜如故。 後數為河間參將袁彪所敗。 茂窘，求救於忠。 忠置酒私第，招茂、彪東西坐。 酒酣，舉觴屬彪字茂曰：「彥實吾弟也，自今毋相厄。 」又舉觴屬茂曰：「袁公善爾，爾慎毋犯河間。 」彪畏忠，唯唯而已。 已，茂為寧杲所擒，寵等相率詣京謀自首。 忠與永成為請於帝，且曰：「必獻萬金乃赦。 」寵、宸不能辦，逃去。 既而瑾誅，有詔許自首。 寵等乃出詣官。 兵部奏赦之，令捕他盜自效。 寵等憚要束，未幾復叛。 黨日眾，所至，陷城殺將吏。
Liu Six was really Liu Chong; his younger brother Liu Seven was Liu Chen. Both were from Wen'an—fierce men skilled in mounted archery. Earlier, harried by bandits, local officials had enlisted Chong, Chen, and their followers Yang Hu and Qi Yanming to assist in arrests; they had repeatedly distinguished themselves. Then Liu Jin's retainer Liang Hong tried to extort bribes from Chong and, rebuffed, denounced them as bandits. Ning Gao and Liu Shangyi were sent to circulate wanted portraits; their families were destroyed. Chong and his band threw in with the great outlaw Zhang Mao. The following winter he was recalled to be censor-in-chief of the left, but went home for his mother's funeral before the order reached him. In the second month of the sixth year rebels broke out across Jiangxi. An edict restored Jin to his former rank with overall military command. Civil and military officials of the southern capital region, Zhejiang, Fujian, Guangdong, and Huguang all fell under his command. He was authorized to act at discretion and to execute regional commanders and subordinates who defied orders. At that time Fuzhou had the Dongxiang rebels Wang Yuwu, Xu Yangsan, Fu Jieyi, Jie Duan, and others; at Nanchang the Yaoyuan rebels Wang Chenger, Wang Haoba, Yin Yongshi, Hong Ruiqi, and others; at Ruizhou the Hualin rebels Luo Guangquan, Chen Fuyi, and others; while at Ganzhou the Damao Mountain rebels He Jiqin and others rose as well. Cen Meng's men demanded a thousand taels; Jin refused, and they deliberately let the rebels slip away. The craftiest rebels mostly survived; several thousand were still at large. Eager to declare victory, Jin ordered another round of amnesty talks. When the Yaoyuan rebels seemed broken, Jin rejoiced, thinking the end was a day or two away, and feasted his officers. The rebels saw the passes unguarded, bribed the native troops with everything they had, and slipped away at dusk. They had gone three days without cooking and expected to die; along the road they abandoned children and cast off women. Only at Guixi did they eat their fill, then swept through Quzhou and Huizhou. Realizing his blunder, Jin issued another amnesty offer. Rebel chiefs such as Wang Haoba had long pretended to submit only to stall the army; they raided as before, and the revolt was never fully crushed. Merit-recording supervising secretary Li Yi and remonstrance officials in both capitals impeached Jin in turn. The court recalled Jin and replaced him with Yu Jian. He was promoted to junior guardian and grand guardian of the heir apparent; his son's privilege remained unchanged. Called back to court, he fell ill on the road and returned home; the throne ordered him recalled by force. In the winter of the fourteenth year he took charge of the Censorate. Zhending officials and commoners petitioned at court to keep him, and the demotion was revoked.
59
遷四川僉事。 昊長身驍捷，善騎射，知兵。 巨寇方四、曹甫等方熾，洪鐘討之久無功。 昊至，閱所部，笑曰：「將不知兵，其何以戰？ 」於是擇健卒千人分數隊，隊立長，教之。 會甫將襲江津，昊從巡撫林俊剿賊，大敗之，俘斬及焚死者二千余人。 明年，方四陷江津，破綦江，薄重慶。 昊夜出百騎，舉火擊賊，賊驚潰。 乘之，斬獲多，遂合羅、回土兵博賊。 賊陳左而伏兵其右，昊以正兵當左，身率百騎搗其伏。 伏潰，趨左，左亦潰，四奔婺川，與甫相攻，眾遂散。 四變姓名走，為他將所獲。 昊再被獎，進副使，與總兵官楊宏擊敗甫。
He was transferred to Sichuan as vice commissioner. Tall, agile, expert with bow and horse, he understood warfare. Great rebels Fang Si and Cao Fu were at their height; Hong Zhong's long campaign had failed. On arrival Hao inspected his troops and laughed: "How can generals who know nothing of war fight?" He chose a thousand sturdy men, divided them into squads each with a leader, and trained them. When Fu threatened Jiangjin, Hao joined grand coordinator Lin Jun, routed the rebels, and killed or captured more than two thousand. Next year Fang Si took Jiangjin, stormed Qijiang, and threatened Chongqing. Hao led a hundred horsemen out at night with torches, struck the camp, and rebels scattered in panic. He pressed the pursuit, took many heads, and joined Luo and Hui tribal soldiers to finish the fight. Rebels held the left while ambushers waited on the right; Hao pinned the left with his main force and personally led a hundred cavalry against the ambush. The ambush broke; he wheeled on the left flank until it too collapsed; Si fled to Wuchuan, clashed with Fu, and the host dispersed. Si fled under a false name and was caught by another commander. Rewarded again, he was promoted to vice commissioner and with regional commander Yang Hong defeated Fu.
60
甫降，而其黨廖麻子並其眾，連陷銅梁、榮昌。 坐奪冠帶。 時洪鐘已召還，巡撫高崇熙恇怯，復主撫。 麻子等陽受約，崇熙遽罷諸軍，令副使張敏徙開縣臨江市民，空其地處之，許給復三年，為請於朝。 昊力爭，謂臨江市蜀襟喉，上達重、敘，下連湖、湘，地土饒衍，奈何棄以資賊，自遺患。 崇熙不從，昊乃益治兵觀變。 其明年，賊果執敏叛。 詔逮崇熙，而擢昊右僉都御史代之。 賊圍中江，將趨成都。 昊以五千騎與總督彭澤敗之。 遊擊閻勛追斬麻子劍州，余眾走，推其黨喻思俸為主。 總兵官陳珣追至富村，賊偽降。 因北渡江，襲殺都指揮姚震，轉入巴山故巢。 尋出走大安鎮，珣不敢前。 而陜西兵與賊戰潰，賊遂越寧羌犯略陽。 珣軍鼓噪，賊夜走，度廣元，為官軍所遏，還趨通、巴招余黨。 諸將率稱病不擊賊，詔逮珣，且讓昊。 昊乃與彭澤督諸軍獲思俸西鄉山中，復與澤平內江賊駱松祥，群盜悉靖。 錄功，進副都御史。
Fu surrendered, but lieutenant Liao Mazhi rallied the remnants and seized Tongliang and Rongchang in succession. He was stripped of his official insignia for the setback. Hong Zhong had been recalled; grand coordinator Gao Chongxi was timid and again favored appeasement. Mazhi feigned agreement; Chongxi hastily disbanded the army and ordered vice commissioner Zhang Min to evacuate Linjiang townsfolk from Kaizhou and resettle them on empty land, with a three-year tax reprieve—petitioning the court for approval. Hao argued fiercely: "Linjiang is the throat of Shu—linked upstream to Chongqing and Xuzhou, downstream to Hunan and Hubei, on fertile ground. Why surrender it to rebels and store up disaster?" Chongxi refused; Hao redoubled military preparations and watched events. Next year the rebels duly seized Zhang Min and rose in revolt. Chongxi was arrested by edict; Hao was promoted to Right Assistant Censor-in-Chief in his place. Rebels besieged Zhongjiang and marched toward Chengdu. Hao and grand coordinator Peng Ze routed them with five thousand cavalry. Guerrilla officer Yan Xun pursued and killed Mazhi at Jianzhou; survivors fled and made lieutenant Yu Sifeng their chief. Regional commander Chen Xun chased them to Fucun, where they pretended to submit. They crossed the Yangtze north, ambushed and killed regional commander Yao Zhen, and withdrew to their old Ba Mountain lair. Soon they broke for Da'an Town; Xun would not advance. Shaanxi troops engaged them and collapsed; rebels crossed into Ningqiang and raided Lüeyang. Chen Xun's men raised a tumult; rebels fled by night, were blocked at Guangyuan, and turned back toward Tongzhou and Bazhou to rally stragglers. Most generals pleaded illness and refused to fight; Xun was arrested by edict and Hao was rebuked. Hao and Peng Ze then took Sifeng in the Xixiang hills and crushed Neijiang rebel Luo Songxiang until every band was quiet. For these exploits he was promoted to Vice Censor-in-Chief.
61
十年，亦不剌寇松藩，番人磨讓六少等乘機亂，為之向導，西土大震。 昊招土番為間，發兵掩擊之。 千戶張倫等夜率熟番攻破賊，獲磨讓六少，亦不剌遁去。 昊以松潘地險阻，番人往往邀劫饋運，乃督參將張傑等修築墻柵，自三舍堡至風洞關，凡五十里。 賜敕褒之。
In the tenth year Ibars raided Songpan; Tibetans led by Mo Rang Liushao rose as guides, and the west was shaken. Hao enlisted tribal Tibetans as agents and launched a surprise attack. Battalion commander Zhang Lun led allied Tibetans in a night assault, captured Mo Rang Liushao, and Ibars escaped. Songpan's terrain invited ambush on supply lines, so Hao had assistant commander Zhang Jie build fifty li of wall from Sanshe Fort to Fengdong Pass. The throne sent a patent of commendation.
62
烏蒙、芒部二府壤接筠連、珙縣，圍亙千里，山箐深阻。 諸蠻僰人子、羿子、仲家子、苗子、倮、佫等雜居其中。 有僰人子普法惡者，通漢語，曉符錄。 妄言彌勒出世，自稱蠻王，煽諸夷作亂。 流民謝文禮、謝文義應之。 都指揮杜琮戰敗，文義奪其胄。 十二年，昊督指揮曹昱進討，法惡敗，走保青山寨。 昊分據水口，絕其汲道，闕南方圍待之。 賊乏水渴，突南圍，官軍遮擊。 法惡中流矢死，諸蠻大奔。 以功，再進右都御史，蔭子錦衣世百戶。
Wumeng and Mangbu adjoined Junlian and Gong across a thousand li of deep mountain ravines. Bo, Yi, Zhongjia, Miao, Luo, Gelao, and other tribes lived intermingled there. A Bo tribesman named Pufa'e spoke Chinese and knew ritual talismans. He proclaimed the coming of Maitreya, styled himself Barbarian King, and stirred the tribes to revolt. Vagabonds Xie Wenli and Xie Wenyi joined him. Regional commander Du Cong was defeated and Wenyi took his helmet. In the twelfth year Hao led commander Cao Yu against him; Pufa'e was beaten back to Green Mountain Fort. Hao seized the water source, cut supply, left the south open as bait, and waited in siege. Rebels, parched, charged the southern gap; government troops intercepted them. Pufa'e fell to an arrow; the tribes scattered in rout. For this he was again made Right Censor-in-Chief and his son ennobled as hereditary Embroidered Guard commander for a hundred households.
63
昊有才氣，能應變，揮霍自喜，所向輒有功。 然官川中久，狎其俗，銳意立功名，卒以是敗。 先是，亦不剌既遁，昊移兵攻小東路番寨未下，茂州群蠻懼見侵，遂糾生苗圍城堡。 參將芮錫等討之，兵敗，指揮龐昇等皆死。 又嘗遣副總兵張傑、副使吳澧擊松潘南北二路番，不利，亡軍士三千余人，匿不以聞。 僰蠻平，不置戍守，遽班師。 請改高縣為州，設長吏，增高、珙、筠連田租千八百石，令指揮魏武度田奪降人業給之軍民。 而珙縣知縣步梁窺昊意，誘殺降人阿尚。 杜琮以亡胄故，怨文義，潛使人購其頭。 於是文義乘群蠻怨，嗾之，遂大訌。 攻高、慶符二縣，破其城。 琮率兵禦之，又敗，死傷七百人。 自黎雅以西，天全六番皆相繼亂。 南京給事中孫懋暨巡按御史盧雍、黎龍先後劾昊。 十四年遂遣官逮昊。 行至河南，疏稱疾篤，留於家。 世宗即位，始就逮，尋削籍歸。 楊一清、胡世寧薦之，為桂萼所駁而止。 久之，卒。
Talented and resourceful, spendthrift and self-satisfied, he won wherever he was sent. Yet long service in Sichuan had made him too familiar with local ways and too hungry for glory—and that ruined him. Earlier, after Ibars's escape, Hao attacked eastern-route Tibetan camps without success; Maozhou tribes feared invasion and rallied Miao allies to besiege government forts. Assistant commander Rui Xi's counterattack failed; commander Pang Sheng and others were killed. He also sent deputy commander Zhang Jie and vice commissioner Wu Li against northern and southern Songpan Tibetans; they lost more than three thousand men—losses he concealed. After the Bo revolt he withdrew without leaving garrisons. He proposed elevating Gaoxian to a prefecture, appointing a chief magistrate, raising rents in Gao, Gong, and Junlian by eighteen hundred shi, and ordering commander Wei Wu to survey fields and distribute surrendered lands to troops and settlers. Gong magistrate Bu Liang, reading Hao's mind, lured and killed the surrendered tribesman A Shang. Du Cong, still angry over his stolen helmet, secretly offered a price for Wenyi's head. Thereupon Wen Yi, seizing on the tribes' resentment, incited them, and a great uprising broke out. They attacked Gao and Qingfu counties and stormed their cities. Du Cong led troops to resist them but was defeated again, with seven hundred dead and wounded. From Liya westward, the six tribes of Tianquan all rose in disorder one after another. Nanjing supervising secretary Sun Mao and touring censors Lu Yong and Li Long impeached Hao in succession. In the fourteenth year officials were dispatched to arrest Hao. When he reached Henan he memorialized that his illness was grave and was allowed to remain at home. When Emperor Shizong took the throne he was at last taken into custody, and soon was struck from the rolls and sent home. Yang Yiqing and Hu Shining recommended him, but Gui E objected and the matter went no further. After a long while he died.
64
贊曰：何鑒綰中樞，能任諸將滅賊，蓋其時楊廷和在政府，閣部同心，故克奏效雲爾。 馬中錫雅負時望，而軍旅非其所長，適用取敗。 然觀劉宸阻降之言，亦可以觀朝事矣。 陸完交結之罪浮於首功，得從八議，有佚罰焉。 洪鐘、陳金威略甚著，而土兵之謠，聞之心惻，斯又統戎旃者所當留意也。
The appraiser says: He Jian held the central command and was able to entrust his generals to destroy the bandits—largely because Yang Tinghe was then in government and the court and ministries acted as one; that is how success was won. Ma Zhongxi bore fine repute in his day, but military affairs were not his strength; put to the wrong use, he met defeat. Yet Liu Chen's words refusing surrender also reveal much about the state of the court. Lu Wan's crime of forming illicit connections outweighed his chief merit; he was allowed the benefit of the Eight Mitigations—his punishment was lenient. Hong Zhong and Chen Jin were highly distinguished in martial strategy, yet the ballad of the native troops moves the heart to pity on hearing it—this too is what those who command armies ought to bear in mind.
