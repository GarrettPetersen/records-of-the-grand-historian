熊概 〈 （葉春）〉 陳鎰李儀 〈 （丁璿）〉 陳泰李棠 〈 （曾翚）〉 賈銓王宇崔恭劉孜 〈 （宋傑邢宥）〉 李侃 〈 （雷復李綱）〉 原傑彭誼牟俸夏壎 〈 （子鍭）〉 高明楊繼宗
Xiong Gai [ (Ye Chun)] Chen Yi and Li Yi [ (Ding Xuan)] Chen Tai and Li Tang [ (Zeng Hui)] Jia Quan, Wang Yu, Cui Gong, and Liu Zi [ (Song Jie and Xing You)] Li Kan [ (Lei Fu and Li Gang)] Yuan Jie, Peng Yi, Mou Feng, and Xia Xun [ (his son Yu)] Gao Ming and Yang Jizong
2
熊概，字元節，豐城人。 幼孤，隨母適胡氏，冒其姓。 永樂九年進士。 授御史。 十六年擢廣西按察使。 峒溪蠻大出掠，布政使議請靖江王兵遏之。 概不可，曰：「吾等居方面，寇至無捍禦，顧煩王耶？ 且寇必不至，戒嚴而已。 」已而果然。 久之，調廣東。
Xiong Gai, whose style was Yuanjie, came from Fengcheng. Left fatherless as a boy, he went with his mother when she married into the Hu clan and bore their surname. He received his jinshi degree in Yongle 9 (1411). He was made an investigating censor. In Yongle 16 (1418) he was promoted to Guangxi surveillance commissioner. When the Dongxi tribes raided on a large scale, the provincial administration commissioner proposed calling on the Prince of Jingjiang's troops to stop them. Gai objected, saying, "We hold posts in the provinces—if bandits come and we cannot defend, must we trouble the prince? Besides, the raiders will surely not come; we need only stand on alert." Events proved him right. After some years he was transferred to Guangdong.
3
洪熙元年正月，命以原官與布政使周幹、參政葉春巡視南畿、浙江。 初，夏原吉治水江南還，代以左通政趙居任，兼督農務。 居任不恤民，歲以豐稔聞。 成祖亦知其誣罔。 既卒，左通政嶽福繼之，庸懦不事事。 仁宗監國時，嘗命概以御史署刑部，知其賢，故有是命。 是年八月，幹還，言有司多不得人，土豪肆惡，而福不任職。 宣宗召福還，擢概大理寺卿，與春同往巡撫。 南畿、浙江設巡撫自此始。
In the first month of Hongxi 1 (1425), retaining his existing rank, he was ordered with Zhou Gan, administration commissioner, and Ye Chun, administration vice commissioner, to inspect the Southern Metropolitan Region and Zhejiang. Earlier, when Xia Yuanji had finished his Jiangnan waterworks and returned, he was replaced by Zhao Juren, left assistant commissioner of the Ministry of Works, who also oversaw agricultural affairs. Juren neglected the people yet year after year reported bumper harvests. Emperor Chengzu knew as well that he was lying. When Juren died, Yue Fu succeeded him as left assistant commissioner—timid and unwilling to attend to business. While the heir apparent was supervising the realm, he had once had Gai serve at the Ministry of Punishments in his capacity as censor and knew him to be able; hence this order. That year in the eighth month Gan returned and reported that many local officials were incompetent, powerful local bullies ran wild, and Fu was not doing his job. Emperor Xuande recalled Fu and promoted Gai to chief minister of the Court of Judicial Review; he and Ye Chun then went out as grand coordinators. Grand coordinators for the Southern Metropolitan Region and Zhejiang date from this appointment.
4
浙西豪持郡邑短長為不法。 海鹽民平康暴橫甚，御史捕之，遁去。 會赦還，益聚黨八百余人。 概捕誅之。 已，悉捕豪惡數十輩，械至京，論如法。 於是奸宄帖息。 諸衛所糧運不繼，軍乏食。 概以便宜發諸府贖罪米四萬二千余石贍軍，乃聞於朝。 帝悅，諭戶部勿以專擅罪概。 概用法嚴，奸民憚之，騰謗書於朝。 宣德二年，行在都御史劾概與春所至作威福，縱兵擾民。 帝弗問，陰使御史廉之，無所得。 由是益任概。 明年七月賜璽書獎勵。 概亦自信，諸當興革者皆列以聞。 時屢遣部官至江南造紙、市銅鐵。 概言水澇民饑，乞罷之。
In western Zhejiang, great clans used knowledge of local officials' failings to break the law with impunity. The salt laborer Ping Kang was wildly brutal; a censor seized him, but he escaped. After an amnesty restored him, he mustered a band of more than eight hundred men. Gai seized and executed him. He then arrested dozens of powerful wrongdoers, shackled them to the capital, and punished them as the law required. Wickedness and crime were brought to heel. Grain shipments to the guard units fell behind, and the soldiers went hungry. Gai on emergency authority drew more than forty-two thousand piculs of commutation grain from the prefectures to provision the troops, then reported to the throne. The Emperor was pleased and told the Ministry of Revenue not to charge Gai with overstepping his authority. Because Gai applied the law harshly, criminals feared him and sent libelous memorials to the capital. In Xuande 2 (1427), the acting censor-in-chief at the capital impeached Gai and Ye Chun for lording it over the regions they visited and allowing troops to harass the people. The Emperor did not inquire further but quietly sent censors to look into it; they found nothing. Thereafter he relied on Gai all the more. In the seventh month of the next year he received an imperial commendation bearing the seal. Gai grew confident as well and memorialized on every matter that should be reformed or abolished. At the time officials from the ministries were repeatedly sent to Jiangnan to make paper and buy copper and iron. Gai reported that floods had left the people in distress and asked that the work be halted.
5
五年還朝，始復姓。 亡何，遷右都御史，治南院事。 行在都御史顧佐疾，驛召概代領其職，兼署刑部。 九年十月錄囚，自朝至日宴，未暇食，忽風眩卒。 賜祭，給舟歸其喪。
In the fifth year he returned to the capital and finally took back his own surname. Soon after he was made right censor-in-chief and directed the southern branch of the censorate. When the acting censor-in-chief Gu Zuo fell ill, Gai was summoned by express relay to head the office and also serve at the Ministry of Punishments. In the tenth month of the ninth year, as he was reviewing prisoners from morning until the evening feast without time to eat, he was suddenly seized by vertigo and died. The court granted funeral sacrifices and sent a boat to carry his coffin home.
6
概性剛決，巡視江南，威名甚盛。 及掌臺憲，聲稱漸損於初。
Gai was firm and resolute by nature; on his tours of Jiangnan his authority was immense. Once he headed the censorate, his renown gradually fell short of what it had been at first.
7
葉春者，海鹽人。 起家掾吏，歷禮部郎中兩淮鹽運使，改四川右參政。 與概巡撫江、浙諸府。 既復奉命與錦衣指揮任啟、御史賴英、太監劉寧巡視。 先後凡三蒞浙西，治事於鄉，人無議其私者。 概遷都御史。 春同日進刑部右侍郎。 卒於官。
Ye Chun was a native of Haiyan. He began as a clerk, rose to director in the Ministry of Rites and commissioner of salt transport for the two Huai circuits, and was transferred to right administration vice commissioner of Sichuan. With Gai he grand-coordinated the Jiangsu and Zhejiang prefectures. He was again ordered, with Jin Yi guard commander Ren Qi, investigating censor Lai Ying, and eunuch Liu Ning, to conduct an inspection tour. Three times in succession he came to western Zhejiang and handled affairs in his home region, yet no one accused him of favoritism. When Gai was made censor-in-chief, Chun was promoted the same day to right vice minister of the Ministry of Punishments. He died in office.
8
陳鎰，字有戒，吳縣人。 永樂十年進士。 授御史。 遷湖廣副使，歷山東、浙江，皆有聲。
Chen Yi, whose style was Youjie, came from Wu county. He passed the jinshi examinations in Yongle 10 (1412). He was made an investigating censor. He was transferred to vice commissioner of Huguang and served in Shandong and Zhejiang, earning a reputation in each post.
9
九年春進右都御史，鎮守如故。 秦中饑，乞蠲租十之四，其余米布兼收。 時瓦剌也先漸強，遣人授罕東諸衛都督喃哥等為平章，又置甘肅行省名號。 鎰以聞，請嚴為之備。 已，命與靖遠伯王驥巡視甘肅、寧夏、延綏邊務，聽便宜處置。 以災沴頻仍，條上撫安軍民二十四事，多議行。
In the spring of the ninth year he was promoted to right censor-in-chief while continuing to command the garrison as before. With famine in Shaanxi, he asked that one-fourth of the land tax be remitted and the rest collected in grain and cloth. At the time the Oirat leader Esen was growing stronger; he sent men to invest Nangor and other commanders of the Han East guard with the title of pingzhang and even established the name of a Gansu provincial administration. Yi reported it and urged strict precautions. He was soon ordered, with Jingyuan Earl Wang Ji, to inspect border affairs in Gansu, Ningxia, and Yan-sui with discretionary powers. With disasters recurring, he submitted twenty-four measures to pacify soldiers and civilians; most were adopted.
10
鎰嘗恐襄、漢間流民嘯聚為亂，請命河南、湖廣、陜西三司官親至其地撫恤之。 得旨允行，而當事者不以為意。 王文亦相繼力言有司怠忽，恐遺禍。 至成化時，乃有項忠之役，人益思鎰言。
Yi had once feared that refugees between the Xiang and Han rivers might band together in revolt and asked that officials of the Henan, Huguang, and Shaanxi administrations go in person to comfort them. The throne approved, but those responsible paid no attention. Wang Wen likewise pressed repeatedly that local officials were slack and that calamity might follow. Only in the Chenghua reign did Xiang Zhong's campaign take place, and people valued Yi's warning all the more.
11
英宗北狩，景帝監國，鎰合大臣廷論王振。 於是振侄王山伏誅。 也先將入犯，以于謙薦，出撫畿內。 事寧，召還，進左都御史。
When Emperor Yingzong was carried off on the northern campaign, Emperor Jingdi supervised the realm; Yi joined the senior ministers in court debate on Wang Zhen. Wang Zhen's nephew Wang Shan was then executed. As Esen was about to invade, on Yu Qian's recommendation Yi was sent out to pacify the capital region. When peace returned he was recalled and made left censor-in-chief.
12
景泰二年，陜西饑，軍民萬余人，「願得陳公活我。 」監司以聞，帝復命之。 鎰至是凡三鎮陜，先後十余年，陜人戴之若父母。 每還朝，必遮道擁車泣。 再至，則歡迎數百里不絕。 其得軍民心，前後撫陜者莫及也。
In Jingtai 2 (1451), with famine in Shaanxi, more than ten thousand soldiers and civilians pleaded, "We wish Lord Chen would keep us alive." The supervising officials reported it, and the Emperor again sent him out. By then Yi had governed Shaanxi three times in all, for more than ten years altogether; the people of Shaanxi revered him as parents. Whenever he returned to the capital they blocked the road, pressed around his carriage, and wept. When he came again, they welcomed him for hundreds of li without end. In winning the hearts of troops and people, no governor of Shaanxi before or after equaled him.
13
三年春召還，加太子太保，與王文並掌都察院。 文威嚴，諸御史畏之若神。 鎰性寬恕，少風裁，譽望損於在陜時。 明年秋以疾致仕。 卒，贈太保，謚僖敏。 天順七年，詔官其子伸為刑部照磨。
In the spring of the third year he was recalled, made Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent, and shared direction of the censorate with Wang Wen. Wen was stern and imposing; the censors feared him like a god. Yi was forgiving by nature and exercised little discipline; his standing fell short of what it had been in Shaanxi. In the autumn of the next year he retired because of illness. After his death he was posthumously made Grand Guardian and given the posthumous title Ximin. In Tianshun 7 (1463) an edict appointed his son Shen registrar of the Ministry of Punishments.
14
李儀，涿人。 永樂間以薦舉授戶部主事。 宣宗既平高煦，義請去趙王護衛。 尚書張本亦言：「往歲孟賢謀逆，趙王未必不知。 高煦亦謂與趙合謀。 儀言是。 」帝不聽。 既而言者益眾。 帝封其詞，遣使諭王如儀指。 王即獻護衛，趙卒無事。 儀尋出知九江府，有惠政。
Li Yi came from Zhuo. In the Yongle reign he was appointed a principal clerk in the Ministry of Revenue by recommendation. Once Emperor Xuande had put down Gao Xu, Yi asked that the Prince of Zhao's guard be removed. Minister Zhang Ben also said, "When Meng Xian plotted rebellion in earlier years, the Prince of Zhao may well have known. Gao Xu likewise claimed he had plotted with Zhao. Yi agreed. The Emperor would not hear of it. Before long more and more officials spoke up. The Emperor sealed up their memorials and sent envoys to instruct the prince along the lines Yi had proposed. The prince immediately surrendered his guard, and Zhao was spared further trouble. Yi was soon sent out as prefect of Jiujiang, where his rule was benevolent.
15
英宗即位之歲，始設諸邊巡撫。 僉都御史丁璿方督大同、宣府軍儲，而儀以右僉都御史巡撫其地，盛有所建置。 明年請以大同東西二路分責於總兵官羅文、方政。 從之。 時朝議遣方政、楊洪出塞，與甘肅將蔣貴、史昭合擊朵兒只伯。 儀言：「四裔為患，自古有之，在備禦有方耳。 和寧殘部，窮無所歸，乍臣乍叛，小為邊寇。 邊將謹待之，將自遁，何必窮兵。 萬一乘虛襲我，少有失，適足為笑，乞敕政等無窮追。 」不納。
In the year Yingzong came to the throne, frontier grand coordinators were established for the first time. While Ding Xuan, vice censor-in-chief, was supervising military grain at Datong and Xuanfu, Yi served as right vice censor-in-chief grand coordinator there and accomplished a great deal. The next year he asked that Datong's eastern and western routes be divided between supreme commanders Luo Wen and Fang Zheng. The court agreed. At the time the court debated sending Fang Zheng and Yang Hong beyond the passes to join Gansu generals Jiang Gui and Shi Zhao in a joint strike on Dorjiban. Yi said, "Barbarians on the four sides have troubled China since antiquity; what matters is sound defense. The Hening remnant, with nowhere left to flee, now submits and now rebels, raiding the border in small bands. If frontier generals treat them carefully, they will withdraw on their own—why exhaust the army? If they should strike when we are exposed, the least setback would make us a laughingstock; I beg that Zheng and the others be ordered not to pursue to the end." The advice was not accepted.
16
督糧參政劉璉不職，儀劾之。 璉乃誣儀淫亂事。 適參將石亨欲奏鎮守中官郭敬罪，先咨儀。 儀誤緘咨牒於核餉主事文卷中，戶部以聞，致亨、敬相奏訐。 詔儀、璉自陳，而切責敬等。 璉止停俸二歲。 儀雖引罪，自負其直，詞頗激，遂被劾下吏瘐死。 正統二年二月也。 儀居官廉謹，邊人素德之。 聞其死，建昭德祠以祀。
Liu Lian, the grain-supervising administration vice commissioner, was incompetent, and Yi impeached him. Lian then falsely accused Yi of sexual misconduct. At that time participating general Shi Heng wished to memorialize against garrison eunuch Guo Jing and first consulted Yi. Yi mistakenly sealed the consultation among the papers of the chief clerk who audited provisions; the Ministry of Revenue reported it, and Heng and Jing accused each other. An edict ordered Yi and Lian to explain themselves while sharply rebuking Jing and the others. Lian was only fined two years' salary. Though Yi admitted fault, confident in his integrity his words were sharp; he was impeached, sent to the law officers, and died in prison of illness. This was in the second month of Zhengtong 2 (1437). In office Yi was honest and careful, and the frontier people had long respected him. When they heard of his death they built the Zhaode Shrine in his honor.
17
丁璿，上元人。 永樂中進士。 由御史擢居是職。 正統五年將征麓川，命乘傳往備儲餉。 尋言用兵便宜，遂命撫雲南。 麓川平，召為左副都御史，所至有聲。
Ding Xuan came from Shangyuan. He passed the jinshi examinations in the Yongle reign. He rose from investigating censor to this post. In Zhengtong 5 (1440), as an expedition against Luchuan was planned, he was ordered to travel by express relay to prepare stores and provisions. Soon he spoke on military expedients and was ordered to pacify Yunnan. When Luchuan was pacified he was recalled as left vice censor-in-chief; wherever he went he won renown.
18
陳泰，字吉亨，光澤人。 幼從外家曹姓，既貴，乃復故。 舉鄉試第一，除安慶府學訓導。
Chen Tai, whose style was Jiheng, came from Guangze. As a boy he took his mother's Cao surname; once he rose high he resumed his own. He placed first in the provincial examinations and was made instructor at the Anqing prefectural school.
19
正統初，廷臣交薦，擢御史，巡按貴州。 官軍征麓川，歲取土兵二千為鄉導，戰失利，輒殺以冒功，泰奏罷之。 再按山西。 時百官俸薄，折鈔又不能即得。 泰上章乞量增祿廩，俾足養廉，然後治贓汙，則貪風自息。 事格不行。 六年夏言：「連歲災異，咎在廷臣，請敕御史給事中糾彈大臣，去其尤不職者，而後所司各考核其屬。 」帝從之。 於是御史馬謹等交章劾吏部尚書郭琎等數十人。 已，復出按山東。 泰素勵操行，好搏擊。 三為巡按，懲奸去貪，威棱甚峻。
At the start of Zhengtong, court officials jointly recommended him; he was made investigating censor and sent to inspect Guizhou. When government troops campaigned against Luchuan they took two thousand native soldiers yearly as guides; when battles went badly they killed them to claim merit—Tai memorialized to stop this. He inspected Shanxi again. At the time official salaries were meager, and paper-money conversion could not be obtained promptly. Tai memorialized asking that salaries and grain be increased enough to sustain integrity; only then, he said, should corruption be punished, and greedy ways would cease of themselves. The proposal was blocked and not carried out. In the summer of the sixth year he said, "Disasters have come year after year; the fault lies with court ministers. I ask that censors and supervising secretaries be ordered to impeach the senior ministers, remove the most derelict, and only then let each office examine its subordinates." The Emperor agreed. Thereupon investigating censor Ma Jin and others submitted successive memorials impeaching Minister of Personnel Guo Jin and several dozen others. Before long he went out again to inspect Shandong. Tai had always cultivated his conduct and liked to take bold action. Three times as inspecting censor he punished the wicked and removed the greedy; his authority was very stern.
20
九年超擢四川按察使，與鎮守都御史寇深相失。 十二年八月，參議陳敏希深指，劾泰擅杖武職，毆輿夫至死。 逮刑部獄，坐斬。 泰奏辯，大理卿俞士悅亦具狀以聞。 皆不聽。
In the ninth year he was promoted over the regular steps to Sichuan surveillance commissioner and fell out with garrison censor-in-chief Kou Shen. In the eighth month of the twelfth year, administration vice commissioner Chen Min, currying favor with Shen, impeached Tai for arbitrarily beating military officers and beating a sedan bearer to death. He was seized and sent to the Ministry of Punishments and sentenced to decapitation. Tai memorialized in his defense; Court of Judicial Review chief minister Yu Shiyue also submitted a full report. Neither was heeded.
21
景帝監國，赦復官。 于謙薦守紫荊關。 也先入犯，關門不守，復論死。 景帝宥之，命充為事官，從總兵官顧興祖築關隘自效。 景泰元年擢大理右少卿，守備白羊口。 四月，都督同知劉安代寧遠伯任禮巡備涿、易、真、保諸城，命泰以右僉都御史參其軍務。 三年兼巡撫保定六府。 尋命督治河道。 自儀真至淮安，浚渠百八十里，塞決口九，築壩三，役六萬人，數月而畢。 七年移撫蘇、松。
When Emperor Jingdi supervised the realm, an amnesty restored his office. On Yu Qian's recommendation he was ordered to defend Zijing Pass. When Esen invaded, the pass was not held and he was again sentenced to death. Emperor Jingdi pardoned him and ordered him to serve on probation, following supreme commander Gu Xingzu in building passes and forts to redeem himself. In Jingtai 1 (1450) he was promoted right vice minister of the Court of Judicial Review and ordered to defend Baiyang Pass. In the fourth month, when Vice Commissioner-in-chief Liu An replaced Ningyuan Earl Ren Li in patrolling Zhuo, Yi, Zhen, Bao, and the other cities, Tai was ordered as right vice censor-in-chief to join the military staff. In the third year he was also made grand coordinator of Baoding's six prefectures. Soon he was ordered to supervise river works. From Yizhen to Huai'an he dredged channels for one hundred and eighty li, blocked nine breaches, built three dams, and employed sixty thousand men, finishing in several months. In the seventh year he was transferred to grand coordinator of Suzhou and Songjiang.
22
天順改元，罷巡撫官，改廣東副使，以憂去。 四川盜起，有言泰嘗蒞其地，有威名，乃復故官，往巡撫。 八年進右副都御史，總督漕運兼巡撫淮、揚諸府。 蒞淮三年，謝政歸。 成化六年卒。
When the Tianshun reign began, grand coordinator posts were abolished; he was made Guangdong vice commissioner and left on mourning. When bandits rose in Sichuan, some said Tai had once served there and had a formidable reputation; he was restored to his former rank and sent to grand-coordinate. In the eighth year he was advanced to right vice censor-in-chief, director-general of grain transport, and grand coordinator of Huai and Yang prefectures. After three years at Huai he retired and returned home. He died in Chenghua 6 (1470).
23
李棠，字宗楷，縉雲人。 宣德五年進士。 授刑部主事，為尚書魏源所器。 金濂代源，以剛嚴懾下。 棠與辯論是非，譴訶不為動，濂亦器之。 進員外郎。 錄囚南畿，多所平反，進郎中。 景帝嗣位，超擢本部侍郎。 未幾，巡撫廣西，提督軍務。 所部多寇，棠以次討平之。 正己帥下，令行政舉。
Li Tang, whose style was Zongkai, came from Jinyun. He passed the jinshi examinations in Xuande 5 (1430). He was appointed a principal clerk in the Ministry of Punishments and was valued by Minister Wei Yuan. When Jin Lian replaced Yuan, he intimidated his subordinates with harsh severity. Tang debated right and wrong with him; though rebuked and scolded he did not waver, and Lian came to respect him as well. He was promoted to vice director. While reviewing prisoners in the Southern Metropolitan Region he reversed many wrongful convictions and was promoted to director. When Emperor Jingdi succeeded, he was promoted over the regular steps to vice minister of the ministry. Before long he grand-coordinated Guangxi and directed military affairs. His jurisdiction had many bandits; Tang suppressed them in succession. He rectified himself and led his subordinates, ordering that administration be carried out.
24
景泰三年，思明土知府黃老，子鈞嗣。 庶兄矰使其子殺父子，滅其家，而以他盜為亂告。 棠檄右參政曾翚副使劉仁宅按其事。 翚等誘執矰父子下之獄。 矰窘則遣使走京師，上書請帝廢太子立己子。 帝大喜，立擢矰都督同知，出其子於獄。 事具《懷獻太子》及《土司傳》。 棠既不得竟黃矰獄，郁郁累疏謝病歸。 不攜嶺表一物，以清節顯。
In Jingtai 3 (1452), Huang Lao, native prefect of Siming, was succeeded by his son Jun. A cousin Zeng had his son kill father and son, exterminate the family, and report other bandits as the culprits. Tang sent a dispatch ordering right administration vice commissioner Zeng Hui and surveillance vice commissioner Liu Renzhai to investigate. Hui and the others lured Zeng and his son into custody and sent them to prison. Hard pressed, Zeng sent an envoy to the capital memorializing that the Emperor depose the heir apparent and establish his own son. The Emperor was greatly pleased, immediately promoted Zeng to vice commissioner-in-chief, and released his son from prison. The affair is given in full in the biographies of the Deposed Heir Apparent and the Native Offices. Unable to bring the Huang Zeng case to completion, Tang repeatedly memorialized to resign on grounds of illness and returned home. He took not a single thing from the Lingnan region and was known for his integrity.
25
曾翚，字時升，泰和人。 宣德八年進士。 治秦府永興王葬，卻有司饋遺。 歷刑部員外郎。 尚書金濂器之，俾典奏牘。 有重獄，諸郎不能決，輒以屬翚。 秦王訐巡撫陳鎰狎妓。 翚按得其情，劾藩府誣大臣，鎰得白。
Zeng Hui, whose style was Shisheng, came from Taihe. He passed the jinshi examinations in Xuande 8 (1433). He oversaw the burial of the Qin prince's Yongxing heir and refused gifts from the relevant offices. He served as vice director in the Ministry of Punishments. Minister Jin Lian valued him and had him manage memorials. When there was a weighty case the various directors could not decide, it was always assigned to Hui. The Prince of Qin accused grand coordinator Chen Yi of consorting with prostitutes. Hui investigated and obtained the facts, impeaching the princely establishment for slandering a great minister, and Yi was cleared.
26
正統十三年進郎中。 以何文淵薦，擢廣西右參政。 李棠檄翚及副使劉仁宅按黃矰父子。 矰使人持千金賄於道，且擁精兵挾之。 二人佯許諾，已，誘執矰下之獄。 棠以聞。 未幾，矰以上書擢都督同知，父子俱出獄，翚等太息而已。 尋以憂去。 服闋，起官河南御史。 清軍者利得軍，多枉及民，翚辨釋甚眾。 南陽諸府多流戶，眾議驅逐，人情惶急，翚與巡撫撫安之。
In Zhengtong 13 (1448) he was promoted to director. On He Wenyuan's recommendation he was made right administration vice commissioner of Guangxi. Li Tang dispatched Hui and surveillance vice commissioner Liu Renzhai to investigate Huang Zeng and his son. Zeng sent men to offer a thousand taels of gold as bribes along the road and brought elite troops to intimidate them. The two pretended to agree; then they lured Zeng into custody and sent him to prison. Tang reported it to the throne. Before long Zeng, by memorializing, was promoted to vice commissioner-in-chief; father and son were both released, and Hui and the others could only sigh. Soon he left on mourning. When mourning ended he was recalled as Henan investigating censor. Those who cleared troops profited by seizing soldiers and often wrongfully implicated civilians; Hui exonerated a great many. The Nanyang prefectures had many displaced households; many proposed expelling them and the people were in panic; Hui, with the grand coordinator, pacified and settled them.
27
天順五年遷山東右布政使。 民墾田無賦者，奸民指為閑田，獻諸戚畹。 部使者來勘，翚曰：「祖制，民墾荒田，永不科稅，奈何奪之？ 」使者奏如言，乃免。 成化初，轉左。 河南歲饑，計開封積粟多，奏請平糶，貧民賴以濟。 召拜刑部左侍郎，仍食從二品俸。 尋巡視浙江，考察官吏，奏罷不職者百余人，他弊政多所厘革。 還朝，久之，謝病去。
In Tianshun 5 (1461) he was transferred to right administration commissioner of Shandong. Land the people had reclaimed without tax was pointed out by scoundrels as idle land and presented to imperial in-laws. When a ministry envoy came to survey, Hui said, "By ancestral institution, land the people reclaim from wasteland is never taxed—how can it be seized?" The envoy memorialized as Hui had said, and the seizure was waived. At the start of Chenghua he was transferred to the left post. With famine in Henan year after year, noting that Kaifeng had much stored grain, he memorialized to sell at fair price and the poor were saved. He was summoned and appointed left vice minister of the Ministry of Punishments, still drawing salary of the second rank. Soon he inspected Zhejiang, examined officials, memorialized to dismiss more than a hundred who were derelict, and reformed many other abuses. Returning to court, after a long time he resigned on grounds of illness.
28
翚操行謹，所至有聲。 及歸，生計蕭然，絕跡公府，鄉人以為賢。
Hui's conduct was careful; wherever he went he won renown. When he returned home his livelihood was bare; he never set foot in official mansions, and his fellow townsmen considered him worthy.
29
賈銓，字秉鈞，邯鄣人。 永樂末進士。 宣德四年授禮科給事中，數有參駁。
Jia Quan, whose style was Bingjun, came from Handan. He passed the jinshi examinations at the end of the Yongle reign. In Xuande 4 (1429) he was appointed supervising secretary in the Office of Scrutiny for Rites and several times submitted impeachments.
30
英宗踐阼，既肆赦，復命讞在京重囚，多所原宥。 從銓請，推之南京。 秩滿，出為大理知府。 王驥征麓川，饋運有勞。 驥薦之。 麓川平，擢雲南左參政，仍知府事。 尋以驥言，還治司事。 正統十二年，左布政使闕，軍民數萬人頌銓，參贊軍務侍郎侯琎等亦疏請，銓遂得擢。 土官十余部，歲當貢馬輸差發銀及海<貝巴>，八府民歲當輸食鹽米鈔。 至景泰初，皆積逋不能償。 銓等為言除之。 治行聞，賜誥旌異。 景泰七年，九載滿，當入都，軍民乞留。 命還任。
When Yingzong took the throne, after a general amnesty he again ordered review of serious prisoners in the capital, and many were pardoned. At Quan's request this was extended to Nanjing. When his term ended he went out as prefect of Dali. When Wang Ji campaigned against Luchuan, Quan had merit in supplying transport. Ji recommended him. When Luchuan was pacified he was raised to left administration vice commissioner of Yunnan while still serving as prefect. Soon, on Ji's word, he returned to manage the provincial administration. In Zhengtong 12 (1447) the left administration commissioner post was vacant; tens of thousands of soldiers and civilians praised Quan, and military affairs vice minister Hou Jin and others also memorialized for him, and Quan was promoted. More than ten native offices each year owed tribute horses, corvée silver, and sea cowries, and the people of the eight prefectures each year owed grain, salt, and paper money. By the start of Jingtai all had accumulated arrears they could not pay. Quan and others memorialized to remit them. His administration was renowned; he was granted an edict of commendation. In Jingtai 7 (1456), when nine years in office were complete and he should enter the capital, soldiers and civilians begged that he remain. He was ordered to return to his post.
31
天順四年與梁楘等舉政績卓異。 戶部初闕尚書，王翺欲擢銓。 帝問李賢，賢曰：「聞其名，未見其人也。 」及是來覲，帝命賢視之，還奏貌寢。 乃以為右副都御史巡撫山東，尋兼撫河南。 山東歲侵，請召還清軍御史。 河南饑，請停征課馬。 皆許之。 成化初，左都御史李秉督師遼東，召銓署院事。 中官唐慎等從征荊、襄還，杖死淮安知事谷淵，自奏丐免。 銓請罪之。 乃付慎等司禮監，命法司罪其從人。 未幾，卒官。 謚恭靖。
In Tianshun 4 (1460) he, with Liang You and others, was cited for outstanding achievement. When the Ministry of Revenue first lacked a minister, Wang Ao wished to promote Quan. The Emperor asked Li Xian; Xian said, "I have heard his name but have not seen the man." When Quan came to audience on this occasion, the Emperor ordered Xian to observe him; returning, Xian reported that his appearance was unprepossessing. He was therefore made right vice censor-in-chief grand coordinator of Shandong and soon also grand coordinator of Henan. Shandong suffered invasion that year; he asked that the investigating censors clearing troops be recalled. Henan suffered famine; he asked that the levy of tribute horses be halted. Both were granted. At the start of Chenghua, when left censor-in-chief Li Bing directed troops in Liaodong, Quan was summoned to act at the censorate. The eunuch Tang Shen and others, returning from campaigning in Jing and Xiang, beat to death Gu Yuan, an official of Huai'an, and memorialized asking to be excused. Quan asked that they be punished. They were handed over to the Directorate of Ceremonial, and the law officers were ordered to punish their followers. Before long he died in office. He was given the posthumous title Gongjing.
32
銓在雲南，治行為一時冠。 比為巡撫，清靜不自表暴，吏民亦安之。
In Yunnan Quan's administration was the finest of the age. As grand coordinator he was quiet and did not display himself, and officials and people were at ease.
33
王宇，字仲宏，祥符人。 童丱時，日記萬言，巡撫侍郎于謙奇之。 登正統四年進士，授南京戶部主事。 秩滿當轉郎中，吏部以宇才，特用為撫州知府。 為政簡靜，而鋤強遏奸，凜不可犯，一府大治。
Wang Yu, whose style was Zhonghong, came from Xiangfu. Even as a child he could memorize ten thousand characters a day; Grand Coordinator Vice Minister Yu Qian marveled at him. He passed the jinshi examinations in Zhengtong 4 (1439) and was appointed a principal clerk in the Nanjing Ministry of Revenue. When his term ended he should have been transferred to director, but the Ministry of Personnel, valuing Yu's talent, specially appointed him prefect of Fuzhou. His rule was simple and quiet, yet he rooted out the strong and checked the wicked; his authority was formidable and the whole prefecture was greatly ordered.
34
天順元年，所司上其治行，詔賜誥命。 頃之，擢山東右布政使，命撫恤所屬饑民。 明年遷右副都御史，巡撫宣府。 中官嚴順、都督張林等令家人承納芻糧。 宇劾奏。 都御史寇深為解，帝切責深。 尋命兼撫大同。 石亨及從子彪驕恣，大同其舊鎮地，征索尤橫。 宇抗疏論其奸，乞置之法。 疏雖不行，聞者敬憚。 督餉郎中楊益不能備芻槁，為宇所劾。 戶部庇之，宇並劾尚書沈固等。 皆輸罪。 遭喪，起復為大理卿。 固辭，不許。
In Tianshun 1 (1457) the relevant office reported his administration; an edict granted him a patent of appointment. Before long he was raised to right administration commissioner of Shandong and ordered to comfort and relieve famine victims in his jurisdiction. The following year he was transferred to right vice censor-in-chief and grand coordinator of Xuanfu. The eunuch Yan Shun and Commissioner-in-chief Zhang Lin and others had their households receive fodder and grain in their stead. Yu memorialized and impeached them. Censor-in-chief Kou Shen spoke in their defense; the Emperor sharply rebuked Shen. Soon he was ordered also to grand-coordinate Datong. Shi Heng and his nephew Biao were arrogant and willful; Datong was their old command, and their exactions were especially overbearing. Yu submitted a forthright memorial on their wickedness and begged that they be punished by law. Although the memorial was not acted on, those who heard of it respected and feared him. Grain-supplying director Yang Yi could not provide sufficient fodder and was impeached by Yu. The Ministry of Revenue shielded him; Yu also impeached Minister Shen Gu and the others. All admitted guilt. On mourning he was recalled to serve as chief minister of the Court of Judicial Review. He firmly declined but was not permitted.
35
宇剛介，所至有盛名。 居大理，平反為多。 七年卒。
Yu was upright and unyielding; wherever he went he had a great reputation. At the Court of Judicial Review he reversed many wrongful convictions. He died in the seventh year.
36
崔恭，字克讓，廣宗人。 正統元年進士。 除戶部主事。 出理延綏倉儲，有能聲。 以楊溥薦，擢萊州知府。 內地輸遼東布，悉貯郡庫，歲久朽敝，守者多破家。 恭別構屋三十楹貯之，請約計歲輸外，余以充本府軍餉，遂放遣守者八百人。 也先犯京師，遣民兵數千入援。 廷議城臨清，檄發役夫。 恭以方春民乏食，請俟秋成。 居府六年，萊人以比漢楊震。
Cui Gong, whose style was Kerang, came from Guangzong. He passed the jinshi examinations in Zhengtong 1 (1436). He was appointed a principal clerk in the Ministry of Revenue. He went out to manage Yan-sui grain stores and had a reputation for ability. On Yang Pu's recommendation he was raised to prefect of Laizhou. Cloth for Liaodong levied from the interior was all stored in the prefectural treasury; after many years it rotted, and many custodians were ruined. Gong separately built thirty bays of housing to store it and asked that beyond the annual quota the surplus be used for the prefecture's military rations, then releasing eight hundred custodians. When Esen invaded the capital region, he sent several thousand militia to aid. The court debated fortifying Linqing and issued orders to levy laborers. Gong, because in spring the people lacked food, asked to wait until the autumn harvest. After six years in the prefecture, the people of Laizhou compared him to Han Yang Zhen.
37
景泰中，超遷湖廣右布政使。 諸司供給，率取之民。 恭與僚佐約，悉罷之。 公安、監利流民擅相殺。 恭下令願附籍者聽，否則迨秋遣歸，眾遂定。 尋遷江西左布政使。 司有廣濟庫，官吏幹沒五十萬。 恭白於巡撫韓雍，典守者鹹獲罪。 定均徭法，酌輕重，十年一役，遂為定例。
In the Jingtai reign he was promoted over the regular steps to right administration commissioner of Huguang. The various offices' supplies were generally taken from the people. Gong agreed with his colleagues to abolish them all. Displaced people in Gong'an and Jianli killed one another at will. Gong ordered that those willing to register might do so; otherwise they would be sent home in autumn, and the crowd was settled. Soon he was transferred to left administration commissioner of Jiangxi. The office had the Guangji treasury; officials embezzled five hundred thousand. Gong reported to Grand Coordinator Han Yong; all those in charge were punished. He fixed the equal-corvée law, weighing light and heavy, with one rotation every ten years, and it became the established rule.
38
天順二年，寧王奠培不法，恭劾之。 削其護衛，王稍戢。 遷右副都御史，代李秉巡撫蘇、松諸府。 按部，進耆老言利病，為興革。 與都督徐恭浚儀真漕河，又浚常、鎮河，避江險。 已，大治吳淞江。 起昆山夏界口，至上海白鶴江，又自白鶴江至嘉定卞家渡，迄莊家涇，凡浚萬四千二百余丈。 又浚曹家港、蒲匯塘、新涇諸水。 民賴其利，目曹家港為「都堂浦」。 初，周忱奏定耗羨則例，李秉改定以賦之輕重遞盈縮。 其例甚平，而難於稽算，吏不勝煩擾。 恭乃罷去，悉如忱舊。
In Tianshun 2 (1458) the Prince of Ning, Mo Pei, acted unlawfully; Gong impeached him. His guard was reduced; the prince somewhat restrained himself. He was made right vice censor-in-chief, replacing Li Bing as grand coordinator of Suzhou, Songjiang, and other prefectures. On inspecting his jurisdiction he brought forward elders to speak of benefits and harms and instituted reforms. With Commissioner-in-chief Xu Gong he dredged the Yizhen grain canal and the Chang and Zhen rivers to avoid Yangzi hazards. He then undertook a major repair of the Wusong River. From Kunshan's Xiakou boundary to Baihe River at Shanghai, and from Baihe River to Bianjiadu in Jiading, reaching Zhuangjia Creek—more than fourteen thousand two hundred zhang were dredged in all. He also dredged Caojia Harbor, Puhui Creek, Xinjing, and other waterways. The people benefited and called Caojia Harbor "Grand Coordinator Creek." At first Zhou Chen had fixed rules for surplus and loss; Li Bing had revised them to vary with the weight of each assessment. The rules were fair but hard to calculate; clerks were overwhelmed. Gong abolished them and restored Chen's old rules entirely.
39
吏部缺右侍郎，李賢、王翺舉恭。 遂召用。 置「勸懲簿」，有聞皆識之。 翺甚倚恭，轉左。 父憂起復。 憲宗即位，乞致仕。 不允。 成化五年，尚書李秉罷。 商輅欲用姚夔，彭時欲用王概，而北人居言路者，謂時實逐秉，喧謗於朝。 時稱疾不出，侍讀尹直以時、概皆已鄉人，恐因此得罪，急言於輅，以恭代秉。 越五月，母喪歸。 服除，起南京吏部，劾罷諸司不識者數人。 十一年春命參贊機務。 居三年，致仕。 又二年卒。 贈太子少保，謚莊敏。
The Ministry of Personnel lacked a right vice minister; Li Xian and Wang Ao recommended Gong. He was summoned and appointed. He kept an "Encouragement and Punishment Register" and recorded everything he heard. Ao relied greatly on Gong and transferred him to the left post. On his father's death he was recalled from mourning. When Xianzong took the throne he begged to retire. This was not granted. In Chenghua 5 (1469) Minister Li Bing was dismissed. Shang Lu wished to use Yao Kui; Peng Shi wished to use Wang Gai; but northerners in the remonstrance offices said Shi had driven Bing out and clamored at court. Shi claimed illness and stayed away; Reader-in-waiting Yin Zhi, fearing Shi and Gai would be implicated as fellow townsmen, urgently persuaded Lu to have Gong replace Bing. Five months later his mother died and he returned home. When mourning ended he was recalled to the Nanjing Ministry of Personnel and impeached several incompetent officials. In the spring of the eleventh year he was ordered to participate in deliberating state affairs. After three years he retired. Two years later he died. He was posthumously made Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent and given the posthumous title Zhuangmin.
40
劉孜，字顯孜，萬安人。 正統十年進士。 授御史，出按遼東。 景帝即位，有建南遷議者。 孜馳奏，乞斬言者以定人心。 期滿當代，朝議邊務方殷，復留一歲。 再按畿輔。 時方築淪州城，以孜言罷。 擢山東按察使。
Liu Zi, whose style was Xianzi, came from Wan'an. He passed the jinshi examinations in Zhengtong 10 (1445). He was made investigating censor and sent to inspect Liaodong. When Jingdi took the throne, some proposed moving the capital south. Zi memorialized by express relay begging that the advocates be executed to settle people's hearts. When his term should have ended, the court held that frontier affairs were pressing and kept him one more year. He inspected the capital region again. At the time Lunzhou city was being built; on Zi's word it was halted. He was made Shandong surveillance commissioner.
41
天順四年，吏部舉天下治行卓異，按察使惟孜一人，遷左布政使。 明年春，以右副都御史巡撫江南十府。 蘇、松財賦，自周忱立法後，代者多紛更。 孜首訪忱遺積，斟酌行之，民稱便。 成化元年，應天饑，方振貸，而江北饑民就食者眾。 孜請盡發諸縣廩，全活無算。 時民間多積困：瀕江官田久廢沒，仍責輸賦； 蘇、松、杭、嘉諸府僉補富戶； 南京廊房既傾圮，猶征鈔； 上元、江寧農民代河泊所綱戶采鰣魚； 應天都稅宣課諸司額外增稅； 江陰諸縣民戶償納荒租； 六合、江浦官牛歲征犢。 孜皆疏罷之。
In Tianshun 4 (1460) the Ministry of Personnel cited outstanding administration; among surveillance commissioners only Zi was named, and he was made left administration commissioner. The next spring he was made right vice censor-in-chief grand coordinator of Jiangnan's ten prefectures. Suzhou and Songjiang finances, since Zhou Chen's law, had been frequently changed by successive holders. Zi first sought Chen's surviving rules, weighed and applied them, and the people found it convenient. In Chenghua 1 (1465), Yingtian suffered famine; relief had just begun when refugees from north of the river came in great numbers. Zi asked to open all county granaries; innumerable lives were saved. The people suffered many accumulated hardships: long-abandoned riverside official fields still had tax levied; Suzhou, Songjiang, Hangzhou, and Jiaxing conscripted wealthy households to make up shortfalls; Nanjing corridor shops had collapsed yet paper-money tax was still levied; Shangyuan and Jiangning farmers were made to gather shad for river-boat households; Yingtian tax offices levied extra taxes; Jiangyin and other counties' households repaid abandoned-land rents; Liuhe and Jiangpu levied yearly calf taxes on official oxen. Zi memorialized to abolish them all.
42
召拜南京刑部尚書，以宋傑代。 四年致仕，道卒。
He was summoned as minister of the Nanjing Ministry of Punishments; Song Jie replaced him. In the fourth year he retired and died on the road.
43

Zi was honest and careful and handled affairs with precision. Yet he applied the law too strictly and contemporaries called him harsh. Jie was a man of generous character. After two years he was dismissed and Xing You replaced him.

You came from Wenchang. He passed the jinshi examinations in Zhengtong 13 (1448). He was made investigating censor and sent to inspect Fujian. Ten people were falsely accused as bandits and were about to be executed when they cried out their innocence. You delayed the case and found the real bandits. In the Tianshun reign he went out as prefect of Taizhou with solid achievements. Through implication in a case he was demoted assistant magistrate of Jinjiang. When Xianzong restored his post he was made prefect of Suzhou. Tax-collecting bullies were punished by law; ten thousand strings of illicit gains were used to dike the Sha River and pave the official road. In great flood the people starved; without waiting for approval he released two hundred thousand piculs of rice for relief. Always honest and upright, in governing Suzhou he was strict but not harsh. Jie recommended him; an edict made him Zhejiang left administration vice commissioner while still managing the prefecture and granted an imperial letter. After half a year he was made right vice censor-in-chief to replace Jie as grand coordinator. He opened the Danyang River and built the Benniu sluice, saving redundant transport costs, and the people found it convenient. Soon he also managed the two Zhe salt administrations, examined subordinates, and dismissed more than one hundred and seventy incompetent men. After several years he cited illness and returned home.

Li Kan, whose style was Xizheng, came from Dong'an. He passed the jinshi examinations in Zhengtong 7 (1442). He was appointed supervising secretary in the Office of Scrutiny for Revenue. When Jingdi supervised the realm, he submitted three proposals: simplify military talent, recruit sturdy civilians, and use war chariots. When Esen pressed on the capital some debated burning fodder outside the walls. Kan said the enemy were light raiders without staying power and begged that it not be burned, lest the people suffer renewed levies. All were approved. His parents were in Rongcheng; Kan wept day and night, begged leave, and braved danger to bring them away. At the start of Jingtai there was debate on recording honors for ministers who died escorting the Emperor. Kan said those who fled should be strictly punished to strengthen ministerial integrity. When the northern Emperor was about to return, he and colleague Liu Fu said the rites should be generous. This offended the throne and he was questioned; Minister Hu Ying explained and the matter ended.

He was transferred again to chief supervising secretary. With military campaigns, salaries and grain of school teachers throughout the realm were reduced. Kan memorialized to restore them. Minister Jin Lian violated an edict in collecting rent; Kan impeached him and sent him to the law officers. Biao, nephew of Shi Heng, encroached on people's property; Kan asked heavy punishment and forbade nobles and eunuchs from seizing commoners' land, with concealing officials punished equally. The Emperor pardoned Heng and Biao but granted the rest. Among daring supervising secretaries Lin Cong was foremost; Kan also was upright with a reputation for integrity. At court they debated changing the heir; the great ministers assented one after another. Kan wept that the heir had not lost virtue; Cong and censor Zhu Ying also objected, and contemporaries praised them. He was raised to vice director of the Heir Apparent's household.

In Tianshun 1 (1457) he was changed to vice director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and advanced to chief minister of the Court of the Imperial Stud. The next year the Shanxi grand coordinator was re-established and Kan was made right vice censor-in-chief. He memorialized, "Lands north of the passes are no different from utter wasteland. Unless one grew up there, none could dwell at ease or know the enemy. Now southerners garrison the northwest, fear the cold, and tremble at bandits. Northerners garrison the south cannot bear heat and mostly flee in secret. Troops cleared north and south should each fill vacancies at home; feelings would be convenient and defense maintained." At the time this was not adopted. He memorialized on inspecting censor Li Jie's crimes; Jie accused Kan in turn. Investigation substantiated Jie's case and he was removed from office. Kan had no corruption charge and was pardoned. In the sixth year he examined subordinates and dismissed more than one hundred and sixty including commissioners Wang Yun and Li Zhengfang. He also said, "Ministers of my age who cannot perform their duties—I have had them retire; I too should be dismissed." An edict did not permit this. Kan was upright and restored discipline; the corrupt hid themselves. That winter on his mother's death he returned; soldiers and civilians wept and blocked his way so he could not leave. When mourning ended he never returned and died at home after more than ten years.

Kan was filial, loved learning, and lived in poverty; at death he could barely afford burial. At the start of Hongzhi, student Jiang Ji and others said former Libationer Hu Yan, Censor-in-chief Gao Ming, and Li Kan deserved posthumous titles. The matter was shelved. Kan had two sons: Dekui, prefect of Yanzhou; and Deren, salt transport commissioner of Hedong.

Lei Fu, whose style was Jingyi, came from Ningyuan in Huguang. He passed the jinshi examinations at the start of Zhengtong. He was appointed courier and served as Guangxi vice commissioner. Hu Zhaocheng of Teng county framed the Yao and seized the county; Fu, with general Fan Xin, campaigned and beheaded him. At the start of Chenghua, on great ministers' joint recommendation, he was made right administration commissioner of Shandong. In the seventh year he was summoned as right vice minister of the Ministry of Rites. Soon he was made right vice censor-in-chief grand coordinator of Shanxi. Following Li Kan, he was upright and law-abiding and won troops' and people's hearts. He defeated bandits at Hongshayan and again at Yansi Ravine and Shiren Village; an imperial letter commended him. Shanxi suffered great flood while the court debated Shaanxi campaigns and ordered advance fodder collection for Yulin. Fu memorialized, "From Shanxi to Yulin roads are perilous; people carry silver to buy grain at soaring prices and many families are ruined by debt. Rain and snow are untimely; the sick and displaced suffer every misery, yet silk, medicines, and fruits due still number tens of thousands. I beg remission following Shandong and treasury relief." The Emperor agreed. When thirty thousand taels proved insufficient, he asked to sell four hundred thousand salt certificates and let people contribute grain for honorary offices. All were approved. In the summer of the tenth year he died in office.

原傑，字子英，陽城人。 正統十年進士。 又二年，授南京御史，尋改北。 巡按江西，捕誅劇盜，奸宄斂跡。 復按順天諸府。 大水，牧官馬者乏芻，馬多斃，有司責償，傑請免之。 開中鹽引入米振饑。 疏入，為部所格，景帝卒從傑議。 超擢江西按察使。 發寧王奠培淫亂事，革其護衛。 治行聞，賜誥旌異，遷山東左布政使。
Yuan Jie, whose style was Ziying, came from Yangcheng. He passed the jinshi in Zhengtong 10 (1445). Two years later he was made Nanjing investigating censor and soon transferred north. Inspecting Jiangxi he captured major bandits and treachery subsided. He inspected Shuntian prefectures again. In great flood horse-keepers lacked fodder and many horses died; offices demanded compensation; Jie asked exemption. He opened middle-salt certificates to bring in grain for famine relief. The ministry blocked it; Emperor Jingdi finally followed Jie's proposal. He was promoted over the regular steps to Jiangxi surveillance commissioner. He exposed Prince of Ning Mo Pei's debauchery and reduced his guard. His administration was renowned; he received commendation and was made Shandong left administration commissioner.

In Chenghua 2 (1466) he was immediately made right vice censor-in-chief grand coordinator there. In famine he gave relief and the people did not flee. He was summoned as left vice minister of the Ministry of Revenue. The Yellow River shifted and breached unpredictably; where it sank one place silted another. Soldiers and civilians reclaimed the silt. Scoundrels called it garden or garrison land, presented it to the prince for reward, and the establishment seized it. Jie asked presenters be banished and recipients punished. This was granted. When Jiangxi bandits rose, because Jie had twice won the people there, an edict sent him. He killed more than six hundred; the rest were dispersed. He was made left vice censor-in-chief and returned to assist the censorate.

荊、襄流民數十萬，朝廷以為憂。 祭酒周洪謨嘗著《流民圖說》，謂當增置府縣，聽附籍為編氓，可實襄、鄧戶口，俾數百年無患。 都御史李賓以聞，帝善之。 十二年，遂命傑出撫。 遍歷山溪，宣朝廷德意，諸流民欣然願附籍。 於是大會湖廣、河南、陜西撫、按官籍之，得戶十一萬三千有奇，口四十三萬八千有奇。 其初至，無產及平時頑梗者，驅還其鄉，而附籍者用輕則定田賦。 民大悅。 因相地勢，以襄陽所轄鄖縣，居竹、房、上津、商、洛諸縣中，道路四達，去襄陽五百余裏。 山林阻深，將吏鮮至，猝有盜賊，府難遙制。 乃拓其城，置鄖陽府，以縣附之。 且置湖廣行都司，增兵設戍，而析竹山置竹溪，析鄖置鄖西，析漢中之洵陽置白河，與竹山、上津、房鹹隸新府。 又於西安增山陽，南陽增南召、桐柏，汝州增伊陽，各隸其舊府。 制既定，薦知鄧州吳遠為鄖陽知府，諸縣皆擇鄰境良吏為之。 流人得所，四境乂安。 將還，以地界湖廣、河南、陜西，事無統紀，因薦御史吳道宏自代。 詔即擢道宏大理少卿，撫治鄖陽、襄陽、荊州、南陽、西安、漢中六府。 鄖陽之有撫治，自此始也。 傑以功進右都御史。
Several hundred thousand displaced people in Jing and Xiang alarmed the court. Libationer Zhou Hongmo's "Illustrated Discourse on Displaced People" urged new prefectures and counties and registration as commoners to fill Xiang and Deng and remove centuries of trouble. Censor-in-chief Li Bin reported it; the Emperor approved. In the twelfth year Jie was ordered out to pacify. Traversing mountains and streams he proclaimed the court's benevolence; the displaced gladly wished to register. He convened Huguang, Henan, and Shaanxi officials to register them: more than 113,000 households and 438,000 persons. Those without property and the stubborn were sent home; registrants received light land-tax assessments. The people were greatly pleased. Yun county under Xiangyang lay among Zhushan, Fang, Shangjin, Shang, Luo, and other counties, roads in all directions, five hundred-odd li from Xiangyang. Mountains were deep, officers rare; sudden bandits were hard to control from afar. He expanded the city and established Yunyang prefecture. He established the Huguang regional military commission, increased garrisons, created Zhuxi, Yunxi, and Baihe, and attached Zhushan, Shangjin, and Fang to the new prefecture. He added Shanyang in Xi'an, Nanzhao and Tongbai in Nanyang, and Yiyang in Ruzhou under their former prefectures. When the system was set he recommended Wu Yuan of Dengzhou as Yunyang prefect and chose good neighboring officials for the counties. The displaced found places; the borders were at peace. About to return, because the region bordered three provinces without unified oversight, he recommended censor Wu Daohong to replace him. An edict raised Daohong to vice minister of the Court of Judicial Review to pacify six prefectures. Yunyang pacification commissioners began from this. Jie was promoted to right censor-in-chief for his achievement.

Garrisoning Liaodong eight years, he restored military discipline. Though not old, he memorialized four times to retire and died at home after more than forty years.

Nanning Earl Mao Sheng garrisoned Jinchí; Feng listed his violations; officers were awed. In Tianshun 1 he went out as Fujian vice commissioner. At the start of Chenghua he was advanced to vice commissioner. He became Jiangxi surveillance commissioner, governed sternly, and entered as chief minister of the Court of the Imperial Stud.
59
八年以左僉都御史巡撫山東。 歲祲，請發濟南倉儲減價以糶，令臨清關稅收米麥濟振。 皆從之。 時大饑，雖獲振，饑民眾，轉徙益多。 俸請敕鄰境撫、按隨所在安輯，秋成資遣復業。 又乞開中淮、浙鹽百萬引，盡蠲州縣逋課。 詔如所請，更命移臨清倉粟十萬石振之。 至七月，俸又言公私困竭，救荒靡策，乞開納粟例，令胥吏得就選，富民授散官，且截留漕糧備振。 十月復言：「今救荒者止救其饑，不謀其寒。 縱得食，終不免僵死，乞貸貧民布棉。 」帝皆嘉納。 俸又檄發東昌、濟寧倉粟十萬余石為軍士月糧，而以德州、臨清寄庫銀易米振濟，奏請伏專擅罪。 帝特宥之。 已，復以俸奏免柴夫折價銀，移河南輸邊粟濟山東，而別給銀為邊餉，山東輸京租二十萬石，給本地用。 十年又饑，請發倉儲出貸。 撫山東五年，盡心荒政，活饑民不可勝數。
In the eighth year he was made left vice censor-in-chief grand coordinator of Shandong. In famine he asked to sell Jinan grain cheaply and use Linqing customs grain for relief. All were granted. Famine was severe; though relief was given, displacement increased. Feng asked neighboring officials pacify the displaced and send them home after harvest. He begged middle-salt on Huai and Zhe for a million certificates and full remission of arrears. The edict granted this and ordered ten thousand piculs from Linqing for relief. By the seventh month Feng begged grain-purchase offices, clerk selection, honorary offices for the wealthy, and retained transport grain for relief. In the tenth month he said, "Today's relief saves hunger, not cold. Even with food they may freeze; I beg cloth and cotton on loan." The Emperor commended and adopted all. Feng released granary grain for soldiers' rations, exchanged deposited silver for relief rice, and asked to confess overstepping authority. The Emperor specially pardoned him. Thereafter Feng won remission of firewood commutation, Henan grain for Shandong, and local retention of capital rent. In the tenth year famine returned; he asked to lend from granaries. Five years in Shandong he devoted himself to famine work and saved countless people.
60
以右副都御史改撫蘇、松。 俸性嚴。 以所部多巨室，欲故摧抑之，乃禁索私租，勸富家出谷備振動千計，怨謗紛然。 中官汪直有事南京，或譖俸。 直歸，未發也。 俸初在山東，與布政陳鉞負氣不相下。 後鉞從容言俸短，直信之。 十四年，俸議事至京，直請執俸下詔獄。 先是，所親學士江朝宗除服還朝，俸迓之九江，聯舟並下。 所至，有司供張頗盛。 直因謂朝宗有所關說，並下獄。 詞連僉事吳扁等十余人，俱被逮。 系獄半歲，謫戍湖廣。
He was made right vice censor-in-chief grand coordinator of Suzhou and Songjiang. Feng was stern by nature. With many great families he forbade private rents and urged grain contributions; complaints arose. Eunuch Wang Zhi had business at Nanjing; some slandered Feng. Zhi returned but had not yet acted. In Shandong Feng had clashed with commissioner Chen Yue. Later Yue spoke of Feng's faults and Zhi believed him. In the fourteenth year Feng came to the capital; Zhi had him seized for the imperial prison. Earlier Academician Jiang Chaozong returned from mourning; Feng met him at Jiujiang and traveled together. Wherever they went local offices entertained them lavishly. Zhi said Chaozong had lobbied; both were imprisoned. The case implicated Vice Commissioner Wu Bian and more than ten others. After half a year they were banished to Huguang.
61
俸在江西時，共成許聰獄，人多議其深文。 至是被禍，皆知為直誣，然無白其冤者。 逾年，卒戍所。
In Jiangxi Feng had joined the Xu Cong case; many criticized his harsh charges. When disaster struck all knew Zhi's false charge, yet none cleared him. After a year he died in exile.
62
夏壎，字宗成，天臺人。 景泰二年進士。 授御史。 天順初，巡按福建，繼清軍江西，發鎮守中官葉達恣橫狀，達為斂威。 以薦超擢廣東按察使。 時用師歲久，役民守城，壎至悉遣之。
Xia Xun, whose style was Zongcheng, came from Tiantai. He passed the jinshi in Jingtai 2 (1451). He was made investigating censor. At Tianshun's start he inspected Fujian, cleared troops in Jiangxi, and exposed eunuch Ye Da; Da restrained himself. On recommendation he was promoted to Guangdong surveillance commissioner. Long campaigns had conscripted city guards; Xun sent them all home.
63
成化初，奏：「瑤、僮弗靖，用兵無功，由有司撫字乖方，賊因得誘良民為徒黨。 劇寇數百，脅從萬千，進則驅之當前，退則殺以抒憤，害常在民，而利常在彼。 況用兵不已，供斂日增。 以易搖之人心，責無窮之軍費。 恐外患未除，內變先作。 請慎選監司守令，撫綏遺民，彼被脅之眾自聞風來歸。 」帝深納其言。 尋遷布政使，調江西。
At Chenghua's start he memorialized, "Yao and Zhuang unrest fails because officials govern badly and bandits lure good people. Major bandits are hundreds but coerce tens of thousands; harm falls on the people while profit stays with bandits. Campaigns do not cease and levies increase daily. With easily swayed hearts to meet endless military costs, I fear internal trouble before external trouble is removed. Choose officials carefully, comfort survivors, and the coerced will return." The Emperor deeply accepted his words. Soon he was made administration commissioner and moved to Jiangxi.
64
八年以右副都御史巡撫四川。 苗、僚時為寇。 壎立互知會捕法，賊為之戢。 古州苗萬余，居爛土久，時議逐之，壎謂非計。 松潘參將堯彧請益戍兵三千，又力陳不可。 皆得寢。 已，奏所部將校多犯法，奏請逾時，輒至遁逸。 請先逮系，然後奏聞。 帝可之。
In the eighth year he was made right vice censor-in-chief grand coordinator of Sichuan. Miao and Lao often raided. Xun established mutual-notification arrest laws and bandits were curbed. Ten thousand Guzhou Miao lived on ruined land; expulsion was proposed; Xun objected. Songpan general Yao Yu asked three thousand more troops; Xun again objected. Both were shelved. He memorialized many officers broke the law and fled when memorials were delayed. He asked to arrest first and report afterward. The Emperor approved.
65
壎剛介，善聽斷，所至民不冤。 在蜀二年，民夷畏服。 然厭繁劇，與時多齟齬。 子堠獻詩勸歸，壎欣然納焉。 年未五十，即求退。 章四上，得請。 既歸，杜門養親，不按賓客。 又五年卒。
Xun was upright and skilled in judgment; the people were not wronged. Two years in Shu, Chinese and tribes obeyed. Yet he disliked complexity and clashed with the times. His son Yu urged retirement in verse and Xun gladly accepted. Not yet fifty, he sought to retire. Four memorials and he was permitted. Once home he shut his door to nurture his parents. Five years later he died.
66
鍭，舉進士。 弘治四年謁選入都，上書請復李文祥、鄒智等官，罷大學士劉吉。 忤旨，下獄，得釋。 久之，除南京大理評事。 疏論賦斂、徭役、馬政、鹽課利弊及宗藩、戚裏侵漁狀。 不報。 鍭素無宦情。 居官僅歲餘，念母老，乞侍養，遂歸。 家居三十余年，竟不復出。
Yu passed the jinshi examinations. In Hongzhi 4 he memorialized to restore Li Wenxiang and Zou Zhi and dismiss Liu Ji. This offended the throne; he was imprisoned then released. He was later made Nanjing Court of Judicial Review reviewing official. He memorialized on levies, corvée, horses, salt, and encroachments by clans and in-laws. No response was given. Yu had never desired office. In office barely a year he begged to serve his aged mother and returned. At home more than thirty years he never came out again.
67
高明，字上達，貴溪人。 幼事母以孝聞。 登景泰二年進士，授御史。 聞內苑造龍舟，切諫。 有指揮為大臣所陷，論死，辯出之。 徐州民訴有司於朝，時例，越訴者戍邊。 明言：「戍邊，防誣訴也。 今訴不誣，法止當杖。 」民有為妖言者，吏貪功，誣以謀反。 明按無反狀，止坐妖言律。 皆報許。
Gao Ming, whose style was Shangda, came from Guixi. As a child he was known for filial service to his mother. He passed the jinshi in Jingtai 2 and was made investigating censor. Hearing the inner park built dragon boats, he remonstrated sharply. A commander was framed and sentenced to death; Ming freed him. Xuzhou people appealed at court; cross-jurisdiction appeals meant frontier banishment. Ming said, "Frontier banishment guards against false appeals. Now the appeal is not false; by law they should only be beaten. Some spread demonic talk; greedy clerks charged rebellion. Ming found no rebellion; only demonic-talk punishment applied. All were approved.
68
巡撫河南，黜屬吏六十人。 再按畿輔，入總諸道章奏。 天順初，尚書陳汝言有罪，偕諸御史劾，下之獄。 四年，御史趙明等劾天下朝覲官，觸帝怒，詰草疏主名。 眾大懼，明獨自承。 都御史寇深言：「頻年章疏，盡出明手，幸勿以細故加罪。 」帝意解，反稱明能。 石亨既誅，僮仆皆收。 明言不宜，坐免者百人。 擢大理寺丞。
Grand-coordinating Henan, he dismissed sixty subordinate officials. Inspecting the capital region again, he took charge of all circuit memorials. At Tianshun's start Minister Chen Ruyan was impeached and imprisoned. In the fourth year Zhao Ming impeached audience officials; the Emperor demanded the drafter's name. All were afraid; Ming alone took responsibility. Kou Shen said, "Memorials have come from Ming; do not punish a small matter." The Emperor eased and praised Ming's ability. After Shi Heng was executed, all his servants were seized. Ming said this was improper and a hundred men were spared. He was raised to Court of Judicial Review vice director.
69
憲宗立，拜南京右僉都御史。 以留都春夏淫雨，請修人事以回天意。 時納馬入監者至萬余人，明請區別。 薦郎中孫瓊、陳鴻漸、梅倫、何宜，主事宋瑛，皆端方廉潔，恬於進取，宜顯擢以風有位。 疏下所司。
When Xianzong took the throne, Ming was made Nanjing right vice censor-in-chief. With excessive rain at the southern capital, he asked to repair human conduct to turn back Heaven's mind. More than ten thousand horse-buyers entered the Directorate; Ming asked distinction. He recommended Sun Qiong, Chen Hongjian, Mei Lun, He Yi, and Song Ying as upright men worthy of promotion. The memorial went to the relevant offices.
70
成化三年，揚州鹽寇起，守兵失利，詔明討之。 造巨艦，名曰「籌亭」，往來江上督戰，並江置邏堡候望。 賊縱跡無所匿，遂平之。 內官鬻私鹽，據法沒入，鹽政大治。 因條上利病十余事，多議行。 仍還原任，以親老乞終養歸。
In Chenghua 3 Yangzhou salt bandits rose; Ming was ordered to suppress them. He built "Planning Pavilion" ships, patrolled the river, and set watch forts. The bandits had nowhere to hide and were pacified. Eunuchs sold private salt; he confiscated by law and salt was ordered. He submitted more than ten reform items; many were adopted. He returned to his post, then begged to nurture aged parents and went home.
71
十四年，上杭盜發。 詔起巡撫福建，督兵往討。 擒誅首惡，余皆減死遣戍。 以上杭地接江西、廣東，盜易嘯聚，請析置永定縣。 移疾徑歸。 久之，卒。 楊繼宗，字承芳，陽城人。 天順初進士。 授刑部主事。 囚多疫死，為時其食飲，令三日一櫛沐，全活甚眾。 又善辨疑獄。 河間獲盜，遣裏民張文、郭禮送京師，盜逸。 文謂禮曰：「吾二人並當死。 汝母老，鮮兄弟，以我代盜，庶全汝母子命。 」禮泣謝，從之。 文桎梏詣部，繼宗察非盜，竟辨出之。
In the fourteenth year bandits rose in Shanghang. An edict recalled him to grand-coordinate Fujian and campaign. He executed ringleaders; the rest were reduced and banished. Because Shanghang bordered Jiangxi and Guangdong he asked to establish Yongding county. Citing illness he went straight home. After a long time he died. Yang Jizong, whose style was Chengfang, came from Yangcheng. He passed the jinshi at the start of Tianshun. He was appointed a principal clerk in the Ministry of Punishments. Many prisoners died of plague; he supplied food and ordered bathing every three days; many were saved. He was also skilled at resolving doubtful cases. Hejian captured bandits and sent Zhang Wen and Guo Li to the capital; the bandits escaped. Wen said to Li, "We two must both die. Your mother is old; let me replace the bandit so you may live. Li wept, thanked him, and agreed. Wen came shackled; Jizong saw he was not the bandit and freed him.
72
成化初，用王翺薦，擢嘉興知府。 以一仆自隨，署齋蕭然。 性剛廉孤峭，人莫敢犯。 而時時集父老問疾苦，為祛除之。 大興社學，民間子弟八歲不就學者，罰其父兄； 遇學官以賓禮。 師儒競勸，文教大興。 御史孔儒清軍，裏老多撻死。 繼宗榜曰：「御史杖人至死者，詣府報名。 」儒怒。 繼宗入見曰：「為治有體。 公但剔奸弊，勸懲官吏。 若比戶稽核，則有司事，非憲體也。 」儒不能難，而心甚銜之。 瀕行，突入府署，發篋視之，敝衣數襲而已。 儒慚而去。 中官過者，繼宗遺以菱芡、歷書。 中官索錢，繼宗即發牒取庫金，曰：「金具在，與我印券。 」中官咋舌不敢受。 入覲，汪直欲見之，不可。 憲宗問直：「朝覲官孰廉？ 」直對曰：「天下不愛錢者，惟楊繼宗一人耳。 」九載秩滿，超遷浙江按察使。 數與中官張慶忤。 慶兄敏在司禮，每於帝前毀繼宗。 帝曰：「得非不私一錢之楊繼宗乎？ 」敏惶恐，遺書慶曰：「善遇之，上已知其人矣。 」聞母喪，立出。 止驛亭下，盡籍廨中器物付有司。 惟攜一仆、書數卷而還。
At Chenghua's start, on Wang Ao's recommendation, he was made Jiaxing prefect. He took only one servant; his quarters were bare. Stern, honest, and aloof, no one dared offend him. Yet he gathered elders to ask hardships and removed them. He expanded community schools; boys of eight who did not study brought fines on kin; school officers were received as guests. Teachers competed in encouragement and culture flourished. Censor Kong Ru cleared troops; many elders were beaten to death. Jizong posted: "Censors who beat people to death must report to the prefecture." Ru was angry. Jizong said, "Government has its proper form. You should root out abuses and punish officials. House-by-house auditing is local business, not a censor's role." Ru could not refute him but resented him. On departure he burst in, opened chests, and found only worn clothing. Ru left ashamed. When eunuchs passed, Jizong gave water chestnuts and calendars. When eunuchs asked for money, he issued for treasury gold saying, "Give me your receipt." The eunuchs gasped and dared not take it. On audience Wang Zhi wished to see him; he refused. Xianzong asked Zhi, "Which audience official is honest?" Zhi answered, "The only man who does not love money is Yang Jizong." After nine years he was promoted to Zhejiang surveillance commissioner. He often clashed with eunuch Zhang Qing. Qing's brother Min in the Directorate often slandered Jizong. The Emperor said, "Is it not Yang Jizong, who keeps not a coin?" Min wrote Qing, "Treat him well; the Emperor knows him." On his mother's death he left at once. He stopped at the relay station and registered all office goods. He took one servant and several books home.
73
服除，以右僉都御史巡撫順天。 畿內多權貴莊田，有侵民業者，輒奪還之。 按行關塞，武備大飭。 星變，應詔陳言，歷指中官及文武諸臣貪殘狀，且請召還中官出鎮者。 益為權貴所嫉。 治中陳翼訐其過，權貴因中之，左遷雲南副使。
When mourning ended he was made right vice censor-in-chief grand coordinator of Shuntian. He returned estates powerful families had seized from the people. Inspecting defenses, he greatly improved military readiness. On a celestial omen he listed eunuchs' and officials' greed and asked recall of garrison eunuchs. He was all the more envied by the powerful. Chen Yi accused him; the powerful struck and he was demoted to Yunnan.
74
孝宗立，遷湖廣按察使。 既至，命汲水百斛，洗滌廳事而後視事，曰：「吾以除穢也。 」居無何，復以僉都御史巡撫雲南。 三司多舊僚，相見歡然。 既而出位揖之曰：「明日有公事，諸君幸相諒。 」遂劾罷不職者八人。 未幾卒。
When Xiaozong took the throne he was made Huguang surveillance commissioner. On arrival he had the hall washed with a hundred hu of water, saying, "I remove filth." Soon he was again Yunnan grand coordinator. Many in the three offices were old colleagues; meeting was joyful. Then he bowed and said, "Tomorrow is public business; please understand." He impeached and dismissed eight incompetent men. Before long he died.
75
繼宗力持風節，而居心慈厚，自處必以禮。 為知府，謁上官必衣繡服，朝覲謁吏部亦然。 或言不可，笑曰：「此朝廷法服也，此而不服，將安用之？ 」為浙江按察時，倉官十余人坐缺糧系獄，至鬻子女以償。 繼宗欲寬之而無由。 一日，送月俸至，命量之，則溢原數。 較他司亦然。 因悟倉吏缺糧之由，將具實以聞。 眾懼，請於繼宗，願捐俸代償。 由是十人者獲釋。 嘗監鄉試得二卷，具朝服再拜曰：「二子當大魁天下，吾為朝廷得人賀耳。 」及拆卷，王華、李旻也，後果相繼為狀元。 人服其鑒。 天啟初，謚貞肅。
Jizong upheld integrity yet was kindly and conducted himself by ritual. As prefect he wore embroidered robes to visit superiors and at the Ministry. Some objected; he laughed, "This is statutory dress—what else is it for?" As Zhejiang commissioner, granary officers sold children to pay shortfalls. Jizong wished to lenient them but had no means. One day his salary exceeded the original amount. Other offices were the same. He understood the cause of shortfalls and was about to report. All begged Jizong to donate salary to pay. Thereby the ten were released. Supervising examinations he bowed to two papers saying they would top the realm. They were Wang Hua and Li Min; they indeed became top graduates. People admired his discernment. At Tianqi's start he received the posthumous title Zhensu.
76
贊曰：明初以十五布政司分治天下，諸邊要害則遣侯伯勛臣鎮扼之。 永樂之季，敕蹇義等二十六人巡行天下，安撫軍民，事竣還朝，不為經制。 宣德初，始命熊概巡撫蘇、松、兩浙。 越數年，而江西、河南諸省以次專設巡撫官。 天順初，暫罷復設，諸邊亦稍用廷臣出鎮或參贊軍務。 蓋以地大物眾，法令滋章，三司謹奉教條，修其常職； 而興利除弊，均賦稅，擊貪濁，安善良，惟巡撫得以便宜從事。 熊概以下諸人，強幹者立聲威，愷悌者流惠愛，政績均有可紀。 于謙、周忱巡撫最為有名，而勛業尤盛，故別著焉。
The commentator says: Early Ming used fifteen provincial commissions; frontiers were garrisoned by meritorious nobles. At Yongle's end Jian Yi and twenty-six others toured the realm—not yet regular. At Xuande's start Xiong Gai first grand-coordinated Suzhou, Songjiang, and Zhe. Within years Jiangxi, Henan, and others gained dedicated grand coordinators. At Tianshun's start they were briefly abolished and restored; frontiers used court ministers. Because the land was vast and laws multiplied, the three offices kept routine duties; but to promote benefit, equalize taxes, and strike corruption, only grand coordinators could act conveniently. From Xiong Gai onward, the forceful established authority and the kind spread benevolence—all worth recording. Yu Qian and Zhou Chen were the most renowned grand coordinators, yet their achievements were so great they are given separate treatment.
