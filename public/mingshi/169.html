1
高谷胡濙王直
Gao Gu, Hu Ying, and Wang Zhi.
2
景泰初，進尚書，兼翰林學士，掌閣務如故。 英宗將還，奉迎禮薄，千戶龔遂榮投書於谷，具言禮宜從厚，援唐肅宗迎上皇故事。 谷袖之入朝，遍示廷臣曰：「武夫尚知禮，況儒臣乎！ 」眾善其言。 胡濙、王直欲以聞。 谷曰：「迎復議上，上意久不決。 若進此書，使上知朝野同心，亦一助也。 」都御史王文不可。 已而言官奏之。 詰所從得，谷對曰：「自臣所。 」因抗章懇請如遂榮言。 帝雖不從，亦不之罪。
At the opening of the Jingtai reign, he was promoted to minister and appointed concurrent Hanlin Academician, continuing to manage Grand Secretariat affairs as before. When Emperor Yingzong was about to return, the planned welcome ceremony was judged too thin. Battalion officer Gong Suirong sent Gao Gu a letter arguing at length that the rites should be generous, citing the Tang precedent of Emperor Suzong welcoming the retired emperor. Gao Gu tucked the letter in his sleeve and brought it to court, showing it to officials throughout the assembly. "Even a soldier understands propriety," he said, "how much more should a Confucian minister!" The assembly approved of what he said. Hu Ying and Wang Zhi wanted to bring the matter to the emperor's attention. Gao Gu said, "The proposal to welcome him back has been submitted again and again, yet His Majesty's mind has long remained unsettled." If we submit this letter and let His Majesty see that court and countryside are of one mind, that would help move the matter forward. Censor-in-Chief Wang Wen would not agree. Before long, censorial officials memorialized about the matter. When asked where the letter had come from, Gao Gu answered, "From my office." He then submitted a forthright memorial earnestly urging that Suirong's proposal be adopted. The emperor did not accept the proposal, but he did not punish Gao Gu.
3
二年進少保、東閣大學士。 易儲，加太子太傅，給二俸。 應天、鳳陽災，命祀三陵，振貧民。 七年進謹身殿大學士，仍兼東閣。 內閣七人，言論多齟齬。 谷清直，持議正。 王文由谷薦，數擠谷。 谷屢請解機務，不許。 都給事中林聰忤權要論死，谷力救，得薄譴。 陳循及文構考官劉儼、黃諫，帝命禮部會谷復閱試卷。 谷力言儼等無私，且曰：「貴胄與寒士競進，已不可。 況不安義命，欲因此構考官乎？ 」帝乃賜循、文子中式，惟黜林挺一人，事得已。
In the second year of the reign he was promoted to Junior Guardian and Grand Secretary of the Eastern Pavilion. When the heir apparent was replaced, he was additionally appointed Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent and granted two salaries. When disasters struck Yingtian and Fengyang, he was ordered to sacrifice at the three imperial tombs and provide relief to the poor. In the seventh year he was promoted to Grand Secretary of the Hall of Self-Discipline while retaining his concurrent post in the Eastern Pavilion. Among the seven members of the Grand Secretariat, opinions frequently clashed. Gao Gu was upright and incorruptible, and he held firm to correct positions in debate. Wang Wen had been recommended by Gao Gu, yet repeatedly worked to push him aside. Gao Gu repeatedly asked to be relieved of secretarial duties, but his requests were denied. When chief supervising secretary Lin Cong offended powerful figures and was sentenced to death, Gao Gu intervened forcefully on his behalf, and Lin received only a mild reprimand. Chen Xun and Wang Wen framed the examination officials Liu Yan and Huang Jian, and the emperor ordered the Ministry of Rites to join Gao Gu in re-examining the papers. Gao Gu argued forcefully that Liu Yan and the others had shown no favoritism, saying, "For noble scions and poor scholars to compete for advancement is already unacceptable." How much worse when men discontent with their allotted fate seek to use this to frame the examiners? The emperor then allowed Chen Xun's and Wang Wen's sons to pass the examinations, dismissed only Lin Ting, and the affair was closed.
4
英宗復位，循、文等皆誅竄，谷謝病。 英宗謂谷長者，語廷臣曰：「谷在內閣議迎駕及南內事，嘗左右朕。 其賜金帛襲衣，給驛舟以歸。 」尋復賜敕獎諭。
When Emperor Yingzong was restored to the throne, Chen Xun, Wang Wen, and the others were executed or banished, and Gao Gu retired on grounds of illness. Emperor Yingzong called Gao Gu a man of worthy character and told the court, "When Gao Gu served in the Grand Secretariat and debated the welcome of the imperial carriage and matters of the Southern Palace, he often guided me." Gao Gu was granted gold, silks, and ceremonial robes, and an imperial courier boat was provided for his journey home. Soon afterward he received another edict commending him.
5
谷既去位，杜門絕賓客。 有問景泰、天順間事，輒不應。 天順四年正月卒，年七十。
Once Gao Gu had left office, he shut his doors and refused all visitors. If anyone asked about events during the Jingtai and Tianshun reigns, he would not answer. He died in the first month of the fourth year of Tianshun, at the age of seventy.
6
谷美豐儀，樂儉素，位至臺司，敝廬瘠田而已。 成化初，贈太保，謚文義。
Gao Gu had a handsome bearing and loved plain living; though he rose to the highest ministerial rank, he owned only a humble dwelling and lean fields. In the early Chenghua reign he was posthumously granted the title Grand Guardian and given the posthumous name Wenyi.
7
胡濙，字源潔，武進人。 生而發白，彌月乃黑。 建文二年舉進士，授兵科給事中。 永樂元年遷戶科都給事中。
Hu Ying, courtesy name Yuanjie, was a native of Wujin. He was born with white hair, which did not turn black until he was a month old. In the second year of the Jianwen reign he passed the jinshi examination and was appointed supervising secretary in the Bureau of Military Affairs. In the first year of the Yongle reign he was transferred to chief supervising secretary in the Bureau of Revenue.
8
惠帝之崩於火，或言遁去，諸舊臣多從者，帝疑之。 五年遣濙頒禦制諸書，並訪仙人張邋遢，遍行天下州郡鄉邑，隱察建文帝安在。 濙以故在外最久，至十四年乃還。 所至，亦間以民隱聞。 母喪乞歸，不許，擢禮部左侍郎。 十七年復出巡江浙、湖、湘諸府。 二十一年還朝，馳謁帝於宣府。 帝已就寢，聞濙至，急起召入。 濙悉以所聞對，漏下四鼓乃出。 先濙未至，傳言建文帝蹈海去，帝分遣內臣鄭和數輩浮海下西洋，至是疑始釋。
When Emperor Huidi died in the palace fire, some said he had escaped, and many former ministers were rumored to have gone with him, which aroused the emperor's suspicion. In the fifth year the emperor sent Hu Ying to distribute imperial works and to seek out the immortal Zhang Tuo, traveling through every prefecture, district, and township in the realm while secretly searching for Emperor Jianwen. For this reason Hu Ying remained abroad longer than anyone else and did not return until the fourteenth year. Wherever he went, he also occasionally reported the people's hidden grievances. When his mother died he asked to return home for mourning, but permission was denied and he was promoted to Vice Minister of Rites instead. In the seventeenth year he was again dispatched on inspection tours through the Jiang-Zhe and Hu-Xiang prefectures. In the twenty-first year he returned to court and hurried to audience with the emperor at Xuanfu. The emperor had already retired for the night, but when he heard that Hu Ying had arrived, he rose at once and summoned him in. Hu Ying reported everything he had learned, and did not leave until the fourth watch. Before Hu Ying returned, rumor had held that Emperor Jianwen had fled overseas, and the emperor had dispatched eunuchs including Zheng He on several voyages across the seas to the Western Ocean; only now was his suspicion finally put to rest.
9
皇太子監國南京，漢王為飛語謗太子。 帝改濙官南京，因命廉之。 濙至，密疏馳上監國七事，言誠敬孝謹無他，帝悅。
While the crown prince governed at Nanjing as regent, the Prince of Han spread slanderous rumors against him. The emperor transferred Hu Ying's post to Nanjing and ordered him to investigate. When Hu Ying arrived, he sent a secret memorial reporting seven points on the regency, attesting that the crown prince was sincere, reverent, filial, and cautious and had no other fault, and the emperor was pleased.
10
宣宗即位，仍遷禮部左侍郎。 明年來朝，乃留行在禮部，尋進尚書。 漢王反，與楊榮等贊親征。 事平，賚予甚厚。 明年賜第長安右門外，給閽者二人，賜銀章四。 生辰，賜宴其第。 四年命兼理詹事府事。 六年，張本卒，又兼領行在戶部。 時國用漸廣，濙慮度支不足，蠲租詔下，輒沮格。 帝嘗切戒之，然眷遇不少替。 嘗曲宴濙及楊士奇、夏原吉、蹇義，曰：「海內無虞，卿等四人力也。 」英宗即位，詔節冗費。 濙因奏減上供物，及汰法王以下番僧四五百人，浮費大省。 正統五年，山西災，詔行寬恤，既而有采買物料之命。 濙上疏言詔旨宜信。 又言軍旗營求差遣，因而擾民，宜罷之。 皆報可。 行在禮部印失，詔弗問，命改鑄。 已，又失，被劾下獄。 未幾，印獲，復職。 九年，年七十，乞致仕，不許。 英宗北狩，群臣聚哭於朝，有議南遷者。 濙曰：「文皇定陵寢於此，示子孫以不拔之計也。 」與侍郎于謙合，中外始有固誌。
When the Xuande Emperor ascended the throne, Hu Ying was again appointed Vice Minister of Rites. The following year he came to court for audience and was retained at the mobile capital's Ministry of Rites, soon advancing to minister. When the Prince of Han rebelled, he joined Yang Rong and others in urging the emperor to lead the campaign in person. After the rebellion was suppressed, rewards were lavish. The following year he was granted a residence outside Chang'an Gate on the right, given two gatekeepers, and bestowed four silver seals. On his birthday the emperor granted a banquet at his residence. In the fourth year he was ordered to concurrently manage affairs of the Household of the Heir Apparent. In the sixth year, after Zhang Ben died, he additionally took charge of the mobile capital's Ministry of Revenue. As state expenditures gradually expanded, Hu Ying worried that revenue would not suffice and habitually blocked edicts remitting taxes. The emperor often warned him sternly, yet his favor toward Hu Ying never diminished. The emperor once held a private banquet for Hu Ying, Yang Shiqi, Xia Yuanji, and Jian Yi, saying, "The realm is at peace, and the four of you deserve the credit." When Emperor Yingzong ascended the throne, an edict ordered the reduction of wasteful expenditures. Hu Ying memorialized to reduce tribute goods and to dismiss four or five hundred Tibetan monks below the rank of Dharma King, producing large savings in wasteful spending. In the fifth year of Zhengtong, when Shanxi suffered disaster, an edict was issued for lenient relief, yet soon afterward came an order to procure materials. Hu Ying memorialized that imperial edicts ought to be honored in practice. He also argued that military households seeking official assignments were harassing the people and that the practice should be abolished. All were approved. The seal of the mobile capital's Ministry of Rites was lost; the emperor ordered no inquiry and commanded that a new one be cast. Before long it was lost again, and he was impeached and imprisoned. Before long the seal was recovered and he was restored to office. In the ninth year, at the age of seventy, he requested retirement but was not permitted. When Emperor Yingzong was captured on the northern campaign, officials gathered weeping at court, and some proposed moving the capital south. Hu Ying said, "Emperor Taizong established the imperial tombs here to show posterity a resolve never to withdraw." Together with Vice Minister Yu Qian, he stiffened resolve throughout court and countryside.
11
景帝即位，進太子太傅。 楊善使也先，濙言上皇蒙塵久，宜附進服食，不報。 上皇將還，命禮部具奉迎儀。 濙等議遣禮部署迎於龍虎臺，錦衣具法駕迎居庸關，百司迎土城外，諸將迎教場門； 上皇自安定門入，進東安門，於東上北門南面坐； 皇帝謁見畢，百官朝見，上皇入南城大內。 議上，傳旨以一轎二馬迎於居庸關，至安定門易法駕，余如奏。 給事中劉福等言禮太薄。 帝報曰：朕尊大兄為太上皇帝，尊禮無加矣。 福等顧雲太薄，其意何居？ 禮部其會官詳察之。 」濙等言：「諸臣意無他，欲陛下篤親親耳。 」帝曰：「昨得太上皇書，具言迎駕之禮宜從簡損，朕豈得違之。 」群臣乃不敢言。 會千戶龔遂榮為書投大學士高谷，言奉迎宜厚，具言唐肅宗迎上皇故事。 谷袖之以朝，與王直等共觀之。 直與濙欲聞之帝，為都御史王文所阻，而給事中葉盛竟以聞。 盛同官林聰復劾直、濙、谷等，皆股肱大臣，有聞必告，不宜偶語竊議。 有詔索書。 濙等因以書進，且言：「肅宗迎上皇典禮，今日正可仿行。 陛下宜躬迎安定門外，分遣大臣迎龍虎臺。 」帝不悅曰：「第從朕命，無事紛更。 」上皇至，居南城宮。 濙請帝明年正旦率群臣朝延安門，不許。 上皇萬壽節，請令百官拜賀延安門，亦不許。 三年正月與王直並進少傅。 易太子，加兼太子太師。 王文惡林聰，文致其罪，欲殺之。 濙不肯署，遂稱疾，數日不朝。 帝使興安問疾。 對曰：「老臣本無疾，聞欲殺林聰，殊驚悸耳。 」聰由是得釋。
When Emperor Jingtai ascended the throne, Hu Ying was promoted to Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent. When Yang Shan was sent as envoy to Esen, Hu Ying proposed that as the retired emperor had long suffered captivity, food and clothing ought to be sent along, but received no reply. When the retired emperor was about to return, the Ministry of Rites was ordered to prepare the welcoming ceremonies. Hu Ying and others proposed that the Ministry of Rites should dispatch officials to welcome him at Longhu Terrace, the Brocade Guard should prepare the imperial carriage to receive him at Juyong Pass, all offices should welcome him outside Tucheng, and generals should welcome him at the drill ground gate; the retired emperor would enter through Anding Gate, proceed through Dong'an Gate, and take his seat facing south at the northern gate on the east side of the upper palace; after the emperor performed the audience, all officials would pay court, and the retired emperor would enter the Southern Palace within the inner palace. When the plan was submitted, an imperial message ordered one palanquin and two horses to welcome him at Juyong Pass, with the imperial carriage to be used from Anding Gate onward, and everything else as proposed. Supervising secretaries Liu Fu and others said the ceremonies were too meager. The emperor replied, "I honor my elder brother as the retired emperor; the honor could not be greater." Why do Fu and the others say the ceremonies are too thin—what do they mean by that? Let the Ministry of Rites convene officials and investigate in detail. Hu Ying and others said, "The officials mean no harm; they only wish Your Majesty to deepen kinship affection." The emperor said, "Yesterday I received a letter from the retired emperor stating at length that the welcome ceremony ought to be simplified. How could I disobey?" The officials then did not dare speak further. Meanwhile Battalion Officer Gong Suirong sent Grand Secretary Gao Gu a letter arguing that the welcome ceremony ought to be generous, citing at length the Tang precedent of Emperor Suzong welcoming the retired emperor. Gao Gu tucked the letter in his sleeve and brought it to court, where he and Wang Zhi and others read it together. Wang Zhi and Hu Ying wanted to report it to the emperor but were blocked by Censor-in-Chief Wang Wen, while supervising secretary Ye Sheng ultimately brought it to the emperor's attention. Ye Sheng's colleague Lin Cong further impeached Wang Zhi, Hu Ying, Gao Gu, and the others, saying that as chief ministers they should report anything they learned and ought not whisper and discuss in private. An edict was issued demanding the letter. Hu Ying and the others submitted the letter and said, "The ceremony by which Tang Suzong welcomed the retired emperor is precisely what may be followed today." Your Majesty ought to welcome him in person outside Anding Gate and dispatch ministers separately to welcome him at Longhu Terrace. The emperor, displeased, said, "Just follow my command—do not make needless changes." When the retired emperor arrived, he took up residence in the Southern Palace. Hu Ying asked that on New Year's Day of the following year the emperor lead the officials in audience at Yan'an Gate, but this was not permitted. On the retired emperor's birthday, Hu Ying asked that all officials be ordered to offer congratulations at Yan'an Gate, but this too was denied. In the first month of the third year he and Wang Zhi were jointly promoted to Junior Tutor. When the heir apparent was replaced, he was additionally appointed Grand Preceptor of the Heir Apparent. Wang Wen hated Lin Cong, fabricated charges against him, and sought to have him executed. Hu Ying refused to sign the document, then claimed illness and stayed away from court for several days. The emperor sent the eunuch Xing'an to inquire after his health. He replied, "This old minister is not actually ill; I was simply shaken to hear that Lin Cong was to be killed." Lin Cong was thereby released.
12
英宗復位，力疾入朝，遂求去。 賜璽書、白金、楮幣、襲衣，給驛，官其一子錦衣，世鎮撫。 濙歷事六朝，垂六十年，中外稱耆德。 及歸，有三弟，年皆七十余，須眉皓白，燕聚一堂，因名之曰「壽愷」。 又七年始卒，年八十九。 贈太保，謚忠安。
When Emperor Yingzong was restored to the throne, Hu Ying forced himself to attend court despite illness and then asked to retire. He was granted an imperial letter, white silver, paper currency, and ceremonial robes, given an imperial courier, and one of his sons was appointed to the Brocade Guard as hereditary garrison commander. Hu Ying served through six reigns, spanning nearly sixty years, and was hailed as a venerable elder throughout court and countryside. When he returned home, he had three younger brothers, all over seventy, with white beards and eyebrows; they gathered in one hall for feasting and named it the Hall of Longevity and Joy. Seven years later he died, at the age of eighty-nine. He was posthumously granted the title Grand Guardian and given the posthumous name Zhong'an.
13
濙節儉寬厚，喜怒不形於色，能以身下人。 在禮部久，表賀祥瑞，以官當首署名，人因謂其性善承迎。 南城人龔謙多妖術，濙薦為天文生，又薦道士仰彌高曉陰陽兵法，使守邊，時頗譏之。
Hu Ying was frugal and generous, never showing joy or anger on his face, and could humble himself before others. Having long served in the Ministry of Rites, when memorials congratulating auspicious omens were submitted, his office was expected to sign first, and people therefore said he was inclined to flatter. Gong Qian of the Southern City practiced many occult arts; Hu Ying recommended him as an astronomy student, and also recommended the Daoist Yang Migao, versed in yin-yang and military strategy, to guard the frontier—at the time this was much ridiculed.
14
王直，字行儉，泰和人。 父伯貞，洪武十五年以明經聘至京。 時應詔者五百余人，伯貞對第一。 授試僉事，分巡廣東雷州。 復呂塘廢渠，清鹽法。 會罷分巡官，召還為戶部主事。 以父喪服闋，不時起，謫居安慶。 建文初，復以薦知瓊州，崖州黎相仇殺，以反聞，且用兵。 伯貞捕其首惡，兵遂罷。 瓊田歲常三獲，以賦軍，軍不時受，俟民乏，乃急斂以要利。 伯貞為立期，三輸之，弊始絕。 居數年，大治，流民占籍者萬余。 憂歸，卒於家。
Wang Zhi, courtesy name Xingjian, was a native of Taihe. His father Wang Bozhen was summoned to the capital in the fifteenth year of Hongwu for his mastery of the Classics. More than five hundred men responded to the imperial summons, and Bozhen ranked first in the examination. He was appointed probationary assistant commissioner and assigned to circuit inspection of Leizhou in Guangdong. He restored the abandoned Lutang canal and reformed the salt laws. When circuit inspectors were abolished, he was recalled and made a principal clerk in the Ministry of Revenue. After completing mourning for his father, he did not resume office promptly and was demoted to reside in Anqing. At the beginning of the Jianwen reign he was again recommended and appointed prefect of Qiongzhou; when Li tribesmen in Yazhou feuded and killed one another, it was reported as rebellion and troops were about to be deployed. Bozhen arrested the ringleaders, and the military action was called off. Qiong fields commonly yielded three harvests a year, levied to supply the army; the army did not accept payment on schedule but waited until the people were destitute, then collected urgently to extort profit. Bozhen established fixed deadlines and three scheduled payments, and the abuse was finally ended. After several years the region was greatly well governed, and more than ten thousand vagrant households were registered. He returned home for mourning and died there.
15
直幼而端重，家貧力學。 舉永樂二年進士，改庶吉士，與曾棨、王英等二十八人同讀書文淵閣。 帝善其文，召入內閣，俾屬草。 尋授修撰。 歷事仁宗、宣宗，累遷少詹事兼侍讀學士。
Wang Zhi was dignified from childhood; though his family was poor, he studied diligently. He passed the jinshi examination in the second year of Yongle, was made a Hanlin bachelor, and studied with Zeng Qi, Wang Ying, and twenty-six others in the Wenyuan Pavilion. The emperor admired his writing, summoned him into the Grand Secretariat, and had him draft documents. Soon afterward he was appointed compiler. Serving through the reigns of Emperor Renzong and Emperor Xuanzong, he rose to Junior Guardian of the Heir Apparent and concurrent Reader-in-Waiting.
16
正統三年，《宣宗實錄》成。 進禮部侍郎，學士如故。 五年出蒞部事。 尚書胡濙悉以部政付之，直處之若素習者。 八年正月代郭琎為吏部尚書。 十一年，戶部侍郎奈亨附王振，構郎中趙敏，詞連直及侍郎曹義、趙新，並下獄。 三法司廷鞫，論亨斬，直等贖徒。 帝宥直、義，奪亨、新俸。
In the third year of Zhengtong, the Veritable Records of Emperor Xuanzong were completed. He was promoted to Vice Minister of Rites while retaining his academician post. In the fifth year he went out to take charge of ministry affairs. Minister Hu Ying entrusted all ministry affairs to him, and Wang Zhi handled them as if he had long been versed in them. In the first month of the eighth year he replaced Guo Jin as Minister of Personnel. In the eleventh year, Vice Minister of Revenue Nai Heng, allied with Wang Zhen, framed bureau director Zhao Min, implicating Wang Zhi and Vice Ministers Cao Yi and Zhao Xin, and all were imprisoned. The three judicial offices tried the case in court, sentencing Heng to decapitation and Wang Zhi and the others to penal servitude redeemable by payment. The emperor pardoned Wang Zhi and Cao Yi and confiscated the salaries of Heng and Zhao Xin.
17
帝將親征也先，直率廷臣力諫曰：「國家備邊最為謹嚴。 謀臣猛將，堅甲利兵，隨處充滿，且耕且守，是以久安。 今敵肆猖獗，違天悖理，陛下但宜固封疆，申號令，堅壁清野，蓄銳以待之，可圖必勝。 不必親禦六師，遠臨塞下。 況秋署未退，旱氣未回，青草不豐，水泉猶塞，士馬之用未充。 兵兇戰危，臣等以為不可。 」帝不從，命直留守。 王師覆於土木。 大臣群請太后立皇子為皇太子，命郕王攝政。 已，勸王即位，以安反側。 時變起倉卒，朝臣議屢上，皆直為首。 而直自以不如于謙，每事推下之，雍容鎮率而已。 加太子太保。
When the emperor was about to lead a personal campaign against Esen, Wang Zhi led court officials in forceful remonstrance, saying, "The state's frontier defense is maintained with the greatest rigor." Strategists and fierce generals, strong armor and sharp weapons, fill every post; farming and guarding proceed together, and thus the realm has long enjoyed peace. Now the enemy runs rampant, defying heaven and violating reason; Your Majesty need only secure the borders, enforce commands, strengthen defenses and clear the countryside, and store strength to await them—then certain victory may be planned. There is no need for Your Majesty personally to command the six armies and go far to the frontier. Moreover, autumn heat has not yet passed, drought air has not yet turned, green grass is not abundant, water sources are still blocked, and men and horses are not fully supplied. War is ominous and battle perilous; we your ministers deem it inadvisable. The emperor did not agree and ordered Wang Zhi to remain and guard the capital. The imperial army was destroyed at Tumu. Ministers collectively asked the empress dowager to establish the prince as crown prince and appoint the Prince of Fu as regent. Soon afterward they urged the prince to ascend the throne to settle unrest. The crisis arose in sudden haste; memorials from court officials were submitted repeatedly, with Wang Zhi always leading. Yet Wang Zhi considered himself inferior to Yu Qian and deferred to him in every matter, remaining composed and steady in leadership. He was made Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent.
18
景泰元年，也先使使議和，且請還上皇，下禮部議未決。 直率群臣上言曰：「太上皇惑細人言，輕身一出，至於蒙塵。 陛下宵衣旰食，征天下兵，與群臣兆姓同心僇力，期滅此朝食，以雪不共戴天之恥。 乃者天誘其衷，也先有悔心之萌，而來求成於我，請還乘輿，此轉禍為福之機也。 望陛下俯從其請，遣使往報，因察其誠偽而撫納之，奉太上皇以歸，少慰祖宗之心。 陛下天位已定，太上皇還，不復蒞天下事。 陛下第崇奉之，則天倫厚而天眷益隆，誠古今盛事也。 」帝曰：「卿等言良然。 但前後使者五輩往，終不得要領。 今復遣使，設彼假送駕為名，來犯京師，豈不為蒼生患。 賊詐難信，其更議之。 」已而瓦剌別部阿剌使復至，胡濙等復以為言。 於是帝御文華殿門，召諸大臣及言官諭以宜絕狀。 直對曰：「必遣使，毋貽後悔。 」帝不悅。 于謙前為解，帝意釋。 群臣既退，太監興安匍匐出呼曰：「若等固欲遣使，有文天祥、富弼其人乎？ 」直大言曰：「廷臣惟天子使，既食其祿，敢辭難乎！ 」言之再，聲色愈厲。 安語塞，乃議遣使，命李實、羅綺往。
In the first year of Jingtai, Esen sent envoys to discuss peace and also requested the return of the retired emperor; the matter was referred to the Ministry of Rites for deliberation but remained unresolved. Wang Zhi led the officials in memorializing, "The retired emperor, misled by petty men's words, ventured forth lightly and came to suffer captivity." Your Majesty rises early and eats late, mobilizes armies from throughout the realm, and joins officials and the myriad people in united effort, expecting to destroy the enemy before breakfast and avenge the shame of mortal enmity. Now heaven has moved their hearts; Esen has begun to repent and comes seeking peace with us, requesting the return of the imperial carriage—this is an opportunity to turn disaster into blessing. We hope Your Majesty will graciously accede to their request, dispatch envoys in reply, observe their sincerity or deceit and receive them accordingly, and bring the retired emperor home, giving some comfort to the hearts of the ancestors. Your Majesty's throne is already established; when the retired emperor returns, he will no longer govern the realm. If Your Majesty simply honor and support him, then family bonds will deepen and heaven's favor will grow ever greater—truly a grand event of past and present. The emperor said, "Your words are indeed sound." But five successive missions have gone and still failed to obtain a clear answer. If we send envoys again and they use the pretense of escorting the imperial carriage to invade the capital, would that not bring calamity upon the people? The enemy's deceit is hard to trust; let the matter be reconsidered. Soon afterward envoys from Ala of another Oirat branch arrived, and Hu Ying and others again spoke on the matter. Thereupon the emperor took his seat at the gate of the Wenhua Hall, summoned the chief ministers and censorial officials, and instructed them that relations ought to be severed. Wang Zhi replied, "Envoys must be sent—do not leave cause for regret." The emperor was displeased. Yu Qian stepped forward to explain, and the emperor's mood eased. After the officials had withdrawn, the eunuch Xing'an crawled out calling, "You insist on sending envoys—are there men like Wen Tianxiang or Fu Bi among you?" Wang Zhi spoke loudly, "Court officials are the emperor's envoys; having received his salary, how dare we shrink from difficulty!" He spoke twice, his voice and expression growing ever fiercer. Xing'an was left speechless, and envoys were then discussed; Li Shi and Luo Qi were ordered to go.
19
既行，而瓦剌可汗脫脫不花及也先使先後至，將遣歸。 使者謂館伴曰：「中國關外十四城皆為我有。 前阿剌知院使來，尚遣人偕往。 今亦必得大臣同行，庶有濟。 」胡濙以聞，下廷議。 直等固請，乃遣楊善等報之。
After they had departed, envoys from the Oirat khan Togto Buqa and from Esen arrived in succession, intending to escort the retired emperor home. The envoys told the escort officials, "The fourteen cities outside the passes of China are all in our possession." When Commissioner Ala came before, you still sent men to accompany him. Now you must also have a chief minister go together, or there will be no success. Hu Ying reported this, and the matter was referred to court for deliberation. Wang Zhi and others pressed firmly, and Yang Shan and others were dispatched in reply.
20
比實還，又以也先使至，具言也先欲和狀。 直與寧陽侯陳懋等上疏，請更遣使賫禮幣往迎上皇，不許。 復上疏曰：「臣等與李實語，具得彼中情事。 其所需衣物資斧者，上皇言也； 而奉迎車駕，也先意也。 昨者脫脫不花及阿剌知院使來，皆有報使。 今也先使以迎請為辭，乃不遣使與偕，是疑敵而召兵也。 」又不許。
By the time Li Shi returned, Esen's envoys had again arrived, fully stating Esen's desire for peace. Wang Zhi and Marquis of Ningyang Chen Mao and others memorialized, requesting that envoys be sent again bearing gifts to welcome the retired emperor, but this was denied. They memorialized again, "In speaking with Li Shi, we fully learned the situation on their side." The clothing, supplies, and funds they need were requested by the retired emperor; and escorting the imperial carriage was Esen's wish. When Togto Buqa and Commissioner Ala came before, return envoys were sent in each case. Now Esen's envoys use welcoming as their plea, yet no envoys are sent to accompany them—this is to distrust the enemy and invite war. Again this was denied.
21
已而實自言於帝。 帝第報也先書，就令楊善迎歸而已。 直等復上言：「今北使已發，願本上皇之心，順臣民之願，因彼悔心，遣使往報，以圖迎復，此不待計而決者也。 不然，眾誌難犯，違天不祥，彼將執為兵端，邊事益棘，京師亦不得高枕臥矣。 」帝乃命群臣擇使，直與陳懋等請仍遣實。 報曰：「候善歸議之。 」御史畢鑾等復上疏，力言：「就令彼以詐來，我以誠往，萬一不測，則我之兵力固在。 」帝終不聽。 已而善竟奉上皇還。
Soon afterward Li Shi spoke to the emperor himself. The emperor merely replied to Esen in writing and ordered Yang Shan to welcome the retired emperor home—that was all. Wang Zhi and others memorialized again, "The northern envoys have already set out; we wish to follow the retired emperor's heart, accord with officials and people's wishes, take advantage of their repentance, and send envoys in reply to plan his welcome and return—this needs no deliberation to decide." Otherwise the people's will cannot be violated, defying heaven is inauspicious, they will seize this as a pretext for war, frontier affairs will grow ever more urgent, and the capital too will not be able to rest in peace. The emperor then ordered officials to choose envoys; Wang Zhi and Chen Mao and others asked that Li Shi be sent again. The reply was, "Wait until Shan returns, then discuss it." Censor Bi Luan and others memorialized again, forcefully stating, "Even if they come with deceit and we go with sincerity, if anything unforeseen occurs, our military strength remains intact." The emperor ultimately would not listen. Before long Yang Shan did indeed escort the retired emperor home.
22
二年，也先遣使入貢，且請答使。 直屢疏言：「邊備未修，芻糧未積，瘡痍未復，宜如其請。 遣使往以觀虛實，開導其善。 」不許。 無何，也先遣騎入塞，以報使為辭。 直與群臣復請之，卒不許。 直等乃上疏言：「陛下銳意治兵，為戰守計，真大有為之主。 然使命不通，難保其不為寇。 宜敕沿邊守臣，發兵遊僥，有警則入保，無事則力耕。 陛下於機務之暇，時召京營總督、總兵，詢以方略，誠接而禮貌之，信賞罰以持其後，斯戰守可言也。 」帝曰「善」。
In the second year, Esen sent envoys to offer tribute and also requested return envoys. Wang Zhi memorialized repeatedly that frontier defenses were not yet repaired, fodder and grain not yet stockpiled, and the realm not yet healed from its wounds, and that the emperor ought to grant their request. Envoys should be sent to gauge their true intentions and guide them toward reconciliation. The emperor refused. Before long Esen sent horsemen across the frontier, citing the failure to send return envoys as his excuse. Wang Zhi and the officials pressed again, but the emperor still refused. Wang Zhi and others then memorialized that the emperor's resolve to train troops and plan for both offense and defense showed him to be a ruler truly capable of great undertakings. Yet without diplomatic contact, there was no guarantee they would not raid again. Frontier commanders ought to be ordered to patrol with troops, withdraw into fortified posts when alarmed, and farm diligently when at peace. In intervals from state affairs, Your Majesty should summon the capital garrison commanders and regional generals, consult them on strategy, receive them with sincerity and courtesy, and sustain them through fair rewards and punishments—only then could offense and defense be truly secured. The emperor approved.
23
明年正月進少傅。 帝欲易太子。 未發。 會思明土知府黃矰以為請。 帝喜，下禮部議。 胡濙唯唯，文武諸臣議者九十一人當署名，直有難色。 陳循濡筆強之，乃署，竟易皇太子。 直進兼太子太師，賜金幣加等。 頓足嘆曰：「此何等大事，乃為一蠻酋所壞，吾輩愧死矣。 」景帝疾亟，直、濙等會諸大臣臺諫，請復立沂王為皇太子，推大學士商輅草疏。 未上，而石亨、徐有貞等奪門迎上皇復位，殺王文等。 疏草留姚夔所，嘗出以示郎中陸昶，嘆曰：「是疏不及進，天也。 」直遂乞休。 賜璽書、金綺、楮幣，給驛歸。
In the first month of the following year he was promoted to Junior Tutor. The emperor wished to replace the heir apparent. He had not yet acted on the plan. Then Huang Wei, native prefect of Siming, memorialized requesting the change. The emperor was pleased and referred the matter to the Ministry of Rites. Hu Ying assented without conviction; ninety-one civil and military officials were to sign the deliberation, and Wang Zhi looked deeply uneasy. Chen Xun dipped the brush and pressed him until he signed, and the heir apparent was replaced. Wang Zhi was promoted to concurrent Grand Preceptor of the Heir Apparent and granted gold and currency of the highest grade. He stamped his foot and sighed, "What a momentous affair, undone by a single tribal chieftain—we ought to die of shame." When Emperor Jingtai's illness grew critical, Wang Zhi, Hu Ying, and others convened chief ministers and censorial officials to request that the Prince of Yi be re-established as heir apparent, and asked Grand Secretary Shang Lu to draft the memorial. Before it could be submitted, Shi Heng, Xu Youzhen, and others forced open the gate to restore the retired emperor and killed Wang Wen and the others. The draft remained with Yao Kui, who once showed it to bureau director Lu Chang and sighed, "That this memorial never reached the throne was heaven's will." Wang Zhi then asked to retire. He was granted an imperial letter, gold brocade, and paper currency, and sent home with an imperial courier.
24
直為人方面修髯，儀觀甚偉。 性嚴重，不茍言笑。 及與人交，恂恂如也。 在翰林二十余年，稽古代言編纂紀註之事，多出其手。 與金溪王英齊名，人稱「二王」，以居地目直曰「東王」，英曰「西王」。 直以次當入閣，楊士奇不欲也。 及長吏部，兼廉慎。 時初罷廷臣薦舉方面大吏，專屬吏部。 直委任曹郎，嚴抑奔競。 凡御史巡方歸者，必令具所屬賢否以備選擢，稱得人。 其子資為南國子博士。 考績至部，文選郎欲留侍直，直不可，曰：「是亂法自我始也。 」朝廷以直老，命何文淵為尚書佐之。 文淵去，又命王翺，部遂有二尚書。 直為尚書十四年，年益高，名德日益重。 帝優禮之，免其常朝。
Wang Zhi had a square face and flowing beard, and an imposing presence. He was grave by nature and never casual in speech or laughter. Yet in dealing with others he was respectful and unassuming. During more than twenty years in the Hanlin Academy, he handled most of the court's classical research, imperial drafting, compilation, and annotation. He was famed alongside Wang Ying of Jinxi; people called them the Two Wangs, naming Wang Zhi the Eastern King and Wang Ying the Western King according to where they lived. By seniority Wang Zhi ought to have entered the Grand Secretariat, but Yang Shiqi opposed it. When he headed the Ministry of Personnel, he became still more incorruptible and cautious. At that time the practice of court recommendation for regional governors had just been abolished, leaving appointments solely to the Ministry of Personnel. Wang Zhi entrusted his bureau directors and strictly suppressed scrambling for advancement. Whenever censors returned from regional inspection, he required reports on the merit of officials under their jurisdiction for promotion, and was praised for appointing the right men. His son Wang Ji served as a doctorate fellow at the Southern Directorate of Education. When his performance review came to the ministry, the personnel selection director wished to keep him at Wang Zhi's side, but Wang Zhi refused, saying, "To bend the law would begin with me." Because Wang Zhi was growing old, the court appointed He Wenyuan as minister to assist him. When Wenyuan left, Wang Ao was appointed, and the ministry briefly had two ministers. Wang Zhi served as minister for fourteen years, and as he grew older his reputation and moral weight only increased. The emperor treated him with special courtesy and exempted him from regular court attendance.
25
比家居，嘗從諸佃仆耕蒔，擊鼓歌唱。 諸子孫更叠舉觴上壽，直嘆曰：「曩者西楊抑我，令不得共事。 然使我在閣，今上復辟，當不免遼陽之行，安得與汝曹為樂哉！ 」天順六年卒，年八十四。 贈太保，謚文端。
At home he would join his tenant laborers in plowing and planting, beating drums and singing as he worked. His sons and grandsons took turns raising cups to wish him long life, and Wang Zhi sighed, "Formerly Yang Shiqi kept me out of the Grand Secretariat and would not let me serve with him." Yet if I had served in the Secretariat when the present emperor was restored, I would surely not have escaped exile to Liaoyang—how could I be enjoying this happiness with you now! He died in the sixth year of Tianshun, at the age of eighty-four. He was posthumously granted the title Grand Guardian and given the posthumous name Wenduan.
26
資仕至翰林檢討，亦以學行稱。 曾孫思，自有傳。
Wang Ji rose to Hanlin reviser and was likewise praised for learning and conduct. His great-grandson Wang Si has a separate biography.
27
贊曰：高谷之清直，胡濙之寬厚，王直之端重，蓋皆有大臣之度焉。 當英、景之間，國勢初更，人心觀望，執政任事之臣多阿意取容。 而谷、濙忄卷忄卷於迎駕之儀，直侃侃於遣使之請，皆力持正議，不隨眾俯仰，故能身負碩望，始終一節，可謂老成人矣。
The encomium reads: Gao Gu's uprightness, Hu Ying's generosity, and Wang Zhi's dignity—all showed the bearing of great ministers. Between the reigns of Yingzong and Jingtai the state was in upheaval, the people watched and waited, and many ministers in power curried favor to please their superiors. Yet Gao Gu and Hu Ying were earnestly concerned over the welcome ceremony, and Wang Zhi spoke forthrightly in urging the dispatch of envoys; all held firm to correct views and refused to follow the crowd, and so they bore great renown and maintained their integrity to the end—truly men of mature judgment.
