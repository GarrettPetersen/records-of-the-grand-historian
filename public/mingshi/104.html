英宗九子。 憲宗外，庶三子見湜未封，殤。 其得封者七王，曰德莊王見潾，曰許悼王見淳，曰秀懷王見澍，曰崇簡王見澤，曰吉簡王見浚，曰忻穆王見治，曰徽莊王見沛。
Emperor Yingzong had nine sons. Besides the son who became Emperor Xianzong, Yingzong's third son by a concubine, Jian Shi, was never granted a fief and died in infancy. Seven sons received princely titles: Zhuang of De (Jian Lin), Dao of Xu (Jian Chun), Huai of Xiu (Jian Shu), Jian of Chong (Jian Ze), Jian of Ji (Jian Jun), Mu of Xin (Jian Zhi), and Zhuang of Hui (Jian Pei).

De princedom — Prince Zhuang Jian Lin, Yingzong's second son by a secondary consort, was enfeoffed in Tianshun 1, took up residence at Jinan Prefecture in Chenghua 3, and died in Zhengde 12.Prince Yi You Rong, Zhuang's second son by his primary consort, succeeded in Zhengde 16 and died in Jiajing 18.Hou Dun, Yi's second son by a secondary consort, was first made Prince of Dongping and later named heir in the Jiajing period; he died soon after. His son Zai Yong then inherited the line, and Hou Dun was posthumously raised to princely rank with the title Huai.Prince Gong Zai Yong, Huai's second son by a secondary consort, succeeded in Jiajing 20 and died in Wanli 2.Prince Ding Yi Kan, Gong's only son by his primary consort, inherited the fief in Wanli 5 and died in Wanli 16.Prince Chang, Ding's only son by his primary consort, succeeded in Wanli 19 and died in Chongzhen 5.You Kan, Chang's eldest son by a secondary consort, was first Prince of Guangzong and named heir in Wanli 43; he died before succeeding. Prince You Shu, Chang's second son by a secondary consort, was first a commandery prince and was promoted during Chongzhen. In the first month of Chongzhen 12, Qing troops took Jinan and captured him.

憲宗十四子。 孝宗外，悼恭太子及他皇子俱未名殤。 其得封者十王，曰興獻王祐杬，曰岐惠王祐棆，曰益端王祐檳，曰衡恭王祐楎，曰雍靖王祐橒，曰壽定王祐榰，曰汝定王祐梈，曰涇簡王祐橓，曰榮莊王祐樞，曰申懿王祐楷。
Emperor Xianzong had fourteen sons. Besides the son who became Emperor Xiaozong, the posthumously titled Crown Prince Dao Gong and the other princes all died in infancy without receiving personal names. Ten received princely titles: Xian of Xing (You Yuan), Hui of Qi (You Lun), Duan of Yi (You Bin), Gong of Heng (You Ge), Jing of Yong (You Yun), Ding of Shou (You Zhi), Ding of Ru (You Bing), Jian of Jing (You Shuan), Zhuang of Rong (You Shu), and Yi of Shen (You Kai).

Qing forces reached Burma in the winter of Shunzhi 18; the Burmese surrendered him, and he was executed in Yunnan the following year.

The Chongzhen Emperor had seven sons. Besides Crown Prince Xian Min Cilang, the second son by the primary consort and the fifth, sixth, and seventh sons by secondary consorts all died in infancy. Two received titles but never established fiefs: Ai of Ding (Ci Jiong) and Dao of Yong (Ci Zhao).

Ding — Prince Ai Ci Jiong, the Chongzhen Emperor's third son by his primary consort, was enfeoffed in Chongzhen 14 but never went to a fief. When rebels took Beijing in Chongzhen 17 he was captured; his fate is unknown.
Yong — Prince Dao Ci Zhao, the Chongzhen Emperor's fourth son by a secondary consort, was enfeoffed in Chongzhen 15 but never went to a fief. When rebels took Beijing in Chongzhen 17 he was captured; his fate is unknown.
