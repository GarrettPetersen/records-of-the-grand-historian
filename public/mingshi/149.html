1
蹇義夏原吉 〈 （俞士吉李文郁鄒師顏）〉
Jian Yi and Xia Yuanji Opening section bracket 〈 (with appended biographies of Yu Shiji, Li Wenyu, and Zou Shiyan)〉
2
燕師入，迎附，遷左侍郎。 數月，進尚書。 時方務反建文之政，所更易者悉罷之。 義從容言曰：「損益貴適時宜。 前改者固不當，今必欲盡復者，亦未悉當也。 」因舉數事陳說本末。 帝稱善，從其言。
When the Prince of Yan's army entered the capital, Jian Yi went over to his side and was made Left Vice Minister. Within a few months he was promoted to minister. The court was then intent on undoing the Jianwen reforms, and every measure that had been altered was struck down. Jian Yi spoke calmly: "Whether to add or cut policy depends on what the times require. The earlier reforms were indeed wrong, but to insist on restoring every one of them now would not be right either." He then cited several examples and explained how each had come about. The emperor approved and took his counsel.
3
永樂二年兼太子詹事。 帝有所傳諭太子，輒遣義，能委曲導意。 帝與太子俱愛重之。 七年，帝巡北京，命輔皇太子監國。 義熟典故，達治體，軍國事皆倚辦。 時舊臣見親用者，戶部尚書夏原吉與義齊名，中外稱曰「蹇、夏」。 滿三考，帝親宴二人便殿，褒揚甚至。 數奉命兼理他部事，職務填委，處之裕如。 十七年以父喪歸，帝及太子皆遣官賜祭。 詔起復。 十九年，三殿災，敕廷臣二十六人巡行天下。 義及給事中馬俊分巡應天諸府，問軍民疾苦，黜文武長吏擾民者數人，條興革數十事奏行之。 還治部事。 明年，帝北征還，以太子曲宥呂震婿主事張鶴朝參失儀，罪義不匡正，逮義系錦衣衛獄。 又明年春得釋。
In Yongle year 2 he was also appointed Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent. Whenever the emperor wished to convey something to the heir, he sent Jian Yi, who could delicately make the emperor's meaning clear. Emperor and heir alike valued him highly. In year 7 the emperor went north to Beijing and left Jian Yi to help the crown prince administer the realm. Jian Yi knew precedent and understood how government worked; both military and civil affairs were entrusted to him. Among the veteran ministers then in favor, Minister of Revenue Xia Yuanji was Jian Yi's equal in renown; inside and outside court they were known as "Jian and Xia." After they completed three triennial reviews, the emperor personally entertained them in a side hall and praised them in the highest terms. He was repeatedly ordered to take charge of other ministries as well; though his duties piled up, he managed them with ease. In year 17 he went home to mourn his father; both emperor and heir sent officials with sacrificial gifts. An edict ordered him back to office before his mourning period had ended. In year 19 fire destroyed the three main palace halls, and the emperor sent twenty-six court officials to tour the realm. Jian Yi and Supervising Secretary Ma Jun toured the prefectures of the Nanjing region, inquiring into civilian and military grievances, removing several civil and military chiefs who oppressed the people, and memorializing dozens of reforms that were adopted. He then resumed his ministry duties. The following year, when the emperor returned from his northern campaign, he blamed Jian Yi for not correcting the heir, who had improperly excused Lü Zhen's son-in-law Director Zhang He for a breach of court etiquette at audience, and had Jian Yi thrown into the Brocade Guard prison. He was released the following spring.
4
仁宗即位，義、原吉皆以元老為中外所信。 帝又念義監國時舊勞，尤厚倚之。 首進義少保，賜冠服、象笏、玉帶，兼食二祿。 歷進少師，賜銀章一，文曰「繩愆糾繆」。 已，復賜璽書曰：「曩朕監國，卿以先朝舊臣，日侍左右。 兩京肇建，政務方殷，卿勞心焦思，不恤身家，二十余年，夷險一節。 朕承大統，贊襄治理，不懈益恭。 朕篤念不忘，茲以已意，創制『蹇忠貞印』賜卿。 俾藏於家，傳之後世，知朕君臣共濟艱難，相與有成也。 」時惟楊士奇亦得賜「貞一」印及敕。 尋命與英國公輔及原吉同監修《太宗實錄》。 義視原吉尤重厚，然過於周慎。 士奇嘗於帝前謂義曰：「何過慮？ 」義曰：「恐鹵莽為後憂耳。 」帝兩是之。 楊榮嘗毀義。 帝不直榮。 義頓首言：「榮無他。 即左右有讒榮者，願陛下慎察。 」帝笑曰：「吾固弗信也。 」宣宗即位，委寄益重。 時方修獻陵，帝欲遵遺詔從儉約，以問義、原吉。 二人力贊曰：「聖見高遠，出於至孝，萬世之利也。 」帝親為規畫，三月而陵成，宏麗不及長陵，其後諸帝因以為制。 迨世宗營永陵，始益崇侈雲。
When Emperor Renzong came to the throne, Jian Yi and Xia Yuanji were both trusted throughout the realm as elder statesmen. The emperor also remembered Jian Yi's service while administering the realm and relied on him all the more. He first promoted Jian Yi to Junior Guardian, granting him court dress, an ivory tablet, and a jade belt, with the salary of two posts. He was later promoted to Junior Preceptor and given a silver seal bearing the words "Correcting faults and remedying errors." Soon afterward the emperor sent another sealed letter: "When I was administering the realm, you, as a veteran of the previous reign, attended me every day. As the two capitals were being built and government was at its busiest, you wore yourself out in service, heedless of your own safety, and for more than twenty years held the same steadfast course through good times and bad. Since I took the throne you have aided my rule, never slackening but growing ever more devoted. I have never forgotten this, and now of my own accord create and bestow upon you the "Jian Loyalty and Integrity Seal." Keep it in your family and pass it down, so posterity may know how we ruler and minister weathered hardship together and achieved what we did." At that time only Yang Shiqi also received a "Integrity and Unity" seal and an imperial letter. Soon he was ordered, together with the Duke of Ying Zhang Fu and Xia Yuanji, to supervise compilation of the 《Veritable Records of the Yongle Emperor》. Jian Yi held Xia Yuanji in particular esteem, but he was excessively cautious in his ways. Shiqi once said to Jian Yi in the emperor's presence, "Why worry so much? Jian Yi replied, "I fear that carelessness will cause trouble later." The emperor agreed with both men. Yang Rong once spoke ill of Jian Yi. The emperor did not take Rong's side. Jian Yi kowtowed and said, "Rong means no harm. If anyone near you speaks against him, I beg Your Majesty to look into it carefully." The emperor laughed and said, "I never believed it in the first place." When Emperor Xuanzong came to the throne, his reliance on Jian Yi grew even greater. While the Xian Mausoleum was being built, the emperor wished to follow his father's testament and keep the work frugal, and consulted Jian Yi and Xia Yuanji. Both men strongly agreed, saying, "Your vision is far-sighted and springs from deepest filial piety; it will benefit generations to come." The emperor personally oversaw the design; the tomb was finished in three months, less grand than the Chang Mausoleum, and later emperors followed this as the model. Only when the Jiajing Emperor built the Yong Mausoleum did tombs grow more lavish.
5
帝征樂安，義、原吉及諸學士皆從，預軍中機務，賜鞍馬甲胄弓劍。 及還，賚予甚厚。 三年從巡邊還。 帝以義、原吉、士奇、榮四人者皆已老，賜璽書曰：「卿等皆祖宗遺老，畀輔朕躬。 今黃髮危齒，不宜復典冗劇，傷朝廷優老待賢之禮。 可輟所務，朝夕在朕左右討論至理，共寧邦家。 官祿悉如舊。 」明年，郭琎代為尚書。 尋以胡濙言，命義等四人議天下官吏軍民建言章奏。 復賜義銀章，文曰「忠厚寬宏」。 七年詔有司為義營新第於文明門內。
When the emperor campaigned against Le'an, Jian Yi, Xia Yuanji, and the Hanlin academicians accompanied him, advising on military affairs, and were given saddles, horses, armor, bows, and swords. On their return he rewarded them lavishly. In year 3 he returned from accompanying the emperor on a border inspection. Seeing that Jian Yi, Xia Yuanji, Yang Shiqi, and Yang Rong were all advanced in years, the emperor sent a sealed letter: "You are all veterans left by my forefathers to assist me. With your hair white and your teeth failing, you should no longer bear heavy administrative burdens, lest the court fail in its duty to honor age and esteem talent. Lay aside your offices and stay at my side day and night to discuss great matters and help secure the realm. Your rank and salary shall remain unchanged. The following year Guo Jin replaced him as minister. Soon, on Hu Ying's advice, he ordered Jian Yi and the other three to review memorials submitted by officials, soldiers, and commoners throughout the realm. He again gave Jian Yi a silver seal bearing the words "Loyal, generous, broad, and magnanimous." In year 7 an edict ordered officials to build Jian Yi a new residence inside the Gate of Civilization.
6
英宗即位，齋宿得疾。 遣醫往視，問所欲言。 對曰：「陛下初嗣大寶，望敬守祖宗成憲，始終不渝耳。 」遂卒，年七十三。 贈太師，謚忠定。
When Emperor Yingzong came to the throne, Jian Yi fell ill while keeping ritual abstinence. The emperor sent a physician and asked if he had anything to say. He answered, "Your Majesty has just taken the throne; I beg you to reverently uphold our ancestors' established institutions and never waver from them." He then died, at the age of seventy-three. He was posthumously made Grand Preceptor with the posthumous title Loyal and Steadfast.
7
義為人質直孝友，善處僚友間，未嘗一語傷物。 士奇常言：「張詠之不飾玩好，傅堯俞之遇人以誠，範景仁之不設城府，義兼有之。 」子英，有詩名，以蔭為尚寶司丞，歷官太常少卿。
Jian Yi was plainspoken, upright, filial, and friendly; he handled colleagues well and never spoke a hurtful word. Shiqi often said, "Zhang Yong's freedom from vanity, Fu Yaoyu's sincerity with others, and Fan Jingren's guilelessness—Jian Yi has them all." His son Ying was known as a poet; through hereditary privilege he became a vice director in the Directorate of Ceremonial and eventually rose to Vice Minister of Rites.
8
夏原吉，字維喆，其先德興人。 父時敏，官湘陰教諭，遂家焉。 原吉早孤，力學養母。 以鄉薦入太學，選入禁中書制誥。 諸生或喧笑，原吉危坐儼然。 太祖诇而異之。 擢戶部主事。 曹務叢脞，處之悉有條理，尚書郁新甚重之。 有劉郎中者，忌其能。 會新劾諸司怠事者。 帝欲宥之，新持不可。 帝怒，問：「誰教若？ 」新頓首曰：「堂後書算生。 」帝乃下書算生於獄。 劉郎中遂言：「教尚書者，原吉也。 」帝曰：「原吉能佐尚書理部事，汝欲陷之耶！ 」劉郎中與書算生皆棄市。 建文初，擢戶部右侍郎。 明年充采訪使。 巡福建，所過郡邑，核吏治，咨民隱。 人皆悅服。 久之，移駐蘄州。 成祖即位，或執原吉以獻。 帝釋之，轉左侍郎。 或言原吉建文時用事，不可信。 帝不聽，與蹇義同進尚書。 偕義等詳定賦役諸制。 建白三十余事，皆簡便易遵守。 曰：「行之而難繼者，且重困民，吾不忍也。 」浙西大水，有司治不效。 永樂元年，命原吉治之。 尋命侍郎李文郁為之副，復使僉都御史俞士吉賫水利書賜之。 原吉請循禹三江入海故跡，浚吳淞下流，上接太湖，而度地為閘，以時蓄泄。 從之。 役十余萬人。 原吉布衣徒步，日夜經畫。 盛暑不張蓋，曰：「民勞，吾何忍獨適。 」事竣，還京師，言水雖由故道入海，而支流未盡疏泄，非經久計。 明年正月，原吉復行，浚白茆塘、劉家河、大黃浦。 大理少卿袁復為之副。 已，復命陜西參政宋性佐之。 九月工畢，水泄，蘇、松農田大利。 三年還。 其夏，浙西大饑。 命原吉率俞士吉、袁復及左通政趙居任往振，發粟三十萬石，給牛種。 有請召民佃水退淤田益賦者，原吉馳疏止之。 姚廣孝還自浙西，稱原吉曰：「古之遺愛也。 」亡何，郁新卒，召還，理部事。 首請裁冗食，平賦役； 嚴鹽法、錢鈔之禁； 清倉場，廣屯種，以給邊蘇民，且便商賈。 皆報可。 凡中外戶口、府庫、田賦贏縮之數，各以小簡書置懷中，時檢閱之。 一日，帝問：「天下錢、谷幾何？ 」對甚悉，以是益重之。 當是時，兵革初定，論「靖難」功臣封賞，分封諸藩，增設武衛百司。 已，又發卒八十萬問罪安南、中官造巨艦通海外諸國、大起北都宮闕。 供億轉輸以鉅萬萬計，皆取給戶曹。 原吉悉心計應之，國用不絀。
Xia Yuanji, courtesy name Weizhe, was descended from a family of Dexing. His father Shimin served as instructor in Xiangyin, and the family settled there. Yuanji lost his father young, studied hard, and supported his mother. Recommended from his home district, he entered the Imperial Academy and was chosen to draft edicts in the palace. While other students laughed and talked, Yuanji sat upright and composed. Taizu noticed and was impressed. He was promoted to a directorship in the Ministry of Revenue. Though ministry business was tangled and heavy, he handled everything in good order, and Minister Yu Xin valued him highly. A bureau director surnamed Liu resented his talent. Once Yu Xin impeached officials in various offices for neglecting their duties. The emperor wished to pardon them, but Yu Xin refused to yield. The emperor grew angry and demanded, "Who put you up to this? Yu Xin kowtowed and said, "The clerks behind the hall." The emperor had the clerks thrown into prison. Director Liu then said, "It was Yuanji who advised the minister." The emperor said, "Yuanji helps the minister run this ministry—are you trying to frame him!" Director Liu and the clerks were all executed in public. Early in the Jianwen reign he was made Right Vice Minister of Revenue. The following year he was appointed an investigating commissioner. Touring Fujian, he examined local governance and heard the people's grievances in every prefecture and county he visited. The people were won over. After some time he moved his headquarters to Qizhou. When the Yongle Emperor took the throne, some seized Xia Yuanji to hand him over as a prisoner. The emperor released him and made him Left Vice Minister. Some said that because Xia Yuanji had held office under Jianwen he could not be trusted. The emperor would not hear of it and promoted him to minister alongside Jian Yi. Together with Jian Yi and others he worked out the regulations on taxes and corvée labor in detail. He proposed more than thirty reforms, all simple and easy to follow. He said, "I will not propose measures that would be hard to sustain and would only burden the people further." Western Zhejiang suffered severe flooding, and local officials could not cope. In Yongle year 1 the emperor put Xia Yuanji in charge of relief. Soon Vice Minister Li Wenyu was appointed his deputy, and Censor-in-Chief Yu Shiji was sent with books on waterworks as imperial gifts. Xia Yuanji proposed following the ancient course of Yu's three rivers to the sea, dredging the lower Wusong to link with Lake Tai, and building sluice gates at suitable points to regulate storage and release. The emperor accepted his proposal. More than a hundred thousand laborers were mobilized. Xia Yuanji wore plain clothes and went on foot, planning the work day and night. In midsummer he refused a parasol, saying, "The people are toiling—how can I enjoy comfort alone? When the work was done he returned to the capital and reported that although water now reached the sea by the old channel, tributary streams were still not fully cleared—a short-term fix, not a lasting solution. The following first month Xia Yuanji went out again to dredge the Baimao Pond, the Liu Family River, and the Great Huangpu. Vice Minister of Justice Yuan Fu was appointed his deputy. Soon the Shaanxi administration vice commissioner Song Xing was sent to assist him as well. By the ninth month the work was finished, the waters drained, and farmland in Suzhou and Songjiang benefited greatly. In year 3 he returned. That summer western Zhejiang suffered severe famine. The emperor sent Xia Yuanji with Yu Shiji, Yuan Fu, and Left Administration Commissioner Zhao Juren to provide relief, distributing three hundred thousand piculs of grain and supplying oxen and seed. When some proposed recruiting peasants to farm reclaimed silt land and raise the tax assessment, Xia Yuanji sent an urgent memorial to stop it. Yao Guangxiao returned from western Zhejiang and said of Xia Yuanji, "He is a man of enduring benevolence such as antiquity knew." Before long Yu Xin died, and Xia Yuanji was recalled to run the ministry. He first proposed cutting redundant expenditures and equalizing taxes and corvée labor; strict enforcement of salt laws and prohibitions on paper money; clearing granaries, expanding military colonies to supply the frontiers and ease the people's burdens, and facilitating trade. The emperor approved every proposal. He kept slips in his pocket recording household counts, treasury balances, and land-tax surpluses and deficits throughout the realm, and reviewed them constantly. One day the emperor asked, "How much money and grain does the realm hold? He answered in full detail, and the emperor valued him all the more for it. At that time, with the realm newly pacified after the civil war, the court debated rewards for supporters of the Yongle succession, enfeoffed imperial princes, and expanded military guards and government offices. Then eight hundred thousand troops were sent to punish Annam, eunuchs built great ships to reach overseas countries, and construction of the Northern Capital palaces began on a vast scale. Supplies and transport costs ran into the tens of millions, all drawn from the Ministry of Revenue. Xia Yuanji planned carefully to meet every demand, and state finances never ran short.
9
六年命督軍民輸材北都，詔以錦衣官校從，治怠事者。 原吉慮犯者眾，告戒而後行，人皆感悅。
In year 6 he was ordered to supervise the transport of building materials to the Northern Capital, with Brocade Guard officers authorized to punish slack workers. Fearing many would be punished, Xia Yuanji warned the workers beforehand, and they were deeply grateful.
10
七年，帝北巡，命兼攝行在禮部、兵部、都察院事。 有二指揮冒月廩，帝欲斬之。 原吉曰：「非律也，假實為盜，將何以加？ 」乃止。
In year 7 the emperor toured the north and left him in charge of the traveling ministries of Rites and War and the Censorate. Two commanders had fraudulently drawn monthly rations, and the emperor wanted them executed. Xia Yuanji said, "That is not lawful; if false claims are treated as theft, what further penalty could there be? The emperor relented.
11
八年，帝北征，輔太孫留守北京，總行在九卿事。 時諸司草創，每旦，原吉入佐太孫參決庶務。 朝退，諸曹郎御史環請事。 原吉口答手書，不動聲色。 北達行在，南啟監國，京師肅然。 帝還，賜鈔幣、鞍馬、牢醴，慰勞有加。 尋從還南京，命侍太孫周行鄉落，觀民間疾苦。 原吉取齏黍以進，曰：「願殿下食此，知民艱。 」九載滿，與蹇義皆宴便殿，帝指二人謂群臣曰：「高皇帝養賢以貽朕。 欲觀古名臣，此其人矣。 」自是屢侍太孫，往來兩京，在道隨事納忠，多所裨益。
In year 8 the emperor campaigned north, leaving Xia Yuanji to assist the heir apparent in guarding Beijing and oversee the traveling court's nine ministries. With the offices still being set up, each morning Xia Yuanji helped the heir decide routine affairs. After court, bureau directors and censors crowded around him with questions. He answered orally and wrote replies without changing expression. He reported north to the traveling court and south to the regency in Nanjing, and both capitals ran in good order. When the emperor returned, he rewarded him with paper money, horses, and provisions, with special words of thanks. Soon he accompanied the emperor back to Nanjing and was ordered to accompany the heir on a tour of villages to see the people's hardships. Xia Yuanji brought coarse pickled grain for the heir to taste, saying, "I hope Your Highness will eat this and know the people's hardship. When nine years of service were complete, he and Jian Yi were feasted in a side hall; the emperor pointed to them and told the ministers, "Taizu nurtured worthy men for my sake. If you wish to see the great ministers of old, here they are." Thereafter he often attended the heir, traveling between the two capitals and offering loyal counsel along the way to great effect.
12
十八年，北京宮室成，使原吉南召太子、太孫。 既還，原吉言：「連歲營建，今告成。 宜撫流亡，蠲逋負以寬民力。 」明年，三殿災，原吉復申前請，亟命所司行之。 初以殿災詔求直言，群臣多言都北京非便。 帝怒，殺主事蕭儀，曰：「方遷都時，與大臣密議，久而後定，非輕舉也。 」言者因劾大臣。 帝命跪午門外質辨。 大臣爭詈言者，原吉獨奏曰：「彼應詔無罪。 臣等備員大臣，不能協贊大計，罪在臣等。 」帝意解，兩宥之。 或尤原吉背初議。 曰：「吾輩歷事久，言雖失，幸上憐之。 若言官得罪，所損不細矣。 」眾始嘆服。
In year 18, when the Beijing palaces were completed, Xia Yuanji was sent south to summon the crown prince and heir apparent. On his return Xia Yuanji said, "Construction has gone on for years; it is now finished. We should resettle refugees, remit tax arrears, and ease the people's burdens. The following year, when fire destroyed the three main halls, Xia Yuanji repeated his proposal, and the emperor immediately ordered it carried out. When the hall fire prompted an edict calling for frank counsel, many ministers argued that moving the capital to Beijing had been a mistake. The emperor was furious and executed Director Xiao Yi, saying, "When we moved the capital I deliberated at length with my senior ministers; it was no hasty decision. The memorialists then impeached the senior ministers. The emperor ordered them to kneel outside the Meridian Gate and debate the charges face to face. The senior ministers quarreled with the memorialists, but Xia Yuanji alone memorialized, "They answered the edict and are not guilty. We senior ministers failed to support your great design—the fault is ours." The emperor relented and pardoned both sides. Some blamed Xia Yuanji for going back on the ministers' earlier position. He said, "We have served long; though we were wrong, the emperor may yet show mercy. If remonstrating officials are punished, the harm will be far greater." Only then did his colleagues admire him.
13
原吉雖居戶部，國家大事輒令詳議。 帝每禦便殿闕門，召語移時，左右莫得聞。 退則恂恂若無預者。 交阯平，帝問：「遷官與賞孰便？ 」對曰：「賞費於一時，有限； 遷官為後日費，無窮也。 」從之。 西域法王來朝，帝欲郊勞，原吉不可。 及法王入，原吉見，不拜。 帝笑曰：「卿欲效韓愈耶？ 」山東唐賽兒反，事平，俘脅從者三千余人至。 原吉請於帝，悉原之。 谷王叛，帝疑長沙有通謀者。 原吉以百口保之，乃得寢。
Although Xia Yuanji headed the Ministry of Revenue, he was routinely consulted on major state affairs. The emperor often received him in a side hall and talked with him at length, out of earshot of attendants. Afterward he behaved with modest deference, as if he had played no special role. When Jiaozhi was pacified, the emperor asked, "Which is better, promoting officials or giving rewards? He answered, "Rewards are a one-time cost and limited; promotions burden the treasury forever." The emperor took his advice. When a Buddhist king from the Western Regions came to court, the emperor wished to greet him with suburban rites; Xia Yuanji objected. When the king entered, Xia Yuanji met him but did not bow. The emperor laughed and said, "Are you trying to imitate Han Yu? When Tang Sai'er rebelled in Shandong and was defeated, more than three thousand coerced followers were brought in as captives. Xia Yuanji asked the emperor to pardon them all. When the Prince of Gu rebelled, the emperor suspected collusion in Changsha. Xia Yuanji pledged his family's lives on their innocence, and the matter was dropped.
14
十九年冬，帝將大舉征沙漠。 命原吉與禮部尚書呂震、兵部尚書方賓、工部尚書吳中等議，皆言兵不當出。 未奏，會帝召賓，賓力言軍興費乏，帝不懌。 召原吉問邊儲多寡，對曰：「比年師出無功，軍馬儲蓄十喪八九，災眚叠作，內外俱疲。 況聖躬少安，尚須調護，乞遣將往征，勿勞車駕。 」帝怒，立命原吉出理開平糧儲。 而吳中入對如賓言，帝益怒。 召原吉系之內官監，並系大理丞鄒師顏，以嘗署戶部也。 賓懼自殺。 遂並籍原吉家，自賜鈔外，惟布衣瓦器。 明年北征，以糧盡引還。 已，復連歲出塞，皆不見敵。 還至榆木川，帝不豫，顧左右曰：「夏原吉愛我。 」崩聞至之三日，太子走系所，呼原吉，哭而告之。 原吉伏地哭，不能起。 太子令出獄，與議喪禮，復問赦詔所宜。 對以振饑、省賦役、罷西洋取寶船及雲南、交阯采辦諸道金銀課。 悉從之。
In the winter of year 19 the emperor planned a major campaign into the steppe. He ordered Xia Yuanji, Ministers Lü Zhen of Rites, Fang Bin of War, and Wu Zhong of Works, among others, to deliberate; all said the army should not march. Before they could memorialize, the emperor summoned Fang Bin, who argued forcefully that war would strain supplies; the emperor was displeased. He summoned Xia Yuanji and asked about border stores. Xia Yuanji answered, "Campaigns in recent years have achieved nothing; nine-tenths of military stores are gone; disasters follow one another; court and country alike are exhausted. Moreover Your Majesty has only lately recovered and still needs care; I beg you to send generals instead and not take the field yourself." The emperor was furious and immediately sent Xia Yuanji to manage grain stores at Kaiping. When Wu Zhong spoke to the emperor as Fang Bin had, the emperor grew still angrier. He had Xia Yuanji imprisoned in the eunuch directorate and also imprisoned Assistant Minister of Justice Zou Shiyan, who had once signed ministry documents. Fang Bin, in fear, killed himself. They inventoried his household and found nothing but plain clothes and earthenware vessels, apart from paper money the emperor had given him. The following year the northern campaign turned back for lack of supplies. For years afterward campaigns beyond the frontier found no enemy. Returning to the Elm River, the emperor fell ill and said to those around him, "Xia Yuanji loved me. Three days after news of the death arrived, the heir ran to the prison, called out Xia Yuanji, and weeping told him the news. Xia Yuanji lay prostrate weeping and could not rise. The heir ordered him released to help plan the funeral and asked what the amnesty edict should include. He proposed famine relief, tax reductions, ending treasure voyages to the Western Ocean, and halting gold and silver levies from Yunnan and Jiaozhi procurement. The heir adopted every recommendation.
15
仁宗即位，復其官。 方原吉在獄，有母喪，至是乞歸終制。 帝曰：「卿老臣，當與朕共濟艱難。 卿有喪，朕獨無喪乎？ 」厚賜之，令家人護喪，馳傳歸葬，有司治喪事。 原吉不敢復言。 尋加太子少傅。 呂震以太子少師班原吉上，帝命鴻臚引震列其下。 進少保，兼太子少傅、尚書如故，食三祿。 原吉固辭，乃聽辭太子少傅祿。 賜「繩愆糾繆」銀章，建第於兩京。
When Emperor Renzong came to the throne, he was reinstated in office. While Xia Yuanji was in prison his mother had died; he now asked to return home to complete mourning. The emperor said, "You are an elder minister; you should share hardship with me. You have lost your mother—have I not lost a parent as well? He gave him generous gifts, sent his family to escort the coffin home by relay post, and ordered officials to manage the funeral. Xia Yuanji did not dare raise the matter again. Soon he was made Junior Tutor of the Heir Apparent. Lü Zhen, as Junior Preceptor of the Heir, ranked above Xia Yuanji; the emperor ordered the Court of State Ceremonial to place Lü Zhen below him instead. He was promoted to Junior Guardian, retaining his posts as Junior Tutor of the Heir and minister, with the salary of three offices. Xia Yuanji firmly declined, and the emperor allowed him to give up the salary of Junior Tutor of the Heir. He received a silver seal bearing the words "Correcting faults and remedying errors," and mansions were built for him in both capitals.
16
已而仁宗崩，太子至自南京。 原吉奉遺詔迎於盧溝橋。 宣宗即位，以舊輔益親重。 明年，漢王高煦反，亦以「靖難」為辭，移檄罪狀諸大臣，以原吉為首。 帝夜召諸臣議。 楊榮首勸帝親征。 帝難之。 原吉曰：「獨不見李景隆已事耶？ 臣昨見所遣將，命下即色變，臨事可知矣。 且兵貴神速，卷甲趨之，所謂先人有奪人之心也。 榮策善。 」帝意遂決。 師還，賚予加等，賜閽者三人。 原吉以無功辭。 不聽。
Soon Emperor Renzong died, and the crown prince came up from Nanjing. Following the testamentary edict, Xia Yuanji welcomed him at the Lugou Bridge. When Emperor Xuanzong came to the throne, he treated this veteran counselor with even greater intimacy and trust. The following year the Prince of Han, Zhu Gaoxu, rebelled, likewise invoking the Pacification, and issued a proclamation denouncing the senior ministers, with Xia Yuanji listed first. The emperor summoned his ministers for a night council. Yang Rong was the first to urge the emperor to take the field in person. The emperor hesitated. Xia Yuanji said, "Have you forgotten what became of Li Jinglong? Yesterday I saw the generals you dispatched; the moment they received orders their faces fell—you can guess how they will perform in battle. Moreover, war favors speed; march at once in full armor—that is how you seize the initiative. Rong's plan is sound. The emperor's mind was made up. When the army returned, rewards were increased and he was given three gatekeepers. Xia Yuanji declined on the grounds that he had done nothing to deserve them. The emperor would not hear of it.
17
三年，從北巡。 帝取原吉橐糗嘗之，笑曰：「何惡也？ 」對曰：「軍中猶有餒者。 」帝命賜以大官之饌，且犒將士。 從閱武兔兒山，帝怒諸將慢，褫其衣。 原吉曰：「將帥，國爪牙，奈何凍而斃之？ 」反覆力諫。 帝曰：「為卿釋之。 」再與蹇義同賜銀印，文曰：「含弘貞靖。 」帝雅善繪事，嘗親畫《壽星圖》以賜。 其他圖畫、服食、器用、銀幣、玩好之賜，無虛日。 五年正月，兩朝實錄成，復賜金幣、鞍馬。 旦入謝，歸而卒，年六十五。 贈太師，謚忠靖。 敕戶部復其家，世世無所與。
In year 3 he accompanied the northern tour. The emperor took the dried grain from Xia Yuanji's pouch and tasted it, laughing, "How foul! He answered, "There are still hungry men in the army. The emperor ordered imperial provisions for him and rewards for the troops. At a military review on Tuer Mountain the emperor, angered by the generals' slackness, had their coats stripped off. Xia Yuanji said, "Generals are the state's claws and teeth—how can we let them freeze to death? He remonstrated again and again. The emperor said, "For your sake I will release them. He and Jian Yi were again given silver seals bearing the words "Broad, tolerant, upright, and tranquil." The emperor was an accomplished painter and once personally painted the "Picture of the Star of Longevity" as a gift. Other gifts of paintings, food, utensils, silver, and curios came almost every day. In the first month of year 5, when the Veritable Records of the two reigns were completed, he was again given gold, coins, saddles, and horses. He went in that morning to give thanks, returned home, and died at the age of sixty-five. He was posthumously honored as Grand Preceptor with the title Loyal and Tranquil. An edict ordered the Ministry of Revenue to restore his household to its former standing, exempting his descendants from levies forever.
18
原吉有雅景，人莫能測其際。 同列有善，即采納之。 或有小過，必為之掩覆。 吏汙所服金織賜衣。 原吉曰：「勿怖，汙可浣也。 」又有汙精微文書者，吏叩頭請死。 原吉不問，自入朝引咎，帝命易之。 呂震嘗傾原吉。 震為子乞官，原吉以震在「靖難」時有守城功，為之請。 平江伯陳瑄初亦惡原吉，原吉顧時時稱瑄才。 或問原吉：「量可學乎？ 」曰：「吾幼時，有犯未嘗不怒。 始忍於色，中忍於心，久則無可忍矣。 」嘗夜閱爰書，撫案而嘆，筆欲下輒止。 妻問之。 曰：「此歲終大辟奏也。 」與同列飲他所，夜歸值雪，過禁門，有欲不下者，原吉曰：「君子不以冥冥墮行。 」其慎如此。
Xia Yuanji was magnanimous beyond measure. When colleagues had good ideas, he adopted them at once. If they slipped, he always covered for them. A clerk stained one of his gold-woven court robes. Xia Yuanji said, "Do not be afraid; stains can be washed out. When another clerk ruined an important document, the man kowtowed and begged for death. Xia Yuanji said nothing; he went to court himself to accept blame, and the emperor ordered a replacement. Lü Zhen had once tried to undermine Xia Yuanji. When Lü Zhen asked an office for his son, Xia Yuanji pleaded for him, citing Lü Zhen's merit in defending the city during the civil war. The Earl of Pingjiang, Chen Xuan, had also disliked him at first, yet Xia Yuanji often praised Chen Xuan's abilities. Someone asked Xia Yuanji, "Can magnanimity be learned? He said, "When I was young I always grew angry when offended. At first I hid it in my face, then in my heart; in time there was nothing I could not bear. Once at night, reviewing death-sentence cases, he stroked his desk and sighed, his brush poised but unable to descend. His wife asked why. He said, "This is the year-end report on capital sentences. Returning late from drinking with colleagues in the snow, some wished not to dismount at the forbidden gate; Xia Yuanji said, "A gentleman does not let his conduct slip in the dark. Such was his scrupulousness.
19
原吉與義皆起家太祖時。 義秉銓政，原吉筦度支，皆二十七年，名位先於三楊。 仁、宣之世，外兼臺省，內參館閣，與三楊同心輔政。 義善謀，榮善斷，而原吉與士奇尤持大體，有古大臣風烈。
Xia Yuanji and Jian Yi both began their careers under Taizu. Jian Yi held personnel power and Xia Yuanji managed finances, each for twenty-seven years, ranking before the Three Yangs. Under Emperors Ren and Xuan they held censorate and provincial posts abroad and advised from the Hanlin within, governing in concert with the Three Yangs. Jian Yi excelled at planning, Yang Rong at decision, while Xia Yuanji and Yang Shiqi especially upheld the larger interest and bore the manner of great ministers of old.
20
子瑄，以蔭為尚寶司丞。 喜談兵。 景泰時，數上章言兵事，有沮者，不獲用。 終南京太常少卿。
His son Xuan became a vice director in the Directorate of Ceremonial through hereditary privilege. He liked to talk about military affairs. Under the Jingtai Emperor he repeatedly memorialized on military affairs, but opponents blocked him and his proposals were never adopted. He ended his career as Vice Minister of Rites at Nanjing.
21
李文郁，襄陽人。 永樂初，以戶部侍郎副原吉治水有勞。 後坐事謫遼東二十年。 仁宗即位，召還，為南京通政參議，致仕。
Li Wenyu was from Xiangyang. Early in Yongle he served as Vice Minister of Revenue under Xia Yuanji on waterworks and performed with distinction. Later he was banished to Liaodong for twenty years after an offense. When Emperor Renzong came to the throne he was recalled, made a vice commissioner in the Nanjing Office of Transmission, and retired.
22
鄒師顏，宣都人。 永樂初，為江西參政，坐事免。 尋以薦擢御史，有直聲。 遷大理丞，署戶部。 與原吉同下獄。 仁宗立，釋為禮部侍郎。 省墓歸，還至通州，卒，貧不能歸葬。 尚書呂震聞於朝，宣宗命驛舟送之。 詔京官卒者，皆給驛，著為令。
Zou Shiyan was from Yidu. Early in Yongle he served as administration vice commissioner of Jiangxi and was dismissed after an offense. Soon he was recommended and made a censor, earning a reputation for blunt honesty. He was transferred to assistant minister of justice and signed ministry documents for the Ministry of Revenue. He was imprisoned together with Xia Yuanji. When Emperor Renzong came to the throne he was released and made Vice Minister of Rites. Returning from tending his parents' graves, he reached Tongzhou on the way home and died, too poor to afford burial transport. Minister Lü Zhen reported this at court, and Emperor Xuanzong ordered a courier boat to return his body. An edict provided relay transport for all capital officials who died away from home, and this became permanent regulation.
23
贊曰：《書》曰「敷求哲人，俾輔於爾後嗣」。 蹇義、夏原吉自筮仕之初，即以誠篤幹濟受知太祖，至成祖，益任以繁劇。 而二人實能通達政體，諳練章程，稱股肱之任。 仁、宣繼體，委寄優隆，同德協心，匡翼令主。 用使吏治修明，民風和樂，成績懋著，蔚為宗臣。 樹人之效，遠矣哉。
The commentator says: The Book of Documents says, "Seek wise men broadly to assist your successors." From the start of their careers Jian Yi and Xia Yuanji won Taizu's trust through sincerity and practical ability; under the Yongle Emperor they were entrusted with ever heavier duties. Both truly understood how government worked and knew regulations inside out, worthy of the name right-hand men. Under Emperors Ren and Xuan they enjoyed the highest trust, working in concert to support excellent rulers. They helped make governance clear, the people content, and achievements splendid, rising to become pillar ministers of the dynasty. How far-reaching is the effect of nurturing worthy men!
