1
蹇義夏原吉 〈 （俞士吉李文郁鄒師顏）〉
Jian Yi and Xia Yuanji ( (See also the biographies of Yu Shiji, Li Wenyu, and Zou Shiyan.)〉
2
燕師入，迎附，遷左侍郎。 數月，進尚書。 時方務反建文之政，所更易者悉罷之。 義從容言曰：「損益貴適時宜。 前改者固不當，今必欲盡復者，亦未悉當也。 」因舉數事陳說本末。 帝稱善，從其言。
When the Prince of Yan's army entered the capital, he welcomed the new regime and was appointed Left Vice Minister of Personnel. Within a few months he was promoted to minister. The court was then intent on reversing the policies of the Jianwen reign, and every measure that had been altered was abolished wholesale. Jian said evenly, "What to add or cut should be judged by what suits the times. The earlier reforms were indeed wrong, but to insist now on restoring everything is not necessarily right either. He then cited several cases and explained how each had come about. The emperor approved and adopted his advice.
3
永樂二年兼太子詹事。 帝有所傳諭太子，輒遣義，能委曲導意。 帝與太子俱愛重之。 七年，帝巡北京，命輔皇太子監國。 義熟典故，達治體，軍國事皆倚辦。 時舊臣見親用者，戶部尚書夏原吉與義齊名，中外稱曰「蹇、夏」。 滿三考，帝親宴二人便殿，褒揚甚至。 數奉命兼理他部事，職務填委，處之裕如。 十七年以父喪歸，帝及太子皆遣官賜祭。 詔起復。 十九年，三殿災，敕廷臣二十六人巡行天下。 義及給事中馬俊分巡應天諸府，問軍民疾苦，黜文武長吏擾民者數人，條興革數十事奏行之。 還治部事。 明年，帝北征還，以太子曲宥呂震婿主事張鶴朝參失儀，罪義不匡正，逮義系錦衣衛獄。 又明年春得釋。
In Yongle 2 he was additionally appointed Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent. Whenever the emperor wished to instruct the heir, he sent Jian, who could convey the intent with tact and finesse. Both emperor and heir held him in the highest regard. In the seventh year, when the emperor went to Beijing, he was ordered to assist the heir in governing the realm. Jian was steeped in precedent and understood how government worked; military and civil affairs alike were entrusted to him. Among the veteran ministers then in favor, Minister of Revenue Xia Yuanji stood equal with Jian in renown; court and country called them "Jian and Xia." When their three-year assessments were complete, the emperor personally entertained them in the side hall and praised them at length. He was repeatedly ordered to take charge of other ministries as well; though duties piled upon him, he managed them with ease. In the seventeenth year he went home for his father's mourning; both emperor and heir sent officials with offerings. An edict recalled him from mourning to office. In the nineteenth year, when fire destroyed the three main halls, twenty-six court ministers were ordered to tour the empire. Jian and supervising secretary Ma Jun toured the prefectures around Yingtian, inquired into the hardships of soldiers and civilians, dismissed several civil and military chiefs who harassed the people, and submitted dozens of proposals for reform, which were put into effect. He then returned to his ministry duties. The next year, when the emperor returned from the northern campaign, the heir had bent the rules to spare Lü Zhen's son-in-law Zhang He for impropriety at audience. Jian was blamed for failing to correct this and was imprisoned in the Brocade Guard jail. He was released the following spring.
4
仁宗即位，義、原吉皆以元老為中外所信。 帝又念義監國時舊勞，尤厚倚之。 首進義少保，賜冠服、象笏、玉帶，兼食二祿。 歷進少師，賜銀章一，文曰「繩愆糾繆」。 已，復賜璽書曰：「曩朕監國，卿以先朝舊臣，日侍左右。 兩京肇建，政務方殷，卿勞心焦思，不恤身家，二十余年，夷險一節。 朕承大統，贊襄治理，不懈益恭。 朕篤念不忘，茲以已意，創制『蹇忠貞印』賜卿。 俾藏於家，傳之後世，知朕君臣共濟艱難，相與有成也。 」時惟楊士奇亦得賜「貞一」印及敕。 尋命與英國公輔及原吉同監修《太宗實錄》。 義視原吉尤重厚，然過於周慎。 士奇嘗於帝前謂義曰：「何過慮？ 」義曰：「恐鹵莽為後憂耳。 」帝兩是之。 楊榮嘗毀義。 帝不直榮。 義頓首言：「榮無他。 即左右有讒榮者，願陛下慎察。 」帝笑曰：「吾固弗信也。 」宣宗即位，委寄益重。 時方修獻陵，帝欲遵遺詔從儉約，以問義、原吉。 二人力贊曰：「聖見高遠，出於至孝，萬世之利也。 」帝親為規畫，三月而陵成，宏麗不及長陵，其後諸帝因以為制。 迨世宗營永陵，始益崇侈雲。
When Emperor Renzong ascended the throne, both Jian and Yuanji were trusted throughout the realm as elder statesmen. The emperor also remembered Jian's service during the regency and relied on him all the more. He first promoted Jian to Junior Guardian, granting court dress, an ivory tablet, and a jade belt, with emoluments from two offices. He was further promoted to Junior Preceptor and given a silver seal inscribed "Correct faults and rectify errors." He then granted another sealed letter: "When I governed the realm as heir, you, an old minister of the previous reign, attended me day after day. As the two capitals were built and affairs pressed upon us, you toiled without regard for yourself or your family. For more than twenty years, through calm and crisis alike, your constancy never wavered. Since I took the throne you have aided my rule, tireless and ever more devoted. I hold this in deep remembrance. By my own wish I have had made the "Seal of Jian's Loyalty and Integrity" and grant it to you. Keep it in your house and pass it to posterity, so they may know how we ruler and minister shared hardship and achieved success together." At that time only Yang Shiqi likewise received the "Integrity and Unity" seal and an edict. Soon he was ordered, together with the Duke of Ying and Xia Yuanji, to supervise compilation of the 《Veritable Records of Emperor Taizong》. Jian esteemed Yuanji as especially solid and dependable, yet he himself was overly cautious in every detail. Shiqi once said to Jian before the emperor, "Why worry so much? Jian replied, "I fear that rashness today may bring trouble tomorrow." The emperor approved both views. Yang Rong once spoke ill of Jian. The emperor did not accept Rong's account. Jian kowtowed and said, "Rong means no harm. If anyone at your side speaks against him, I beg Your Majesty to look closely before you judge." The emperor smiled and said, "I never believed it in the first place." When Emperor Xuanzong ascended the throne, his reliance on Jian grew still greater. While the Xian Mausoleum was under construction, the emperor wished to follow the late emperor's testament and keep it frugal, and consulted Jian and Yuanji. Both strongly endorsed the plan: "Your vision is far-sighted and born of deepest filial piety; it will benefit generations to come. The emperor personally oversaw the design. The tomb was finished in three months—splendid, but less grand than the Chang Mausoleum—and later emperors took this as the model." Only when Emperor Shizong built the Yong Mausoleum did tombs grow markedly more lavish.
5
帝征樂安，義、原吉及諸學士皆從，預軍中機務，賜鞍馬甲胄弓劍。 及還，賚予甚厚。 三年從巡邊還。 帝以義、原吉、士奇、榮四人者皆已老，賜璽書曰：「卿等皆祖宗遺老，畀輔朕躬。 今黃髮危齒，不宜復典冗劇，傷朝廷優老待賢之禮。 可輟所務，朝夕在朕左右討論至理，共寧邦家。 官祿悉如舊。 」明年，郭琎代為尚書。 尋以胡濙言，命義等四人議天下官吏軍民建言章奏。 復賜義銀章，文曰「忠厚寬宏」。 七年詔有司為義營新第於文明門內。
When the emperor campaigned against Le'an, Jian, Yuanji, and the academicians accompanied him, took part in military planning, and were granted saddles, horses, armor, bows, and swords. On their return, the rewards were exceptionally generous. In the third year he returned from accompanying the border inspection. Seeing that Jian, Yuanji, Shiqi, and Rong were all advanced in years, the emperor granted a sealed letter: "You are elders left by our forefathers to assist me. Your hair is white and your steps unsteady; it is no longer fitting that you bear heavy routine duties, to the detriment of the court's duty to honor age and worth. Lay aside your offices and remain at my side morning and evening to discuss great affairs, so that together we may secure the realm." Your salaries and rank would remain unchanged. The following year Guo Jin replaced him as minister. Soon, on Hu Ying's advice, Jian and the other three were ordered to review memorials from officials, soldiers, and civilians throughout the realm. Jian was again granted a silver seal inscribed "Loyal, generous, broad, and magnanimous." In the seventh year an edict ordered officials to build Jian a new residence inside the Wenhua Gate.
6
英宗即位，齋宿得疾。 遣醫往視，問所欲言。 對曰：「陛下初嗣大寶，望敬守祖宗成憲，始終不渝耳。 」遂卒，年七十三。 贈太師，謚忠定。
When Emperor Yingzong ascended the throne, Jian fell ill while observing ritual purification. Physicians were sent to attend him and asked whether he had anything to say. He answered, "Your Majesty has newly taken the throne. I beg you to honor and keep the institutions of our ancestors, unchanged to the end." He then died, aged seventy-three. He was posthumously honored as Grand Preceptor with the posthumous title Loyal and Settled.
7
義為人質直孝友，善處僚友間，未嘗一語傷物。 士奇常言：「張詠之不飾玩好，傅堯俞之遇人以誠，範景仁之不設城府，義兼有之。 」子英，有詩名，以蔭為尚寶司丞，歷官太常少卿。
Jian was plain, upright, filial, and friendly; he handled relations among colleagues well and never spoke a word that wounded another. Shiqi often said, "Zhang Yong's disdain for ornament, Fu Yaoyu's sincerity toward others, Fan Jingren's open heart—Jian possessed all three." His son Ying was known as a poet; by hereditary privilege he became vice commissioner of the Imperial Seals Office and eventually Vice Minister of Rites.
8
夏原吉，字維喆，其先德興人。 父時敏，官湘陰教諭，遂家焉。 原吉早孤，力學養母。 以鄉薦入太學，選入禁中書制誥。 諸生或喧笑，原吉危坐儼然。 太祖诇而異之。 擢戶部主事。 曹務叢脞，處之悉有條理，尚書郁新甚重之。 有劉郎中者，忌其能。 會新劾諸司怠事者。 帝欲宥之，新持不可。 帝怒，問：「誰教若？ 」新頓首曰：「堂後書算生。 」帝乃下書算生於獄。 劉郎中遂言：「教尚書者，原吉也。 」帝曰：「原吉能佐尚書理部事，汝欲陷之耶！ 」劉郎中與書算生皆棄市。 建文初，擢戶部右侍郎。 明年充采訪使。 巡福建，所過郡邑，核吏治，咨民隱。 人皆悅服。 久之，移駐蘄州。 成祖即位，或執原吉以獻。 帝釋之，轉左侍郎。 或言原吉建文時用事，不可信。 帝不聽，與蹇義同進尚書。 偕義等詳定賦役諸制。 建白三十余事，皆簡便易遵守。 曰：「行之而難繼者，且重困民，吾不忍也。 」浙西大水，有司治不效。 永樂元年，命原吉治之。 尋命侍郎李文郁為之副，復使僉都御史俞士吉賫水利書賜之。 原吉請循禹三江入海故跡，浚吳淞下流，上接太湖，而度地為閘，以時蓄泄。 從之。 役十余萬人。 原吉布衣徒步，日夜經畫。 盛暑不張蓋，曰：「民勞，吾何忍獨適。 」事竣，還京師，言水雖由故道入海，而支流未盡疏泄，非經久計。 明年正月，原吉復行，浚白茆塘、劉家河、大黃浦。 大理少卿袁復為之副。 已，復命陜西參政宋性佐之。 九月工畢，水泄，蘇、松農田大利。 三年還。 其夏，浙西大饑。 命原吉率俞士吉、袁復及左通政趙居任往振，發粟三十萬石，給牛種。 有請召民佃水退淤田益賦者，原吉馳疏止之。 姚廣孝還自浙西，稱原吉曰：「古之遺愛也。 」亡何，郁新卒，召還，理部事。 首請裁冗食，平賦役； 嚴鹽法、錢鈔之禁； 清倉場，廣屯種，以給邊蘇民，且便商賈。 皆報可。 凡中外戶口、府庫、田賦贏縮之數，各以小簡書置懷中，時檢閱之。 一日，帝問：「天下錢、谷幾何？ 」對甚悉，以是益重之。 當是時，兵革初定，論「靖難」功臣封賞，分封諸藩，增設武衛百司。 已，又發卒八十萬問罪安南、中官造巨艦通海外諸國、大起北都宮闕。 供億轉輸以鉅萬萬計，皆取給戶曹。 原吉悉心計應之，國用不絀。
Xia Yuanji, courtesy name Weizhe, came of a family from Dexing. His father Shimin was an instructor in Xiangyin, and the family settled there. Yuanji lost his father early, studied diligently, and supported his mother. Recommended from his district, he entered the Imperial Academy and was chosen to draft edicts in the palace. While other students laughed and talked, Yuanji sat upright and composed. Taizu noticed and took him for remarkable. He was promoted to director in the Ministry of Revenue. Though ministry business was tangled and heavy, he handled everything in good order, and Minister Yu Xin held him in high regard. A certain Director Liu envied his talent. Once Xin impeached officials in various offices for neglect of duty. The emperor wished to pardon them, but Xin refused to yield. The emperor grew angry and demanded, "Who put you up to this? Xin kowtowed and answered, "The clerks behind the hall." The emperor then had those clerks imprisoned. Director Liu then said, "It was Yuanji who advised the minister." The emperor said, "Yuanji helps the minister run this ministry—do you mean to ruin him?" Director Liu and the clerks were all executed in the marketplace. At the start of the Jianwen reign he was promoted to Right Vice Minister of Revenue. The following year he was appointed investigation commissioner. Touring Fujian, he examined local governance and heard the people's grievances in every prefecture and county he visited. All were won over. After some time he was posted to Qizhou. When Chengzu took the throne, some seized Yuanji to present him as a captive. The emperor released him and appointed him Left Vice Minister. Some said that Yuanji had held power under Jianwen and could not be trusted. The emperor paid no heed and promoted him to minister together with Jian Yi. Together with Jian and others he worked out the regulations on taxes and corvée labor in detail. He proposed more than thirty measures, all simple and easy to enforce. He said, "Measures that would be hard to sustain in practice would only burden the people further, and I cannot bear that." Western Zhejiang suffered severe flooding, and local officials could not control it. In Yongle 1 he was ordered to take charge of the relief effort. Soon Vice Minister Li Wenyu was appointed his deputy, and Censor-in-Chief Yu Shiji was again dispatched to present him with an imperial treatise on waterworks. Yuanji proposed restoring the old course by which Yu the Great's three rivers had reached the sea: dredging the lower Wusong, connecting it upstream to Tai Lake, and building sluice gates at measured points to store and release water as seasons required. The emperor approved the plan. More than a hundred thousand men were put to work. Yuanji wore plain clothes, walked on foot, and directed the work day and night. In the height of summer he would not raise a sunshade, saying, "The people are laboring—how can I alone seek ease?" When the work was done he returned to the capital and reported that although the main channel again reached the sea, tributaries were still not fully cleared and drained, and this was no lasting remedy. In the first month of the following year Yuanji went out again to dredge Baimao Pond, the Liujia River, and the Great Huangpu. Vice Minister of Justice Yuan Fu was appointed his deputy. Soon afterward Shaanxi Administrative Commissioner Song Xing was also ordered to assist him. By the ninth month the work was complete, the waters had drained, and the fields of Suzhou and Songjiang profited greatly. After three years he returned. That summer western Zhejiang was stricken with severe famine. Yuanji was ordered to lead Yu Shiji, Yuan Fu, and Left Commissioner Zhao Juren in famine relief, distributing three hundred thousand shi of grain and providing oxen and seed. When some proposed summoning the people to cultivate newly exposed silt fields and raise the tax assessment, Yuanji sent an urgent memorial to forbid it. On returning from western Zhejiang, Yao Guangxiao praised Yuanji, saying, "This is the enduring benevolence of the ancients." Before long Yu Xin died, and Yuanji was recalled to manage the ministry's affairs. His first proposals were to cut redundant posts and equalize taxes and corvée labor; to enforce the salt laws and restrictions on coin and paper money; to clear the granary depots and expand garrison farming, thereby supplying the frontier, easing the people's burden, and aiding merchants. All were approved. He wrote the figures for households across the empire, treasuries, storehouses, and gains and losses in land tax on small slips, kept them in his robe, and reviewed them constantly. One day the emperor asked, "How much money and grain does the realm hold?" Yuanji answered in full detail, and the emperor came to rely on him all the more. At that time, with the wars newly ended, the court debated rewards for the Jingnan meritorious, enfeoffed the princely domains, and expanded the military guards and myriad offices. Then he again sent eight hundred thousand troops to punish Annam, had eunuchs build great ships to reach overseas states, and raised the palace halls of the Northern Capital on a vast scale. Supplies and transport costs ran into the tens of millions, all to be met by the Ministry of Revenue. Yuanji calculated every need with care, and the treasury never fell short.
9
六年命督軍民輸材北都，詔以錦衣官校從，治怠事者。 原吉慮犯者眾，告戒而後行，人皆感悅。
In the sixth year he was ordered to oversee the delivery of building materials to the Northern Capital by soldiers and civilians; an edict sent brocade-clad guard officers with him to punish slackness. Fearing that many would offend, Yuanji issued warnings before setting out, and all were grateful and reassured.
10
七年，帝北巡，命兼攝行在禮部、兵部、都察院事。 有二指揮冒月廩，帝欲斬之。 原吉曰：「非律也，假實為盜，將何以加？ 」乃止。
In the seventh year, when the emperor toured the north, Yuanji was ordered concurrently to handle the affairs of the traveling palace Ministries of Rites and War and of the Censorate. Two commanders had fraudulently drawn monthly rations, and the emperor wished to execute them. Yuanji said, "That is not what the law allows. If borrowing were treated as theft, what punishment would remain for actual theft?" The emperor then stayed his hand.
11
八年，帝北征，輔太孫留守北京，總行在九卿事。 時諸司草創，每旦，原吉入佐太孫參決庶務。 朝退，諸曹郎御史環請事。 原吉口答手書，不動聲色。 北達行在，南啟監國，京師肅然。 帝還，賜鈔幣、鞍馬、牢醴，慰勞有加。 尋從還南京，命侍太孫周行鄉落，觀民間疾苦。 原吉取齏黍以進，曰：「願殿下食此，知民艱。 」九載滿，與蹇義皆宴便殿，帝指二人謂群臣曰：「高皇帝養賢以貽朕。 欲觀古名臣，此其人矣。 」自是屢侍太孫，往來兩京，在道隨事納忠，多所裨益。
In the eighth year the emperor marched north; Yuanji assisted the Grand Heir Apparent in guarding Beijing and directed the affairs of the Nine Ministers at the traveling palace. The offices were then still being established; each morning Yuanji entered to help the Grand Heir Apparent decide routine business. After court, department directors, secretaries, and censors gathered round him with matters for decision. Yuanji answered aloud even as he wrote, never altering voice or expression. Reports went north to the traveling palace; memorials went south to the regent—and the capital ran in sober order. When the emperor returned, he bestowed paper money, saddled horses, and meat and wine, rewarding Yuanji with special warmth. Soon he accompanied the court back to Nanjing and was ordered to attend the Grand Heir Apparent on a tour of villages to witness the people's hardships. Yuanji brought coarse millet and offered it, saying, "May Your Highness eat this and know the people's hardship." When nine years of service were complete, he and Jian Yi were both feasted in the Informal Hall. The emperor pointed to the two and told the assembled ministers, "The High Emperor raised worthy men and left them to me." If you would see the great ministers of antiquity, here they are." From then on he often attended the Grand Heir Apparent, traveling between the two capitals and offering loyal counsel on whatever arose, to great benefit.
12
十八年，北京宮室成，使原吉南召太子、太孫。 既還，原吉言：「連歲營建，今告成。 宜撫流亡，蠲逋負以寬民力。 」明年，三殿災，原吉復申前請，亟命所司行之。 初以殿災詔求直言，群臣多言都北京非便。 帝怒，殺主事蕭儀，曰：「方遷都時，與大臣密議，久而後定，非輕舉也。 」言者因劾大臣。 帝命跪午門外質辨。 大臣爭詈言者，原吉獨奏曰：「彼應詔無罪。 臣等備員大臣，不能協贊大計，罪在臣等。 」帝意解，兩宥之。 或尤原吉背初議。 曰：「吾輩歷事久，言雖失，幸上憐之。 若言官得罪，所損不細矣。 」眾始嘆服。
In the eighteenth year, when the Beijing palace was finished, Yuanji was sent south to summon the Crown Prince and Grand Heir Apparent. On his return Yuanji said, "Construction has continued year after year; now it is declared complete. It is time to succor the displaced, remit overdue taxes, and ease the people's burden." The next year the Three Main Halls burned. Yuanji renewed his earlier request, and the emperor urgently ordered the responsible offices to act on it. After the hall fire an edict first called for blunt counsel, and many ministers argued that keeping the capital at Beijing was unwise. The emperor was enraged and executed Director Xiao Yi, saying, "When the capital was moved, I deliberated at length in secret with the senior ministers. It was no hasty decision." The memorialists then impeached the senior ministers. The emperor ordered them to kneel outside the Meridian Gate and answer the charges in person. The ministers quarreled and reviled the memorialists, but Yuanji alone submitted, "They answered the edict and are not guilty. We senior ministers failed to unite in support of the great plan—the fault is ours." The emperor's anger eased, and he pardoned both sides. Some reproached Yuanji for going back on the earlier understanding. He said, "We have served long. Though our words were wrong, the emperor may yet show us mercy. But if the remonstrating officers were punished, the harm would be far greater." Only then did the others sigh and acknowledge he was right.
13
原吉雖居戶部，國家大事輒令詳議。 帝每禦便殿闕門，召語移時，左右莫得聞。 退則恂恂若無預者。 交阯平，帝問：「遷官與賞孰便？ 」對曰：「賞費於一時，有限； 遷官為後日費，無窮也。 」從之。 西域法王來朝，帝欲郊勞，原吉不可。 及法王入，原吉見，不拜。 帝笑曰：「卿欲效韓愈耶？ 」山東唐賽兒反，事平，俘脅從者三千余人至。 原吉請於帝，悉原之。 谷王叛，帝疑長沙有通謀者。 原吉以百口保之，乃得寢。
Though Yuanji served in the Ministry of Revenue, on great affairs of state he was always summoned to deliberate in detail. The emperor often received him at the Informal Hall by Que Gate and talked with him at length; those nearby could not hear what was said. On withdrawing he was modest and deferential, as though he had played no part in the matter. When Jiaozhi was pacified, the emperor asked, "Which is better—to transfer officials or to grant rewards?" He answered, "Rewards are a one-time expense and finite; transferring officials creates expense for years to come, without end." The emperor accepted his advice. When the Dharma King of the Western Regions came to court, the emperor wished to greet him with full suburban ceremony, but Yuanji objected. When the Dharma King entered, Yuanji met him but did not bow. The emperor laughed and said, "Do you mean to imitate Han Yu?" In Shandong Tang Sai'er rose in rebellion; when the affair was settled, more than three thousand coerced followers taken captive were brought to court. Yuanji asked the emperor to pardon them all. When the Prince of Gu, Zhu Tan, rebelled, the emperor suspected conspirators in Changsha. Yuanji pledged the lives of his entire clan in their defense, and the investigation was dropped.
14
十九年冬，帝將大舉征沙漠。 命原吉與禮部尚書呂震、兵部尚書方賓、工部尚書吳中等議，皆言兵不當出。 未奏，會帝召賓，賓力言軍興費乏，帝不懌。 召原吉問邊儲多寡，對曰：「比年師出無功，軍馬儲蓄十喪八九，災眚叠作，內外俱疲。 況聖躬少安，尚須調護，乞遣將往征，勿勞車駕。 」帝怒，立命原吉出理開平糧儲。 而吳中入對如賓言，帝益怒。 召原吉系之內官監，並系大理丞鄒師顏，以嘗署戶部也。 賓懼自殺。 遂並籍原吉家，自賜鈔外，惟布衣瓦器。 明年北征，以糧盡引還。 已，復連歲出塞，皆不見敵。 還至榆木川，帝不豫，顧左右曰：「夏原吉愛我。 」崩聞至之三日，太子走系所，呼原吉，哭而告之。 原吉伏地哭，不能起。 太子令出獄，與議喪禮，復問赦詔所宜。 對以振饑、省賦役、罷西洋取寶船及雲南、交阯采辦諸道金銀課。 悉從之。
In the winter of the nineteenth year the emperor planned a major campaign into the desert. He ordered Yuanji, together with Ministers of Rites Lu Zhen, War Fang Bin, and Works Wu Zhong and others, to deliberate; all said the army should not march. Before they could submit their report, the emperor summoned Fang Bin, who forcefully argued that war would strain supplies; the emperor was displeased. He then summoned Yuanji and asked how much lay in frontier stores. Yuanji answered, "In recent years our armies have marched without success, and nine tenths of military stores are gone. Disasters have followed one upon another; court and country alike are exhausted. Moreover Your Majesty has only lately recovered and still needs care. I beg that generals be sent to campaign and that the imperial carriage not be put to the hardship." The emperor was furious and immediately ordered Yuanji out to manage grain stores at Kaiping. When Wu Zhong entered audience and spoke as Fang Bin had, the emperor grew still angrier. He had Yuanji seized and imprisoned in the Directorate of Palace Eunuchs, and also imprisoned Assistant Minister of Justice Zou Shiyan, who had once acted for the Ministry of Revenue. Fang Bin, in fear, took his own life. They then inventoried Yuanji's household; apart from imperial gifts of paper money, he owned only plain clothes and earthenware vessels. The next year, on the northern campaign, supplies ran out and the army withdrew. After that the emperor campaigned beyond the frontier year after year, yet never encountered the enemy. On returning to Yumu Stream the emperor fell ill and, turning to those beside him, said, "Xia Yuanji truly cares for me." Three days after word of the emperor's death arrived, the Crown Prince ran to the prison, called for Yuanji, and weeping told him the news. Yuanji prostrated himself and wept until he could not rise. The Crown Prince ordered him released to help plan the funeral rites and again asked what the amnesty edict should contain. He answered: famine relief, reduced taxes and corvée, an end to the Western Ocean treasure fleets, and the abolition of gold and silver procurement missions in Yunnan and Jiaozhi. All were adopted.
15
仁宗即位，復其官。 方原吉在獄，有母喪，至是乞歸終制。 帝曰：「卿老臣，當與朕共濟艱難。 卿有喪，朕獨無喪乎？ 」厚賜之，令家人護喪，馳傳歸葬，有司治喪事。 原吉不敢復言。 尋加太子少傅。 呂震以太子少師班原吉上，帝命鴻臚引震列其下。 進少保，兼太子少傅、尚書如故，食三祿。 原吉固辭，乃聽辭太子少傅祿。 賜「繩愆糾繆」銀章，建第於兩京。
When Emperor Renzong ascended the throne, Yuanji's office was restored. While Yuanji was in prison his mother had died; he now asked leave to return home and observe full mourning. The emperor said, "You are a veteran minister and should share these hard times with me. You have lost your mother—do you think I have lost nothing?" He gave Yuanji rich gifts, ordered his family to escort the coffin, sent post-horses to hurry him home for burial, and had the officials manage the funeral. Yuanji did not dare raise the matter again. Soon he was further appointed Junior Preceptor of the Heir Apparent. Lu Zhen, though senior as Junior Preceptor of the Heir Apparent, had ranked above Yuanji; the emperor ordered the Director of Ceremonial to conduct Lu Zhen to take his place below him. He was promoted to Junior Guardian, retaining his post as minister while also serving as Junior Preceptor of the Heir Apparent, with emoluments from three offices. Yuanji firmly declined; the emperor then allowed him to forgo the Junior Preceptor's stipend. He received a silver seal inscribed "Correct faults and rectify errors," and mansions were built for him in both capitals.
16
已而仁宗崩，太子至自南京。 原吉奉遺詔迎於盧溝橋。 宣宗即位，以舊輔益親重。 明年，漢王高煦反，亦以「靖難」為辭，移檄罪狀諸大臣，以原吉為首。 帝夜召諸臣議。 楊榮首勸帝親征。 帝難之。 原吉曰：「獨不見李景隆已事耶？ 臣昨見所遣將，命下即色變，臨事可知矣。 且兵貴神速，卷甲趨之，所謂先人有奪人之心也。 榮策善。 」帝意遂決。 師還，賚予加等，賜閽者三人。 原吉以無功辭。 不聽。
Before long Emperor Renzong died, and the heir arrived from Nanjing. Yuanji, acting on the late emperor's testament, welcomed him at the Lugou Bridge. When Emperor Xuanzong ascended the throne, he treated Yuanji with still greater intimacy as a veteran counselor. The next year the Prince of Han, Zhu Gaoxu, rebelled, likewise invoking the Jingnan precedent and issuing a manifesto condemning the senior ministers, with Yuanji named first. That night the emperor summoned his ministers to deliberate. Yang Rong was the first to urge the emperor to take the field in person. The emperor hesitated. Yuanji said, "Have we not already seen what became of Li Jinglong? Yesterday I saw the generals you sent out—they turned pale the moment orders were issued. One can already tell how they will perform in battle. Besides, war prizes speed. March at once in full armor—that is how one seizes the initiative before the enemy does. Rong's plan is sound. The emperor's mind was made up. When the army returned, his rewards were raised one grade and three gatekeepers were granted him. Yuanji declined on the ground that he had earned no merit. The emperor would not accept his refusal.
17
三年，從北巡。 帝取原吉橐糗嘗之，笑曰：「何惡也？ 」對曰：「軍中猶有餒者。 」帝命賜以大官之饌，且犒將士。 從閱武兔兒山，帝怒諸將慢，褫其衣。 原吉曰：「將帥，國爪牙，奈何凍而斃之？ 」反覆力諫。 帝曰：「為卿釋之。 」再與蹇義同賜銀印，文曰：「含弘貞靖。 」帝雅善繪事，嘗親畫《壽星圖》以賜。 其他圖畫、服食、器用、銀幣、玩好之賜，無虛日。 五年正月，兩朝實錄成，復賜金幣、鞍馬。 旦入謝，歸而卒，年六十五。 贈太師，謚忠靖。 敕戶部復其家，世世無所與。
In the third year he accompanied the northern inspection tour. The emperor took dry rations from Yuanji's bag and tasted them, laughing, "How wretched is this! Yuanji answered, "There are still hungry men in the army." The emperor ordered imperial kitchen fare bestowed on him and rewards given to the troops as well. On accompanying a military review at Tuer Mountain, the emperor grew angry that the generals were disrespectful and ordered their garments stripped off. Yuanji said, "Generals are the state's claws and fangs—how can we let them freeze to death?" He remonstrated again and again with all his strength. The emperor said, "For your sake I shall release them." He and Jian Yi were again granted silver seals together, inscribed "Broad, forbearing, upright, and tranquil." The emperor was skilled at painting and once personally painted the 《Star of Longevity》 as a gift. Other gifts—paintings, food, utensils, silver, and curios—were bestowed almost every day. In the first month of the fifth year, when the Veritable Records of the two reigns were completed, he received gold coins and saddle horses again. He went in at dawn to give thanks; on returning home he died, aged sixty-five. He was posthumously honored as Grand Preceptor with the posthumous title Loyal and Serene. An edict ordered the Ministry of Revenue to restore his household to prosperity, exempting his descendants forever from tax and corvée.
18
原吉有雅景，人莫能測其際。 同列有善，即采納之。 或有小過，必為之掩覆。 吏汙所服金織賜衣。 原吉曰：「勿怖，汙可浣也。 」又有汙精微文書者，吏叩頭請死。 原吉不問，自入朝引咎，帝命易之。 呂震嘗傾原吉。 震為子乞官，原吉以震在「靖難」時有守城功，為之請。 平江伯陳瑄初亦惡原吉，原吉顧時時稱瑄才。 或問原吉：「量可學乎？ 」曰：「吾幼時，有犯未嘗不怒。 始忍於色，中忍於心，久則無可忍矣。 」嘗夜閱爰書，撫案而嘆，筆欲下輒止。 妻問之。 曰：「此歲終大辟奏也。 」與同列飲他所，夜歸值雪，過禁門，有欲不下者，原吉曰：「君子不以冥冥墮行。 」其慎如此。
Yuanji bore himself with quiet elegance; no one could plumb his inner depths. Whenever a colleague had a good idea, he adopted it at once. If someone slipped in a small matter, he always covered it for them. A clerk stained the gold-woven robe the emperor had granted him. Yuanji said, "Do not be afraid—stains can be washed out." When another clerk stained a delicate official document, the man kowtowed begging for death. Yuanji made no inquiry but went to court himself to accept blame; the emperor ordered the document replaced. Lu Zhen had once tried to undermine Yuanji. When Lü begged an office for his son, Yuanji pleaded on his behalf because Lü had defended the city during the Jingnan campaigns. The Earl of Pingjiang, Chen Xuan, had at first disliked Yuanji, yet Yuanji often praised Chen's ability. Someone asked Yuanji, "Can forbearance be learned?" He answered, "In my youth I was never offended without growing angry. At first I bore it in my face, then in my heart; in time there was nothing I could not endure." Once at night, reviewing criminal dossiers, he stroked the desk and sighed; his brush was poised to descend, then he stayed his hand. His wife asked why. He said, "This is the year-end report on capital sentences." Once, drinking with colleagues elsewhere, he returned at night through snow. At the forbidden gate some wished not to dismount; Yuanji said, "A gentleman does not let his conduct fail in the dark." Such was his scrupulous care.
19
原吉與義皆起家太祖時。 義秉銓政，原吉筦度支，皆二十七年，名位先於三楊。 仁、宣之世，外兼臺省，內參館閣，與三楊同心輔政。 義善謀，榮善斷，而原吉與士奇尤持大體，有古大臣風烈。
Yuanji and Jian both began their careers under Taizu. Jian held the power of appointments; Yuanji managed the treasury—each for twenty-seven years, their standing ahead of the Three Yang. Under Emperors Ren and Xuan they also held posts in the outer ministries and inner councils, and with the Three Yang they governed in concert. Jian excelled at planning, Rong at decision; Yuanji and Shiqi especially upheld the larger pattern, with the bearing of great ministers of old.
20
子瑄，以蔭為尚寶司丞。 喜談兵。 景泰時，數上章言兵事，有沮者，不獲用。 終南京太常少卿。
His son Xuan, by hereditary privilege, became vice commissioner of the Imperial Seals Office. He delighted in discussing military affairs. During the Jingtai reign he repeatedly memorialized on military affairs, but opponents blocked his proposals and they were not adopted. He ended his career as Vice Minister of Rites at Nanjing.
21
李文郁，襄陽人。 永樂初，以戶部侍郎副原吉治水有勞。 後坐事謫遼東二十年。 仁宗即位，召還，為南京通政參議，致仕。
Li Wenyu was a native of Xiangyang. At the start of Yongle, as Vice Minister of Revenue he assisted Yuanji in water control and earned merit. Later he was banished to Liaodong for twenty years on account of an offense. When Emperor Renzong ascended the throne he was recalled, appointed Nanjing Vice Commissioner of the Transmission Office, and later retired.
22
鄒師顏，宣都人。 永樂初，為江西參政，坐事免。 尋以薦擢御史，有直聲。 遷大理丞，署戶部。 與原吉同下獄。 仁宗立，釋為禮部侍郎。 省墓歸，還至通州，卒，貧不能歸葬。 尚書呂震聞於朝，宣宗命驛舟送之。 詔京官卒者，皆給驛，著為令。
Zou Shiyan was a native of Yidu. At the start of Yongle he served as Jiangxi Administrative Commissioner and was dismissed for an offense. Soon, on recommendation, he was promoted to censor and gained a reputation for blunt integrity. He was transferred to Assistant Minister of Justice and temporarily acted for the Ministry of Revenue. He was imprisoned together with Yuanji. When Emperor Renzong ascended the throne he was released and appointed Vice Minister of Rites. On returning from tending his parents' graves he reached Tongzhou and died, too poor to transport his coffin home. Minister Lü Zhen reported this to court; Emperor Xuanzong ordered a courier boat to convey his coffin home. An edict decreed that when capital officials died, courier transport should be provided for their funerals, and this was made a standing rule.
23
贊曰：《書》曰「敷求哲人，俾輔於爾後嗣」。 蹇義、夏原吉自筮仕之初，即以誠篤幹濟受知太祖，至成祖，益任以繁劇。 而二人實能通達政體，諳練章程，稱股肱之任。 仁、宣繼體，委寄優隆，同德協心，匡翼令主。 用使吏治修明，民風和樂，成績懋著，蔚為宗臣。 樹人之效，遠矣哉。
The encomium says: The 《Book of Documents》 says, "Seek out the wise broadly and set them to assist your successor." From the very start of their careers Jian Yi and Xia Yuanji won Taizu's trust through sincerity and practical ability; under Chengzu their burdens grew still heavier. Both men truly understood how government worked and were thoroughly versed in regulations—they were rightly called the state's right hand. Under Emperors Ren and Xuan their trust was generous; with one heart they aided and corrected enlightened rulers. Through them official governance was clarified, the people's customs harmonized, and their achievements stood splendid—they ranked among the dynasty's greatest ministers. The fruit of nurturing worthy men—how far its reach!
