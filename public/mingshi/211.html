馬永梁震 〈 （祝雄）〉 王效 〈 （劉文）〉 周尚文 〈 （趙國忠）〉 馬芳 〈 （子林孫炯爌飈）〉 何卿沈希儀石邦憲
Ma Yong and Liang Zhen ( Zhu Xiong) Wang Xiao ( Liu Wen) Zhou Shangwen ( Zhao Guozhong) Ma Fang ( sons Lin; grandsons Jiong, Kuang, and Biao) He Qing, Shen Xiyi, and Shi Bangxian
2
馬永，字天錫，遷安人。 生而魁岸，驍果有謀。 習兵法，好《左氏春秋》。 嗣世職為金吾左衛指揮使。 正德時，從陸完擊賊有功，進都指揮同知。 江彬練兵西內，永當隸彬，稱疾避之。 守備遵化，寇入馬蘭峪，參將陳乾被劾，擢永代。 戰柏崖、白羊峪，皆有功。
Ma Yong, styled Tiansi, was a native of Qian'an. From birth he was imposing in stature—fierce, resolute, and full of stratagem. He studied military manuals and loved the Zuo Commentary. He inherited his hereditary post as commander of the Left Branch of the Golden Guard. During the Zhengde reign he followed Lu Wan in fighting rebels with distinction and was promoted to assistant commander-in-chief. When Jiang Bin drilled troops in the Western Palace, Yong was to be placed under his command; he pleaded illness to avoid it. While serving as garrison commander at Zunhua, bandits entered Mala Pass; Assistant Commander Chen Qian was impeached, and Yong was promoted to replace him. In fighting at Baiya and Baiyang Ravine he won merit on each occasion.
3
十三年進都督僉事，充總兵官，鎮守薊州。 盡汰諸營老弱，聽其農賈，取備直給健卒，由是永所將獨雄於諸鎮。 武宗至喜峰口，欲出塞，永叩馬諫。 帝註視久之，笑而止。 中路擦崖當敵沖，無城堡，耕牧者輒被掠。 永令人持一月糧，營崖表，版築其內。 城廨如期立，乃遷軍守之。 錄功，進署都督同知。
In the thirteenth year he was promoted to junior grand mentor, appointed supreme commander, and posted to garrison Jizhou. He cleared out the old and weak from every camp, let them farm or trade, redirected reserve pay to his able-bodied troops, and so the force under Yong alone outmatched every other garrison. When Emperor Wuzong reached Xifeng Pass and wished to ride beyond the border, Yong seized his horse's reins and remonstrated. The emperor fixed his gaze on him for a long while, then smiled and gave up the plan. The central route at Caya lay in the enemy's path; there was no fortress, and farmers and herders were constantly raided. Yong had men carry a month's rations, camp on the cliff face, and build an earthen rampart within. The walled compound was finished on schedule, and he then moved troops in to hold it. His merit was recorded, and he was promoted to acting commander-in-chief.
4
嘉靖元年，金山礦盜作亂。 遣指揮康雄討平之，塞其礦。 朵顏把兒孫結諸部邀賞不得，盜邊。 永迎擊洪山口，而伏兵斷其後，斬獲過當，進右都督。 已，復馘其驍將，把兒孫不敢復擾邊。 大同兵變，殺巡撫張文錦，命桂勇為總兵官往鎮，而議將撫之。 永言：「逆賊幹紀，朝廷赦其脅從，恩至渥也，顧猶抗命。 今不剿，春和北寇南牧，叛卒勾連，禍滋大。 宜亟調鄰鎮兵，克期攻城，曉譬利害，懸破格之賞，令賊自相斬為功，元兇不難殄也。 」乃命永督諸軍與侍郎胡瓚往。 會亂平，乃還鎮。
In the first year of Jiajing, bandits rose up at the Jinshan mines. He sent Commander Kang Xiong to suppress them, restored order, and sealed the mines shut. Ba'er's grandson of the Tumed united various tribes when their demand for rewards was refused, then raided the border. Yong met them at Hongshankou while ambush troops cut off their retreat; kills and captures far exceeded the norm, and he was promoted to right commander-in-chief. Later he again beheaded their fiercest commander, and Ba'er's grandson dared not trouble the border again. When the Datong army mutinied and killed Grand Coordinator Zhang Wenjin, Gui Yong was appointed supreme commander to take charge of the region, while the court debated whether to pacify the rebels. Yong said, "These rebels have violated discipline. The court has already pardoned those who were coerced—grace could hardly be greater—yet they still defy orders. If we do not exterminate them now, in spring the northern barbarians will drive their herds south, the mutineers will collude with them, and the disaster will only grow. Neighboring garrisons should be mobilized at once, a date fixed for storming the city, the stakes explained plainly, and extraordinary rewards posted so the rebels would cut one another down for merit—the ringleaders would not be hard to destroy. " He was then ordered to take command of all troops and go with Vice Minister Hu Zan. By the time he arrived the revolt had already been pacified, and he returned to his post.
5
永上書為陸完請恤典，且乞宥議禮獲罪諸臣。 帝大怒，奪永官，寄祿南京後府。 巡按御史丘養浩言：「永仁以恤軍，廉以律已，固邊防，卻強敵，軍民安堵，資彼長城。 聞永去，遮道乞留，且攜子女欲遂逃移。 夫陸完久死炎瘴，非有權勢可托。 永徒感國士知，欲救區區之報。 不負知己，寧負國家？ 祈曲賜優容，俾還鎮。 」順天巡撫劉澤及給事、御史交章救之，俱被譴。 永竟廢不用。 永杜門讀書，清約如寒士。 久之，用薦僉書南京前府。 大同軍再亂，廷臣交薦。 召至，已就撫，復還南京。
Yong memorialized to request posthumous honors for Lu Wan and also begged pardon for the officials punished in the Rites Controversy. The emperor was furious, stripped Yong of his office, and had his salary attached at the Nanjing Rear Court. Touring Censor Qiu Yanghao said, "Yong is benevolent in comforting the troops, incorrupt in restraining himself, steadfast in frontier defense, and resolute against strong enemies; soldiers and civilians live in peace, relying on him as their long wall. When word spread that Yong was leaving, people blocked the road begging him to stay, and some even brought their wives and children, ready to flee with him. Lu Wan had long since died in the malarial south and had no power left to rely on. Yong was moved only by a true minister's regard for him and wished to repay a small debt of gratitude. Would he betray his intimate friend rather than betray the state? He begged a special indulgence and permission for Yong to return to his garrison. " Shuntian Grand Coordinator Liu Ze and various remonstrance officials and censors presented memorials in his defense, and all were rebuked. Yong was ultimately dismissed and never employed again. Yong shut his doors and devoted himself to reading, living as plainly as a poor scholar. After a long interval, on recommendation he served as registrar of the Nanjing Front Court. When the Datong army rebelled again, court officials recommended him one after another. When he was summoned, the rebels had already submitted, and he returned once more to Nanjing.
6
十四年，遼東兵變。 罷總兵官劉淮，以永代之。 大清堡守將徐顥誘殺泰寧衛九人。 部長把當孩怒，寇邊，永擊斬之。 其族屬把孫借朵顏兵報讎，復為永所卻。 已，復入犯。 中官王永戰敗，永坐戴罪。
In the fourteenth year, the Liaodong army mutinied. Supreme Commander Liu Huai was dismissed, and Yong was appointed in his place. Xu Hao, the garrison commander at Daqing Fort, induced the killing of nine men of the Taiping Guard. The tribal chief Badanghai was enraged and raided the border; Yong attacked and beheaded him. His clansman Basun borrowed Tumed troops to avenge him and was again driven back by Yong. Later they invaded again. The eunuch Wang Yong was defeated in battle, and Yong was punished by having to atone for the offense.
7
遼東自軍變後，首惡雖誅，漏綱者眾。 悍卒無所憚，結黨叫呼，動懷不逞。 廣寧卒佟伏、張鑒等乘旱饑，倡眾為亂，諸營軍憚永無應者。 伏等登譙樓，鳴鼓大噪，永率家眾仰攻。 千戶張斌被殺，永戰益力，盡殲之。 事聞，進左都督。
Since the military disturbance in Liaodong, though the chief culprits were executed, many escaped the net. Hard-bitten soldiers had nothing to fear; they formed factions, shouted defiance, and often harbored reckless designs. Guangning soldiers Tong Fu, Zhang Jian, and others, taking advantage of drought and famine, stirred the masses to revolt, but troops in the other camps feared Yong and none joined them. Fu and his followers climbed a watchtower, beat drums, and raised a great clamor; Yong led his household retainers in an assault from below. Thousand-commander Zhang Bin was killed, but Yong fought all the harder and annihilated them completely. When the affair was reported, he was promoted to left commander-in-chief.
8
永畜士百余人，皆西北健兒，驍勇敢戰。 遼東變初定，帝問將於李時。 時薦永，且曰：「其家眾足用也。 」帝曰：「將須文武兼，寧專恃勇乎？ 」時曰：「遼土新定，須有威力者鎮之。 」至是，竟得其力。 都御史王廷相言：「永善用兵，且廉潔，宜仍用之薊鎮，作京師藩屏。 」未及調，卒。 遼人為罷市。 喪過蘇州，州人亦灑泣。 兩鎮並立祠。
Yong kept more than a hundred trained men, all hardy fighters from the northwest—fierce and bold in battle. When the Liaodong disturbance was first pacified, the emperor asked Li Shi whom to appoint as general. Shi recommended Yong and said, "His household retainers are more than sufficient for the task. " The emperor said, "A general must combine civil and martial ability—can one rely on courage alone? " Shi said, "Liaodong has only just been pacified; a man of real authority and force is needed to hold it. " In the end, he indeed got his force. Censor-in-Chief Wang Tingxiang said, "Yong is skilled in the use of troops and moreover incorrupt; he should still be employed at the Jizhou garrison as a bulwark for the capital. " Before the transfer could be made, he died. The people of Liaodong closed their markets in mourning. When his funeral cortège passed through Suzhou, the townspeople wept as well. In both garrisons temples were erected in his honor.
9
永為將，厚撫間諜，得敵人情偽，故戰輒勝。 雅知人，所拔卒校，後多至大帥。 尚書鄭曉稱永與梁震有古良將風。
As a general Yong generously paid his spies and learned the enemy's true intentions and deceptions, so in battle he usually prevailed. He had a keen eye for talent; many of the soldiers and officers he promoted later rose to supreme command. Minister Zheng Xiao said that Yong and Liang Zhen had the bearing of the great generals of old.
10
梁震，新野人。 襲榆林衛指揮使。 嘉靖七年進署都指揮僉事，協守寧夏興武營。 尋充延綏遊擊將軍。 廉勇，好讀兵書，善訓士，力挽強命中，數先登。 擢延綏副總兵。 與總兵官王效卻敵鎮遠關，進都督僉事。
Liang Zhen was a native of Xinye. He inherited the hereditary post of commander of the Yulin Guard. In the seventh year of Jiajing he was promoted to acting assistant commander-in-chief and jointly garrisoned the Xingwu Camp in Ningxia. Soon he served as roaming commander of Yan-sui. Incorrupt and brave, he loved reading military books, was skilled at training troops, could draw a heavy bow with unerring aim, and often led the assault. He was promoted to deputy supreme commander of Yan-sui. Together with Supreme Commander Wang Xiao he repelled the enemy at Zhenyuan Pass and was promoted to junior grand mentor.
11
吉囊、俺答犯延綏，震敗之黃甫川。 尋犯響水、波羅，參將任傑大敗之。 吉囊復以十萬騎入寇，震大破之幹溝，獲首功百余。 先後被獎賚。 已，增俸一等。 幹溝凡三十里，當敵沖。 震浚使深廣，築土墻其上，寇不復輕犯。
Jirong and Altan invaded Yan-sui; Zhen defeated them at Huangfu River. Soon they invaded Xiangshui and Boluo, where Assistant Commander Ren Jie inflicted a crushing defeat on them. Jirong again invaded with a hundred thousand horsemen; Zhen routed them at Ganhogou and took more than a hundred heads for merit. He was rewarded again and again. Later his salary was raised one grade. Ganhogou stretched thirty li and lay directly in the enemy's path. Zhen had the moat dug deeper and wider and built an earthen wall above it, so the enemy no longer ventured to violate it lightly.
12
十四年進都督同知，充陜西總兵官。 尋論黃甫川功，進右都督。 明年移鎮大同。 大同亂兵連殺巡撫張文錦、總兵官李瑾。 繼瑾者魯綱，威不振，兵益驕，文武大吏不敢要束。 廷議以為憂，移震往。 震素畜健兒五百人，至則下令軍中，申約束。 鎮兵素憚震，由是帖服。 寇入犯，震破之牛心山，斬級百余。 寇慎，駐近邊伺隙。 時車駕祀山陵，震伏將士於諸路。 寇果入，大破之宣寧灣，又破之紅崖兒，斬獲甚眾。 進左都督，蔭一子百戶。 震父棟，前陣亡。 震辭蔭子，乞父祭葬，帝喜而許之。 毛伯溫督師，與震修鎮邊諸堡，不數月工成。 卒，贈太子太保，賜其家銀幣，加贈太保，謚武壯。
In the fourteenth year he was promoted to commander-in-chief and appointed supreme commander of Shaanxi. Soon, for his merit at Huangfu River, he was promoted to right commander-in-chief. The following year he was transferred to garrison Datong. Datong's mutinous soldiers in succession killed Grand Coordinator Zhang Wenjin and Supreme Commander Li Jin. Lu Gang followed Jin as supreme commander, but his authority never took hold; the troops grew more arrogant, and civil and military superiors dared not restrain them. The court viewed the situation with alarm and transferred Zhen to Datong. Zhen had long maintained a corps of five hundred crack troops; upon arrival he issued orders throughout the army and enforced discipline. The garrison troops had long feared Zhen, and thus submitted to his authority. When bandits invaded, Zhen defeated them at Niuxin Mountain and took more than a hundred heads. The bandits grew wary and camped near the border, waiting for an opportunity. At the time the emperor was performing rites at the imperial tombs; Zhen laid ambushes with troops along the roads. The bandits entered as expected; he routed them at Xuanning Bay and again at Hongya'er, killing and capturing a great many. He was promoted to left commander-in-chief, and one son was granted hereditary ennoblement as a hundred-household commandant. Zhen's father Dong had previously fallen in battle. Zhen declined the ennoblement for his son and petitioned instead to perform the sacrificial rites and burial for his father; the emperor was pleased and granted his request. Mao Bowen took command of the forces, and together with Zhen repaired the border garrison forts; the work was completed within a few months. He died and was posthumously granted the title of grand tutor of the crown prince; his family received silver and coins, he was further granted grand preceptor, and given the posthumous name Wuzhuang.
13
震有機略，號令明審。 前後百十戰，未嘗少挫。 時率健兒出塞劫敵營，或議其啟釁。 震曰：「凡啟釁者，謂寇不擾邊，我橫挑邀功也。 今數深入，乃不思一挫之耶？ 」震歿，健兒無所歸。 守臣以聞，編之伍，邊將猶頗得其力。
Zhen was resourceful in strategy, and his commands were clear and firm. Over the course of more than a hundred battles, he never once suffered a serious setback. He sometimes led his crack troops beyond the border to raid enemy camps, and some criticized this as provoking hostilities. Zhen said, "Provoking hostilities means the bandits are not harassing the border, yet we recklessly pick a fight to claim merit. They raid deep into our territory again and again—should we not at least try to deal them one blow?" When Zhen died, his crack troops were left with nowhere to go. The local commander reported this to the court; the men were enrolled in regular units, and border generals still found them quite useful.
14
代震者遼東祝雄，起家世蔭。 歷都督僉事。 自山西副總兵遷鎮大同。 被劾解職，起鎮薊州。 善撫士，治軍肅。 寇入塞，率子弟為士卒先。 子少卻，行法不貸。 世宗書其名禦屏。 為將三十年，布袍氈笠，不異卒伍。 既歿，遺貲僅供殮具。 薊人祠祀之。
Zhen's successor was Zhu Xiong of Liaodong, who had risen through hereditary ennoblement. He rose to the rank of junior grand mentor. He was transferred from deputy supreme commander of Shanxi to garrison Datong. After being impeached and dismissed, he was later recalled to garrison Jizhou. He was skilled at winning over his men and ran a disciplined army. When bandits raided across the border, he led his sons and kin at the head of the troops. When one of his sons fell slightly back, he enforced military law without exception. The Jiajing Emperor had his name inscribed on the imperial screen. For thirty years as a general he wore cotton robes and a felt hat, no different from the common rank and file. When he died, his estate barely covered the cost of his coffin and burial. The people of Jizhou erected a shrine in his honor.
15
王效，延綏人。 讀書能文辭，嫻韜略。 騎射絕人，中武會試。 嘉靖中，累官都指揮僉事，充延綏右參將。 出神木塞，搗寇雙乃山，斬獲多。 尋擢延綏副總兵。 十一年冬，進署都督僉事，充總兵官，代周尚文鎮寧夏。 吉囊犯鎮遠關，效與梁震敗之柳門。 追北蜂窩山，蹙溺之河，斬首百四十有奇。 璽書獎賚。
Wang Xiao was a native of Yan-sui. He was well read and skilled with language, and versed in military strategy. His horsemanship and archery were unmatched, and he passed the military metropolitan examination. During the Jiajing reign he rose to acting assistant commander-in-chief and served as right assistant commander of Yan-sui. Sortieing from Shenmu Pass, he struck the bandits at Shuannai Mountain and took many heads and prisoners. Soon he was promoted to deputy supreme commander of Yan-sui. In the winter of the eleventh year he was promoted to acting junior grand mentor, appointed supreme commander, and replaced Zhou Shangwen as garrison commander of Ningxia. Jirong attacked Zhenyuan Pass; Xiao and Liang Zhen defeated him at Liumen. Pursuing them north to Fengwo Mountain, he drove them into the river and drowned them, taking more than one hundred forty heads. He received an imperial commendation and reward.
16
吉囊十萬騎復窺花馬池，效、震拒之不得入，轉犯幹溝。 震分兵擊，遂趨固原。 總兵官劉文力戰，寇趨青山峴，大掠安定、會寧。 效方敗別部於鼠湖，追至沙湖，疾移師往援，破之安定，再破之靈州，先後斬首百五十余級。 總制三邊尚書唐龍以大捷聞，而巡按御史奏諸將失事罪。 給事中戚賢往勘，奏：「安、會二縣多殺掠，文當罪。 然麾下卒僅八千，倍道蒙險，攖八九萬方張之寇，殊死戰，宜以功贖。 震幹溝，效鼠湖、沙湖、安定、靈州之戰，以孤軍八百，當寇萬余，功俱足錄。 龍亦善調度。 」詔文奪職，震、效賚銀幣，龍一子入監。 是役也功多，執政尼之，故賞薄。 御史周鈇以為言，龍、效、震各加一級，效進都督同知。 尋以清水營功，進右都督。 寇以輕騎犯寧夏，效伏兵打鎧口，俟其半入橫擊，敗之，而防河卒復以戰艘邀斬其奔渡者。 捷聞，進左都督。 寇憤，設伏誘敗之，貶右都督。 十六年移鎮宣府。 逾年卒，謚武襄。
Jirong returned with a hundred thousand horsemen to probe Huama Pool; Xiao and Zhen kept them from entering, so they turned to raid Ganhogou. Zhen split his forces to engage them, but the bandits then pushed toward Guyuan. Supreme Commander Liu Wen fought hard, but the bandits pushed toward Qingshan Xian and plundered Anding and Huining on a large scale. Xiao had just routed a separate band at Shuhu and pursued them to Shahu; he then rushed troops to the rescue, defeated the enemy at Anding, routed them again at Lingzhou, and in all took more than one hundred fifty heads. Grand Coordinator of the Three Borders Tang Long reported a great victory, while the touring censor memorialized that the generals had failed in their duties. Supervising Secretary Qi Xian went to investigate and memorialized: "An and Huining counties suffered heavy killing and plundering, and Wen should be punished. Yet he had only eight thousand men under his command; marching by forced marches through perilous terrain, they grappled with eighty or ninety thousand bandits at the height of their power and fought to the death—their merits should redeem their faults. At Ganhogou, Zhen, and at Shuhu, Shahu, Anding, and Lingzhou, Xiao, each with a lone force of eight hundred against more than ten thousand bandits—their achievements all merit recognition. Long, too, had exercised good coordination. An edict stripped Wen of office, rewarded Zhen and Xiao with silver and coins, and enrolled one of Long's sons in the Imperial College. In this campaign achievements were many, but those in power blocked full recognition, so the rewards were meager. Censor Zhou Fu raised the matter; Long, Xiao, and Zhen each received a promotion of one rank, and Xiao was advanced to commander-in-chief. Soon, for his merit at Qingshui Camp, he was promoted to right commander-in-chief. Bandits raided Ningxia with light cavalry; Xiao ambushed them at Dagaikou, waited until half had crossed, then struck them from the flank and routed them, while river-defense troops in warships intercepted and cut down those fleeing across the water. When news of the victory arrived, he was promoted to left commander-in-chief. The bandits, enraged, set an ambush and lured him into defeat; he was demoted to right commander-in-chief. In the sixteenth year he was transferred to garrison Xuanfu. A year later he died and was given the posthumous name Wuxiang.
17
效言行謹飭，用兵兼謀勇，威名著西陲。 與馬永、梁震、周尚文並為名將。
Xiao was strict and disciplined in word and deed, combined strategy and courage in battle, and his fame was established on the western frontier. He ranked among the famed generals together with Ma Yong, Liang Zhen, and Zhou Shangwen.

Liu Wen was a native of Yanghe Guard. He inherited the hereditary post of assistant commandant. He rose through repeated promotions to acting junior grand mentor and right deputy supreme commander of Liangzhou. In the eighth year of Jiajing he was appointed supreme commander and posted to garrison Shaanxi. He inflicted a crushing defeat on the rebelling Tibetan tribes of Tao and Min, including the Ruolong and Ban'er clans, taking more than three hundred sixty heads. In the eleventh year bandits raided westward and, on their return, were about to strike east of the river in Ningxia; Wen defeated them. For his accumulated earlier achievements, he was promoted to commander-in-chief. Later he was dismissed, then recalled to garrison Yan-sui, and subsequently transferred to Gansu. Zhou Shangwen, styled Yanzhang, was a native of Xi'an Rear Guard. From childhood he studied and gained a rough grasp of the classics. He was full of stratagem and excelled at horsemanship and archery. At sixteen he inherited the post of assistant commandant. He repeatedly campaigned beyond the border with distinction and was promoted to commander. When Zhu Zhifang rebelled, he sealed the Yellow River crossing, captured the rebels Ding Guang and others, and was promoted to take charge of guard affairs. Hui rebels rose throughout the interior, sheltering in the southern mountains; Shangwen pacified them one after another. Censor Liu Tianhe impeached the eunuch Liao Tang and had him imprisoned by imperial order; the case implicated Shangwen as well. Under torture he was ordered to implicate Tianhe, but he never confessed; only after a long time was he released. Later he served as garrison commander at Jiezhou. By stratagem he captured rebelling Tibetans and was promoted to acting assistant commander-in-chief, serving as roaming commander of Gansu. In the first year of Jiajing he was transferred to serve as assistant commander of Ningxia. Soon he was promoted to assistant commander-in-chief and appointed deputy supreme commander of Liangzhou. While a censor was on inspection tour in Zhuanglang, bandits suddenly appeared. Shangwen quickly split his forces to shield the censor, while personally leading his men to drive the bandits off with arrows. Once, while pursuing bandits beyond the border, the enemy forces grew ever larger. Only half of Shangwen's force had come up, and his men were all afraid. He calmly dismounted, fought with his back against the cliff, and casualties were roughly even on both sides. Assistant General Ding Gao arrived with reinforcements, and the bandits finally withdrew. Shangwen was badly wounded and requested leave to return home. Soon he was restored to his former post. Jirong repeatedly crossed the frozen river to raid. Shangwen built a wall one hundred twenty li long and poured water over it; when it froze the surface was too slick to climb. When the ice broke up he had strongmen with long poles and iron hooks hook and kill those attempting to cross. In the ninth year he was promoted to acting junior grand mentor and appointed supreme commander of Ningxia. When Wang Qiong built the border wall, Shangwen supervised the work. He also dredged canals and opened military colonies, to the benefit of troops and civilians alike. When bandits raided Xihai and passed through Ningxia, Grand Coordinator Yang Zhixue proposed sending troops to intercept them. Shangwen refused; he was impeached and dismissed from office. After a long interval he was recalled as deputy supreme commander of Shanxi. When bandits came from Piantou Pass toward Kelan, Shangwen fought them over three hundred li and routed them; he and his son Junzuo were both wounded, and he was rewarded with silver and coins. Soon he was appointed supreme commander and posted to garrison Yan-sui. When bandits attacked Hongshan Beacon, he fought hard and routed them, and was rewarded. Jirong again raided Qingping Fort on a large scale, and Shangwen was penalized by salary forfeiture.

Shangwen was an outstanding commander, proud and overbearing, and everywhere he went he clashed with civil officials. Civil officials in turn often checked him, so the two sides grew ever more at odds. Grand Coordinator Jia Qi impeached Shangwen as aged and obstinate, and the Ministry of War asked that he be transferred to Gansu. The emperor did not agree, and both men had their salaries forfeited. Touring Censor Zhang Guangzu said the two could not possibly serve together; Shangwen was dismissed from office and Qi's rank was reduced as well. Jirong launched a major invasion and pushed as far as Guyuan. Tianhe was already governor-general by then and urged Shangwen to win distinction in battle. He fought fiercely at Heishui Park, killed Jirong's son known as the Little Ten Kings, and took more than one hundred thirty heads for merit. He was then promoted to assistant commander-in-chief.

In the twenty-first year, on recommendation he was appointed campaigning supreme commander of the Eastern Official Hall and concurrently placed in charge of Rear Court affairs. Yan Song was Minister of Rites, and his son Shifan served as director of the Rear Court in an arrogant and overbearing manner. Shangwen rebuked him to his face and was about to impeach him, but Song apologized and the matter was dropped. Shifan was transferred to managing director to keep him away from Shangwen, and he nursed a deep grudge. That autumn he was posted as supreme commander to garrison Datong and requested additional pay and horses. The Ministry of War said Shangwen's requests were excessive; he had just been sternly rebuked by imperial edict, yet he and Grand Coordinator Zhao Jin could not get along. He asked to retire and was refused, and the two men grew more hostile by the day. Censor Wang Sanpin asked that Shangwen be transferred to another garrison. The court deliberated: Datong lay on the enemy's main line of attack, and Shangwen was using the dispute as a pretext to escape his post; the court should not fall for his scheme. Zhao Jin was instead appointed grand coordinator of Gansu. Jirong invaded with tens of thousands of horsemen and attacked the vanguard guard. Shangwen fought them at Heishan, killed Jirong's son Manhandai, and pursued the enemy as far as Liangcheng. With many kills and captures to his credit, he was promoted to right commander-in-chief. Before long the bandits came from Xuanfu toward the capital region, then passed through the Datong passes and withdrew northward. Shangwen intercepted them and took some prisoners and spoils. Later the bandits returned in force, entered Buge Valley, and prepared to drive south. Shangwen made ready at Yanghe and sent cavalry out in every direction to intercept the bandits. The bandits withdrew, and the emperor issued an edict commending and rewarding him.

He went on to extend construction west from Yanghe to Yajiao Mountain in Shanxi, more than four hundred li in all, with more than a thousand watchtowers. He reclaimed more than forty thousand qing of garrison colony land and added more than thirteen thousand troops. The emperor praised his achievements, promoted him to left commander-in-chief, granted him the title of grand guardian of the crown prince, and permanently abolished the garrison colony tax. When the rebel Chongzhuo summoned the Little Prince to raid the frontier, Shangwen intercepted his messenger. He was further granted the rank of grand guardian, and his son was ennobled as hereditary thousand-household commander of the Embroidered Uniform Guard. Throughout the Ming dynasty, Shangwen alone among supreme commanders was granted the three grand tutor ranks.

At first Altan and Jirong's sons were at the height of their power, and the border regions suffered from their raids year after year, Datong most of all. From the time Shangwen took command, he worked with Governor-General Wanda and Grand Coordinator Zhan Rong to plan offense, defense, and frontier preparedness, and the people enjoyed several years of relief. Shangwen also recruited defectors to weaken the enemy, and deserters came over in steady succession. In the eighth month of the twenty-seventh year, Altan hid troops near Wubao, lured Commandant Gu Xiang and his men out, and surrounded them on Mituo Mountain. Shangwen urgently sent Deputy Supreme Commander Lin Chun, Assistant Commander Lü Yong, Roaming Commander Li Mei, and his sons Junzuo and Junren beyond the border to relieve them, and the siege was finally broken. Gu Xiang, Commandant Zhou Feng, Thousand-Household Commanders Lü Kai and Hao Jing, and others had already been killed in battle. Shangwen fought on as he withdrew, and when he reached Yekou an ambush force suddenly sprang up. He fought desperately and killed one of their leaders. The two sides faced each other for more than a month before the enemy withdrew. Shangwen laid an ambush, cut down the enemy rearguard, and withdrew. All three of Shangwen's sons had been sentenced to penal frontier service, and now, through their father's merit, they were released. Altan invaded Xuanfu with tens of thousands of horsemen; Wanda ordered Shangwen into action, and he routed the enemy at Caojiazhuang. His merit was recorded, he was granted the additional title of grand tutor of the crown prince, and he received extraordinary rewards. He died that year at the age of seventy-five.

Shangwen lived simply and cared for his men, and they served him with their lives. He made excellent use of spies and understood the enemy's inner workings, so he won merit in battle again and again. After the twentieth year Altan raided the frontier repeatedly. The veteran generals Wang Xiao, Ma Yong, and Liang Zhen had all died earlier; Shangwen alone remained, and his reputation was the greatest of them all. Yan Song and his son plotted to bring him down. His achievements were too great; the emperor was relying on him to hold off powerful enemies, and slander could not reach him. When he died, posthumous honors were denied; Supervising Secretary Shen Shu remonstrated on his behalf. Song provoked the emperor's anger, and Shen Shu was imprisoned by imperial order. Zhao Guozhong, styled Bozin, was a native of Jinzhou Guard and inherited the hereditary post of commander. In the eighth year of Jiajing he passed the military metropolitan examination, was promoted to assistant commander-in-chief, and posted to garrison Aiyang. He was promoted to right assistant commander of Jinyi. After repeated victories over the enemy he was promoted in rank and rewarded with gold and coins, then made acting junior grand mentor and appointed supreme commander of Liaodong. He distinguished himself in repelling the enemy, taking more than one hundred seventy heads. He was promoted to assistant commander-in-chief and received rewards above the usual measure. The enemy entered through Yahugu Pass with eight hundred horsemen. Assistant Commander-in-Chief Kang Yun was killed in battle, along with three lieutenant generals; Guozhong was ordered to atone for the failure by winning merit in battle. The bandits repeatedly raided Yulin Fort, Gaotai, and the Geli River. In a series of surprise attacks he took more than one hundred fifty heads for merit and was promoted one rank. While Altan was out hunting, a tiger charged roaring before him, and Fang brought it down with a single shot. Altan then gave him a fine bow, arrows, and a good horse, and kept him in attendance. Fang pretended to serve willingly, but secretly made his way home by a hidden route. When Zhou Shangwen was garrison commander at Datong, he was impressed by Fang and appointed him squad leader. In the autumn of the twenty-ninth year of Jiajing, bandits raided Huairou and Shunyi. Fang galloped out, killed the enemy general, and was appointed chief banner officer of Yanghe Guard. The bandits once entered Weiyuan, hid crack cavalry at the salt fields, and sent twenty horsemen forward to provoke a fight. Fang saw through the ruse. With a hundred horsemen he closed on the ambush site, divided his best troops into three groups, and attacked in succession. He charged fiercely again and again, and the enemy cavalry fell back for ten li; he took ninety heads in all. He soon fought them off again at Xinping. The bandits encamped at Yema River and set a day for battle. Fang judged that the bandits would soon withdraw, pressed the attack hard, and took still more heads. The men were still exchanging congratulations when Fang suddenly spurred his horse and cried, "The enemy is upon us! " He urged them to take the high ground and personally covered the retreat. Before long the raiders came swarming in as Fang had foreseen. Fang fought all the harder, and the raiders withdrew. Not long after he fought them again at Nihe River and routed them completely. He rose through repeated promotions to assistant commandant. For his achievements he was promoted to acting assistant commander-in-chief and appointed roaming commander of Xuanfu. Again for merit he was jump-promoted to assistant commander-in-chief and served under the governor-general as an assistant commander. A battle at Zhenshan Stockade went badly, and his salary was forfeited. Soon after, in a successful raid on the enemy he was promoted two ranks and made right commander-in-chief. Qing suppressed them, taking more than two hundred heads and accepting the surrender of several hundred followers. Zheng fled to Wusa, and Qing ordered the native official Anning to seize him and deliver him up. Anning pretended to comply but hid Zheng and refused to hand him over. Grand Coordinator Tang Mu reported what had happened, and the emperor stripped Qing of his rank and insignia. When Sichuan and Guizhou forces joined the campaign and the rebels were finally destroyed, his rank and insignia were restored. In the spring of the fifth year he was promoted to deputy commander-in-chief and continued to command Songpan. With the Long clan extinct, Mangbu was reorganized as Zhenxiong Prefecture and placed under regular appointed officials. Before long Zheng's remnant follower Sha Bao rebelled again. Qing advanced with Assistant Commander Wei Wu, Administrative Assistant Yao Rugao, and others, beheaded seven rebel leaders including Sha Bao, and wiped out the rest. When merit was recorded, Wu ranked first and Qing second; each received rewards according to his service. The tribes of the Black Tiger Five Stockades rose up and besieged the Chang'an forts, and the Wudu, Zhegu, and other tribes followed in rebellion. Qing defeated and pacified them all and was promoted on the spot to regional assistant commander. More than ten Weimao stockades joined forces to raid military supplies and attack Maozhou and the Changning forts, demanding pacification payments. Qing and Vice Commissioner Zhu Wan built an outer wall around Maozhou to pin the tribes down. He soon wore their forces down by stratagem, won battle after battle, stormed Shengou, and burned their blockhouses and stockades. Hard pressed, the tribes begged to atone for their crimes. Qing demanded the ringleaders, but the tribes refused. He then split his forces, stormed the Qiangou and Hunshui stockades, and annihilated them. The tribes then competed to surrender the ringleaders, swearing blood oaths by pricking their flesh, severing fingers and ears, and vowing never to rebel again. Qing then carved a wooden covenant with them, resettled their clans under fixed boundaries, and the road to Songpan was open once more. Grand Coordinator Pan Jian and others reported the achievements of the two men; an edict granted silver and silks, and Qing was promoted to acting regional vice commander while remaining in command. After some time he retired on grounds of illness.

二十三年，塞上多警。 召卿，以疾辭。 帝怒，奪其都督，命以都指揮使詣部聽調。 未幾，寇逼畿輔，命營盧溝橋。 松潘副總兵李爵為巡撫丘養浩劾罷，詔以卿代。 給事中許天倫言卿賄養浩劾爵，自為地。 帝怒，褫卿及養浩官，令巡按冉崇禮核實。 時兵事棘，翁萬達復薦卿，還其都督僉事，都東官廳軍馬。 已而崇禮具言爵貪汙，「卿鎮松潘十七年，為蜀保障，軍民頌德，且貧，安所得賄？ 」帝意乃解。 四川白草番為亂，副總兵高岡鳳被劾。 兵部尚書路迎奏卿代之。 卿再蒞松潘，將士鹹喜。 乃會巡撫張時徹討擒渠惡數人，俘斬九百七十有奇，克營砦四十七，毀碉房四千八百，獲馬牛器械儲積各萬計。 進署都督同知。 卿素有威望，為番人所憚。 自威茂迄松潘、龍安夾道築墻數百里，行旌往來，無剽奪患。 先後蒞鎮二十四年，軍民戴之若慈母。 再以疾歸。
In the twenty-third year the frontier was rife with alarms. Qing was summoned, but he declined on grounds of illness. The emperor was enraged, stripped him of his regional commander's rank, and ordered him to report to the ministry as a regional commander awaiting assignment. Before long, when raiders threatened the capital region, he was ordered to encamp at Lugou Bridge. When Songpan Deputy Commander Li Jue was impeached and dismissed by Grand Coordinator Qiu Yanghao, an edict appointed Qing in his place. Supervising Secretary Xu Tianlun charged that Qing had bribed Qiu Yanghao to impeach Li Jue and clear the way for himself. The emperor was furious, stripped both Qing and Yanghao of office, and ordered Investigating Censor Ran Chongli to investigate. With military affairs critical, Weng Wanda recommended Qing again; his rank as regional assistant commander was restored and he was put in command of the Eastern Office forces. Later Ran Chongli reported in full that Li Jue was corrupt: "Qing has held Songpan for seventeen years as Sichuan's shield; soldiers and civilians praise his virtue, and he is poor besides—where would he find the money for a bribe? " The emperor's anger subsided. When the Baicao tribes of Sichuan rebelled, Deputy Commander Gao Gangfeng was impeached. Minister of War Lu Ying recommended Qing to replace him. When Qing returned to Songpan, officers and soldiers alike rejoiced. He then joined Grand Coordinator Zhang Shiche in hunting down several ringleaders, killing and capturing more than nine hundred seventy of the enemy, taking forty-seven camps and stockades, destroying four thousand eight hundred blockhouses, and seizing horses, cattle, weapons, and stores by the tens of thousands. He was promoted to acting regional vice commander. Qing had long commanded respect, and the tribes feared him. From Weimao through Songpan and Long'an he had walls built for hundreds of li along the road, so that official processions could pass without fear of robbery. Over twenty-four years in command, soldiers and civilians revered him as they would a loving mother. He retired again on grounds of illness.

三十三年，倭寇海上。 詔卿與沈希儀各率家眾赴蘇、松軍門。 明年充副總兵，總理浙江及蘇、松海防。 卿，蜀中名將，不諳海道，年已老，兵與將不習，竟不能有所為。 為巡按御史周如鬥劾罷，卒。
In the thirty-third year Japanese pirates raided the coast. An edict ordered Qing and Shen Xiyi each to lead their household troops to the Suzhou-Songjiang military headquarters. The following year he was made deputy commander-in-chief with overall charge of coastal defense in Zhejiang, Suzhou, and Songjiang. Qing was a famed general of Shu, but he knew nothing of the sea; he was old, his men and he were strangers to one another, and in the end he achieved nothing. Investigating Censor Zhou Rudou impeached him and had him removed from office; he died soon after.

沈希儀，字唐佐，貴縣人。 嗣世職為奉議衛指揮使。 機警有膽勇，智計過絕於人。 正德十二年，調征永安。 以數百人搗陳村砦，馬陷淖中，騰而上，連馘三酋，破其余眾。 進署都指揮僉事。 義寧賊寇臨桂，還巢，希儀追之。 巢有兩隘，賊伏兵其一，使熟瑤紿官兵入。 希儀策其詐，急從別隘直抵賊巢。 賊倉卒還救，遂大破之。 荔浦賊八千渡江東掠，希儀率五百人駐白面砦，待其歸。 砦去蛟龍、滑石兩灘各數里。 希儀以滑石灘狹，雖眾可薄，蛟龍灘廣，濟則難圖，欲誘致之滑石。 乃樹旗百蛟龍灘，守以羸卒，然柴以疑之。 賊果趨滑石。 希儀預以小艦載勁卒伏葭葦中。 賊渡且半，乘瀧急沖之，兩岸軍噪而前，賊眾多墜水死，收所掠而還。 從副總兵張祐連破臨桂、灌陽、古田賊。 進署都指揮同知，掌都司事。
Shen Xiyi, styled Tangzuo, was a native of Guixian. He inherited his family's hereditary post as commander of the Fengyi Guard. Quick-witted and bold, he possessed a cunning that set him apart from other men. In the twelfth year of the Zhengde reign he was transferred to campaign in Yong'an. Leading several hundred men he stormed Chen Village Stockade; when his horse sank in the mud he sprang free, took three chieftains' heads in succession, and routed the rest. He was promoted to acting regional assistant commander. Bandits from Yining raided Lin Gui and withdrew to their lair; Xiyi pursued them. The lair had two passes. The bandits lay in wait at one and sent acculturated Yao to lure the government troops in. Sensing the ruse, Xiyi rushed by the other pass straight into the bandits' stronghold. The bandits rushed back to its defense and were utterly routed. Eight thousand Lipu bandits crossed the river to raid the east country. Xiyi took five hundred men to White Face Stockade and waited for their return. The stockade stood several li from each of the Jiaolong and Huashi fords. Huashi Ford was narrow—even a large force could be pressed there—while Jiaolong Ford was wide and hard to attack once they had crossed. Xiyi meant to lure them to Huashi. He planted a hundred banners at Jiaolong Ford, left a weak garrison, and lit bonfires to feign strength. The bandits did as he hoped and headed for Huashi. Xiyi had already hidden picked men aboard small boats in the reeds. When the bandits were half across, he drove into them on the swift current while troops on both banks shouted and closed in. Many drowned; he recovered the loot and withdrew. Serving under Deputy Commander Zhang You, he broke the bandits of Lin Gui, Guanyang, and Gutian in succession. He was promoted to acting assistant commander-in-chief and given charge of the regional military commission.

嘉靖五年，總督姚鏌將討田州岑猛。 用希儀計，間猛婦翁歸順土酋岑璋，使圖猛，而分兵五哨進。 希儀將中哨，當工堯。 工堯，賊要地，聚眾守之。 希儀夜遣軍三百人，緣山上，繞出其背。 比明合戰，則所遣軍已立幟山巔，賊大潰敗。 猛走歸順，為璋所執，田州平。 希儀功最，鏌抑之，止受賚。 鏌議設流官，希儀曰：「思恩以流官故，亂至今未已。 田州復然，兩賊且合從起。 」鏌不從。 以希儀為右參將，分守思、田。 希儀請還鄉治裝。 以參將張經代守。 甫一月，田州復叛，鏌罷歸。 王守仁代，多用希儀計，思、田復定。
In Jiajing 5, Grand Coordinator Yao Mo prepared to campaign against Cen Meng of Tianzhou. Following Xiyi's plan, he drove a wedge between Meng and his father-in-law Cen Zhang, chieftain of Guishun, set Zhang to plot against Meng, and sent five columns forward. Xiyi led the center column against Gongyao. Gongyao was a key stronghold, defended by a mass of rebels. By night Xiyi sent three hundred men up the mountain to get behind them. At dawn, as battle was joined, his detachment already stood on the summit with banners flying, and the rebels broke and fled. Meng fled to Guishun, was seized by Zhang, and Tianzhou was pacified. Xiyi had done the most, but Yao Mo downplayed him and he received only an imperial gift. Mo proposed installing regular officials. Xiyi said, "Si'en was given regular officials, and the unrest there has never ended. Do the same at Tianzhou, and the two rebel camps will join forces and rise together." Mo would not listen. Xiyi was made right assistant regional commander with divided duty over Si'en and Tianzhou. Xiyi asked leave to go home and pack. Assistant Regional Commander Zhang Jing was sent to hold the garrison in his place. Barely a month later Tianzhou rebelled again; Mo was dismissed and sent home. Wang Shouren replaced him, relied heavily on Xiyi's plans, and Si'en and Tianzhou were pacified once more.

改右江柳慶參將，駐柳州。 象州、武宣、融縣瑤反，討破之。 謝病歸，頃之還故任。 柳在萬山中，城外五里即賊巢，軍民至無地可田，而官軍素罷不任戰。 又賊耳目遍官府，閨闥動靜無不知。 希儀謂欲大破賊，非狼兵不可，請於制府。 調那地狼兵二千來，戍兵稍振。 乃求得與瑤通販易者數十人，持其罪而厚撫之，使诇賊。 賊動靜，希儀亦無不知。 希儀每出兵，雖肘腋親近不得聞。 至期鳴號，則諸軍鹹集。 令一人挾旗引諸軍行，不測所往。 及駐軍設伏，賊必至，遇伏輒奔。 官軍擊之，無不如誌。 已，賊寇他所，官軍又先至。 遠村僻聚，賊度官軍所不逮者，往寇之，官軍又未嘗不在，賊驚以為神。 希儀得賊巢婦女畜產，果鄰巢者悉還之，惟取陰助賊者。 諸瑤盡詟伏，無敢向賊。
He was reassigned as right-river Liujing assistant regional commander and posted to Liuzhou. The Yao of Xiangzhou, Wuxuan, and Rong County rebelled; he defeated them in campaign. He pleaded illness and retired; before long he was back in his old post. Liuzhou sat deep in the mountains; five li beyond the walls lay bandit lairs. Soldiers and civilians had nowhere to farm, and the regular troops were slack and unfit for war. The bandits also had spies in every yamen; not a whisper in the inner quarters escaped them. Xiyi said that only Lang troops could break the bandits for good and petitioned the governor-general. Two thousand Nadi Lang troops were called up, and the garrison regained some fighting spirit. He then found several dozen men who traded with the Yao, pardoned their crimes with generous rewards, and set them to spy on the bandits. In turn, Xiyi knew every move the bandits made. Whenever Xiyi marched out, not even his closest aides were told beforehand. At the appointed hour he sounded the muster, and every unit gathered. One man with a banner led the columns, and no one knew where they were bound. Wherever he laid an ambush, the bandits walked into it—and always broke and ran. Every government strike landed exactly as he had planned. When the bandits struck elsewhere, government troops were already there. Even remote hamlets the bandits thought beyond government reach would find troops waiting when they came to raid. They took it for sorcery. When he seized women, livestock, and goods from a bandit lair, he returned everything that belonged to neighboring lairs and kept only those who had secretly helped the bandits. The Yao were thoroughly cowed and dared not side with the bandits.

希儀初至，令熟瑤得出入城中，無所禁。 因厚賞其黠者，使為諜。 後漸令瑤婦入見其妻，賚以酒食繒帛。 其夫常以賊情告者，則陰厚之。 諸瑤婦利賞，爭勸其夫輸賊情，或自入府言之。 以故，賊益無所匿形。 希儀每於風雨晦冥夜，偵賊所止宿，分遣人賫銃潛伏舍旁。 中夜銃舉，賊大駭曰：「老沈來矣！ 」鹹挈妻子匍匐上山。 兒啼女號，或寒凍觸厓石死，爭怨悔作賊非計。 至曉下山，則寂無人聲。 他巢亦然，眾愈益驚。 潛遣人入城偵之，則希儀故居城中不出也。 賊膽落，多易面為熟瑤。
When Xiyi first arrived, he let acculturated Yao come and go in the city without restriction. He richly rewarded the shrewdest among them and made them his spies. Gradually he had Yao women visit his wife, lavishing wine, food, silk, and cloth on them. He secretly rewarded the husbands of those who brought bandit intelligence. Greedy for the rewards, Yao women urged their husbands to inform on the bandits—or came to the yamen themselves. Thus the bandits had nowhere left to hide. On stormy, pitch-dark nights Xiyi would track where bandits slept and send men with muskets to lie in wait beside their houses. At midnight the muskets fired. The bandits cried in terror, "Old Shen is here! " Everyone grabbed wife and child and crawled up the mountain. Babies wailed and women screamed; some froze against the cliffs and died. All cursed the life of banditry. At dawn they came down the mountain to find the valley empty and silent. The same thing happened at every lair, and terror spread. Spies sent secretly into the city found Xiyi had never left his residence. The bandits' nerve broke; many disguised themselves as acculturated Yao.

Wei Fujian, chieftain of the Maping Yao, had eluded capture again and again. Word came that Fujian had fled to the neighboring Three-layer Lair. Xiyi secretly led troops against it, only to find Fujian had gone raiding elsewhere with the Three-layer bandits. Xiyi took all the wives and children of Three-layer Lair captive. He had always handed such captives to the Lang troops; this time he shut them in empty houses and fed them. He sent acculturated Yao to tell the husbands, "Produce Wei Fujian and your families will be returned. " At the news, every Yao headman came before Xiyi. They were shown their wives and children unharmed. Together they lured Fujian from his lair, bound him, and delivered him up in exchange for their families. Xiyi gouged out Fujian's eyes, dismembered him, and hung the pieces at the city gates. The Yao submitted to Xiyi's authority and scarcely dared rob again. From then on, for hundreds of li around Liucheng, no one dared plunder.

希儀嘗上書於朝，言狼兵亦瑤、僮耳。 瑤、僮所在為賊，而狼兵死不敢為非，非狼兵順，而瑤、僮逆也。 狼兵隸土官，瑤、僮隸流官。 土官令嚴足以制狼兵，流官勢輕不能制瑤、僮。 若割瑤、僮分隸之旁近土官，土官世世富貴，不敢有他望。 以國家之力制土官，以土官之力制瑤、僮，皆為狼兵，兩廣世世無患矣。 時不能用。 至十六年而有思恩岑金之變。
Xiyi once memorialized the throne: the Lang troops were Yao and Zhuang too. Where Yao and Zhuang served regular officials they turned bandit, yet Lang troops under native chiefs would not dare misbehave on pain of death. The difference was not nature but governance. Lang troops answered to native officials; Yao and Zhuang answered to regular officials. Native chiefs ruled with enough severity to command Lang troops; regular magistrates lacked the weight to control Yao and Zhuang. Partition the Yao and Zhuang among nearby native chiefs, and those chiefs would grow rich and loyal for generations, with no ambition beyond their domains. Let the state hold the native chiefs, let the native chiefs hold the Yao and Zhuang—all would fight as Lang troops do, and the Two Guangs would know peace for generations. The court did not adopt the proposal. In the sixteenth year came the Si'en rebellion led by Cen Jin.

初，思恩土官岑浚既誅，改設流官，以其酋二人韋貴、徐五為土巡檢，分掌其兵各萬余。 夷民不樂漢法，凡數叛。 鎮安有男子名金，自言浚子。 鎮安土官乃潛召其舊部酋長，出金而與之盟曰：「若小主也。 」諸酋羅拜，擁金歸，聚兵五千，將攻城，復故地，遠近洶洶。 浚誅時，其酋楊留者無所歸，率黨千余人詣賓州，應募為打手。 希儀在賓，留入言，欲往見小主人。 希儀故患金，及聞留言，益大駭。 因好謂留曰：「是岑浚第九子耶？ 我向征田州固聞之。 」因自語：「岑氏其復乎？ 」欲以深動留，留果喜。 已，召留密室，言：「予我重賂，即為金復官。 」且出，復呼入曰：「韋貴、徐五今分將思恩兵，必讎金，善防之。 」留益大信。 金遂從五千人因留以見。 門者奔告，請無納。 希儀罵曰：「金，土官子，非賊，奈何不納？ 」引入，厚結之，又引以詣兵備副使，隨以計漸散其五千人。 卒縛金，留亦自恨死，思恩復寧。 已，從總督張經大破斷藤峽、弩灘賊，受賚歸。
After the Si'en native official Cen Jun was executed, the post was given to regular magistrates and two of his chiefs, Wei Gui and Xu Wu, were made native sub-inspectors, each commanding more than ten thousand men. The tribesmen chafed under Han law and rebelled repeatedly. At Zhen'an a man named Jin claimed to be Jun's son. The Zhen'an native official secretly summoned Jun's old chieftains, presented Jin, and swore them to him: "This is your young lord. " The chiefs bowed in ranks, escorted Jin home, mustered five thousand men, and prepared to storm the city and recover the old domain. Alarm spread far and wide. When Jun was executed, his chief Yang Liu, left without a master, took more than a thousand followers to Bin Prefecture and enlisted as mercenary brawlers. Xiyi was stationed at Bin when Liu came to say he wished to visit the young lord. Xiyi had already been worried about Jin; Liu's words filled him with dread. He feigned delight and asked Liu, "Is this Cen Jun's ninth son? I heard as much when I campaigned in Tianzhou. " Then, as if to himself: "So the Cen are to rise again? " He meant to stir Liu deeply—and Liu was pleased. Later he called Liu into a private room. "Pay me well," he said, "and I will restore Jin to office. " As Liu left, he called him back: "Wei Gui and Xu Wu now divide Si'en's troops between them. They will hate Jin—watch them closely. " Liu trusted him completely. Jin then came with his five thousand men, led by Liu, to an audience. The gatekeeper ran in and begged that Jin not be admitted. Xiyi scolded him: "Jin is a native official's son, not a rebel—why turn him away? " Jin was admitted, treated with honor, and presented to the vice military intendant; by degrees Xiyi's stratagem scattered the five thousand. In the end Jin was bound. Liu died of remorse, and Si'en was quiet again. He then followed Grand Coordinator Zhang Jing in a great victory over the bandits of Duan Gorge and Nubay, received an imperial gift, and went home.
51
希儀鎮柳、慶久，渠魁宿猾捕誅殆盡。 先後搗巢，斬馘積五千余級，未嘗悉奏功，故多不敘。 十九年復謝病，柳人祀之山雲祠。 旋起四川左參將，分守敘、滬及貴州迤西諸處。 其冬，擢署都督僉事，充總兵官，鎮貴州。 復謝病歸。 塞上多警，召天下名將至京師，希儀在召中。 希儀鎮柳、慶，每戰必先登，身數被創，陰雨輒痛劇，故數謝病。 至京，亦以病辭。 帝疑其規避，褫都督官，令赴部候用。 翁萬達薦其才。 會江、淮多盜，議設督捕總兵官，乃復希儀署都督僉事以往。
Xiyi had long held Liu and Qing in garrison. The ringleaders, hardened by years of raiding, were hunted down and executed almost to the last man. In repeated raids on their strongholds he took more than five thousand heads, yet he never reported the full tally of his achievements, and much went unrecorded in the official dispatches. In the nineteenth year he again pleaded illness, and the people of Liuzhou enshrined him at the Shanyun Shrine. He was soon recalled as left assistant regional commander of Sichuan, with divided duty over Xu, Hu, and the western reaches of Guizhou. That winter he was promoted to acting regional assistant commander, appointed regional commander, and posted to garrison Guizhou. Once again he pleaded illness and went home. With alarms rising along the frontier, famous generals from across the realm were summoned to the capital, and Xiyi was among them. While Xiyi held Liu and Qing, he was always first over the wall in battle. He was wounded again and again, and rainy weather would sharpen the pain, which is why he so often pleaded illness. Once in the capital he declined service on grounds of illness. The emperor suspected him of evading duty, stripped him of his regional commander's rank, and ordered him to report to the ministry to await assignment. Weng Wanda recommended his ability. Banditry was rife in Jiang and Huai, and the court proposed creating a bandit-suppression regional commander; Xiyi was again given acting regional assistant commander rank and sent there.
52
二十六年以為廣東副總兵。 命自今將領至自川、廣、雲、貴者，毋推京營及西北邊，著為令。 從總督張嶽大破賀縣賊倪仲亮等，予實授，仍賚銀幣。 瓊州五指山熟黎素畏法，供徭賦，知州邵浚虐取之。 其酋那燕遂結崖州、感恩、昌化諸黎為亂。 總督歐陽必進議並萬州、陵水黎討之，分兵五道。 希儀適病，最後至，謂必進曰：「萬州、陵水黎未有黨惡之實，奈何並誅，益樹敵？ 莫若止三道。 」必進從之。 希儀乃偕參將武鸞、俞大猷等直入五指山下，斬那燕及其黨五千四百有奇，俘獲者五之一，招降三千七百人。 捷聞，進都督同知，改貴州總兵官。 復從嶽平銅仁叛苗龍許保、吳黑苗。 又以病歸。 倭寇海上，命督川、廣兵赴剿。 無功，為周如鬥劾罷。
In the twenty-sixth year he was made deputy supreme commander of Guangdong. An edict declared that henceforth commanders arriving from Sichuan, Guangdong, Yunnan, and Guizhou were not to be shunted off to the capital garrisons or the northwest frontier—a rule thereafter kept in force. With Governor-General Zhang Yue he won a great victory over the He County rebels led by Ni Zhongliang and others. He received his commission in full rank and was again granted silver and silks. The acculturated Li of Wuzhi Mountain in Qiongzhou had long lived in awe of the law and paid their corvée and taxes. Prefect Shao Jun extorted from them with cruelty. Their chief Na Yan then rallied the Li of Yazhou, Gan'en, and Changhua in revolt. Governor-General Ouyang Bijin proposed also punishing the Li of Wanzhou and Lingshui, and divided his forces into five columns. Xiyi happened to be ill and arrived last. He told Bijin, "The Li of Wanzhou and Lingshui have no proven record of siding with the rebels. Why punish them along with the guilty and only breed more enemies? Better to leave it at three columns." Bijin agreed. Xiyi then joined Assistant Regional Commanders Wu Luan and Yu Dayou and others in a direct descent on Wuzhi Mountain. They killed Na Yan and more than five thousand four hundred of his followers, took one captive for every five killed, and persuaded three thousand seven hundred to surrender. When word of the victory reached the capital, he was promoted to regional vice commander and made regional commander of Guizhou. Again following Zhang Yue he pacified the Tongren Miao rebels Long Xubao and Wu Heimiao. Once again he went home on grounds of illness. When Japanese pirates struck along the coast, he was ordered to lead Sichuan and Guangdong forces against them. He achieved nothing and was impeached and dismissed by Zhou Rudou.
53
希儀為人坦率，居恒謔笑，洞見肺腑。 及臨敵，應變出奇，人莫測。 尤善撫士卒。 常染危病，卒多自戕以禱於神。 最後一人，至以箭穿其喉。 其得士心如此。
Xiyi was a frank and open man. In ordinary times he joked and laughed, yet everything in his heart showed plainly on his face. When battle came he adapted to circumstance with unexpected stratagems, and no one could read his next move. Above all he knew how to win over his soldiers. When he fell gravely ill, many of his men would mutilate themselves in prayer to the gods for his sake. One man, the last of them, drove an arrow through his own throat. Such was the depth of devotion he commanded among his troops.
54
石邦憲，字希尹，貴州清平衛人。 嘉靖七年嗣世職為指揮使。 累功，進署都指揮僉事，充銅仁參將。 苗龍許保、吳黑苗叛，總督張嶽議征之，而賊陷印江、石阡，邦憲坐逮問。 嶽以銅仁賊巢穴，而邦憲有謀勇，乃奏留之。 邦憲遂與川、湖兵進貴州，破苗砦十有五。 竄山箐者，搜戮殆盡。 上功，邦憲第一。 未及敘，而許保等突入思州，執知府李允簡以去。 邦憲急邀，奪之歸。 坐是停俸戴罪。 賊既破思州，復糾余黨，與湖廣蠟爾山苗合，欲攻石阡。 不克，還過省溪。 千戶安大朝等邀之，斬獲大半，盡奪其輜重，賊不能軍。 邦憲乃使使購老穀、老犭革等執許保送軍門，而黑苗竄如故。 復以計購烏朗土官田興邦等斬黑苗，賊盡平。 遂進署都督僉事，充總兵官，代沈希儀鎮貴州。
Shi Bangxian, styled Xiyin, was a native of Qingping Guard in Guizhou. In the seventh year of the Jiajing reign he inherited his family's hereditary post as commander. Through repeated victories he rose to acting assistant commander-in-chief and was made assistant regional commander of Tongren. The Miao rebels Long Xubao and Wu Heimiao rose in revolt. Governor-General Zhang Yue proposed an expedition, but the rebels captured Yinjiang and Shiqian, and Bangxian was arrested and put under investigation. Yue knew Tongren was the rebels' stronghold and that Bangxian had both strategy and courage; he memorialized the throne to keep Bangxian in the field. Bangxian then advanced into Guizhou with Sichuan and Huguang troops and captured fifteen Miao stockades. Those who fled into the mountain ravines were hunted down and slain almost to the last. When merit was reported, Bangxian ranked first. Before rewards could be issued, Xubao and his men suddenly broke into Sizhou and carried off the prefect Li Yunjian. Bangxian intercepted them in haste and brought the prefect back. For this his salary was suspended and he was ordered to serve while bearing the demerit. After sacking Sizhou the rebels rallied their scattered followers and joined forces with the La'ershan Miao of Huguang to attack Shiqian. They failed to take the city and, on their return march, passed through Shengxi. Company Commander An Dachao and others cut them off, killing or capturing more than half their force and seizing all their baggage, until the rebels could no longer hold an army together. Bangxian then sent agents to pay Lao Gu, Lao Ge, and others to seize Xubao and deliver him to headquarters, while Heimiao remained in hiding as before. He then paid the Wulang native official Tian Xingbang and others to kill Heimiao by stratagem, and the rebels were completely pacified. He was then promoted to acting regional assistant commander, appointed regional commander, and replaced Shen Xiyi as garrison commander of Guizhou.
55
臺黎砦苗關保倡亂，四川容山、廣西洪江諸苗應之。 遠近騷然，撫剿莫能定。 邦憲與湖廣兵分道討破之，傳檄十八砦，許執首惡贖罪。 諸苗聽撫，設盟受約而還。
At Taili stockade the Miao chief Guanbao raised a revolt, and the Miao of Rongshan in Sichuan, Hongjiang in Guangxi, and other districts answered him. Alarm spread far and wide, and neither persuasion nor force could bring the situation under control. Bangxian divided his forces with Huguang troops and defeated the rebels, then sent proclamations to eighteen stockades offering amnesty to those who handed over the ringleaders. The Miao submitted to pacification; once oaths were sworn and terms accepted, he withdrew his forces.
56
播州宣慰楊烈殺長官王黼，黼黨李保等治兵相攻且十年，總督馮嶽與邦憲討平之。 真州苗盧阿項為亂，邦憲以兵七千編筏渡江，直抵磨子崖。 策賊必夜襲，先設備。 賊至，擊敗之。 賊求援於播州吳鯤。 諸將懼，邦憲曰：「水西宣慰安萬銓，播州所畏也。 吾調水西兵攻烏江，聲楊烈縱鯤助逆罪，烈奚暇救人乎？ 」已，水西兵至。 邦憲進逼其巢，乘風縱火，斬關而登，賊大奔潰，擒賊首父子，斬獲四百七十余人。 進署都督同知。
Yang Lie, the Bozhou surveillance commissioner, killed District Magistrate Wang Fu. Fu's followers, led by Li Bao, mustered troops and fought one another for nearly ten years before Governor-General Feng Yue and Bangxian put them down. The Zhenzhou Miao chief Lu Axiang rebelled. Bangxian took seven thousand men, built rafts, crossed the river, and drove straight to Mozi Cliff. Judging the rebels would strike at night, he laid his traps in advance. When they came, he routed them. The rebels sought help from Wu Kun of Bozhou. The other commanders grew fearful, but Bangxian said, "An Wanquan, the Shuixi surveillance commissioner, is the man Bozhou fears. If I summon Shuixi troops against the Wujiang and charge Yang Lie with allowing Kun to abet rebellion, will Lie have any leisure to send relief?" Before long the Shuixi troops arrived. Bangxian pressed the rebel stronghold, set fires on the wind, broke through their gate, and stormed the heights. The rebels broke and fled. He captured the chief and his son, and took more than four hundred seventy heads and prisoners. He was promoted to acting regional vice commander.
57
破地隆阡叛苗四砦，又破答千諸砦，擒其渠魁。 地隆阡遺賊龍老三、龍得奎結龍停苗老夭、扳凳苗石章保等縱兵掠，執石耶洞土官妻冉氏以歸，攻梅平砦。 官軍要擒老三。 得奎走免，復與老夭等攻破平南營囤。 邦憲偵冉氏在老夭所，陽議贖，而潛擊殺老夭。 官軍遂入龍停砦，並執扳凳砦苗龍老內，令執獻章保。 於是諸苗悉降。 白洗、養鵝諸苗叛，討擒其魁，降百余砦。
He destroyed four rebel Miao stockades at Dilongqian, then broke the stockades at Daqian and elsewhere and captured their ringleaders. Remnants at Dilongqian—Long Laosan and Long Dekui—joined the Longting Miao chief Lao Yao, the Bandeng Miao chief Shi Zhangbao, and others in raiding. They abducted the wife of the Shiyedong native official, a woman surnamed Ran, and attacked Meiping stockade. The government forces set their sights on capturing Laosan. Dekui escaped, then joined Lao Yao and the others in storming the Pingnan camp depot. Learning that Mistress Ran was in Lao Yao's camp, Bangxian openly negotiated her ransom while secretly launching a raid that killed Lao Yao. Government troops then entered Longting stockade and also seized Long Laonei of Bandeng stockade, ordering him to capture Zhangbao and deliver him up. Thereupon all the Miao surrendered. The Baixi and Yang'e Miao and others rebelled; he defeated them, captured their chiefs, and brought more than a hundred stockades to surrender.
58
湖廣漵浦瑤沈亞當等為亂，總督石勇檄邦憲討之，生擒亞當，斬獲二百有奇。 漵浦甫平，銅仁、都勻苗相煽叛。 邦憲亟馳還，率守備安大朝進剿。 先破彪山砦賊，乘勝略定諸砦。 獲賊首龍老羅、王三等，余黨盡平。 又與總督黃光升，修湖北墩臺、烽堠百十所，招降冷水溪諸洞苗二十八砦。
The Xupu Yao of Huguang, led by Shen Yadang, rose in revolt. Governor-General Shi Yong ordered Bangxian against them; he captured Yadang alive and took more than two hundred heads and prisoners. Xupu had barely been pacified when the Miao of Tongren and Duyun stirred one another to rebel. Bangxian raced back at once and led Garrison Commander An Dachao in pursuit. He first broke the rebels at Biaoshan stockade, then rode the momentum to bring the other stockades under control. He captured the rebel chiefs Long Laoluo, Wang San, and others, and the last of their followers were pacified. Working with Governor-General Huang Guangsheng, he restored more than a hundred and ten watch platforms and beacon mounds in Hubei and persuaded twenty-eight stockades of the Cold Water Creek cave Miao to surrender.
59
播州容山副長官土舍韓甸與正長官土舍張問相攻，甸屢勝，遂糾生苗剽湖、貴境，垂二十年。 問亦糾黨自助。 邦憲討之，斬百余人。 問潛出，被獲。 官軍乘勝入甸巢。 會暮，大雨，迷失道。 守備葉勛、百戶魏國相等陷伏中，死焉。 邦憲奪圍出，還軍鎮遠。 再征之，賊沿江守。 邦憲佯與爭，而別自上流三十里編竹以渡。 水陸並進，大破之。 斬甸，容山平。 進右都督。
Han Dian, the Bozhou Rongshan deputy district magistrate, and Zhang Wen, the principal district magistrate, fought one another. Dian won again and again and then rallied unpacified Miao to raid the Huguang and Guizhou frontier for nearly twenty years. Wen in turn rallied allies of his own. Bangxian campaigned against them and took more than a hundred heads. Wen slipped out in secret and was captured. Riding their victory, government troops entered Dian's stronghold. At dusk a heavy rain fell and they lost the trail. Garrison Commander Ye Xun, Company Commander Wei Guoxiang, and others walked into an ambush and were killed. Bangxian fought his way out and withdrew to Zhenyuan. On a second expedition the rebels held the river line. Bangxian feigned a contest for the crossing while, thirty li upstream, his men bound rafts of bamboo and slipped across. He attacked by land and water at once and broke them completely. Dian was beheaded, and Rongshan was pacified. He was promoted to right commander-in-chief.
60
邦憲生長黔土，熟苗情。 善用兵，大小數十百戰，無不摧破。 前後進秩者四，賚銀幣十有三。 所得俸賜，悉以饗士，家無贏資。 為總兵官十七年，威鎮蠻中。 與四川何卿、廣西沈希儀並稱一時名將。 明年卒官。 贈左都督。
Bangxian had grown up in Guizhou and knew the Miao country intimately. A master of arms, he fought scores and hundreds of engagements large and small without once failing to break the enemy. In all he was promoted four times and granted silver and silks on thirteen occasions. Everything he earned in salary and gifts he spent on his men; his household held no surplus. For seventeen years as regional commander his authority held the entire frontier in awe. He stood with He Qing of Sichuan and Shen Xiyi of Guangxi among the age's finest generals. The following year he died in office. He was posthumously granted the rank of left commander-in-chief.
61
贊曰：嗚呼，明至中葉，曷嘗無邊材哉！ 如馬永、梁震、周尚文、沈希儀之徒，出奇制勝，得士卒死力，雖古名將何以加焉？ 然功高賞薄，起蹶靡常。 此無異故，其抗懷奮激，無以結歡在朝柄政重人，宜其齟齬不相入也。 馬芳三代為將，父子兄弟先後殉國，偉矣哉！
The commentator says: Alas—down to the mid-Ming, when was the dynasty ever truly bereft of men of frontier genius? Men such as Ma Yong, Liang Zhen, Zhou Shangwen, and Shen Xiyi won by surprise and mastery of the field and commanded their soldiers' utmost loyalty—what ancient famous general could have done more? Yet their achievements were great while their rewards were meager, and their fortunes rose and fell without steady pattern. The reason is plain: their proud, impassioned temperaments could not curry favor with the powerful men who held the reins at court, and so it was only to be expected that they would chafe against them and never find a place. Three generations of the Ma Fang family served as generals; fathers, sons, and brothers in turn gave their lives for the state—how magnificent!
