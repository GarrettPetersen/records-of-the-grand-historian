鄭嶽，字汝華，莆田人。 弘治六年進士。 授戶部主事，改刑部主事。 董天錫偕錦衣千戶張福決囚，福坐天錫上，嶽言其非體。 且言：「糾劾非鎮監職，而董讓行之。 太常本禮部屬，而崔誌端專之。 內外效尤，益無忌憚」。 忤旨，系獄。 尚書周經、侍郎許進等救，不聽。 贖杖還職。 尋進員外郎。 許進督師大同，貴近惡其剛方，議代之。 罷職總兵官趙袴謀起用，京軍屢出無功。 嶽言進不可代，袴不可用，京軍不可出。 朝論韙之。
Zheng Yue, styled Ruhua, was from Putian. He became a metropolitan graduate in the sixth year of the Hongzhi reign (1493). He was appointed a secretary in the Ministry of Revenue, then transferred to the Ministry of Justice. When Dong Tianxi and Zhang Fu, a chiliarch of the Embroidered Uniform Guard, jointly tried prisoners, Fu was seated above Tianxi; Yue protested that this violated proper protocol. He also said: "Impeachment is not the duty of frontier eunuch supervisors, yet Dong is permitted to conduct it. The Court of Imperial Sacrifices is subordinate to the Ministry of Rites, yet Cui Zhiduan monopolizes its affairs. Inside and outside the court others follow suit, growing ever more brazen." He offended the emperor and was thrown into prison. Minister Zhou Jing, Vice Minister Xu Jin, and others pleaded for him, but the emperor refused to listen. After paying a fine in lieu of corporal punishment, he returned to his post. He was soon promoted to vice director. When Xu Jin commanded the army at Datong, court favorites resented his stern integrity and plotted to replace him. The dismissed regional commander Zhao Ku sought reinstatement, while capital troops had been sent out repeatedly without success. Yue argued that Jin must not be replaced, that Ku must not be reappointed, and that capital troops must not be deployed again. Court opinion sided with him.
3
遷湖廣僉事，歸宗藩侵地於民。 施州夷民相仇殺者，有司以叛告。 嶽擒治其魁，余悉縱遣。 荊、嶽饑，勸富民出粟，馳河泊禁。 屬縣輸糧遠衛，率二石致一石。 嶽以其直給衛，而留粟備振，民乃獲濟。
Transferred to Huguang as vice commissioner, he restored to commoners lands seized by princely estates. When Yi communities in Shizhou feuded and killed one another, local officials reported them as rebels. Yue arrested and punished the ringleaders and released all the rest. When Jing and Yue prefectures suffered famine, he urged wealthy households to release grain and urgently lifted bans on river fisheries. Subordinate counties shipped grain to distant guards, and on average only one picul arrived for every two sent. Yue paid the guards in cash instead, kept the grain for relief stores, and the people were thereby saved.
4
正德初，擢廣西副使。 土官岑猛當徙福建，據田州不肯徙。 嶽許為奏改近地，猛乃請自效。 尋改廣東。 遷江西按察使，就遷左布政使。 宸濠奪民田億萬計，民立砦自保。 宸濠欲兵之，嶽持不可。 會提學副使李夢陽與巡按御史江萬實相訐，嶽承檄按之。 夢陽執嶽親信吏，言嶽子澐受賕，欲因以脅嶽。 宸濠因助夢陽奏其事，囚掠澐。 巡撫任漢顧慮不能決，帝遣大理卿燕忠會給事中黎奭按問。 忠等奏勘嶽子私有跡，而夢陽挾制撫、按，俱宜斥。 嶽遂奪官為民。 宸濠敗，中外交薦，起四川布政使。 以憂不赴。
Early in the Zhengde reign he was promoted to vice commissioner of Guangxi. The native official Cen Meng was ordered to relocate to Fujian, but he held Tianzhou and refused to move. Yue promised to petition for reassignment to a nearer region, whereupon Meng volunteered his service. He was soon transferred to Guangdong. He became surveillance commissioner of Jiangxi and was then promoted directly to left administration commissioner. Prince Chen Hao seized vast tracts of commoners' land, and the people built stockades to defend themselves. When Chen Hao wanted to send troops against them, Yue insisted that he must not. When education vice commissioner Li Mengyang and touring censor Jiang Wanshi accused each other, Yue was ordered to investigate. Mengyang seized Yue's trusted clerk, alleging that Yue's son Yun had taken bribes, hoping thereby to coerce Yue. Chen Hao then backed Mengyang's memorial and imprisoned and tortured Yun. Grand coordinator Ren Han could not decide the case, so the emperor sent chief justice Yan Zhong and supervising secretary Li Yi to conduct an inquiry. Zhong and his colleagues reported traces of private gain involving Yue's son, and that Mengyang had coerced the grand coordinator and censors; all should be dismissed. Yue was stripped of office and reduced to commoner status. After Chen Hao's defeat, officials inside and outside the court recommended him, and he was recalled as administration commissioner of Sichuan. He did not take up the appointment because he was in mourning.
5
劉玉，字鹹栗，萬安人。 祖廣衡，永樂末進士。 正統間，以刑部郎中出修浙江荒政，積粟數百萬，督治陂塘為旱澇備。 景泰初，歷左副都御史，鎮守陜西。 請遇災傷，毋俟勘報，即除其賦，庶有司不得借覆核陰行科率，從之。 還治院事。 福建、浙江盜起，命往督兵捕。 議創壽寧縣於官臺山，以清盜窟。 討平處州賊。 已，復巡撫遼東。 居官以廉節稱。 終刑部尚書。 父喬，成化初進士。 累官湖廣左布政使。 玉登弘治九年進士，授輝縣知縣。 發粟振饑，奏蠲虛稅，復業者千家。 擢御史。 初，孫伯堅、金琦、王寧皆以傳奉得官，已，又以指揮胡震為都指揮，分守通州。 玉抗疏言：「傳奉不已，繼之內批，累聖德，乞皆罷之。 」不納。
Liu Yu, styled Xianli, was from Wan'an. His grandfather Liu Guangheng became a metropolitan graduate at the end of the Yongle reign. During the Zhengtong reign, as a director in the Ministry of Justice he went to manage famine relief in Zhejiang, stockpiled millions of piculs of grain, and oversaw repair of reservoirs and ponds against drought and flood. Early in the Jingtai reign he served as left vice censor-in-chief and guarded Shaanxi. He petitioned that when disaster struck, taxes should be remitted at once without waiting for investigation, so local officials could not use re-verification as a pretext for covert exactions; the court agreed. He returned to administer affairs at the Censorate. When bandits rose in Fujian and Zhejiang, he was ordered to supervise troops in pursuit. He proposed creating Shouning County at Guantai Mountain to eliminate bandit strongholds. He campaigned and pacified the bandits of Chuzhou. Later he again served as grand coordinator of Liaodong. In office he was known for integrity and frugality. He ended his career as minister of justice. His father Liu Qiao became a metropolitan graduate early in the Chenghua reign. He rose through the ranks to left administration commissioner of Huguang. Yu became a metropolitan graduate in the ninth year of Hongzhi (1496) and was appointed magistrate of Huixian. He distributed grain to relieve famine, petitioned to remit fictitious taxes, and a thousand households resumed their livelihoods. He was promoted to censor. Earlier Sun Bojian, Jin Qi, and Wang Ning had all received office through special imperial transmission; later the commander Hu Zhen was also made regional commander and assigned to guard Tongzhou. Yu submitted a forceful memorial: "Special transmissions never cease, followed by inner rescripts; this burdens the emperor's virtue; I beg that all such appointments be abolished. " His plea was not accepted.
6
世宗即位，召為左僉都御史。 論遏亂功，進右副都御史。 嘉靖元年改左。 歷刑部左、右侍郎。 初，偕九卿爭興獻帝不宜稱皇，及帝欲考獻帝，又偕廷臣伏闕哭爭。 六年秋坐李福達獄削籍，卒於家。
When the Shizong Emperor ascended the throne, Yu was summoned as left vice censor-in-chief. For merit in quelling disorder he was promoted to right vice censor-in-chief. In the first year of Jiajing (1522) he was transferred to the left post. He served successively as left and right vice minister of justice. Earlier he had joined the nine ministers in arguing that the Xingxian Emperor ought not be styled emperor; when the emperor wished to honor his father as imperial forebear, Yu again joined court officials in weeping and protesting at the palace gate. In the autumn of the sixth year he was struck from the rolls in the Li Fuda case and died at home.
7
玉所居僅庇風雨。 天文、地理、兵制、刑律皆有論著。 隆慶初，贈刑部尚書，謚端毅。 子愨，南京工部右侍郎。 歷官亦有聲。
Yu's dwelling barely kept out wind and rain. He wrote treatises on astronomy, geography, military institutions, and penal law. Early in the Longqing reign he was posthumously made minister of justice with the posthumous title Duanyi (Upright and Resolute). His son Zi Que served as right vice minister of works at Nanjing. In successive offices he too earned a fine reputation.
8
汪元錫，字天啟，婺源人。 正德六年進士。 授兵科給事中。 三遷都給事中。 陜西鎮守中官廖鸞族子鎧，冒功為錦衣千戶，隨鸞於陜。 元錫爭之，言鎧父鵬已亂中州，勿使鎧復亂陜右。 乞征還鸞，置鎧父子於理。 偏頭關之捷，錄功太濫，偕同官言太監張忠、總兵官劉暉等不宜賞。 湖廣鎮守太監杜甫請巡歷所部，帝許之，元錫等據祖制力爭。 帝幸昌平、宣府、大同，元錫偕同官邢寰累疏諫； 復言宣府守將朱振等皆扈從西巡，寇乘虛入塞，何以禦之？ 已，聞帝將選禁軍親征四海治部寇，復極陳不可。 安遠侯柳文鎮湖廣，奏攜參隨七十余人，元錫乞寢所奏。 車駕還京，以應州之捷大賚文武群臣。 元錫等言：「是役殺邊民無算，六軍多傷。 今君臣欣喜交賀，而軍民系賊庭，南向號哭，臣等何忍受賜？ 」中旨以納粟都指揮馬昊守備儀真，復遣內官分守潼關、山海關，駕又幸大喜峰口，欲招三衛花當、把兒孫，元錫等皆抗章諫。
Wang Yuanxi, styled Tianqi, was from Wuyuan. He became a metropolitan graduate in the sixth year of the Zhengde reign (1511). He was appointed a supervising secretary in the Bureau of Military Affairs. After three promotions he became chief supervising secretary. Liao Luan, the eunuch guarding Shaanxi, had a clansman Kai who falsely claimed merit to become a chiliarch of the Embroidered Uniform Guard and accompanied Luan in Shaanxi. Yuanxi protested, saying Kai's father Peng had already thrown the central provinces into disorder and Kai must not be allowed to do the same in western Shaanxi. He begged that Luan be recalled and that father and son Kai be brought to justice. After the victory at Piankou Pass rewards were distributed too lavishly; together with colleagues he argued that the eunuch Zhang Zhong, regional commander Liu Hui, and others ought not be rewarded. Du Fu, the eunuch guarding Huguang, requested to tour his jurisdiction; when the emperor assented, Yuanxi and others forcefully protested on the basis of ancestral institutions. When the emperor visited Changping, Xuanfu, and Datong, Yuanxi and his colleague Xing Huan repeatedly memorialized in remonstrance; he added that the defending generals of Xuanfu such as Zhu Zhen had all joined the western tour, so if raiders seized the opportunity to enter the passes, how could they be resisted? Later, learning that the emperor planned to select palace troops to campaign in person against the Sihai Zhibu bandits, he again forcefully argued that this must not be done. When Marquis of Anyuan Liu Wen, guarding Huguang, memorialized that he would bring more than seventy attendants, Yuanxi begged that the memorial be shelved. When the imperial carriage returned to the capital, the victory at Yingzhou was used to bestow lavish rewards on civil and military officials. Yuanxi and others said: "In this campaign countless border people were killed and the six armies suffered heavy casualties. Now ruler and ministers rejoice and congratulate one another, while soldiers and civilians remain captive in the rebel camp, weeping toward the south—how can we bear to accept rewards?" " By inner rescript the grain-paying regional commander Ma Hao was assigned to guard Yizhen; inner eunuchs were again dispatched to guard Tong Pass and Shanhai Pass; the emperor again visited Daxingfeng Pass, intending to summon Huadang and Ba'ersun of the three guards—and Yuanxi and others all submitted forceful remonstrances.
9
帝欲南幸，舒芬、黃鞏切諫得罪，給事御史遂不敢爭。 及帝將親征宸濠，元錫復諫沮。 宸濠就執，元錫、寰偕六科馳疏請回鑾。 十五年，帝在南京，元錫等復屢申前請，且言：「供億繁費，使牒旁午。 奸宄冒官校，少女充離宮。 陛下不以宗社為重，專事逸遊，豈能長保天下。 」語甚危切。
When the emperor wished to travel south, Shu Fen and Huang Gong remonstrated sharply and were punished, after which supervising secretaries and censors dared not protest. When the emperor was about to campaign in person against Chen Hao, Yuanxi again remonstrated to dissuade him. Once Chen Hao was captured, Yuanxi and Huan, together with the six bureaus, hurriedly memorialized requesting that the emperor return to the capital. In the fifteenth year, while the emperor was at Nanjing, Yuanxi and others again repeatedly pressed their earlier requests, saying: "Supplies are costly and abundant, and messengers' documents arrive incessantly. Evildoers pose as official attendants, and young girls fill the detached palaces. Your Majesty does not treat the altars of state as paramount but devotes yourself to idle travel—how can you long preserve the realm? " Their language was grave and urgent.
10
中旨以內官晁進、楊保分守蘭州、肅州，元錫等言：「二州逼強寇，不可增官守，累居民。 」群小不悅，矯旨責之。 詔改團營西官廳為威武團練營，以江彬、許泰等提督之，別擇地為團營教場。 元錫言：「拓地則擾居民，興工則費財力，以朝廷自將之軍而彬等概加提督，則僭名分。 」不從。 會帝崩，事已。
By inner rescript the eunuchs Chao Jin and Yang Bao were sent to guard Lanzhou and Suzhou separately; Yuanxi and others said: "These two prefectures border powerful enemies; additional garrisons must not be imposed to burden the people. " The petty faction was displeased and forged an edict to rebuke them. An edict changed the western official hall of the Tuan barracks to the Weiwu Tuanlian Camp under the supervision of Jiang Bin, Xu Tai, and others, and another site was chosen for the Tuan barracks drill ground. Yuanxi said: "Expanding the grounds will disturb residents; launching construction will waste wealth and labor; placing troops the court commands in person entirely under Bin's supervision usurps proper ranks and titles. " His plea was not accepted. When the emperor died, the matter came to an end.
11
世宗即位，疏言：「都督郤永以附江彬下獄，宜釋而用之。 錦衣都指揮郭鰲等十人皆彬黨，宜下獄治。 」鹹報可。 張銑、許泰系獄，帝忽宥其死。 元錫爭，不聽。 屢遷至太仆卿。 嘉靖六年，帝以李福達獄下三法司於理。 元錫不能平，有後言，聞於張璁，並下獄奪職。 後用薦起故官。 歷戶部左、右侍郎，致仕，卒。
When the Shizong Emperor ascended the throne, he memorialized: "Regional commander Xi Yong was imprisoned for siding with Jiang Bin and should be released and employed. Ten men including Guo Ao, regional commander of the Embroidered Uniform Guard, are all Jiang Bin's faction and should be imprisoned and punished. " The court approved all of this. Zhang Xian and Xu Tai were imprisoned, but the emperor suddenly pardoned them from death. Yuanxi protested, but the emperor would not listen. After repeated promotions he reached minister of the imperial stud. In the sixth year of Jiajing the emperor sent the three judicial offices to try the Li Fuda case. Yuanxi could not accept this calmly and made remarks afterward; when Zhang Cong heard of them, Yuanxi was imprisoned and stripped of office. Later, on recommendation, he was restored to his former office. He served successively as left and right vice minister of revenue, retired, and died.
12
邢寰，黃梅人。 正德三年進士。 數言事，有直聲。
Xing Huan was from Huangmei. He became a metropolitan graduate in the third year of the Zhengde reign (1508). He repeatedly spoke out on public affairs and earned a reputation for forthrightness.

寇天敘，字子惇，榆次人。 由鄉舉入太學。 與崔銑、呂柟善。 登正德三年進士，除南京大理評事，進寺副。 累遷應天府丞。 武宗駐南京，從官衛士十余萬，日費金萬計，近幸求索倍之。 尹齊宗道憂懼卒，天敘攝其事，日青衣皂帽坐堂上。 江彬使者至，好語之曰：「民窮官帑乏，無可結歡，丞專待譴耳。 」彬使累至皆然，彬亦止。 他權幸有求，則曰：「俟若奏即予。 」禁軍攫民物，天敘與兵部尚書喬宇選拳勇者與搏戲。 禁軍卒受傷，慚且畏，不敢橫。 其隨事禁制多類此。 駕駐九月，南京不大困者，天敘與宇力也。
Kou Tianxu, styled Zidun, was from Yuci. Through provincial recommendation he entered the Imperial Academy. He was on good terms with Cui Shuan and Lu Nan. He passed the metropolitan examination in the third year of Zhengde, was appointed an evaluator at the Nanjing Court of Judicial Review, and was promoted to vice director of the court. He rose through the ranks to vice prefect of Yingtian Prefecture. When the Wuzong Emperor resided at Nanjing, attendant officials and guards numbered more than a hundred thousand; daily expenses ran to tens of thousands in gold, and the demands of favorites doubled that. Yin Qizong died of worry and fear; Tianxu took charge of affairs, daily sitting in court dressed in plain blue robes and black caps. When Jiang Bin's envoys arrived, he spoke to them kindly: "The people are impoverished and the official treasury is empty; there is nothing with which to win favor—the vice prefect can only await punishment. " Bin's envoys came repeatedly and received the same answer, and Bin eventually desisted. When other powerful favorites made requests, he would say: "Wait until you memorialize and it will be granted at once. " When palace troops seized civilians' goods, Tianxu and Minister of War Qiao Yu selected men skilled in boxing to wrestle with them. Palace soldiers were injured, ashamed and fearful, and dared not act arrogantly. His prohibitions on various matters were mostly of this kind. During the nine months the emperor resided there, the fact that Nanjing was not greatly distressed was due to the efforts of Tianxu and Yu.
14
嘉靖三年，以右僉都御史巡撫宣府。 未行，改鄖陽。 甫二月，又改甘肅。 回賊犯山丹，督將士擒其長脫脫木兒。 西域貢獅子、犀牛、西狗，天敘請卻之，不聽。 進右副都御史，巡撫陜西。 寇入固原，擊敗之，斬首百余。 又討平大盜王居等，累賜銀幣。 織造太監至，有司議奏罷之。 天敘曰：「甫至遽請罷，即不罷，焰且益張。 」會歲祲，乃請蠲租稅，發粟振饑民； 因言織造非儉歲所宜設，帝立召還。 歷兵部右侍郎，卒。 家貧，喪事不具。 天敘在太學時，嘗聞父疾，馳六晝夜抵家，父疾亦廖。
In the third year of Jiajing he was appointed right vice censor-in-chief and grand coordinator of Xuanfu. Before he departed he was transferred to Yunyang. After only two months he was again transferred to Gansu. When Hui bandits invaded Shandan, he supervised officers and soldiers in capturing their leader Tuotuomu'er. When the Western Regions presented lions, rhinoceroses, and western dogs, Tianxu requested that these be declined, but the emperor refused. He was promoted to right vice censor-in-chief and grand coordinator of Shaanxi. When bandits entered Guyuan, he defeated them and took more than a hundred heads. He also campaigned and pacified the great bandit Wang Ju and others, and was repeatedly rewarded with silver and silks. When the weaving eunuch arrived, local officials discussed memorializing to abolish the post. Tianxu said: "If we request abolition as soon as he arrives, even should the post not be abolished, his arrogance will only grow. " It happened to be a year of crop failure; he then requested remission of rents and taxes and distribution of grain to relieve the hungry; adding that weaving establishments were inappropriate in years of scarcity, whereupon the emperor immediately recalled the eunuch. He served as right vice minister of war and died. His family was poor and could not afford a proper funeral. When Tianxu was in the Imperial Academy, he once heard his father was ill, galloped home in six days and nights, and his father's illness likewise abated.

He returned home for mourning. Liu Jin dismissed all who, after mourning ended, had long failed to take up office; Zhou was stripped of office on this charge. When Jin was executed, Zhou was summoned to office but declined because his mother was elderly. Early in the Jiajing reign he was restored to his former office. He was promoted to vice commissioner of Jinteng. The native chieftain Mang Xin was cruel; Zhou devised a plan and captured him. When Mubang and Mengyang went to war, Zhou sent envoys to proclaim the court's will, and Mubang thereupon ceded territory. After repeated promotions he became left administration commissioner of Guangxi. Government troops had campaigned against the Gutian bandits for long without success; Zhou sent envoys to pacify them, and the ringleader said: "This is the former Tang commissioner who had my son enter school. In the present affair, if we only wish them to resume tribute, troops need not be used and officials need not be sent. If we wish to punish them, there are seven reasons why this cannot be done; I beg to set them forth one by one:

古帝王不以中國之治治蠻夷，故安南不征，著在《祖訓》。 一也。
Ancient emperors did not govern barbarians with the methods used to govern China; therefore Annam was not to be attacked—this is recorded in the Ancestral Instructions. This is the first reason.

After the Taizong Emperor destroyed Li Jilong, he sought descendants of the Chen house but found none and only then made Annam into commanderies and counties. Later warfare continued without end, and the Renzong Emperor always regretted it. The Zhang Emperor fulfilled his predecessor's will by abandoning the territory; today we should follow that precedent. This is the second reason.

When outer barbarians divide and contend among themselves, it is China's good fortune. From the Five Dynasties through the Yuan, Annam changed among eight ruling houses—Qu, Liu, Shao, Wu, Ding, Li, Li, and Chen—rising and falling in succession, and external alarms south of the ranges thus grew rare. Now they are in strife—this is precisely when we ought not intervene; why harm innocent people to overawe petty villains, cutting the heartland to patch the limbs—without benefit and with harm? This is the third reason.

If one says it is China's near border and we ought to seize it amid disorder, I would point out that in Ma Yuan's southern campaign he deeply penetrated Langbo, soldiers died to nearly half their number, and the bronze pillar he erected as Han's utmost border lies close to today's Siming Prefecture. Our dynasty once pacified the region, yet they repeatedly submitted and rebelled; Chinese soldiers and horses who perished numbered in the hundreds of thousands; more than twenty years of wealth and labor were exhausted, gaining only the empty name of several dozen commanderies and counties. Moreover there are precedents of campaigns that did not succeed, as under Song Taizong and Shenzong and under Yuan Xianzong and Shizu? These should serve as stern warnings. This is the fourth reason.
21

When foreign states offer tribute, it is to their own benefit. On one hand they accept the reign calendar to overawe their neighbors; on the other they open trade to enrich their state. Therefore, though warfare rages now, they have repeatedly submitted memorials and tribute goods, knocking at the passes to seek entry, yet border officials turned them away because the names did not match. This means they wished to offer tribute but were unable to—it is not that they resisted and refused tribute. To blame them on this account is an argument that does not hold. This is the fifth reason.
22
興師則需餉。 今四川有采木之役，貴州有凱口之師，而兩廣積儲數十萬，率耗於田州岑猛之役。 又大工頻興，所在軍儲悉輸將作，興師數十萬，何以給之？ 六也。
Raising troops requires supplies. Now Sichuan has timber-cutting corvée, Guizhou has the Kaikou campaign, and the accumulated stores of the two Guang provinces—numbering several hundred thousand—were mostly consumed in the campaign against Cen Meng of Tianzhou. Moreover, major construction projects arise frequently, and military stores everywhere are sent to the Directorate of Works. To raise an army numbering in the hundreds of thousands—how can it be supplied? This is the sixth reason.
23

Yet what I fear is not limited to this. The Tang dynasty's decline began with Emperor Ming's campaign against Nanzhao. The Song dynasty's decline began with Shenzong's campaign against the Liao. Now northern enemies grow stronger daily and occupy our Ordos. Border troops repeatedly rebel and destroy our defenses. With pressing concerns to the north, to open discussion of a southern campaign—if something unforeseen happens, who will bear the blame? This is the seventh reason.

Embroidered Uniform Guard military men are ignorant of the larger principles. If they slightly distort the truth of right and wrong and cause them not to submit, it would only damage our prestige. Even if investigation establishes the facts, to attack is impossible and not to attack is impossible; with no footing for advance or retreat, how can one formulate policy? Moreover, though the edict to muster troops for deployment has only just been issued, the harm of levies and harassment has already appeared—the worry lies not with outer barbarians but within the realm. I beg that the dispatch of investigating officials be halted and all levies be canceled—the realm would be greatly fortunate.

When the Shizong Emperor ascended the throne, Zhong was restored to his former office. Before he could take up the post, he was promoted to vice commissioner in Guangdong. He was transferred again to vice education commissioner in Guangxi, teaching by his own example. He selected the best students and gathered them at the Five Classics Academy, lecturing and debating in the hall once every five days. On his third transfer he became Right Administration Commissioner of Guangdong. He offended the governor-general and the investigating censor and was judged unfit for office, facing dismissal. Huo Tao, acting head of the Ministry of Personnel, said Zhong had always been incorrupt and talented and ought to be retained. As some in the government were displeased with him, he was demoted to Right Vice Commissioner of Sichuan. In the eighteenth year he was promoted to Right Vice Censor-in-Chief and appointed regional governor of Shandong. During a famine year he ordered double grain payments for locust catchers; the locust plague ended and the hungry were fed. He captured the notorious bandit Guan Jiguang; neighboring jurisdictions claimed the credit, but Zhong did not dispute it. He was promoted to Vice Censor-in-Chief and put in charge of Nanjing grain reserves. When Censor Jin Can inspected Sichuan he had once recommended Zhong. Zhong never thanked him; Can resented this and now dredged up other matters to impeach him on false charges. Just as a transfer was being discussed, Zhong died. During the Guangzong reign he was posthumously granted the title Zhuangjie.

中守官廉。 自廣西歸，欲飯客，貸米鄰家。 米至，又乏薪，將以浴器爨。 會日已暮，竟不及飯而別。 少學於同里楊珠，既而擴充之，沈潛邃密，學者稱谷平先生。 門人羅洪先、王龜年、周子恭皆能傳其學。 中族人楷，又傳洪先之學。
Zhong served as an incorrupt official. Returning from Guangxi he wanted to entertain guests and borrowed rice from a neighbor. When the rice arrived he still had no firewood and was about to cook with a bathing tub. Evening came before he could eat, and his guests departed unfed. In youth he studied under Yang Zhu of his home district, then developed that learning further; steeped in profound scholarship, he was known to scholars as Master Guping. His disciples Luo Hongxian, Wang Guinian, and Zhou Zigong all carried on his teaching. Zhong's clansman Kai in turn carried on Hongxian's teaching.

Kai, styled Bangzheng. A provincial graduate, he was appointed magistrate of Tangxi. After completing mourning for his mother, he was reassigned to Qingtian. At the time Japanese pirates ravaged the southeast; Kai stockpiled grain to support the defenses. Qingtian had no city wall. When the pirates arrived Kai held them at Shabu; unable to cross, they withdrew, and he used the interval to build city walls. When the pirates returned he manned the ramparts, killing several each day until they fled. He was transferred to magistrate of Changle, where he was also known for effective governance.

歐陽鐸，字崇道，泰和人。 正德三年進士。 授行人。 上書極論時政，不報。 使蜀府，王厚遺之，不受。 歷工部郎中，改南兵部。 出為延平知府。 毀淫祠數十百所，以其材葺學宮。 司禮太監蕭敬家奴殺人，置之法。 調福州，議均徭曰：「郡多士大夫，其士大夫又多田產。 民有產者無幾耳，而徭則盡責之民。 請分民半役。 」士大夫率不便。 巡按御史汪珊力持之，議乃行。 嘉靖三年擢廣東提學副使。 累遷南京光祿卿，歷右副都御史，巡撫應天十府。 蘇、松田不甚相懸。 下者畝五升，上者至二十倍。 鐸令賦最重者減耗米，派輕賫； 最輕者征本色，增耗米。 陰輕重之，賦乃均。 諸推收田，從圩不從戶，詭寄無所容。 州縣荒田四千四百余頃，歲勒民償賦。 鐸以所清漏賦及他奇羨補之。 議徭役及裁郵置費凡數十百條，民皆稱便。 遷南京兵部侍郎，進吏部右侍郎。 九廟災，自陳去。
Ouyang Duo, styled Chongdao, was from Taihe. He passed the jinshi examination in the third year of Zhengde. He was appointed a courier in the Ministry of Rites. He submitted a memorial vigorously criticizing current policy, but received no response. While on mission to the princely establishment of Shu, Wang tried to lavish gifts on him, but he refused. He served as a director in the Ministry of Works and was transferred to the Nanjing Ministry of War. He was appointed prefect of Yanping. He destroyed dozens of illicit shrines and used their timber to repair the school precinct. When a household slave of the eunuch Xiao Jing of the Directorate of Ceremonial murdered someone, Duo had him punished by law. Transferred to Fuzhou, he proposed equalizing corvée obligations: "This prefecture has many gentlemen, and they also hold most of the land. Few commoners own property, yet corvée labor falls entirely on them. I ask that gentlemen and commoners each bear half the labor obligations. " The gentry generally objected. Investigating Censor Wang Shan forcefully upheld the proposal, and it was adopted. In the third year of Jiajing he was promoted to vice education commissioner in Guangdong. He rose to Director of Imperial Entertainment at Nanjing, then served as Right Vice Censor-in-Chief and regional governor of the ten Yingtian prefectures. Landholdings in Suzhou and Songjiang were not greatly unequal in size. Lower-grade fields paid five sheng per mu in tax, while the highest paid as much as twenty times that. Duo ordered those with the heaviest tax assessments to reduce surcharge grain and shift lighter surcharges to them; while those with the lightest assessments paid in kind and took on increased surcharge grain. By quietly redistributing the burden, taxes were equalized. For all reassigned tax land, registration followed dyke enclosures rather than households, leaving no room for fraudulent shifting of obligations. Prefectures and counties had more than 4,400 qing of abandoned farmland, and each year the people were forced to pay tax on it. Duo made up the shortfall from recovered uncollected taxes and other surplus revenue. He drafted dozens of proposals on corvée labor and cutting postal expenses, all of which the people found beneficial. He was transferred to Vice Minister of War at Nanjing and promoted to Right Vice Minister of Personnel. After the fire at the Nine Temples he offered his resignation.

Duo was a man of letters with refined private conduct. Though he rose to high office, his household remained austere. At his death he was posthumously made Minister of Works and granted the posthumous title Gongjian.

陶諧，字世和，會稽人。 弘治八年鄉試第一。 明年成進士，選庶吉士，授工科給事中。 請命儒臣日講《大學衍義》，孝宗嘉納之。
Tao Xie, styled Shihe, was from Kuaiji. He ranked first in the provincial examination in the eighth year of Hongzhi. The next year he passed the jinshi examination, was selected as a Hanlin bachelor, and was appointed supervising secretary in the Ministry of Works section. He asked that Confucian officials lecture daily on the Extended Meaning of the Great Learning, and the Xiaozong Emperor approved the proposal.

正德改元，劉瑾等亂政。 諧請以瑾等誤國罪告先帝，罪之勿赦。 瑾摘其譌字令對狀，伏罪乃宥之。 帝命中官崔杲等往江南、浙江織造，杲等復乞長蘆鹽引。 諧再疏爭，皆不聽。 諧當出理邊儲，以工科掌印無人，請俟行日遣官代署。 瑾遂中諧，下詔獄廷杖，斥為民。 旋榜為奸黨。 又誣以巡視十庫時缺布不奏，復械至闕下杖之，謫戍肅州。 瑾誅，釋還鄉，其黨猶用事，竟不獲召。
When the Zhengde reign began, Liu Jin and his faction threw the government into disorder. Xie requested that Jin and his associates be charged before the late emperor with the crime of ruining the state and punished without pardon. Jin seized on a textual error and forced him to face judgment; only after admitting guilt was he spared. The emperor sent eunuchs such as Cui Gao to Jiangnan and Zhejiang to supervise weaving, and Gao again requested Changlu salt certificates. Xie memorialized twice in protest, but both times his objections went unheeded. Xie was soon to leave to manage frontier grain stores; as no one else held the Works-section seal, he asked that an official be sent to act for him when he departed. Jin then set a trap for Xie, who was imprisoned, beaten at court, and stripped of office. He was soon listed among the treacherous faction. He was then falsely accused of failing to report cloth shortages during an inspection of the ten treasuries, beaten again at the palace gates, and exiled to Suzhou as a soldier. After Jin was executed he was released and returned home, but Jin's allies still held power and he was never recalled.

嘉靖元年復官。 未至，除江西僉事，轉河南管河副使。 命沿河植柳，傍藝葭葦，有事采以為埽。 總理都御史請推行之諸道，歲省費鉅萬。 遷參政，歷左、右布政使，皆在河南。 久之，擢右副都御史，提督南、贛、汀、漳軍務。 疏言：「守令遷太驟，宜以六年為期。 言官忤旨，當優容。 養病官才力堪任者，毋終棄。 」時南京御史馬敭等劾王瓊被逮，而新例養病久者率不復收敘，故諧以為言。 又奏：「今天下差徭煩重。 既有河夫、機兵、打手、富戶、力士諸役，乃編審裏甲，復征曠丁課及供億諸費。 乞皆罷免。 」帝采納之。
In the first year of Jiajing he was restored to office. Before he could take up his post he was appointed vice commissioner in Jiangxi, then transferred to vice commissioner for river management in Henan. He ordered willows planted along the riverbanks and reeds cultivated beside them, to be harvested for river dikes when needed. The supervising censor-in-chief asked that the policy be extended to all circuits, saving tens of thousands of taels each year. He was promoted to vice commissioner and served successively as Left and Right Administration Commissioner of Henan. After some time he was promoted to Right Vice Censor-in-Chief and put in charge of military affairs in southern Jiangxi and southern Fujian. In a memorial he wrote: "Magistrates and prefects are transferred too frequently; a six-year term would be appropriate. Remonstrance officials who offend the throne ought to be treated with forbearance. Officials on sick leave whose talents still qualify them for office should not be permanently cast aside. " At the time Nanjing Censor Ma Yang and others were arrested for impeaching Wang Qiong, and under a new rule officials on long sick leave were generally not reappointed—hence Xie's memorial. He also memorialized: "Corvée levies throughout the realm have become burdensome. In addition to river laborers, militia soldiers, brawlers, wealthy households, and strongmen, the lijia system was recompiled and fees for vacant households and supply costs were levied again. I ask that all of these be abolished. " The emperor adopted his proposals.

尋遷兵部右侍郎，總督兩廣軍務。 海寇陳邦瑞、許折桂等突入波羅廟，欲犯廣州，為指揮李筜所蹙。 邦瑞投水死，折桂還所執指揮二人，乞就撫。 諧居折桂等東莞，編為總甲，使約束其黨五百人為新民。 兵部以降賊群聚，恐乘隙為變，令解散其黨。 已，陽春賊趙林花等攻城，與德慶賊鳳二全相倚為患，諧討破百二十五砦。 帝曰：「諧功足錄，第前縱患者誰？ 」乃僅賚銀幣。 瓊山沙灣洞賊黎佛二等殺典史，諧復剿平。 為總督三年，俘斬累萬。 母憂歸。 起兵部左侍郎。 九廟災，自陳致仕歸。 卒，贈兵部尚書。 隆慶初，謚莊敏。
Soon afterward he was promoted to Right Vice Minister of War and put in overall command of military affairs in Guangdong and Guangxi. Sea bandits Chen Bangrui, Xu Zhegui, and others suddenly entered Boluo Temple intending to attack Guangzhou, but were driven back by Commander Li Dang. Bangrui drowned himself; Zhegui returned the two commanders he had captured and asked to submit to pacification. Xie settled Zhegui and his followers in Dongguan, organized them into zongjia units, and had them keep their five hundred followers in line as new subjects. The Ministry of War, fearing that the surrendered bandits might rebel if left gathered together, ordered their followers dispersed. Soon after, Yangchun bandits led by Zhao Linhua attacked a city and, backed by Deqing bandit Feng Erquan, caused trouble; Xie attacked and destroyed 125 stockades. The emperor said: "Xie's merit deserves recognition—but who earlier indulged the troublemakers? " He then granted only silver and silks. Li Fo'er and other bandits of Shawan Cave in Qiongshan killed a registrar; Xie again suppressed and pacified them. As governor-general for three years he captured and killed tens of thousands. He returned home to observe mourning for his mother. He was promoted to Left Vice Minister of War. After the fire at the Nine Temples he petitioned to retire and returned home. At his death he was posthumously made Minister of War. Early in the Longqing reign he was posthumously granted the title Zhuangmin.

Sun Dashun, styled Jingxi. He passed the jinshi examination in the forty-fifth year of Jiajing. He rose to Right Administration Commissioner of Fujian. When silver was lost from the treasury, fifty clerks and runners were all imprisoned. Dashun said to the left commissioner: "There are only two or three thieves—why imprison everyone? Allow me to handle this for you. " He then released the prisoners and had them track down the thieves, and the real culprits were found. He ended his career as Right Vice Censor-in-Chief and regional governor of Guangxi.

弟大臨，字虞臣。 嘉靖三十五年進士及第，授編修。 吳時來劾嚴嵩，大臨為定疏草。 時來下詔獄，詰所共謀。 大臨不顧，日餉之藥物，時來亦忍死無一言。 萬歷初，累官吏部侍郎。 卒，贈吏部尚書，謚文僖。 大臨少應舉杭州，鄰婦夜奔，拒之，旦遂徙舍。 為人寬然長者，而內持貞介，不以勢利易。
His younger brother Dalin, styled Yuchen. He placed among the top graduates in the thirty-fifth year of Jiajing and was appointed a Hanlin compiler. When Wu Shilai impeached Yan Song, Dalin drafted the memorial for him. When Shilai was imprisoned he was questioned about his co-conspirators. Dalin ignored the inquiry and daily supplied him with medicine and food; Shilai also endured death without betraying anyone. Early in the Wanli reign he rose to Vice Minister of Personnel. At his death he was posthumously made Minister of Personnel and granted the posthumous title Wenxi. As a young man taking the examination at Hangzhou, a neighbor's wife came to him at night; he refused her and moved lodgings at dawn. He was by nature an easygoing elder, yet inwardly held to integrity and would not bend to power or profit.

Pan Xun, styled Bohe, was from Shanyang. He passed the jinshi examination in the third year of Zhengde. He was appointed supervising secretary in the Ministry of Works section. By nature he was firm and resolute and impeached without fear. In criticizing great officials such as Wang Ding, Liu Ji, Ning Gao, and Chen Tianxiang, many of his proposals were adopted.

I urge Your Majesty to dwell only in a secure residence, follow only the Great Way, be intimate only with upright men, honor only Confucian learning, review troops only at a great inspection, complete cases only by enforcing the law, allow no interference in government except by close kin, and grant rewards only for genuine military merit. Within the inner court there are zithers, lutes, bells, and drums—the music of human relations; there is no need to seek delight in wandering palaces or find pleasure in consorting with petty men; outside, all within the civilized realm are Your subjects; there is no need to gather court officials as private retainers or collect distant men as warriors. Right Chief Commander Mao Lun was sentenced to death for associating with Liu Jin, and hereditary privilege was stripped. In the seventh year of Jiajing he rose to Right Vice Censor-in-Chief and regional governor of Henan. The great bandit Chen Qing of Luzhou held Qingyang Mountain in rebellion; Jiang Chao, governor of Shanxi, and Chang Dao successively attacked the bandits without success, so an edict ordered Xun to join the suppression. Xun plotted with Dao, saying: "The bandits hold the heights and are hard to meet in battle. But to touch powerful favorites brought immediate disaster, and all considered Mao in peril. Yet Bin daily attended the emperor's amusements and paid no heed. Requests to return the imperial carriage and remonstrance against the southern tour—Mao joined in all. When Chen Hao rebelled the emperor was at the southern capital; Mao followed on the journey. He requested urgent settlement of rewards for suppressing the rebels; afterward he again repeatedly requested return to the capital, leading colleagues to prostrate at the palace gate—all went unheeded.
56
出為廣東參議，遷副使。 嘉靖四年，有錦衣官校偵事廣東，懋與按察使張祐疑其偽，執之。 事聞，逮下詔獄，謫藤縣典史。 屢遷至廣西布政使。 十六年入為應天府尹。 坐所進鄉試錄忤旨，致仕，卒。
He was appointed vice commissioner of Guangdong and later promoted to vice commissioner. In the fourth year of Jiajing a Brocade Guard attendant was sent to spy in Guangdong; Mao and Surveillance Commissioner Zhang You suspected he was an impostor and arrested him. When the matter was reported he was arrested, imprisoned, and demoted to registrar of Teng County. He rose to administration commissioner of Guangxi. In the sixteenth year he entered court as prefect of Yingtian. Because the provincial examination records he submitted offended the throne, he retired and died.
57
王儀，字克敬，文安人。 嘉靖二年進士。 除靈璧知縣。 以能，調嘉定。 七年擢御史，巡按陜西。 秦府豪占民產，儀悉奪還民。 延綏大饑，朝命陜西布政使胡忠為巡撫，儀論罷之。 已，巡按河南。 趙府輔國將軍祐椋招亡命殺人劫奪積十余年，莫敢發。 儀偕巡撫吳山奏之，奪爵禁錮。 會儀出為蘇州知府，甫三月，祐椋潛入都，奏儀捃摭，並訐都御史毛伯溫以私憾入己罪。 且言：「臣嘗建醮祈皇嗣，為知府王天民訕笑」，請並按問。 帝心知祐椋罪，而悅其建醮語。 為遣使覆按，解儀、伯溫任，下天民獄。 使者奏儀不誣，第祐椋罪在赦前，宜輕坐。 帝終憐祐椋愛己，竟復其爵，除儀名，伯溫、山、天民皆得罪。 終嘉靖世，多以誹謗齋醮獲重禍，由祐椋訐奏始。
Wang Yi, styled Kejing, was from Wen'an. He passed the jinshi examination in the second year of Jiajing. He was appointed magistrate of Lingbi. For ability he was transferred to Jiading. In the seventh year he was promoted to censor and inspected Shaanxi. Powerful families of the Qin princely establishment seized commoners' property; Yi took it all back for the people. Yan-sui suffered great famine; the court ordered Shaanxi Administration Commissioner Hu Zhong as governor; Yi memorialized to dismiss him. Soon after he inspected Henan. Auxiliary State General You Liang of the Zhao princely establishment recruited fugitives to kill and plunder for more than ten years; no one dared expose it. Yi joined Governor Wu Shan in memorializing; his rank was stripped and he was confined. As Yi went out as prefect of Suzhou, after only three months You Liang secretly entered the capital and memorialized that Yi had dredged up charges and also denounced Censor-in-Chief Mao Bowen for inserting personal resentment into his own crimes. He also said: "Your servant once performed a ritual to pray for an imperial heir and was mocked by Prefect Wang Tianmin"—and requested joint investigation. The emperor knew in his heart that You Liang was guilty, yet was pleased by his ritual language. He sent envoys to reinvestigate, relieved Yi and Bowen of office, and imprisoned Tianmin. The envoys reported that Yi had not falsely charged him, but You Liang's crime fell before an amnesty and ought to be lightly punished. The emperor ultimately pitied You Liang for loving him and restored his rank, removed Yi from office, and Bowen, Shan, and Tianmin all suffered punishment. Throughout the Jiajing reign many suffered heavy punishment for slandering ritual fasting—beginning from You Liang's denunciation.
58
儀去蘇州，士民走闕下乞留，帝不許。 既而薦起知撫州。 蘇州士民復走闕下乞還儀，至再，不報。 歸訴於巡撫侯位。 位以聞，帝乃許之。 至則嘆曰：「蘇賦當天下什二，而田額淆無可考，何以定賦？ 」乃履畝丈之，使縣各為籍。 以八事定田賦，以三條核稅課，徭役、雜辦維均。 治為知府第一，進浙江副使，飭蘇、松、常、鎮兵備。 時巡撫歐陽鐸均田賦，儀佐之，以治蘇者推行於旁郡。 坐與操江王學夔討賊敗績，停俸戴罪。 未幾，殪賊江中，進秩一等，遷山西右參政，分守冀、寧。 寇抵清源城，儀洞開城門，寇疑引去。 按行所部，築城郭，積糗糧，榆次、平定間遂皆有城。
When Yi left Suzhou, scholars and commoners went to the palace gate begging that he be kept; the emperor did not permit it. Soon after he was recommended and appointed prefect of Fuzhou. Suzhou scholars and commoners again went to the palace gate begging that Yi be returned—a second time—with no reply. Returning, they appealed to Governor Hou Wei. Wei reported it and the emperor then permitted it. On arrival he sighed: "Suzhou's tax assessment equals one-tenth of the realm, yet land registers are confused beyond reckoning—how can tax be fixed? " He then measured fields by pacing and had each county make its own register. With eight measures he fixed land tax, with three articles he verified tax levies, and corvée and miscellaneous charges were all equalized. His governance ranked first among prefects; he was promoted to vice commissioner of Zhejiang and put in charge of military affairs in Su, Song, Chang, and Zhen. At the time Governor Ouyang Duo equalized land tax; Yi assisted him, extending Suzhou methods to neighboring prefectures. He was punished with suspended salary pending redemption for failing with Wang Xuekui of the River Command in suppressing bandits. Before long the bandits were destroyed on the river; he was advanced one rank and transferred to Right Vice Commissioner of Shanxi, with defense responsibility for Ji and Ning. When raiders reached Qingyuan city Yi opened the city gate wide; the raiders suspected a trap and withdrew. Touring his jurisdiction he built city walls and stockpiled grain; between Yuci and Pingding there were then walls everywhere.
59
二十一年擢右僉都御史，巡撫宣府。 冠入龍門，總兵官郤永等敗之。 儀進右副都御史。 尋以築邊垣，賚銀幣。 寇自萬全右衛入，遊騎犯完、唐。 奪俸二級。 考察拾遺，貶一官。 已，勘上失事罪，貶秩如初。 久之，除肅州兵備副使，協巡撫楊博徙哈密遺種於境外。 稍遷右參政，復拜右僉都御史，巡撫甘肅。 未行，俺答犯京師，詔儀馳鎮通州。 仇鸞部卒掠民貲，捕笞之，枷市門外。 鸞訴於帝，逮訊斥為民，卒。 隆慶初，子緘訟冤，復官賜恤。
In the twenty-first year he was promoted to Right Vice Censor-in-Chief and regional governor of Xuanfu. Raiders entered Longmen; Regional Commander Chi Yong and others defeated them. Yi was advanced to Vice Censor-in-Chief. Soon after, for building the frontier wall, he was granted silver and silks. Raiders entered from Wanquan Right Guard and roaming horsemen violated Wan and Tang. Two ranks of salary were stripped. In the performance review for overlooked matters he was demoted one rank. Soon after, investigating the crime of the defeat above, his demotion was restored as before. After a long time he was appointed vice commissioner for military affairs at Suzhou and, assisting Governor Yang Bo, moved Hami remnant populations beyond the border. He was gradually transferred to vice commissioner and again appointed Right Vice Censor-in-Chief and regional governor of Gansu. Before he departed, Altan invaded the capital region and an edict ordered Yi to hurry to secure Tongzhou. Qiu Luan's camp soldiers plundered commoners' goods; he captured and caned them and put them in the cangue at the market gate. Luan complained to the emperor; Yi was arrested, interrogated, expelled as a commoner, and died. Early in the Longqing reign his son Jian petitioned about the injustice; office was restored and condolence gifts granted.
60
緘，官按察使，分巡遼陽，以知兵名。
Jian served as surveillance commissioner touring Liaodong and was known for military knowledge.
61
王學夔，安福人。 正德時，以吏部主事諫南巡，跪闕下，受杖。 嘉靖初，奏請裁戚畹，又申救言官。 歷考功、文選郎中，廉謹為時所稱。 嘗撫治鄖陽。 有偽稱皇子者，諸司議用兵。 學夔曰：「妄豎子耳。 」密捕致之辟。 累遷南京吏、禮、兵三部尚書。 隆慶、萬歷間，存問者再。 年九十四卒。 贈太子少保。
Wang Xuekui was from Anfu. During the Zhengde reign, as a director in the Ministry of Personnel he remonstrated against the southern tour, knelt at the palace gate, and was beaten. Early in the Jiajing reign he memorialized requesting reduction of imperial in-laws and again interceded for remonstrance officials. He served as director of the Performance Evaluation and Selection bureaus and was praised by contemporaries for integrity and prudence. He once governed and pacified Yunyang. Someone falsely claimed to be an imperial prince; various offices debated using troops. Xuekui said: "A deluded youth only. " He secretly captured him and executed him. He rose to Minister of Personnel, Rites, and War at Nanjing. During the Longqing and Wanli reigns he was twice sent greetings of inquiry. He died at the age of ninety-four. He was posthumously made Junior Mentor of the Heir Apparent.
62
曾鈞，字廷和，進賢人。 嘉靖十一年進士。 授行人。 擢南京禮科給事中。 時四方銀場得不償費，且為盜窟，鈞奏罷之。
Ceng Jun, styled Tinghe, was from Jinxian. He passed the jinshi examination in the eleventh year of Jiajing. He was appointed a courier in the Ministry of Rites. He was promoted to supervising secretary in the Ministry of Rites section at Nanjing. At the time silver mines throughout the realm cost more than they yielded and also became bandit lairs; Jun memorialized to abolish them.
63
鈞剛廉疾俗。 首劾罷參贊尚書劉龍。 已，劾翊國公郭勛、禮部尚書嚴嵩。 未幾劾工部侍郎蔣淦、延綏巡撫趙錦。 最後劾罷操江都御史柴經。 直聲震一時。
Jun was firm, incorrupt, and hated vulgar custom. First he impeached and removed Assistant Minister Liu Long. Soon after he impeached State-supporting Duke Guo Xun and Minister of Rites Yan Song. Before long he impeached Vice Minister of Works Jiang Gan and Yan-sui Governor Zhao Jin. Finally he impeached and removed River Command Censor-in-Chief Chai Jing. His upright voice shook the age.
64
出為雲南副使。 兩司詣黔國公率廷謁，鈞始正其禮，且厘還所侵麗江民地。 遷四川參政。 黔寇亂，撫定之。 屢遷河南左布政使。 三十一年以右副都御史總理河道。 徐、邳等十七州縣連被水患，帝憂之，趣上方略。 鈞請浚劉伶臺至赤晏廟八十里，築草灣老黃河口，增高家堰長堤，繕新莊等舊閘。 閱數月，工成。 進工部右侍郎。 治河四年，入為南京刑部右侍郎。 久之，乞歸。 家居十余年卒。 贈刑部尚書，謚恭肅。
He was appointed vice commissioner of Yunnan. When visiting the Duke of Qian State the two commissioners all bowed in court audience; Jun first corrected the ritual and also restored to Lijiang commoners the land the duke had seized. He was transferred to vice commissioner of Sichuan. Qian bandits rebelled; he pacified and settled them. He rose to Left Administration Commissioner of Henan. In the thirty-first year he was made Right Vice Censor-in-Chief and put in overall charge of the waterways. Seventeen prefectures and counties such as Xu and Pi suffered successive flood disasters; the emperor was worried and urgently requested strategic plans. Jun requested dredging from Liulingtai to Chiyan Temple for eighty li, building the Caowan Old Yellow River mouth, raising the long dike at Gaojia Weir, and repairing old sluices such as Xinzhuang. After several months the work was completed. He was promoted to Right Vice Minister of Works. For four years he managed the river and entered court as Right Vice Minister of Justice at Nanjing. After a long time he requested to return home. He lived at home for more than ten years and died. He was posthumously made Minister of Justice and granted the posthumous title Gongsu.
65
贊曰：鄭嶽等居官，歷著風操。 箴主闕，抑近幸，本末皆有可觀。 斤斤奉職，所至以治辦聞，殆列卿之良歟！ 唐胄論安南，切於事理。 歐陽鐸之均田賦，惠愛在民； 令久於其任，幾與周忱比矣。
The eulogy says: Zheng Yue and the others in office over the years displayed moral conduct. They remonstrated about the ruler's faults and restrained close favorites; in fundamentals and particulars all had merit. Conscientiously fulfilling duty, wherever they went they were known for effective governance—perhaps they were the good among vice ministers! Tang Zhou's discussion of Annam was close to reason and fact. Ouyang Duo's equalization of land tax brought kindness and care to the people; had he long remained in office he would nearly have been comparable to Zhou Chen.
