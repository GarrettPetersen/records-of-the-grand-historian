1
《易》曰：“天垂象，聖人則之。 ”宦者四星，在皇位之侧，故《周礼》置官，亦备其数。 阍者守中門之禁，寺人掌女宫之戒。 又云“王之正内者五人”。 《月令》：“仲冬，命阉尹审門闾，谨房室。 ”《詩》之《小雅》，亦有《巷伯》刺谗之篇。 然宦人之在王朝者，其來旧矣。 将以其体非全气，情志专良，通关中人，易以役养乎？ 然而后世因之，才任稍广，其能者，則勃貂、管苏有功于楚、晋，景监、缪贤著庸于秦、趙。 及其敝也，則竖刁乱齐，伊戾祸宋。
The Yijing says: “Heaven displays its signs, and the sage reads them as his pattern.” The “Four Eunuch Stars” sit beside the imperial seat, which is why the Zhou ritual canon created matching posts. Gatekeepers policed the middle doors; “palace men” watched over the inner palace of consorts. The same text speaks of five officers for the sovereign’s private apartments. The “Monthly Commands” for deep winter tell the chief eunuch to check every portal and bolt the inner rooms. The Shijing’s Lesser Ya includes “The Lane Steward,” a poem that lashes tale-bearing. Yet eunuchs at court are nothing new under Heaven. Was it because they were thought bodiless, single-minded, able to move inside the seraglio, and cheap to keep? Later dynasties leaned on them harder: able men like Bo Diao and Guan Su aided Chu and Jin; Jing Jian and Miao Xian served Qin and Zhao well. In decline came villains like Shu Diao who wrecked Qi, or Yi Li who poisoned Song.
2
汉兴，仍袭秦制，置中常侍官。 然亦引用士人，以参其选，皆银珰左貂，给事殿省。 及高后称制，乃以張卿为大谒者，出入卧内，受宣詔命。 文帝時，有趙谈、北宫伯子，颇见亲幸。 至于孝武，亦爱李延年。 帝数宴后庭，或潜游离馆，故请奏机事，多以宦人主之。 至元帝之世，史游为黄門令，勤心纳忠，有所补益。 其后弘恭、石显以佞险自进，卒有萧、周之祸，损秽帝德焉。
The Han kept Qin’s machinery and kept “regular attendants” on the books. At first educated men still filled many of those posts, silver seals at the belt, sable on the left, serving the inner ministries. Under the Gao empress, Zhang Qing became chief usher, slipping into the private chambers to carry out her orders. Emperor Wen favored Zhao Tan and Beigong Bozi. Emperor Wu loved Li Yannian too. Because the sovereign banqueted in the harem and stole away to country villas, secret state papers fell mostly to eunuch hands. Under Yuan, Shi You as yellow-gate prefect strove to give honest advice that actually helped. Then Hong Gong and Shi Xian climbed by slander until Xiao Wangzhi and Zhou Kan fell—an ugly stain on the throne’s honor.
3
中兴之初，宦官悉用阉人，不复杂调他士。 至永平中，始置员数，中常侍四人，小黄門十人。 和帝即祚幼弱，而窦宪兄弟专总权威，内外臣僚，莫由亲接，所与居者，唯庵宦而已。 故鄭众得专谋禁中，终除大憝，遂享分土之封，超登宫卿之位。 于是中官始盛焉。
After Guangwu restored the Han, every inner attendant was a castrate; outsiders were no longer rotated in. Yongping fixed the roster: four regular attendants, ten junior yellow gates. He came to the throne as a boy while the Dou brothers held every lever; courtiers could not reach him—only the eunuchs shared his rooms. So Zheng Zhong could scheme inside the palace, destroy the Dou clan, win a marquisate, and vault into the highest eunuch offices. That was the moment inner-court clerks became a power.
4
自明帝以后，迄乎延平，委用渐大，而其员稍增，中常侍至有十人，小黄門二十人，改以金珰右貂，兼领卿署之职。 邓后以女主临政，而万机殷远，朝臣國议，无由参断帷幄，称制下令，不出房闱之间，不得不委用刑人，寄之國命。 手握王爵，口含天宪，非复掖廷永巷之职，闺牖房闼之任也。 其后孙程定立顺之功，曹腾参建桓之策，续以五侯合谋，梁冀受钺，迹因公正，恩固主心，故中外服从，上下屏气。 或称伊、霍之勋，无谢于往载； 或谓良、平之画，复兴于当今。 虽時有忠公，而竟见排斥。 举动回山海，呼吸变霜露。 阿旨曲求，則光宠三族； 直情忤意，則参夷五宗。 汉之纲纪大乱矣。
From Ming to Yanping their role swelled: ten regular attendants, twenty junior yellow gates, gold and right-hand sable, and titles that straddled the outer ministries. Empress Deng ruled from behind the screen; ministers could not argue policy in her cabinet; every edict stayed inside the harem, so she handed the realm’s life to castrates. They clutched noble rank and spoke with the force of law—far beyond the old job of guarding harem corridors. Sun Cheng made Shun emperor; Cao Teng helped pick Huan; the Five Marquises and Liang Ji’s fall looked like public justice, so the court dared not breathe. Some were hailed as Yi Yin or Huo Guang reborn; others as clever as Zhang Liang and Chen Ping in our own day. Honest men appeared now and then, but they were always shoved aside. Their whims moved mountains; their sighs turned spring to killing frost. Flatter three generations rewarded; speak truth and watch three clans wiped out to the fifth mourning grade. Han governance unraveled completely.
5
若夫高冠长剑，纡硃怀金者，布满宫闱； 苴茅分虎，南面臣人者，盖以十数。 府署第馆，棋列于都鄙； 子弟支附，过半于州國。 南金、和宝、冰纨、雾縠之积，盈仞珍藏； 嫱媛、侍兒、歌单、舞女之玩，充备绮室。 狗馬饰雕文，土木被缇绣。 皆剥割萌黎，竞恣奢欲。 构害明贤，专树党类。 其有更相援引，希附权强者，皆腐身熏子，以自衒达。 同敝相济，故其徒有繁，败國蠹败之事，不可单書。 所以海内嗟毒，志士穷栖，寇剧缘间，摇乱区夏。 虽忠良怀愤，時或奋发，而言出祸从，旋见孥戮。 因复大考钩党，转相诬染。 凡称善士，莫不离被灾毒。 窦武、何进，位崇戚近，乘九服之嚣怨，协群英之势力，而以疑留不断，至于殄败。 斯亦运之极乎！ 虽袁绍龚行，芟夷无余，然以暴易乱，亦何云及！ 自曹腾说梁冀，竟立昏弱。 魏武因之，遂迁龟鼎。 所谓“君以此始，必以此终”，信乎其然矣！
High hats and long blades, vermillion sashes, gold seals—the inner palace swarmed with them; dozens held feudal commissions and ruled like princes. Their townhouses checkerboarded the capital; their clients overran half the empire’s provinces. Vaults groaned with southern gold, He pearls, gauze like mist; every silk room overflowed with singers and dancers. Even the kennels glittered with inlay; garden rocks wore brocade. All of it was skinned from peasants who fed their appetites. They framed the worthy and packed the factions. Men gelded themselves and smeared their sons’ faces to ride a patron’s coattails. Villainy fed villainy until their host was legion—no single chronicle can list the rot that ate the realm. The empire choked on them, gentlemen hid, rebels rose, and the central plains convulsed. Loyal men raged, spoke out, and saw their kin cut down overnight. Then the partisan inquisitions spread, one name dragging another. Anyone called “good” was ruined. Dou Wu and He Jin held supreme rank, harnessed popular fury and elite swords—and still dithered themselves to death. Had fate’s wheel ground to its limit? Yuan Shao slaughtered the eunuchs to the last man, yet traded one tyranny for another—what was gained? When Cao Teng talked Liang Ji into enthroning a puppet, the rot was sealed. Cao Cao’s house used that opening to shift the mandate’s bronze. “By this you rise, by this you fall”—the old proverb was painfully right.
6
鄭众字季产，南陽犨人也。 为人谨敏有心几。 永平中，初给事太子家。 肃宗即位，拜小黄門，迁中常侍。 和帝初，加位钩盾令。
Zheng Zhong, courtesy name Jichan, came from Hua in Nanyang commandery. He was careful, sharp, and quick on the uptake. During Yongping he entered service in the crown prince’s residence. Emperor Zhang named him junior yellow gate, then regular attendant. When He succeeded, Zhong added the title chief of the hook-shield office.
7
時窦太后秉政，后兄大将军宪等并窃威权，朝臣上下莫不附之，而众独一心王室，不事豪党，帝亲信焉。 及宪兄弟图作不轨，众遂首谋诛之，以功迁大长秋。 策勋班赏，每辞多受少。 由是常与议事。 中官用权，自众始焉。
The Dou empress ruled; her brother Xian monopolized power; the whole court clung to the Dou—only Zheng Zhong stayed loyal to the boy emperor, shunned the clique, and won his private trust. When the Dou brothers turned traitor, Zhong led the plot to kill them and rose to grand prolonger of autumn. At every distribution of honors he asked for less, not more. Hence he was constantly called into policy talks. Eunuch power as the Han knew it begins with him.
8
十四年，帝念众功美，封为鄛乡侯，食邑千五百户。 永初元年，和熹皇后益封三百户。
In year fourteen the court enfeoffed him as marquis of Zhaoxiang at fifteen hundred households for past service. Yongchu one saw Empress Dowager Hexi add three hundred households to his estate.
9
元初元年卒，养子闳嗣。 闳卒，子安嗣。 后國绝。 桓帝延熹二年，绍封众曾孙石雠为关内侯。
He died in Yuanchu one; his adopted son Hong succeeded. Hong died; his son An took the title. Later the marquisate lapsed. Emperor Huan’s Yanxi two revived the line, enfeoffing Zheng’s descendant Shi Chou as a marquis within the passes.
10
伦有才學，尽心敦慎，数犯严颜，匡弼得失。 每至休沐，辄闭門绝宾，暴体田野。 后加位尚方令。 永元九年，监作秘剑及诸器械，莫不精工坚密，为后世法。
Cai Lun was learned, careful, often defied the emperor’s glare, and set policy aright. On his days off he barred his door and worked his own fields like a farmer. He rose to prefect of the palace workshop. Yongyuan nine put him in charge of secret blades and ordnance—work so fine it set the standard for ages after.
11
元初元年，邓太后以伦久宿卫，封为龍亭侯，邑三百户。 后为长乐太仆。 四年，帝以經傳之文多不正定，乃选通儒谒者劉珍及博士良史诣东观，各雠校汉家法，令伦监典其事。
Empress Deng, noting his long palace service, made him marquis of Longting at three hundred households in Yuanchu one. He later became grand coachman of Changle Palace. In year four the emperor sent Liu Zhen and a team of scholars to the Eastern Lodge to collate the canon; Lun oversaw the project.
12
伦初受窦后讽旨，诬陷安帝祖母宋贵人。 及太后崩，安帝始亲万机，敕使自致廷尉。 伦耻受辱，乃沐浴整衣冠，饮药而死。 國除。
He had once obeyed Empress Dou’s whispered order to frame Emperor An’s grandmother, the Song Lady. When Dou died and An took the reins, the emperor ordered Lun to report to the commandant of justice. Rather than face trial he bathed, dressed, and drank poison. His fief was struck from the rolls.
13
孙程字稚卿，涿郡新城人也。 安帝時，为中黄門，给事长乐宫。
Sun Cheng, courtesy name Zhiqing, hailed from Xincheng in Zhuo commandery. Under Emperor An he was a palace attendant attached to Changle.
14
時邓太后临朝，帝不亲政事。 小黄門李閏与帝乳母王聖常共谮太后兄执金吾悝等，言欲废帝，立平原王翼，帝每忿惧。 及太后崩，遂诛邓氏而废平原王，封閏雍乡侯； 又小黄門江京以谗谄进，初迎帝于邸，以功封都乡侯，食邑各三百户。 閏、京并迁中常侍，江京兼大长秋，与中常侍樊丰、黄門令劉安、钩盾令陳达及王聖、聖女伯荣扇动内外，竞为侈虐。 又帝舅大将军耿宝、皇后兄大鸿胪阎显更相阿党，遂枉殺太尉杨震，废皇太子为济陰王。
Empress Deng held court while the emperor stayed out of state affairs. Li Run and the wet nurse Wang Sheng whispered that Deng Kui meant to depose An and put Prince Yi of Pingyuan on the throne; the boy emperor lived in fear. When Deng died they slaughtered her kin, deposed Prince Yi, and enfeoffed Li Run; Jiang Jing, who had flattered his way up and met the emperor at the princely lodge, also won a marquisate at three hundred households. Run and Jing became regular attendants; Jing doubled as grand prolonger of autumn; with Fan Feng, Liu An, Chen Da, Wang Sheng, and Borong they roiled court and capital in luxury and terror. The emperor’s uncle Geng Bao and the Yan empress’s brother Yan Xian shielded each other, murdered Yang Zhen, and stripped the crown prince to prince of Jiyin.
15
明年帝崩，立北乡侯为天子。 显等遂专朝争权，乃讽有司奏诛樊丰，废耿宝、王聖，及党与皆见死徙。
The next year An died and the court enthroned the Marquis of Beixiang. Yan Xian’s faction seized the government, engineered Fan Feng’s death, cashiered Geng Bao and Wang Sheng, and killed or exiled their allies.
16
阎显時在禁中，忧迫不知所为，小黄門樊登劝显发兵，以太后詔召越骑校尉冯詩、虎贲中郎将阎崇，屯朔平門，以御程等。 诱詩入省，太后使授之印，曰：“能得济陰王者封万户侯，得李閏者五千户侯。 ”显以詩所将众少，使与登迎吏士于左掖門外。 詩因格殺登，归营屯守。 显弟卫尉景遽从省中还外府，收兵至盛德門。 程傳召诸尚書使收景。 尚書郭镇時卧病，聞之，即率直宿羽林出南止車門，逢景从吏士，拔白刃，呼白：“无干兵。 ”镇即下車，持节詔之。 景曰：“何等詔？ ”因聽镇，不中。 镇引剑击景墯車，左右以戟叉其匈，遂禽之，送廷尉狱，即夜死。 旦日，令侍御史收显等送狱，于是遂定。 下詔曰：
Yan Xian panicked in the palace; Fan Deng told him to call out troops; they used the empress’s name to summon Feng Shi and Yan Chong to Shuoping Gate to block Sun Cheng’s party. They tricked Feng Shi inside and gave him a seal: “Ten thousand households for the prince of Jiyin, five thousand for Li Run.” Thinking Feng Shi’s men too few, Yan sent him with Fan Deng to rally troops outside the left palace gate. Feng Shi killed Fan Deng on the spot and sealed his camp. Yan Jing raced from the palace, raised men, and reached Shengde Gate. Sun Cheng sent word to the secretariat to seize Yan Jing. Guo Zhen, sick in bed, leapt up, led the night guards out the south gate, barred Yan Jing’s path, bared steel, and roared, “Drop your weapons!” Guo Zhen dismounted and read the imperial staff-order to him. Yan Jing demanded, “What edict do you mean?” He feigned to heed Guo Zhen yet refused to submit. Guo Zhen cut Yan Jing from his chariot, guards pinned him with crossed halberds, he was jailed—and dead before dawn. At daybreak edicts went out to seize Yan Xian’s party, and the coup was sealed. The throne then promulgated an edict:
17
夫表功录善，古今之通义也。 故中常侍长乐太仆江京、黄門令劉安、钩盾令陳达与故車骑将军阎显兄弟谋议恶逆，倾乱天下。 中黄門孙程、王康、长乐太官丞王國、、中黄門黄龍、彭恺、孟叔、李建、王成、張贤、史汎、馬國、王道、李元、杨佗、陳予、趙封、李刚、魏猛、苗光等，怀忠愤发，戮力协谋，遂埽灭元恶，以定王室。 《詩》不云乎：“无言不雠，元德不报。 ”程为谋首，康、國协同。 其封程为浮陽侯，食邑五户； 康为华容侯，國为郦侯，各九千户； 黄龍为湘南侯，五千户； 彭恺为西平昌侯，孟叔为中庐侯，李建为复陽侯，各四千二百户； 王成为广宗侯，張贤为祝阿侯，史汎为临沮侯，馬國为文平侯，王道为范县侯，李元为褒信侯，杨佗为山都侯，陳予为下隽侯，趙封为析县侯，李刚为枝江侯，各四千户； 魏猛为夷陵侯，二千户； 苗光为东阿侯，千户。
Honoring loyal service is what every age owes its servants. Jiang Jing, Liu An, and Chen Da had conspired with the late Yan Xian to wreck the dynasty. Sun Cheng led Wang Kang, Wang Guo, Huang Long, Peng Kai, and the rest of the eunuch band in a palace strike that destroyed the Yan faction and restored the Liu house. As the Odes remind us, no loyal word lacks its answer, and great virtue earns its recompense. Sun Cheng masterminded the coup; Wang Kang and Wang Guo were his chief partners. Sun Cheng became marquis of Fuyang with a token fief of five registered households. Wang Kang took Huarong at nine thousand households, Wang Guo took Li at the same rank. Huang Long became marquis of Xiangnan at five thousand households. Peng Kai, Meng Shu, and Li Jian received marquisates at four thousand two hundred households apiece. Ten more allies—from Wang Cheng through Li Gang—were enfeoffed at four thousand households each. Wei Miao received Yiling at two thousand households. Miao Guang alone held Dong’e at one thousand households.
18
是为十九侯。 加赐車、馬、金、银、钱、帛，各有差。 李閏以先不豫谋，故不封。 遂擢拜程骑都尉。
History remembers them as the nineteen eunuch marquises. The court piled on chariots, bullion, coin, and brocade in graded gifts. Li Run won no title, since he had not joined the first conspiracy. Sun Cheng was raised to chief commandant of cavalry.
19
永建元年，程与張贤、孟叔、馬國等为司隶校尉虞诩讼罪，怀表上殿，呵叱左右。 帝怒，遂免程官，因悉遣十九侯就國，后徙封程为宜城侯。 程既到國，怨恨恚怼，封还印绶、符策，亡归京师，往來山中。 詔書追求，复故爵士，赐車馬衣物，遣还國。
In Yongjian one Sun Cheng marched his allies to the throne hall with a petition defending Yu Xu and browbeat the guards. The emperor stripped Sun Cheng, banished the whole cohort to their estates, and later moved his own fief to Yicheng. At his domain Sun Cheng sulked, handed back his seals, fled to Luoyang, and hid in the mountains. Imperial messengers hunted him down, restored his title, showered gifts on him, and escorted him back to his fief.
20
三年，帝念程等功勋，悉征还京师。 程与王道、李元皆拜骑都尉，余悉奉朝请。 陽嘉元年，程病甚，即拜奉車都尉，位特进。 及卒，使五官中郎将追赠車骑将军印绶，赐谥刚侯。 侍御史持节监护丧事，乘舆幸北部尉傳，瞻望車骑。
Three years later the court recalled every one of the nineteen for their past service. Sun Cheng, Wang Dao, and Li Yuan again became chiefs of cavalry; the others held sinecure court rank. Yangjia one, as Sun Cheng lay dying, the emperor named him chief commandant of the carriage escort with extraordinary precedence. At his death a palace envoy brought the chariot-and-horse general’s seal and the posthumous epithet “Gang,” the unbending marquis. An investigating censor oversaw the obsequies while the emperor drove to the northern district post house to watch the hearse depart.
21
程临终，遗言上書，以國傳弟美。 帝许之，而分程半，封程养子寿为浮陽侯。 后詔書录微功，封兴渠为高望亭侯。 四年，詔宦官养子悉聽得为后，袭封爵，定著乎令。
Dying, Sun Cheng asked to deed his whole estate to his brother Sun Mei. The throne agreed, yet halved the fief and gave the other half to his adopted son Sun Shou as marquis of Fuyang. A later edict remembered lesser helpers and made Xing Qu marquis of Gaowangting. Year four codified what had been practice: eunuch heirs could inherit marquisates by statute.
22
王康、王國、彭恺、王成、趙封、魏猛六人皆早卒。 黄龍、杨佗、孟叔、李建、張贤、史汎、王道、李元，李刚九人与阿母山陽君宋娥更相货赂，求高官增邑，又诬罔中常侍曹腾、孟贲等。 永和二年，发觉，并遣就國，减祖四分之一。 宋娥夺爵归田舍。 唯馬國、陳予、苗光保全封邑。
Six of the band—Wang Kang, Wang Guo, Peng Kai, Wang Cheng, Zhao Feng, and Wei Miao—died young. Nine survivors joined the emperor’s nurse Song E in bribing for promotions and slandering Cao Teng and Meng Ben. Yonghe two exposed the ring; each man was sent home and lost a quarter of his rent roll. Song E lost her title and went back to her fields. Only Ma Guo, Chen Yu, and Miao Guang kept their estates whole.
23
初，帝见废，监太子家小黄門籍建、傅高梵、长秋长趙熹、丞良贺、药长夏珍皆以无过获罪，建等坐徙朔方。 及帝即位，并擢为中常侍。 梵坐臧罪，减死一等。 建后封东乡侯，三百户。
When the prince was cashiered, Ji Jian, Gao Fan, Zhao Xi, Liang He, and Xia Zhen—blameless—were packed off to Shuofang. Emperor Shun raised every one of them to regular attendant. Gao Fan alone took a corruption conviction and escaped death by one degree. Ji Jian later won a minor marquisate at Dong township, three hundred households.
24

Cao Teng never nursed a grudge and still praised Chong Hao as a capable officer—a gesture men admired.

Early in Huan’s reign the first three were already regular attendants; Zuo Guan and Tang Heng still held junior yellow-gate posts.

Liang Ji’s two sisters wed Shun and Huan; he inherited his father’s baton as grand general and terrorized the empire for two reigns. He murdered Li Gu and Du Qiao; his empress sister poisoned rivals; the whole court went mute. Emperor Huan lived in terror, nursing hatred yet afraid to whisper a plot. When the empress died in Yanxi two, Huan seized a moment in the privy to ask Tang Heng who at court hated the Liang clan. Tang Heng named Shan Chao and Zuo Guan, once humiliated when Liang Buyi of Henan jailed their brothers until they kowtowed at his gate. Xu Huang and Ju Yuan nursed the same grudge yet still held their tongues. He then closeted Shan Chao and Zuo Guan and said, “The Liang brothers own the government; every minister dances to their tune.” “I mean to kill them—will you stand with me?” They answered, “They are traitors who should have died long ago.” “We are only slaves—we must hear your own resolve.” The emperor said, “Then plot in secret.” “Easy enough,” they said, “unless you waver halfway as you always have.” “Treason is treason,” snapped the emperor. “There is nothing left to doubt.” He called in the other three, swore blood on Shan Chao’s arm, and Shan Chao seized Liang Ji and exterminated his entire network. Zuo Guan and Tang Heng rose to regular attendant. Shan Chao took Xinfeng at twenty thousand households; Xu Huang and Ju Yuan each took fifteen thousand households and fifteen million cash; Zuo Guan and Tang Heng each took thirteen thousand households and thirteen million cash. Because all five were girdled the same day, the world called them the Five Marquises. Eight lesser attendants such as Liu Pu and Zhao Zhong also won village marquisates. After that the eunuchs held the sword of state and the Han slid faster toward ruin.

They pressed common girls into concubines decked like consorts, while retainers rolled in ox carts flanked by cavalry. They adopted stray cousins, bought heirs, and registered slaves as sons so marquisates could pass down the line. Brothers and in-laws held every lucrative post and squeezed farmers like bandits.

Xiapi lay under Donghai commandery; Chancellor Huang Fu, hearing charges against Xu Xuan, seized every kinsman and interrogated them without mercy. His staff pleaded with him to stop. Huang Fu replied, “Xu Xuan is a public enemy; kill him now and die tomorrow—I can rest in peace.” He executed Xu Xuan in the marketplace and left the corpse as a warning until the county shook. Xu Huang howled to the throne; the emperor flung Huang Fu into convict labor with shaved head and cangue. Their kin and retainers terrorized every circuit until peasants turned rebel. Seven years on Tang Heng died with the same posthumous general’s honors as Shan Chao. Xu Huang’s funeral drew imperial cash and a gift of tomb land.

Han Yan also cited Ju Gong, Ju Yuan’s brother in Pei, for graft and called him to the capital. Ju Yuan surrendered his great marquis’s seal, took a lesser fief, and died in retirement. The Five Marquises’ lines were cut to petty marquisates at three million a year; cadet branches lost everything. Liu Pu’s clique was downgraded to marquises inside the passes.

Endless wars emptied the vault; even official pay and noble rents were pledged in advance. Hou Lan bought a marquis’s patent with five thousand bolts of silk. He claimed a share in Liang Ji’s execution and rose to marquis of Gao district.

Jibei Chancellor Teng Yan rounded them up, executed dozens, and left the bodies in the highway. Hou Lan and Duan Gui complained; the emperor judged Teng Yan’s slaughter excessive and cashiered him. Teng Yan of Beihai, style Boxing, later governed the capital well and was remembered as a gentleman.

Grand Commandant Yang Bing impeached Hou Shen; a prison cart was sent; he died by his own hand en route. Capital Governor Yuan Feng searched Hou Shen’s baggage train—three hundred carts of treasure beyond reckoning. Hou Lan briefly lost his post, then won it back.

He threw up sixteen walled compounds with towers and pools that aped the imperial palace. He even built his own future tomb, shattering living quarters and desecrating graves to raise a hundred-foot gallery. He abducted women and children; Zhang Jian demanded Hou Lan’s death. Hou Lan’s agents hijacked the courier; the memorial never arrived. Zhang Jian razed the mansions, seized the goods, and published every crime. He added that Hou Lan’s mother had brokered influence across the provinces while alive. That memorial too was blocked. Hou Lan struck back with a partisan warrant, killing Li Ying, Du Mi, and their circle. He succeeded Cao Jie as grand coachman of Changle Palace.

Xiping one an impeachment stripped his seals; Hou Lan committed suicide. His political clients fell with him.

Cao Jie became Changle guard commandant and marquis of Yuyang with three thousand extra households. Wang Fu rose to regular attendant and kept the yellow-gate office; Zhu Yu took Du district at fifteen hundred households; Gong Pu, Zhang Liang, and five others took three hundred households each; eleven more became marquises inside the passes at two thousand hu a year.

The Yijing warns: tread frost and know ice is coming. The commentary means the evil was long in brewing. Trace this chapter’s road to ruin—it was never one day’s work.

贊曰：“任失无小，过用則违。 况乃巷职，远参天机。 舞文巧态，作惠作威。 凶家害國，夫岂异归！
The hymn says: wrong men in office, however petty, break the Way. How much worse when alley-born attendants touch the throne’s pivot! They twist edicts, play at grace and terror. They devour families and devour the realm—and meet the same end!
