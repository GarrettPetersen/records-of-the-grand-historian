1
张升字彦真，陈留尉氏人，富平侯放之孙也。 升少好学，多关览，而任情不羁。 其意相合者，则倾身交结，不问穷贱； 如乖真志好者，虽王公大人，终不屈从。 常叹曰：“死生有命，富贵在天。 其有知我，虽胡越可亲； 苟不相识，从物何益？ ”
Zhang Sheng, whose courtesy name was Yanzhen, came from Weishi in Chenliu commandery; he was a grandson of Marquis Fuping Zhang Fang. As a youth he loved books and ranged through many fields, but he followed his moods and refused to be tamed. When he found a kindred spirit he gave himself wholly to the bond, never asking whether the man was poor or lowborn. If someone's aims ran counter to his own, not even princes of the blood could make him bend. He used to sigh: "Life and death are fated; wealth and rank rest with Heaven. Men who understand me may be as distant as the Hu and Yue tribes, yet they are kin. If they do not know me, what good is a heap of possessions?" "
2
仕郡为纲纪，以能出守外黄令。 吏有受赇者，即论杀之。 或讥：“升守领一时，何足趋明威戮乎？ ”对曰：“昔仲尼暂相，诛齐之侏儒，手足异门而出，故能威震强国，反其侵地。 君子仕不为己，职思其忧，岂以久近而异其度哉？ ”遇党锢去官，后竟见诛，年四十九。
He served his commandery as chief clerk, then for his competence was posted magistrate of Waihuang. Any clerk caught taking bribes he tried at once and put to death. Critics said: "Your tenure is a single turn of the seasons—why rush to blood and terror?" He answered: "Confucius held office only briefly, yet he executed Qi's dancing dwarfs and had their limbs dragged out separate gates—enough to overawe a great power and win back stolen land. A gentleman serves the public, not his purse; the charge is to shoulder its cares—why should the length of a posting change the rule?" The faction proscription stripped him of office; later he was put to death at forty-nine.
3
著贼、诔、颂、碑、书，凡六十篇。
His writings—rhapsodies, laments, hymns, inscriptions, and letters—number sixty pieces.
4
赵壹字元叔，汉阳西县人也。 体貌魁梧，身长九尺，美须豪眉，望之甚伟。 而恃才倨傲，为乡党所摈，乃作《解摈》。 后屡抵罪，几至死，友人救，得免。 壹乃贻书谢恩曰：
Zhao Yi, courtesy name Yuanshu, was a native of Xixian in Hanyang commandery. He was a giant of nine Han feet, with a noble beard and fierce brows—every inch the hero. Talent made him proud, and his neighbors shunned him; he answered with his essay "On Shaking Off Rejection." He later fell afoul of the law more than once and nearly perished; friends ransomed him back to life. He then wrote a letter of thanks that began:
5
昔原大夫赎桑下绝气，传称其仁； 秦越人还虢太子结脉，世著其神。 设曩之二人不遭仁遇神，则结绝之气竭矣。 然而□脯出乎车軨，针石运乎手爪。 今所赖者，非直车軨之糒脯，手爪之针石也。 乃收之于斗极，还之于司命，使干皮复含血，枯骨复被肉，允所谓遭仁遇神，真所宜传而著之。 余畏禁，不敢班班显言，窃为《穷鸟赋》一篇。 其辞曰：
Long ago Zhao Dun redeemed a starving man beneath a mulberry—the chronicles praise his kindness; Bian Que of Qin restarted the crown prince of Guo's halted pulse—the world calls that divine healing. Had those two patients missed such rescuers, the last breath would have left their throats. Yet dried meat was handed from the chariot rail, needles and lancets worked in human hands. What I lean on today is no mere trail ration or bedside lancet. You plucked me from the Pole Star, handed me back to the Director of Fate—withered hide drinks blood again, dry bones take flesh—this is the "benevolence and spirit" the ages should record. I dare not speak openly under the ban; I have instead drafted a "Rhapsody on the Destitute Bird." The text runs:
6
有一穷鸟，戢翼原野。 毕网加上，机□在下，前见苍隼，后见驱者，缴弹张右，羿子彀左，飞丸激矢，交集于我。 思飞不得，欲鸣不可，举头畏触，摇足恐堕。 内独怖急，乍冰乍火。 幸赖大贤，我矜我怜，昔济我南，今振我西。 鸟也虽顽，犹识密思。 内以书心，外用告天。 天乎祚贤，归贤永年，且公且侯，子了孙孙。
There was a starving bird, wings folded on the waste. Nets roofed the sky, snares waited below; a goshawk hung ahead, beaters closed from behind; slings and bolts to the right, the archer Yi's bow to the left—shot and shaft rained on me at once. It could neither fly nor cry; lifting its head risked a blow, shifting a foot risked the drop. Inside it burned and froze by turns with terror. A great man pitied me—once he saved me in the south, now he lifts me in the west. Dull as a bird is, it knows hidden grace. It inscribes gratitude on the heart and cries it to Heaven. May Heaven bless that worthy, grant him long life, raise him duke and marquis, and crown his sons and grandsons.
7
又作《刺世疾邪赋》，以舒其怨愤。 曰：
He also wrote the "Rhapsody on Vilifying the Age and Detesting Villainy" to vent his rage. It says:
8
伊五帝之不同礼，三王亦又不同乐，数极自然变化，非是故相反驳。 德政不能救世混乱，赏罚岂足惩时清浊？ 春秋时祸败之始，战国愈复增其荼毒。 秦、汉无以相逾越，乃更加其怨酷。 宁计生民之命，唯利己而自足。
The Five Thearchs kept different rites, the Three Kings different music—when a cycle runs its course, things change of themselves; they were not contradicting one another for sport. Good government cannot cure an age gone mad; rewards and fines will not scour its clean and foul. The Spring and Autumn era opened the road to ruin; the Warring States poured poison on the wound. Qin and Han strove to outdo one another only in spite and cruelty. They never weighed the people's lives—only fattened themselves and called it enough.
9
于兹迄今，情伪万方。 佞谄日炽，刚克消亡。 舐痔结驷，正色徒行。 妪□禹名势，抚拍豪强。 偃蹇反俗，立致咎殃。 捷慑逐物，日富月昌。 浑然同惑，孰温孰凉？ 邪夫显进，直士幽藏。
From that hour to this, truth and sham wear ten thousand masks. Sycophancy burns hotter each day; the straight and strong are snuffed out. The flatterer rides four-in-hand; the honest man walks. Harridans borrow the great name of Yu to fawn on the powerful. Stand aloof from the crowd and trouble finds you at once. The craven time-server chases every wind—richer each day, fatter each month. The mob shares one stupidity—who can tell warmth from chill? Villains stride the halls; the upright hide in the dark.
10
原斯瘼之攸兴，实执政之匪贤。 女谒掩其视听兮，近习秉其威权。 所好则钻皮出其毛羽，所恶则洗垢求其瘢痕。 虽欲谒诚而尽忠，路绝嶮而靡缘。 九重既不可启，又群吠之狺狺。 安危于于旦夕，肆嗜欲于目前。 奚异涉海之失舵，积薪而待燃？ 荣纳由于闪揄，孰知辩其蚩妍？ 故法禁屈挠干势族，恩泽不逮于单门。 宁饥寒于尧、舜之荒岁兮，不饱暖于当今之丰年。 乘理虽死而非亡，违义虽生而匪存。
Trace this sickness to its root—the men at the helm are not worthies. Palace whispers seal their ears and eyes; favorites clutch the awful seal. What they favor grows feathers from bare skin; what they hate is scrubbed until a scar appears. Though you would pour out every loyal drop, the road is a cliff without a ledge. The nine inner gates stay barred; a kennel of curs snarls beyond. Life or death hangs between sunrise and dusk, yet they gorge their lusts while they can. What is that but sailing the deep without a rudder or stacking kindling for the spark? Rank and salary go to the sidling sneak—who can tell beauty from filth? So the statutes buckle before great houses, while mercy never drips on the poor lone door. Better shiver in a bad year of Yao and Shun than feast in the fat years of today. Cling to the right and death cannot unmake you; betray it and life is a walking corpse.
11
有秦客者，乃为诗曰：“河清不可俟，人命不可延。 顺风激靡草，富贵者称贤。 文籍虽满腹，不如一囊钱。 伊优北堂上，抗脏倚门边。
A western visitor sang: "You cannot wait for the Yellow River to run clear; you cannot bargain with fate for more years. Wind bends the dry grass—so the rich pass for sages. A bellyful of books is worth less than a purse of cash. The yes-man struts the north hall; the straight man leans on the gate.
12
鲁生闻此辞，系而作歌曰：势家多所宜，咳唾自成珠。 被褐怀金玉，兰蕙化为刍。 贤者虽独悟，所困在群愚。 且各守尔分，勿复空驰驱。 哀哉复哀哉，此是命矣夫！
A man of Lu heard the verse and sang back: "Great houses can do no wrong—spittle turns to pearls. The poor may carry jade in the heart, yet orchid grace is chewed like hay. The sage may wake alone, but the mob of fools pens him in. Each of you keep your station—do not waste breath in useless striving. Alas, alas—this is Heaven's sentence!"
13
光和元年，举郡上计，到京师。 是时，司徒袁逢受计，计吏数百人，皆拜伏庭中，莫敢仰视。 壹独长揖而已。 逢望而异之，令左右往让之，曰：“下郡计吏而揖三公，何也？ ”对曰：“昔郦食其长揖汉王，今揖三公，何遽怪哉？ ”逢则敛衽下堂，执其手，延置上坐，因问西方事，大悦，顾谓坐中曰：“此人汉阳赵元叔也。 朝臣莫有过之者，吾请为诸君分坐。 ”坐者皆属观。 既出，往造河南尹羊陟，不得见。 壹以公卿中非陟无足以托名者，乃日往到门，陟自强许通，尚卧未起。 壹径入上堂，遂前临之，曰：“窃伏西州，承高风旧矣。 乃今方遇而忽然，奈何命也！ ”因举声哭，门下惊，皆奔入满侧。 陟知其非常人，乃起，延与语，大奇之。 谓曰：“子出矣。 ”陟明旦大从车骑，奉谒造壹。 时，诸计吏多盛饬车马帷幕，而壹独柴车草屏，露宿其傍，延陟前坐于车下，左右莫不叹愕。 陟遂与言谈，至熏夕，极欢而去，执其手曰：“良璞不剖，必有泣血以相明者矣！ ”陟乃与袁逢共称荐之。 名动京师，士大夫想望其风采。
In Guanghe 1 the commandery sent him up as chief clerk; he reached Luoyang. Steward Yuan Shou was receiving the year-end returns; hundreds of clerks lay face down in the yard, none daring lift his eyes. Zhao Yi alone gave the long crossbow salute and stood straight. Yuan watched from afar, startled, and sent aides to scold him: "Who is a back-country clerk to salute the Three Excellencies?" He replied: "Li Yiji once gave Han Gaozu only a deep bow; I do the same for the Three Dukes—why the fuss?" Yuan straightened his robe, came down the steps, took his hand, seated him above, questioned him on the west, and beamed: "This is Zhao Yuanshu of Hanyang. No man at court tops him—I will share my cushion with you all." Every guest twisted round to stare. Leaving the hall he called on Yang Zhi, intendant of Henan, and was turned away. Zhao Yi reckoned Yang Zhi the only minister worth a name, so he haunted the gate daily until Yang grudgingly admitted him—yet Yang still lay abed. Zhao Yi walked straight to the inner chamber, stood over the couch, and said: "I have hidden in the western marches, yet long admired your high repute. Now we meet and you turn cold—what is left but fate?" He burst into a wail; the porters panicked and crowded the room. Yang saw he was no commoner, rose, and talked until he marveled. He told him, "You may go now." Next morning Yang arrived in full escort to return the call. Other clerks rode silken curtains and fine teams; Zhao Yi slept in the dust beside a brush cart and bade Yang sit with him in the road—every onlooker gaped. They talked from noon till dusk in perfect joy; Yang clasped his hand: "Uncut jade weeps blood until a true judge sees it!" Yang Zhi and Yuan Feng then jointly recommended him. His name thundered through Luoyang; every scholar dreamed of meeting him.
14
及西还，道经弘农，过侯太守皇甫规，门者不即通，壹遂遁去。 门吏惧，以白之，规闻壹名大惊，乃追书谢曰：“蹉跌不面，企德怀风，虚心委质，为日久矣。 侧闻仁者愍其区区，冀承清诲，以释遥悚。 今旦，外白有一尉两计吏，不道屈尊门下，更启乃知已去。 如印绶可投，夜岂待旦。 惟君明睿，平其夙心。 宁当慢慠，加于所天。 事在悖惑，不足具责。 倘可原察，追修前好，则何福如之！ 谨遣主簿奉书。 下笔气结，汗流竟趾。 ”壹报曰：“君学成师范，缙绅归慕，仰高希骥，历年滋多。 旋辕兼道，渴于言侍，沐浴晨兴，昧旦守门，实望仁君，昭其悬迟。 以贵下贱，握发垂接。 高可敷玩坟典，起发圣意； 下则抗论当世，消弭时灾。 岂悟君子，自生怠倦，失恂恂善诱之德，同亡国骄惰之志！ 盖见机而作，不俟终日，是以夙退自引，畏使君劳。 昔人或历说而不遇，或思士而无从，皆归之于天，不尤于物。 今壹自谴而已，岂敢有猜！ 仁君忽一匹夫，于德何损？ 而远辱手笔，追路相寻，诚足愧也。 壹之区区，曷云量己？ 其嗟可去，谢也可食，诚则顽薄，实识其趣。 但关节□动，膝炙坏溃，请俟他日，乃奉其情。 辄诵来贶，永以自慰。 ”遂去不顾。
On the road west he stopped at Hongnong to see Grand Warden Huangfu Gui; the porter stalled him, and Zhao Yi vanished. The terrified porter told his master; Huangfu, hearing it was Zhao Yi, sent a frantic apology: "A blunder kept us apart; I have long stood on tiptoe for your virtue and offered my heart—too long." I hear the humane pity my trifling self—I beg one clear word to ease my distant fear. This dawn my man said a captain and two clerks had come; I failed to welcome them at the gate—when I opened the message they were gone. Had I known, I would have flung away my seal that very night—why wait for morning? Only your clear mind can quiet the heart I have long nursed. Surely you will not heap cold pride on the man you call lord of life? The fault was blind confusion—not worth a full reckoning of blame. If you can forgive and renew our old bond, what blessing could match that?" I respectfully dispatch my chief clerk to deliver this letter. As I set brush to silk my chest tightens and sweat runs to my feet. Zhao Yi answered: "You have finished your studies and become a teacher to others; the gentry flock to you; they have long gazed up hoping to run in your traces. I doubled the road to reach you, hungering for conversation; I bathed, rose before light, and waited at your gate—truly I hoped a humane lord would end my long vigil. You might stoop from high rank to low, interrupt even your toilet to welcome a caller. On the lofty plane you might unfold the canon and stir a sage's purpose; on the practical plane debate the troubles of the day and lift present woes. Never did I think a gentleman would grow indolent, abandon the gentle art of leading others, and sink to the arrogance of a dying kingdom!" The wise move when they see the sign, not at sunset—so I slipped away early, lest I burden you further. Ancient worthies sometimes preached and found no patron, sometimes yearned for a lord and met closed doors—they blamed Heaven, not the world. I blame only myself—how should I suspect you? For a humane lord to overlook one nobody—what stain is that on his virtue? Yet you troubled your brush and sent men down the road after me—enough to humble me. What trifle am I to speak of knowing my measure? When the rebuke may be withdrawn, the apology may be accepted—I am coarse, yet I understand the point of the Analects. My joints trouble me and old moxa sores weep—ask me another day and I will lay out my heart. For now I treasure your letter and draw comfort from it. With that he walked away and never looked back.
15
州郡争致礼命，十辟公府，并不就，终于家。 初袁逢使善相者相壹，云“仕不过郡吏”，竟如其言。
Provinces competed to honor him; the high ministries called him ten times—he never went, and died at home. Once Yuan Feng had a face-reader study Zhao Yi, who predicted he would never rise above a commandery clerk—it proved true.
16
著赋、颂、箴、诔、书、论及杂文十六篇。
His rhapsodies, hymns, admonitions, laments, letters, essays, and miscellanea number sixteen.
17
刘梁字曼山，一名岑，东平宁阳人也。 梁宗室子孙，而少孤贫，卖书于市以自资。
Liu Liang, courtesy name Manshan, also known as Cen, came from Ningyang in Dongping. A cadet of the Han imperial clan, he was orphaned and poor as a boy and peddled books in the market to live.
18
常疾世多利交，以邪曲相党，乃著《破群论》。 时之览者以为：“仲尼作《春秋》，乱臣知惧。 今此论之作，俗士岂不愧心！ ”其文不存。
He loathed an age of mercenary cliques and crooked cabals, and wrote "Breaking the Clique." Contemporary readers said: "Confucius wrote the Spring and Autumn and wicked ministers trembled. With this essay abroad, can shallow men not blush?" The text itself is lost.
19
又著《辩和同之论》。 其辞曰：
He also wrote the "Disquisition on Harmony versus Conformity." It begins:
20
夫事有违而得道，有顺而失义，有爱而为害，有恶而为美。 其故何乎？ 盖明智之所得，暗伪之所失也。 是以君子之于事也，无适无莫，必考之以义焉。
Some deeds run against the grain yet hit the Way; some follow custom yet miss justice; some kindness harms, some hatred heals. Why is that? Because the clear-sighted seize what the deluded forfeit. So the gentleman never says "only this" or "only that"—he tests every case by right.
21
得由和兴，失由同起，故以可济否谓之和，好恶不殊谓之同。 《春秋传》曰：“和如羹焉，酸苦以剂其味，君子食之以平其心。 同如水焉，若以水济水，谁能食之？ 琴瑟之专一，谁能听之？ ”是以君子之行，周而不比，和而不同； 以救过为正，以匡恶为忠。 经曰：“将顺其美，匡救其恶，则上下和睦能相亲也。 ”
Success comes from harmony, ruin from blind sameness: harmony blends sour and bitter to save a dish; sameness is water poured on water. The Zuo tradition says: "Harmony is like soup—sour and bitter balance the taste; the gentleman eats it to steady his mind. Sameness is like plain water—who can drink water thickened with water? If lute and zither play one note forever, who can bear to listen?" Hence the gentleman is all-embracing without cabal, harmonious without mere conformity; to right a fault is uprightness, to cure vice is loyalty. The canon says: "Second the good in a ruler, mend the bad—then court and country meet in trust." "
22
昔楚恭王有疾，召其大夫曰：“不谷不德，少主社稷。 失先君之绪，覆楚国之师，不谷之罪也。 若以宗庙之灵，得保首领以殁，请为灵若厉。 ”大夫许诸。 及其卒也，子囊曰：“不然。 夫事君者，从其善，不从其过。 赫赫楚国，而君临之，抚正南海，训及诸夏，其宠大矣。 有是宠也，而知其过，可不谓恭乎！ ”大夫众之。 此讳而得道者也。 及灵王骄淫，暴虐无度，芋尹申亥从王之欲，以殡于乾溪，殉之二女。 此顺而失义者也。 鄢陵之役，晋楚对战，阳穀献酒，子反以毙。 此爱而害之者也。 臧武仲曰：“孟孙之恶我，药石也； 季孙之爱我，美疢也。 疢毒滋厚，石犹生我。 ”此恶而为美者也。 孔子曰：“智之难也！ 有臧武仲之智，而不容于鲁国，抑有由也。 作不顺而施不恕也。 ”盖善其知义，讥其违道也。
King Gong of Chu fell ill and told his ministers: "I lack virtue; young, I took the throne. I broke my father's line and shattered Chu's hosts—the fault is mine. If the ancestors let me die with my head on my shoulders, bury me as "Ling" or "Li." The ministers agreed. At his death Zinang said: "Not so. A servant follows his lord's virtue, not his errors. Mighty Chu lay in his hand; he steadied the southern sea and taught the central plain—his glory was vast. To hold such power yet know his own wrong—surely that is true reverence!" The ministers accepted this. That is hiding a ruler's shame yet serving the Way. King Ling turned wanton and savage; Shen Hai indulged his whims, laid him out at Qianxi, and buried two concubines alive at his side. That is obeying a lord into injustice. At Yanling, when Jin met Chu, a native of Yanggu pressed wine on chief minister Zifan, who drank himself to death. That is fondness that killed him. Zang Wuzhong said: "Mengsun's hatred is bitter physic; Jisun's love is a pretty ulcer." The sore may fester—yet the stone still saves my life. That is harshness turned to blessing. Confucius said: "How rare is true wisdom! Zang Wuzhong was wise yet could not stay in Lu—there was cause. His acts defied custom and his kindness lacked measure. So the sage praised his sense of justice and faulted his breach of the Way.
23
夫知而违之，伪也； 不知而失之，暗也。 暗为伪焉，其患一也。 患之所在，非徒在智之不及，又在及而违这者矣。 故曰“智及之，仁不能守之，虽得之，必失之”也。 《夏书》曰：“念兹在兹，庶事恕施。 ”忠智之谓矣。
To know the right and flout it is hypocrisy; not to know and miss it is folly. Folly and hypocrisy breed the same harm. The danger lies not only in short wit but in having wit and turning it against the good. Hence the saying: "Insight without humanity cannot keep a gain—it slips away." The Xia Documents say: "Keep the charge in mind; in every task be generous." That is loyal wisdom.
24
故君子之行，动则思义，不为利回，不为义疚，进退周旋，唯道是务。 苟失其道，则兄弟不阿； 苟得其义，虽仇雠不废。 故解狐蒙祁奚之荐，二叔被周公之害，勃鞮以逆文为成，傅瑕以顺厉为败，管苏以憎忤取进，申侯以爱从见退：考之以义也。 故曰：“不在逆顺，以义为断； 不在憎爱，以道为贵。 ”《礼记》曰：“爱而知其恶，憎而知其善。 ”考义之谓也。
So the gentleman weighs every step by duty—never swerves for gain nor bends for favor; come or go, he serves only the Way. Miss the Way and even brothers will not humor you; Hit the mark and even foes may stand by you. Thus Xie Hu won Qi Xi's nomination from obscurity; Guan and Cai felt the Duke of Zhou's weight; Bo Di's defiance of Duke Wen proved loyal; Fu Xia's obedience to Prince Li brought ruin; Guan Su rose by blunt honesty; Shen Hou fell by flattery—all judged by right. So the maxim runs: "Judge not by yes or no but by justice; weigh not love or hate but hold the Way dear." The Book of Rites says: "Love a man yet mark his faults; hate him yet admit his virtues." That is judging by right.

Never cry "I alone can"—seek wise men; remember Han Xin, whom Guangwu esteemed." Zhou Gong was paragon sage; Shi Que pure minister—they curbed favor with awe, cut kin by duty." Do not blame the times and neglect your own conduct." Do not say no men remain—none has seen your truth." Forget riches, spurn station—then fortune endures." Bend principle to fit the crowd—and nothing worth watching remains." Your fathers' high honor—follow it forever." Hang this admonition on your sash for life." Cai Yong and the rest extolled his lines above all others.
46
后迁外黄令，帝敕同僚临送，祖于上东门，诏东观画彪像以劝学者。 彪到官，有德政，上书荐县人申徒蟠等。 病卒于官，文章多亡。
Promoted magistrate of Waihuang, he was escorted by peers from the Upper East Gate, and the court ordered his portrait hung at the Eastern Pavilion to rouse scholars. At post he governed well and recommended Shen Tu Pan and other locals. He died in harness; many works are gone.
47
子岱，亦知名。
His son Gao Dai likewise won a name.
48
张超字子并，河间鄚人也，留侯良之后也。 有文才。 灵帝时，从车骑将军朱雋征黄巾，为别部司马。 著赋、颂、碑文、荐、檄、笺、书、谒文、嘲，凡十九篇。 超又善于草书，妙绝时人，世共传之。
Zhang Chao, courtesy name Zibing, came from Mo in Hejian and traced descent from Marquis Zhang Liang. He wrote well. Under Emperor Ling he served Zhu Jun against the Yellow Turbans as major of a detached column. His rhapsodies, hymns, inscriptions, recommendations, manifestos, notes, letters, calling cards, and lampoons number nineteen. He excelled at cursive hand, unmatched in his day, and copyists spread his sheets.
49
祢衡字正平，平原般人也。 少有才辩，而尚气刚傲，好矫时慢物。 兴平中，避难荆州。 建安初，来游许下。 始达颍川，乃阴怀一剌，既而无所之适，至于刺字漫灭。 是时，许都新建，贤士大夫，四方来集。 或问衡曰：“盍从陈长文、司马伯达乎？ ”对曰：“吾焉能从屠沽兒耶！ ”又问：“荀文若、赵稚长云何？ ”衡曰：“文若可借面吊丧，稚长可使监厨请客。 ”唯善鲁国孔融及弘农杨脩。 常称曰：“大兒孔文举，小兒杨德祖。 余子碌碌，莫足数也。 ”融亦深爱其才。
Mi Heng, courtesy name Zhengping, was from Bo in Pingyuan commandery. Young, he was brilliant and disputatious, proud of temper, fond of defying fashion and sneering at the great. In Xingping he took refuge in Jingzhou. Early in Jian'an he drifted to Xu. Reaching Yingchuan he tucked a single name card in his sleeve, found no door worth knocking, until the ink wore off. Xu was new-built then, and talent flocked from every quarter. Someone said: "Why not call on Chen Qun or Sima Lang?" He snapped: "Am I to trail tavern brats?" They asked next: "What of Xun Yu and Zhao Yan?" Mi Heng said: "Xun Yu has a face fit only for borrowed mourning calls; Zhao Yan could keep the kitchen and pour the wine." Only Kong Rong of Lu and Yang Xiu of Hongnong earned his respect. He called them "the great lad Kong Wenju, the lesser Yang Dezu." The others were hacks—not worth reckoning." Kong Rong loved his gifts in return.
50
衡始弱冠，而融年四十，遂与为交友。 上疏荐之曰：
Mi was barely twenty, Kong forty, yet they became sworn friends. Kong Rong then memorialized the throne:
51
臣闻洪水横流，帝思俾乂，旁求四方，以招贤俊。 昔孝武继统，将弘祖业，畴咨熙载，群士响臻。 陛下睿圣，纂承基绪，遭遇厄运，劳谦日昃。 惟岳降神，异人并出。 窃见处士平原祢衡，年二十四，字正平，淑质贞亮，英才卓砾。 初涉艺文，升堂睹奥。 目所一见，辄诵于口； 耳所瞥闻，不忘于心。 性与道合，思若有神。 弘羊潜计，安世默识，以衡准之，诚不足怪。 忠果正直，志怀霜雪。 见善若惊，疾恶若仇。 任座抗行，史鱼厉节，殆无以过也。 鸷鸟累伯，不如一鹗。 使衡立朝，必有可观。 飞辩骋辞，溢气坌涌，解疑释结，临敌有余。 昔贾谊求试属国，诡系单于； 终军欲以长缨，牵致劲越。 弱冠慷慨，前世美之。 近日路粹、严象，亦用异才，擢拜台郎，衡宜与为比。 如得龙跃天衢，振翼云汉，扬声紫微，垂光虹蜺，足以昭近署之多士，增四门之穆穆。 钧天广乐，必有奇丽之观； 帝室皇居，必蓄非常之宝。 若衡等辈，不可多得。 《激楚》、《杨阿》，至妙之容，台牧者之所贪； 飞兔、騕褭，绝足奔放，良、乐之所急。 臣等区区，敢不以闻。
I have heard that when the Deluge raged, the sage sought helpers everywhere. Emperor Wu widened the Han line, asked the realm for shining talent, and worthies answered in hosts. Your Majesty, sage and wise, took up the torch in dark days and toils humbly dawn to dusk. The sacred peak breathes forth spirit; rare men rise at once. I note the recluse Mi Heng of Pingyuan, twenty-four, style Zhengping—clear in character, blazing in talent. First steps in letters took him straight to the inner room of learning. What his eye skimmed his tongue could repeat; what his ear caught his mind would not lose. His nature meets the Dao; his thought seems almost divine. Sang Hongyang's silent sums, Zhang Anshi's wordless recall—Mi Heng matches them without strain. Loyal, blunt, straight—his purpose is ice and snow. He starts at the sight of good, hunts evil like a foe. Ren Zuo's blunt courage, the historian Yu's steel—none outdoes him. A mob of kites is worth less than one sea-eagle. Set him in the hall and the court would have something to see. His wit races, his breath boils—he untangles riddles and still has force for the foe. Jia Yi asked to try office on the frontier, swore to rope the Chanyu; Zhong Jun vowed a long cord to haul in southern Yue. Young heroes—past ages sang their praise. Lu Cui and Yan Xiang rose by rare gifts to terrace posts—Mi Heng belongs in their company. Let him vault the sky road like a dragon, beat wings in the River of Stars, thunder in the Purple Palace, trail rainbow light—then your inner court will show its wealth of talent and the four gates grow grave with awe. Heaven's great symphony must hold wonders never seen; the royal house surely hoards gems beyond price." Men like Mi Heng do not come twice in an age. "Rushing Chu" and "Yang'e"—the acme of grace—what every governor craves; Flying Rabbit, Yaoniao—steeds no rein can hold—what Bo Le would give his eye for. We humbly dare not keep this from your ears.
52
融既爱衡才，数称述于曹操。 操欲见之，而衡素相轻疾，自称狂病，不肯往，而数有恣言。 操怀忿，而以其才名，不欲杀之。 闻衡善击鼓，乃召为鼓史，因大会宾客，阅试音节。 诸史过者，皆令脱其故衣，更着岑牟、单绞之服。 次至衡，衡方为《渔阳》参挝，蹀□而前，容态有异，声节悲壮，听者莫不慷慨。 衡进至操前而止，吏呵之曰：“鼓史何不改装，而轻敢进乎？ ”衡曰：“诺。 ”于是先解衵衣，次释余服，裸身而立，徐取岑牟、单绞而着之，毕，复参挝而去，颜色不怍。 操笑曰：“本欲辱衡，衡反辱孤。 ”
Kong Rong loved his genius and praised him again and again to Cao Cao. Cao Cao wished to meet him, but Mi Heng had long despised him, pleaded madness, stayed away, and kept pouring out insults. Cao nursed a grudge yet spared him for fear of ruining his own name. Learning Mi Heng was a master drummer, he named him drum clerk and, at a great banquet, made him prove his skill. Every drummer had to strip and don the clown cap and motley wrap. When Mi Heng's turn came he struck the "Yuyang" roll, strutting forward with a wild air, the beat fierce and mournful—every guest sat upright. He halted before Cao Cao; aides barked: "Why have you not changed costume, drummer?" "Very well," said Mi Heng. He peeled off his shirt, shed the rest, stood naked, then slowly donned cap and motley, finished the roll, and walked off without a blush. Cao laughed: "I meant to humble him—he humbled me." "
53
孔融退而数之曰：“正平大雅，固当尔邪？ ”因宣操区区之意。 衡许往。 融复见操，说衡狂疾，今求得自谢。 操喜，敕门者有客便通，待之极晏。 衡乃着布单衣、疏巾，手持三尺棁杖，坐大营门，以杖捶地大骂。 吏曰：“外有狂生，坐于营门，言语悖逆，请收案罪。 ”操怒，谓融曰：“祢衡竖子，孤杀之犹雀鼠耳。 顾此人素有虚名，远近将谓孤不能容之，今送与刘表，视当何如。 ”于是遣人骑送之。 临发，众人为之祖道，先供设于城南，乃更相戒曰：“祢衡勃虐无礼，今因其后到，咸当以不起折之也。 ”及衡至，众人莫肯兴，衡坐而大号。 众问其故，衡曰：“坐者为冢，卧者为尸。 尸冢之间，能不悲乎！ ”
Kong Rong took him aside: "Zhengping—was this worthy of you?" He relayed Cao Cao's small-minded terms. Mi Heng agreed to call. Kong Rong told Cao the "madness" had passed and Mi would apologize. Cao was pleased, told the gate to pass any visitor, and waited till very late. Mi Heng came in thin robe and kerchief, a three-foot baton in hand, sat at the camp gate, and drummed the ground with curses. Guards reported: "A madman sits at the gate, shouting treason—arrest him." Cao snarled to Kong Rong: "That whelp Mi Heng—I could crush him like a bug." Yet he has a famous name; if I kill him the world will call me narrow—pack him off to Liu Biao instead." He had him escorted south by horse. Friends gathered south of the city to see him off and agreed: "Mi Heng is insufferable—when he comes late we will all stay seated to humble him." When he came no one stood; Mi Heng sat down and burst into a wail. They asked why; he said: "Seated guests are burial mounds; those who sprawl are corpses. Between tomb and corpse—should I not weep?" "
54
刘表及荆州士大夫，先服其才名，甚宾礼之，文章言议，非衡不定。 表尝与诸文人共草章奏，并极其才思。 时衡出，还见之，开省未周，因毁以抵地。 表怃然为骇。 衡乃从求笔札，须臾立成，辞义可观。 表大悦，益重之。
Liu Biao and the Jingzhou scholars, awed by his fame, honored him as chief guest—no memorial passed without his polish. Once Liu Biao and his writers drafted a joint memorial, straining every wit. Mi Heng stepped out; on return he skimmed the draft, tore it up, and flung it down. Liu Biao gaped, stunned. He demanded brush and paper, and in moments produced a new text, phrasing and sense superb. Liu Biao was delighted and prized him higher.
55
后复侮慢于表，表耻，不能容，以江夏太守黄祖性急，故送衡与之，祖亦善待焉。 衡为作书记，轻重疏密，各得体宜。 祖持其手曰：“处士，此正得祖意，如祖腹中之所欲言也。 ”
Later he insulted Liu Biao, who could not stomach the shame—knowing Huang Zu of Jiangxia was quick-tempered, he palmed Mi Heng off on him; Huang Zu welcomed him kindly. As his secretary Mi Heng gauged every tone—light or heavy, tight or loose—perfectly. Huang Zu clasped his hand: "Sir, this is exactly what I meant—words drawn from my own belly." "
56
祖长子射，为章陵太守，尤善于衡。 尝与衡俱游，共读蔡邕所作碑文，射爱其辞，还恨不缮写。 衡曰：“吾虽一览，犹能识之，唯其中石缺二字，为不明耳。 ”因书出之，躬驰使写碑，还校，如衡所书，莫不叹伏。 射时大会宾客，人有献鹦鹉者，射举卮于衡曰：“愿先生赋之，以娱嘉宾。 ”衡揽笔而作，文无加点，辞采甚丽。
Huang She, Huang Zu's heir as Zhangling grand warden, loved him best. They once read a Cai Yong inscription together; Huang She loved the text but had no copy. Mi Heng said: "One reading was enough—only two characters on the stone are chipped away." He wrote it from memory; Huang She sent a man to the stone—the copy matched; all marveled. At a banquet someone brought a parrot; Huang She raised his cup: "Sir, please compose a rhapsody to delight our guests." Mi Heng took brush and wrote at once—no blot, no erasure, the lines gorgeous.
57
后黄祖在蒙冲船上，大会宾客，而衡言不逊顺，祖惭，乃呵之。 衡更熟视曰：“死公！ 云等道？ ”祖大怒，令五百将出，欲加箠。 衡方大骂，祖恚，遂令杀之。 祖主簿素疾衡，即时杀焉。 射徒跣来救，不及。 祖亦悔之，乃厚加棺敛。 衡时年二十六，其文章多亡云。
Later, on a war-boat banquet, Mi Heng spoke rudely; Huang Zu, embarrassed, barked at him. Mi Heng glared: "You dead dotard!" What filth is that from your mouth? Huang Zu roared for the guards to drag him out for a beating. Mi Heng kept cursing; Huang Zu, in a fury, ordered him slain. The chief clerk, who loathed him, struck at once. Huang She ran barefoot to stop it—too late. Huang Zu repented and buried him with honor. He was twenty-six; most of his writings are lost.
58
赞曰：情志既动，篇辞为贵。 抽心呈貌，非雕非蔚。 殊状共体，同声异气。 言观丽则，永监淫费。
The verdict runs: When heart stirs, the word is the jewel. Lay bare the heart, show the face—neither carve nor smother. Many shapes, one frame; one song, many winds. Read the lovely pattern—and forever guard against wanton waste.
