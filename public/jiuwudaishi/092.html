姚顗，字伯真，京兆萬年人。 曾祖希齊，湖州司功參軍。 祖宏慶，蘇州刺史。 父荊，國子祭酒。 顗少蠢，敦厚，靡事容貌，任其自然，流輩未之重，唯兵部侍郎司空圖深器之，以女妻焉。 顗性仁恕，多為仆妾所欺，心雖察之，而不能面折，終身無喜怒。 不知錢百之為陌，黍百之為銖，凡家人市貨百物，入增其倍，出減其半，不詢其由，無擔石之儲，心不隕獲。 唐末，隨計入洛，出遊嵩山，有白衣丈夫拜於路側，請為童仆。 顗辭不納。 乃曰：「鬼神享於德，君子孚於信。 余則鬼也，將以托賢者之德，通化工之信，幸無辭焉。 昔余掌事陰府，承命攝人之魂氣，名氏同而其人非，且富有壽算，復而歸之，則筋骸已敗，由是獲譴，使不得為陽生。 公中夏之相輔也，今為謁中天之祠，若以某姓名求之，神必許諾。 」顗因為之虔禱而還，白衣迎於山下，曰：「余免其苦矣。 」拜謝而退。 顗次年擢進士第。 梁貞明中，歷校書郎、登封令、右補闕、禮部員外郎，召入翰林，累遷至中書舍人。 唐莊宗平梁，以例貶復州司馬，歲餘牽復，授左散騎常侍，歷兵吏部侍郎、尚書左丞。 唐末帝即位，講求輔相，乃書朝中清望官十餘人姓名置於瓶中，清夜焚香而挾之，既而得盧文紀與顗，遂拜中書侍郎、平章事。 制前一日，嵩山白衣來謁，謂顗曰：「公明日為相。 」其言無差，冥數固先定矣。 高祖登極，罷相為刑部尚書，俄遷戶部尚書。 天福五年冬卒，年七十五。 贈左僕射。 子惟和嗣。 顗疏於財，而禦家無術，既死，斂葬之資不備，家人俟賻物及鬻第方能舉喪而去。 士大夫愛其廉而鄙其拙。
Yao Yi, whose courtesy name was Bozhen, came from Wannian in Jingzhao. His great-grandfather Xiqi had served as registrar in Huzhou. His grandfather Hongqing had been prefect of Suzhou. His father Jing had been rector of the Imperial University. In youth Yi seemed slow-witted yet was honest and unassuming; he cared nothing for his looks and let things be. Contemporaries paid him little heed, but Sikong Tu, vice minister of war, valued him deeply and married his daughter to him. Yi was gentle and forgiving by nature. Servants and concubines often took advantage of him; he saw it clearly enough, yet could never rebuke them openly, and he went through life without showing anger or delight. He did not know that a hundred coins make a string or a hundred grains of millet a zhu. When the household bought or sold, they doubled every incoming price and halved every outgoing one, and he never asked why. He had scarcely a bushel of grain in store, yet his spirit did not break under the waste. Late in the Tang he went to Luoyang for the metropolitan examination and visited Mount Song. A man in white bowed beside the road and asked to become his servant. Yi refused and would not take him in. The man then said, "Ghosts and spirits feed on virtue; the gentleman lives by faith. I am a ghost, and wish to rest upon a worthy man's virtue and carry Heaven's transforming trust—please do not refuse." Long ago I served in the realm of darkness, charged with taking souls. When the name matched but not the person, and the allotted years were still many, the body I returned to was already ruined. For that I was punished and barred from living among men. You are destined to aid the central realm. You are now going to pray at the shrine of the Central Heaven. If you plead there under a certain name, the spirits will surely grant it." Yi prayed earnestly for him and returned. The man in white met him at the foot of the mountain and said, "I am freed from my torment." He bowed in thanks and went away. The next year Yi passed the jinshi examination. Under Liang in the Zhenming era he served as collator, magistrate of Dengfeng, right remonstrator, and vice director of rites, entered the Hanlin Academy, and rose step by step to secretariat drafter. When Zhuangzong of Tang overthrew Liang, Yi was demoted by precedent to aide at Fuzhou; after a year he was recalled, made left regular attendant, and later served as vice ministers of war and personnel and as left vice director of the secretariat. When Emperor Modi of Tang came to the throne he sought chief ministers. He wrote the names of a dozen eminent officials on slips, put them in a jar, burned incense at night, and drew one out. Lu Wenji and Yi were chosen, and both were made vice director of the secretariat and chief councillor. On the eve of the appointment edict, the man in white from Mount Song came to see him and said, "Tomorrow you will be chief minister." His words proved exact; what was ordained in the unseen had long been settled. When Gaozu took the throne, Yi was removed as chief minister and made minister of justice, then soon minister of revenue. He died in winter of the fifth year of Tianfu, aged seventy-five. He was posthumously made left vice director of the secretariat. His son Weihe succeeded him. Yi cared little for money and knew nothing of household management. After his death there was not enough for burial; his family had to wait for gifts of condolence and the sale of the house before they could bury him and leave. Men of letters admired his integrity but despised his helplessness.

呂琦，字輝山，幽州安次人也。 祖壽，瀛州景城主簿。 父兗，滄州節度判官，累至檢校右庶子。 劉守光攻陷滄州，琦父兗被擒，族之。 琦時年十五，為吏追攝，將就戮焉。 有趙玉者，幽、薊之義士也，久遊於兗之門下，見琦臨危，乃紿謂監者曰：「此子某之同氣也，幸無濫焉。 」監者信之，即引之俱去。 行一舍，琦困於徒步，以足病告，玉負之而行，逾數百里，因變姓名，乞食於路，乃免其禍。 年弱冠，以家門遇禍，邈無所依，乃勵志勤學，多遊於汾、晉。 唐天祐中，莊宗方開霸府，翹佇賢士，墨制授琦代州軍事判官，秩滿歸太原，監軍使張承業重琦器量，禮遇尤厚。 天成初，拜琦殿中侍御史，遷駕部員外郎，兼侍御史知雜事。 會河陽帑吏竊財事發，詔軍巡院鞫之。 時軍巡使尹訓怙勢納賂，枉直相反，俄有訴冤於闕下者，詔琦按之，既驗其奸，乃上言請治尹訓，沮而不行。 琦連奏不已，訓知其不免，自殺於家，其獄遂明，蒙活者甚眾，自是朝廷多琦之公直。 高祖建義於太原，唐末帝幸懷州，趙德鈞駐軍於團柏谷，末帝以琦嘗在德鈞幕下，因令賫都統使官告以賜之，且犒其軍焉。 及觀軍於北陲，館於忻州，會晉祖降下晉安寨，遣使告於近郡，琦適遇其使，即斬之以聞，尋率郡兵千人間道而歸。 高祖入洛，亦弗之責，止改授秘書監而已。 天福中，預修《唐書》，權掌選部，皆有能名焉。 累遷禮部、刑部、戶部、兵部侍郎，階至金紫光祿大夫，爵至開國子。
Lu Qi, courtesy name Huishan, was from Anci in Youzhou. His grandfather Shou had been registrar of Jingcheng in Yingzhou. His father Yan had been military judge at Cangzhou and rose to supernumerary right guardian of the heir apparent. When Liu Shouguang took Cangzhou, Qi's father Yan was captured and the whole clan put to death. Qi was fifteen at the time; officers seized him and he was about to be killed. A man named Zhao Yu, a champion of You and Ji who had long been in Yan's service, saw Qi in danger and lied to the guard: "This boy is my kinsman—please do not kill him by mistake." The guard believed him and led Qi away with him. After one stage on the road Qi could walk no farther and pleaded a foot ailment. Yu carried him on his back for hundreds of li. They changed their names and begged along the way, and so escaped death. When he came of age, his family destroyed and with nowhere to turn, he threw himself into study and traveled widely through Fen and Jin. In the Tianyou era Zhuangzong was opening his headquarters and sought worthy men. Qi was appointed military judge at Daizhou by informal edict. When his term ended he returned to Taiyuan, where Commissioner Zhang Chengye valued his character and treated him with special honor. At the start of Tiancheng he was made palace attending censor, then vice director of the transport bureau, and also attending censor in charge of miscellaneous cases. When a treasury clerk at Heyang was caught stealing, the court ordered the military patrol office to investigate. The patrol commissioner Yin Xun abused his power and took bribes, twisting justice. Soon someone appealed at the palace gates. Qi was ordered to investigate, proved the wrongdoing, and memorialized for Yin Xun's punishment, but the request was blocked. Qi kept memorializing without stop. Xun knew he could not escape and killed himself at home. The case was cleared and many lives saved. From then on the court prized Qi's fairness. When Gaozu raised his banner at Taiyuan, Emperor Modi went to Huaizhou while Zhao Dejun camped at Tuanbai Valley. Modi, knowing Qi had served under Dejun, sent him to present the chief commander's commission and reward the army. While inspecting troops on the northern border he stayed at Xinzhou. The Lord of Jin had taken Jin'an Fortress and sent messengers to nearby prefectures. Qi met the messenger, beheaded him at once and reported it, then led a thousand prefectural troops back by a hidden road. When Gaozu entered Luoyang he did not punish him, but only reassigned him as director of the palace library. During Tianfu he helped compile the Book of Tang and headed personnel selection, winning a reputation for competence in both. He rose through the vice ministries of rites, punishments, revenue, and war, attaining grand master of golden beryl radiance and the title viscount of a newly founded state.

琦美風儀，有器概，雖以剛直聞於時，而內實仁恕。 初，高祖謀求輔相，時宰臣李崧力薦琦於高祖，雲可大用。 高祖數召琦於便殿，言及當世事，甚奇之，方將倚以為相，忽遇疾而逝，人皆惜之。
Qi had fine presence and breadth of spirit. Though known for stern integrity, at heart he was gentle and forgiving. When Gaozu was seeking chief ministers, Li Song strongly recommended Qi, saying he could be put to great use. Gaozu often summoned him to the informal hall to discuss affairs of the day and found him extraordinary. He was about to make him chief minister when Qi fell ill and died suddenly; everyone mourned the loss.

梁文矩，字德儀，鄆州人。 父景，秘書少監。 梁福王友璋好接賓客，文矩少遊其門，初試太子校書，轉秘書郎。 友璋領鄆州，奏為項城令，及移鎮徐方，辟為從事。 友璋卒，改兗州觀察判官。 時莊宗遣明宗襲據鄆州，文矩以父母在鄆，一旦隔越，不知存亡，為子之情，戀望如灼，遂間路歸鄆，尋謁莊宗。 莊宗喜之，授天平軍節度掌書記，在明宗幕下。 明宗歷汴、恒二鎮，皆隨府遷職。 天成初，授右諫議大夫，知宣武軍軍州事，歷御史中丞、吏部侍郎、禮部尚書、西都副留守，判京兆府事，縱改兵部尚書。 文矩以嘗事霸府，每懷公輔之望。 時高祖自外鎮入覲，嘗薦於明宗曰：「梁文矩早事陛下，甚有勤勞，未升相輔，外論慊之。 」明宗曰：「久忘此人，吾之過也。 」尋有旨降命，會丁外憂而止。 清泰初，拜太常卿。 高祖即位，授吏部尚書，改太子少師。 文矩喜清靜之教，聚道書數千卷，企慕赤松、留侯之事，而服食尤盡其善。 後因風痹，上章請退，以太子太保致仕，居洛陽久之。 天福八年，以疾卒，時年五十九。 贈太子太傅。
Liang Wenju, courtesy name Deyi, was from Yanzhou. His father Jing had been vice director of the palace library. Prince Youzhang of Fu in Liang loved to entertain guests; Wenju frequented his house in youth. He first passed as collator to the heir apparent, then became a secretary. When Youzhang governed Yanzhou he recommended Wenju as magistrate of Xiangcheng. When he moved his command to Xuzhou he took Wenju on as staff officer. After Youzhang's death he became judge on the Yanzhou observation staff. Zhuangzong then sent Mingzong to take Yanzhou by surprise. Wenju's parents were there; cut off at once, he did not know if they lived or died. Filial longing burned like fire; he took a hidden road back to Yanzhou and soon presented himself to Zhuangzong. Zhuangzong was pleased and made him secretariat secretary on the Tianping staff under Mingzong. As Mingzong served at Bian and Heng in turn, Wenju followed each posting. At the start of Tiancheng he was made right advisory censor and acting military prefect of the Xuanwu army, then served as censor-in-chief, vice minister of personnel, minister of rites, vice defender of the western capital with jurisdiction over Jingzhao, and finally minister of war. Having once served the hegemon's headquarters, he always hoped to become chief minister. When Gaozu came from an outer command to pay court he once told Mingzong, "Liang Wenju served Your Majesty early with great labor; he has not been made chief minister, and public opinion is dissatisfied." Mingzong said, "I had long forgotten this man—it is my fault." Soon an appointment edict was about to issue, but he entered mourning for his father and it was halted. At the start of Qingtai he was made director of the court of imperial sacrifices. When Gaozu took the throne he was made minister of personnel, then junior tutor to the heir apparent. Wenju loved the way of quiet purity, gathered thousands of Daoist volumes, aspired to Master Redpine and the Marquis of Liu, and pursued elixirs with the utmost care. Later, afflicted with wind paralysis, he memorialized to retire and left office as grand mentor to the heir apparent, living long in Luoyang. In the eighth year of Tianfu he died of illness, aged fifty-nine. He was posthumously made grand tutor to the heir apparent.

史圭，常山人也。 其先與王武俊來於塞外，因家石邑。 高祖曾，歷鎮陽牙校。 父鈞，假安平、九門令。 圭好學工詩，長於吏道。 唐光化中，歷阜城、饒陽尉，改房子、寧晉、元氏、樂壽、博陸五邑令。 為寧晉日，擅給驛廩，以貸饑民，民甚感之。 及為樂壽令，里人為之立碑。 同光中，任圜為真定尹，擢為本府司錄，不應命。 郭崇韜領其地，辟為從事，及明宗代崇韜，以舊職縻之。 明宗即位，入為文昌正郎，安重誨薦為河南少尹，判府事，尋命為樞密院直學士。 時圭以受知於重誨，重誨奏令圭與同列閻至俱升殿侍立，以備顧問，明宗可之。 尋自左諫議大夫拜尚書右丞，有入相之望。 圭敏於吏事，重誨本不知書，為事剛愎，每於明宗前可否重務，圭恬然終日，不能剖正其事。 長興中，重誨既誅，圭出為貝州刺史，未幾罷免，退歸常山。 由是閉門杜絕人事，雖親戚故人造者不見其面，每遊別墅，則乘婦人氈車以自蔽匿，人莫知其心。 高祖登極，征為刑部侍郎，判鹽鐵副使，皆宰臣馮道之奏請也。 始圭在明宗時為右丞，權判銓事，道在中書，嘗以堂判衡銓司所註官，圭怒，力爭之，道亦微有不足之色，至是圭首為道所舉，方愧其度量遠不及也。 旋改吏部侍郎，分知銓事，而圭素厲廉守節，大著公平之譽。
Shi Gui was from Changshan. His forebears had come from beyond the frontier with Wang Wujun and settled at Shiyi. His great-grandfather Zeng had served as camp officer at Zhengyang. His father Jun had held acting appointments as magistrate of Anping and Jimen. Gui loved learning and poetry and excelled in administration. Under Tang Guanghua he served as assistant magistrate of Fucheng and Raoyang, then as magistrate of Fangzi, Ningjin, Yuanshi, Leshou, and Bolu in turn. As magistrate of Ningjin he on his own authority opened relay granaries to lend grain to the starving; the people were deeply grateful. When he became magistrate of Leshou, the villagers erected a stele in his honor. During Tongguang, when Ren Yuan was metropolitan governor of Zhending, he offered Gui the post of recorder, but Gui declined. Guo Chongtao took the region and recruited him as staff officer. When Mingzong replaced Chongtao, he kept Gui in the same post. When Mingzong came to the throne Gui entered court as principal secretary in the Wenchang bureau. An Chonghui recommended him as vice governor of Henan with jurisdiction over prefectural affairs, and soon he was made direct academician of the privy council. Gui then enjoyed An Chonghui's favor. Chonghui memorialized that Gui and his colleague Yan Zhi should both attend in the hall as advisers. Mingzong approved. Soon he rose from left advisory censor to right vice director of the secretariat, with prospects of becoming chief minister. Gui was sharp in administration, but Chonghui was barely literate and obstinate; day after day before Mingzong he decided weighty affairs, and Gui sat calmly all day unable to set things right. During Changxing, after Chonghui was executed, Gui was sent out as prefect of Beizhou, soon dismissed, and retired to Changshan. He then shut his doors to the world. Kin and old friends who called did not see his face. When he visited his villa he rode in a woman's felt carriage to hide himself, and no one knew what was in his heart. When Gaozu took the throne Gui was summoned as vice minister of punishments and deputy salt and iron commissioner, all on Feng Dao's recommendation. Earlier, as right vice director under Mingzong, Gui had headed selection. Feng Dao in the secretariat once used his chief councillor's ruling to override the selection bureau's appointments. Gui grew angry and fought it; Dao too showed displeasure. Now Gui was the first Dao recommended, and he was ashamed that his breadth of spirit fell far short of Dao's. Soon he was made vice minister of personnel and shared headship of selection. Gui had always been strict in integrity, and won great fame for fairness.

圭前為河南少尹日，有嵩山術士遺圭石藥如鬥，謂圭曰：「服之可以延壽，然不可中輟，輟則疾作矣。 」圭後服之，神爽力健，深寶惜焉。 清泰末，圭在常山，遇秘瓊之亂，時貯於衣笥，為賊所劫，後不復得。 天福中，疾生胸臆之間，常如火灼，圭知不濟，求歸鄉里，詔許之。 及涉河，竟為藥氣所蒸，卒於路，歸葬石邑，時年六十八。
When Gui had been vice governor of Henan, a Mount Song adept gave him a stone medicine the size of a peck measure and said, "If you take it you can prolong life, but you must not stop midway—if you stop, illness will follow." Gui later took it and felt clear in spirit and strong in body; he treasured it deeply. At the end of Qingtai Gui was at Changshan when Mi Qiong's rebellion broke out. The medicine was in his clothes chest and was seized by bandits; he never recovered it. During Tianfu illness arose in his chest and burned like fire. Gui knew he would not recover and asked to return home; the court granted it. When he crossed the river he was overcome by vapors from the medicine, died on the road, and was buried at Shiyi, aged sixty-eight.

裴皞，字司東，系出中眷裴氏，世居河東為望族。 皞容止端秀，性卞急，剛直而無隱，少而好學，苦心文藝，雖遭亂離，手不釋卷。 唐光化三年，擢進士第，釋褐授校書郎，歷諫職。 梁初，當路推其文學，遷翰林學士、中書舍人。 唐莊宗時，擢為禮部侍郎，後以語觸當事，改太子賓客，旋授兵部尚書，以老致仕。 天福初，起為工部尚書，復告老，以右僕射致仕。 皞累知貢舉，稱得士，宰相馬裔孫、維翰皆其所取進士也。 後裔孫知貢舉，率新進士謁皞，皞喜，為詩曰：「詞場最重是持衡，天遣愚夫受盛名，三主禮闈年八十，門生門下見門生。 」當世榮之。 維翰嘗私見皞，皞不為迎送，人問之，皞曰：「我見桑公於中書，庶僚也； 今見我於私第，門生也。 」聞者以為允。 卒年八十五。 贈太子太保。
Pei Hao, courtesy name Sidong, came from the central branch of the Pei clan, a great family long settled in Hedong. Hao's bearing was refined and elegant; he was quick-tempered, upright, and frank. From youth he loved learning and labored at letters; though amid turmoil he never put down his books. In the third year of Tang Guanghua he passed the jinshi examination; upon leaving office dress he was made collator and served in remonstrance posts. At the start of Liang those in power prized his literary talent; he was made Hanlin academician and secretariat drafter. Under Zhuangzong of Tang he was made vice minister of rites; later his words offended those in power and he was made household companion to the heir apparent, then minister of war, and retired on account of age. At the start of Tianfu he was recalled as minister of works, again asked to retire on account of age, and left office as right vice director of the secretariat. Hao repeatedly oversaw the metropolitan examination and was said to have chosen worthy men; chief councillors Ma Yisun and Wei Han were both jinshi he had selected. Later Yisun oversaw the examination and led the new jinshi to visit Hao. Hao was pleased and wrote a poem: "In the literary arena the weightiest thing is holding the scales; Heaven sends this foolish man great fame—three times presiding over the Rites Gate, eighty years old, a disciple's disciple meets his disciple." His contemporaries took pride in it. Wei Han once paid a private visit to Hao, who did not come out to welcome or escort him. When asked, Hao said, "When I saw Lord Sang at the secretariat, he was a colleague; now he visits me at home—he is a disciple." Hearers judged this fair. He died aged eighty-five. He was posthumously made grand mentor to the heir apparent.
8
承範溫厚寡言，善希人旨，桑維翰、李崧尤重之，嘗薦於高祖，雲可大用。 承範知之，持重自養，雖遇盛夏，而猶服襦袴，加之以純綿，蓋慮有寒濕之患也。 然竟不獲其志，其命也夫！
Chengfan was gentle and reticent, adept at reading others' wishes. Sang Weihan and Li Song prized him highly and once recommended him to Gaozu as fit for great use. Chengfan knew this and kept himself reserved. Even in midsummer he wore jacket and trousers padded with cotton, lest cold and damp harm him. Yet he never attained his aims—such was his lot!

盧導，字熙化，其先范陽人也。 祖伯卿，唐殿中侍御史。 父如晦，國子監丞，贈戶部侍郎。 導少而儒雅，美詞翰，善談論。 唐天祐初，登進士第，釋褐除校書郎，由均州鄖鄉縣令、入為監察御史，三遷職方員外郎，充史館修撰，改河南縣令禮部郎中，賜紫，轉右司郎中兼侍御史知雜事。 以病免，閑居於漢上，久之。 天成中，以本官征還，拜右諫議大夫。 長興末，為中書舍人，權知貢舉。 明年春，潞王自鳳翔擁大軍赴闕，唐閔帝奔於衛州，宰相馮道、李愚集百官於天宮寺，將出迎潞王。 時軍眾離潰，人情奔駭，百官移時未有至者。 導與舍人張昭遠先至，馮道請導草勸進箋，導曰：「潞王入朝，郊迎可也； 若勸進之事，安可造次。 且潞王與主上，皆太后之子，或廢或立，當從教令，安得不稟策母後，率爾而行！ 」馮道曰：「凡事要務實，勸進其可已乎？ 」導曰：「今主上蒙塵在外，遽以大位勸人，若潞王守道，以忠義見責，未審何詞以對！ 不如率群臣詣宮門，取太后進止，即去就善矣。 」道未及對，會京城巡檢安從進報曰：「潞王至矣，安得百僚無班。 」即紛然而去。 是日，潞王未至，馮道等止於上陽門外，又令導草勸進箋，導執之如初。 李愚曰：「舍人之言是也，吾輩信罪人矣。 」導之守正也如是。 晉天福中，由禮部侍郎遷尚書右丞，判吏部尚書銓事，秩滿，拜吏部侍郎。 六年秋，卒於東京，時年七十六。
Lu Dao, courtesy name Xihua, came of Fanyang stock. His grandfather Boqing had been a palace attending censor under Tang. His father Ruhui had been assistant director of the imperial university and was posthumously made vice minister of revenue. From youth Dao was cultured and graceful, gifted in letters and skilled in conversation. Early in Tang Tianyou he passed the jinshi, became collator, then magistrate of Yunxiang in Junzhou, entered as investigating censor, rose thrice to vice director of appointments, served as historiographer, became magistrate of Henan and director of rites, received purple robes, and became director of the right secretariat while also attending censor in charge of miscellaneous cases. Illness forced his retirement, and he lived quietly on the Han for many years. During Tiancheng he was recalled and made right advisory censor. At the end of Changxing he was secretariat drafter and oversaw the metropolitan examination. The next spring the Prince of Lu marched from Fengxiang with a great army. Emperor Min of Tang fled to Weizhou. Feng Dao and Li Yu gathered officials at Tiangong Temple to go out and welcome him. Armies were scattered and the people panicked; for a long while no officials came. Dao and drafter Zhang Zhaoyuan arrived first. Feng Dao asked Dao to draft a memorial urging accession. Dao said, "When the Prince of Lu comes to court, a suburban welcome suffices; as for urging him to take the throne, how can we act rashly? Moreover, the prince and the emperor are both the empress dowager's sons. Deposing or enthroning should follow her command—how can we act without her word!" Feng Dao said, "In affairs one must be practical—can urging accession be set aside?" Dao said, "The emperor is in exile abroad; to rush the throne upon another—if the prince holds to loyalty and rebukes us, what could we answer! Better to lead the ministers to the palace gate, obtain the empress dowager's decision, and then act rightly." Before Dao could answer, capital patrol commissioner An Congjin reported that the Prince of Lu had arrived—how could the officials be without formation? They scattered at once and left. That day the prince had not yet arrived; Feng Dao and others waited outside the Shangyang Gate and again asked Dao to draft the accession memorial. Dao held firm as before. Li Yu said, "The drafter is right—we are truly guilty men." Such was Dao's steadfast integrity. During Jin Tianfu he rose from vice minister of rites to right vice director of the secretariat, heading personnel selection; when his term ended he became vice minister of personnel. In autumn of the sixth year he died in the eastern capital, aged seventy-six.

鄭韜光，字龍府，洛京清河人也。 曾祖絪，為唐宰相。 祖祗德，國子祭酒， 〈 （《新唐書·宰相世系表》：祗德，兵部尚書。）〉 贈太傅。 父顥，河南尹，贈太師。 其先世居滎陽，自隋、唐三百餘年，公卿輔相，蟬聯一門。 韜光，唐宣宗之外孫，萬壽公主之所出也，生三日，賜一子出身，銀章朱紱。 及長，美容止，神爽氣澈，不妄喜怒，秉執名節，為甲族所稱。 自京兆府參軍歷秘書郎、集賢校理、太常博士、虞部比部員外郎、司門戶部郎中、河南京兆少尹、太常少卿、諫議大夫、給事中。 梁貞明中，懇求休退，上表漏名，責授寧州司馬。 莊宗平梁，遷工、禮、刑部侍郎。 天成、長興中，歷尚書左右丞。 國初，以戶部尚書致仕。 自繈褓迄於懸車，凡事十一君，越七十載，所仕無官謗，無私過，三持使節，不辱君命，士無賢不肖，皆恭己接納。 晚年背傴，時人咸曰鄭傴不迂。 平生交友之中無怨隙，親族之間無愛憎，恬和自如，性尚平簡，及致政歸洛，甚愜終焉之志。 天福五年秋，寢疾而卒，年八十。 贈右僕射。
Zheng Taoguang, courtesy name Longfu, was from Qinghe in the Luoyang region. His great-grandfather Yin had been a Tang chief councillor. His grandfather Zhide had been rector of the imperial university, (Note: (New Book of Tang, "Tables of Chief Councillor Lineages": Zhide was minister of war.)] He was posthumously made grand tutor. His father Hao had been metropolitan governor of Henan and was posthumously made grand preceptor. His family had long lived at Xingyang; for more than three hundred years from Sui through Tang, ministers and chief councillors had followed one another in a single house. Taoguang was a grandson of Tang Xuanzong on the maternal side, born of Princess Wanshou. On the third day of life he was granted first-son entry into office, with silver seal and crimson sash. Grown, he had fine bearing and a clear, bright spirit; he did not show anger or joy without cause, held to honor, and was praised among the great families. He rose from aide in Jingzhao through secretary, collator in the Hall of Assembled Worthies, doctor of imperial sacrifices, vice directors in parks and comparative standards, directors in passes and household registration, vice governor of Henan and Jingzhao, vice director of imperial sacrifices, advisory censor, and drafting attendant. Under Liang Zhenming he earnestly sought retirement; his memorial omitted his name and he was demoted to aide at Ningzhou. When Zhuangzong overthrew Liang he was made vice minister of works, rites, and punishments. During Tiancheng and Changxing he served as left and right vice directors of the secretariat. At the founding of the dynasty he retired as minister of revenue. From infancy to retirement he served eleven rulers over more than seventy years without official censure or private fault. Thrice he bore diplomatic missions without disgracing his sovereign's charge, and received every man, worthy or not, with humble courtesy. In old age his back was bent; people said "Bent Zheng" was never stubborn. Among friends he left no rancor; among kin no favoritism or hatred. Tranquil and at ease, he loved plain simplicity, and when he retired to Luoyang he was deeply content to end his days there. In autumn of the fifth year of Tianfu he fell ill in bed and died, aged eighty. He was posthumously made right vice director of the secretariat.

王權，字秀山，太原人，積世衣冠。 曾祖起，官至左僕射、山南西道節度使，冊贈太尉，謚曰文懿，唐史有傳。 祖龜，浙東觀察使。 父蕘，右司員外郎。 權舉進士，解褐授秘書省校書郎、集賢校理，歷左拾遺、右補闕。 梁祖革命，御史司憲崔沂表為侍御史，遷兼職方員外郎知雜事。 歲餘，召入翰林為學士，在院加戶部郎中、知制誥，歷左諫議大夫、給事中，充集賢殿學士判院事，俄拜御史中丞。 唐莊宗平梁，以例出為隨州司馬，會赦，量移許州。 月餘，入為右庶子，遷戶兵吏三侍郎、尚書左丞、禮部尚書判銓。 清泰中，權知貢舉，改戶部尚書，華資美級，罕不由之。 高祖登極，轉兵部尚書。 天福中，命權使於契丹，權以前世累為將相，未嘗有奉使而稱陪臣者，謂人曰：「我雖不才，年今耄矣，豈能遠使於契丹乎！ 違詔得罪，亦所甘心。 」由是停任。 先是，宰相馮道使於契丹才回，權亦自鳳翔冊禮使回，故責詞略曰：「若以道路迢遙，即鸞閣之臺臣亦往； 若以筋骸衰減，即鳳翔之冊使才回。 既黷憲章，須從殿黜」云。 其實權不欲臣事契丹，故堅辭之，非避事以違命也。 逾歲授太子少傅致仕。 六年秋，以疾卒，年七十八。 贈左僕射。
Wang Quan, courtesy name Xiushan, was from Taiyuan, a family of officials for generations. His great-grandfather Qi rose to left vice director of the secretariat and military governor of Shannan West; posthumously made grand marshal with the posthumous title Wényì; the Tang histories record him. His grandfather Gui had been observation commissioner of Zhedong. His father Rao had been vice director of the right secretariat. Quan passed the jinshi, became collator in the secretariat and collator in the Hall of Assembled Worthies, and served as left reminder and right remonstrator. When Liang's founder changed the dynasty, censor-in-chief Cui Yi recommended him as attending censor; he was then made vice director of appointments in charge of miscellaneous cases. After a year he entered the Hanlin as academician, added director of household registration and charge of drafting edicts, served as left advisory censor and drafting attendant, headed the Hall of Assembled Worthies academy, and soon became censor-in-chief. When Zhuangzong of Tang overthrew Liang he was demoted by precedent to aide at Suizhou; after an amnesty he was transferred in reduced rank to Xuzhou. After a month he became right guardian of the heir apparent, then vice minister of revenue, war, and personnel, left vice director of the secretariat, and minister of rites heading selection. During Qingtai he oversaw the metropolitan examination, then became minister of revenue. Splendid posts and fine ranks rarely escaped his hand. When Gaozu took the throne he was made minister of war. During Tianfu he was ordered envoy to the Khitan. Quan, whose forebears for generations had been generals and chief ministers, had never known a mission in which one styled oneself a subordinate minister. He said, "Though I lack talent, I am old now—how can I go on a distant mission to the Khitan! To disobey the edict and accept punishment is what I would willingly bear." For this his appointment was halted. Earlier Feng Dao had just returned from a Khitan mission, and Quan had just returned from Fengxiang as investiture envoy. The reprimand in brief said, "If the road is far, terrace ministers of the Phoenix Pavilion have also gone; if one's frame is weakened, the investiture envoy from Fengxiang has just returned. Having violated the statutes, dismissal from court is required." In truth Quan did not wish to serve the Khitan as a subordinate minister and firmly declined; it was not evading duty to disobey orders. After a year he was made junior tutor to the heir apparent and retired. In autumn of the sixth year he died of illness, aged seventy-eight. He was posthumously made left vice director of the secretariat.

韓惲，字子重，太原晉陽人。 曾祖俊，唐龍武大將軍。 祖士則，石州司馬。 父逵，代州刺史。 惲世仕太原，昆仲為軍職，惟惲親狎儒士，好為歌詩，聚書數千卷。 乾寧中，後唐莊宗納其妹為妃，初為嫡室，故莊宗深禮其家，而惲以文學署交城、文水令，入為太原少尹。 莊宗平定趙、魏，為魏州支使。 莊宗即位，授右散騎常侍，從駕至洛陽，轉尚書戶部侍郎。 天成初，改秘書監。 俄而馮道為丞相，與惲有同幕之舊，以惲性謹厚，尤左右之，尋遷禮部尚書。 丁內憂，服闋，授戶部尚書。 明宗晏駕，馮道為山陵使，引惲為副使。 清泰初，以充奉之勞，授檢校尚書右僕射、絳州刺史，逾年入為太子賓客。 高祖登極，以惲先朝懿戚，深加禮遇，除授貝州刺史。 時範延光有跋扈之狀，惲懼其見逼，遲留不敢赴任，高祖不悅，復授太子賓客，尋改兵部尚書。 天福七年夏，車駕在鄴，惲病腳氣，卒於龍興寺，時年六十餘。
Han Yun, courtesy name Zizhong, was from Jinyang in Taiyuan. His great-grandfather Jun had been general of the dragon martial guard under Tang. His grandfather Shize had been military governor's aide at Shizhou. His father Kui had been prefect of Daizhou. The Hans for generations served at Taiyuan; his brothers held military posts, but Yun alone was close to scholars, loved poetry, and collected thousands of books. During Qianning Zhuangzong of Later Tang took Yun's younger sister as consort; she was first principal wife, so Zhuangzong deeply honored the family. Yun was appointed by literary merit magistrate of Jiaocheng and Wenshui, then vice governor of Taiyuan. When Zhuangzong pacified Zhao and Wei, Yun served as branch commissioner at Weizhou. When Zhuangzong took the throne Yun was made right regular attendant, followed the court to Luoyang, and became vice minister of revenue. At the start of Tiancheng he was made director of the palace library. Soon Feng Dao became chief councillor; he and Yun had served on the same staff. Feng especially favored Yun for his careful, sincere nature, and soon made him minister of rites. After mourning for his mother he was made minister of revenue. When Mingzong died Feng Dao headed the tomb commission and made Yun his deputy. At the start of Qingtai, for his funeral labors he was made supernumerary right vice director of the secretariat and prefect of Jiangzhou; after a year he became household companion to the heir apparent. When Gaozu took the throne he treated Yun, a close kinsman of the former court, with deep courtesy and made him prefect of Beizhou. Fan Yanguang then showed an overbearing manner; Yun feared pressure and delayed, not daring to take up his post. Gaozu was displeased, made him household companion to the heir apparent again, then minister of war. In summer of the seventh year of Tianfu, while the court was at Ye, Yun fell ill with foot ailment and died at Longxing Temple, aged somewhat over sixty.

李懌，京兆人也。 祖褒，唐黔南觀察使。 父昭，戶部尚書。 懌幼而能文，進士擢第，解褐為校書郎、集賢校理、清河尉。 入梁，歷監察御史、右補闕、殿中侍御史、起居舍人、禮部員外郎、知制誥，換都官郎中，賜緋，召入翰林為學士，正拜舍人，賜金紫，仍舊內職。 莊宗平汴、洛，責授懷州司馬，遇赦，量移孟州，入為衛尉少卿。 天成初，復拜中書舍人，充翰林學士，在職轉戶部侍郎右丞，充承旨。 時常侍張文寶知貢舉，中書奏落進士數人，仍請詔翰林學士院作一詩一賦，下禮部，為舉人格樣。 學士竇夢徵、張礪輩撰格詩格賦各一，送中書，宰相未以為允。 夢徵等請懌為之，懌笑而答曰：「李懌識字有數，頃歲因人偶得及第，敢與後生髦俊為之標格！ 假令今卻稱進士，就春官求試，落第必矣。 格賦格詩，不敢應詔。 」君子多其識大體。 天福中，自工部尚書轉太常卿，歷禮部、刑部二尚書，以多病留司於洛下，不交人事。 開運未，遇契丹入洛，家事罄空，尋以疾卒，年七十餘。
Li Yi was from Jingzhao. His grandfather Bao had been observation commissioner of Qiannan under Tang. His father Zhao had been minister of revenue. Yi wrote well from youth, passed the jinshi, and upon leaving office dress became collator, collator in the Hall of Assembled Worthies, and assistant magistrate of Qinghe. Under Liang he served as investigating censor, right remonstrator, palace attending censor, recorder of the sovereign's movements, vice director of rites, and drafter of edicts; became director of the capital bureau, received crimson robes, entered the Hanlin as academician, was formally made drafter, received gold seal and purple robes, and kept his inner-court post. When Zhuangzong pacified Bian and Luoyang Yi was demoted to aide at Huaizhou; after an amnesty he was transferred in reduced rank to Mengzhou, then became vice director of the imperial stud. At the start of Tiancheng he was again made secretariat drafter and Hanlin academician; while in office he became vice minister of revenue and right vice director, and served as drafting commissioner. Regular attendant Zhang Wenbao then oversaw the metropolitan examination. The secretariat memorialized to fail several jinshi and asked that the Hanlin draft one regulated poem and one fu for the Ministry of Rites as models for candidates. Academicians Dou Mengzheng, Zhang Li, and others each drafted one model poem and one model fu and sent them to the secretariat; the chief councillors did not approve. They asked Yi to do it. Yi smiled and said, "Li Yi knows only so many characters; I passed the examination a few years ago by chance through others' favor—how dare I set the standard for the brilliant young men of today! If I were called a jinshi today and went to the Ministry of Rites to sit the examination, I would certainly fail." As for model fu and model verse, I dare not obey the edict." Men of virtue widely praised his sense of the larger principle. During Tianfu he moved from minister of works to director of imperial sacrifices, served as minister of rites and minister of punishments, and because of frequent illness remained on duty in Luoyang without engaging in worldly affairs. At the end of Kaiyun, when the Khitan entered Luoyang, his household was stripped bare; soon he died of illness, aged somewhat over seventy.
