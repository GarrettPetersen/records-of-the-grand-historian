李崧，深州饒陽人。 父舜卿，本州錄事參軍。 崧幼而聰敏，十餘歲為文，家人奇之。 弱冠，本府署為參軍。 其父嘗謂宗人李鏻曰：「大醜生處，形奇氣異，前途應不居徒勞之地，賴吾兄誨激之。 」大醜即崧之小字也。 同光初，魏王繼岌為興聖宮使，兼領鎮州節鉞，崧以參軍從事。 時推官李蕘掌書，崧見其起草不工，密謂掌事呂柔曰：「令公皇子，天下瞻望，至於尺牘往來，章表論列，稍須文理合宜。 李侍御起草，未能盡善。 」呂曰：「公試代為之。 」呂得崧所作，示盧質、馮道，皆稱之。 繇是擢為興聖宮巡官，獨掌奏記。 莊宗入洛，授太常寺協律郎。 王師伐蜀，繼岌為都統，以崧掌書記。 蜀平，樞密使郭崇韜為宦官誣構，繼岌遂殺崇韜父子，外尚未知。 崧白繼岌曰：「王何為作此危事？ 至於不容崇韜，至洛誅之未晚。 今懸軍五千里，無咫尺書詔，便殺重臣，非謀也。 」繼岌曰：「吾亦悔之。 」崧召書吏三四人，登樓去梯，取黃紙矯寫詔書，倒使都統印發之。 翌日，告諸軍，軍情稍定。 及自蜀還，明宗革命，任圜以宰相判三司，用崧為鹽鐵推官，賜緋。 丁內艱，歸鄉里。 服闋，鎮帥範延光奏署掌書記。 延光為樞密使，拜拾遺，直樞密院，遷補闕、起居郎、尚書郎，充職如故。 長興末，改翰林學士。 清泰初，拜端明殿學士、戶部侍郎。
Li Song was from Raoyang in Shen Prefecture. His father, Shunqing, served as the prefectural registrar. Song was bright as a boy, and by his early teens he could already compose prose, to his family's astonishment. When he reached manhood, the prefectural command appointed him as adjutant. His father once told his kinsman Li Lin, "Big Ugly was born under an odd star—his bearing is unusual and his spirit distinctive. He is not meant to waste his life in drudgery; he will need my elder brother's teaching to drive him forward." " Big Ugly was Song's childhood name. Early in the Tongguang reign, Prince Jiji of Wei served as commissioner of the Xingsheng Palace while also holding the Zhenzhou command; Song accompanied him as adjutant. The investigating censor Li Rao was then in charge of correspondence. Song found his drafts clumsy and quietly told the steward Lu Rou, "The Prince is the Emperor's son—the whole realm watches him. Every note that goes out and every memorial he submits ought to read with some polish and propriety." Censor Li's drafts are not quite up to the mark. " Lu replied, "Why don't you try drafting them yourself?" " Lu took Song's drafts to Lu Zhi and Feng Dao, and both praised them highly. Song was then promoted to touring officer of the Xingsheng Palace and given sole charge of memorials. After Emperor Zhuangzong took Luoyang, Song was appointed harmonizing officer in the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. When the imperial forces marched on Shu, Jiji served as overall commander and Song became his chief secretary. After Shu fell, eunuchs framed Commissioner Guo Chongtao, and Jiji executed Chongtao and his son before word reached the outside world. Song said to Jiji, "Your Highness, why commit such a reckless act?" Even if Chongtao could not be tolerated, it would not be too late to execute him once you reached Luoyang. You have an army stranded five thousand li from home, with not a line of imperial writ to authorize it, and you kill a great minister—that is no strategy at all. " Jiji said, "I regret it as well." " Song called three or four clerks upstairs, had the ladder pulled away, drafted an edict on yellow paper under the Prince's name, and issued it with the overall commander's seal impressed in reverse. The next day he announced the edict to the troops, and morale gradually steadied. After the Shu campaign, when Emperor Mingzong seized the throne, Chancellor Ren Huan, who oversaw the Three Commissions, appointed Song salt-and-iron investigating officer and granted him scarlet robes. He went into mourning for his mother and returned home. After the mourning period, Military Governor Fan Yanguang recommended him as chief secretary. When Fan Yanguang became commissioner of the Bureau of Military Affairs, Song was appointed remonstrance official and attached to the Bureau. He rose through remonstrance supplementer, attendant of the bedchamber, and secretariat officer while keeping his secretarial post. Late in the Changxing reign he was made Hanlin academician. Early in the Qingtai reign he was made Duanming Hall academician and Vice Minister of Revenue.

先是，長興三年冬，契丹入雲中，朝廷欲命重將鎮太原。 時晉祖為六軍副使，以秦王從榮不軌，懇求外任，深有北門之望，而大臣以晉高祖方權兵柄，難以議之。 一日，明宗怒其未奏，範延光、趙延壽等無對，退歸本院，共議其事，方欲以康義誠為之。 時崧最在下位，聳立請曰：「朝廷重兵多在北邊，須以重臣為帥，以某所見，非石太尉不可也。 」會明宗令中使促之，眾乃從其議。 翌日，晉祖既受太原之命，使心腹達意於崧云：「壘浮圖須與合卻尖。 」蓋感之深也。 及清泰末，晉祖入洛，崧與呂琦俱竄匿於伊闕民家。 旬日，晉高祖召為戶部侍郎，判戶部。 逾月，拜中書侍郎、同平章事，與桑維翰並兼樞密使。 維翰鎮相州，未幾，廢樞密院，事歸中書，加尚書右僕射。 從幸鄴，丁外艱，恩制起復，崧上章數四，懇辭其命，優詔不允。 復上章，不報，崧不得已而視事。 晉少帝嗣位，復用桑維翰為樞密使，命崧兼判三司。 未幾，代維翰為樞密使，與馮玉對掌機密。 開運末，崧、玉信契丹之詐，經略瀛、鄭，中渡之敗，落其奸謀。 契丹入京師，趙延壽、張礪素稱崧之才，契丹主善遇之，以崧為太子太師，充樞密使。 契丹主嘗謂左右曰：「我破南朝，只得李崧一人而已。 」從契丹北行，留於鎮州。
Earlier, in the winter of the third year of Changxing, the Khitan invaded Yunzhong, and the court sought a senior general to hold Taiyuan. The future Jin Founder was then deputy commander of the Six Armies. With Prince Congrong of Qin plotting rebellion, he urgently sought a post away from court and clearly coveted command of Taiyuan, but the senior ministers hesitated to propose him while he already wielded so much military authority. One day the Emperor grew angry that no recommendation had been submitted. Fan Yanguang, Zhao Yanshou, and the others had no answer. They withdrew to the Bureau of Military Affairs to deliberate and were on the verge of naming Kang Yicheng. Song, then the junior man present, rose and said, "Most of the empire's main forces lie on the northern frontier. We need a senior minister as commander—and in my view only Grand Marshal Shi will do." " At that moment the Emperor sent a palace envoy to hurry them along, and the group adopted Song's recommendation. The next day, after receiving the Taiyuan command, the Jin Founder sent a confidant to tell Song, "When you raise a pagoda, the capstone must be set in place."—meaning Song had completed what he had begun. " He was profoundly grateful. Late in Qingtai, when the Jin Founder marched into Luoyang, Song and Lü Qi went into hiding in a farmhouse at Yique. Within ten days Emperor Gaozu of Jin recalled him as Vice Minister of Revenue with charge of the ministry. A month later he was made Vice Director of the Secretariat and Grand Councillor, sharing with Sang Weihan the concurrent post of military affairs commissioner. Weihan took command at Xiangzhou. Soon afterward the Bureau of Military Affairs was abolished and its duties returned to the Secretariat, and Song was further promoted to Right Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs. While accompanying the court at Ye he mourned his father. An edict recalled him to office, but Song submitted several memorials pleading to be excused; gracious replies refused him. He memorialized again without response and at last resumed office against his will. After the Young Emperor of Jin succeeded, Sang Weihan was restored as military affairs commissioner and Song was ordered to oversee the Three Commissions as well. Soon he replaced Weihan as commissioner and shared control of state secrets with Feng Yu. Late in Kaiyun, Song and Yu fell for Khitan deception in their operations around Ying and Zheng; the rout at Zhongdu laid bare the enemy's plot. When the Khitan took the capital, Zhao Yanshou and Zhang Li, who had long praised Song's ability, secured him favorable treatment. The Khitan ruler made him Grand Preceptor of the Heir Apparent and military affairs commissioner. The Khitan ruler once told his attendants, "In conquering the Southern Court I gained only one man worth having—Li Song." " When the Khitan withdrew north, Song was left behind at Zhenzhou.

高祖平汴、洛，乃以崧之居第賜蘇逢吉，第中宿藏之物，皆為逢吉所有。 是年秋，鎮州逐麻答，崧與馮道、和凝十數人歸闕，授太子太傅。 崧對朝之權右，謙挹承顏，未嘗忤旨。 嘗以宅券獻蘇逢吉，不悅。 崧二弟嶼、㠖，酣酒無識，與楊邠、蘇逢吉子弟杯酒之間，時言及奪我居第，逢吉知之。 〈 （《宋史·陶蒨傳》：李崧以宅券獻逢吉，逢吉不悅，而崧子弟數出怨言，崧懼，移疾不出。 崧族子昉，嘗往候崧，崧語昉曰：「邇來朝廷於我有何議？ 」昉曰：「無他，聞唯陶給事往往於稠人中厚誣叔父。 」崧嘆曰：「蒨自單州判官，吾取為集賢校理，不數年擢掌詔命，吾何負於陶氏子哉！ 」及崧遇禍，昉嘗因公事詣蒨，蒨問昉：「識李侍郎否？ 」昉斂衽應曰：「遠從叔耳。 」蒨曰：「李氏之禍，蒨出力焉。 」昉聞之汗出。）〉 有部曲葛延遇者，逋李嶼船傭，嶼撻之，督其所負，遇有同輩李澄亦事逢吉，葛延遇夜寄宿於澄家，以嶼見督情告，遂一夕同謀告變。 逢吉覽狀示史宏肇，其日逢吉遣吏召崧至第，從容語及葛延遇告變之事，崧以幼女為托，逢吉遣吏送於侍衛獄。 既行，崧恚曰：「自古未有不亡之國，不死之人。 」及為吏所鞫，乃自誣伏罪，舉家遇害，少長悉屍於市，人士冤之。 〈 （《東都事略·王溥傳》：世宗嘗問：「漢相李崧蠟彈書結契丹，有記其詞者否？ 」溥曰：「崧有此，肯示人耶？ 蘇逢吉輩陷之爾。 」世宗遂優贈崧官。）〉 崧與徐臺符同學相善，乾祐三年秋，臺符夢崧謂曰：「予之冤橫，得請於帝矣。 」及蘇、史之誅，並梟首於市，當崧所誅之地。 未幾，葛延遇、李澄亦以戮死。 〈 （《宋史·李昉傳》：晉侍中崧，與昉同宗且同里，時人謂崧為「東李家」，昉為「西李」。 漢末，崧被誅，至宋，其子璨自蘇州常熟縣令赴調，昉為訟其父冤，且言周太祖已為昭雪，贈官，還其田宅，錄璨而官之。 然璨幾五十，尚淹州縣之職。 詔授璨著作佐郎，後官至資善大夫。）〉
After Gaozu secured Bianliang and Luoyang, he gave Song's mansion to Su Fengji, along with everything Song had stored there. That autumn Zhenzhou expelled the Khitan governor Maduo. Song returned to court with Feng Dao, He Ning, and a dozen others and was made Grand Preceptor of the Heir Apparent. Before the powerful at court he was humble and obliging and never crossed the Emperor. He once offered Su Fengji the deed to his former house, to Fengji's displeasure. Song's younger brothers Yu and [㠖] were drunken and foolish. Over wine with the sons of Yang Bin and Su Fengji they would complain that their home had been taken, and Fengji heard of it. ( (Song History, Biography of Tao Qian: Li Song offered Fengji the deed to his house. Fengji took offense, and Song's kin repeatedly voiced grievances. Song grew afraid, pleaded illness, and stopped attending court. Song's nephew Fang once called on him. Song asked, "What is the court saying about me these days?" " Fang replied, "Nothing else—only that Attendant Tao Qian keeps slandering you loudly in public gatherings." " Song sighed, "Qian was only a prefectural judge when I brought him into the Hall of Assembled Worthies and within a few years put him in charge of edicts. What did I ever do to deserve this from Tao's protégé?" " After Song's fall, Fang once visited Qian on official business. Qian asked, "Do you know Vice Director Li?" " Fang straightened his robes and said, "He is my distant uncle." " Qian said, "I had a hand in the Li family's ruin." " Fang broke into a cold sweat on hearing this.)) A retainer named Ge Yanyu owed Li Yu money for boat hire. Yu beat him and demanded payment. Yanyu had a fellow named Li Cheng who also worked for Fengji. One night Yanyu stayed at Cheng's house and told him how Yu had hounded him. That night the two conspired to lodge a treason accusation. Fengji reviewed the accusation and showed it to Shi Hongzhao. That day he summoned Song to his mansion and calmly mentioned Ge Yanyu's charge. Song begged him to look after his young daughter. Fengji had him sent to the guard prison. As he was led away, Song said bitterly, "Since antiquity no state has endured forever, and no man has escaped death." " Under interrogation he confessed to crimes he had not committed. His entire family was executed, bodies of every age left in the marketplace, and scholars across the realm regarded it as a gross injustice. ( (Eastern Capital Epitome, Biography of Wang Pu: Emperor Shizong once asked whether there was any record of the wording of a wax-sealed letter in which Han Chancellor Li Song had sought an alliance with the Khitan. " Pu replied, "If Song had done such a thing, would he have let anyone see it?" Su Fengji and his faction framed him—that is all. " The Emperor then posthumously restored Song's honors.)) Song and Xu Taifu had been classmates and close friends. In the autumn of the third year of Qianyou, Taifu dreamed that Song told him, "My grievous wrong has been heard in Heaven." " When Su and Shi were put to death, their heads were displayed in the marketplace at the very spot where Song had been executed. Soon afterward Ge Yanyu and Li Cheng were executed as well. ( (Song History, Biography of Li Fang: Jin Attendant-in-Waiting Song and Fang were kinsmen from the same hometown. People called Song's branch the Eastern Li and Fang's the Western Li. At the end of Later Han, Song was executed. Under the Song, his son Can traveled from his post as magistrate of Changshu in Suzhou to take up a new appointment. Fang pleaded his kinsman's innocence, noting that Taizu of Zhou had already cleared Song's name, restored his offices posthumously, returned his property, and secured an appointment for Can. Yet Can was nearly fifty and still languished in local posts. An edict made Can Assistant Editorial Director, and he eventually rose to Grand Master of the Palace for Venerating Goodness.))

蘇逢吉，長安人。 父悅，逢吉母早喪，而悅鰥居，旁無侍者。 性嗜酒，雖所飲不多，然漱醪終日。 他人供膳，皆不稱旨，俟逢吉庖炙，方肯下箸。 悅初仕蜀，官升朝列，逢吉初學為文，嘗代父染翰。 悅嘗為高祖從事，甚見禮遇，因從容薦逢吉曰：「老夫耄矣，才器無取。 男逢吉粗學援毫，性復恭恪，如公不以犭屯犬之微，願令事左右。 」高祖召見，以神精爽惠，甚憐之。 有頃，擢為賓佐，凡有謀議，立侍其側。 高祖素嚴毅，及鎮太原，位望崇重，從事稀得謁見，惟逢吉日侍左右。 兩使文簿，堆案盈幾，左右不敢輒通，逢吉置於懷袖，俟其悅色則諮之，多見其可。
Su Fengji was from Chang'an. His father Yue had lost Fengji's mother early and lived alone as a widower without anyone to wait on him. He loved wine. Though he drank little at a sitting, he kept a cup at his lips from morning to night. Food prepared by anyone else never suited him. He would eat only after Fengji had cooked. Yue had begun his career in Shu and risen to court rank. Fengji was learning to write and sometimes drafted documents for his father. Yue had served Gaozu as an aide and was treated with great respect. He took occasion to recommend Fengji, saying, "I am old now and have no talent worth mentioning." My son Fengji has some schooling in letters and a respectful disposition. If Your Lordship would not disdain this humble man's slight gift, I beg you to let him serve at your side. " Gaozu received him and, finding him quick-witted and alert, took a strong liking to him. Soon he was promoted to staff adviser and stood at Gaozu's side whenever plans were discussed. Gaozu was stern by nature. Once he held Taiyuan his stature was immense, and staff members seldom gained an audience—only Fengji attended him every day. Memorials from both commissioners piled the desks high, and attendants dared not pass them through. Fengji tucked them into his robe and waited until Gaozu was in good humor before raising them. Most were approved.

高祖建號於太原，逢吉自節度判官拜同平章事、集賢殿大學士。 車駕至汴，朝廷百司庶務，逢吉以為己任，參決處置，並出胸臆，雖有當有否，而事無留滯。 會翰林學士李濤從容侍帝，言及霸府二相，官秩未崇，逢吉旋加吏部尚書，未幾，轉左僕射，監修國史。 從征杜重威於鄴下，數乘醉抵辱周太祖。 及高祖大漸，與楊邠、史宏肇等臥內同受顧命。 李濤與逢吉論甥舅之契，相得甚歡，濤之入相，逢吉甚有力焉。 會濤上章，請出兩樞密為方鎮，帝怒，罷濤相，勒歸私第，時論疑濤承逢吉之風旨。 先是，高祖踐阼之後，逢吉與蘇禹珪俱在中書，有所除拜，多違舊制，用舍升降，率意任情，至有自白丁而升宦路、由流外而除令錄者，不可勝數。 物論紛然。 高祖方倚信二相，莫敢言者。 逢吉尤貪財貨，無所顧避，求進之士，稍有物力者，即遣人微露風旨，許以美秩。 及楊邠為相，稍奪二蘇之權，自是盡斂手而已。 邠每懲二蘇之失，艱於除拜，至於諸司補吏，與門胄出身，一切停罷。 時論以邠之蔽，固亦由逢吉、禹珪本不能至公於物之所致也。 初，高祖至汴，以故相馮道、李崧為契丹所俘，佇於真定，乃以崧第賜逢吉，道第賜禹珪，崧於西洛有別業，亦為逢吉所有。 及真定逐契丹，崧、道歸朝，崧弟嶼以逢吉占據其第，時出怨言。 未幾，崧以西京宅券獻於逢吉，不悅。 會崧有仆夫欲誣告謀反，逢吉誘致其狀，即告史宏肇，令逮捕其家。 逢吉遣直省吏召崧至第，即令監至侍衛獄。 翌日，所司以獄辭上，其李嶼款招云：「與兄崧、弟鳷，與家僮二十人商議，比至山陵發引之時，同放火謀亂，其告是實。 」蓋自誣之辭也。 逢吉仍以筆添註「二十人」字為「五十人」，封下有司，盡誅崧家。 時人冤之，歸咎於逢吉。
When Gaozu proclaimed his reign at Taiyuan, Fengji rose from military commissioner judge to Grand Councillor and Grand Academician of the Hall of Assembled Worthies. When the court reached Bianliang, Fengji took charge of the myriad affairs of government. Decisions poured from his own judgment. Some were wise and some were not, but nothing sat idle on his desk. Hanlin Academician Li Tao remarked in passing that the two chancellors from Gaozu's former headquarters still held modest ranks. Fengji was promptly made Minister of Personnel, then soon Left Vice Director with charge of compiling the national history. On the campaign against Du Chongwei at Ye, he repeatedly insulted the future Zhou Founder while drunk. When Gaozu lay dying, he received the final imperial charge alongside Yang Bin, Shi Hongzhao, and others at the bedside. Li Tao and Fengji were related by marriage and were on excellent terms. Fengji had done much to secure Tao's appointment as chancellor. When Tao memorialized asking that the two military commissioners be sent out as regional governors, the Emperor was furious, stripped him of the chancellorship, and sent him home. Many suspected the memorial reflected Fengji's wishes. Earlier, after Gaozu took the throne, Fengji and Su Yugui controlled appointments from the Secretariat. They routinely ignored precedent, promoting and demoting at whim. Commoners were elevated to office and men from irregular backgrounds were made magistrates and registrars beyond counting. Public opinion was in an uproar. Gaozu still trusted the two chancellors completely, and no one dared object. Fengji was especially greedy and shameless about it. Any office seeker with even modest wealth would receive a subtle hint that a handsome post could be arranged. When Yang Bin became chancellor he gradually stripped the two Su ministers of power, and from then on they kept their hands to themselves. Yang Bin repeatedly rebuked the two Su ministers and made appointments difficult. Clerical posts in every office and promotions through family connections were halted entirely. Public opinion blamed Yang Bin's narrowness, but it also reflected that Fengji and Yugui had never been fair in the first place. When Gaozu first reached Bianliang, former chancellors Feng Dao and Li Song were still Khitan captives at Zhending. Song's mansion was given to Fengji and Dao's to Yugui, and Song's estate in western Luoyang went to Fengji as well. When Zhending expelled the Khitan, Song and Dao returned. Song's brother Yu complained that Fengji had seized their home. Soon Song offered Fengji the deed to his Luoyang property, to Fengji's displeasure. A servant of Song's tried to frame him for treason. Fengji coaxed out a written accusation, reported it to Shi Hongzhao, and ordered Song's family arrested. Fengji sent a clerk to summon Song to his mansion and had him taken straight to the guard prison. The next day the court received the confession. Li Yu stated that he, his brother Song, his brother Zhi, and twenty servants had plotted to set fires and rebel when the imperial funeral procession departed. " It was plainly a forced confession. Fengji altered "twenty men" to "fifty men" in the document, sealed it, and sent it down. Song's entire family was executed. The public regarded it as a gross injustice and blamed Fengji.

Early in Later Jin's Tianfu era, noting how often the court proclaimed general amnesties, Yun submitted a "Refutation of Amnesty," citing Guanzi: "Amnesties bring small gains and great harm; in time their harm cannot be overcome." "Without amnesties there is small harm and great good; in time their good cannot be overcome." The Han Annals also records that when Wu Han lay dying, the Emperor asked his final counsel. He replied, "I only wish Your Majesty would never proclaim amnesties." " Why is that?" Because amnesty is not true grace, and withholding amnesty is not cruelty—it is simply punishing the guilty. I observe that since antiquity, whenever flood or drought struck, emperors issued gracious edicts, pardoned offenders, and opened prisons hoping to move Heaven and end the disaster. This is mistaken. Suppose two men go to law, one guilty and one innocent. If the guilty is pardoned, the innocent is wronged. Why favor the guilty and slight the innocent? That is a path to disaster, not a way to end it. From then on commoners rejoice at disasters and urge one another to crime, saying the state loves amnesties and will surely pardon them to end the calamity. Thus the state teaches the people to do evil. Heaven blesses the good and punishes the wicked. To pardon the wicked and call it ending disaster is to make Heaven aid evildoers. Considered closely, that cannot be so. If Heaven sends disaster, it is likely to warn the ruler. Restrain desire, practice thrift, pity widows and orphans, enforce justice, pardon no guilty man rashly, kill no innocent man, let virtue spread below and sagely fame rise above—then even flood and drought will not become calamity. How can rash pardons of the guilty save anyone from disaster? Or display their virtue? Thus it is clear that amnesties must not be practiced! " The Emperor read it with approval, issued an edict praising him, and sent the essay to the History Office.

Ren Yanhao was from Bing Prefecture. He practiced divination and the reading of celestial signs. When Gaozu of Jin was besieged at Taiyuan, Yanhao was among his closest confidants. Yanhao sought audience through his craft and was treated with great honor. Early in Tianfu, Yanhao was made a Taiyuan clerk, then magistrate of Jiaocheng and Wenshui, all through Gaozu's patronage. When Gaozu held Taiyuan, Yanhao spoke constantly of outside affairs and came and went freely. Everyone around Gaozu feared him. At Wenshui he extorted wealth and bribes. When the people tried to complain, Yanhao learned of it. One day he preemptively accused the county clerks of rallying the people to raid the treasury. Gaozu was enraged and sent cavalry who seized a dozen townspeople and executed their families. The cries of the wronged echoed along the roads. After Gaozu took the throne, Yanhao rose to Director of the Palace Bureau. Arrogant with favor, he inspired fear and treated even chancellors with contempt. Liu Chong in Hedong gnashed his teeth at him daily. When Prince Chengxun of Wei died and was buried at Taiyuan, Yanhao chose the site. A monk told Liu Chong, "The site is ill-omened. There may be further mourning." " Soon Gaozu died. Chong reported the monk's words and had Yanhao exiled to Lin Prefecture. Passing through Wenshui, townspeople pelted him with tiles and cursed him. Officials barely saved him from the mob. Once he reached exile, Liu Chong had him killed and his property confiscated.
