顯德三年春正月乙未朔，帝不受朝賀。 前司空蘇禹珪卒。 丁酉，李穀奏，破淮賊於上窯。 戊戌，發丁夫十萬城京師羅城。 庚子，詔取此月八日幸淮南。 殿中監馬從赟免所居官，坐幹沒外孫女霍氏之資產，為人所訟故也。 辛丑，以宣徽南院使向訓為權東京留守，以端明殿學士王樸為副留守。 壬寅，車駕發京師，丁未，李穀奏，自壽州引軍退守正陽。 辛亥，李重進奏，大破淮賊於正陽，斬首二萬餘級，伏屍三十里，臨陣斬賊大將劉彥貞，生擒偏將鹹師朗已下，獲戎甲三十萬副、馬五百匹。 先是，李穀駐軍於壽春城下，以攻其城，既而淮南援軍大至，乃與將佐謀曰：「賊軍舟棹將及正陽，我師無水戰之備，萬一橋梁不守，則大軍隔絕矣，不如全師退守正陽浮橋，以俟鑾輅。 」諸將皆以為然，遂燔其糧草而退。 軍回之際無復嚴整，公私之間頗多亡失，淮北役夫亦有陷於賊境者。 帝聞之，急詔侍衛都指揮使李重進率師赴之。 時淮賊乘李穀退軍之勢，發戰棹數百艘沿淮而上，且張斷橋之勢，彥貞以大軍列陣而進。 李重進既至正陽，聞淮軍在近，率諸將渡橋而進，與賊軍遇，重進等合勢擊之，一鼓而敗之。 〈 （馬令《南唐書》：世宗親征，行至圉鎮，聞蒨軍卻，意唐兵必追之，遣李重進急趨正陽，曰：「唐兵且至，宜急擊之。 」彥貞等聞蒨退軍，皆以為怯，裨將鹹師朗曰：「追之可大獲。 」劉仁贍使人喻之曰：「君來赴援，未交戰而敵人退，不可測也，慎勿追逐。 君為大將，安危以之，脫有不利，大事去矣。 」前軍張全約亦曰：「不可追。 」彥貞曰：「軍容在我，汝輩何知？ 沮吾事者斬！ 」比至正陽，而重進先至，未及食而戰。 彥貞施利刃於拒馬，又刻木為獸，號捷馬牌，以皮囊布鐵蒺藜於地。 周兵見而知其怯，一鼓而敗之，彥貞死於陣。）〉 殺獲之外，降者三千餘人，皆為我將趙晁所殺。 甲寅，車駕至正陽。 以侍衛都指揮使李重進為淮南道行營都招討使，命宰臣李穀判壽州行府事。 乙卯，車駕渡淮。 丙辰，至壽州城下，營於州西北淝水之陽，詔移正陽浮橋於下蔡。 庚申，耀兵於城下。 〈 （《春明退朝錄》云：家有《範魯公雜錄》，記世宗親征忠正，駐蹕城下，中夜有白虹自淝水起，亙數丈，下貫城中，數刻方沒。）〉 壬戌，今上奏，破淮賊萬餘眾於渦口，斬偽兵馬都監何延錫等，獲戰船五十艘。
In the third year of Xiande, on the first day of the first month of spring, the emperor declined the usual New Year's court homage. Former Minister of Works Su Yugui died. On dingyou, Li Gu reported a victory over the Huainan rebels at Shangyao. On wuxu, the court mobilized a hundred thousand corvée laborers to build up the capital's outer ramparts. On gengzi, an edict scheduled the eighth of the month for the emperor's departure for Huainan. Ma Congyun, director of the palace domestic service, was dismissed from office for embezzling assets belonging to his granddaughter by marriage, the Huo family, after a lawsuit was brought against him. On xinchou, Xiang Xun of the southern palace secretariat bureau was appointed acting regent of the eastern capital, with Wang Pu of the Duanming Hall as his deputy. On renyin the emperor left the capital; on dingwei Li Gu reported that he had pulled his forces back from Shouzhou to defend Zhengyang. On xinhai, Li Chongjin reported a crushing victory at Zhengyang over the Huainan rebels—over twenty thousand heads, corpses littering thirty li of ground. Liu Yanzhen, the enemy's chief commander, was slain in the fighting; deputy commanders including Xian Shilang were taken alive, along with three hundred thousand suits of armor and five hundred horses. Earlier Li Gu had besieged Shouchun, but when large Huainan reinforcements arrived he told his officers: "The enemy fleet is nearing Zhengyang and we are not equipped for river fighting. If the bridge should fail, the main army would be stranded. Better to pull back in full strength to the floating bridge at Zhengyang and wait for the emperor." The generals all agreed, burned their stores, and retreated. The withdrawal was disorderly; public and private property alike suffered heavy losses, and corvée workers north of the Huai were also caught behind enemy lines. On hearing this, the emperor urgently ordered Li Chongjin, commander of the palace guards, to march to their relief. The Huainan rebels exploited Li Gu's retreat, sending hundreds of warships up the Huai while positioning to cut the bridge; Yanzhen advanced with the main army in battle order. Li Chongjin reached Zhengyang, learned the Huai army was close, and led his generals across the bridge to engage. They met the enemy head-on and, combining forces, routed them at the first clash. 〈Note (Per Ma Ling's 《History of Southern Tang》: Shizong marched in person. At Yu Town he heard that Li Gu's army had pulled back and expected Zhou forces would pursue, so he sent Li Chongjin hurrying to Zhengyang with the words, "The Zhou army is coming—strike at once." Yanzhen and his officers, hearing of Li Gu's retreat, took it for cowardice. Deputy commander Xian Shilang said, "A pursuit would bring rich spoils." Liu Renzhan sent word: "You came as reinforcements; the enemy withdrew without fighting—their intentions are unclear. Do not pursue." As supreme commander, the army's fate rests on you. One setback could lose us everything. Vanguard commander Zhang Quanyue likewise urged, "Do not pursue." Yanzhen retorted, "Command is mine—what do you know?" Anyone who blocks me will be beheaded!" When they reached Zhengyang, Chongjin was already there; they had not even eaten before the fighting began. Yanzhen armed his abatis with blades, set up carved wooden beasts called "swift-horse boards," and strewed caltrops from leather bags across the field. The Zhou soldiers saw these devices and knew their foe lacked nerve; one charge routed them, and Yanzhen fell in battle.)〉 Beyond the dead and captured, more than three thousand who surrendered were all slaughtered by our general Zhao Chao. On jiayin the emperor arrived at Zhengyang. Li Chongjin, commander of the palace guards, was appointed overall commander of the Huainan campaign, and chief minister Li Gu was assigned to run the field headquarters at Shouzhou. On yimao the emperor crossed the Huai River. On bingchen he camped south of the Fei River northwest of Shouzhou and ordered the Zhengyang pontoon bridge relocated to Xiaocai. On gengshen he paraded his army before the city walls. 〈Note (The 《Record of Leaving Court at Chunming》 notes that a family copy of the 《Miscellaneous Records of Duke Fan of Lu》 records how, during Shizong's personal campaign against Zhongzheng, a white rainbow rose from the Fei at midnight, arched several zhang across the sky, and plunged into the city before fading moments later.)〉 On renxu, Zhao Kuangyin reported routing over ten thousand Huainan rebels at Wokou, killing the enemy overseer of horses He Yanshi and others, and seizing fifty warships.
2
二月丙寅，幸下蔡。 斬前濟州馬軍都指揮使康儼於路左，坐橋道不謹也。 朗州節度使王進逵奏，領兵入淮南界。 戊辰，廬壽巡檢使司超奏，破淮賊三千於盛唐，獲都監偽吉州刺史高弼以獻。 詔釋之。 兵部尚書張昭奏，準詔撰集兵法，分為十卷，凡四十二門，目之為《制旨兵法》，上之。 優詔褒美，仍以器幣賜之。 壬申，今上奏，破淮賊萬五千人於清流山，乘勝攻下滁州，擒偽命江州節度使、充行營應援使皇甫暉，常州團練使、充應援都監姚鳳以獻。 〈 （王钅至《默記》：李景聞世宗親至淮上，而滁州其控扼，且援壽州，命大將皇甫暉、監軍姚鳳提兵十萬扼其地。 太祖以周師數千與暉遇於清流關隘路，周師大敗，暉整全師入憩滁州城下，會翊日再出。 太祖兵聚關下，且虞暉兵再至，聞諸村人，雲有鎮州趙學究在村中教學，多智計，村民有爭訟者，多請以決曲直。 太祖往訪之，學究曰：「我有奇計，所謂因敗為勝，轉禍為福。 今關下有徑路，人無行者，雖牌軍亦不知之，乃山之背也，可以直抵城下。 方值西澗水大漲之時，彼必謂我既敗之後，無敢躡其後者，誠能由山背小路率兵浮西澗水至城下，斬關而入，可以得志。 」太祖大喜，且命學究以指其路，學究亦不辭，而遣人前導，即下令誓師，夜從小徑行，三軍跨馬浮西澗以迫城，暉果不為備。 奪門以入，暉始聞之，率親兵擐甲與太祖巷戰，三縱而三擒之，遂下滁州。）〉 甲戌，江南國主李景遣泗州牙將王知朗賫書一函至滁州，本州以聞，書稱唐皇帝奉書於大周皇帝，其略云：「願陳兄事，永奉鄰歡，設或俯鑒遠圖，下交小國，悉班卒乘，俾乂蒼黔，慶雞犬之相聞，奉瓊瑤以為好，必當歲陳山澤之利，少助軍旅之須。 虔俟報章，以答高命，道塗朝坦，禮幣夕行」云。 書奏不答。 乙亥，今上縶送所獲江南二將皇甫暉、姚鳳至行在，詔釋之。 壬午，江南國主李景遣其臣偽翰林學士戶部侍郎鐘謨、偽工部侍郎文理院學士李德明等奉表來上，敘願依大國稱臣納貢之意，仍進金器千兩，錦綺綾羅二千匹及禦衣、犀帶、茶茗、藥物等，又進犒軍牛五百頭，酒二千石。 是日，賜謨等錦綺綾羅二百匹，銀器一百兩，襲衣、金帶、鞍馬等。 丙戌，侍衛馬軍指揮韓令坤奏，收下揚州。 〈 （《東都事略·韓令坤傳》：率兵襲揚州，將吏開門以迎之，令坤整眾而入，市不易肆，人甚悅。）〉 丁亥，壽州城內左神衛軍使徐象等一十八人來奔。 庚寅，朗州節度使王進逵上言領兵入鄂州界，攻長山寨，殺賊軍三千餘眾。 辛卯，今上表偽命天長軍制置使耿謙以本軍降，獲糧草二千餘萬。 侍衛馬軍都指揮使韓令坤上言，泰州降。 癸巳，荊南上言，朗州節度使王進逵為部將潘叔嗣所殺。 〈 （《九國誌·王逵傳》：領眾逼宜春，道出長沙，耀兵金波亭，有蜜蜂集傘蓋中，占者以為不利，遂留長沙，令行營副使毛立領兵南下，以潘叔嗣、張文表為前鋒。 叔嗣怒，至澧陵擁眾而還。 逵聞兵叛，乃乘輕舸奔歸武陵，叔嗣追殺之於朗州城外。）〉 遣人詣潭州，請周行逢為帥，行逢至朗州，斬叔嗣於市。
In the second month, on bingyin, the emperor went to Xiaocai. Kang Yan, former commander of the Jizhou horse army, was beheaded by the roadside for failing to secure the bridges and roads. Wang Jinkui, military governor of Langzhou, reported leading his troops into Huainan. On wuchen, inspection commissioner Si Chao reported defeating three thousand Huainan rebels at Sheng Tang and presenting the captured enemy overseer and false prefect of Jizhou, Gao Bi. An edict ordered him released. Minister of War Zhang Zhao presented the 《Imperially Directed Art of War》, a ten-juan compilation of forty-two chapters on military method that he had assembled by imperial command. The emperor replied with a commendatory edict and bestowed ritual vessels and silks. On renshen, Zhao Kuangyin reported routing fifteen thousand Huainan rebels at Qingliushan, then storming Chuzhou and capturing Huangfu Hui, the enemy's Jiangzhou governor and relief commissioner, and Yao Feng, defense commissioner of Changzhou and relief overseer, whom he sent to court. 〈Note (Per Wang Zhi's 《Silent Records》: When Li Jing learned that Shizong had reached the Huai in person, he sent Huangfu Hui and army overseer Yao Feng with a hundred thousand men to hold Chuzhou, the key to the region and a lifeline for Shouzhou.) Taizu met Hui at Qingliu Pass with only a few thousand Zhou troops and was badly beaten. Hui brought his intact army to camp below Chuzhou, planning to march out again the next day. Taizu held the pass, anxious that Hui would return. Villagers told him of Zhao the scholar from Zhenzhou, a village schoolmaster famed for his wits, to whom locals brought disputes for judgment. Taizu sought him out. The scholar said, "I have a plan to turn defeat into victory and disaster into gain." Below the pass is an unused trail—even the garrison guards do not know it. It runs behind the mountain straight to the city walls. The West Stream is swollen now; they will assume that after beating us no one would dare follow. Lead men along the back trail, ford the stream, break in at the gate, and you can win. Delighted, Taizu had the scholar guide the way. The scholar obliged, sent scouts ahead, and Taizu swore in the troops for a night march along the hidden path. The whole army forded the West Stream and closed on the city; Hui was caught completely off guard. They burst through the gate. Hui rallied his household guard for street fighting with Taizu, was captured three times after being released three times, and Chuzhou fell.)〉 On jiaxu, Li Jing of Jiangnan sent Wang Zhilang of Sizhou with a letter to Chuzhou, which was forwarded to court. Addressed from "the Tang emperor" to "the Great Zhou emperor," it offered brotherly ties and lasting peace, pledged troops to pacify the people, jade gifts in friendship, and yearly tribute from mountain and marsh to help supply the army. It closed by saying he awaited a reply and that tribute would follow at once once the road was clear. The court made no reply to the letter. On yihai, Zhao Kuangyin sent the captured Jiangnan generals Huangfu Hui and Yao Feng to the imperial camp, where an edict ordered them released. On renwu, Li Jing sent Zhong Mo, Li Deming, and other ministers with a memorial offering vassal submission and tribute: a thousand liang of gold vessels, two thousand bolts of silks, imperial robes, rhinoceros belts, tea, medicines, five hundred oxen, and two thousand shi of wine for the troops. That same day the court rewarded Mo and his party with two hundred bolts of silk, a hundred liang of silverware, robes, gold belts, and horses. On bingxu, Han Lingkun, commander of the palace horse army, reported the capture of Yangzhou. 〈Note (The 《Outline History of the Eastern Capital》 biography of Han Lingkun records that he stormed Yangzhou, the garrison opened the gates to him, and he entered in good order without disturbing the markets—a welcome relief to the populace.)〉 On dinghai, Xu Xiang of the Left Divine Guard and seventeen others defected from Shouzhou. On gengyin, Wang Jinkui reported leading troops into Ezhou, storming Changshan Stockade, and killing over three thousand enemy soldiers. On xinmao, Zhao Kuangyin reported that Geng Qian, the enemy commissioner at Tianchang, had surrendered with his army, yielding more than twenty million units of grain and fodder. Han Lingkun, commander of the palace horse army, reported the surrender of Taizhou. On guisi, Jingnan reported that Wang Jinkui of Langzhou had been killed by his subordinate Pan Shusi. 〈Note (The 《Record of the Nine States》 biography of Wang Kui records that marching on Yichun via Changsha, he paraded at Jinbo Pavilion; bees swarmed his parasol, diviners called it a bad sign, so he stayed in Changsha and sent deputy Mao Li south with Pan Shusi and Zhang Wenbiao in the vanguard.) Enraged, Shusi turned back at Liling with his troops. Kui fled by light boat to Wuling when he heard of the mutiny; Shusi overtook and killed him outside Langzhou.)〉 Envoys were sent to Tanzhou inviting Zhou Xingfeng to take command; when he reached Langzhou he executed Shusi in the market.

三月丙申，行光州刺史何超奏，光州偽命都監張承翰以城歸順，尋授承翰集州刺史。 庚子，文武百僚再上表請聽樂，詔允之。 行舒州刺史郭令圖奏，收下舒州。 〈 （《隆平集·王審琦傳》：世宗征淮，舒州堅壁不下，以郭令圖為刺史，命審琦、司超將兵攻城，一夕拔之。 令圖入，復見逐於郡人。 審琦方進軍援黃州，聞令圖被逐，乃選騎銜枚襲城，夜敗其眾而復納之。）〉 江南國主李景表送先隔過朝廷兵士一百五十人至行在。 其軍即蜀軍也，秦、鳳之役，為王師所擒，配隸諸軍，及渡淮，輒復南逸。 帝怒其奔竄，盡戮之。 丙午，江南國主李景遣其臣偽司空孫晟、偽禮部尚書王崇質等奉表來上，仍進金一千兩、銀十萬兩、羅綺二千匹，又進賞給將士茶絹金銀羅帛等。 庚戌，兩浙奏，遣大將率兵攻常州。 延州留後李彥頵奏，蕃眾與部民為亂，尋與兵司都監閻綰掩殺，獲其酋帥高鬧兒等十人，磔於市。 彥頵本賈人也，貪而好利，蕃漢之民怨其侵刻，故至於是。 辛亥，賜江南李景書曰：
In the third month, on bingshen, acting Guangzhou prefect He Chao reported that enemy overseer Zhang Chenghan had surrendered the city; Chenghan was soon made prefect of Jizhou. On gengzi, civil and military officials again petitioned to resume court music; the emperor granted their request. Acting Shuzhou prefect Guo Lingtu reported the capture of Shuzhou. 〈Note (The 《Longping Collection》 biography of Wang Shenqi records that during the Huai campaign Shuzhou held out until Guo Lingtu was made prefect and Shenqi and Si Chao stormed it in a single night.) Lingtu entered the city only to be driven out again by local residents. Shenqi was marching to relieve Huangzhou when he learned of Lingtu's expulsion; he sent cavalry on a silent night raid, routed the rioters, and restored Lingtu to office.)〉 Li Jing of Jiangnan returned one hundred fifty Zhou soldiers who had been stranded on his side of the lines. They were Shu troops captured in the Qin-Feng campaign, distributed among Zhou units, who had repeatedly deserted southward once they reached the Huai. Enraged by their desertions, the emperor had them all put to death. On bingwu, Li Jing sent Sun Sheng, Wang Chongzhi, and other ministers with another memorial, along with a thousand liang of gold, a hundred thousand liang of silver, two thousand bolts of silk, and gifts of tea, silks, and precious metals for the troops. On gengxu, the Two Zhes reported sending a senior general with troops to attack Changzhou. Yanzhou governor Li Yanbin reported a revolt by tribesmen and locals; with overseer Yan Wan he ambushed the rebels, captured ten chiefs including Gao Naor, and had them dismembered in public. Yanbin had been a merchant before his appointment; his greed and extortion had alienated both tribal and Chinese subjects, which led to the uprising. 頃自有唐失禦，天步方艱，巢、蔡喪亂之餘，朱、李戰爭之後，中夏多故，六紀於茲，海縣瓜分，英豪鼎峙，自為聲教，各擅烝黎，連衡而交結四夷，乘釁而憑陵上國。 華風不競，否運所鐘，凡百有心，孰不興憤？ 朕猥承先訓，恭荷永圖，德不迨於前王，道不方於往古。 然而擅一百州之富庶，握三十萬之甲兵，且農戰交修，士卒樂用，思欲報累朝之宿怨，刷萬姓之包羞。 是以踐位已來，懷安不暇，破幽、并之巨寇，收秦、鳳之全封，兵不告疲，民有餘力。 一昨回軍隴上，問罪江幹，我實有辭，咎將誰執？ 朕親提金鼓，尋渡淮、淝，上順天心，下符人欲，前鋒所向，彼眾無遺，棄甲僵屍，動盈川谷。 收城徇地，已過滁陽，豈有落其爪牙，折其羽翼，潰其心腹，扼其吭喉而不亡者哉！
Since Tang lost the Mandate and the throne fell into peril—after the catastrophes of Huang Chao and Cai Jing, after the wars of Zhu Wen and Li Keyong—the central realm has been in turmoil for sixty years. The empire was carved among rival lords, each proclaiming his own rule and people, allying with one another and with foreign powers, and pressing upon the heartland whenever chance allowed. Chinese order could not hold its own; ill fortune had taken hold. Who among the loyal did not burn with anger? I have humbly inherited my predecessor's charge and the dynasty's enduring design, yet my virtue falls short of the sage kings of old and my governance cannot match antiquity. Yet I command the wealth of a hundred prefectures and three hundred thousand armed men; agriculture and war are both in order and the troops are eager to fight. I mean to avenge the wrongs of generations and restore the people's honor. Since my accession I have had no time for repose: I crushed the great enemies in You and Bing and recovered Qin and Feng intact. The army has not wearied and the people still have strength to spare. Only lately I brought the army down from Longyou to call you to account on the Yangtze. My cause is just—who else is to blame? I took the field in person and crossed the Huai and Fei, with Heaven above and the people's will below. Wherever my van struck, the enemy left none alive; abandoned armor and corpses choked the valleys. We have taken cities and overrun lands as far as Chuyang. When claws are torn out, wings broken, the heart routed, and the throat seized—how can such a foe survive?

蚤者，泗州主將遞送到書一函； 尋又使人鐘謨、李德明至，賫所上表及貢奉衣服腰帶、金銀器幣、茶藥牛酒等； 今又使人孫晟等並到行朝。 觀其降身聽命，引咎告窮，所謂君子見機，不俟終日，茍非達識，孰能若斯？ 但以奮武興戎，所以討不服； 惇信明義，所以懷遠人。 五帝三王盛德大業，恒用此道，以正萬邦。
First your Sizhou commander forwarded a letter; then Zhong Mo and Li Deming came with your memorial and gifts of robes, belts, gold and silver, tea, medicines, oxen, and wine; and now Sun Sheng and others have arrived at my camp as well. You humble yourself, accept blame, and plead your plight—the wise man, seeing how matters stand, does not wait until nightfall. Only a man of true discernment could act as you have. Yet arms are raised to chastise the defiant; while sincerity and justice are how distant peoples are won over. The Five Emperors and Three Kings governed all under Heaven by this same principle of martial force tempered with righteousness.
6
朕今躬統戎師，龔行討伐，告於郊廟社稷，詢於將相公卿，天誘其衷，國無異論。 茍不能恢復內地，申畫邊疆，便議班旋，真同戲劇，則何以光祖宗之烈，厭士庶之心，匪獨違天，兼且咈眾。 但以淮南部內，已定六州，盧、壽、濠、黃，大軍悉集，指期克日，拉朽焚枯，其餘數城，非足介意。 必若盡淮甸之土地，為大國之提封，猶是遠圖，豈同迷復。 如此則江南吏卒悉遣放還，江北軍民並當留住，免違物類之性，俾安鄉土之情。 至於削去尊稱，願輸臣禮，非無故事，實有前規。 蕭詧奉周，不失附庸之道； 孫權事魏，自同藩國之儀。 古也雖然，今則不取，但存常號，何爽歲寒。 儻堅事大之心，終不迫人於險，事資真愨，辭匪枝遊，俟諸郡之悉來，即大軍之立罷。 質於天地，信若丹青，我無彼欺，爾無我詐，言盡於此，更不煩雲，茍曰未然，請自茲絕。
I now lead the army in person on a punitive campaign, having announced it at the altars and consulted my generals and ministers. Heaven inclines my heart, and the court speaks with one voice. If I cannot recover our lost territory and secure the border, yet talk of withdrawing the army, it would be mere theater. How could I honor our ancestors or satisfy soldiers and people? That would defy Heaven and the will of the realm alike. Within southern Huainan six prefectures are already secured—Lu, Shou, Hao, and Huang—and the main army is gathered for the final assault. The rest will fall like rotten timber; the few cities left are hardly worth mention. To demand all Huainan as Zhou territory is still a distant ambition—not the confused retreat you imagine. Southern officials and troops would return south; northern soldiers and civilians would stay north—respecting natural loyalties and letting each people remain on native soil. As for renouncing your royal title and accepting vassal status, precedent exists for that as well. Xiao Cha served Zhou and kept the role of a loyal vassal; Sun Quan served Wei with the ceremonies proper to a feudatory prince. Such was antiquity, but I will not have it now. Keep your usual title—there is no shame in that. If you remain loyal to the great power, I will not press you to the brink. Deal in sincerity, not evasive words. When every prefecture has submitted, the army will withdraw at once. I swear this before Heaven and Earth, as binding as ink on silk: no deceit on either side. I have said all I mean to say. If you disagree, let all relations end here.

Spring is near and affairs press upon you; take care of your health and cherish yourself well. Though we are not far apart, our lands still differ. You are much in my thoughts, even in dreams. He also sent a letter to Li Jing's generals and officials, which read:
8
朕自類祃出師，麾旄問罪，絕長淮而電擊，指建業以鷹揚，旦夕之間，克捷相繼。 至若兵興之所自，釁起之所來，勝負之端倪，戎甲之次第，不勞盡諭，必想具知。 近者金陵使人，繼來行闕，追悔前事，委質大朝，非無謝咎之辭，亦有罷軍之請，但以南邦之土地，本中夏之封疆，茍失克復之期，大辜朝野之望，已興是役，固不徒還，必若自淮以南，畫江為界，盡歸中國，猶是遠圖。 所雲願為外臣，乞比湖、浙，彼既服義，朕豈忍人，必當別議封崇，待以殊禮。 凡爾將佐，各盡乃心，善為國家之謀，勉擇恒久之利。
Since I took the field from the war shrine, my banners have marched to judgment. We crossed the Huai like lightning and turned toward Jianye like a striking hawk; victories have followed in quick succession. How the war began, who provoked it, how battles have gone, and how our armies have advanced—you know all this without my recounting it. Envoys from Jinling have come repeatedly to express regret and sue for peace, even asking that the army withdraw. But the south was always central Chinese territory. To fail to recover it would betray the hopes of court and people alike. Having begun this campaign, I will not return empty-handed. To keep everything south of the Huai is still asking too much. You ask to become an outer vassal like the rulers of Hu and Zhe. Since you have submitted, I will not press you harshly. Your rank and honors will be settled separately and you will receive exceptional treatment. To all you commanders: give your full loyalty, counsel your state wisely, and choose what will bring lasting good.

Soon Mengjun marched from Hailing on Yangzhou; Lingkun met him, routed his force, and took Mengjun alive. Li Jing sent his brother Li Da, Prince of Qi, with a large army across the river from Guabu to camp a li from Liuhe. After several days they abandoned their camp and advanced on the imperial army. Zhao Kuangyin led the attack and routed them; countless survivors drowned fleeing into the river. In the ninth month, on bingwu, Wang Pu was made vice minister of revenue and vice commissioner of the Bureau of Military Affairs; Jiao Jixun was transferred from the Right to the Left Feathered Forest Army as senior general; Yang Chengxin was made commander of the Right Feathered Forest Army; Song Yanwo was appointed commander of the Right Divine Martial Army.
16
冬十月辛酉，葬宣懿皇后於懿陵。 癸亥，以右神武統軍宋延渥為廬州行營副部署。 乙丑，舒州刺史郭令圖責授虢州教練使，坐棄郡逃歸也。 丙寅，詔曰：「諸司職員皆系奏補，當執役之際，悉藉公勤，及聽選之時，尤資幹敏，茍非慎擇，漸致因循。 應諸司寺監，今後收補役人，並須人材俊利，身言可采，書劄堪中，自前行止，委無訛濫，勒本司關送吏部，引驗人材，考校筆劄。 其中選者，連所試書跡及正身引過中書，余從前後格敕處分，仍每年只得一度奏補。 」丁卯，宣懿皇后神主入廟，時有司請為後立別廟，禮也。 己巳，詔：「漳河以北郡縣，並許鹽貨通商，逐處有鹼鹵之地，一任人戶煎煉。 」壬申，以武平軍節度副使、知潭州軍府事宇文瓊為武清軍節度使，知潭州軍府事。 癸酉，淮南招討使李重進奏，破淮賊於盛唐，斬二千級。 太子賓客致仕薛仁謙卒。 丙子，襄州節度使、守太尉、兼中書令、陳王安審琦加守太師。 審琦鎮漢上十餘年，至是來朝，故以命寵之。 癸未，右拾遺趙守微杖一百，配沙門島。 守微本村民也，形貌樸野，粗學為文。 前年徒步上書，帝以急於取士，授右拾遺，聞者駭其事。 至是為妻父所訟，彰其醜行，故逐之。 〈 （《東都事略·張昭傳》云：世宗好拔奇取俊，有自布衣上書、下僚言事者，多不次進用。 昭諫曰：「昔唐初劉洎、馬周起徒步，太宗擢用為相，其後朱樸、柳璨在下僚，昭宗亦以大用，然則太宗用之於前而國興，昭宗用於後而國亡，士之難知也如此。 臣願陛下存舊法而用人，以劉、馬為鑒，朱、柳為戒，則善矣。」）〉 甲申，宣授今上同州節度使兼殿前都指揮使，宣授內外馬步軍都軍頭袁彥為曹州節度使兼侍衛步軍都指揮使。 戊子，右神武統軍張彥超卒。
In the tenth month of winter, on xinyou, Empress Xuanyi was interred at Yiling. On guihai, Song Yanwo, commander of the Right Divine Martial Army, was appointed deputy commander of the Luzhou campaign. On yichou, Guo Lingtu of Shuzhou was demoted to training commissioner at Guozhou for abandoning his post and fleeing home. On bingyin an edict declared that all office staff were appointed by memorial and must be chosen for diligence and ability, warning that careless selection would breed complacency. All offices must hereafter recruit only capable men of good character and competent writing, certify their records, and send them to the Ministry of Personnel for examination. Successful candidates were presented in person with their writing samples to the Secretariat; other matters followed existing regulations, and appointments by memorial were limited to once per year. On dingmao the spirit tablet of Empress Xuanyi was installed in the ancestral temple; the ritual offices properly requested a separate shrine for the empress. On jisi an edict permitted salt trade in all counties north of the Zhang River and allowed households to boil salt wherever brine deposits existed. On renshen, Yuwen Qiong was made Wuqing Army governor while continuing as acting Tanzhou prefect. On guiyou, Li Chongjin reported defeating Huainan rebels at Sheng Tang and taking two thousand heads. Retired crown prince mentor Xue Renqian died. On bingzi, Prince of Chen Wang Shenqi of Xiangzhou was promoted to Grand Preceptor while retaining his other honors. Shenqi had governed the Han River region for over ten years and had now come to court, and this appointment was meant to honor him. On guiwei, Right Reminder Zhao Shouwei was flogged a hundred strokes and exiled to Shamen Island. Shouwei was a villager by origin, plain in appearance, with only rudimentary literary training. Two years earlier he had walked to court with a memorial; eager for talent, the emperor made him Right Reminder, to widespread astonishment. Now his father-in-law sued him and exposed his misconduct, leading to his expulsion. 〈Note (The Zhang Zhao biography in the 《Outline History of the Eastern Capital》 records that Shizong favored promoting unusual talent and often advanced commoners who submitted memorials or junior officials who spoke up.) Zhao remonstrated: "In early Tang, Liu Ji and Ma Zhou rose from humble origins and Taizong made them chancellors. Later Zhu Pu and Liu Can were promoted by Zhaozong from junior posts—yet Taizong's choices strengthened the state while Zhaozong's destroyed it. Talent is hard to judge." I urge Your Majesty to follow established methods of appointment, taking Liu and Ma as models and Zhu and Liu as warnings.")〉 On jiashen, Zhao Kuangyin was formally appointed Tongzhou governor and commander of the palace front guard; Yuan Yan, chief of all horse and foot armies, was made Caozhou governor and commander of the palace foot guard. On wuzi, Zhang Yanchao, commander of the Right Divine Martial Army, died.
17
十一月己丑朔，詔廢天下無名祠廟。 庚子，日南至，帝不受朝賀，以宣懿皇后遷祔日近也。 乙巳，江南進奉使孫晟下獄死，江南進奉使鐘謨責授耀州司馬。 戊申，放華山隱者陳摶歸山。 帝素聞摶有道術，征之赴闕，月餘放還舊隱。 庚戌，殿前都指揮使張永德奏，敗濠州送糧軍二千人於下蔡，奪米船十餘艘。 宰臣李穀以風痹請告十旬，三上表求解所任，不允。
On the first day of the eleventh month, an edict abolished unauthorized shrines throughout the empire. On gengzi, the winter solstice, the emperor declined court homage because the rites for installing Empress Xuanyi's tablet were imminent. On yisi, Jiangnan envoy Sun Sheng died in prison; Zhong Mo was demoted to marshal at Yaozhou. On wushen, the Huashan recluse Chen Tuan was released to return to his mountain retreat. The emperor had long heard of Tuan's mastery of the Way, summoned him to court, and after a month sent him back to his retreat. On gengxu, Zhang Yongde reported defeating two thousand Haozhou grain convoy troops at Xiaocai and capturing more than ten supply boats. Chief minister Li Gu, suffering from paralysis, requested three months' leave and three times asked to resign; the emperor refused.
18
十二月己未朔，以給事中張鑄為光祿卿，鑄訴以官名與祖諱同，尋改秘書監，判光祿寺事。 辛酉，以許州節度使韓通兼侍衛馬步軍都虞候。 壬戌，以右領軍大將軍、權判三司張美領三司使。 壬申，以滑州節度使兼殿前都指揮使、駙馬都尉張永德為殿前都點校。 發陳、蔡、宋、亳、潁、曹、單等州丁夫城下蔡。 辛巳，故襄邑令劉居方贈右補闕，男士衡賜學究出身，獎廉吏也。 癸亥，詔兵部尚書張昭纂修太祖實錄及梁均王、唐清泰帝兩朝實錄。 〈 （《五代會要》云：同修撰官委張昭定名奏請，至四年正月，張昭奏請國子祭酒尹拙、太子詹事劉溫叟同編修。）〉 又詔曰：「史館所少書籍，宜令本館諸處求訪補填。 如有收得書籍之家，並許進書人據部帙多少等第，各與恩澤，如是卷帙少者，量給資帛。 如館內已有之書，不在進納之限。 仍委中書門下，於朝官內選差三十人，據見在書籍，各求真本校勘，署校官姓名，逐月具功課申報中書門下。 」戊子，淮南道招討使李重進奏，破淮賊二千人於塌山北。
On the first day of the twelfth month, Zhang Zhu was appointed director of imperial banquets but pleaded that the title matched his ancestor's taboo name; he was soon made director of the Secretariat while overseeing the banquets office. On xinyou, Han Tong of Xuzhou was also appointed chief adjutant of the palace horse and foot guards. On renxu, Zhang Mei was appointed commissioner of the Three Departments. On renshen, Zhang Yongde was appointed chief inspector of the palace front guard while retaining his governorship and command. Corvée laborers from Chen, Cai, Song, Bo, Ying, Cao, Shan, and other prefectures were mobilized to fortify Xiaocai. On xinsi, the late magistrate of Xiangyi Liu Jufang was posthumously honored as Right Supplementation Censor and his son Shiheng granted scholar status, in reward for upright service. On guihai an edict ordered Zhang Zhao to compile the Veritable Records of Taizu and the records of the Liang and Tang reigns of Prince Jun and Emperor Qingtai. 〈Note (The 《Institutional Essentials of the Five Dynasties》 records that Zhang Zhao was to name co-compilers; in the first month of year four he proposed Yin Zhuo and Liu Wensou as joint compilers.)〉 Another edict ordered the History Office to search widely for books missing from its collection. Households possessing books were invited to present them, with rewards graded by the number of volumes; smaller donations received proportionate payment in goods and silk. Books already held by the office were not eligible for presentation rewards. The Secretariat was to select thirty court officials to collate the existing collection, sign their work, and report monthly on their progress. On wuzi, Li Chongjin reported defeating two thousand Huainan rebels north of Tashan.
