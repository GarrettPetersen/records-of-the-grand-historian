Monograph on Criminal Law [ (Editorial note: The preface to this monograph was absent in the Yongle Encyclopedia source.)〉
2
梁太祖開平三年十一月，詔太常卿李燕、御史蕭頃、中書舍人張兗、戶部侍郎崔沂、大理卿王鄯、刑部郎中崔誥，共刪定律令格式。 四年十二月，宰臣薛貽矩奏：「太常卿李燕等重刊定律令三十卷，式二十卷，格一十卷，並目錄一十三卷，律疏三十卷，凡五部一十帙，共一百三卷。 敕中書舍人李仁儉詣閣門奉進，伏請目為《大梁新定格式律令》，仍頒下施行。 」從之。 〈 （原註：是時，大理卿李保殷進所撰《刑律總要》十二卷。）〉
In the eleventh month of Kaiping year 3 (909), Emperor Taizu of Liang ordered Li Yan as grand master of ceremonies, Xiao Huan as censor, Zhang Chong as secretariat drafter, Cui Yi as vice minister of revenue, Wang Shan as chief judge of the Court of Judicial Review, and Cui Gao as a secretary in the Ministry of Punishments to jointly revise the corpus of statutes, ordinances, administrative regulations, and procedural formats. In the twelfth month of the following year, chief minister Xue Yiju reported that Li Yan’s commission had produced a newly engraved set: thirty scrolls of statutes and ordinances, twenty of regulations, ten of formats, thirteen of tables of contents, and thirty of statutory commentary—five parts bound in ten fascicles, 103 scrolls in all. The throne then directed secretariat drafter Li Renjian to present the completed codes at the Gate of Credentials and asked that they be formally named the Newly Fixed Formats, Statutes, and Ordinances of the Great Liang and put into effect throughout the realm. The court approved the request. [ (Marginal note: At the same time Li Baoyin, chief judge of the Court of Judicial Review, submitted his own twelve-scroll General Summary of Criminal Statutes.)〉
3
唐莊宗同光元年十二月，御史臺奏：「當司刑部、大理寺本朝法書，自朱溫僭逆，刪改事條，或重貨財，輕入人命，或自徇枉過，濫加刑罰。 今見在三司收貯刑書，並是偽廷刪改者，兼偽廷先下諸道追取本朝法書焚毀，或經兵火所遺，皆無舊本節目。 只定州敕庫有本朝法書具在，請敕定州節度使速寫副本進納，庶刑法令式，並合本朝舊制。 」從之。 未幾，定州王都進納唐朝格式律令，凡二百八十六卷。 二年二月，刑部尚書盧價奏，纂集《同光刑律統類》凡一十三卷，上之。
In the twelfth month of Tongguang year 1 (923), the Censorate reported that the Tang Ministry of Punishments and Court of Judicial Review had once possessed the dynasty’s authentic law books, but after Zhu Wen’s rebellion those texts were altered—sometimes making goods weigh more than lives, sometimes letting personal bias inflate punishments. Every penal code still archived in the three judicial offices was a Liang revision, and the usurpers had previously ordered the provinces to hunt down and burn Tang law books; whatever escaped the flames no longer retained its original structure. Only the edict archive at Dingzhou still held a complete Tang set; the memorial asked the emperor to command the Dingzhou governor to copy and forward it at once so statutes, ordinances, and regulations could again match Tang practice. The throne agreed. Soon afterward Wang Du of Dingzhou delivered 286 scrolls of Tang formats, statutes, and ordinances. In the second month of year 2, Minister of Punishments Lu Jia compiled the thirteen-scroll Tongguang Comprehensive Categories of Penal Statutes and presented them to the throne.
4
周太祖廣順元年六月，敕侍御史盧億、刑部員外郎曹匪躬、大理正段濤同議定重寫法書一百四十八卷。 先是，漢隱帝末，因兵亂法書亡失，至是大理奏重寫律令格式、統類編敕，凡改點畫及義理之誤字凡二百一十有四，以晉、漢及國初事關刑法敕條，凡二十六件，分為二卷，附於編敕，目為《大周續編敕》，命省、寺行用焉。 〈 （《宋史》：盧億，周初為侍御史，漢末兵亂，法書亡失，至是大理奏重寫律令格式、統類編敕，乃詔億與刑部員外曹匪躬、大理正段濤同加議定舊本，以京兆府改同五府，開封、大名府改同河南府，長安、萬年改為次赤縣，開封、浚儀、大名、元城改為赤縣，又定東京諸門薰風等為京城門，明德等為皇城門，啟運等為宮城門，升龍等為宮門，崇元等為殿門，廟諱書不成字，凡改點畫及義理之誤字二百一十有四。 又以晉、漢及周初事關刑法敕條者，分為二卷，附編敕，目為《大周續編敕》。 詔行之。）〉
In the sixth month of Guangshun year 1 (951), Emperor Taizu of Later Zhou ordered attending censor Lu Yi, vice director Cao Feigong in the Ministry of Punishments, and senior reviewing judge Duan Tao to redraft the legal corpus in 148 scrolls. After the law library vanished in the wars at the end of Later Han, the Court of Judicial Review now asked for a full rewrite of statutes, ordinances, regulations, formats, compendia, and compiled edicts. The editors corrected 214 mistaken characters or clauses, gathered twenty-six penal edicts from Jin, Han, and the new Zhou reign into two supplementary scrolls called the Continued Compiled Edicts of the Great Zhou, and put the set into use across the central government. [ (From the Song History: Lu Yi was an attending censor at the Zhou founding. When Han’s archives burned, the Court of Judicial Review petitioned for a rewrite; Yi, Cao Feigong, and Duan Tao were told to revise the old text—elevating Jingzhao to the rank of the five metropolitan prefectures, aligning Kaifeng and Daming with Henan prefecture, demoting Chang’an and Wannian to “secondary crimson” counties while raising Kaifeng, Junyi, Daming, and Yuancheng to crimson counties, renaming eastern-capital gates (Xunfeng as a capital gate, Mingde as an imperial-city gate, and so on through palace and hall gates), and avoiding taboo graphs that could not be written whole—214 textual and substantive corrections in all.) They also sorted twenty-six Jin, Han, and early-Zhou penal edicts into two scrolls appended to the compiled edicts under the title Continued Compiled Edicts of the Great Zhou. An edict ordered the work promulgated.)〉
5
二年二月，中書門下奏：「準元年正月五日赦書節文，今後應犯竊盜贓及和奸者，並依晉天福元年已前條制施行。 諸處犯罪人等，除反逆罪外，其餘罪並不籍沒家產、誅及骨肉，一依格令處分者。 請再下明敕，頒示天下。 」乃下詔曰：「赦書節文，明有厘革，切慮邊城遠郡，未得審詳，宜更申明，免至差誤。 其盜賊，若是強盜，並準自來格條斷遣； 其犯竊盜者，計贓絹滿三匹已上者，並集眾決殺，其絹以本處上估價為定，不滿三匹者，等第決斷。 應有夫婦人被強奸者，男子決殺，婦人不坐； 其犯和奸者，並準律科斷，罪不至死。 其餘奸私罪犯，準格律處分。 應諸色罪人，除謀反大逆外，其餘並不得誅殺骨肉、籍沒家產。 」先是，晉天福中敕，凡和奸者，男子婦人並處極法，至是始改從律文焉。
In the second month of year 2, the Secretariat cited the amnesty of 5 January in Guangshun year 1: henceforth petty theft and consensual adultery were to be judged under the rules that had applied before Jin’s Tianfu year 1 (936). Except for treason, no offender’s household was to be confiscated and no relatives executed; all other crimes were to follow the code and administrative regulations. They asked that a clarifying edict be reissued empire-wide. The emperor then proclaimed that the amnesty had already reformed several penalties and that remote border districts might not have understood it; the rules needed to be stated again to prevent misapplication. Robbery by force would still be sentenced under the longstanding format articles; petty thieves whose loot was valued at three bolts of silk or more by local price tables were to be executed before the assembled crowd, while lesser amounts drew graduated punishments. In cases of rape within marriage the assailant was to be put to death and the woman was not punished; consensual adultery was to be judged under the statutes and did not carry the death penalty. Other sexual offenses would follow the regulations and formats. For all other crimes short of plotting rebellion or great treason, kin were not to be killed and estates were not to be confiscated. Under Jin’s Tianfu edicts consensual adultery had condemned both parties to death; this was the first Zhou measure to restore the statutory standard.
6
世宗顯德四年五月，中書門下奏：「準宣，法書行用多時，文意古質，條目繁細，使人難會，兼前後敕格，互換重疊，亦難詳定。 宜令中書門下並重刪定，務從節要，所貴天下易為詳究者。 伏以刑法者御人之銜勒，救弊之斧斤，故鞭撲不可一日弛之於家，刑法不可一日廢之於國，雖堯、舜淳古之代，亦不能舍此而致理矣。 今奉制旨刪定律令，有以見聖君欽恤明罰敕法之意也。 竊以律令之書，政理之本，經聖賢之損益，為古今之章程，歷代以來，謂之彜典。 今朝廷之所行用者律一十二卷、律疏三十卷、式二十卷、令三十卷、《開成格》一十卷、《大中統類》一十二卷、後唐以來至漢末編敕三十二卷及皇朝制敕等。 折獄定刑，無出於此。 律令則文辭古質，看覽者難以詳明； 格敕則條目繁多，檢閱者或有疑誤。 加之邊遠之地，貪猾之徒，緣此為奸，浸以成弊。 方屬盛明之運，宜伸畫一之規，所冀民不陷刑，吏知所守。 臣等商量，望準聖旨施行，仍差侍御史知雜事張湜、太子右庶子劇可久、殿中侍御史率汀、職方郎中鄧守中、倉部郎中王瑩、司封員外郎賈比、太常博士趙礪、國子博士李光贊、大理正蘇曉、太子中允王伸等一十人，編集新格，勒成部帙。 律令之有難解者，就文訓釋； 格敕之有繁雜者，隨事刪除。 止要諧理省文，兼且直書易會。 其中有輕重未當，便於古而不便於今，矛盾相違，可於此而不可於彼，盡宜改正，無或牽拘。 候編集畢日，委御史臺、尚書省四品以上及兩省五品以上官參詳可否，送中書門下議定，奏取進止。 」詔從之。 自是湜等於都省集議刪定，仍令大官供膳。
In the fifth month of Xiande year 4 (957), the Secretariat told Emperor Shizong that the penal corpus had grown ancient, minute, and overlapping after long use, so that officials and subjects alike struggled to apply it. They proposed a fresh abridgment by the Secretariat that would be concise enough for the empire to study and apply. Penal law, they wrote, was the bridle of governance and the axe of reform: just as the family cannot do without the rod for a single day, the state cannot do without criminal statutes for a single day—even Yao and Shun’s golden age rested on punishment to keep order. The commission to revise the statutes showed the emperor’s intent to temper punishments while keeping the law clear. Statutes and ordinances were the foundation of government, refined by the sages into the enduring institutions of every age. The working library included twelve scrolls of statutes, thirty of commentary, twenty of regulations, thirty of ordinances, ten Kaicheng formats, twelve Dazhong compendia, thirty-two scrolls of compiled edicts from Later Tang through Later Han, and current imperial edicts. No trial or sentence was supposed to fall outside this stack of books. The statutes and ordinances were written in archaic prose that readers could barely parse; the formats and edicts were so numerous that reviewers often stumbled. In distant provinces greedy clerks exploited the confusion, and abuse spread. In an age that called for clarity, they sought one uniform standard so subjects would not fall into punishment and officials would know what to enforce. They asked to proceed under the imperial command and named a ten-man editorial board—led by Zhang Shi as attending censor in charge of miscellaneous affairs, with Ju Keyong, Shuai Ting, Deng Shouzhong, Wang Ying, Jia Bi, Zhao Li, Li Guangzan, Su Xiao, Wang Shen, and others—to compile a new format code in fascicles. Obscure statutory language would be glossed in place; redundant edicts and formats would be cut to the matter at hand. The goal was coherent, shortened prose that anyone could follow. Any rule that was too harsh, too lenient, suited to antiquity but not to the present, or self-contradictory was to be rewritten without hesitation. When the draft was finished, senior censors and ministry officials, together with fifth-rank and above officers of the two secretariats, would review it; the Secretariat would deliberate and memorialists would await the throne’s decision. The emperor approved. Zhang Shi’s team then met at the Department of State Affairs to edit the code, with the Court of Imperial Entertainments ordered to feed them.
7
五年七月，中書門下奏：「侍御史知雜事張湜等九人，奉詔編集刑書，悉有條貫，兵部尚書張昭等一十人，參詳旨要，更加損益。 臣質、臣溥據文評議，備見精審。 其所編集者，用律為正； 辭旨之有難解者，釋以疏意； 義理之有易了者，略其疏文。 式令之有附近者次之，格敕之有廢置者又次之。 事有不便於今、該說未盡者，別立新條於本條之下； 其有文理深古、慮人疑惑者，別以朱字訓釋。 至於朝廷之禁令，州縣之常科，各以類分，悉令編附。 所冀發函展卷，綱目無遺，究本討源，刑政咸在。 其所編集，勒成一部，別有目錄，凡二十一卷。 刑名之要，盡統於茲，目之為《大周刑統》，欲請頒行天下，與律疏令式通行。 其《刑法統類》、《開成格》、編敕等，采掇既盡，不在法司行使之限，自來有宣命指揮公事及三司臨時條法，州縣見今施行，不在編集之數。 應該京百司公事，逐司各有見行條件，望令本司刪集，送中書門下詳議聞奏。 」敕宜依，仍頒行天下。 乃賜侍御史知雜事張湜等九人各銀器二十兩，雜彩三十匹，賞刪定《刑統》之勞也。
In the seventh month of year 5 the Secretariat reported that Zhang Shi’s nine compilers had produced an orderly draft and that Zhang Zhao’s ten reviewers had refined it further. Chief ministers Zhi and Pu, reviewing the text, found the work precise and thorough. The compendium took the statutes as its backbone; where wording was obscure, it borrowed the statutory commentary; where the sense was plain, commentary was dropped. Nearby regulations and ordinances followed next, then obsolete formats and edicts. Rules ill suited to the present or incompletely explained received new sub-articles beneath the old ones; passages so archaic that readers might stumble were glossed in vermilion ink. Court bans and standing county regulations were sorted by category and appended. The editors hoped that opening a single volume would reveal every topic and trace every penalty to its source. The finished work comprised twenty-one scrolls with a separate table of contents. It gathered every essential penal category into one book titled the Great Zhou Penal Compendium (Xingtong), which they asked to promulgate empire-wide alongside the statutes, commentary, ordinances, and regulations. Older works such as the Comprehensive Categories of Penal Law, Kaicheng Formats, and compiled edicts had been fully absorbed and would no longer circulate in the judicial offices; ad hoc edicts and three-department rules still in local use were excluded from the compilation. Each capital agency was asked to compile its own in-house rules and submit them to the Secretariat for review. An edict approved the plan and ordered empire-wide promulgation. Zhang Shi and the other nine chief compilers each received twenty taels of silverware and thirty bolts of patterned silk as reward for completing the Xingtong.
8
唐同光二年六月己巳，敕：「應御史臺河南府行臺馬步司左右軍巡院，見禁囚徒，據罪輕重，限十日內並須決遣申奏。 仍委四京、諸道州府，見禁囚徒，速宜疏決，不得淹停，兼恐內外形勢官員私事寄禁，切要止絕，俾無冤滯。 」三年五月己未，敕：「在京、及諸道州府，所禁罪人，如無大過，速令疏決，不得淹滯。 」六月甲寅，敕：「刑以秋冬，雖關惻隱，罪多連累，翻慮滯淹。 若或十人之中，止為一夫抵死，豈可以輕附重，禁錮逾時。 言念哀矜，又難全廢。 其諸司囚徒，罪無輕重，並宜各委本司，據罪詳斷申奏，輕者即時疏理，重者候過立春，至秋分然後行法。 如是事系軍機，須行嚴令，或謀惡逆，或畜奸邪，或行劫殺人，難於留滯，並不在此限。」
On 6 June in Tongguang year 2 (924), Tang Zhuangzong ordered every prisoner held by the Censorate, Henan prefecture, the mobile secretariat, and the army patrol courts to be sentenced within ten days according to the gravity of the offense and reported to the throne. All four capitals and provincial prisons were to dispose of cases quickly, without detaining the innocent; officials who jailed people for private grudges were to be stopped so that no one languished unjustly. In the fifth month of the following year another edict told capital and provincial jails to release or sentence detainees who had no serious charge without delay. A third edict in the sixth month acknowledged that although executions were traditionally reserved for autumn and winter out of mercy, cases with many co-defendants risked endless detention. If only one man in ten deserved death, it was wrong to let the light suffer with the heavy and keep everyone shackled past season. Yet mercy could not be abandoned altogether. Every agency was therefore to judge its own prisoners: minor offenders were to be released at once, capital cases were to wait from spring until the autumn equinox before execution. Military emergencies, treason, sedition, or robbery-homicide were exempt from the seasonal stay.
9
天成元年十一月庚申，敕：「應天下州使系囚，除大辟罪以上，委所在長吏，速推勘決斷，不得傍追證對，經過食宿之地，除當死刑外，並仰釋放，兼不許懲治。 」二年春。 左拾遺李同上言：「天下系囚，請委長吏逐旬親自引問，質其罪狀真虛，然後論之以法，庶無枉濫。 」從之。 六月，大理少卿王郁上言：「凡決極刑，合三覆奏，近年以來，全不守此。 伏乞今後前一日令各一覆奏。 」奉敕宜依。 八月，西京奏：「奉近敕，在京犯極刑者，令決前一日各一覆奏，緣當府地遠，此後凡有極刑，不審準條疏覆奏。 」奉敕旨：「昨六月二十日所降敕文，只為應在洛京有犯極刑者覆奏，其諸道已降旨命，準舊例施行。 今詳西京所奏，尚未明近敕，兼慮諸道有此疑惑，故令曉諭。 」十月辛丑，德音：「為政之要，切在無私； 聽訟之方，惟期不濫。 天下諸州府官員，如有善推疑獄及曾雪冤濫兼有異政者，當具姓名聞奏，別加甄獎。」
In the eleventh month of Tiancheng year 1 (926), Later Tang ordered that ordinary prisoners outside capital offenses be tried and sentenced by local governors without dragging travelers into collateral interrogations, and that detainees in transit—except those facing death—be released and not mistreated. The following spring, left remonstrator Li Tong proposed that every prefectural chief personally review all local prisoners every ten days, verify the charges, and then apply the law so that injustice would not accumulate. The court agreed. In the sixth month Vice Director Wang Yu of the Court of Judicial Review noted that capital sentences required three memorial reviews, a rule lately ignored. He asked that henceforth one review be submitted on the day before execution. The throne assented. In the eighth month the Western Capital reported confusion: the new rule required a day-before memorial for executions in Luoyang, but Chang’an was far away and officials were unsure whether they too must memorialize. The throne clarified that the June 20 edict applied only to capital cases in Luoyang; the provinces should follow their existing precedents. Because Chang’an still seemed uncertain—and other circuits might share the doubt—the court issued a further explanation. In the tenth month an amnesty proclaimed that good government depended on impartiality; and fair hearings meant never punishing beyond the charge. Any prefectural official who resolved hard cases or redressed wrongful convictions was to be named and rewarded.
10
長興元年二月，制曰：「欲通和氣，必在伸冤； 將設公方，實資獎善。 州縣官僚能雪冤獄活人生命者，許非時選，仍加階超資註官，與轉服色，已著緋者與轉兼官。 」二年二月辛亥，敕：「朕猥以眇躬，薦承鴻業，念彼疲瘵，勞於寐興。 或慮官不得人，因成紊亂； 或慮刑非其罪，遂至怨嗟。 王化所興，獄訟為本，茍無訓勵，必有滯淹。 近日諸道百姓，或諸多違犯，或小可鬥爭。 官吏曲縱胥徒，巧求瑕釁。 初則滋張節目，作法拘囚； 終則誅剝貨財，市恩出拔。 外憑公道，內循私情，無理者轉務遷延，有理者卻思退縮。 積成訛弊，漸失紀綱。 自今後切委逐處官吏州牧縣宰等，深體余懷，各舉爾職。 凡闕推究，速與剸裁。 如敢茍縱依違，遂成枉濫，或經臺訴屈，或投匭申冤，勘問不虛，其元推官典並當責罰，其逐處觀察使、刺史，別議朝典。 宜令諸道州府，各依此處分，所管屬郡，委本道嚴切指揮。 」八月丁卯，敕：「三京、諸道州府刑獄，近日訪聞，依前禁系人，多不旋決，諸道宜令所在各委長吏，專切推窮，不得有滯淹。」
In the second month of Changxing year 1 (930) an ordinance declared that harmony required redressing injustice; and public fairness required rewarding those who did good. County and prefectural officers who saved lives by overturning wrongful convictions might be promoted off-cycle, gain extra rank, receive higher robe colors, or—if already in crimson—take an additional concurrent post. In the second month of year 2 an edict opened with the emperor’s worry for the afflicted common people who kept him awake at night. He feared that wrong officials bred disorder; and that punishments not matching crimes bred resentment. Because civilized rule begins in the courts, neglect of prison policy meant inevitable backlog. Lately some subjects committed serious crimes, others mere brawls. Yet magistrates indulged their clerks and hunted for pretexts. First they multiplied procedural counts to justify detention; then they stripped property and sold release as a favor. Outwardly they spoke of justice while inwardly they followed private whim: the guilty were prolonged, the innocent were driven to withdraw their suits. Errors and abuses piled up over time until statutes and discipline themselves began to slip away. Henceforth I earnestly charge every local official—governors, magistrates, and the rest—to take my intent deeply to heart and each perform the duties proper to your office. Wherever inquiry has been neglected, dispose of the case swiftly and decisively. If any official dares to indulge wrongdoing or hedge and delay until injustice results—or if a petitioner wins a hearing at the Censorate or lodges a grievance in the petition box and investigation proves the complaint true—the original investigators and clerks shall all be punished, while the surveillance commissioners and prefects in each jurisdiction shall face separate sanctions under court law. Every circuit and prefecture should act according to this disposition, and each circuit should issue strict orders to the commanderies under its jurisdiction. In the eighth month, on dingmao, an edict stated: "In the prisons of the three capitals and of prefectures throughout the circuits, recent inquiries show that detainees are still, as before, often left without prompt decision. Each circuit should assign its chief local official to investigate every case with urgent care and permit no backlog or delay."
11
四月，前濮州錄事參軍崔琮上言：「諸道獄囚，恐不依法拷掠，或不勝苦致斃，翻以病聞，請置病囚院，兼加醫藥。 」中書覆云：「有罪當刑，仰天無恨； 無病致斃，沒地銜冤。 燃死灰而必在至仁，照覆盆而須資異鑒。 《書》著『欽哉』之旨，『《禮》』標『侀也』之文，因彰善於泣辜，更推恩於扇暍。 所請置病囚院，望依，仍委長吏，專切經心。 或有病囚，當時遣醫人診候，治療後，據所犯輕重決斷。 如敢故違，致病囚負屈身亡，本處官吏，並加嚴斷。 兼每及夏至，五日一度，差人洗刷枷匣。」
In the fourth month, Cui Cong, former registrar of Puzhou, memorialized: "Prisoners in the circuits may be tortured unlawfully, or die under torment that is then reported as illness. I ask that sick-prisoner wards be set up and that medical care be provided." The Secretariat replied: "When guilt deserves punishment, the condemned may face Heaven without resentment;" but when a healthy man is tortured to death, his grievance fills the earth itself." To revive what seemed dead ash requires the utmost benevolence; to see justice done in what seemed a covered basin requires extraordinary discernment. The Book of Documents sets forth the injunction "Be reverent!"; the Book of Rites marks the phrase "Pity them!"—signs of mercy for the wrongfully punished and of grace extended to those scorched by injustice. The request to establish sick-prisoner wards is approved; chief officials in each place are to oversee the matter with devoted care. Whenever a prisoner falls ill, a physician should examine him at once; after treatment, sentence should follow the gravity of the offense. If any official deliberately violates this and a sick prisoner dies with his grievance unredressed, every local official involved shall face severe punishment. Moreover, at each summer solstice, every five days send men to wash the cangues and fetters."
12
應順元年二月戊午，詔：「應三京、諸道州府系囚，據罪輕重，疾速斷遣。 比來停滯，須奏取裁，不便區分，故為留滯。 今後凡有刑獄，據理斷遣。 如有敕推按，理合奏聞，不在此限。」
In the second month of Yingshun, on wuwu, an edict ordered: "Detainees in the three capitals and in prefectures throughout the circuits should, according to the gravity of their offenses, be judged and released without delay." Cases had lately stalled because every decision required a memorial to the throne, which made proper classification awkward and left prisoners waiting. Henceforth, whenever a criminal matter arises, judgment should follow the merits of the case. Where the throne orders a special investigation, memorials are still required; those cases lie outside this rule."
13
清泰元年五月丁丑，詔：「在京諸獄及天下州府見系罪人，正當暑毒之時，未免拘囚之苦，誠知負罪，特軫予懷。 恐法吏生情，滯於決斷。 詔至，所在長吏親自慮問，據輕重疾速斷遣，無淹滯。」
In the fifth month of Qingtai, on dingchou, an edict said: "In the capital prisons and in prisons across the realm, those now held in custody suffer confinement just as summer heat reaches its height. I know they are guilty, yet my heart is especially moved on their behalf." I fear that judicial officers may indulge private motives and delay judgment. When this edict arrives, the chief official in each place should personally review the cases, judge and release according to severity, and permit no backlog or delay."
14
晉天福二年八月，敕下刑部大理寺御史臺及三京、諸道州府：「今後或有系囚染疾者，並令逐處軍醫看候，於公廨錢內量支藥價，或事輕者，仍許家人看候。 」四年九月，相州節度使桑維翰奏：「管內所獲賊人，從來籍沒財產，雲是鄴都舊例，格律未見明文。 」敕：「今後凡有賊人，準格定罪，不得沒納家資。 天下諸州，準此處分。 」三月庚午，詳定院奏：「前守洪洞縣主簿盧燦進策云：『伏以刑獄至重，朝廷所難，尚書省分職六司，天下謂之會府，且諸道決獄，若關人命，即刑部不合不知。 欲請州府凡斷大辟罪人訖，逐季具有無申報刑部，仍俱錄案款事節，並本判官、馬步都虞候、司法參軍、法直官、馬步司判官名銜申聞，所貴或有案內情曲不圓，刑部可行覆勘。 如此則天下遵守法律，不敢輕易刑書，非惟免有銜冤，抑亦勸其立政者。 』臣等參詳，伏以人命至重，國法須精，雖載舊章，更宜條理，誠為允當，望賜施行。 」從之。 五月，詔曰：「刑獄之難，古今所重，但關人命，實動天心，或有冤魂，則傷和氣。 應諸道州府，凡有囚徒，據推勘到案款，一一盡理，子細檢律令格敕。 其間或有疑者，準令文讞，大理寺亦疑，申尚書省，省寺明有指歸，州府然後決遣。」
In the eighth month of the second year of Tianfu, an edict went to the Ministry of Justice, the Court of Judicial Review, the Censorate, and the three capitals and every circuit: "Whenever a detainee falls ill, local military physicians should attend him, medicine paid from public-office funds as appropriate; in lighter cases, family members may still attend." In the ninth month of the fourth year, Sang Weihan, commissioner of Xiangzhou, memorialized: "Bandits taken in my jurisdiction have always had their property confiscated—said to follow an old Ye capital practice—but the code contains no explicit rule for this." An edict replied: "Henceforth, whenever bandits are captured, sentence them under the code and do not confiscate their families' property." Every prefecture in the realm should follow this rule. On gengwu of the third month, the Commission for Detailed Review memorialized: "Lu Can, former principal recorder of Hongtong County, proposed: 'Criminal cases are supremely grave and among the hardest matters for the court; the Department of State Affairs divides the six ministries and is the realm's chief office—yet when a circuit decides a capital case, the Ministry of Justice should not be left uninformed.'" He asked that whenever a prefecture completes a capital sentence, each quarter it report to the Ministry of Justice and submit the full case record, circumstances, and the names and titles of the presiding judge, the infantry and cavalry inspectors, the judicial aide, the legal reviewer, and the division judges, so that if any point in the case remains incomplete, the ministry may review it. Thus the realm would honor the law and not treat the penal code lightly—sparing the wronged and encouraging sound governance alike. The memorialists reviewed the proposal and observed that human life is supremely grave and state law must be exact; though the old statutes record the principle, they should be set in clearer order. The proposal was deemed fitting and recommended for enactment. The throne approved. In the fifth month, an edict declared: "The difficulty of prisons is a burden ancients and moderns alike have taken seriously; whenever a human life is at stake, Heaven's heart is truly moved; lingering wronged spirits injure the realm's harmonious qi." In every circuit and prefecture, for every prisoner, once investigation is complete, examine each point of the record carefully against the statutes, regulations, and edicts. Where doubt remains, follow statutory interpretation; if the Court of Judicial Review is still uncertain, memorialize the Department of State Affairs; only when department and court give clear direction may the prefecture judge and dispose of the case."
15
五年三月丙子，詔曰：「自大中六年已來，嫠耳稱冤，決杖流配，訴雖有理，不在申明。 今後據其所陳，與為勘斷，剺耳之罪，準律別科。 」六年秋七月庚辰，詔曰：「政教所切，獄訟惟先，推窮須察於事情，斷遣必遵於條法，用宏欽恤，以致和平。 應三京、鄴都及諸道州府，見禁諸色人等，宜令逐處長吏，常切提撕，疾速決遣，每務公當，勿使滯淹。 」天福八年四月壬申，敕：「朕自臨寰宇，思致和平，思將以四海為家，慮有一物失所。 每念狴牢之內，或多枉撓之人，屬此炎蒸，倍宜軫憫，冀絕滯淹之嘆，用資欽恤之仁。 應三京、鄴都及諸道州府見禁罪人等，宜令逐處長吏，嚴切指揮本推司及委本所判官，疾速結絕斷遣，不得淹延，及致冤濫，仍付所司。」
In the third month of the fifth year, on bingzi, an edict stated: "Since the sixth year of Dazhong, widows who mutilated their ears to proclaim injustice were still flogged and exiled—even when their grievance was justified, they were denied a hearing." Henceforth, investigate and judge according to what they allege; the offense of ear-cutting should be sentenced separately under the code." In the seventh month of the sixth year, on gengchen, an edict said: "Among the duties of government, litigation comes first: investigation must probe the facts, and judgment must obey the code—thereby extending compassionate care and securing peace." In the three capitals, Ye, and every circuit prefecture, the chief official in each place should keep constant watch over all detainees, judge and release them swiftly, seek fairness in every case, and permit no backlog. In the fourth month of the eighth year of Tianfu, on renshen, an edict read: "Since I assumed the realm I have sought peace; I would make the four seas one household, yet I fear that even one creature may be wronged." Whenever I think of those in prison, many are wrongfully oppressed; in this season of blazing heat compassion is doubly due, that the sighs of delay may cease and the throne's merciful care be made real. All detainees in the three capitals, Ye, and every circuit should be ordered by local chief officials, with strict instructions to investigating offices and assigned judges, to conclude cases swiftly, without delay or wrongful abuse; the responsible offices are charged accordingly."

開運二年五月壬戌，殿中丞桑簡能上封事曰：「伏以天地育萬物，廣博厚之恩； 帝王牧黎元，行寬大之令。 是知恤刑緩獄，乃為政之先； 布德行惠，實愛民之本。 今盛夏之月，農事方殷，是雷風長養之時，乃動植蕃蕪之際。 宜順時令，以宏至仁。 竊以諸道州府都郡縣應見禁罪人，或有久在囹圄，稍滯區分，胥吏侮文，枝蔓乃眾。 捶楚之下。 或陷無辜； 縲紲之中，莫能自理。 茍一人拘系，則數人營財，物用既殫，工業亦罷。 若此之類，實繁有徒，切恐官吏因循，浸成斯弊。 伏乞降詔旨，令所在刑獄，委長吏親自錄問，量罪疾速斷遣，務絕冤濫，勿得淹留，庶免虛禁平人，妨奪農力，冀召和氣，以慶明時。 」敕曰：「囹圄之中，縲紲之苦，奸吏茍窮於枝蔓，平人用費於貨財，由茲滯淹，兼致屈塞。 桑簡能體茲軫憫，專有敷陳，請長吏躬親，免獄官抑逼，深為允當，宜再頌行。 宜依。 」十月甲子，秘書省著作郎邊玕上封事曰：「臣聞從諫如流，人君之令範； 極言無隱，臣子之常規。 蓋欲表大國之任人，致萬邦之無事，前文備載，可舉而行。 伏以皇帝陛下，德合上玄，運膺下武，旰食宵衣而軫念，好生惡殺以推仁，幾措典刑，固無冤枉。 然以照臨之內，州郡尤多，若不再具舉明，伏恐漸成奸弊。 臣竊見諸道刑獄，前朝曾降敕文，凡是禁系罪人，五日一度錄問。 但以年月稍遠，漸致因循。 或長吏事煩，不暇躬親點檢； 或胥徒啟幸，妄要追領證明。 慮有涉於淫刑，即恐傷於和氣。 伏乞特降詔敕。 自今後諸道並委長吏五日一度，當面同共錄問，所冀處法者無恨，銜冤者獲伸。 俾令四海九州，鹹歌聖德； 五風十雨，永致昌期。 」敕曰：「人之命無以復生，國之刑不可濫舉。 雖一成之典，務在公平； 而三覆其詞，所宜詳審。 凡居法吏，合究獄情。 邊玕近陟周行，俄陳讜議，更彰欽恤，宜允申明。」
In the fifth month of the second year of Kaiyun, on renxu, Palace Director Sang Jianneng submitted a sealed memorial: "Heaven and Earth nurture the ten thousand things with vast and generous favor;" emperors shepherd the common people and issue orders of leniency and breadth." Thus compassionate punishment and eased prisons are the first duties of governance; and to spread virtue and enact grace is truly the root of cherishing the people. Now, at the height of summer, farming presses at its peak—the season when thunder and wind nurture growth, when every living thing flourishes. The season itself calls for the utmost benevolence. I observe that in the chief prefectures, commanderies, and counties of every circuit, among prisoners now held, some have languished long in jail while their cases stall; clerks trifle with the paperwork until the tangle of delays grows thick. Beneath rod and whip, some innocent men are trapped; within fetters, none can plead his own cause. When one man is detained, several must raise funds for him; household goods are spent and trades and crafts come to a halt. Such cases are legion, and I fear that if officials follow routine, this abuse will only deepen. I beg that an edict order prisons everywhere to have the chief official personally examine cases, judge and release according to guilt without delay, end wrongful abuse, and forbid detention—sparing common people false imprisonment, preserving farm labor, and summoning harmonious qi to bless this enlightened age." An edict replied: "In prison, fetters bring suffering; corrupt clerks spin out endless complications while common people spend their wealth—delay piles up and injustice is blocked." Sang Jianneng voiced this compassionate concern in a focused proposal, asking that chief officials act in person and spare prisoners from jailers' pressure; the court found this deeply fitting and ordered it proclaimed again." Approved as proposed. On jiazi of the tenth month, Bian Gan, archivist of the Secretariat, submitted a sealed memorial: "I have heard that accepting remonstrance like a flowing stream is a ruler's model;" and to speak plainly without concealment is a minister's constant duty." This is how a great state shows that it employs men rightly and brings the myriad realms to peace; the ancients recorded it fully, and we may act on it now. Your Majesty's virtue joins with Heaven and your mandate answers the age; you rise at dawn and sleep late with care, loving life and hating killing, extending benevolence until canonical punishments nearly fall still and, in truth, injustice is rare. Yet within your illuminated realm prefectures and commanderies are countless; unless this is proclaimed again in full detail, corrupt practice may gradually return. I have observed that in former dynasties edicts ordered that in every circuit's prisons, every detained person should be examined and recorded once every five days. But as years passed, the rule slipped into neglect. Sometimes the chief official is overwhelmed and cannot inspect in person; sometimes clerks and runners seek advantage and falsely demand witnesses and documents. Where excessive punishment follows, harmonious qi is harmed. I beg that a special edict be issued. Henceforth, in every circuit the chief official should every five days examine detainees face to face, so that the punished may feel no resentment and the aggrieved may obtain redress. Then the four seas and nine regions may sing of sagely virtue; and with timely winds and rains, reach an age of lasting prosperity." An edict replied: "A human life, once taken, cannot be restored; the state's punishments must not be lightly applied." Though the code once fixed demands fairness in execution, threefold review of the words is what should be examined in detail. Every judicial officer should thoroughly investigate the circumstances of each prison case. Bian Gan has lately risen in court rank and promptly offered forthright counsel, further displaying the throne's merciful care; approval and proclamation are granted."

三年十一月丁未，左拾遺竇儼上疏曰：「臣伏睹名例律疏云：死刑者，古先哲王，則天垂象，本欲生之，義期止殺，絞斬之坐，皆刑之極也。 又準天成三年閏八月二十三日敕，行極法日，宜不舉樂，減常膳； 又刑部式，決重杖一頓處死，以代極法，斯皆人君哀矜不舍之道也。 竊以蚩尤為五虐之科，尚行鞭樸； 漢祖約三章之法，止有死刑。 絞者筋骨相連，斬者頭頸異處，大辟之目，不出兩端，淫刑所興，近聞數等。 蓋緣外地，不守通規，肆率情性，或以長釘貫篸人手足，或以短刀臠割人肌膚，乃至累朝半生半死，俾冤聲而上達，致和氣以有傷。 將宏守位之仁，在峻惟行之令，欲乞特下明敕，嚴加禁斷者。 」敕曰：「文物方興，刑罰須當，有罪宜從於正法，去邪漸契於古風。 竇儼所貢奏章，實裨理道，宜依所奏，準律令施行。」
In the eleventh month of the third year, on dingwei, Left Remonstrator Dou Yan memorialized: "The commentary to the Statutes on Names states: 'For the death penalty, the ancient sage kings, following Heaven's signs, meant to preserve life and to halt killing; strangulation and decapitation stand at the extreme of punishment.'" Moreover, by the edict of the intercalary eighth month, twenty-third day, of the third year of Tiancheng, on days when capital punishment is carried out, music should cease and the regular meal be reduced; and in the Ministry of Justice formulary, where a single heavy beating replaces execution—all these embody the ruler's compassionate reluctance to abandon life. Chiyou devised the five cruel punishments, yet still used the whip; the Han founder's three-chapter law recognized only death. Strangulation leaves flesh and bone joined; decapitation severs head from neck—the great executions know only these two forms, yet lately several grades of excessive punishment have been heard. Outer regions ignore the universal code and follow private whim—driving long nails through hands and feet, slicing flesh with short knives, leaving men half alive for reign after reign until cries of injustice reach Heaven and harmonious qi is wounded. To extend the benevolence proper to the throne and to make execution strict and lawful, I beg that a clear edict forbid and cut off such practices." An edict replied: "As culture and institutions rise, punishments must be fitting; the guilty should face proper law, and the purging of evil should return toward ancient custom." Dou Yan's memorial truly serves good governance; it is ordered implemented according to the statutes and regulations."

漢乾祐二年正月，敕：「政貴寬易，刑尚哀矜，慮滋蔓之生奸，實軫傷而是念。 今屬三元改候，四序履端，將冀和平，無如獄訟。 應三京、鄴都、諸道州府見系罪人，委逐處長吏躬親慮問，其於決斷，務在公平，但見其情，即為具獄，勿令率引，遂致淹停，無縱舞文，有傷和氣。 」四月甲午，敕曰：「月戒正陽，候當小暑，乃挺重出輕之日，是恤刑議獄之辰，有罪者速就勘窮，薄罰者畫時疏決，用符時令，勿縱滯淹。 三京、鄴都、諸道州府在獄見系罪人，宜令所司疾速斷遣，無致淹滯枉濫。 」五月辛未，敕：「政化所先，獄訟攸切，不惟枉撓，兼慮滯淹。 適當長養之時，正屬熇蒸之候，累行條貫，俾速施行，靡不丁寧，未曾奏報，再頌告諭，無或因循。 應三京、鄴都、諸道州府，詔至，宜具疏放已行未行申奏，無致逗留」
In the first month of the second year of Qianyou, an edict declared: "Government prizes breadth and ease; punishments honor compassionate care—fearing that spreading abuse breeds wickedness, yet truly moved by every injury." Now, as the three origins turn and the year's four seasons begin anew, I look for peace—and nothing stands more in the way than litigation. All detainees in the three capitals, Ye, and every circuit should be examined by local chief officials in person; judgment must be fair—when the facts are clear, complete the record at once; do not prolong cases with leading questions or distort the law to harm harmonious qi." On jiawu of the fourth month, an edict said: "The monthly warning marks proper yang and minor heat approaches—the day for easing the severe and releasing the light, the season for merciful punishment and prison deliberation; the guilty should be swiftly investigated, the lightly punished promptly released, in accord with the season and without delay." Detainees in the three capitals, Ye, and every circuit should be judged and released swiftly by the responsible offices, without delay, detention, or wrongful abuse." In the fifth month, on xinwei, an edict stated: "Among the first duties of government, litigation is urgent—not only wrongful oppression but delay and detention as well." This is the season of growth and blazing heat; edicts have been issued again and again for swift execution, urged in every detail, yet never reported back; proclaim them once more and permit no further neglect." In the three capitals, Ye, and every circuit, when the edict arrives each region should memorialize in full which releases have been carried out and which have not—permit no delay."
19
周廣順三年四月乙亥，敕：「朕以時當化育，氣屬炎蒸，乃思縲紲之人，是軫哀矜之念，慮其非所，案鞫淹延，或枉濫窮屈而未得申宣，或饑渴疾病而無所控告。 以罪當刑者，惟彼自召，法不可移； 非理受苦者，為上不明，安得無慮。 欽恤之道，夙宵靡寧。 應諸道州府見系罪人，宜令官吏疾速推鞫，據輕斷遣，不得淹滯。 仍令獄吏，灑掃牢獄，當令虛歇； 洗滌枷械，無令蚤虱； 供給水漿，無令饑渴。 如有疾患，令其家人看承，囚人無主，官差醫工診候，勿致病亡。 循典法之成規，順長嬴之時令，俾無淹滯，以致治平。 」又，賜諸州詔曰：「朕以敷政之勤，惟刑是重，既未能化人於無罪，則不可為上而失刑。 況時當長嬴，事貴清適，念囹圄之閉固，復桎梏之拘縻，處於炎蒸，何異焚灼。 在州及所屬刑獄見系罪人，卿可躬親錄問，省略區分。 於入務不行者，令俟務開系； 有理須伸者，速期疏決。 俾皆平允，無至滯淹。 又以獄吏逞任情之奸，囚人被非法之苦，宜加檢察，勿縱侵欺。 常令凈掃獄房，洗刷枷匣，知其饑渴，供與水漿，有病者聽骨肉看承，無主者遣醫工救療，勿令非理致斃，以致和氣有傷。 卿忠幹分憂，仁明蒞事，必能奉詔，體我用心，眷委於茲，興寐無已。 余從敕命處分。」
In the fourth month of the third year of Guangshun, on yihai, an edict read: "At a time of nurturing transformation and blazing heat, I think of those in fetters with compassionate care—fearing that investigation drags on, that the wrongfully oppressed cannot appeal, or that hunger, thirst, and sickness find no voice." Those whose crime warrants punishment have summoned it themselves; the law cannot be bent; but those who suffer without reason do so because authority above is unclear—how can I not be concerned? The way of merciful care leaves me restless morning and night. All detainees in every circuit should be swiftly investigated by officials, judged and released according to severity, without delay. Prison officers are also ordered to sweep the prisons clean and keep cells empty whenever possible; wash cangues and restraints so lice and fleas do not infest them; provide water and thin gruel so prisoners do not hunger or thirst. When a prisoner falls ill, let his family nurse him; if he has no family, assign a government physician to treat him and prevent death from sickness. Follow established legal procedure, observe the season of growth, clear every backlog, and bring about good order." A separate edict to every prefecture read: "In governing diligently I hold punishment in the gravest regard; having not yet taught all men to live without crime, a ruler cannot afford to let the law lapse. Now the season of flourishing growth calls for clarity and ease. Men shut in prisons, bound in fetters, in sweltering heat—what is that but baking them alive? For every detainee in your prefecture and its subordinate prisons, examine each case yourself, simplifying distinctions and sorting categories. Where court is in recess and proceedings cannot proceed, postpone detention until sessions resume; where a grievance is just and must be redressed, set a swift date for release and judgment. See that every case is fair and equitable, and none left to stagnate. Prison officers too often indulge private cruelty while prisoners suffer unlawful abuse—inspect them closely and do not permit extortion or oppression. Keep cells swept clean, wash cangues and restraint boxes, supply water when prisoners hunger or thirst, let families nurse the sick and assign physicians to prisoners without kin, and never let unlawful treatment kill a man and wound the realm's harmonious qi. You are loyal, capable, benevolent, and discerning in office—you can surely carry out this edict and grasp my intent. In entrusting you with this charge, my thoughts follow you waking and sleeping. I await your disposition as the edict commands."

內外官當贖之法，梁、唐皆無定制，多示優容，或因時分輕重。 晉天福六年五月，尚書刑部員外郎李象請：「今後凡是散官，不計高低，若犯罪不得當贖，亦不得上請詳定院覆奏。 應內外文武官，有品官者自從品官法，無品官有散試官者，應內外帶職廷臣賓從、有功將校等，並請同九品官例。 其京都運巡使及諸道州府衙前職員、內外雜任鎮將等，並請準律，不得上請當贖。 其巡司馬步司判官，雖有曾歷品官者，亦請同流外職。 準律。 杖罪以下，依決罰例，徒罪以上，仍依當贖法。 」至周顯德五年七月，新定《刑統》：「今後定罪，諸道行軍司馬、節度副使、副留守，準從五品官例； 諸道兩使判官、防禦團練副使，準從六品官例； 節度掌書記、團判官、兩蕃營田等使判官，準從七品官例； 諸道推巡及軍事判官，準從八品官例； 諸軍將校內諸司使、使副、供奉、殿直，臨時奏聽敕旨。 」由是內外品官當贖之法，始有定制焉。
Rules for commutations by civil and military officials—under Liang and Tang there was no fixed code; leniency varied with the times and with perceived severity. In the fifth month of Tianfu year 6 (941), Li Xiang, vice director in the Ministry of Punishments, petitioned: "Henceforth every honorary official (san'guan), regardless of grade, who commits a crime shall neither commute sentence nor petition the Review and Verification Office for rehearing. Civil and military officials with substantive ranks should follow ranked-official law; those without rank but holding honorary or trial appointments—including courtiers with concurrent titles, imperial attendants, and meritorious commanders—should all follow the ninth-rank precedent. Capital transport and patrol agents, yamen clerks in provincial offices, miscellaneous appointees, and garrison captains should follow the ordinary statutes and may not petition to commute. Judicial officers of circuit horse and foot patrol offices, even if they had once held substantive rank, should be treated like outer-service posts. Per the statutory code. For offenses punishable by beating and below, follow ordinary sentencing; for exile and above, commutation law still applies. In the seventh month of Xiande year 5 (958), the newly issued Criminal Compendium (Xingtong) fixed sentencing: circuit campaigning marshals, deputy commissioners, and deputy overseers—fifth-rank adjunct precedent; judicial officers to dual commissioners, defense and training vice commissioners—sixth-rank adjunct precedent; commission secretaries, regimental judicial officers, and judicial officers of frontier farm commissioners—seventh-rank adjunct precedent; circuit investigation patrol and military judicial officers—eighth-rank adjunct precedent; army commanders, inner-bureau commissioners and vice commissioners, palace attendants, and duty officers in the halls—memorialize ad hoc and await imperial decision. Thus for the first time civil and military officials had a fixed code governing commutation of punishment."
