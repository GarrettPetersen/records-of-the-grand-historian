周廣順元年，太祖初即大位，惟新庶政，時太常卿邊蔚上疏請改舞名，其略云：「前朝改祖孝孫所定二舞名，文舞曰《治安之舞》，武舞曰《振德之舞》，今請改《治安》為《政和之舞》，《振德》為《善勝之舞》。 前朝改貞觀中二舞名，文舞曰《觀象之舞》，武舞曰《講功之舞》，今請改《觀象》為《崇德之舞》，《講功》為《象成之舞》。 又議改《十二成》，今改為順。 《十二順樂曲》名：祭天神奏《禋成》，請改為《昭順之樂》； 祭地祗奏《順成》，請改為《寧順之樂》； 祭宗廟奏《裕成》，請改為《肅順之樂》； 祭天地、宗廟，登歌奏《肅成》，今請改為《感順之樂》； 皇帝臨軒奏《政成》，請改為《治順之樂》； 王公出入奏《弼成》，請改為《忠順之樂》； 皇帝食舉奏《德成》，請改為《康順之樂》； 皇帝受朝、皇后入宮奏《扆成》，請改為《雍順之樂》； 皇太子軒懸出入奏《胤成》，請改為《溫順之樂》； 元日、冬至皇帝禮會，登歌奏《慶成》，請改為《禮順之樂》； 郊廟俎入奏《骍成》，請改為《禋順之樂》； 皇帝祭享、酌獻、讀祝及飲福、受胙奏《壽成》，請改為《福順之樂》。 梁武帝改《九夏》為《十二雅》，以協陽律、陰呂、十二管旋宮之義，祖孝孫改為《十二和》。 開元中，乃益三和，前朝去二和，改一雅。 今去雅，只用《十二順》之曲。 祭孔宣父、齊太公廟降神奏《師雅》，請同用《禮順之樂》； 三公升殿、會訖下階履行同用《弼成》，請同用《忠順之樂》； 享先農及籍田同用《順成》，請同用《寧順之樂》。 」曲詞文多不載。 〈 （《五代會要》：邊蔚請添召樂師，令在寺習樂。 敕太常寺見管兩京雅樂節級樂工共四十人外，更添六十人，內三十八人宜抽教坊貼部樂官兼充，餘二十二人宜令本寺照名充填。 仍令三司定支春冬衣糧，月報聞奏。 其舊管四十人，亦量添請。）〉
In the first year of the Zhou reign era Guangshun, as the Taizu had only just taken the throne and was renewing government throughout, Bian Wei, Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, memorialized the throne to change the names of certain court dances. In summary he wrote: "The previous dynasty had altered the names of the two dances that Zu Xiaosun had fixed—the civil dance had been called Dance of Pacifying the Realm and the military dance Dance of Shaking Virtue. We ask that Pacifying the Realm be renamed Dance of Harmonious Government, and Shaking Virtue Dance of Good Victory." The previous dynasty had likewise renamed the two dances of the Zhenguan period: the civil dance had been Dance of Observing the Images and the military dance Dance of Expounding Merit. He asked that Observing the Images become Dance of Honoring Virtue, and Expounding Merit Dance of Images Accomplished. He also proposed renaming the Twelve Accomplishments, substituting "accord" for "accomplishment" throughout. Among the Twelve Accord melodies: when sacrificing to the Spirit of Heaven, Yin Accomplishment was played—he asked that it be renamed Music of Bright Accord; for sacrifices to the Earth Spirits, Accord Accomplished—he asked that it become Music of Tranquil Accord; for sacrifices at the ancestral temples, Abundant Accomplishment—he asked that it become Music of Solemn Accord; when sacrificing to Heaven and Earth and at the ancestral temples, the ascending hymn had been Solemn Accomplishment—he now asked that it become Music of Responsive Accord; when the emperor presided at the imperial audience hall, Government Accomplished was played—he asked that it become Music of Ordered Accord; when princes and dukes entered and exited, Assistant Accomplishment was played—he asked that it become Music of Loyal Accord; when the emperor raised his food vessel at meals, Virtue Accomplished was played—he asked that it become Music of Healthy Accord; when the emperor received court and the empress entered the palace, Screen Accomplishment was played—he asked that it become Music of Harmonious Accord; when the crown prince, with the full suspended ensemble, entered and exited, Heir Accomplishment was played—he asked that it become Music of Gentle Accord; on New Year's Day and at the winter solstice, when the emperor held ceremonial assemblies, the ascending hymn had been Celebratory Accomplishment—he asked that it become Music of Ritual Accord; when the sacrificial stands were brought in at suburban and temple rites, Ox Accomplishment was played—he asked that it become Music of Sacrificial Accord; when the emperor offered sacrifice, poured the libation, read the prayer, drank the blessing, and received the sacrificial flesh, Longevity Accomplishment was played—he asked that it become Music of Fortunate Accord. Emperor Wu of Liang had replaced the Nine Summers with the Twelve Elegances, aligning them with the yang pitches, yin notes, and the principle of the twelve tubes in circulatory tonality; Zu Xiaosun later renamed them the Twelve Harmonies. During the Kaiyuan reign three harmonies were added; the previous dynasty then removed two harmonies and converted one elegance. He now proposed dropping the elegances and using only the melodies of the Twelve Accord. when invoking the descending spirit at the temples of Confucius and Duke Tai of Qi, Master Elegance had been played—he asked that Music of Ritual Accord be used instead; when the three highest ministers ascended the hall and, when the assembly ended, descended the steps to depart, both occasions had used Assistant Accomplishment—he asked that Music of Loyal Accord serve for both; offerings to the Spirit of Agriculture and the ceremonial plowing of the sacred field had both used Accord Accomplishment—he asked that Music of Tranquil Accord be used for both. " The lyric texts for these pieces are largely omitted here. [ (From the Institutional History of the Five Dynasties: Bian Wei asked that more musicians be recruited and trained at the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. An edict directed that beyond the forty graded elegant-music musicians in the two capitals already under the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, sixty more be added—thirty-eight to be seconded from attached musicians of the Court Entertainment Bureau, and the remaining twenty-two to be filled from the temple's own roster. The Three Offices were also to set their spring and winter clothing and grain allowances, with monthly reports to the throne. The existing complement of forty was likewise to receive proportionate increases in support.)]
3
世宗顯德元年即位，有司上太祖廟室酌獻，奏《明德之舞》。
When the Shizong took the throne in the first year of Xiande, the responsible offices offered the libation at the Taizu's shrine hall to the accompaniment of the Dance of Bright Virtue.
4
五年六月，命中書舍人竇儼參詳太常雅樂。 十一月，翰林學士竇儼上疏論禮樂刑政之源，其一曰：「請依《唐會要》所分門類，上自五帝，迄於聖朝，凡所施為，悉命編次，凡關禮樂，無有闕漏，名之曰大周通禮，俾禮院掌之。 」其二曰：「伏請命博通之士，上自五帝，迄於聖朝，凡樂章沿革，總次編錄，系於歷代樂錄之後，永為定式，名之曰大周正樂，俾樂寺掌之。 依文教習，務在齊肅。 」詔曰：「竇儼所上封章，備陳政要，舉當今之急務，疾近世之因循，器識可嘉，辭理甚當，故能立事，無愧蒞官。 所請編集大周通禮、大周正樂，宜依。 仍令於內外職官前資前名中，選擇文學之士，同共編集，具名以聞。 委儼總領其事。 所須紙筆，下有司供給。」
In the sixth month of his fifth year, the emperor ordered Dou Yi, Attendant Gentleman of the Secretariat, to assist in reviewing the court's elegant music. In the eleventh month, Hanlin academician Dou Yi memorialized the throne on the foundations of ritual, music, penal law, and administration. His first proposal read: "Following the topical divisions of the Tang Institutional Compendium, let everything enacted from the age of the Five Emperors down to our sacred dynasty be compiled in full, with nothing omitted where ritual and music are concerned. Name the work Comprehensive Rites of Great Zhou and charge the Ritual Office with its custody." " His second proposal read: "I humbly ask that learned scholars compile, from the Five Emperors to our own dynasty, the full history of every musical piece in proper sequence, appending it to the music records of past ages as a permanent standard. Name the work Correct Music of Great Zhou and place it under the Music Office." Musicians are to be trained from these texts, with uniformity and solemnity as the goal. " The imperial response read: "Dou Yi's memorial sets out the essentials of government, names the urgent tasks of our day, and condemns the slavish conformity of recent times. His judgment is admirable and his reasoning sound; he is fit to carry projects through and has nothing to be ashamed of in his office." His request to compile the Comprehensive Rites of Great Zhou and Correct Music of Great Zhou is approved. Let literary scholars be chosen from among qualified present and former officials, inside and outside the court, to compile the work jointly, and report their names. Dou Yi is placed in overall charge of the project. Whatever paper and writing materials are needed shall be furnished by the appropriate offices."

六年春正月，樞密使王樸奉詔詳定雅樂十二律旋相為宮之法，並造律準，上之。 其奏疏略曰：
In the first month of spring in his sixth year, Wang Pu, Commissioner of the Bureau of Military Affairs, was ordered to work out the method by which the twelve pitches of court music circulate as tonics; he also built a pitch standard and presented both to the throne. The gist of his memorial read:
6
夫樂作於人心，成聲於物，聲氣既和，反感於人心者也。 所假之物，大小有數。 九者，成數也，是以黃帝吹九寸之管，得黃鐘之聲，為樂之端也。 半之，清聲也。 倍之，緩聲也。 三分其一以損益之，相生之聲也。 十二變而復黃鐘，聲之總數也。 乃命之曰十二律。 旋叠為均，均有七調，合八十四調，播之於八音，著之於歌頌。 宗周而上，率由斯道，自秦而下，旋宮聲廢。 洎東漢雖有大予丞鮑鄴興之，人亡而音息，無嗣續之者。 漢至隋垂十代，凡數百年，所存者黃鐘之宮一調而已。 十二律中，惟用七聲，其餘五律，謂之啞鐘，蓋不用故也。 唐太宗復古道，乃用祖孝孫、張文收考正雅樂，而旋宮八十四調復見於時，在懸之器，方無啞者，安、史之亂，京都為墟，器之與工，十不存一，所用歌奏，漸多紕繆。 逮乎黃巢之餘，工器都盡，購募不獲，文記亦亡，集官詳酌，終不知其制度。 時有太常博士殷盈孫，案《周官·考工記》之文，鑄緌鐘十二，編鐘二百四十，處士蕭承訓校定石磬，今之在懸者是也。 雖有樂器之狀，殊無相應之和。 逮乎朱梁、後唐，歷晉與漢，皆享國不遠，未暇及於禮樂。 以至於十二緌鐘，不問聲律宮商，但循環而擊，編鐘、編磬徒懸而已。 絲、竹、匏、土，僅有七聲，作黃鐘之宮一調，亦不和備，其餘八十三調，於是乎泯絕，樂之缺壞，無甚於今。
Music originates in the human heart and takes shape as sound through physical instruments; once tone and breath are harmonized, they move the heart in turn. The instruments used for this have fixed proportions of large and small. Nine is the number of completion; the Yellow Emperor therefore blew a nine-inch tube, obtained the yellow-bell tone, and thereby founded music. Halving the length yields the clear tone. Doubling the length yields the slow tone. Adding or subtracting one-third of the length produces the mutually generating tones. After twelve changes the cycle returns to yellow bell—the full count of tones. These were named the twelve standard pitches. Circulating and layering them into keys yields seven modes per key, eighty-four modes in all, spread across the eight classes of instruments and embodied in songs and hymns. Down to the Zhou, all followed this path; from Qin onward, circulatory tonality fell into disuse. By the Eastern Han, although Bao Ye, Assistant Director of the Grand Music Office, revived it, when he died the tradition died with him and no successor carried it on. From Han through Sui stretched nearly ten dynasties and several hundred years; only the yellow-bell tonic mode survived. Of the twelve pitches only seven tones were employed; the remaining five were called mute bells, for they went unused. Emperor Taizong of Tang restored the ancient way, set Zu Xiaosun and Zhang Wenshou to correct court music, and brought the eighty-four circulatory modes back into use so that no suspended bell was left mute. After the An Lushan rebellion the capital lay in ruins; scarcely one instrument or craftsman in ten survived, and the pieces performed grew ever more corrupt. After Huang Chao's ravages, craftsmen and instruments were utterly lost; none could be bought or recruited, and the written records perished as well. When officials were gathered to deliberate, they could not recover the system at all. Yin Yingsun, erudite of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, followed the Artificers' Record in the Offices of Zhou to cast twelve sequence bells and two hundred forty chime-bells; the scholar Xiao Chengxun calibrated the stone chimes—these are the instruments that hang in the court today. Though the instruments existed in form, they produced no true harmony. Through Later Liang, Later Tang, Later Jin, and Later Han, none of these dynasties lasted long enough to attend to ritual and music. In the end the twelve sequence bells were struck in rotation with no regard for pitch or mode; chime-bells and stone chimes were mere ornaments on the rack. The string, bamboo, gourd, and clay instruments produced only seven tones in the yellow-bell tonic mode, and even that was imperfect; the other eighty-three modes had vanished entirely. Music has never been so broken as it is now.
7
陛下天縱文武，奄宅中區，思復三代之風，臨視樂懸，親自考聽，知其亡失，深動上心。 乃命中書舍人竇儼參詳太常樂事，不逾月調品八音，粗加和會。 以臣嘗學律歷，宣示古今樂錄，令臣討論，臣雖不敏，敢不奉詔。 遂依周法，以黍校定尺度，長九寸，虛徑三分，為黃鐘之管，與見在黃鐘之聲相應。 以上下相生之法推之，得十二律管。 以為眾管互吹，用聲不便，乃作律準，十三弦宣聲，長九尺張弦，各如黃鐘之聲。 以第八弦六尺，設柱為林鐘； 第三弦八尺，設柱為大簇； 第十弦五尺三寸四分，設柱為南呂； 第五弦七尺一寸三分，設柱為姑洗； 第十二弦四尺七寸五分，設柱為應鐘； 第七弦六尺三寸三分，設柱為蕤賓； 第二弦八尺四寸四分，設柱為太呂； 第九弦五尺六寸三分，設柱為夷則； 第四弦七尺五寸一分，設柱為夾鐘； 第十一弦五尺一分，設柱為無射； 第六弦六尺六寸八分，設柱為中呂； 第十三弦四尺五寸，設柱為黃鐘之清聲。 十二律中，旋用七聲為均，為均之主者，宮也，徵、商、羽、角、變宮、變徵次焉，發其均主之聲，歸乎本音之律，七聲叠應而不亂，乃成其調。 均有七調，聲有十二均，合八十四調，歌奏之曲，由之出焉。
Your Majesty, Heaven-gifted in civil and military affairs, holds the central realm and seeks to restore the ways of the Three Dynasties. Inspecting the suspended ensemble and listening for yourself, you saw how much had been lost—and were deeply moved. You then set Dou Yi, Attendant Gentleman of the Secretariat, to review the court's music; within a month the eight classes of instruments were tuned into rough harmony. Because I have studied pitch and calendrics, you showed me the music records of past and present and ordered me to investigate them. Though I am not quick-witted, how could I refuse your command? Following the Zhou method, I used millet grain to fix the standard measure—a tube nine inches long with a hollow diameter of three tenths—and made the yellow-bell pipe, which matched the yellow-bell tone then in use. By the method of ascending and descending mutual generation I derived the twelve pitch tubes. Because blowing many tubes against one another was impractical, I built a pitch standard—a nine-foot frame with thirteen stretched strings, each tuned to the yellow-bell pitch. on the eighth string, six feet long, a bridge was set for forest bell; on the third string, eight feet long, a bridge was set for great cluster; on the tenth string, five feet three and four-tenths inches, a bridge was set for southern pitch; on the fifth string, seven feet one and three-tenths inches, a bridge was set for maiden wash; on the twelfth string, four feet seven and five-tenths inches, a bridge was set for responding bell; on the seventh string, six feet three and three-tenths inches, a bridge was set for luxuriant guest; on the second string, eight feet four and four-tenths inches, a bridge was set for great pitch; on the ninth string, five feet six and three-tenths inches, a bridge was set for barren rule; on the fourth string, seven feet five and one-tenth inches, a bridge was set for wedged bell; on the eleventh string, five feet one inch, a bridge was set for unshooting; on the sixth string, six feet six and eight-tenths inches, a bridge was set for middle pitch; on the thirteenth string, four feet five inches, a bridge was set for the clear tone of yellow bell. Among the twelve pitches, seven tones circulate as keys; gong is the lord of each key, followed by zhi, shang, yu, jiao, altered gong, and altered zhi. When the lord tone is struck, the melody returns to its fundamental pitch; the seven tones answer one another in layers without confusion, and a mode is thereby formed. Each key holds seven modes, and twelve keys yield eighty-four modes in all—the pieces sung and performed proceed from them.
8
伏以旋宮之聲久絕，一日而補，出臣獨見，恐未詳悉，望集百官及內外知音者較其得失，然後依調制曲。 八十四調，曲有數百，見存者九曲而已，皆謂之黃鐘之宮。 今詳其音數，內三曲即是黃鐘宮聲，其餘六曲，錯雜諸調，蓋傳習之誤也。 唐初雖有還宮之樂，至於用曲，多與禮文相違。 既不敢用唐為則，臣又懵學獨力，未能備究古今，亦望集多聞知禮文者，上本古曲，下順常道，定其義理。 於何月行何禮，合用何調何曲，聲數長短，幾變幾成，議定而制曲，方可久長行用。 所補雅樂旋宮八十四調，並所定尺、所吹黃鐘管、所作律準，謹同上進。
I humbly note that circulatory tonality has been dead for ages; to restore it in a day on my judgment alone may not suffice. I ask that all officials and every knowledgeable person at court and beyond compare its strengths and flaws, and only then compose pieces to fit the modes. The eighty-four modes once encompassed hundreds of pieces; only nine tunes survive today, and all are called the yellow-bell gong mode. On examining their pitch sequences, only three tunes are genuinely in the yellow-bell gong; the other six mingle modes incorrectly—errors of transmission, no doubt. Early Tang did restore circulatory music, but the pieces actually used often conflicted with ritual prescriptions. I dare not take Tang as our model, and working alone I am too dull to master past and present fully. I also ask that learned men versed in ritual texts be assembled to ground the work in ancient pieces above and align it with established practice below, and so fix its principles. Which mode and piece should accompany which rite in which month, how long each passage should be, how many modulations and cadences—once these are settled and the pieces composed, the system can endure. I respectfully present the restored eighty-four circulatory modes of court music, together with the fixed measure, the yellow-bell pipe, and the pitch standard I have built.

The Shizong approved the proposal and ordered the Department of State Affairs to gather all officials for full deliberation. Zhang Zhao, Minister of War, and others submitted their deliberation:

昔帝鴻氏之制樂也，將以範圍天地，協和人神，候八節之風聲，測四時之正氣。 氣之清濁不可以筆授，聲之善否不可以口傳，故鳧氏鑄金，伶倫截竹。 為律呂相生之算，宮商正和之音。 乃播之於管弦，宣之於鐘石，然後覆載之情訢合，陰陽之氣和同，八風從律而不奸，五聲成文而不亂。 空桑、孤竹之韻，足以禮神； 《雲門》、《大夏》之容，無虧觀德。 然月律有旋宮之法，備於太師之職。 經秦滅學，雅道淩夷。 漢初制氏所調，惟存鼓舞，旋宮十二均更用之法，世莫得聞。 漢元帝時，京房善《易》、別音，探求古義，以《周官》均法，每月更用五音，乃立準調，旋相為宮，成六十調。 又以日法析為三百六十，傳於樂府，而編懸復舊，律呂無差。 遭漢中微，雅音淪缺，京房準法，屢有言者，事終不成。 錢樂空記其名，沈重但條其說，六十律法，寂寥不傳。 梁武帝素精音律，自造四通十二笛，以鼓八音。 又引古五正、二變之音，旋相為宮，得八十四調，與律準所調，音同數異。 侯景之亂，其音又絕，隋朝初定雅樂，群黨沮議，歷載不成。 而沛公鄭譯，因龜茲琵琶七音，以應月律，五正、二變，七調克諧，旋相為宮，復為八十四調。 工人萬寶常又減其絲數，稍令古淡。 隋高祖不重雅樂，令儒官集議。 博士何妥駁奏，其鄭、萬所奏八十四調並廢。 隋氏郊廟所奏，惟黃鐘一均，與五郊迎氣，雜用蕤賓，但七調而已，其餘五鐘，懸而不作。 三朝宴樂，用縵樂九部，迄於革命，未能改更。 唐太宗爰命舊工祖孝孫、張文收整比鄭譯、萬寶常所均七音八十四調，方得絲管並施，鐘石俱奏，七始之音復振，四廂之韻皆調。 自安、史亂離，鹹秦蕩覆。 崇牙樹羽之器，掃地無餘； 戛擊搏拊之工，窮年不嗣。 郊廟所奏，何異南箕，波蕩不遷，知音始絕。
When the Di Hong clan first established music, it was meant to encompass Heaven and Earth and harmonize humanity with the spirits—to heed the winds of the eight seasonal divisions and measure the true qi of the four seasons. The clarity and turbidity of qi cannot be passed on in writing; the quality of sound cannot be conveyed by word of mouth. Hence the Fu clan cast bells and Ling Lun cut bamboo pipes. They worked out the mathematics of mutual generation among the pitches and established the properly harmonious tones of gong and shang. They spread it through strings and pipes and proclaimed it on bells and stones; only then did Heaven and Earth resonate in joy, yin and yang harmonized, the eight winds kept to the pitches without straying, and the five tones formed ordered patterns. the tones of the hollow mulberry and lone bamboo sufficed to honor the spirits; the dances Cloud Gate and Great Summer did not fail to display virtue for all to see. Yet the monthly pitches employed circulatory tonality, a duty entrusted to the Grand Master of Music. After Qin suppressed learning, the elegant tradition fell into decay. In early Han the Zhi clan's tuning preserved only drum-accompanied dances; the method of rotating through the twelve circulatory keys was lost to the world. Under Emperor Yuan of Han, Jing Fang, versed in the Changes and expert in pitch discrimination, sought out ancient principles. Applying the key method of the Offices of Zhou, he rotated the five tones monthly, established a pitch standard, circulated tones as tonics, and produced sixty modes. He further divided the day-cycle method into three hundred sixty parts and handed it down to the Music Bureau; the suspended chimes were restored and the pitches aligned without error. When Han fell into mid-dynasty decline, elegant music decayed; though Jing Fang's pitch standard was often discussed, the project was never carried through. Qian Yue preserved nothing but bare names; Shen Chong merely summarized the theories—the sixty pitch laws faded into silence. Emperor Wu of Liang was deeply versed in pitch theory; he devised twelve flutes in four sets to animate the eight classes of instruments. He also drew on the ancient five correct tones and two altered tones, circulating them as tonics to produce eighty-four modes; the pitch standard tuned the same tones by a different numerical method. Hou Jing's rebellion extinguished the tradition again; when Sui first sought to fix court music, factional obstruction blocked deliberation for years. But Zheng Yi, Duke of Pei, took the seven tones of the Kucha pipa and matched them to the monthly pitches; combining the five correct and two altered tones into seven harmonious modes and circulating them as tonics, he restored the eighty-four modes. The craftsman Wan Baochang further reduced the number of strings, giving the music a somewhat antique simplicity. Emperor Gaozu of Sui cared little for court music and ordered Confucian officials to deliberate. Erudite He Tuo memorialized in rebuttal, and the eighty-four modes submitted by Zheng and Wan were abolished entirely. Sui suburban and temple music used only the yellow-bell key; for the five suburban rites welcoming the seasonal qi, luxuriant guest was mixed in—seven modes in all. The other five bells hung on the rack but were never struck. Court banquets relied on the nine divisions of slow music, and down to the dynastic transition no change was made. Emperor Taizong of Tang then set the veteran masters Zu Xiaosun and Zhang Wenshou to reconcile the seven tones and eighty-four modes of Zheng Yi and Wan Baochang; only then could strings and pipes sound together and bells and stones join in—the seven origin tones revived and harmony restored on every side. After the An Lushan rebellion, the capital region of Xian and Qin was laid waste. the lofty racks with their feathered standards were swept away entirely; craftsmen skilled in striking, beating, and clapping went years without successors. suburban and temple performances were as meaningless as the southern Winnowing Basket constellation—restless noise without true movement, and those who understood music vanished.

臣等竊以音之所起，出自人心，夔、曠不能長存，人事不能常泰，人亡則音息，世亂則樂崩，若不深知禮樂之情，安能明制作之本。 陛下心苞萬化，學富三雍。 觀兵耀武之功，已光鴻業； 尊祖禮神之致，尤軫皇情。 乃眷奉常，痛淪樂職，親閱四懸之器，思復九奏之音，爰命廷臣，重調鐘律。 樞密使王樸，采京房之準法，練梁武之通音，考鄭譯、寶常之七均，校孝孫、文收之九變，積黍累以審其度，聽聲詩以測其情，依權衡嘉量之前文，得備數和聲之大旨，施於鐘虡，足洽《簫韶》。 臣等今月十九日於太常寺集，命太樂令賈峻奏王樸新法黃鐘調七均，音律和諧，不相淩越。 其餘十一管諸調，望依新法教習，以備禮寺施用。 其五郊天地、宗廟、社稷、三朝大禮，合用十二管諸調，並載唐史、《開元禮》，近代常行。 廣順中，太常卿邊蔚奉敕定前件祠祭朝會舞名、樂曲、歌詞，寺司合有薄籍，伏恐所定與新法曲調聲韻不協，請下太常寺檢詳校試。 如或乖舛，請本寺依新法聲調，別撰樂章舞曲，令歌者誦習，永為一代之法，以光六樂之書。
We humbly hold that music arises from the human heart; even masters like Kui and Kuang cannot endure forever, nor can peace last unbroken—when the master dies the music dies, when the age falls into chaos music collapses. Without deep knowledge of ritual and music, how can one grasp the foundations of cultural order? Your Majesty's heart embraces all transformations under Heaven; your learning is steeped in the classics of the three Yong halls. your martial achievements have already brought glory to the great enterprise; and in honoring ancestors and the spirits, your imperial concern runs especially deep. You turned your attention to the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, grieved at how its musical duties had fallen away, personally inspected the four suspended ensembles, sought to restore the ninefold performances, and ordered court ministers to retune the bells and pitches. Wang Pu, Commissioner of the Bureau of Military Affairs, drew on Jing Fang's pitch standard, mastered the comprehensive tonal system of Emperor Wu of Liang, studied the seven keys of Zheng Yi and Wan Baochang, and collated the nine modulations of Zu Xiaosun and Zhang Wenshou. Measuring grain by grain to fix the scale and listening to the Sound Poems to test the tones, he followed the ancient texts on weights and measures, grasped the principle of numbered harmony, and applied it to bells and racks until the music matched the Xiao Shao. On the nineteenth of this month we assembled at the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and had Director of Grand Music Jia Jun perform the seven keys of Wang Pu's new yellow-bell tuning; the pitches were harmonious and none overrode the others. We ask that the remaining eleven pipes and their modes be taught by the new method, so the ritual office may put them into practice. The five suburban rites, sacrifices to Heaven and Earth, ancestral temples, altars of soil and grain, and the great audiences of state should employ all twelve pipes and their modes, as recorded in the Tang histories and the Kaiyuan Rites and practiced in recent generations. During Guangshun, Bian Wei, Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, had been ordered to fix the dance names, melodies, and lyrics for those sacrifices and court assemblies; the temple office should hold the records on file. We fear the established pieces may not match the new tuning, and ask that the Court of Imperial Sacrifices examine and test them. If discrepancies are found, we ask that the temple compose new ritual and dance pieces to the new tuning, have the singers learn them by heart, and establish them as the permanent standard of our age—to bring honor to the canon of the six arts of music.

世宗覽奏，善之。 乃下詔曰：「禮樂之重，國家所先。 近朝以來，雅音廢墜，雖時運之多故，亦官守之因循。 遂使擊拊之音，空留梗概； 旋相之法，莫究指歸。 樞密使王仆，博識古今，懸通律呂，討尋舊典，撰集新聲，定六代之正音，成一朝之盛事。 其王樸所奏旋宮之法，宜依張昭等議狀行。 仍令有司依調制曲，其間或有疑滯，更委王樸裁酌施行。 」自是雅樂之音，稍克諧矣。
The Shizong read the memorial and approved it. He then issued an edict: "Ritual and music are what the state must put first. In recent reigns elegant music has fallen into ruin—partly because of the turmoil of the times, but also because officials have clung to routine. so that the sounds of drum and clapper survive only as hollow outlines; and the method of circulating tones—no one can trace where it leads. Wang Pu, Commissioner of the Bureau of Military Affairs, is learned in antiquity and the present, master of pitched instruments string and bell, has recovered the old canons and composed new music, fixed the correct tones of six dynasties, and achieved a triumph for our age. Wang Pu's method of circulatory tonality shall be implemented as Zhang Zhao and the others recommended in their deliberation. Let the responsible offices compose pieces to fit the modes; should any doubts arise, Wang Pu is again entrusted to resolve them and put the system into practice. " From that point the tones of court music gradually came into harmony.
