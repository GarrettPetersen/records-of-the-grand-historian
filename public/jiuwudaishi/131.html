劉皞，字克明，晉丞相譙國公句之弟也。 句，《晉書》有傳。 皞少離鄉里，唐天祐中，梁將劉鄩襲太原，軍至樂平，時皞客於縣舍，為鄩軍所俘。 謝彥章見之，知其儒者，待之以禮，謂其鄉人劉去非曰：「為君得一宗人。 」即令皞見之，去非詢其爵裏，乃親族也，對泣久之，自是隨去非客於彥章門下。 彥章得罪，去非為郢州刺史，皞隨之郡。 莊宗平河洛，去非以嘗從劉守奇歸梁，深懼獲罪，乃棄郡投高季興於荊南，皞累為荊州攝官。 既而兄句明宗朝為學士，遣人召歸。 梁漢颙鎮鄧州，辟為從事，入為監察御史，歷水部員外郎、史館修撰。 長興末，宰臣趙鳳鎮邢臺，表為節度判官。 清泰初，入為起居郎，改駕部員外郎，兼侍御史知雜事，移河南少尹、兵部郎中，轉太府卿。 漢祖受命，用為宗正卿。 周初，改衛尉卿。
Liu Hao, whose style name was Keming, was the younger brother of Ju Gou, Duke of Qiao and chancellor of Jin. Ju has a biography in the 《Book of Jin》. Hao had left his hometown while still young. During the Tianyou reign of Tang, the Liang general Liu Yan raided Taiyuan; when his army reached Leping, Hao was staying at the county guesthouse and was taken prisoner by Yan's troops. Xie Yanzhang saw him, recognized him as a man of learning, and treated him with courtesy. He told his fellow townsman Liu Qufei, "I have found a kinsman for you." He had Hao brought before him at once. Qufei asked about his rank and home district and discovered they were relatives; they faced each other and wept for a long while. From that time on Hao followed Qufei as a client in Yanzhang's household. When Yanzhang fell from favor, Qufei was appointed prefect of Ying, and Hao accompanied him to the prefecture. After Zhuangzong pacified the Heluo region, Qufei, having once followed Liu Shouqi in defecting to Liang, was deeply afraid of being punished. He abandoned his prefecture and fled to Gao Jixing in Jingnan, while Hao held a series of acting posts in Jingzhou. Before long his elder brother Ju became a Hanlin academician under Emperor Mingzong and sent for him to return home. Liang Hanfu, as military commissioner of Dengzhou, recruited him as a staff officer. He entered the capital as investigating censor and later served as vice director of the Water Ministry and compiler at the Historiography Institute. Near the end of the Changxing era, chief minister Zhao Feng was posted to Xingtai and recommended him as military administration judge. At the start of the Qingtai era he entered court service as diarist, was reassigned as vice director of the Chariotry Ministry, and concurrently served as attendant censor in charge of miscellaneous affairs. He was then appointed vice commissioner of Henan, director of the Ministry of War, and finally grand steward of the Treasury. When the founder of Later Han took the throne, he was appointed Director of the Imperial Clan. At the beginning of Later Zhou he was reassigned as Director of the Guard.

廣順元年冬十月，稅居於東京，夜夢鬼詫之曰：「公於我冢上安床，深不奉益。 」皞問鬼姓氏，曰李丕文。 皞曰：「君言殊誤，都城內豈可冢耶？ 」曰：「冢本在野，張十八郎展城時圍入。 」忽寤。 又半月，復夢前鬼曰：「公不相信，屈觀吾舍可乎？ 」即以手掊地，豁然見華第，花木叢萃，房廊雕煥，立皞於西廡。 久之，見一團火如電，前來漸近，即前鬼也。 引皞深入，出其孥，泣拜如有所托。 皞問丕文鬼事，曰：「冥司各有部屬，外不知也。 」皞曰：「余官何至？ 」再三不對，苦訊之，曰：「齊王判官。 」皞曰：「張令公為齊王，去世久矣。 今鄆州高令公為齊王，余方為列卿，豈復為賓佐乎？ 」鬼曰：「不知也。 」皞既寤，欲掘而視之。 既而又告人曰：「鬼雖見訴，其如吾稅舍何？ 」乃止。 廣順二年春，朝廷以皞為高麗冊使。 三月，至鄆，節度使高行周以皞嗜酒，留連累日，旦夕沈醉。 其月二十三日，晨興櫛發，狀如醉寐，男泳視之，已卒矣。 〈 （《太平廣記》云：銜命使吳越，路由鄆州，卒於郵亭。）〉 時年六十一。 其年八月，鄆帥齊王高行周亦夢鬼請齊王判官，得無是乎！ 皞從儒學，好聚書，嗜酒無儀檢，然衷抱無他，急於行義，士友以此多之。
In the tenth month of winter in the first year of Guangshun, while renting a house in the Eastern Capital, he dreamed at night that a ghost reproached him: "You have set your bed on my grave mound — a grave disrespect." Hao asked the ghost's surname; it replied, "Li Piwen." Hao said, "You are quite mistaken — how could there be a burial mound inside the capital?" It answered, "The mound was originally in the open country; when Officer Zhang the Eighteenth expanded the city walls, it was taken inside." He woke with a start. Half a month later he dreamed of the same ghost again, who said, "Since you do not believe me, would you be so kind as to visit my house?" With its hand it broke open the ground, and a splendid mansion suddenly appeared, its flowers and trees thickly clustered and its halls and corridors carved and bright. It set Hao down in the west gallery. After a long while he saw a ball of fire like lightning approaching from the distance — it was the same ghost as before. It led Hao deeper inside, brought out its wife and children, and they wept and bowed as though placing someone in their trust. Hao asked about Piwen's affairs in the underworld; it said, "Each office in the Nether Bureau has its own jurisdiction — outsiders cannot know." Hao asked, "To what office will I rise?" It refused to answer again and again; when he pressed it hard, it said, "Aide to the Prince of Qi." Hao said, "Director Zhang was Prince of Qi, and he died long ago." Now High Director at Yanzhou is Prince of Qi, and I am presently a minister of the nine courts — how could I again become someone's staff aide?" The ghost said, "I do not know." When Hao awoke, he wanted to dig there and see for himself. But then he told others, "The ghost may have pleaded its case, but what am I to do about my rented house?" So he dropped the idea. In the spring of the second year of Guangshun, the court appointed Hao envoy to invest the king of Goryeo. In the third month he reached Yan, where Military Commissioner Gao Xingzhou, knowing Hao's love of wine, kept him for many days on end, drinking himself into a stupor morning and night. On the twenty-third day of that month, when he rose in the morning to comb his hair, he looked as though drunk and asleep; his son Yong looked in on him and found him already dead. Opening angle bracket marking the start of an editorial note. (The 《Extensive Records of the Taiping Era》 states: While on an imperial mission to Wuyue, he passed through Yanzhou and died at a courier station.)〉 He was sixty-one years old. In the eighth month of that year the Yan commander, Prince of Qi Gao Xingzhou, also dreamed of a ghost requesting the Prince of Qi's aide — could this not have been the same affair! Hao had devoted himself to classical learning, loved to collect books, and was fond of wine and careless of decorum, yet he harbored no hidden designs and was quick to act on principle; for this reason his scholarly friends held him in high regard.

張沆，字太元，徐州人。 父嚴，本州牙將。 沆少力學，攻詞賦，登進士第。 唐明宗子秦王好文，然童年疏率，動不由禮。 每賓僚大集，手自出題，令面前賦詩，少不如意，則壞裂抵棄。 沆初以刺謁，秦王屬合座客各為《南湖廳記》，因謂沆曰：「聞生名久矣，請為此文。 」沆不獲已，從之。 及群士記成，獨取沆所為勒之於石，繇是署為河南府巡官。 秦王敗，勒歸鄉里。 晉初，桑維翰秉政，沆以文幹進，用為著作佐郎、集賢校理，遷右拾遺。 維翰出鎮，奏為記室。 從維翰入朝，授殿中侍御史。 歲餘，自侍御史改祠部員外郎知制誥，召入翰林為學士。 維翰罷相，馮玉用事，不欲沆居禁密，改右諫議大夫，罷其職。 漢祖至汴，轉右常侍，復用為學士，未幾，遷工部尚書充職。 明年，以營奉葬事求解職，改禮部尚書。 及歸朝，復為學士。 太祖以沆耳疾罷職，改刑部尚書。 廣順二年秋，命為故齊王高行周冊贈使，復命而卒。 贈太子少保。
Zhang Hang, whose style name was Taiyuan, was a native of Xuzhou. His father Yan had been a military adjutant in their home prefecture. In his youth Hang applied himself diligently to study, specialized in regulated verse and fu, and passed the jinshi examination. The Prince of Qin, a son of Emperor Mingzong of Tang, loved literature, but even as a boy he was careless and impulsive, and his conduct seldom accorded with propriety. Whenever his guests and staff assembled in large numbers, he would set topics himself and order them to compose poetry on the spot; if anything fell even slightly short of his liking, he would tear it up and cast it aside. Hang first gained access by presenting a memorial. The Prince of Qin ordered every guest at the gathering to compose a 《Record of the South Lake Hall》 and said to Hang, "I have long heard your name — please write this piece for us." Unable to refuse, Hang complied. When the scholars' compositions were finished, the prince took only Hang's essay and had it carved in stone; on this account Hang was appointed touring officer of the Henan prefectural government. When the Prince of Qin was defeated, the inscription was removed and Hang returned to his hometown. Early in the Jin dynasty Sang Weihan held power; Hang advanced on the strength of his literary ability and was appointed assistant compiler and collator at the Jixian Institute, then promoted to right remonstrance official. When Weihan went out to command a military circuit, he recommended Hang as his secretary. When he followed Weihan back to court, he was appointed palace attendant censor. A little more than a year later he was transferred from attendant censor to vice director of the Ancestral Temple Ministry with responsibility for drafting edicts, and was summoned into the Hanlin Academy as an academician. When Weihan left the chancellorship, Feng Yu came to power and did not want Hang in the inner secretariat; Hang was reassigned as right remonstrance official and removed from his Hanlin post. When the founder of Later Han reached Bian, Hang was transferred to right regular attendant and again appointed Hanlin academician; before long he was promoted to Minister of Works while retaining that post. The following year, citing his duty in arranging imperial burial affairs, he asked to be relieved of his Hanlin duties and was reassigned as Minister of Rites. When he returned to court he was again made a Hanlin academician. Taizu removed him from the Hanlin post because of his ear ailment and reassigned him as Minister of Justice. In the autumn of the second year of Guangshun he was appointed envoy to confer posthumous honors on the former Prince of Qi, Gao Xingzhou; he returned from his mission and died. He was posthumously enfeoffed as Junior Mentor of the Heir Apparent.

沆性儒雅，好釋氏，雖久居祿位，家無餘財，死之日，圖書之外，唯使鄆之資耳。 嗣子尚幼，親友慮其耗散，上言於太祖，乃令三司差人主葬，余資市邸舍，以贍其孤焉。 沆記覽文史，好征求僻事，公家應用，時出一聯以炫奇筆，故不為馮玉所重。 雖有聵疾，猶出入金門，凡五六年。 漢隱帝末年，楊、史遇害，翌日，沆方知之，聽猶未審，忽問同僚曰：「竊聞盜殺史公，其盜獲否？ 」是時京師恟懼之次，聞者笑之。 有士人申光遜者，與沆友善，沆未病時，夢沆手出小佛塔示光遜，視其上有詩十四字云：「今生不見故人面，明月高高上翠樓。 」光遜既寤，心惡之，俄聞沆卒。
Hang was refined and courteous by nature and fond of Buddhism. Though he had long held salaried office, his household had no surplus wealth; on the day of his death, apart from his books, all he left was the allowance from his mission to Yan. His heir was still a child, and relatives and friends feared the estate would be squandered; they petitioned Taizu, who ordered the Three Offices to send men to manage the burial and use the remaining funds to buy townhouses to support the orphan. Hang had a wide memory for literature and history and loved to hunt out obscure facts; for official documents he would sometimes produce a striking couplet to show off his unusual talent, and for this reason Feng Yu did not hold him in high regard. Though he suffered from deafness, he still went in and out of the palace gates for some five or six years. In the final year of Emperor Yin of Later Han, when Yang and Shi were murdered, Hang learned of it only the next day. His hearing had still not cleared, and he suddenly asked his colleagues, "I have heard in secret that robbers killed Director Shi — have the robbers been caught?" At that moment the capital was in the grip of panic and fear, and those who heard him could only laugh. A scholar named Shen Guangxun was on friendly terms with Hang. Before Hang fell ill, Guangxun dreamed that Hang took out a small Buddhist pagoda and showed it to him; on it was a poem of fourteen characters: "In this life I shall not see an old friend's face; the bright moon climbs high above the green tower." When Guangxun awoke he was troubled by the dream; before long he heard that Hang had died.

張可復，字伯恭，德州平原人也。 父達，累贈戶部侍郎。 可復略通儒術，少習吏事。 梁末，薄遊於魏，鄴王羅紹威表為安陽簿。 唐天成初，依晉公霍彥威於青州，為從事。 晉公以其滑稽好避事，目為「奸兔兒」。 長興中入朝，拜監察御史，六遷至兵部郎中，賜金紫。 晉天福中，自西京留守判官入為秘書少監，改左司郎中。 開運中，遷左諫議大夫。 漢乾祐初，湘陰公鎮徐方，朝行中選可以從戎者，因授武寧軍節度副使、檢校禮部尚書。 及世宗鎮澶淵，改鎮寧軍節度行軍司馬。 三年，征拜給事中。 世宗嗣位，以澶淵幕府之舊，拜右散騎常侍。 顯德元年秋，以疾卒，年七十有三。 制贈戶部尚書。 可復無他才，惟以謹願保長年，加之迂懦，多為同列輕俊者所侮，而累階至金紫，居三品之秩，亦其命耶！
Zhang Kefu, whose style name was Bogong, was a native of Pingyuan in Dezhou. His father Da was repeatedly granted posthumous enfeoffment as Vice Minister of the Household Ministry. Kefu had a modest grasp of Confucian learning and in his youth trained in clerical work. Near the end of Liang he traveled briefly in Wei, where the Prince of Ye, Luo Shaowei, recommended him as recorder of Anyang. At the beginning of the Tiancheng era he entered the service of Duke Jin Huo Yanwei at Qingzhou as a staff officer. The duke, finding him witty but fond of shirking responsibility, nicknamed him "the crafty rabbit." During the Changxing era he entered court service and was appointed investigating censor; after six promotions he reached director of the Ministry of War and was granted the gold seal and purple sash. During the Tianfu era of Later Jin he moved from judicial administrator under the Western Capital's military governor to vice director of the Secretariat, then to director of the Left Department. During the Kaiyun era he was promoted to left remonstrance official. At the beginning of the Qianyou era of Later Han, when the Duke of Xiangyin was military commissioner at Xuzhou, the court chose from among the traveling officials those fit for military service and accordingly appointed Kefu vice military commissioner of Wuning circuit and acting Minister of Rites. When the future Emperor Shizong was posted at Chanyuan, Kefu was reassigned as military expedition secretary of Zhenning circuit. In the third year he was summoned to court and appointed vice director of the Secretariat. When Shizong succeeded to the throne, in recognition of their old ties from the Chanyuan staff, he was appointed right attendant at large. In the autumn of the first year of Xiande he died of illness at the age of seventy-three. By imperial edict he was posthumously enfeoffed as Minister of the Household Ministry. Kefu had no other talent; he preserved a long life solely through cautious dutifulness, and moreover he was dull and timid, so that colleagues who were quick and sharp often looked down on him. Yet he rose step by step to the gold seal and purple sash and the third rank of office — was this too simply his allotted fate!

於德辰，字進明，元城人也。 幼敏悟，篤誌好學，及射策文場，數上不調。 後唐明宗鎮邢州，德辰往謁焉，明宗見而器之，因得假官於屬邑。 後繼歷州縣，歷仕晉、漢、周，官至工部尚書。
Yu Dechen, whose style name was Jinming, was a native of Yuancheng. As a boy he was quick and perceptive, devoted in aspiration and fond of study, but at the civil examination he failed to pass several times in succession. When Emperor Mingzong of Later Tang was posted to Xingzhou, Dechen went to visit him. Mingzong took notice of him and valued his ability, and Dechen thereby obtained a provisional appointment in a subordinate county. He went on to serve in a series of prefectural and county posts through the Jin, Han, and Zhou dynasties, eventually rising to Minister of Works.

王延，字世美，鄚州長豐人也。 少為儒，善詞賦，會鄉曲離亂，不獲從鄉薦，因客於浮陽，隨滄帥戴思遠入梁。 嘗以所為賦謁梁相李琪，琪覽之，欣然曰：「此道近難其人，王生升我堂矣。 」繇是人士稱之。 尋薦為即墨縣令，歷徐、宋、鄆、青四鎮從事。 長興初，鄉人馮道、趙鳳在相位，擢拜左補闕。 逾年，以水部員外知制誥遷中書舍人，賜金紫。 清泰末，以本官權知貢舉。 時有舉子崔頎者，故相協之子也。 協素與吏部尚書盧文紀不睦，及延將入貢院，文紀謂延曰：「舍人以謹重聞於時，所以去冬老夫在相位時，與諸相首以長者聞奏，用掌文衡。 然貢闈取士，頗多面目。 說者云：『越人善泅，生子方晬，乳母浮之水上。 或駭然止之，乳母曰，其父善泅，子必無溺。 』今若以名下取士，即此類也。 舍人當求實才，以副公望。 」延退而謂人曰：「盧公之言，蓋為崔頎也。 縱與其父不悅，致意何至此耶！ 」來春，以頎登甲科。 其年，改御史中丞，歲滿，轉尚書右丞。 奉使兩浙，吳人深重之。 復命，授吏部侍郎，改尚書左丞，拜太常卿，歷工、禮、刑三尚書。 以疾求分司西洛，授太子少保。 既而連月請告，為留臺所糾，改少傅致仕。 廣順二年冬卒，時年七十有三。
Wang Yan, whose style name was Shimei, was a native of Changfeng in Meizhou. In his youth he studied the classics and excelled at regulated verse and fu. When his home district was thrown into chaos he could not obtain local nomination for office, so he went as a client to Fuyang and followed the Cangzhou commander Dai Siyuan into Liang territory. He once presented a fu he had written to the Liang chancellor Li Qi. Qi read it and said with delight, "In this art it has lately been hard to find the right man — Master Wang has raised me to the hall." From that time men of letters spoke highly of him. Before long he was recommended as magistrate of Jimo and went on to serve as staff officer at the four military circuits of Xu, Song, Yan, and Qing. At the beginning of the Changxing era his fellow townsman Feng Dao and Zhao Feng held the chancellorship and promoted him to left remonstrator. A year later he was promoted from vice director of the Water Ministry with drafting duties to central secretariat drafter and granted the gold seal and purple sash. Near the end of the Qingtai era he retained his original post while given provisional charge of the civil service examination. At that time one of the candidates was Cui Hao, son of the former chancellor Cui Xie. Xie had long been at odds with Minister of Personnel Lu Wennji. As Yan was about to enter the examination compound, Wennji said to him, "As a drafter you are known throughout the realm for your caution and gravity. That is why last winter, when I was still in the chancellorship, the other chief ministers and I first reported you to the throne as a man of standing and entrusted you with the literary examinations." Yet in the examination hall, when selecting candidates, there is all too much favoritism shown to familiar faces. People say, "The people of Yue are skilled swimmers; when a child is barely a month old, the wet-nurse floats him on the water." Some onlookers, alarmed, would try to stop her, but the wet-nurse would say, "His father is a skilled swimmer — the child will certainly not drown." 'If candidates are chosen by patronage and connections rather than merit, it is exactly this sort of thing.' As drafter, you should seek genuine talent, to meet the expectations placed upon you. After Yan withdrew, he said to others, "Lord Lu's remarks were surely directed at Cui Hao. Even if he is displeased with the father, why would he go so far in making his point! The following spring, Hao passed the examinations with the highest rank. That same year he was appointed Assistant Censor-in-Chief; when his term expired, he was transferred to Vice Director of the Right in the Secretariat. While on an imperial mission to the Two Zhes, the people of Wu held him in the highest regard. After he reported back from his mission, he was appointed Vice Minister of Personnel, then reassigned as Vice Director of the Left in the Secretariat, invested as Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, and later served in succession as Minister of Works, Minister of Rites, and Minister of Punishments. On grounds of illness he requested a supernumerary post in the Western Capital at Luoyang and was appointed Junior Tutor to the Crown Prince. Before long he took sick leave month after month and was censured by the Luoyang supervisory office; he was reassigned as Junior Preceptor and granted retirement. He died in the winter of the second year of Guangshun, at the age of seventy-three.

Li Zhisun, whose style name was Huaji, was a native of Daliang. From youth he was frivolous, sharp-tongued, and utterly lacking in propriety. During the Liang dynasty he circulated poems and verses among the inner courtiers, and through these connections he acquired an undeserved reputation. People of the day nicknamed him "Li Luo Yin." He served in succession as staff officer under several military commissioners, entered court as Left Remonstrance and Supplementation Officer, and later held the posts of vice director of the Ministries of Punishments and War, judge of the Revenue Office, and Director of the Right Bureau. After accepting heavy bribes from salt monopoly commissioner Wang Jingyu, he was demoted and sent to Jun Prefecture. At the beginning of Later Han he returned to court, was appointed Director of the Right Bureau, and concurrently served as attendant censor in charge of miscellaneous affairs. During the Guangshun era he was appointed Right Grand Master of Remonstrance and Discussion. At that time Wang Jun was Grand Councilor. Zhisun, having old ties with Jun, asked Jun to recommend him for an envoy mission to Jiangsu and Zhejiang, and Jun put the request before the throne. The Founding Emperor had long known of Zhisun's reputation and was deeply reluctant to approve the appointment. Jun said, "If this man disgraces the mission, he can always be punished afterward. Unwilling to refuse Jun's request outright, the Founding Emperor approved the appointment. Once Zhisun received his commission, he gave free rein to his absurd impulses, borrowing money from others and lavishly outfitting his entourage. Once on the road, he extorted loans in every prefecture and district he passed through, and even sent a letter to Fu Yanqing in Qing Prefecture demanding a loan of a million in cash. At courier stations his behavior was vulgar and disorderly in the extreme. When Wang Jun heard of this he reported it to the throne, and Zhisun was demoted to military adjutant at Di Prefecture. When Emperor Shizong acceded to the throne, he was eager to recruit talent. He had long heard that Zhisun was eccentric and outspoken, fond of submitting sealed memorials that might contain something useful, and wishing to hear news from beyond the capital, he ordered Zhisun recalled and promptly restored his rank. Within a few months he submitted memorials daily, mostly denouncing those close to power while angling for his own advancement. He also petitioned to be appointed envoy across the sea. Emperor Shizong flew into a rage. Because Zhisun's disgraceful conduct was growing more notorious by the day, he ordered his name struck from the rolls and sent him into exile on Shamen Island. As Zhisun was about to depart, he told his intimates, "I once met a skilled physiognomist who said that after three banishments I would reach the chancellorship. This makes the third — you had best wait for me. A little more than a year later he died at sea. Such was the measure of his absurd self-delusion. Opening angle bracket marking the start of an editorial note. (The 《Supplement to the History of the Five Dynasties》 states: Li Zhisun rose to Grand Master of Remonstrance and Discussion. Fond of frivolity, he was known to his contemporaries as "Li Luo Yin." In letters exchanged among friends and relatives, he often quoted street slang and passed it off as witty couplets. Once a court official returning from a mission gave him local products as a gift, still expecting them to be returned. Zhisun saw through the gesture and sent a letter of thanks saying, "For the moment I had planned to send them back, but I fear Your Excellency's two nets will not bring them round again." In the spring of the third year of Xiande, the imperial army marched on Guangling and threw the Jiangnan region into alarm. Li Jing appointed Shen Minister of Works and ordered him to present tribute at the imperial camp. Emperor Shizong sent Right Regular Attendant Liu Yue to accompany him and lavished generous gifts upon him. When he followed the imperial procession to the capital, he was lodged at the Capital Pavilion Station and treated with exceptional courtesy. Whenever summoned to audience he was given fine wine and questioned about affairs in Jiangnan. Shen would only say, "Wu fears Your Majesty's divine martial prowess and seeks only to submit and face north — I guarantee there is no treachery. Earlier, while Zhang Yongde was defending Xiacai, he had long been at odds with Li Chongjin. At banquets with his officers he often aired Chongjin's faults. One day, while drunk, Yongde loudly declared that Chongjin was secretly plotting treason. The officers were all shocked, and morale was thrown into turmoil. Later he secretly sent a trusted follower by courier to report to the throne, but Emperor Shizong paid no heed and was not concerned. One day Chongjin left his staff at Shouyang, went straight to Yongde's headquarters, feasted with him all day, and then departed. From that point morale gradually settled. Li Jing learned of this and secretly sent someone bearing a wax-sealed letter to Chongjin, urging him to rebel. Chongjin presented the letter to the throne. Emperor Shizong read it and saw that every word was slander meant to sow discord. Emperor Shizong, enraged that Shen's earlier assurances had been false, urgently summoned Palace Guard Commandant Han Tong and ordered Shen seized and imprisoned. Shen and more than a hundred of his followers were all executed. Opening angle bracket marking the start of an editorial note. (The 《Book of Southern Tang》 states: Emperor Shizong ordered Chief Palace Aide Cao Han to escort him to the Right Army Patrol Court. Wine was still offered, and after several cups Han rose and said, "My lord, you have given offense and are granted the privilege of taking your own life. Ji calmly straightened his robes, took up his court tablet, bowed twice toward the southeast, and said, "I have received deep grace from the throne — I accept death in gratitude. Only then did they learn what had actually happened. Commentators observed that since Shen had once brought calamity upon the people of Liang, it was only fitting that he should meet the law in a Liang prison. The way of retribution — could it be mere coincidence!
