馮道，字可道，瀛州景城人。 其先為農為儒，不恒其業。 道少純厚，好學能文，不恥惡衣食，負米奉親之外，惟以披誦吟諷為事，雖大雪擁戶，凝塵滿席，湛如也。 天祐中，劉守光署為幽州掾。 守光引兵伐中山，訪於僚屬，道常以利害箴之，守光怒，置於獄中，尋為人所救免。 守光敗，遁歸太原，監軍使張承業辟為本院巡官。 承業重其文章履行，甚見待遇。 時有周元豹者，善人倫鑒，與道不洽，謂承業曰：「馮生無前程，公不可過用。 」時河東記室盧質聞之曰：「我曾見杜黃裳司空寫真圖，道之狀貌酷類焉，將來必副大用，元豹之言不足信也。 」承業尋薦為霸府從事，俄署太原掌書記，時莊宗並有河北，文翰甚繁，一以委之。 莊宗與梁軍夾河對壘，一日，郭崇韜以諸校伴食數多，主者不辦，請少罷減。 莊宗怒曰：「孤為效命者設食都不自由，其河北三鎮，令三軍別擇一人為帥，孤請歸太原以避賢路。 」遽命道對面草詞，將示其眾。 道執筆久之，莊宗正色促焉，道徐起對曰：「道所掌筆硯，敢不供職。 今大王屢集大功，方平南寇，崇韜所諫，未至過當，阻拒之則可，不可以向來之言，喧動群議，敵人若知，謂大王君臣之不和矣。 幸熟而思之，則天下幸甚也。 」俄而崇韜入謝，因道為之解焉，人始重其膽量。 莊宗即位鄴宮，除省郎，充翰林學士，自綠衣賜紫。 梁平，遷中書舍人、戶部侍郎。 丁父憂，持服於景城。 〈 （《談苑》：道聞父喪，即徒步見星以行，家人從後持衣囊追及之。）〉 遇歲儉，所得俸余悉賑於鄉里，道之所居惟蓬茨而已，凡牧宰饋遺，鬥粟匹帛無所受焉。 時契丹方盛，素聞道名，欲掠而取之，會邊人有備，獲免。
Feng Dao, whose style was Kedao, came from Jingcheng in Ying Prefecture. His forebears had tilled the fields and studied the classics by turns, never settling into a single livelihood. As a young man Feng Dao was guileless and diligent, devoted to study and adept at writing, and unashamed of coarse food and plain dress. Beyond carrying grain on his back to provide for his parents, he spent his days reading and reciting poetry; even when snow piled against the door and dust coated his mat, he remained perfectly at ease. In the Tianyou period Liu Shouguang appointed him a staff officer in Youzhou. When Shouguang marched against Zhongshan and sought counsel from his officials, Dao repeatedly warned him of the risks and rewards involved. Shouguang flew into a rage and threw him in prison, but he was soon rescued and set free. When Shouguang fell, Dao made his way to Taiyuan, where Zhang Chengye, the military overseer, took him on as a headquarters inspector. Zhang Chengye admired both his literary gifts and his character, and showed him exceptional regard. There was a man named Zhou Yuanbao, skilled at reading character, who disliked Dao and told Zhang Chengye, "Young Feng has no bright future ahead of him. You should not rely on him too heavily. " Lu Zhi of the Hedong secretariat overheard and said, "I once saw the portrait of Chief Minister Du Huangshang. Feng Dao's face is the very image of his. He is destined for high office, and Yuanbao's judgment cannot be trusted." " Zhang Chengye soon recommended him as an aide in the princely headquarters, and shortly afterward appointed him chief secretary at Taiyuan. With Zhuangzong holding all of Hebei, the volume of official correspondence was immense, and Feng Dao handled all of it. Zhuangzong's forces faced the Liang army across the Yellow River. One day Guo Chongtao noted that too many junior officers were dining together for the commissary to supply and asked that the number be cut back. Zhuangzong burst out in anger: "I cannot even decide how to feed the men who die for me! Let each of the three armies in the three Hebei circuits pick its own commander. I will go back to Taiyuan and make way for better men." " He ordered Feng Dao at once to draft a proclamation on the spot, intending to read it to the army. Feng Dao held his brush for a long while. Zhuangzong glared and pressed him. Dao rose slowly and said, "The brush and ink are in my charge. How could I fail to do my duty? Your Highness has won victory after victory and is on the verge of crushing the southern enemy. Guo Chongtao's advice was not out of line. You may refuse it, but you must not, in a fit of anger, stir up the whole camp with words like these. If the enemy hears of it, they will think lord and minister are divided." If Your Highness will think this through carefully, the realm will be greatly fortunate." " Guo Chongtao soon came in to apologize, and Feng Dao spoke on his behalf. From that day men began to admire his nerve. When Zhuangzong assumed the throne at Ye, Feng Dao was made a bureau officer and Hanlin academician, promoted from the green robes of a junior official to purple. After the Liang were subdued, he was promoted to Secretariat drafter and vice minister of revenue. When his father died he went into mourning and observed the full rites at home in Jingcheng. 〈Note: (Tan Yuan: On hearing of his father's death, Feng Dao set out on foot by starlight. His family followed with a bag of clothes and caught up with him.)] In a year of famine he gave every spare coin of his salary to his neighbors. His home was nothing but a thatched hut, and he refused every gift of grain or cloth sent by local officials. The Khitan were then at the height of their power and had long heard of Feng Dao. They meant to capture him, but the border people were on alert and he was spared.

明宗入洛，遽謂近臣安重誨曰：「先帝時馮道郎中何在？ 」重誨曰：「近除翰林學士。 」明宗曰：「此人朕素諳委，甚好宰相。 」俄拜端明殿學士，端明之號，自道始也。 未幾，遷中書侍郎、刑部尚書、平章事。 凡孤寒士子，抱才業、素知識者皆與引用； 唐末衣冠，履行浮躁者必抑而鎮之。 有工部侍郎任贊，因班退，與同列戲道於後曰：「若急行，必遺下《兔園冊》。 」道知之，召贊謂曰：「《兔園冊》皆名儒所集，道能諷之，中朝士子止看文場秀句，便為舉業，皆竊取公聊，何淺狹之甚耶！ 」贊大愧焉。 〈 （《歐陽史》云：《兔園策》者，鄉校俚儒教田夫牧子之所誦也。 《北夢瑣言》云：《兔園策》乃徐、庾文體，非鄙樸之談，但家藏一本，人多賤之。 《困學紀聞》云：《兔園策府》三十卷，唐蔣王惲令僚佐杜嗣先仿應科目策，自設問對，引經史為訓註。 惲，太宗子，故用梁王兔園名其書，馮道《兔園策》謂此也。）〉 復有梁朝宰臣李琪，每以文章自擅，曾進《賀平中山王都表》云：「復真定之逆賊」。 道讓琪曰：「昨來收復定州，非真定也。 」琪昧於地理，頓至折角。 其後百僚上明宗徽號凡三章，道自為之，其文渾然，非流俗之體，舉朝服焉。 道尤長於篇詠，秉筆則成，典麗之外，義含古道，必為遠近傳寫，故漸畏其高深，由是班行肅然，無澆漓之態。 繼改門下侍郎、戶部吏部尚書、集賢殿弘文館大學士，加尚書左僕射，封始平郡公。 一日，道因上謁既退，明宗顧謂侍臣曰：「馮道性純儉，頃在德勝寨居一茅庵，與從人同器食，臥則芻槁一束，其心晏如也。 及以父憂退歸鄉里，自耕樵采，與農夫雜處，略不以素貴介懷，真士大夫也。 」天成、長興中，天下屢稔，朝廷無事。 明宗每禦延英，留道訪以外事，道曰：「陛下以至德承天，天以有年表瑞，更在日慎一日，以答天心。 臣每記在先皇霸府日，曾奉使中山，徑井陘之險，憂馬有蹶失，不敢怠於銜轡； 及至平地，則無復持控，果為馬所顛仆，幾至於損。 臣所陳雖小，可以喻大。 陛下勿以清晏豐熟，便縱逸樂，兢兢業業，臣之望也。 」明宗深然之。 他日又問道曰：「天下雖熟，百姓得濟否？ 」道曰：「穀貴餓農，穀賤傷農，此常理也。 臣憶得近代有舉子聶夷中《傷田家詩》云：『二月賣新絲，五月糶秋穀。 醫得眼下瘡，剜卻心頭肉。 我願君王心，化作光明燭。 不照綺羅筵，遍照逃亡屋。 』」明宗曰：「此詩甚好。 」遂命侍臣錄下，每自諷之。 道之發言簡正，善於裨益，非常人所能及也。 時以諸經舛繆，與同列李愚委學官田敏等，取西京鄭覃所刊石經，雕為印版，流布天下，後進賴之。 明宗崩，唐末帝嗣位，以道為山陵使，禮畢，出鎮同州，循故事也。 道為政閑淡，獄市無撓。 一日，有上介胡饒，本出軍吏，性粗獷，因事詬道於牙門，左右數報不應。 道曰：「此必醉耳！ 」因召入，開尊設食，盡夕而起，無撓慍之色。 未幾，入為司空。
When Emperor Mingzong entered Luoyang, he turned at once to his close adviser An Chonghui and asked, "Where is Bureau Director Feng Dao, who served under the late emperor? " An Chonghui replied, "He was recently made Hanlin academician." " Mingzong said, "I have known and trusted this man for years. He would make an excellent chief minister." " He was soon appointed academician of the Hall of Illuminated Clarity. The title itself began with Feng Dao. Before long he was promoted to vice director of the Secretariat, minister of justice, and Grand Councilor. He promoted every poor scholar of talent he had long known; and he invariably restrained and humbled those late-Tang gentlemen whose conduct was frivolous. Ren Zan, vice minister of works, once joked behind Feng Dao's back as court was dismissed: "If he hurries, he'll drop his Garden of the Hare primer." " Feng Dao heard of it and summoned Ren Zan. "The Garden of the Hare was compiled by eminent scholars," he said. "I can recite it by heart. Court scholars today skim clever examination couplets and call that learning. They steal from their betters. How shallow can one be?" " Ren Zan was deeply ashamed. 〈Note: (Ouyang Xiu's History of the Five Dynasties says: The Garden of the Hare Primers were what rustic village teachers had farm boys memorize. Beimeng Suoyan says: The Garden of the Hare Primers are written in the style of Xu Ling and Yu Xin, not in crude prose, yet though every household keeps a copy, most men look down on it. Kunxue Jiwen says: The Garden of the Hare Policy Treasury, in thirty juan, was compiled when Tang Prince Yun of Jiang ordered his aide Du Sixian to imitate civil-service policy questions, framing questions and answers and citing the classics and histories in commentary. Yun was a son of Emperor Taizong of Tang, hence the title drawn from the Liang prince's Garden of the Hare. Feng Dao's "Garden of the Hare Primers" refers to this work.)] Li Qi, a chief minister of the Liang, also prided himself on his writing. He once submitted a memorial on the pacification of Zhongshan and Wang Du that read, "We have recovered the rebel of Zhending." Feng Dao corrected him: "What was recovered was Dingzhou, not Zhending." " Li Qi knew nothing of geography and was utterly humiliated. Later, when the officials presented three chapters of honorific titles for Mingzong, Feng Dao wrote them himself. The prose was seamless and elevated, far from the common run, and the whole court admired it. Feng Dao excelled above all at poetry and prose. He took up the brush and finished at a stroke. Beyond elegance, his meaning held to the ancient way, and his work was copied far and wide. Men gradually stood in awe of his depth, and the court grew solemn, free of frivolity. He was next made vice director of the Chancellery, minister of revenue and of personnel, grand academician of the Hall of Assembled Worthies and the Hongwen Institute, promoted to left vice director of the Department of State Affairs, and enfeoffed as Duke of Shiping. One day, after Feng Dao had paid his respects and withdrawn, Mingzong said to his attendants, "Feng Dao is pure and frugal by nature. At Desheng Stockade he lived in a thatched hut, ate from the same bowl as his men, and slept on a bundle of straw, yet his mind was perfectly at ease. When he went home in mourning for his father, he plowed, gathered firewood, and lived among farmers without letting his former rank trouble him. He is a true gentleman." In the Tiancheng and Changxing reigns harvests were plentiful year after year and the court was at peace. Whenever Mingzong held court at Yanying he kept Feng Dao to discuss affairs of state. Feng Dao said, "Your Majesty rules with supreme virtue and Heaven has sent years of plenty as a sign. You must be more careful with each passing day to answer Heaven's will. I often recall that in the late emperor's days, on a mission to Zhongshan I crossed the peril of Jingxing Pass. I feared the horse might stumble and never slackened the reins. Once I reached level ground I let go, and the horse threw me. I was nearly killed. What I say is a small matter, but it stands for a larger one. Do not, because the realm is peaceful and the harvest rich, give way to ease and pleasure. Diligence and caution are what I ask of you." Mingzong was deeply moved. On another day he asked Feng Dao, "The realm has good harvests, but are the people truly provided for? " Feng Dao said, "When grain is dear the farmer starves; when grain is cheap the farmer is ruined. That is the constant law. I recall a recent candidate, Nie Yizhong, whose Lament for the Farm Household runs: "In the second month I sell the new silk; in the fifth month I pawn the autumn grain. To cure the sore before my eyes, I gouge out the flesh of my heart." "I wish the ruler's heart might become a bright shining candle, not shining on brocade banquets alone, but lighting every house of the displaced." Mingzong said, "That poem is excellent." He had his attendants copy it down and often recited it to himself. Feng Dao spoke with brevity and integrity and gave counsel of real benefit, beyond what ordinary men could offer. Because the classics were full of errors, he and his colleague Li Yu commissioned the academic official Tian Min and others to take the stone classics engraved by Zheng Tan in the Western Capital, cut them into printing blocks, and circulate them through the realm. Later generations of scholars owed him a great debt. When Mingzong died and the Last Tang Emperor succeeded, Feng Dao was made director of the imperial tomb. When the rites were complete he was posted to Tong Prefecture, following established precedent. His rule was easy and mild. Prisons and markets were undisturbed. One day a senior aide named Hu Rao, originally a military clerk and coarse by nature, reviled Feng Dao at the headquarters gate over some trifle. Attendants reported it repeatedly, but Feng Dao did not respond. Feng Dao said, "He must be drunk. He summoned Hu Rao in, opened wine, and set out food. They sat together until evening without a trace of annoyance. Before long he was recalled to the capital as Minister of Works.

及晉祖入洛，以道為首相。 二年，契丹遣使加徽號於晉祖，晉祖亦獻徽號於契丹，謂道曰：「此行非卿不可。 」道無難色。 晉祖又曰：「卿官崇德重，不可深入沙漠。 」道曰：「陛下受北朝恩，臣受陛下恩，何有不可！」 〈 （楊內翰《談苑》云：道與諸相歸中書，食訖，外廳堂吏前白道言北使事。 吏人色變手戰，道取紙一幅，署云：「道去。 」即遣寫敕進，堂吏泣下。 道遣人語妻子，不復歸家，即日舍都亭驛，不數日北行。 晉祖餞宴，語以家國之故，煩耆德遠使，自酌卮酒賜之，泣下。）〉 及行，將達西樓，契丹主欲郊迎，其臣曰：「天子無迎宰相之禮。 」因止焉，其名動殊俗也如此。 〈 （《談苑》云：契丹賜其臣牙笏及臘日賜牛頭者為殊禮，道皆得之，作詩以紀曰：「牛頭偏得賜，象笏更容持。 」契丹主甚喜，遂潛諭留意，道曰：「南朝為子，北朝為父，兩朝皆為臣，豈有分別哉！ 」道在契丹，凡得所賜，悉以市薪炭，征其意，云：「北地苦寒，老年所不堪，當為之備。 」若將久留者。 契丹感其意，乃遣歸，道三上表乞留，固遣乃去，猶更住館中月餘。 既行，所至留駐，凡兩月方出境，左右語道曰：「當北土得生還，恨無羽翼，公獨宿留，何也？ 」道曰：「縱急還，彼以筋腳馬，一夕即追及，亦何可脫，但徐緩即不能測矣。 」眾乃服。 四年二月，始至京師。）〉 及還，朝廷廢樞密使，依唐朝故事，並歸中書，其院印付道，事無巨細，悉以歸之。 尋加司徒、兼侍中，進魯國公。 晉祖曾以用兵事問道，道曰：陛下歷試諸艱，創成大業，神武睿略，為天下所知，討伐不庭，須從獨斷。 臣本自書生，為陛下在中書，守歷代成規，不敢有一毫之失也。 臣在明宗朝，曾以戎事問臣，臣亦以斯言答之。 」晉祖頗可其說。 道嘗上表求退，晉祖不之覽，先遣鄭王就省，謂曰：「卿來日不出，朕當親行請卿。 」道不得已出焉。 當時寵遇，無與為比。
When the Jin founder entered Luoyang, he made Feng Dao chief minister. In the second year the Khitan sent envoys to bestow an honorific title on the Jin founder, who in turn presented one to the Khitan. He told Feng Dao, "This mission cannot succeed without you." Feng Dao showed not the slightest hesitation. The Jin founder added, "Your rank is high and your virtue great. You must not go deep into the desert." Feng Dao replied, "Your Majesty received favor from the northern court. I received favor from Your Majesty. How could I refuse?" 〈Note: (Hanlin Academician Yang's Tan Yuan says: Feng Dao and the other chief ministers returned to the Secretariat. When the meal was finished, a clerk from the outer hall came forward to inform him of the northern envoy mission. The clerk turned pale and his hands shook. Feng Dao took a sheet of paper and wrote, "Feng Dao goes." He had an edict drafted at once and submitted. The hall clerk wept. He sent word to his wife and children that he would not come home, lodged that day at the Capital Pavilion post station, and within a few days set out north. The Jin founder gave a farewell banquet, spoke of state and family, and said he was troubling his aged virtue with so distant a mission. He poured a cup of wine with his own hand and gave it to him, weeping.)] As he traveled and was about to reach the Western Tower, the Khitan ruler wished to welcome him in the suburbs. His ministers said, "The Son of Heaven has no rite of going out to welcome a chief minister." They stopped him. Such was the way his name stirred foreign peoples. 〈Note: (Tan Yuan says: Among the Khitan, ivory tablets for ministers and ox heads on the La festival were special honors. Feng Dao received them all and wrote a poem: "Ox head uniquely bestowed, ivory tablet yet allowed to hold." The Khitan ruler was greatly pleased and secretly urged him to stay. Feng Dao said, "The southern court is son, the northern court is father. I am subject to both. How can there be any distinction?" While among the Khitan, whatever gifts he received he spent entirely on fuel and charcoal, revealing his intent: "The north is bitterly cold. Old age cannot bear it. I must prepare." As if he meant to stay for good. The Khitan were moved and sent him back. Feng Dao submitted three memorials begging to stay, but they firmly sent him away. He still lingered in the guest lodge for more than a month. Once he set out he lingered wherever he went and took two months to leave their territory. His attendants said, "In the north, to escape alive one wishes for wings. Yet you alone delay. Why?" Feng Dao said, "Even if we hurry, they have swift horses and would overtake us in a night. How could we escape? Only by going slowly can they not guess our intent." His companions were convinced. In the second month of the fourth year he finally reached the capital.)] On his return the court abolished the Bureau of Military Affairs. Following Tang precedent, its functions reverted to the Secretariat, and its seal was given to Feng Dao. Matters great and small all passed through his hands. He was soon made Minister of Education and concurrent Palace Secretary and advanced to Duke of Lu. The Jin founder once asked Feng Dao about military affairs. Feng Dao said, "Your Majesty has passed through every hardship and founded a great enterprise. Your divine martial prowess and wise strategy are known throughout the realm. Punishing the rebellious must be Your Majesty's sole decision. I was originally a scholar. Serving Your Majesty in the Secretariat, I keep the established rules of successive dynasties and dare not permit the slightest error. In Mingzong's court, when military affairs were asked of me, I answered with these same words. The Jin founder greatly approved. Feng Dao once submitted a memorial requesting retirement. The Jin founder did not read it but sent the Prince of Zheng to visit him, saying, "If you do not come out tomorrow, I shall go in person to fetch you." Feng Dao had no choice but to appear. No one in that age was favored as he was.

When the Jin Lesser Emperor succeeded, Feng Dao was made acting Grand Marshal and enfeoffed as Duke of Yan. Feng Dao once asked a court acquaintance, "What do people say about me in the Secretariat? The man replied, "Praise and blame are about equal." Feng Dao said, "Ordinary men call agreement right and disagreement wrong. Those who find fault with me are probably nine in ten. Confucius was a sage, yet Shusun Wushu slandered him. How much more should a man as slight as I expect it! Yet what I hold to, I have never changed from first to last. Later someone slandered Feng Dao to the Lesser Emperor: "He is a chief minister for peaceful times but useless in crisis, like a Chan monk who cannot call hawks!" On this account Feng Dao was posted out as military governor of Tong Prefecture. After more than a year he was transferred to Nanyang and made Palace Secretary. When the Khitan entered Bian, Feng Dao was summoned from Xiang and Deng. The Khitan ruler asked at ease, "How can the people of the realm be saved? Feng Dao said, "At this hour, even if the Buddha were reborn he could not save them. Only the emperor can." Afterward the gentry and officials were spared harm, thanks to the secret protection of Feng Dao and Zhao Yanshou. In the third month of that year he followed the Khitan north and reached Changshan with the Jin court nobles and officials. Before long the Khitan emperor died and Prince Yongkang took command of the army. As they marched north they left their clansman Jiali to hold Changshan. The Han troops were furious and together drove out Jiali, soon recovering the city. Feng Dao led his colleagues in four directions to pacify the people, adapting measures to circumstances until each man was settled. When some credited him with the victory, Feng Dao said, "What can a scholar-official do? It was all the generals' doing. Feng Dao carried such moral authority that men held him up as their model. He picked out the hardest-working, most seasoned of the generals and installed Cavalry Commandant Bai Zairong as provisional commander. Army and populace settled at once, and Feng Dao was the driving force. While at Changshan, Feng Dao saw Chinese men and women seized by the Khitan. He paid from his own funds to ransom them, placed them in a Buddhist nunnery, and later tracked down their families one by one and sent them home. The Khitan had earlier left Feng Dao at Changshan together with Li Song, He Ning, and the civil and military officials. That year, on the twenty-ninth day of the intercalary seventh month, they issued an edict recalling Song and ordering ten court scholars chosen to go to Muye Mountain on official business. The Khitan prince Mada summoned Feng Dao and the others to his tent to explain matters. Li Song happened to arrive first, grasped what was intended, and could not keep fear from his face. Mada meant to dispatch them together with the court scholars the following day. Li Song did not wait for Feng Dao, but left first with He Ning. They ran into each other outside the tent gate, then parted and went their separate ways home. Before long Li Jun and his men set fires and battled the Khitan, drums beating and spears crossing. Had they all arrived together that day, met Mada, and betrayed even a moment's hesitation, every one of them would have been taken prisoner. Contemporaries held that Feng Dao, for all his plain dress, possessed supreme integrity and, once he stood at court, commanded great respect. Hidden heaven-sent rewards of this kind were many.

In low station or high, in youth or old age, in serving parents, serving the ruler, serving elders, and facing others—I have long enjoyed Heaven's indulgence, endured many hardships yet received much blessing, and once fell into barbarian hands yet returned to China; this was not human design but Heaven's protection. Those blessed within the four quarters of the world will have a place to return to after a hundred years. Do not fill my mouth with pearls and jade; lay me out in garments of the season, bury me in a bamboo-mat coffin, and choose ground that does not take from the living—for I do not measure up to the ancients. Sacrifice with a single ram, as a restraint against slaughter; offerings should be things that do not take life. Do not set up a spirit-way stele, because no stele was placed before tombs in the Three Dynasties. Do not request a posthumous title, for I lack virtue. I also recall that from my days as a staff officer and chief minister through my border commands, any matter of slight benefit to the state was recorded in the official registers. Of the essays and poems I wrote, many were lost in the turmoil of events; what could be recovered has been compiled in a family collection. How many read my intent with understanding and how many condemn it, I do not know. I have estates, a home, and many books, and three sons who can carry on my work. Each day I wash five times and thrice examine myself; I still learn each day what I lack, and each month do not forget what I can do. As son, younger brother, minister, teacher and elder, husband, and father, with sons, a nephew-son, and grandsons, I already have more than enough in the way I have lived. But for the age it is not enough. What is not enough? I have failed to bring unity to the realm and settle the empire for my sovereign. I truly feel ashamed of the offices I have held—how can I repay what Heaven and Earth have given me? At times I open a book; at times I drink a cup. I savor food, enjoy music, and clothe myself in finery—growing old content with this age, indeed! To grow old and find joy in oneself—what greater joy could there be! Such is the Preface to a Contented Elder, written at the time in Qianyou year 3 by Zhu Mingyue.
10
及太祖平內難，議立徐州節度使劉赟為漢嗣，遣道與秘書監趙上交、樞密直學士王度等往迎之。 道尋與赟自徐赴汴，行至宋州，會澶州軍變。 樞密使王峻遣郭崇領兵至，屯於衙門外，時道與上交等宿於衙內。 是日，赟率左右甲士闔門登樓，詰崇所自，崇言太祖已副推戴。 左右知其事變，以為道所賣，皆欲殺道等以自快。 趙上交與王度聞之，皆惶怖不知所為，惟道偃仰自適，略無懼色，尋亦獲免焉。 道微時嘗賦詩云：「終聞海嶽歸明主，未省乾坤陷吉人。 」至是其言驗矣。 〈 （《青箱雜記》載馮道詩全篇云：莫為危時便愴神，前程往往有期因，終聞海嶽歸明主，未省乾坤陷吉人。 道德幾時曾去世，舟車何處不通津，但教方寸無諸惡，狼虎叢中也立身。）〉 廣順初，復拜太師、中書令，太祖甚重之，每進對不以名呼。 及太祖崩，世宗以道為山陵使。 會河東劉崇入寇，世宗召大臣議欲親征，道諫止之，世宗因言：「唐初，天下草寇蜂起，並是太宗親平之。 」道奏曰：「陛下得如太宗否？ 」世宗怒曰：「馮道何相少也！ 」乃罷。 及世宗親征，不及扈從，留道奉太祖山陵。 時道已抱疾。 及山陵禮畢，奉神主歸舊宮，未及祔廟，一夕薨於其第，時顯德元年四月十七日也，享年七十有三。 世宗聞之，輟視朝三日，冊贈尚書令，追封瀛王，謚曰文懿。
After the Founding Emperor quelled the internal crisis, the court resolved to install Liu Yun, military governor of Xuzhou, as heir to the Later Han and sent Feng Dao with Zhao Shangjiao, director of the Secretariat, Wang Du, privy council academician, and others to welcome him. Feng Dao and Liu Yun soon set out from Xuzhou for Bianliang. When they reached Songzhou, they encountered the mutiny of the troops at Chanzho. Privy commissioner Wang Jun sent Guo Chong with troops, who encamped outside the yamen gate. Feng Dao and Zhao Shangjiao and the others were lodging inside. That day Liu Yun led his armored guards, shut the gate, and mounted the tower to question Guo Chong's origin. Chong replied that the Founding Emperor had already been acclaimed. When his attendants learned what had happened, they believed Feng Dao had betrayed them and all wanted to kill him and his companions to vent their rage. Zhao Shangjiao and Wang Du were terrified and did not know what to do. Only Feng Dao reclined at ease without the slightest sign of fear, and in the end they all escaped harm. In his early days Feng Dao once wrote a poem: "In the end I hear that seas and mountains return to a wise ruler; I have never seen Heaven and Earth destroy a good man. " And so at last his words came true. 〈Note: (Green Box Miscellany records Feng Dao's complete poem: Do not let perilous times at once break your spirit; the road ahead often has its appointed cause; in the end I hear that seas and mountains return to a wise ruler; I have never seen Heaven and Earth destroy a good man. When has a worthy man ever truly left the world? Where have boats and carts not found a crossing? Keep your heart free of evil, and even among wolves and tigers you may stand upright.)] Early in the Guangshun era he was again appointed grand preceptor and director of the Secretariat. The Founding Emperor held him in high esteem and never addressed him by name when he came to audience. When the Founding Emperor died, Emperor Shizong appointed Feng Dao commissioner for the imperial tomb. When Liu Chong of Hedong invaded, Shizong summoned his ministers to discuss a personal campaign. Feng Dao remonstrated against it, whereupon Shizong said: "In early Tang, bandits rose everywhere like hornets, and Taizong personally pacified them all. " Feng Dao replied: "Can Your Majesty be like Taizong? " Shizong angrily said: "How little respect Feng Dao shows his emperor! " With that the audience ended. When Shizong marched in person, Feng Dao could not accompany him and was left behind to oversee the Founding Emperor's tomb. By then Feng Dao was already ill. When the tomb rites were finished, he escorted the spirit tablet back to the old palace. Before it could be enshrined in the ancestral temple, he died one evening at his residence on the seventeenth day of the fourth month of Xiande year 1, at the age of seventy-three. When Shizong heard the news, he suspended court for three days, posthumously bestowed on him the title of director of the Department of State Affairs, enfeoffed him as Prince of Ying, and gave him the posthumous name Wenyi.
11
道歷任四朝，三入中書，在相位二十餘年，以持重鎮俗為己任，未嘗以片簡擾於諸侯，平生甚廉儉。 逮至末年，閨庭之內，稍徇奢靡，其子吉，尤恣狂蕩，道不能制，識者以其不終令譽，鹹嘆惜之。 〈 （《五代史補》：馮道之鎮同州也，有酒務吏乞以家財修夫子廟，道以狀付判官參詳其事。 判官素滑稽，因以一絕書判後云：「荊棘森森繞杏壇，儒官高貴盡偷安，若教酒務修夫子，覺我慚惶也大難。 」道覽之有愧色，因出俸重創之。 馮瀛王道之在中書也，有舉子李導投贄所業，馮相見之，戲謂曰：「老夫名道，其來久矣，加以累居相府，秀才不可謂不知，然亦名導，於禮可乎？ 」李抗聲對曰：「相公是無寸底道字，小子有寸底導字，何謂不可也！ 」公笑曰：「老夫不惟名無寸，諸事亦無寸，吾子可謂知人矣。 」了無怒色。 馮吉，瀛王道之子，能彈琵琶，以皮為弦，世宗嘗令彈於御前，深欣善之，因號其琵琶曰「繞殿雷」也。 道以其惰業，每加譴責，而吉攻之愈精，道益怒，凡與客飲，必使庭立而彈之，曲罷或賜以束帛，命背負之，然後致謝。 道自以為戒勖極矣，吉未能悛改，既而益自若。 道度無可奈何，嘆曰：「百工之司藝而身賤，理使然也。 此子不過太常少卿耳。 」其後果終於此。）〉
Feng Dao served four dynasties, entered the Secretariat three times, and held the chancellorship for more than twenty years. He made steady governance and the settling of custom his duty, never troubling the regional lords with so much as a note, and lived his life in great frugality. In his final years his household grew somewhat extravagant. His son Ji was especially wild and unrestrained, and Feng Dao could not restrain him. Those who knew him sighed that he had not preserved his good name to the end. 〈Note: (Supplement to the History of the Five Dynasties: When Feng Dao was military governor of Tongzhou, a clerk in the wine monopoly asked to repair the Confucius temple with his family fortune. Feng Dao forwarded the petition to the judicial assistant for review. The judicial assistant was a wit by nature and wrote a quatrain on the back of the judgment: "Thorns stand thick around the apricot altar; lofty Confucian officials all live in ease. If a wine-office clerk repairs Confucius's temple, how hard it is not to feel ashamed! " When Feng Dao read it his face showed shame, and he paid for a major rebuilding out of his own salary. When Prince of Ying Feng Dao was at the Secretariat, a candidate named Li Dao submitted his examination work. Feng Dao saw him and said in jest: "This old man's name is Dao, and has been so for a long time. Having served repeatedly as chancellor, you cannot claim not to know that. Yet your name is also Dao—is that proper? " Li Dao answered boldly: "Your Excellency's Dao is the character without an inch at the bottom; this student's Dao is the character with an inch at the bottom. Why should it not be acceptable! " Feng Dao laughed and said: "This old man not only has no inch in his name—in all matters I have no inch either. You may truly be called one who knows men. " He showed no anger at all. Feng Ji, son of Prince of Ying Feng Dao, could play the pipa with leather strings. Shizong once had him perform before the throne, was deeply pleased, and named his pipa "Hall-Ringing Thunder." Feng Dao rebuked him constantly for this idle pursuit, but Ji practiced ever more diligently, which only angered his father further. Whenever Feng Dao entertained guests, he made Ji stand in the courtyard and play. When the piece ended he sometimes rewarded him with bolts of silk and ordered him to carry them on his back before he might give thanks. Feng Dao thought he had admonished and disciplined him to the utmost, but Ji would not reform and only grew more self-assured. Seeing there was nothing to be done, Feng Dao sighed: "Craftsmen who master an art yet remain low in station—that is simply the way of things. This son will rise no higher than vice director of the Court of Imperial Rites. " In the end that is exactly where he ended.)]
12
史臣曰：道之履行，郁有古人之風； 道之宇量，深得大臣之禮。 然而事四朝，相六帝，可得為忠乎！ 夫一女二夫，人之不幸，況於再三者哉！ 所以飾終之典，不得謚為文貞、文忠者，蓋謂此也。
The historiographer writes: In conduct and integrity Feng Dao richly possessed the spirit of the ancients; in breadth of character he deeply fulfilled the duties of a great minister. Yet to have served four dynasties and been chancellor to six emperors—can that be called loyalty? For one woman to have two husbands is a misfortune—how much more when it happens again and again! That is why, in the rites honoring the dead, he was not given the posthumous names Wen Zhen or Wen Zhong.
