杜重威，其先朔州人，近世徙家於太原。 祖興，振武牙將。 父堆金，事唐武皇為先鋒使。 重威少事明宗，自護聖軍校領防州刺史。 其妻即晉高祖妹也，累封宋國大長公主。 天福初，命重威典禁軍，遙授舒州刺史。 二年，張從賓構亂，據汜水，晉高祖遣重威與候益率眾破之，以功授潞州節度使。 與楊光遠降範延光於鄴城，改許州節度使，兼侍衛親軍馬步軍副指揮使，尋加同平章事。 未幾，移鎮鄆州，遷侍衛親軍馬步軍都指揮使。 〈 （《通鑒》：馮道、李崧屢薦重威之能，以為都指揮使，充隨駕御營使。）〉 及鎮州安重榮稱兵向闕，命重威禦之，敗重榮於宗城。 重榮奔據常山，重威尋拔其城，斬重榮首傳於闕下，授成德軍節度使。 所得重榮家財及常山公帑，悉歸於己，晉高祖知而不問。 至鎮，復重斂於民，稅外加賦，境內苦之 〈 （《通鑒》：重威所至黷貨，民多逃亡，嘗出過市，謂左右曰：「人言我驅盡百姓，何市人之多也！」）〉
Du Chongwei came from Shuozhou stock; in recent generations his family had settled in Taiyuan. His grandfather Xing had served as a militia officer at Zhenwu. His father Duijin had served Li Keyong as commissioner of the vanguard. As a young man he entered the service of Emperor Mingzong, rising from a company officer in the Imperial Guard to the prefecture of Fang. His wife was a younger sister of the Later Jin founder; she was eventually enfeoffed as Grand Princess of Song. At the opening of the Tianfu reign he was placed in command of the palace guard and given the honorary title of prefect of Shu. In the second year, when Zhang Congbin rebelled and held the Meng crossing, the Jin founder sent Chongwei with Hou Yi to crush him; for this he was made military commissioner of Lu. He and Yang Guangyuan induced Fan Yanguang to surrender at Ye; he was then made military commissioner of Xu, deputy commander of the palace armies, and soon after also co-director of the Secretariat-Chancellery. Not long after he was shifted to Yan Prefecture and advanced to full commander of the palace cavalry and infantry. 〈Note: (The Comprehensive Mirror records: Feng Dao and Li Song repeatedly commended Chongwei's ability, securing his appointment as commander-in-chief and assigning him to oversee the emperor's accompanying camp.)〉 When An Chongrong of Zhenzhou raised an army and marched on the capital, Chongwei was ordered against him and defeated him at Zongcheng. Chongrong fled to Changshan, which Chongwei soon took; he beheaded Chongrong and sent his head to the capital, receiving appointment as military commissioner of Chengde. He appropriated all of Chongrong's household property and the Changshan treasury for himself; the Jin founder knew yet asked nothing. Once in his command, he taxed the people again with heavy exactions beyond the regular levies, and the region groaned under it 〈Note: (The Comprehensive Mirror states: Wherever Chongwei served he plundered, and the people fled in droves. Once, passing through the marketplace, he said to his attendants, "They say I have driven out every commoner—then why are there so many people in the market!")〉
2
少帝嗣位，與契丹絕好，契丹主連年伐晉，重威但閉壁自守。 部內城邑相繼破陷，一境生靈受屠戮，重威任居方面，未嘗以一土一騎救之。 每敵騎數十驅漢人千萬過城下，如入無人之境，重威但登陴註目，略無邀取之意。 開運元年秋，加北面行營招討使。 二年，領大軍下新州、滿城、遂城。 契丹主自古北口回軍，追躡王師，重威等狼狽而旋，至陽城，為契丹所困。 會大風狂猛，軍情憤激，府彥卿、張彥澤等引軍四出，敵眾大潰，諸將欲追之，重威曰：「逢賊得命，更望復子乎！ 」遂收軍馳歸常山。 先是，重威於州內括借錢帛，吏民大被其苦，人情鹹怨，重以境內雕弊，十室九空，重威遂無留意，連上表乞歸朝，不俟報即時上路。 朝廷以邊上重鎮，主帥擅離，茍有奔沖，慮失禦備，然亦無如之何，即以馬全節代之，重威尋授鄴都留守。 會鎮州軍食不繼，遣殿中監王欽祚就本州和市，重威私第有粟十餘萬斛，遂錄之以聞。 朝廷給絹數萬匹，償其粟直。 重威大忿曰：「我非反逆，安得籍沒耶！ 」三年冬，晉少帝詔重威與李守貞等率師經略瀛、鄚。 師至瀛州城下，晉騎將梁漢璋與契丹接戰，漢璋死焉。 重威即時回軍，次武強，聞契丹主南下，乃西趨鎮州，至中渡橋，與契丹夾滹水而營。 十二月八日，宋彥筠、王清等率數千人渡滹沲，陣於北岸，為敵所破。 時契丹遊軍已至欒城，道路隔絕，人情危蹙，重威密遣人詣敵帳，潛布腹心。 契丹主大悅，許以中原帝之，重威庸暗，深以為信。 一日，伏甲於內，召諸將會，告以降敵之意，諸將愕然。 以上將既變，乃俯首聽命，遂連署降表，令中門使高勛賫送敵帳，軍士解甲，舉聲慟哭。 是日，有大霧起於降軍之上。 契丹主使重威衣赭袍以示諸軍，尋偽加守太傅，鄴都留守如故。 契丹主南行，命重威部轄晉軍以從，既至東京，駐晉軍於陳橋，士伍饑凍，不勝其苦。 重威每出入衢路，為市民所詬，俯首而已。 契丹下令括率京城錢帛，將相公私，雷同率配，重威與李守貞各萬緡，乃告契丹主曰：「臣等以十萬漢軍降於皇帝，不免配借，臣所不甘。 」契丹主笑而免之。 尋群盜斷澶州浮梁，契丹乃遣重威歸藩。 明年三月，契丹主北去，至相州城下，重威與妻石氏詣牙帳貢獻而回。
After the Young Emperor succeeded, the court broke with the Khitan; the Khitan khan invaded Jin year upon year, while Chongwei only shut himself behind his walls. Town after town in his command fell; the people of the whole region were butchered, yet though he held a frontier post he never sent a single soldier or patch of territory to their relief. Often a few dozen enemy horsemen would drive millions of Han subjects past the walls, as through empty land; Chongwei would only climb the ramparts and watch, scarcely meaning to strike at them. In the autumn of the first Kaiyun year he was made commander of the northern expeditionary force. In the second year he led the main army against Xin, Mancheng, and Suicheng. The Khitan khan marched back from Guyuan and pursued the imperial force; Chongwei's column fell back in disorder and at Yangcheng was trapped by the Khitan. A fierce wind sprang up and the troops' anger boiled over; Fu Yanqing, Zhang Yanze, and others led sorties on every side and the enemy broke completely. When the generals wanted to pursue, Chongwei said, "We met the enemy and barely kept our lives—do we still expect to see our children again? " He then drew off the army and rode back to Changshan. He had earlier forced loans of money and cloth from the prefecture, to the people's great suffering, and resentment ran deep. With the land inside his borders wasted and nine houses in ten empty, he lost heart, sent memorial after memorial asking leave to return to court, and took the road without waiting for a reply. The court saw a critical frontier whose commander had quit on his own; any sudden thrust might leave them undefended, yet they could do nothing. Ma Quanjie replaced him at once, while Chongwei was soon named defender of Yedu. When Zhenzhou ran short of rations, the palace supervisor Wang Qinzuo was sent to buy grain there; more than one hundred thousand piculs were found in Chongwei's private store and reported to court. The court paid him several ten thousands of bolts of silk as the grain's price. Furious, he cried, "I am no rebel—why treat me like a confiscation case! " In the winter of the third year the Young Emperor ordered Chongwei and Li Shouzhen east against Ying and Mo. Below the walls of Yingzhou the Jin cavalry commander Liang Hanzhang met the Khitan in battle and was killed. Chongwei withdrew at once and encamped at Wuqiang; hearing the Khitan khan was moving south, he hurried to Zhenzhou and at Zhongdu Bridge pitched camp across the Hutuo from the enemy. On the eighth of the twelfth month Song Yanzhi and Wang Qing led a few thousand men across the river and formed on the north bank; the enemy shattered them. Khitan scouts had already reached Luancheng, the roads were cut, and morale was desperate. Chongwei secretly sent envoys to the enemy camp and planted intimates there. The Khitan khan was delighted and promised him the Central Plains throne; dull and credulous, Chongwei believed it completely. One day he hid armed men inside, summoned the generals, and declared he would surrender; they were stunned. With their commander already decided, they had no choice but to obey. They signed a joint surrender, sent the inner-gate commissioner Gao Xun with it to the enemy camp, the troops stripped their armor, and a great wailing broke out. That day a great fog rose over the surrendered host. The Khitan khan had him dress in ochre robes to display before the armies; he was soon given the false title Grand Tutor while keeping Yedu. The khan marched south and put the Jin troops under Chongwei; at the Eastern Capital they were held at Chenqiao, starving and freezing past endurance. Whenever he passed through the streets townspeople cursed him; he only lowered his head. The Khitan ordered a levy on the capital's wealth from ministers down, public and private alike; Chongwei and Li Shouzhen each owed ten thousand strings. They pleaded, "We led a hundred thousand Han troops to surrender to Your Majesty, yet are still forced to pay—this we cannot stomach. " The khan laughed and let them off. Soon rebels severed the Cangzhou pontoon bridge; the Khitan then sent him back to his post. The next year, in the third month, the khan marched north; at Xiangzhou Chongwei and his wife Lady Shi came to the tent with tribute and returned.

高祖車駕至闕，以重威為宋州節度使，加守太尉。 重威懼，閉城拒命，詔高行周率兵攻討，重威遣其子宏遂等告急於鎮州州麻答，乞師救援，旦以宏遂為質，麻答遣蕃將楊兗赴之。 未幾，鎮州軍逐滿達勒，楊兗至洺州而回。 十月，高祖親征，車駕至鄴城之下，遣給事中陳觀等賫詔入城，許其歸命，重威不納。 數日，高祖親率諸軍攻其壘，不克，王師傷夷者萬餘人。 〈 （《宋史·杜漢徽傳》云：從高行周討杜重威於鄴城，屢為流矢所中，身被重創，猶力戰，觀者壯之。）〉 高祖駐軍數旬，城中糧盡，屑麹餅以給軍士，吏民逾壘而出者甚眾，皆無人色。 至是，重威牙將詣行宮請降，復遣節度判官王敏奉表請罪，賜優詔敦勉，許其如初。 重威即遣其子宏遂、妻石氏出候高祖，重威繼踵出降，素服俟罪，復其衣冠，賜見，即日制授檢校太師、守太傅、兼中書令。 鄴城士庶，殍殕者十之六七。 先是，契丹遣幽州指揮使張璉，以部下軍二千餘人屯鄴，時亦有燕軍一千五百人在京師，會高祖至闕，有上變者，言燕軍謀亂，盡誅於繁臺之下，咸稱其冤。 有逃奔於鄴者，備言其事，故張璉等懼死，與重威膠固守城，略無叛誌。 高祖亦悔其前失，累令宣諭，許以不死。 璉等於城上揚言曰：「繁臺之誅，燕軍何罪？ 既無生理，以死為期。 」璉一軍在圍中，重威推食解衣，盡力姑息。 燕軍驕悍，憑陵吏民，子女金帛，公行豪奪。 及重威請命，璉等邀朝廷信誓，詔許璉等卻歸本土。 及出降，盡誅璉等將數十人，其什長已下放歸幽州，將出漢境，剽略而去。 高祖遣三司使王章、樞密副使郭威，錄重威部下將吏盡誅之，籍其財產與重威私帑，分給將士。
When Liu Zhiyuan's procession reached the capital, Chongwei was named military commissioner of Song and honorary Grand Tutor. Terrified, he shut the gates and defied the court. Gao Xingzhou was ordered against him; Chongwei sent his son Hongsui to Ma Da at Zhenzhou begging troops, offering the boy as hostage each dawn; Ma Da sent the tribal officer Yang Yan. Soon Zhenzhou's garrison expelled Mandale, and Yang Yan reached Luozhou only to turn back. In the tenth month Liu Zhiyuan campaigned in person; below Ye he sent Chen Guan with an edict into the city offering mercy if he surrendered; Chongwei refused. Days later the emperor led the assault but failed; imperial casualties ran above ten thousand. 〈Note: (The History of Song, Biography of Du Hanhui: In the campaign against Du Chongwei at Ye under Gao Xingzhou he was hit again and again by stray arrows and bore heavy wounds all over, yet kept fighting; observers were stirred by his courage.)〉 The emperor camped there for weeks; inside, grain ran out and the garrison ate bran cakes; multitudes climbed out over the walls to escape, faces bloodless. Then his staff officers came to the emperor's camp to sue for surrender; Wang Min followed with a memorial of repentance; a generous edict reassured him that terms would stand as before. He sent Hongsui and Lady Shi ahead to meet the emperor, then came out himself in mourning dress to await judgment; his robes were restored, he was received in audience, and that day was made Grand Master, honorary Grand Tutor, and Chancellor. Of Ye's townspeople, six or seven in ten died. Earlier the Khitan had left Zhang Lian of Youzhou with just over two thousand men in Ye, while fifteen hundred Yan troops were also in the capital. When Liu Zhiyuan arrived, a report claimed the Yan soldiers meant revolt; all were killed below Fantai, and many called it a wrongful slaughter. Escapees reached Ye with full accounts, so Zhang Lian and the rest, fearing execution, held the city grimly with Chongwei, with no real intent to rebel. The emperor too regretted his earlier act and repeatedly proclaimed they would not be put to death. From the wall Zhang Lian called out, "At Fantai, what crime did the Yan soldiers commit? Given no way to live, we look only to die fighting." Zhang Lian's force was trapped in the siege; Chongwei shared his rations and gave them his own clothes, indulging them to the limit. The Yan soldiers were brutal and overbearing; they openly seized people's daughters, gold, and goods. When Chongwei sought to capitulate, Zhang Lian demanded sworn guarantees; an edict promised the Yan troops safe return home. After the surrender dozens of officers including Zhang Lian were executed; lower ranks had already been sent toward Youzhou; just before leaving Han territory they looted the countryside on their way out. The emperor sent Wang Zhang of the Three Bureaus and Guo Wei, vice director of the Secretariat, to register and execute all of Chongwei's officers, seize their property with his private hoard, and distribute it among the troops.

車駕還宮，高祖不豫，既而大漸。 顧命之際，謂近臣將佐曰：「善防重威。 」帝崩，遂收重威，重威子宏璋、宏璉、宏傑誅之。 詔曰：「杜重威猶貯禍心，未悛逆節，梟音不改，虺性難馴。 昨朕小有不安，罷朝數日，而重威父子潛肆兇言，怨謗大朝，扇惑小輩； 今則顯有陳告，備驗奸期。 既負深恩，須置極法，其杜重威父子並處斬。 所有晉朝公主及外親族，一切如常，仍與供給。 」重威父子已誅，陳屍於通衢，都人聚觀者詬罵蹴擊，軍吏不能禁，屍首狼籍，斯須而盡。
After the return to the palace the emperor fell ill and soon grew desperately sick. On his deathbed he told his close advisers, "Guard against Chongwei carefully. " When the emperor died Chongwei was seized at once, with his sons Hongzhang, Honglian, and Hongjie. The edict read, "Du Chongwei still nurses treason and has not mended his rebellion; his nature remains vicious and his malice intractable. When I fell briefly ill and missed court for several days, father and son secretly spoke violent words, reviling the dynasty and stirring up petty followers; now clear reports have come in and their treachery is fully proved. Having betrayed profound favor, they merit the harshest punishment: father and son shall be beheaded. All Later Jin princesses and affinal kin are to be treated as before, with support continued. Once they were dead their bodies were left on the highway; crowds cursed them, kicked them, and tore them apart beyond the guards' control until nothing remained.

Honglian, another of Chongwei's sons, had risen to prefect of Chen. 〈Note: (The Longping Records state: Dang Jin had been a boy slave to Du Chongwei, military commissioner of Tianxiong; Chongwei prized his plain dutifulness and, though he grew up, still kept him among the serving women. 李守貞，河陽人也。 少桀黠落魄，事本部為牙將。 晉高祖鎮河陽，用為典客，後移數鎮，皆從之。 及即位，累遷至客省使。 天福中，李金全以安州叛，淮夷入寇，晉高祖命馬全節討之，守貞監護其軍，賊平，守貞為宣徽使。 少帝即位，授滑州節度兼侍衛馬軍都指揮使，未幾，改侍衛都虞候。 開運元年春，敵眾犯澶、魏，少帝幸澶州，契丹遣將麻答以奇兵由鄆州馬家口濟河，立柵於東岸，守貞率師自澶州馳赴之。 契丹大敗，溺死者數千人，獲馬數百匹，偏裨七十餘人。 有頃，敵退。 晉少帝還京，以守貞為兗州節度使，依前侍衛都虞候。 五月，以守貞為青州行營都部署，率兵二萬東討楊光遠，命符彥卿為副。 十一月，光遠子承勛等乞降，守貞入城，害光遠於別第。 光遠有孔目官吏宋顏者，盡以光遠財寶、名姬、善馬告於守貞，得之置於帳下。 近例，官軍克復城隍，必降德音，洗滌瑕穢，時樞密使桑維翰以光遠同惡數十輩潛竄未出，搜索甚急，故制書久不下。 或有告宋顏匿於守貞處者，朝廷取而殺之，守貞由是怨維翰。 時行營將士所給賞賜，守貞盡以黦茶、染木、姜藥之類分給之，軍中大怨，乃以帛包所得物，如人首級，目之為守貞頭，懸於樹以詛之。 守貞班師，加同平章事，以楊光遠東京第賜之。 守貞因取連宅軍營，以廣其第，大興土木，治之歲餘，為京師之甲。 行幸賜宴，恩禮無比。
Li Shouzhen came from Heyang. In youth he was unruly, sharp-witted, and destitute; he served his circuit as a militia officer. When the Jin founder governed Heyang he used him on ceremonial duty, and Shouzhen followed him through each subsequent command. After his accession Shouzhen rose step by step to Commissioner of the Guest Bureau. During Tianfu, when Li Jinquan rebelled at Anzhou and Huai enemies raided in, the founder sent Ma Quanjie to suppress them with Shouzhen overseeing the campaign; after victory he became Palace Secretary. Under the Young Emperor he became commissioner of Hua and commander of the palace cavalry, then soon chief commander of the palace guards. In the first Kaiyun spring the enemy struck Cang and Wei; the Young Emperor went to Cangzhou; the Khitan general Ma Da crossed at Yanzhou's Ma family ford with picked troops and entrenched on the east bank; Shouzhen raced from Cangzhou to meet him. The Khitan were routed; thousands drowned; hundreds of horses and more than seventy officers were taken. Shortly afterward the enemy withdrew. The Young Emperor returned to the capital, made him commissioner of Yan, and kept him as chief palace guard commander. In the fifth month he was named eastern expedition commander with twenty thousand men against Yang Guangyuan, with Fu Yanqing as his deputy. In the eleventh month Chengxun asked to surrender; Shouzhen entered the city and killed Guangyuan in his private quarters. A clerk named Song Yan revealed all of Guangyuan's wealth, noted concubines, and best horses; Shouzhen took them into his own camp. By custom, recapture of a city brought an amnesty to clear petty guilt; but Sang Weihan at the Secretariat kept the edict back while dozens of Guangyuan's accomplices were hunted down. When word came that Song Yan was with Shouzhen, court agents seized and killed him, and Shouzhen bore a grudge against Weihan. When rewards came, Shouzhen handed out only cheap tea, dye-woods, ginger, and the like; the troops in fury bundled the goods like severed heads, called them "Shouzhen heads," and hung them on trees as curses. On his return he was made co-chancellor and given Guangyuan's mansion in the Eastern Capital. He seized neighboring garrison compounds to expand the estate, built on a grand scale for more than a year, and produced the finest house in the capital. When the emperor traveled and feasted him, the honor shown him had no equal.

In the spring of the second Kaiyun year the Khitan came south with their whole host; their van reached Tangyin in Xiangzhou, and Shouzhen was ordered to hold Hua. The Young Emperor went again to Cangzhou and named Shouzhen superintendent of the northern camp; with Du Chongwei as campaign commander they marched north, and after the victory at Yangcheng drew off the army. In the fourth month the emperor returned to the capital, made him deputy commander of the palace guard, shifted him to Songzhou, and gave him the honorary title Grand Master. In the third year spring he was ordered to patrol the frontier. At Hengshui he captured Zhao Siying, prefect of Mo, and returned. Soon he replaced Gao Xingzhou as commander of the palace armies and was moved to Yanzhou, nursing considerable resentment. When Chancellor Li Song was made Grand Mentor, Shouzhen told the Secretariat academician Yin Peng, "What has the Secretariat done to be made chief minister so soon! Earlier Sang Weihan, a founding veteran, had been director of the Secretariat; Shouzhen had always stood beneath him and feared him; working with Li Yantao, Feng Yu, and others he drove Weihan out of the Secretariat. Li Song was loosely connected to him, and Shouzhen treated him with contempt. That summer, when the Khitan raided the frontier, Shouzhen was made northern expedition commander. The Young Emperor gave him a private feast in the inner hall before his departure; an entertainer offered the line, "The Son of Heaven need not fear the northern foe—Shouzhen's face will hold Youzhou. When it ended he looked pleased with himself and repeated the line abroad as boast. He then marched north of Dingzhou, met a Khitan detachment, beheaded the tribal officer Jiali, and returned. In the ninth month he was also made Grand Mentor. The Khitan sent the prefect of Ying in feigned surrender to the Young Emperor, asking for a great army to meet him, and the court believed it. In the tenth month Du Chongwei was named northern campaign commander, with Shouzhen as cavalry superintendent and acting prefect of the Youzhou field headquarters. Earlier, when Shouzhen passed through Ye again with his troops, Du Chongwei heaped gifts on him and flattered him; pleased, Shouzhen praised him at court and asked that command of the campaign be entrusted to him. Now Shouzhen and Chongwei united at Ye and pressed on to Yingzhou, which would not answer. The Bei prefect Liang Hanzhang was beaten and killed by the tribal officer Gao Mouhan, and the imperial force withdrew. At Shenzhou they heard the main Khitan host was coming, hurried to Zhenzhou, and at Zhongdu ford met the enemy, camping south of the Hutuo. Soon enemy horsemen crossed to Luancheng and cut the supply road; Wang Qing was killed; Chongwei and Shouzhen surrendered to the Khitan; Shouzhen was made Minister of Works, kept Yanzhou, and followed the Khitan to Bian. In the capital the Khitan were everywhere, and townspeople lived as if in torment. The two commanders came and went in triumph while people in the streets cursed them without a hint of shame. Soon great bandit bands rose in Hebei and east of the capital, the Cangzhou bridge was severed, the Khitan khan was alarmed and sent each commander back to his post; Shouzhen went to Wenyang. When Liu Zhiyuan entered Bian, Shouzhen, fearful, came to court, was made Grand Guardian, and was shifted to Hezhong. Before long the emperor died and Du Chongwei was executed; Shouzhen grew still more uneasy and secretly harbored treason.

Zhao Siwan came from Weifu. At the end of Emperor Zhuangzong's Tongguang reign, when Zhao Zaili held Weicheng, Siwan served in his command and followed him through his campaigns. When Zaili died Zhao Yanshou enrolled his troops and gave them to his son Zan; Siwan was their headman. When the founder secured the Yellow River basin, Zan moved from Hezhong to metropolitan governor of Jingzhao. Zan, having long served the Khitan, feared the state would never tolerate him; with Hou Yi of Fengxiang he plotted to bring in Shu troops and sent his aide Li Shu to request audience at court. Zan set out without waiting for a reply, leaving Siwan and several hundred men in Jingzhao. When the founder sent Wang Jingchong toward Fengxiang the column passed through Jingzhao, where Siwan and his hundreds were stationed. These men had been Zhao Zaili's household guards and were not tattooed; Jingchong and Qi Zangzhen wanted to tattoo them at Jingzhao to prevent desertion. Jingchong hinted at the plan; Siwan loudly volunteered to tattoo himself first to lead the rest; Jingchong was impressed. Zangzhen whispered, "Siwan is violent and hard to control—better kill him. Siwan once before the crowd took a human gall, swallowed it in wine, and told them, "Swallow a thousand of these and your boldness will be matchless. 〈Note: (Taiping Guangji: the rebel Zhao Siwan, from his rising to his fall, ate sixty-six human livers, every one face-flayed and sliced.)〉

二年夏，食既盡，思綰計無從出，時左驍衛上將軍致仕李肅寓居城中，因與判官程讓能同言於思綰曰：「太尉比與國家無嫌，但負罪懼誅，遂為急計。 今朝廷三處用兵，一城未下，太尉若翻然效順，率先歸命，以功補過，庶幾無患。 若坐守窮城，端然待斃，則何貴於智也。」 〈 （《洛陽搢紳舊聞記》：思綰主藍田副鎮，有罪已發。 李公肅時為環衛將兼雍耀三白渠使、雍耀莊宅使、節度副使，權軍府事，護而脫之，來謝於李公。 公歸宅，張夫人詰之曰：「趙思綰庸賤人，公何以免其禍？ 又何必見之乎？ 」曰：「某比不言，夫人問，須言之。 思綰者雖賤類，審觀其狀貌，真亂臣賊子，恨位下未有朕跡，不然，除去之，可也。 」夫人曰：「既不能除去，何妨以小惠啖之，無使銜怨。 」自後夫人密遣人令思綰之妻來參，夫人厚以衣物賜之，前後與錢物甚多。 及漢朝，公以上將軍告老歸雍。 未久，思綰過雍，遂閉門據雍城叛，衣冠之族，遭塗炭者眾，公全家免禍。 終以計勸思綰納款，遂拔雍城。）〉 思綰然之，即令讓能為章表，遣牙將劉成琦入朝。 制授思綰華州留後、檢校太保，以常彥卿為虢州刺史，遣內臣賫官告國信賜之。 既受命，遲留未發。 郭從義、王峻等籌之曰：「狼子野心，終不可用，留之必貽後悔耳！ 」既而從義、王峻等緩轡入城，陳列步騎至牙署，遣人召思綰曰：「太保登途，不暇出祖，對飲一杯，便申仳別。 」思綰至，則執之，遂斬於市，並族其家。 〈 （《東都事略·郭從義傳》云：思綰困甚，從義遣人誘之，佯許以華州節鉞。 思綰信之，遂開門送款，從義入城，思綰謁見，即遣武士執之。）〉 思綰臨刑，市人爭投瓦石以擊之，軍吏不能禁。 是日，並部下叛黨新授虢州刺史常彥卿等五百餘人並誅之。 籍思綰家財，得二十餘萬貫，入於官。 始思綰入城，丁口僅十餘萬，及開城，惟餘萬人而已，其餓殍之數可知矣。
In the second year's summer, when food was gone, the retired Left Tiger Guard general Li Su lived in the city and, with his aide Cheng Rangneng, told Siwan, "You lately had no quarrel with the court but feared punishment and took desperate measures. The court fights on three fronts and has not taken one city—if you turn and submit first, using merit to atone, you may escape harm. If you sit in this broken city and wait to die, what wisdom is that worth?" 〈Note: (Old Anecdotes of Luoyang Gentry: Siwan commanded the Lantian sub-garrison; his guilt had already been exposed. Li Gongsu was then a palace guard general and concurrently commissioner of Yongyao's Sanbai Canal, commissioner of Yongyao estates, and deputy military commissioner; handling military prefecture affairs, he shielded and freed him; Siwan came to thank him. When he returned home Lady Zhang questioned him, "Zhao Siwan is a vulgar wretch—why did you spare him from disaster? And why receive him at all?" He said, "I ordinarily keep silent, but since you ask I must speak. Siwan is base-born, but studying his bearing he is a true traitor who only lacks rank and an imperial record; otherwise he should be removed. His wife said, "Since you cannot remove him, you might as well win him with small kindness so he bears no grudge. Thereafter Lady Zhang secretly had Siwan's wife come to call; she gave her generous gifts of clothing and much money besides. Under the Han he retired as a senior general and returned to Yong. Before long Siwan passed through Yong, shut the gates, seized the city, and rebelled; many gentry families were ruined, but Gongsu's household escaped harm. In the end he persuaded Siwan to submit, and the city was recovered.)〉 Siwan agreed, had Rangneng draft the memorial, and sent his staff officer Liu Chengqi to court. An edict made him acting commissioner of Hua and honorary Grand Tutor, named Chang Yanqing prefect of Guo, and sent a palace envoy with the commission and state credence. Once commissioned, he lingered and would not depart. Guo Congyi and Wang Jun counseled, "A wolf cub keeps its wild heart—he can never be trusted; keeping him will breed regret! Soon Congyi and Wang Jun entered the city at an easy pace, arrayed foot and horse at the yamen, and sent for Siwan, "Grand Tutor, you are on the road and we cannot give you a full send-off—share one cup and then part. When Siwan came they seized him and beheaded him in the market, exterminating his household as well. 〈Note: (Eastern Capital Outline, Biography of Guo Congyi: When Siwan was desperate, Congyi sent envoys to tempt him and falsely promised the Hua commission. Siwan believed him, opened the gates to submit; Congyi entered; when Siwan came to audience he was seized at once.)〉 At execution the townspeople pelted him with tiles and stones; the guards could not restrain them. That same day more than five hundred of his followers, including the newly appointed Guo prefect Chang Yanqing, were all executed. His household property was registered at more than two hundred thousand strings and taken for the state. When he first took the city the population was barely one hundred thousand; when the gates opened only ten thousand remained—the toll of starvation is plain.
