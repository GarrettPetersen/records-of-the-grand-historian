杜重威，其先朔州人，近世徙家於太原。 祖興，振武牙將。 父堆金，事唐武皇為先鋒使。 重威少事明宗，自護聖軍校領防州刺史。 其妻即晉高祖妹也，累封宋國大長公主。 天福初，命重威典禁軍，遙授舒州刺史。 二年，張從賓構亂，據汜水，晉高祖遣重威與候益率眾破之，以功授潞州節度使。 與楊光遠降範延光於鄴城，改許州節度使，兼侍衛親軍馬步軍副指揮使，尋加同平章事。 未幾，移鎮鄆州，遷侍衛親軍馬步軍都指揮使。 〈 （《通鑒》：馮道、李崧屢薦重威之能，以為都指揮使，充隨駕御營使。）〉 及鎮州安重榮稱兵向闕，命重威禦之，敗重榮於宗城。 重榮奔據常山，重威尋拔其城，斬重榮首傳於闕下，授成德軍節度使。 所得重榮家財及常山公帑，悉歸於己，晉高祖知而不問。 至鎮，復重斂於民，稅外加賦，境內苦之 〈 （《通鑒》：重威所至黷貨，民多逃亡，嘗出過市，謂左右曰：「人言我驅盡百姓，何市人之多也！」）〉
Du Zhongwei's forebears were from Shuozhou; in recent generations his family had moved to Taiyuan. His grandfather Xing had been a military adjutant in the Zhenwu command. His father Duijin had served Emperor Wu of Tang as vanguard commissioner. In his youth Zhongwei served Emperor Mingzong, rising from an officer in the Husheng Army to prefect of Fangzhou. His wife was a younger sister of Gaozu of Later Jin and was eventually ennobled as Grand Princess of Song. Early in the Tianfu era he was placed in command of the palace guard and given the nominal post of prefect of Shuzhou. In the second year Zhang Congbin rebelled and held Sishui. Gaozu sent Zhongwei and Hou Yi to crush him, and Zhongwei was made military governor of Luzhou for his service. With Yang Guangyuan he forced Fan Yanguang to surrender at Yecheng. He was transferred to Xuzhou as military governor, made deputy commander of the palace guard, and soon elevated to Grand Councilor. Before long he was reassigned to Yanzhou and promoted to commander-in-chief of the palace guard. [Editorial note: (The Zizhi Tongjian records that Feng Dao and Li Song repeatedly praised Zhongwei's talents, secured his appointment as commander-in-chief, and made him commissioner of the imperial escort camp.) When An Chongrong of Zhenzhou marched on the capital, Zhongwei was sent against him and defeated him at Zongcheng. Chongrong fled to Changshan. Zhongwei soon took the city, sent Chongrong's head to the capital, and was made military governor of Chengde. He kept for himself all of Chongrong's family wealth and the public treasury of Changshan. Gaozu knew but said nothing. Once in office he taxed the people heavily again, piling surcharges on top of regular levies until the whole circuit groaned under him. [Editorial note: (The Zizhi Tongjian says that wherever Zhongwei served he plundered the people until many fled. Once, passing through the market, he told his attendants, "They say I have driven every commoner away—then why is the market so crowded?")
2
少帝嗣位，與契丹絕好，契丹主連年伐晉，重威但閉壁自守。 部內城邑相繼破陷，一境生靈受屠戮，重威任居方面，未嘗以一土一騎救之。 每敵騎數十驅漢人千萬過城下，如入無人之境，重威但登陴註目，略無邀取之意。 開運元年秋，加北面行營招討使。 二年，領大軍下新州、滿城、遂城。 契丹主自古北口回軍，追躡王師，重威等狼狽而旋，至陽城，為契丹所困。 會大風狂猛，軍情憤激，府彥卿、張彥澤等引軍四出，敵眾大潰，諸將欲追之，重威曰：「逢賊得命，更望復子乎！ 」遂收軍馳歸常山。 先是，重威於州內括借錢帛，吏民大被其苦，人情鹹怨，重以境內雕弊，十室九空，重威遂無留意，連上表乞歸朝，不俟報即時上路。 朝廷以邊上重鎮，主帥擅離，茍有奔沖，慮失禦備，然亦無如之何，即以馬全節代之，重威尋授鄴都留守。 會鎮州軍食不繼，遣殿中監王欽祚就本州和市，重威私第有粟十餘萬斛，遂錄之以聞。 朝廷給絹數萬匹，償其粟直。 重威大忿曰：「我非反逆，安得籍沒耶！ 」三年冬，晉少帝詔重威與李守貞等率師經略瀛、鄚。 師至瀛州城下，晉騎將梁漢璋與契丹接戰，漢璋死焉。 重威即時回軍，次武強，聞契丹主南下，乃西趨鎮州，至中渡橋，與契丹夾滹水而營。 十二月八日，宋彥筠、王清等率數千人渡滹沲，陣於北岸，為敵所破。 時契丹遊軍已至欒城，道路隔絕，人情危蹙，重威密遣人詣敵帳，潛布腹心。 契丹主大悅，許以中原帝之，重威庸暗，深以為信。 一日，伏甲於內，召諸將會，告以降敵之意，諸將愕然。 以上將既變，乃俯首聽命，遂連署降表，令中門使高勛賫送敵帳，軍士解甲，舉聲慟哭。 是日，有大霧起於降軍之上。 契丹主使重威衣赭袍以示諸軍，尋偽加守太傅，鄴都留守如故。 契丹主南行，命重威部轄晉軍以從，既至東京，駐晉軍於陳橋，士伍饑凍，不勝其苦。 重威每出入衢路，為市民所詬，俯首而已。 契丹下令括率京城錢帛，將相公私，雷同率配，重威與李守貞各萬緡，乃告契丹主曰：「臣等以十萬漢軍降於皇帝，不免配借，臣所不甘。 」契丹主笑而免之。 尋群盜斷澶州浮梁，契丹乃遣重威歸藩。 明年三月，契丹主北去，至相州城下，重威與妻石氏詣牙帳貢獻而回。
When Emperor Shaodi came to the throne, ties with the Khitan were severed. The Khitan khan invaded Jin year after year, and Zhongwei only shut his gates and held his walls. Town after town in his circuit fell, and the people were slaughtered, yet Zhongwei, though he held a frontier command, never sent a single soldier to their aid. Whenever a few dozen enemy horsemen drove thousands of Han captives past his walls as if the land were empty, Zhongwei only climbed the ramparts to watch and never made the least move to stop them. In the autumn of the first Kaiyun year he was made northern campaign commissioner. In the second year he led a great army against Xinzhou, Mancheng, and Suicheng. The Khitan khan turned back through Gubeikou and pursued the imperial army. Zhongwei and his men retreated in disorder and, reaching Yangcheng, were trapped by the Khitan. A violent gale sprang up and the troops' anger boiled over. Fu Yanqing, Zhang Yanze, and others led forces out on every side and the enemy broke in rout. When the generals wanted to pursue, Zhongwei said, "We met the enemy and barely kept our lives—do you still expect to win our sons back? " He then withdrew the army and raced back to Changshan. Earlier he had forced loans of money and cloth on the prefecture until officials and commoners alike were desperate and hatred ran deep. The circuit was ruined—nine houses in ten stood empty—and Zhongwei lost all heart for the post. He petitioned again and again to return to court and set out without waiting for a reply. The court knew this was a vital frontier post and feared that if the commander abandoned it a sudden enemy thrust would leave them defenseless, yet it could do nothing. Ma Quanjie was sent to replace him, and Zhongwei was soon made defender of the Yedu capital. When Zhenzhou ran short of rations, the court sent Palace Supervisor Wang Qinzuo to buy grain there. More than a hundred thousand hu of grain were found in Zhongwei's private storehouses and duly inventoried and reported. The court paid him with tens of thousands of bolts of silk in compensation for the grain. Zhongwei flew into a rage. "I am no rebel," he cried. "How can you confiscate my property? " In the winter of the third year Emperor Shaodi of Jin ordered Zhongwei, Li Shouzhen, and others to lead troops against Ying and Mo. When the army reached Yingzhou, the Jin cavalry general Liang Hanzhang fought the Khitan and was killed. Zhongwei immediately withdrew, halting at Wuqiang. Hearing that the Khitan khan was marching south, he hurried west to Zhenzhou and encamped at Zhongdu Bridge facing the Khitan across the Hutuo River. On the eighth of the twelfth month Song Yanjun, Wang Qing, and others led several thousand men across the Hutuo and formed up on the north bank, only to be routed by the enemy. Khitan raiders had already reached Luancheng, the roads were cut, and panic spread. Zhongwei secretly sent envoys to the enemy camp to sound out surrender. The Khitan khan was delighted and promised to make him emperor of the Central Plains. Dull and credulous, Zhongwei believed him completely. One day he hid armed men inside, summoned the generals, and announced his plan to surrender. They were stunned. With the supreme commander already turned traitor, they had no choice but to obey. They signed a joint surrender memorial and sent Inner Gate Commissioner Gao Xun to deliver it to the enemy camp. The soldiers laid down their arms and wept aloud. That day a heavy fog rose over the surrendering army. The Khitan khan had Zhongwei dress in an imperial crimson robe and parade before the armies. He was given the nominal title of Defender Grand Mentor and kept his post at Yedu. The Khitan khan marched south and made Zhongwei lead the Jin army in his train. At the Eastern Capital the Jin troops were camped at Chenqiao, where they starved and froze beyond endurance. Whenever Zhongwei passed through the streets the townspeople cursed him, and he could only bow his head. The Khitan ordered a levy of money and silk on the capital, assessing officials and commoners alike. Zhongwei and Li Shouzhen were each assessed ten thousand strings of cash. They told the Khitan khan, "We surrendered a hundred thousand Han troops to Your Majesty, yet we are still forced to pay levies. We cannot bear it. " The Khitan khan laughed and exempted them. Soon bandits destroyed the floating bridge at Chunzhou, and the Khitan sent Zhongwei back to his post. In the third month of the following year the Khitan khan marched north. When he reached Xiangzhou, Zhongwei and his wife Lady Shi went to his tent with tribute and then returned.
3
高祖車駕至闕，以重威為宋州節度使，加守太尉。 重威懼，閉城拒命，詔高行周率兵攻討，重威遣其子宏遂等告急於鎮州州麻答，乞師救援，旦以宏遂為質，麻答遣蕃將楊兗赴之。 未幾，鎮州軍逐滿達勒，楊兗至洺州而回。 十月，高祖親征，車駕至鄴城之下，遣給事中陳觀等賫詔入城，許其歸命，重威不納。 數日，高祖親率諸軍攻其壘，不克，王師傷夷者萬餘人。 〈 （《宋史·杜漢徽傳》云：從高行周討杜重威於鄴城，屢為流矢所中，身被重創，猶力戰，觀者壯之。）〉 高祖駐軍數旬，城中糧盡，屑麹餅以給軍士，吏民逾壘而出者甚眾，皆無人色。 至是，重威牙將詣行宮請降，復遣節度判官王敏奉表請罪，賜優詔敦勉，許其如初。 重威即遣其子宏遂、妻石氏出候高祖，重威繼踵出降，素服俟罪，復其衣冠，賜見，即日制授檢校太師、守太傅、兼中書令。 鄴城士庶，殍殕者十之六七。 先是，契丹遣幽州指揮使張璉，以部下軍二千餘人屯鄴，時亦有燕軍一千五百人在京師，會高祖至闕，有上變者，言燕軍謀亂，盡誅於繁臺之下，咸稱其冤。 有逃奔於鄴者，備言其事，故張璉等懼死，與重威膠固守城，略無叛誌。 高祖亦悔其前失，累令宣諭，許以不死。 璉等於城上揚言曰：「繁臺之誅，燕軍何罪？ 既無生理，以死為期。 」璉一軍在圍中，重威推食解衣，盡力姑息。 燕軍驕悍，憑陵吏民，子女金帛，公行豪奪。 及重威請命，璉等邀朝廷信誓，詔許璉等卻歸本土。 及出降，盡誅璉等將數十人，其什長已下放歸幽州，將出漢境，剽略而去。 高祖遣三司使王章、樞密副使郭威，錄重威部下將吏盡誅之，籍其財產與重威私帑，分給將士。
When Gaozu's imperial procession reached the capital, he made Zhongwei military governor of Songzhou and Defender Grand Preceptor. Terrified, Zhongwei shut the gates and defied the court. Gaozu ordered Gao Xingzhou to attack him. Zhongwei sent his son Hongsui to Ma Da at Zhenzhou begging for relief and offering Hongsui as hostage. Ma Da sent the tribal general Yang Yan to his aid. Before long the Zhenzhou garrison drove out Ma Da, and Yang Yan turned back when he reached Mingzhou. In the tenth month Gaozu marched in person. When he reached Yecheng he sent Supervising Secretary Chen Guan and others into the city with an edict promising mercy if Zhongwei submitted. Zhongwei refused. Several days later Gaozu led the assault on the walls himself but failed to take them. More than ten thousand imperial soldiers were killed or wounded. [Editorial note: (The Song History biography of Du Hanhui records that in Gao Xingzhou's campaign against Du Zhongwei at Yecheng he was hit again and again by stray arrows and bore grave wounds, yet still fought on fiercely to the admiration of all who watched.) Gaozu besieged the city for weeks until grain ran out and the defenders ground chaff into cakes to feed the troops. Crowds of officials and commoners climbed over the walls to escape, their faces gray as death. At last one of Zhongwei's adjutants came to the imperial camp to surrender. Wang Min was sent with a memorial begging pardon. Gaozu issued a gracious edict of encouragement and promised to treat him as before. Zhongwei sent out his son Hongsui and his wife Lady Shi to wait on Gaozu, then followed to surrender in plain dress to await judgment. Restored to his robes, he was received in audience and that same day appointed Honorary Grand Preceptor, Defender Grand Tutor, and Concurrent Director of the Secretariat. Six or seven people in ten among the population of Yecheng had starved to death. Earlier the Khitan had sent Youzhou commander Zhang Lian to garrison Yecheng with more than two thousand men. Fifteen hundred Yan troops were also in the capital. When Gaozu reached the throne, someone reported that the Yan troops were plotting rebellion and they were all executed below Fantai—a killing widely called unjust. Fugitives who reached Yecheng told the whole story, so Zhang Lian and his men, fearing death, clung to Zhongwei and held the city with no thought of surrender. Gaozu too regretted his earlier mistake and repeatedly proclaimed that they would not be executed. From the walls Zhang Lian and his men cried out, "What crime had the Yan troops committed at Fantai? We have no hope of life—only death awaits us. " Zhang Lian's entire force was trapped in the siege, and Zhongwei shared his food and clothing and did everything he could to placate them. The Yan troops were arrogant and brutal. They bullied officials and commoners and openly seized their women, children, gold, and silk. When Zhongwei sued for mercy, Zhang Lian and his men demanded a sworn pledge from the court. An edict promised they could return home. After the surrender dozens of Zhang Lian's officers were executed. The squad leaders and rank and file had already been sent back toward Youzhou, but as they left Han territory they looted their way out. Gaozu sent Commissioner of the Three Departments Wang Zhang and Vice Director of the Palace Secretariat Guo Wei to register all of Zhongwei's officers and officials and execute them. Their property and Zhongwei's private treasury were inventoried and distributed among the troops.
4
車駕還宮，高祖不豫，既而大漸。 顧命之際，謂近臣將佐曰：「善防重威。 」帝崩，遂收重威，重威子宏璋、宏璉、宏傑誅之。 詔曰：「杜重威猶貯禍心，未悛逆節，梟音不改，虺性難馴。 昨朕小有不安，罷朝數日，而重威父子潛肆兇言，怨謗大朝，扇惑小輩； 今則顯有陳告，備驗奸期。 既負深恩，須置極法，其杜重威父子並處斬。 所有晉朝公主及外親族，一切如常，仍與供給。 」重威父子已誅，陳屍於通衢，都人聚觀者詬罵蹴擊，軍吏不能禁，屍首狼籍，斯須而盡。
When the emperor returned to the palace, Gaozu fell ill and soon sank into his final decline. On his deathbed he told his closest ministers and generals, "Guard yourselves well against Zhongwei. " When the emperor died, Zhongwei was seized and executed together with his sons Hongzhang, Honglian, and Hongjie. The edict read, "Du Zhongwei still nurses treachery in his heart and has not repented of his rebellion. The owl's cry is unchanged; the viper's nature cannot be tamed. When I was briefly unwell and suspended court for several days, Zhongwei and his sons secretly spread seditious talk, slandered the throne, and incited the rabble; now denunciations have come forward and their treacherous plot is fully confirmed. Having betrayed deep favor, they must suffer the utmost penalty. Du Zhongwei and his sons are all to be executed. All princesses of the Jin dynasty and their affinal kin are to be treated as before and continue to receive support. " After the executions their bodies were displayed in the street. Crowds of the capital cursed them and kicked and struck the corpses until the guards could not hold them back. In moments the bodies were torn to shreds.

宏璉，重威之子也，累官至陳州刺史。 〈 （《隆平集》：黨進，幼為天雄軍節度使杜重威奴，重威愛其淳謹，雖長，猶令與婢妾雜侍。 重威敗，周祖得之，以為鐵騎都虞候。 重威之後寒餓，進常分俸以給，士大夫或愧焉。）〉
Honglian, a son of Zhongwei, rose through the ranks to prefect of Chenzhou. [Editorial note: (The Longping ji records that Dang Jin was in his youth a slave of Du Zhongwei, military governor of Tianxiong. Zhongwei prized his simple honesty and, even after he grew up, still had him wait among the maidservants and concubines. When Zhongwei fell, the founder of Zhou took him into service and made him chief controller of the Iron Cavalry. After Zhongwei's ruin he fell into want. Jin regularly shared his salary to support him, to the shame of some scholar-officials.)
6
李守貞，河陽人也。 少桀黠落魄，事本部為牙將。 晉高祖鎮河陽，用為典客，後移數鎮，皆從之。 及即位，累遷至客省使。 天福中，李金全以安州叛，淮夷入寇，晉高祖命馬全節討之，守貞監護其軍，賊平，守貞為宣徽使。 少帝即位，授滑州節度兼侍衛馬軍都指揮使，未幾，改侍衛都虞候。 開運元年春，敵眾犯澶、魏，少帝幸澶州，契丹遣將麻答以奇兵由鄆州馬家口濟河，立柵於東岸，守貞率師自澶州馳赴之。 契丹大敗，溺死者數千人，獲馬數百匹，偏裨七十餘人。 有頃，敵退。 晉少帝還京，以守貞為兗州節度使，依前侍衛都虞候。 五月，以守貞為青州行營都部署，率兵二萬東討楊光遠，命符彥卿為副。 十一月，光遠子承勛等乞降，守貞入城，害光遠於別第。 光遠有孔目官吏宋顏者，盡以光遠財寶、名姬、善馬告於守貞，得之置於帳下。 近例，官軍克復城隍，必降德音，洗滌瑕穢，時樞密使桑維翰以光遠同惡數十輩潛竄未出，搜索甚急，故制書久不下。 或有告宋顏匿於守貞處者，朝廷取而殺之，守貞由是怨維翰。 時行營將士所給賞賜，守貞盡以黦茶、染木、姜藥之類分給之，軍中大怨，乃以帛包所得物，如人首級，目之為守貞頭，懸於樹以詛之。 守貞班師，加同平章事，以楊光遠東京第賜之。 守貞因取連宅軍營，以廣其第，大興土木，治之歲餘，為京師之甲。 行幸賜宴，恩禮無比。
Li Shouzhen was a native of Heyang. In youth he was clever, unruly, and down on his luck, and served his home command as a military adjutant. When Gaozu of Jin governed Heyang he employed him as master of guests. When Gaozu moved through several postings, Shouzhen followed him each time. When Gaozu took the throne, Shouzhen rose step by step to commissioner of the guest bureau. During Tianfu, Li Jinquan rebelled at Anzhou and Huai tribes invaded. Gaozu sent Ma Quanjie to suppress them with Shouzhen supervising the army. After the rebels were crushed, Shouzhen became commissioner of the palace secretariat. When Emperor Shaodi came to the throne, Shouzhen was made military governor of Huazhou and commander of the palace guard cavalry, then soon made chief controller of the palace guard. In the spring of the first Kaiyun year the enemy invaded Chun and Wei, and Emperor Shaodi went to Chunzhou. The Khitan sent Ma Da with a surprise force across the river at Majiakou in Yanzhou and threw up palisades on the east bank. Shouzhen raced from Chunzhou to meet them. The Khitan were routed. Several thousand drowned, several hundred horses were captured, and more than seventy officers were taken. Before long the enemy withdrew. Emperor Shaodi of Jin returned to the capital and made Shouzhen military governor of Yanzhou while keeping him as chief controller of the palace guard. In the fifth month Shouzhen was made overall commander of the Qingzhou field army and led twenty thousand men east against Yang Guangyuan, with Fu Yanqing as his deputy. In the eleventh month Yang Guangyuan's son Chengxun and others sued for surrender. Shouzhen entered the city and killed Guangyuan in a separate residence. Guangyuan had a clerk named Song Yan who revealed all of his treasure, famous beauties, and fine horses to Shouzhen. Shouzhen took them for his own camp. By recent custom, when the imperial army recovered a city a gracious edict was issued to absolve the people. But Director of the Palace Secretariat Sang Weihan, because dozens of Guangyuan's accomplices were still in hiding and the hunt was intense, delayed the proclamation. When someone reported that Song Yan was hidden with Shouzhen, the court seized and executed him. Shouzhen henceforth resented Weihan. The rewards given to the field army Shouzhen distributed entirely as dark tea, dyewood, ginger, and medicines. The troops were furious. They wrapped the goods in cloth like severed heads, called them "Shouzhen's heads," and hung them on trees to curse him. When Shouzhen returned in triumph he was made Grand Councilor and given Yang Guangyuan's mansion in the Eastern Capital. He seized adjoining army camps to enlarge his estate and spent more than a year on lavish construction until it was the finest mansion in the capital. On imperial progresses he was honored with banquets whose favor and ceremony had no equal.

He was made Defender Grand Mentor and transferred to Hezhong. Before long Gaozu died and Du Zhongwei was executed. Shouzhen grew ever more uneasy and secretly nursed treasonous designs.

When Gaozu pacified the He and Luo regions, Zan moved from Hezhong to become governor of Jingzhao. Zan, having long served the Khitan, always feared the state would never tolerate him. He conspired with Hou Yi of Fengxiang to bring in Shu troops as reinforcements and sent administrative aide Li Shu to court to request an audience. [Editorial note: (Extensive Records records that the rebel minister Zhao Siwan, from the time he incited rebellion until defeat, ate sixty-six human livers in all, invariably slicing them from the face.)〉

二年夏，食既盡，思綰計無從出，時左驍衛上將軍致仕李肅寓居城中，因與判官程讓能同言於思綰曰：「太尉比與國家無嫌，但負罪懼誅，遂為急計。 今朝廷三處用兵，一城未下，太尉若翻然效順，率先歸命，以功補過，庶幾無患。 若坐守窮城，端然待斃，則何貴於智也。」 〈 （《洛陽搢紳舊聞記》：思綰主藍田副鎮，有罪已發。 李公肅時為環衛將兼雍耀三白渠使、雍耀莊宅使、節度副使，權軍府事，護而脫之，來謝於李公。 公歸宅，張夫人詰之曰：「趙思綰庸賤人，公何以免其禍？ 又何必見之乎？ 」曰：「某比不言，夫人問，須言之。 思綰者雖賤類，審觀其狀貌，真亂臣賊子，恨位下未有朕跡，不然，除去之，可也。 」夫人曰：「既不能除去，何妨以小惠啖之，無使銜怨。 」自後夫人密遣人令思綰之妻來參，夫人厚以衣物賜之，前後與錢物甚多。 及漢朝，公以上將軍告老歸雍。 未久，思綰過雍，遂閉門據雍城叛，衣冠之族，遭塗炭者眾，公全家免禍。 終以計勸思綰納款，遂拔雍城。）〉 思綰然之，即令讓能為章表，遣牙將劉成琦入朝。 制授思綰華州留後、檢校太保，以常彥卿為虢州刺史，遣內臣賫官告國信賜之。 既受命，遲留未發。 郭從義、王峻等籌之曰：「狼子野心，終不可用，留之必貽後悔耳！ 」既而從義、王峻等緩轡入城，陳列步騎至牙署，遣人召思綰曰：「太保登途，不暇出祖，對飲一杯，便申仳別。 」思綰至，則執之，遂斬於市，並族其家。 〈 （《東都事略·郭從義傳》云：思綰困甚，從義遣人誘之，佯許以華州節鉞。 思綰信之，遂開門送款，從義入城，思綰謁見，即遣武士執之。）〉 思綰臨刑，市人爭投瓦石以擊之，軍吏不能禁。 是日，並部下叛黨新授虢州刺史常彥卿等五百餘人並誅之。 籍思綰家財，得二十餘萬貫，入於官。 始思綰入城，丁口僅十餘萬，及開城，惟餘萬人而已，其餓殍之數可知矣。
In the summer of the second year food was exhausted and Siwan could think of no way out. Retired Left Xiaowei Palace Guard General Li Su was then living quietly in the city. He spoke together with administrative aide Cheng Rangneng to Siwan, saying, "Grand Marshal, you have had no quarrel with the state until now—you only feared execution for your crime and so took desperate measures. Now the court is fighting on three fronts and has not taken a single city. If Grand Marshal suddenly turns and submits, taking the lead in returning to allegiance and using merit to offset fault, you may yet escape harm. If you sit holding a hopeless city, calmly awaiting death—where then is the value of wisdom?" [Editorial note: (Records of Old Affairs among the Gentry of Luoyang records that Siwan commanded the sub-garrison at Lantian and his crime had already come to light. Li Gongsu was then a guard general and concurrently commissioner of the Three White Canals of Yong and Yao, commissioner of the Yong and Yao estates, and deputy military governor in charge of headquarters affairs. He shielded Siwan and secured his release, and Siwan came to thank him. When he returned home, Lady Zhang questioned him. "Zhao Siwan is a base and worthless man," she said. "Why did you spare him from punishment? Why must you even receive him? " He said, "I would not have spoken of this before, but since you ask, I must tell you. Siwan may be of low birth, but a close look at his bearing shows a true rebel and traitor. I regret only that his rank is low and he has not yet shown open signs of rebellion—otherwise he could have been removed. " His wife said, "If he cannot be removed, why not win him with a small favor so he bears no grudge? " Thereafter Lady Zhang secretly had Siwan's wife come to call on her and gave her generous gifts of clothing and much money and goods over time. When the Han dynasty was established, he retired as a senior general and returned to Yong. Before long Siwan passed through Yong, shut the gates, seized the city, and rebelled. Many gentry families were ruined, but the minister's entire household escaped harm. In the end he persuaded Siwan by stratagem to submit, and thus Yong city was taken.) Siwan agreed. He had Rangneng draft a memorial at once and sent military adjutant Liu Chengqi to court. By imperial order Siwan was made acting governor of Huazhou and Honorary Defender Grand Mentor, Chang Yanqing was made prefect of Guozhou, and an inner court official was sent with the commission and imperial credentials. After accepting the appointment he lingered and did not depart. Guo Congyi, Wang Jun, and others deliberated and said, "A wolf cub will always have the heart of a wolf—he can never be trusted. If we leave him alive we are sure to regret it! " Shortly afterward Congyi and Wang Jun entered the city at an easy pace, drew up infantry and cavalry before headquarters, and sent for Siwan, saying, "Grand Mentor is on the road and has no time for a full farewell—let us share one cup face to face and then part. " When Siwan arrived they seized him, executed him in the market, and exterminated his clan. [Editorial note: (Outline History of the Eastern Capital, biography of Guo Congyi, records that Siwan was in desperate straits. Congyi sent men to entice him and falsely promised him the military governorship of Huazhou. Siwan believed it, opened the gates, and offered submission. Congyi entered the city, and when Siwan came to audience he at once sent warriors to seize him.) As Siwan faced execution, townspeople vied to hurl tiles and stones at him, and the guards could not hold them back. That same day more than five hundred of his subordinates and fellow rebels, including the newly appointed Guozhou prefect Chang Yanqing, were executed with him. Siwan's family property was inventoried: more than two hundred thousand strings of cash were seized for the state treasury. When Siwan first seized the city the registered population was barely a hundred thousand. When the gates were opened only about ten thousand remained—the toll of starvation can be imagined.
