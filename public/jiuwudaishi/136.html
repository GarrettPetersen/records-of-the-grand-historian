1
王建，陳州項城人。 唐末，隸名於忠武軍。 秦宗權據蔡州，懸重賞以募之，建始自行間得補軍候。 廣明中，黃巢陷長安，僖宗幸蜀。 時梁祖為巢將，領眾攻襄、鄧，宗權遣小校鹿晏宏從監軍楊復光率師攻之，建亦預行。 是歲，復光入援京師，明年破賊收京城。 初，復光以忠武軍八千人立為八都，晏宏與建各一都校也。 復光死，晏弘率八都迎扈行在，至山南，乃攻剽金、商諸郡縣，得兵數萬，進逼興元，節度使牛叢棄城而去，晏宏因自為留後，建等為屬郡刺史，不令之任。 俄而晏宏正授節旄，恐部下謀己，多行忍虐，由是部眾離心。 建與別將韓建友善，晏宏益猜二建，偽待之厚，引入臥內。 二建懼，夜登城慰守陴者，因月下共謀所向，謂韓建曰：「僕射甘言厚德，是疑我也，禍難無日矣，早宜擇利而行。 」韓曰：「善。 」因率三千人趨行在，僖宗嘉之，賜與巨萬。 分其兵為五都，仍以舊校主之，即晉暉、李師泰、張造與二建也，因號曰隨駕五都，田令孜皆錄為假子。 及僖宗還宮，建等分典神策軍，皆遙領刺史。
Wang Jian came from Xiangcheng in Chen Prefecture. In the closing years of the Tang, he was mustered into the Zhongwu Army. When Qin Zongquan seized Cai Prefecture and posted lavish bounties to raise troops, Jian stepped forward from the ranks on his own and was made a military scout. During the Guangming reign period, Huang Chao captured Chang'an and Emperor Xizong fled to Shu. At the time the future Liang founder served Huang Chao as a general and marched on Xiang and Deng; Zongquan sent the junior officer Lu Yanhong under the supervising commissioner Yang Fuguang to oppose him, and Jian marched with them. That year Fuguang marched to relieve the capital; the following year he broke the rebels and retook the city. At first Fuguang divided the Zhongwu Army's eight thousand men into eight commands, with Yanhong and Jian each leading one. When Fuguang died, Yanhong led the eight commands to join the court on the move. Reaching Shannan, he plundered Jin and Shang and their districts, swelling his force to tens of thousands, then advanced on Xingyuan until Military Governor Niu Cong fled the city. Yanhong declared himself acting governor, named Jian and others prefects of outlying districts, and kept them from taking their posts. Soon Yanhong received formal command with the ceremonial insignia. Fearing mutiny among his men, he ruled with growing cruelty, and his troops began to turn away from him. Jian was close to the deputy commander Han Jian. Yanhong grew ever more suspicious of the two men named Jian, showered them with false kindness, and drew them into his sleeping quarters. Both men were afraid. That night they climbed the wall to cheer the guards on the battlements and, in the moonlight, plotted their next move. Wang Jian told Han Jian, "The Vice Director's honeyed words and lavish favors mean he suspects us. Trouble will come any day now—we should seize our chance and leave while we can." Han said, "Agreed." They then led three thousand men straight to the traveling court. Emperor Xizong praised them and rewarded them with a fortune. Their force was split into five commands under their former commanders—Jin Hui, Li Shitai, Zhang Zao, and the two Jians—and became known as the Five Commands in Attendance on the Imperial Progress. Tian Lingzi took them all as adoptive sons. When Emperor Xizong returned to the capital, Jian and the others each commanded a portion of the Shence Army while holding prefectural titles in name only.
2
光啟初，從僖宗再幸興元，令孜懼逼，求為西川監軍，楊復恭代為觀軍容使。 建等素為令孜所厚，復恭懼不附己，乃出五將為郡守，以建為壁州刺史。 天子還京，復恭以楊守亮鎮興元，尤畏建侵己，屢召之。 建不安其郡，因招合溪洞豪猾，有眾八千，寇閬州，陷之，復攻利州，刺史王珙棄城而去。 建播剽二郡，所至殺掠，守亮不能制。 東川節度使顧彥朗，初於關輔破賊時與建相聞，每遣人勞問，分貨幣軍食以給之，故建不侵梓、遂。 西川節度使陳敬瑄憂其膠固，謀於監軍田令孜，曰：「王八，吾子也，彼無他腸，作賊山南，實進退無歸故也。 吾馳咫尺之書，可以坐置麾下。 」即飛書招建。 建大喜，遣使謂彥朗曰：「監軍阿父遣信見招，仆欲諸成都省阿父，因依陳太師得一大郡，是所願也。 」即之梓州見彥朗，留家寄東川，選精甲三千之成都。 行次鹿頭，或謂敬瑄曰：「建，今之劇賊，鴟視狼顧，專謀人國邑，儻其即至，公以何等處之？ 彼建雄心，終不居人之下，公如以將校遇之，是養虎自貽其患也。 」敬瑄懼，乃遣人止建，遽修城守。 建怒，遂據漢州，領輕兵至成都。 敬瑄讓之曰：「若何為者，而犯吾疆理？ 」建軍吏報曰：「閬州司徒比寄東川，而軍容太師使者繼召，今復拒絕，何也？ 司徒不惜改轅而東，來北省太師，反為拒絕，慮顧梓州復相嫌間，謂我何心故也。 使我來報，且欲寄食漢州，公勿復疑。 」時光啟三年。 居浹旬，建盡取東川之眾，設梯沖攻成都，三日不克而退，復保漢州。 月餘，大剽蜀土，進逼彭州，百道攻之，敬瑄出兵來援，建解圍，縱兵大掠，十一州皆罹其毒，民不聊生。
Early in the Guangqi reign, when the emperor again went to Xingyuan, Lingzi, fearing he was being cornered, asked to become supervising commissioner for Xichuan, and Yang Fugong took his place as Commissioner for Observing the Army. Jian and the others had long enjoyed Lingzi's favor. Fugong feared they would not side with him and posted the five generals as prefectural administrators, making Jian prefect of Bi. After the emperor returned to the capital, Fugong stationed Yang Shouliang at Xingyuan and, fearing Jian above all, summoned him again and again. Uneasy in his post, Jian rallied tough men from the hill country and stream valleys until he had eight thousand followers. He stormed Lang Prefecture, took it, then attacked Li Prefecture, whose prefect Wang Gong fled the city. Jian spread terror through both prefectures, killing and looting wherever he went, and Shouliang could not stop him. Gu Yanlang, military governor of Dongchuan, had known Jian since the campaigns against the rebels in the Guanzhong region. He sent envoys with gifts and shared money and army rations with him, so Jian left Zi and Sui Prefectures alone. Chen Jingxuan, military governor of Xichuan, worried that Jian had dug in too deep and consulted the supervising commissioner Tian Lingzi. "Wang the Eighth is my adopted son," he said. "He has no hidden agenda. He turned bandit in Shannan only because he had nowhere else to go." A single letter from me will bring him under my banner without my stirring from my seat." He at once sent an urgent summons to Jian. Jian was delighted and sent word to Yanlang: "My adoptive father the supervising commissioner has summoned me. I mean to go to Chengdu to see him and, with Grand Preceptor Chen's backing, win a major prefecture—that is all I want." He went to Zizhou to see Yanlang, left his family in Dongchuan as hostages, and marched three thousand picked armored troops toward Chengdu. At Lutou Pass someone warned Jingxuan, "Jian is the fiercest bandit alive—rapacious, restless, bent on seizing every domain he can reach. If he arrives tomorrow, how do you mean to treat him?" Jian has grand ambitions and will never serve under another man. If you receive him as an ordinary commander, you will be raising a tiger to devour you later." Jingxuan took fright, sent men to turn Jian back, and hurriedly strengthened the city's defenses. Enraged, Jian seized Han Prefecture and led light troops straight to Chengdu. Jingxuan rebuked him: "What do you think you are doing, violating my borders?" An officer from Jian's staff answered, "The Minister of Works of Lang recently left his family in Dongchuan as hostages while the Commissioner for Observing the Army and the Grand Preceptor's envoys summoned him again and again. Why do you turn him away now?" The Minister of Works changed course and marched east to pay his respects to the Grand Preceptor, only to be turned away. He fears Gu of Zizhou will take this as proof of bad faith and wonder what he is really after. He sent me to explain that he only wants to quarter his men on Han Prefecture's supplies—please put your suspicions to rest." This was in the third year of Guangqi. Within ten days Jian mustered every soldier he could draw from Dongchuan, raised scaling ladders and battering rams, and assaulted Chengdu. After three days of failure he withdrew and fell back on Han Prefecture. For more than a month he ravaged Shu, then closed on Peng Prefecture and attacked from every side. When Jingxuan sent relief, Jian broke off the siege and unleashed his men in a wider rampage. Eleven prefectures felt his scourge, and the people could barely survive.
3
建軍勢日盛，復攻成都，敬瑄患之，顧彥朗亦懼侵己。 昭宗即位，彥朗表請雪建，擇大臣為蜀帥，移敬瑄他鎮，乃詔宰臣韋昭度鎮蜀，以代敬瑄。 敬瑄不受代，天子怒，命顧彥朗、楊守亮討之，時昭度以建為牙內都校，董其部兵。 〈 （《鑒戒錄》云：昭度以部兵置行府。）〉 及王師無功，建謂昭度曰：「相公興數萬之眾，討賊未效，餉運交不相屬。 近聞遷洛以來，藩鎮相噬，朝廷姑息不暇，與其勞師以事蠻方，不如從而赦之，且以兵威靖中原，是國之本也。 相公盍歸朝覲，與主上畫之。 」昭度持疑未決。 一日，建陰令軍士於行府門外擒昭度親吏，臠而食之，建徐啟昭度曰：「蓋軍士乏食，以至於是耶！ 」昭度大懼，遂留符節與建，即日東還。 才出劍門，建即嚴兵守門，不納東師。 月餘，建攻西川管內八州，所至響應，遂急攻成都，田令孜登城謂建曰：「老夫與八哥相厚，太師久以知聞，有何嫌恨，如是困我之甚耶！ 」建曰：「軍容父子之恩，心何敢忘，但天子付以兵柄，太師孤絕朝廷故也。 茍太師悉心改圖，何福如之！ 」又曰：「吾欲與八哥軍中相款，如何？ 」曰：「父子之義，何嫌也。 」是夜，令孜攜蜀帥符印入建軍授建。 建泣謝曰：「太師初心太過，致有今日相戾，既此推心，一切如舊。 」翌日，敬瑄啟關迎建，以蜀帥讓之，建乃自稱留後，表陳其事。 明年春，制授檢校太傅、成都尹、西川節度副大使知節度事、管內觀察處置、雲南八國招撫等使，時龍紀元年也。 移敬瑄於雅州安置，仍以其子為刺史，既行，建令人殺之於路，令孜仍舊監軍事。 數月，或告令孜通鳳翔書問，下獄餓死。 〈 （《蜀梼杌》云：「敬瑄廢處雅州，以其子為刺史。 既行，建遣殺於三江，令孜仍監其軍，復以令孜陰附鳳翔，下獄餓死。）〉
Jian's army grew stronger by the day, and he attacked Chengdu again. Jingxuan was alarmed, and Gu Yanlang feared he would be next. After Emperor Zhaozong came to the throne, Yanlang memorialized asking that Jian be pardoned, that a senior minister be sent to command Shu, and that Jingxuan be moved elsewhere. The court then ordered Chief Minister Wei Zhaodu to take command of Shu in Jingxuan's place. Jingxuan refused to step down. The emperor was furious and ordered Gu Yanlang and Yang Shouliang to attack him. Zhaodu meanwhile made Jian commander of the headquarters guard and put him in charge of his own troops. [ (According to the 《Jianjie Lu》, Zhaodu stationed his own troops and set up a field headquarters.)] When the imperial campaign failed, Jian told Zhaodu, "Your Excellency raised an army of tens of thousands, yet the expedition against the rebel has accomplished nothing, and supply trains no longer keep pace. Since the court moved back to Luoyang, the provinces have been tearing one another apart and the throne has barely kept up. Rather than exhaust the army in the southern wilds, you would do better to pardon the rebel and turn your soldiers' strength to pacifying the central plains—that is the root of the realm. Your Excellency should return to court, present yourself to the throne, and lay out the plan there." Zhaodu hesitated and could not make up his mind. One day Jian secretly had his soldiers seize one of Zhaodu's personal clerks outside the field headquarters, butcher him, and eat his flesh. Jian then told Zhaodu calmly, "I suppose the men were simply out of food and driven to this?" Terrified, Zhaodu left his credentials with Jian and headed east the same day. Hardly had he passed through Jianmen Pass when Jian sealed the gate with a strong guard and refused to let the eastern army follow. Within a month Jian overran eight prefectures in the Xichuan circuit, meeting welcome everywhere he marched, then pressed hard on Chengdu. Tian Lingzi climbed the wall and called down to Jian, "Your old adoptive father and Brother the Eighth have always been close, and the Grand Preceptor has known it for years. What grudge could justify pressing us this hard?" Jian answered, "How could I forget the bond between adoptive father and son? But the emperor gave you the army because the Grand Preceptor has cut himself off from the court." If the Grand Preceptor will truly change course, what greater fortune could there be!" He added, "I would like to meet Brother the Eighth in camp for a friendly visit. Would that suit you?" He replied, "Between father and son, what cause for suspicion could there be?" That night Lingzi entered Jian's camp carrying the commander of Shu's tally and seal and surrendered them to him. Jian wept and thanked him. "The Grand Preceptor's first intentions went too far and brought us to this breach," he said. "Now that you have opened your heart to me, let everything be as it was." The next day Jingxuan opened the gates to welcome Jian and yielded command of Shu to him. Jian declared himself acting governor and memorialized the court on what had happened. The following spring an edict made him Acting Grand Preceptor, Prefect of Chengdu, deputy military governor of Xichuan with full authority over the circuit, and commissioner for observation, disposition, and pacification of the eight kingdoms of Yunnan. This was in the first year of Longji. Jingxuan was transferred to Ya Prefecture for detention, with his son left as prefect there. Once they had set out, Jian had them killed on the road. Lingzi continued to supervise military affairs as before. Months later someone accused Lingzi of secret correspondence with Fengxiang. He was thrown into prison and starved to death. [ (According to the 《Shu Taowu》, "Jingxuan was deposed and confined at Ya Prefecture, with his son left as prefect. After they set out, Jian had them killed at Sanjiang. Lingzi continued to supervise the army until he was again accused of secretly siding with Fengxiang, imprisoned, and starved to death.")]
4
建雄猜多機略，意常難測，既有蜀土，復欲窺伺東川，又以彥朗婚姻之舊，未果行。 會彥朗卒，弟彥暉代為梓帥，交情稍怠。 李茂貞乘其有間，密構彥暉，因與茂貞連盟，關征疆吏之間，與蜀人得失。 大順末，建出師攻梓州，彥暉求援於鳳翔，李茂貞出師援之，建即圍解，自是秦、川交惡者累年。 後建大起蜀軍，敗岐、梓之兵於利州，彥暉懼，乞和，請與岐人絕，許之。 景福中，山南之師寇東川，彥暉求援於建，建出兵赴之，大敗興元之眾。 洎軍旋，建承虛奄襲梓州，據彥暉，置於成都，遂兼有兩川，自此軍鋒益熾。 天復初，李茂貞、韓全誨劫遷車駕在鳳翔，梁祖攻圍歷年。 建外修好於汴，指茂貞罪狀，又陰與茂貞間使往來，且言堅壁勿和，許以出師赴援，因分命諸軍攻取興元。 比及梁祖解圍，茂貞山南諸州皆為建所有，自置守將。 及茂貞垂翅，天子遷雒陽，建復攻茂貞之秦、隴等州，茂貞削弱不能守。 或勸建因取鳳翔，建曰：「此言失策，吾所得已多，不俟復增岐下。 茂貞雖常才，然名望宿素，與朱公力爭不足，守境有餘。 韓生所謂入為扞蔽，出為席藉是也。 適宜援而固之，為吾盾鹵耳。 」及梁祖將謀強禪，建與諸藩同謀興復，乃令其將康晏率兵三萬會於鳳翔，數與汴將王重師戰，不利而還。 趙匡凝之失荊、襄也，弟匡明以其幹奔蜀，建因得夔、峽、忠、萬等州。 及梁祖開國，蜀人請建行劉備故事。 建自帝於成都，改元永平。 五年，改元通正。 是年冬，改元天漢，又改元光天。 在位十二年，年七十二。 子衍嗣。
Jian was fierce, suspicious, and endlessly scheming, his intentions hard to read. Once he held Shu he again cast his eye on Dongchuan, but the marriage tie with Yanlang still held him back for a time. When Yanlang died, his younger brother Yanhui took command at Zi, and the old friendship began to fray. Li Maozhen seized the breach between them and secretly won Yanhui over, drawing him into an alliance. At the frontier passes the border officials and the men of Shu traded blows over every advantage gained or lost. Late in the Dashun era Jian marched on Zizhou. Yanhui called on Fengxiang for help, and Li Maozhen marched to his relief, forcing Jian to raise the siege. From then on Qin and Shu were enemies for years. Later Jian mobilized a great Shu army and routed the Qi and Zi forces at Li Prefecture. Terrified, Yanhui sued for peace and asked to cut ties with the Qi domain. Jian agreed. During the Jingfu reign the Shannan army invaded Dongchuan. Yanhui appealed to Jian, who marched to his aid and crushed the Xingyuan troops. On the army's return Jian struck Zizhou while it was exposed, seized Yanhui, and kept him in Chengdu. He now held both Chuan circuits, and his armies grew fiercer than ever. Early in the Tianfu era Li Maozhen and Han Quanhui seized the emperor and held him at Fengxiang while the Liang founder besieged them year after year. Outwardly Jian courted Bian and denounced Maozhen's crimes. In secret he exchanged envoys with Maozhen, urging him to hold his walls and refuse peace while promising relief, and at the same time ordered his armies to seize Xingyuan. By the time the Liang founder lifted the siege, Jian held all of Maozhen's Shannan prefectures and appointed his own garrison commanders. When Maozhen's power collapsed and the emperor moved to Luoyang, Jian attacked Maozhen's Qin, Long, and other prefectures again. Maozhen was too weak to hold them. Some urged Jian to seize Fengxiang while he could. Jian said, "That would be a mistake. I already hold more than enough; there is no need to add the Qi capital as well. Maozhen is only a man of ordinary ability, but his name and standing are old and weighty. He cannot match Lord Zhu in a contest of force, yet he can hold his borders well enough. This is what Han Fei meant when he said a state needs shields within and mats without." The right move is to support him and keep him strong as my shield on the frontier." When the Liang founder prepared to force the throne from the Tang, Jian joined the other governors in a plan to restore the dynasty and sent his general Kang Yan with thirty thousand men to Fengxiang. Kang Yan clashed repeatedly with the Bian general Wang Chongshi, failed, and withdrew. When Zhao Kuangning lost Jing and Xiang, his brother Kuangming fled to Shu with his staff. Jian thereby gained Kui, Xia, Zhong, Wan, and the other prefectures. When the Liang founder founded his dynasty, the people of Shu urged Jian to follow Liu Bei's example and declare himself emperor. Jian declared himself emperor at Chengdu and proclaimed the Yongping era. In his fifth year he changed the era name to Tongzheng. That winter he changed the era to Tianhan, then again to Guangtian. He reigned twelve years and died at the age of seventy-two. His son Yan succeeded him.
5
衍，建之幼子也。 建卒，衍襲偽位，改元乾德。 六年十二月，改明年為鹹康。 秋九月，衍奉其母、徐妃同遊於青城山，駐於上清宮。 時宮人皆衣道服，頂金蓮花冠，衣畫雲霞，望之若神仙，及侍宴，酒酣，皆免冠而退，則其髻髽然。 又構怡神亭，以佞臣韓昭等為狎客，雜以婦人，以恣荒宴，或自旦至暮，繼之以燭。 偽嘉王宗壽侍宴，因以社稷國政為言，言發涕流，至於再三。 同宴佞臣潘在迎等並奏衍云：「嘉王好酒悲。 」因翻恣諧謔，取笑而罷。 自是忠正之臣結舌矣。
Yan was Jian's youngest son. When Jian died, Yan took the throne and proclaimed the Qiande era. In the twelfth month of his sixth year he renamed the coming year Xian'kang. In the ninth month Yan took his mother and Consort Xu on an excursion to Mount Qingcheng and stayed at the Shangqing Palace. The palace women all wore Daoist robes and golden lotus crowns, their garments painted with clouds and mist so that from a distance they looked like immortals. When they served at the banquet and the wine had gone deep, they removed their caps and withdrew, revealing their topknots plainly. He also built the Yishen Pavilion, where flatterers such as Han Zhao served as his boon companions amid a crowd of women, and he gave himself over to reckless revelry that sometimes ran from dawn to dusk by candlelight. Prince Zongshou of Jia attended the banquet and spoke of the realm and its government, weeping as he did so, again and again. The flatterers at the same table, Pan Zaiying and the rest, all told Yan, "Prince Jia is a drunk who weeps over his cups." They then turned the evening to coarse jokes and ridicule until the matter was laughed away. From that day forward loyal ministers held their tongues.

With the central realm torn by turmoil, Yan was left undisturbed. When Tang Emperor Zhuangzong conquered Liang, he sent envoys to announce victory in Shu. The Shu court was alarmed and sent a formal reply addressed as "The Lord of Great Shu writes to the Emperor of Great Tang," with wording just defiant enough to offend Zhuangzong. He sent the guest-bureau envoy Li Yan on a return mission and sought to buy palace treasures, but the Shu authorities refused to let any of them leave. Yan was childish and dull, and he left military and civil affairs entirely to others. One Wang Zongbi served as commander of the Six Armies and held overall authority in the field; Song Guangsi served as commissioner of confidential affairs and controlled affairs within the court. When Yan reached Shu, Guangsi and others entertained him privately and spoke of recent events in the central realm. Yan answered with recent events of his own to refute them; the full exchange appears in his biography. Guangsi and his colleagues heard Yan's sharp replies, feared him, and marveled at his ability. On his return Yan reported to Zhuangzong, "Wang Yan is a witless boy. Zongbi and the rest hold the army but care only to enrich their households, not the people's welfare. Court and camp alike pursue nothing but extravagance. Old ministers of merit are cast aside, while the tribal peoples of the south bear grievances like open sores. In my judgment, when a great army arrives they will crumble at the first sight of it. Zhuangzong agreed wholeheartedly, gathered troops and horses, and set his mind on conquering Shu. Before the Tang campaign began, the Dongchuan military governor Song Chengbao urged Yan, "Tang armies are strong. If we do not act now, how will we save ourselves later? Build five hundred warships at Jia Prefecture along the river and raise five thousand sailors to descend through the gorges, while I lead eastern forces from Xiang and Deng so that land and water advance together. Along the northeastern frontier, hold the passes with strong garrisons. Send southern forces from Jiangling to advance when the moment favors them, or fall back and hold Xiakou if it does not. Choose thirty thousand of Shu's best fighters, strike Qi and Yong swiftly, seize He and Tong in the east, and summon the Khitan in the north with rich rewards. Advance when the chance is ripe; otherwise hold Sanguan Pass to secure our borders. Even if the plan fails, it will shake the enemy's resolve. Yan refused.

Zhixiang asked, "How many bags can your cart hold at most?" The man answered, "At most two bags, even with all my strength." Zhixiang took this as an ill omen, and in the end the realm fell after only two generations. Zhixiang fell out with Dong Zhang and marched against him. Zhang had always been fierce in battle and, hearing that Zhixiang was coming, assumed he was marching to his own destruction. The generals wavered, and Li Gao, Zhixiang's aide, was deeply alarmed. On the eve of battle Zhixiang wished to appear at ease and personally wrote a letter to Dong Zhang. Soon he found that whenever he lifted the brush he wrote Dong's name as the character for "heavy" instead, and he brooded over it for a long time. Gao beside him was delighted and led the generals to congratulate him before his horse. Zhixiang was baffled and said, "The outcome is not yet known—why congratulate me?" Gao said, "In the character Dong, the grass radical sits above the character for heavy. Your Highness has removed the grass and written only heavy—Dong has lost his head. This is surely an omen of victory." The whole army took heart, and in a single battle Dong Zhang was defeated.)]

史臣曰：昔張孟陽為《劍閣銘》云：「惟蜀之門，作固作鎮，世濁則逆，道清斯順。 」是知自古坤維之地，遇亂代則閉之而不通，逢興運則取之如俯拾。 然唐氏之入蜀也，兵力雖勝，帝道猶昏，故數年間得之復失。 及皇上之平蜀也，煦之以堯日，和之以舜風，故比戶之民，悅而從化。 且夫王衍之遭季世也，則赤族於秦川； 孟昶之遇明代也，則受封於楚甸。 雖俱為亡國之主，何幸與不幸相去之遠也！
The historiographer remarks: Long ago Zhang Mengyang wrote in the Inscription on Jiange Pass, "Shu alone is the empire's gate, its fortress and its barrier: in turbid ages it turns rebellious, in ages of clear governance it submits. This shows that from antiquity the southwest has been closed off in ages of disorder and seized with ease whenever fortune smiled on the throne. Yet when the Tang entered Shu, though their armies prevailed, the imperial Way was still clouded, and within a few years what they gained they lost again. When the present emperor pacified Shu, he warmed it with Yao's sun and harmonized it with Shun's wind, so that household after household rejoiced and submitted. When Wang Yan met a degenerate age, his entire clan was exterminated in the Qinchuan region; but when Meng Chang met a bright age, he received enfeoffment in the Chu region. Though both were rulers of fallen realms, how vast was the distance between their fortune and their ruin!
