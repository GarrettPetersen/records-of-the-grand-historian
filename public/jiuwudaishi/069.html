1
張憲，字允中，晉陽人，世以軍功為牙校。 憲始童丱，喜儒學，勵志橫經，不舍晝夜。 太原地雄邊服，人多尚武，恥於學業，惟憲與里人藥縱之精力遊學，弱冠盡通諸經，尤精《左傳》。 嘗袖行所業，謁判官李襲吉，一見欣歎。 既辭，謂憲曰：「子勉之，將來必成佳器。 」石州刺史楊守業喜聚書，以家書示之，聞見日博。
Zhang Xian, courtesy name Yunzhong, came from Jinyang; his family had for generations earned yabing staff appointments through military service. From boyhood Xian loved the Confucian classics and set himself to master them, studying without pause by day or night. Taiyuan was a strong frontier district where men favored arms and looked down on book learning; only Xian and a neighbor, Yao Zongzhi, threw themselves into study abroad with all their strength. By twenty he had mastered every classic and knew the 《Zuo Commentary》 especially well. He once carried his latest writings in his sleeve to visit the magistrate's aide Li Xiji, who at first sight cried out in delight. As Xian was leaving, he told him, "Apply yourself—you are bound to become a man of real promise. " The prefect of Shizhou, Yang Shouye, was a collector of books and let him read his family library, so Xian's learning widened steadily.
2
莊宗為行軍司馬，廣延髦俊，素知憲名，令朱守殷齎書幣延之。 歲餘，釋褐交城令，秩滿，莊宗嗣世，補太原府司錄參軍。 時霸府初開，幕客馬鬱、王緘，燕中名士，盡與之遊。 十二年，莊宗平河朔，念藩邸之舊，征赴行台。 十三年，授監察，賜緋，署魏博推官，自是恒簪筆扈從。 十五年，王師戰胡柳，周德威軍不利，憲與同列奔馬北渡； 梁軍急追，殆將不濟。 至晚渡河，人皆陷水而沒，憲與從子朗履冰而行； 將及岸，冰陷，朗泣，以馬箠引之，憲曰：「吾兒去矣，勿使俱陷。 」朗曰：「忍季父如此，俱死無恨。 」朗偃伏引箠，憲躍身而出。 是夜，莊宗令於軍中求憲，或曰：「與王緘俱歿矣！ 」莊宗垂涕求屍，數日，聞其免也，遣使慰勞。 尋改掌書記、水部郎中，賜金紫，曆魏博觀察判官。 從討張文禮，鎮州平，授魏、博、鎮、冀十郡觀察判官，改考功郎中，兼御史中丞，權鎮州留事。 莊宗即位，詔還魏都，授尚書工部侍郎，充租庸使。 八月，改刑部侍郎，判吏部銓，兼太清宮副使。 莊宗遷洛陽，以憲檢校吏部尚書、興唐尹、東京副留守，知留守事。 憲學識優深，尤精吏道，剖析聽斷，人不敢欺。
While Zhuangzong was expedition marshal he sought out eminent men far and wide; knowing Xian's reputation, he sent Zhu Shouyin with letters and gifts to summon him. A year later he took his first office as magistrate of Jiaocheng; when his term ended and Zhuangzong came to the throne, he was made recorder of the Jin prefectural government. As the princely headquarters first opened, staff advisers Ma Yu and Wang Jian—famous scholars of Yan—all became his companions. In the twelfth year, after Zhuangzong pacified Hebei, he recalled his old friends from the princely household and summoned Xian to the field secretariat. In the thirteenth year he was made a censor, given crimson robes, and appointed pushing officer for Weibo; thereafter he always carried the brush and attended the ruler. In the fifteenth year the imperial army fought at Huliu; Zhou Dewei's force was beaten, and Xian and his fellows galloped north to cross the river. The Liang troops pressed close behind, and they were almost unable to get across. They crossed at dusk; most men fell into the water and drowned, but Xian and his nephew Lang walked on the ice. Near the bank the ice broke; Lang wept and pulled him with his riding crop. Xian said, "Let go, my boy—do not let us both go under. " Lang said, "To see my uncle suffer this and not die with you—I could not bear it. " Lang lay flat and kept pulling with the crop while Xian sprang clear. That night Zhuangzong had the army search for Xian; some said, "He and Wang Jian are both dead! " Zhuangzong wept and asked for their bodies; days later, hearing Xian had escaped, he sent envoys to comfort and reward him. He was soon made chief secretary and director of the Water Bureau, given gold and purple insignia, and served as observing secretary for Weibo. On the campaign against Zhang Wenli, after Zhen Prefecture was pacified he was made observing secretary for the ten prefectures of Wei, Bo, Zhen, and Ji, then director of the Merit Office and concurrent vice censor-in-chief, with acting charge of Zhen Prefecture. When Zhuangzong ascended the throne, an edict recalled him to the Wei capital and made him vice minister of works and commissioner for land tax and corvée. In the eighth month he was transferred to vice minister of punishments, put in charge of personnel selection, and made deputy commissioner of the Grand Pure Palace. When Zhuangzong moved the capital to Luoyang, Xian was made acting minister of personnel, prefect of Xingtang, and deputy eastern-capital commandant in charge of metropolitan affairs. Xian's learning ran deep, and he knew official practice especially well; in hearing cases and weighing evidence no one dared cheat him.
3
三年春，車駕幸鄴，時易定王都來朝，宴於行宮，將擊鞠。 初，莊宗行即位之禮，卜鞠場吉，因築壇於其間，至是詔毀之。 憲奏曰：「即位壇是陛下祭接天神受命之所，自風燥雨濡之外，不可輒毀，亦不可修。 魏繁陽之壇，漢汜水之墠，到今猶有兆象。 存而不毀，古之道也。 」即命治之於宮西。 數日，未成。 會憲以公事獲謫，閣門待罪，上怒，戒有司速治行宮之庭，礙事者畢去，竟毀即位壇。 憲私謂郭崇韜曰：「不祥之甚，忽其本也。」
In the spring of the third year the emperor went to Ye; the king of Yiding had come to court, and at the traveling palace a feast was held before a game of cuju. When Zhuangzong had first performed his accession rites, divination on the cuju field had been favorable, so an altar was built there; now he ordered it torn down. Xian submitted, "The accession altar is where Your Majesty received Heaven's mandate in sacrifice; apart from weathering, it must neither be casually destroyed nor casually restored. The altar at Fanyang under Wei and the mound at Sishui under Han still show their traces today. To keep them and not destroy them—that is the ancient way. " He was told at once to restore it west of the palace. After several days the work was still unfinished. Just then Xian was demoted on an official charge and waited at the gate to answer for it; the emperor was angry and told the offices to clear the traveling palace court at once, removing everything in the way, and the accession altar was destroyed after all. Xian said privately to Guo Chongtao, "This is a grave ill omen—they are forgetting their foundation."
4
秋，崇韜將兵征蜀，以手書告憲曰：「允中避事久矣，餘受命西征，已奏還公黃閣。 」憲報曰：「庖人之代屍祝，所謂非吾事也。 」時樞密承旨段徊當權任事，以憲從龍舊望，不欲憲在朝廷。 會孟知祥鎮蜀川，選北京留守，徊揚言曰：「北門，國家根本，非重德不可輕授； 今之取才，非憲不可。 」趨時者因附徊勢，巧中傷之。 又曰：「憲有相業，然國祚中興，宰相在天子麵前，得失可以改作； 一方之事，製在一人，惟北面事重。 」十一月，授憲銀青光祿大夫、檢校吏部尚書、太原尹、北京留守，知府事。
In autumn Chongtao marched against Shu and wrote Xian by hand: "Yunzhong, you have long stayed out of office; I have been ordered west and have already asked to bring you back to the Yellow Pavilion. " Xian replied, "For the cook to stand in for the sacrificer—that is what people call 'not my business.' " At that time Duan Hui of the Secretariat held power; because of Xian's standing as an old companion of the throne, he did not want Xian at court. When Meng Zhixiang was posted to Shu, they had to choose a northern-capital regent; Hui declared openly, "The northern gate is the root of the state and cannot be given lightly except to a man of great weight; for this choice there is no one but Xian. " Those who trimmed to the moment clung to Hui's influence and found clever ways to wound him. He also said, "Xian has the talent of a chief minister, but in this revival of the dynasty a chancellor stands before the Son of Heaven and his mistakes can be corrected; affairs of a whole region rest in one man's hands, and only the northern command matters. " In the eleventh month Xian was made Silver-Blue Gleam Grand Master, acting minister of personnel, prefect of Taiyuan, and northern-capital regent in charge of the prefecture.
5
四年二月，趙在禮入魏州。 時憲家屬在魏，關東俶擾，在禮善待其家，遣人齎書至太原誘憲。 憲斬其使，書不發函而奏。 既而明宗為兵眾所劫，諸軍離散，地遠不知事實，或謂憲曰：「蜀軍未至，洛陽窘急，總管又失兵權，製在諸軍之手，又聞河朔推戴，事若實然，或可濟否？ 」憲曰：「治亂之機，間不容發，以愚所斷，事未可知。 愚聞藥縱之言，總管德量仁厚，素得士心，餘勿多言，誌此而已。 」四月五日，李存渥自洛陽至，口傳莊宗命，並無書詔，惟云天子授以隻箭，傳之為信。 眾心惑之，時事莫測。 左右獻畫曰：「存渥所乘馬，已戢其飾，復召人謀事，必行陰禍，因欲據城。 寧我負人，宜早為之所，但戮呂、鄭二宦，且係存渥，徐觀其變，事萬全矣。 」憲良久曰：「吾本書生，無軍功而致身及此，一旦自布衣而紆金紫，向來仕宦非出他門，此畫非吾心也。 事苟不濟，以身徇義。」 〈 （《東都事略·張昭傳》：昭勸憲奉表明宗以勸進，憲曰：「吾書生也，天子委以保厘之任，吾豈苟生者乎！ 」昭曰：「此古之大節，公能行之，忠臣也。 」憲既死，論者以昭能成憲之節。）〉 翌日，符彥超誅呂、鄭，軍城大亂，燔剽達曙。 憲初聞有變，出奔沂州。 既而有司糾其委城之罪，四月二十四日，賜死於晉陽之千佛院。 幼子凝隨父走，亦為收者加害。 明宗郊禮大赦，有司請昭雪，從之。 憲沈靜寡欲，喜聚圖書，家書五千卷，視事之餘，手自刊校。 善彈琴，不飲酒，賓僚宴語，但論文嘯詠而已，士友重之。
In the second month of the fourth year Zhao Zaili marched into Wei Prefecture. Xian's family were then in Wei; the east was in uproar; Zaili treated them kindly and sent a messenger with a letter to Taiyuan to win Xian over. Xian executed the envoy, left the letter unopened, and reported to the throne. Soon Mingzong was seized by the troops and the armies broke apart; far from the capital no one knew the truth, and some asked Xian, "The Shu army has not come, Luoyang is desperate, the commander has lost his soldiers, power lies with the armies, and we hear Hebei is raising a ruler—if things are really like that, is there still hope? " Xian said, "The turn between order and chaos allows no delay; by my poor reading, nothing is certain yet. I recall what Yao Zong said: the commander is humane and generous and has long held the soldiers' hearts. Say no more—remember only that. " On the fifth of the fourth month Li Cunwo arrived from Luoyang with Zhuangzong's orders by word of mouth and no written edict—only that the emperor had given a single arrow as proof to pass on. Men were uncertain, and no one could tell how events would turn. His attendants urged a plan: "Cunwo's horse already has its trappings stripped off; he is summoning men again to plot—he means treachery and to seize the city. Better we wrong others than be wronged ourselves—strike first; kill only the two eunuchs Lü and Zheng, hold Cunwo, and watch how things develop—the plan will be safe. " Xian was silent a long time and said, "I was only a scholar; without battle merit I have come this far, from common cloth to gold and purple in a day. My whole career has never passed through another man's door—this plan is not mine. If it fails, I will give my life for what is right." 〈Note (The 《Eastern Capital Epitome · Biography of Zhang Zhao》: Zhao urged Xian to memorialize Mingzong to advance him to the throne. Xian said, "I am a scholar; the Son of Heaven gave me charge of the northern capital—how could I cling to life! " Zhao said, "That is the great integrity of the ancients; if you can do it, you are a true loyalist. " After Xian's death, commentators said Zhao had helped him keep his integrity.)〉 The next day Fu Yanchao killed Lü and Zheng; the garrison city erupted in chaos, with fire and looting until dawn. When Xian first heard of the turmoil he fled toward Yi Prefecture. Soon the authorities charged him with abandoning the city; on the twenty-fourth of the fourth month he was ordered to die at the Thousand Buddha Monastery in Jinyang. His young son Ning fled with him and was killed by those who captured them. At Mingzong's suburban sacrifice and great amnesty the offices asked that Xian be cleared, and the request was granted. Xian was calm and frugal, loved books, and kept five thousand volumes at home; when office work allowed, he collated them with his own hands. He played the zither well and did not drink; at feasts with colleagues he spoke only of literature and poetry, and men of learning respected him.

憲長子守素，仕晉，位至尚書。
Xian's eldest son Shousu served the Jin state and rose to minister.
7
王正言，鄆州人。 父誌，濟陰令。 正言早孤貧，從沙門學，工詩，密州刺史賀德倫令歸俗，署郡職。 德倫鎮青州，表為推官； 移鎮魏州，改觀察判官。 莊宗平定魏博，正言仍舊職任，小心端慎，與物無競。 嘗為同職司空頲所淩，正言降心下之。 頲誅，代為節度判官。 同光初，守戶部尚書、興唐尹。 時孔謙為租庸副使，常畏張憲挺特，不欲其領使，乃白郭崇韜留憲於魏州，請宰相豆盧革判租庸。 未幾，復以盧質代之。 孔謙白云：「錢穀重務，宰相事多，簿籍留滯。 」又云：「盧質判二日，便借官錢，皆不可任。 」意謂崇韜必令己代其任，時物議未允而止，謙沮喪久之。 李紹宏曰：「邦計國本，時號怨府，非張憲不稱職。 」即日征之。 孔謙、段徊白崇韜曰：「邦計雖重，在侍中眼前，但得一人為使即可。 魏博六州戶口，天下之半，王正言操守有餘，智力不足，若朝廷任使，庶幾與人共事； 若專製方隅，未見其可。 張憲才器兼濟，宜以委之。 」崇韜即奏憲留守魏州，征王正言為租庸使。 正言在職，主諾而已，權柄出於孔謙。 正言不耐繁浩，簿領縱橫，觸事遺忘，物論以為不可，即以孔謙代之，正言守禮部尚書。
Wang Zhengyan came from Yan Prefecture. His father Zhi had been magistrate of Jiyin. Zhengyan lost his father young and was poor; he studied with Buddhist monks and wrote poetry well; He Delun, prefect of Mi, had him leave the clergy and gave him a post in the prefecture. When Delun was posted to Qing Prefecture he recommended him as pushing officer; and when Delun moved to Wei Prefecture, Zhengyan became observing secretary. After Zhuangzong pacified Weibo, Zhengyan kept his post; he was careful and unassuming and did not contend with others. He was once bullied by a fellow officer, Sikong Hui, but Zhengyan humbled himself and yielded. When Hui was executed, Zhengyan replaced him as military-governor secretary. At the start of the Tongguang era he was minister of revenue and prefect of Xingtang. Kong Qian was then deputy commissioner for land tax and corvée and feared Zhang Xian's stiff integrity; not wanting Xian to head the office, he urged Guo Chongtao to keep Xian at Wei and asked the chancellor Dou Luge to run land tax and corvée. Before long Lu Zhi replaced Dou in the role. Kong Qian told Chongtao, "Money and grain are vital; the chancellor has too much else to do, and the books pile up. " He also said, "Lu Zhi borrowed official money after only two days on the job—none of them are fit for the post. " He expected Chongtao to put him in charge, but public opinion would not allow it and the plan stopped; Qian was discouraged for a long while. Li Shaohong said, "State finance is the root of the realm and is called the house of complaints—only Zhang Xian is fit for the job. " Xian was summoned the same day. Kong Qian and Duan Hui told Chongtao, "Finance is important, but it is right before the vice director—any one man can run it. Weibo's six prefectures hold half the empire's population; Wang Zhengyan has integrity to spare but not enough wit—at court he might manage as someone's subordinate; but left alone over a region, he would not do. Zhang Xian has both talent and capacity and should be given the post. " Chongtao then had Xian stay as regent at Wei and summoned Wang Zhengyan as commissioner for land tax and corvée. In office Zhengyan only nodded assent; Kong Qian held the real power. Zhengyan could not bear the tangle of paperwork and forgot things at every turn; opinion said he was unfit, Kong Qian replaced him, and Zhengyan was left as minister of rites.
8
三年冬，代張憲為興唐尹，留守鄴都。 時武德使史彥瓊，監守鄴都，廩帑出納，兵馬製置，皆出彥瓊，將佐官吏，頤指氣使，正言不能以道禦之，但趑趄聽命。 至是，貝州戍兵亂，入魏州，彥瓊望風敗走，亂兵剽劫坊市。 正言促召書吏寫奏章，家人曰：「賊已殺人縱火，都城已陷，何奏之有。 」是日，正言引諸僚佐謁趙在禮， 〈 （《通鑒》：正言索馬，不能得，乃帥僚佐步出府門謁在禮。）〉 望塵再拜請罪。 在禮曰：「尚書重德，勿自卑屈，餘受國恩，與尚書共事，但思歸之眾，倉卒見迫耳。 」因拜正言，厚加慰撫。 明宗即位，正言求為平盧軍行軍司馬，因以授之，竟卒於任。
In the winter of the third year he replaced Zhang Xian as prefect of Xingtang and regent at Ye. The Martial Virtue commissioner Shi Yanqiong then controlled Ye—grain, funds, troops, and horses all passed through him, and officers and clerks took orders from his gesture; Zhengyan could not check him by principle and only shrank back and obeyed. Then the Bei garrison mutinied and marched into Wei; Yanqiong fled at the first alarm; the mutineers looted the streets. Zhengyan hurried clerks to draft a memorial; his family said, "The rebels are killing and burning—the capital is lost; what is there to memorialize." " That day Zhengyan led his staff to call on Zhao Zaili, 〈Note (The 《Comprehensive Mirror》: Zhengyan asked for a horse and could not get one, so he led his staff on foot out the gate to visit Zaili.)〉 bowed twice in the dust and begged forgiveness. Zaili said, "Minister, you are a man of weight—do not abase yourself; I owe the state and have served with you; the men only wanted to go home and forced my hand in haste. " He bowed to Zhengyan in return and comforted him warmly. When Mingzong came to the throne, Zhengyan asked to be expedition marshal of Pinglu and received the post; he died in office.
9
胡裝，禮部尚書曾之孫。 汴將楊師厚之鎮魏州，裝與副使李嗣業有舊，因往依之，薦授貴鄉令。 及張彥之亂，嗣業遇害，裝罷秩，客於魏州。 莊宗初至，裝謁見，求假官，司空頲以其居官貪濁，不得調者久之。 十三年，莊宗還太原，裝候於離亭； 謁者不內，乃排闥而入，曰：「臣本朝公卿子孫，從兵至此。 殿下比襲唐祚，勤求英俊，以壯霸圖。 臣雖不才，比於進九九，納豎刁、頭須，亦所庶幾。 而羈旅累年，執事者不垂顧錄，臣不能赴海觸樹，走胡適越，今日歸死於殿下也！ 」莊宗愕然曰：「孤未之知，何至如是！ 」賜酒食慰遣之，謂郭崇韜曰：「便與擬議。 」是歲，署館驛巡官。 未幾，授監察御史裏行，遷節度巡官，賜緋魚袋； 尋曆推官、檢校員外郎。 裝學書無師法，工詩非作者，僻於題壁，所至宮亭寺觀，必書爵裏，人或譏之，不以為愧。 時四鎮幕賓皆金紫，裝獨恥銀艾。 十七年，莊宗自魏州之德勝，與賓僚城樓餞別，既而群僚離席，裝獨留，獻詩三篇，意在章服。 莊宗舉大鍾屬裝曰：「員外能釂此乎？ 」裝飲酒素少，略無難色，為之一舉而釂，莊宗即解紫袍賜之。 同光初，以裝為給事中，從幸洛陽。 時連年大水，百官多窘，裝求為襄州副使。 四年，洛陽變擾，節度使劉訓以私忿族裝，誣奏雲裝欲謀亂，人士冤之。
Hu Zhuang was a grandson of Zeng, former minister of rites. When the Liang general Yang Shihou held Wei Prefecture, Zhuang, who knew vice commissioner Li Siye, went to join him and was recommended as magistrate of Guixiang. When Zhang Yan rebelled, Siye was killed; Zhuang lost his office and stayed on in Wei as a guest. When Zhuangzong first arrived, Zhuang paid his respects and asked for a post; Sikong Hui, citing his corruption in office, long blocked his appointment. In the thirteenth year, as Zhuangzong returned to Taiyuan, Zhuang waited at the farewell pavilion; the usher would not let him in, so he forced the door and said, "I am a descendant of Tang ministers and have followed the army here. Your Highness has lately taken up Tang's mandate and seeks able men to strengthen your cause. Though I lack talent, compared with presenting Jiu Jiu or taking in Shu Diao and Guan Zhong, I am not wholly unworthy. Yet I have wandered for years without notice from those in charge; I cannot flee abroad or go elsewhere—today I come to die before Your Highness! " Zhuangzong was astonished and said, "I did not know it had come to this! " He gave him food and wine and told Guo Chongtao, "Draw up a proposal for him. " That year he was made touring officer of the post stations. Soon he was made acting investigating censor, then circuit touring officer, with a crimson fish tally; and soon served as pushing officer and acting vice director. Zhuang learned calligraphy without real training, wrote verse without being a poet, and had a habit of inscribing walls—at every shrine and pavilion he wrote his rank and home; people mocked him, but he was unashamed. Staff guests of the four circuits all wore gold and purple; Zhuang alone was ashamed of his silver and green insignia. In the seventeenth year Zhuangzong left Wei for Desheng and bid farewell to his staff on the city tower; when the others left, Zhuang alone stayed and offered three poems, angling for higher regalia. Zhuangzong raised a great goblet and said to Zhuang, "Can you drain this, Vice Director? " Zhuang drank little as a rule, but showed no hesitation, downed it in one draft, and Zhuangzong at once gave him his purple robe. At the start of Tongguang he was made palace attendant and followed the court to Luoyang. Floods came year after year and many officials were hard pressed; Zhuang asked to be vice commissioner of Xiang Prefecture. In the fourth year, when Luoyang was in turmoil, military governor Liu Xun, from private spite, wiped out Zhuang's clan and falsely accused him of plotting rebellion; men of standing thought it a grave injustice.

崔貽孫， 〈 （《新唐書·宰相世係表》：貽孫字伯垂。）〉 祖元亮，左散騎常侍。 〈 （《世係表》：元亮，字晦孫，虢州刺史。）〉 父芻言，潞州判官。 貽孫以門族登進士第，以監察升朝，曆清資美職。 及為省郎，使於江南回，以橐裝營別墅於漢上之穀城，退居自奉。 清江之上，綠竹遍野，狹徑濃密，維舟曲岸，人莫造焉，時人甚高之。 及李振貶均州，貽孫曲奉之。 振入朝，貽孫累遷丞郎。 同光初，除吏部侍郎，銓選疏謬，貶官塞地，馳驛至潞州，致書於府帥孔曰：「十五年穀城山裏，自謂逸人； 二千里沙塞途中，今為逐客。 」以其年八十，奏留府下。 明年，量移澤州司馬，遇赦還京。 宰相鄭玨以姻戚之分，復擬吏部侍郎，天官任重，昏耄罔知，後遷禮部尚書，致仕而卒。 〈 （《北夢瑣言》：崔貽孫年過八十，求進不休，囊橐之資，素有貯積，性好幹人，喜得小惠。）〉 有子三人，自貽孫左降之後，各於舊業爭分其利，甘旨醫藥，莫有奉者。 貽孫以書責之云：「生有明君宰相，死有天曹地府，吾雖考終，豈放汝耶！」
Cui Yisun, 〈Note (The 《New Book of Tang · Genealogical Tables of Chancellors》: Yisun's courtesy name was Bochui.)〉 His grandfather Yuanliang had been left attendant cavalier. 〈Note (The 《Genealogical Tables》: Yuanliang, courtesy name Huisun, was prefect of Guo.)〉 His father Chuyan had been secretary of Lu Prefecture. Yisun passed the jinshi through family standing, entered court as a censor, and held a series of prestigious posts. As a ministry officer, returning from a mission to the south, he used his travel funds to build a villa at Gucheng on the Han and retired there. Green bamboo covered the clear river; narrow paths ran thick; he moored on a secluded bend where visitors rarely came, and contemporaries admired him greatly. When Li Zhen was demoted to Jun Prefecture, Yisun fawned on him. When Zhen returned to court, Yisun rose steadily to director and gentleman posts. At the start of Tongguang he was made vice minister of personnel; his appointments were slipshod, he was demoted to the frontier, and rode post-haste to Lu, writing the prefect Kong: "For fifteen years in the Gucheng hills I called myself a recluse; now, two thousand li through the desert, I am an exile. " Because he was eighty, he was allowed to remain at the prefectural seat. The next year he was transferred to military aide of Ze Prefecture; after an amnesty he returned to the capital. Chancellor Zheng Jue, a relation by marriage, had him proposed again as vice minister of personnel; the post was weighty, but he was senile and unaware; later he was made minister of rites, retired, and died. 〈Note (The 《Idle Talk of Northern Dreams》: Cui Yisun was past eighty but never stopped seeking promotion; he had long hoarded his funds; he loved to meddle and delighted in small favors.)〉 He had three sons; after his demotion each fought over the profits of his old businesses, and none brought him food or medicine. Yisun wrote to rebuke them: "In life there are enlightened rulers and chancellors; in death Heaven and the underworld keep accounts—I may die in my bed, but I will not let you off!"

孟鵠，魏州人。 莊宗初定魏博，選幹吏以計兵賦，以鵠為度支孔目官。 明宗時，為邢洺節度使，每曲意承迎，明宗甚德之。 及孔謙專典軍賦，徵督苛急，明宗嘗切齒。 及即位，鵠自租庸勾官擢為客省副使、樞密承旨，遷三司副使，出為相州刺史。 會範延光再遷樞密，乃征鵠為三司使。 初，鵠有計畫之能，及專掌邦賦，操割依違，名譽頓減。 期年發疾，求外任，仍授許州節度使。 謝恩退，帝目送之，顧為侍臣曰：「孟鵠掌三司幾年，得至方鎮？ 」範延光奏曰：「鵠於同光世已為三司勾官，天成初為三司副使，出刺相州，入判三司又二年。 」帝曰：「鵠以幹事，遽至方鎮，爭不勉旃。 」鵠與延光俱魏人，厚相結托，暨延光掌樞務，援引判三司，又致節鉞，明宗知之，故以此言譏之。 到任未周歲，卒。 贈太傅。
Meng Hu came from Wei Prefecture. When Zhuangzong first pacified Weibo, he chose able men to reckon troops and taxes and made Hu a revenue clerk. Under Mingzong he was military governor of Xing and Ming and always courted favor; Mingzong thought well of him. When Kong Qian controlled military finance and collections grew harsh, Mingzong once gnashed his teeth in anger. On his accession Hu rose from land-tax clerk to vice guest commissioner and Secretariat commissioner, then deputy three-offices commissioner, and was sent out as prefect of Xiang. When Fan Yanguang returned to the Secretariat, Hu was summoned as three-offices commissioner. At first Hu had a gift for planning, but once he controlled state revenue he squeezed and wavered, and his reputation collapsed. Within a year he fell ill, asked for an outside post, and was made military governor of Xu. As he withdrew after giving thanks, the emperor watched him go and said to his attendants, "How many years did Meng Hu run the three offices to reach a frontier command? " Fan Yanguang replied, "In Tongguang he was already a three-offices clerk; at the start of Tiancheng he was deputy commissioner, went out to Xiang, and ran the three offices again for two years. " The emperor said, "Hu reached a frontier command through busywork alone—why should he not try harder? " Hu and Yanguang were both from Wei and were close allies; when Yanguang took the Secretariat he pushed Hu into the three offices and a military command—Mingzong knew it, and his remark was meant as sarcasm. He died before completing a year in office. He was posthumously made grand tutor.

孫嶽，冀州人也。 強幹有才用，曆府衛右職。 天成中，為潁耀二州刺史、閬州團練使，所至稱治，遷鳳州節度使。 受代歸京，秦王從榮欲以嶽為元帥府都押衙，事未行，馮贇舉為三司使，時預密謀。 朱、馮患從榮之恣橫，嶽曾極言其禍之端，康義誠聞之不悅。 及從榮敗，義誠召嶽同至河南府檢閱府藏。 時紛擾未定，義誠密遣騎士射之，嶽走至通利坊，為騎士所害，識與不識皆痛之。
Sun Yue came from Ji Prefecture. He was forceful and capable and held senior posts in the guard and metropolitan offices. In Tiancheng he was prefect of Ying and Yao and training commissioner of Lang; wherever he went he was praised for good government, and he was made military governor of Feng. On returning after his term, Prince Congrong of Qin wanted him as chief adjutant of the marshal's staff; before that happened Feng Yun recommended him as three-offices commissioner, when he was involved in secret plotting. Zhu and Feng feared Congrong's arrogance; Yue had once warned at length where disaster would begin, and Kang Yicheng took offense. When Congrong fell, Yicheng summoned Yue to inspect the Henan treasury with him. While disorder still reigned, Yicheng secretly sent riders to shoot him; Yue fled to Tongli Ward and was killed; all who knew him, and many who did not, mourned.

His son Lian served as guard general and military-governor deputy.

Zhang Yanlang came from Kaifeng in Bian Prefecture. He served Liang as a land-tax clerk and became grain commissioner of Yan Prefecture. When Mingzong took Yan he kept Yanlang as grain commissioner; later, when posted to Xuanwu and Chengde, he made him chief clerk of his original followers. 臣濫承雨露，擢處鈞衡，兼叨選部之銜，仍掌計司之重。 況中省文章之地，洪爐陶鑄之門，臣自揣量，何以當處。 是以繼陳章表，疊貢情誠，乞請睿恩，免貽朝論。 豈謂禦批累降，聖旨不移，決以此官，委臣非器，所以強收涕泗，勉遏怔忪，重思事上之門，細料盡忠之路。 竊以位高則危至，寵極則謗生，君臣莫保於初終，分義難防於毀譽。 臣若保茲重任，忘彼至公，徇情而以免是非，偷安而以固富貴，則內欺心腑，外負聖朝，何以報君父之大恩，望子孫之延慶。 臣若但行王道，惟守國章，任人必取當才，決事須依正理，確違形勢，堅塞幸門，則可以振舉宏綱，彌縫大化，助陛下含容之澤，彰國家至理之風，然而讒邪者必起憾詞，憎嫉者寧無謗議，或慮至尊未悉，群謗難明，不更拔本尋源，便俟甘瑕受玷，臣心可忍，臣恥可消。 隻恐山林草澤之人，稱量聖製； 冠履軒裳之士，輕慢朝廷。
I have undeservedly received imperial grace, been raised to the balance of power, and also hold the selection bureau's title while running the accounts office. The central secretariat is a forge of policy and prose; I measure myself and do not see how I can fill the post. I have therefore submitted memorial after memorial, begging your wise favor to spare me court criticism. Yet imperial rescripts came again and again, and your will did not change—you fixed on this office for an unfit man; so I dried my tears, steadied myself, and thought again how to serve you and remain loyal. High office brings danger; peak favor breeds slander—rulers and ministers rarely keep faith to the end, and duty is hard to defend against praise and blame. If I held this heavy post but forgot fairness, bent the rules for comfort, or clung to ease to keep my rank, I would cheat my own heart and fail the court—how could I repay your grace or hope for my children's blessing? If I only followed the kingly way, kept the laws, appointed the able, judged by right reason, and blocked favoritism, I could uphold the great order and help your tolerant rule—but slanderers would resent me, the envious would defame me, and if Your Majesty did not see the root, the calumny might stick; still, I could bear it and clear my name. I fear only that recluses and common folk will judge your sacred policy; and men of rank will hold the court in contempt.

臣又以國計一司，掌其經費，利權二務，職在捃收。 將欲養四海之貧民，無過薄賦； 贍六軍之勁士，又藉豐儲。 利害相隨，取與難酌，若使罄山采木，竭澤求魚，則地官之教化不行，國本之傷殘益甚，取怨黔首，是黷皇風。 況諸道所征賦租，雖多數額，時逢水旱，或遇蟲霜，其間則有減無添，所在又申逃係欠。 乃至軍儲官俸，常汲汲於供須； 夏稅秋租，每懸懸於繼續。 況今內外倉庫，多是罄空； 遠近生民，或聞饑歉。 伏惟朝廷尚添軍額，更益師徒，非時之博糴難為，異日之區分轉大。 竊慮年支有闕，國計可憂。 望陛下節例外之破除，放諸項以儉省，不添冗食，且止新兵，務急去繁，以寬經費，減奢從儉，漸俟豐盈，則屈者知恩，叛者從化，弭兵有日，富俗可期。
I further reflect that the state accounts office controls expenditures, and its twin duties of profit and power lie in collection. To support the empire's poor, nothing beats light taxes; to feed the six armies' strong soldiers again requires full granaries. Benefit and harm go together; giving and taking are hard to balance—if we strip the hills and drain the marshes, local government cannot teach the people, the state's root is harmed, the masses resent us, and we profane your rule. Moreover the circuits' taxes, though quotas are high, often meet flood, drought, insects, or frost; collections fall rather than rise, and everywhere officials report flight and arrears. Even military stores and official salaries constantly strain supply; summer tax and autumn rent hang ever on the next collection. Moreover inner and outer granaries are now mostly empty; and people near and far report hunger. The court still adds army quotas and troops—emergency grain purchases are hard, and future burdens will grow. I fear the year's budget will fall short and the state accounts are in peril. I beg Your Majesty to cut extraordinary spending, simplify expenses, add no redundant mouths, halt new troops, strip away complexity to ease the budget, practice frugality, and wait for abundance—then the oppressed will know grace, rebels will submit, war may end, and prosperity may return.
17
臣又聞治民尚清，為政務易，易則煩苛並去，清則偏黨無施。 若擇其良牧，委在正人，則境內蒸黎，必獲蘇息，官中倉庫，亦絕侵欺。 伏望誡見在之處官，無乖撫俗； 擇將來之蒞事，更審求賢。 儻一一得人，則農無所苦； 人人致理，則國復何憂。 但奉公善政者，不惜重酬； 昧理無功者，勿頒厚俸。 益彰有道，兼絕徇情。 伏望陛下，念臣布露之前言，閔臣驚憂於後患，察臣愚直，杜彼讒邪，臣即但副天心，不防人口，庶幾萬一，仰答聖明。
I have also heard that governing the people favors clarity and governing the state favors simplicity—simplicity removes harsh rules, clarity ends faction. If we choose good governors and entrust upright men, the people within the borders will recover, and official stores will be free of theft. I beg you to warn present local officials not to fail in comforting the people; and in choosing future appointees, seek the worthy with greater care. If each post gets the right man, farmers will not suffer; if every man governs well, what worry remains for the state? Only for those who serve the public with good government should heavy reward not be spared; Those who lack understanding and achieve nothing should not receive lavish salaries. This will display the Way more clearly and cut off favoritism. I beg Your Majesty to remember what I have said, pity my fear of what may follow, see my blunt honesty, and stop the slanderers—I will then follow Heaven's will and not fear men's tongues, and in some small way repay your wisdom.

The Last Emperor replied graciously, summoned him to the informal hall, and said, "Your memorial strikes at the age's sickness; your blunt words have helped correct my faults. State finance is weighty; we can discuss it daily—do not worry overmuch. " Yanlang had no choice but to accept.

延朗有心計，善理繁劇。 晉高祖在太原，朝廷猜忌，不欲令有積聚，係官財貨留使之外，延朗悉遣取之，晉高祖深銜其事。 及晉陽起兵，末帝議親征，然亦采浮論，不能果決； 延朗獨排眾議，請末帝北行，識者韙之。 晉高祖入洛，送台獄以誅之。 其後以選求計使，難得其人，甚追悔焉。
Yanlang was shrewd and skilled at managing complex affairs. While Gaozu of Jin was at Taiyuan the court was suspicious and did not want him to build up stores; beyond what the commissioner left, Yanlang sent men to seize everything—Gaozu deeply resented it. When Jinyang rose in arms the Last Emperor debated leading the campaign himself, but listened to loose talk and could not decide; Yanlang alone overruled the others and urged the Last Emperor to march north—men of judgment approved. When Gaozu of Jin entered Luoyang he sent Yanlang to prison and had him executed. Later, when choosing a finance commissioner, no one equal could be found, and he deeply regretted it.

劉延朗，宋州虞城人也。 末帝鎮河中時，為鄆城馬步都虞候，後納為腹心。 及鎮鳳翔，署為孔目吏。 末帝將圖起義，為捍禦之備，延朗計公私粟帛，以贍其急。 及西師納降，末帝赴洛，皆無所闕焉，末帝甚賞之。 清泰初，除宣徽北院使，俄以劉延皓守鄴，改副樞密使，累官至檢校太傅。 時房皓為樞密使，但高枕閑眠，啟奏除授，一歸延朗，由是得志。 凡藩侯郡牧，自外入者，必先賂延朗，後議進貢，賂厚者先居內地，賂薄者晚出邊藩，故諸將屢有怨訕，末帝不能察之。 及晉高祖入洛，延朗將竄於南山，與從者數輩，過其私第，指而歎曰：「我有錢三十萬貫聚於此，不知為何人所得。 」其愚暗如此。 尋捕而殺之。
Liu Yanlang came from Yucheng in Song Prefecture. When the Last Emperor held Hezhong he was chief inspector of horse and foot at Yancheng and later became a trusted confidant. When posted to Fengxiang he was made chief clerk. When the Last Emperor planned his uprising and prepared defenses, Yanlang tallied public and private grain and cloth to meet the emergency. When the western army surrendered and the Last Emperor went to Luoyang, nothing was wanting, and the emperor greatly rewarded him. At the start of Qingtai he was made commissioner of the northern Palace Secretariat bureau; soon, when Liu Yanhao held Ye, he became vice Secretariat commissioner and rose to acting grand tutor. Fang Hao was then Secretariat commissioner but slept at ease; appointments and dismissals all passed through Yanlang, and he had his way. Every lord or prefect coming from outside had to bribe Yanlang before tribute was discussed—heavy bribes won inner posts, light bribes meant late border assignments—so generals often complained, and the Last Emperor did not see it. When Gaozu of Jin entered Luoyang, Yanlang was about to flee to the southern hills; with a few followers he passed his house, pointed, and sighed, "I have three hundred thousand strings of cash here—I wonder who will get it. " He was as foolish as that. He was soon captured and killed.
