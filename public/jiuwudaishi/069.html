1
張憲，字允中，晉陽人，世以軍功為牙校。 憲始童丱，喜儒學，勵志橫經，不舍晝夜。 太原地雄邊服，人多尚武，恥於學業，惟憲與里人藥縱之精力遊學，弱冠盡通諸經，尤精《左傳》。 嘗袖行所業，謁判官李襲吉，一見欣歎。 既辭，謂憲曰：「子勉之，將來必成佳器。 」石州刺史楊守業喜聚書，以家書示之，聞見日博。
Zhang Xian, courtesy name Yunzhong, was a native of Jinyang. For generations his family had earned the rank of yaxiao through military service. From childhood Xian loved Confucian learning, set his heart on mastering the classics, and studied without rest day or night. Taiyuan was a powerful frontier region where most people prized martial prowess and looked down on scholarship. Only Xian and a townsman named Yao Zong threw themselves into study away from home; by his coming of age Xian had mastered all the classics and was especially versed in the Zuo Commentary. Once he tucked his writings into his sleeve and called on the judicial intendant Li Xiji, who exclaimed in admiration at first sight. As Xian was leaving, Li said to him, "Apply yourself—you are sure to become a man of real accomplishment. " Yang Shouye, prefect of Shizhou, was a collector of books and showed Xian his family library, so that his learning broadened day by day.
2
莊宗為行軍司馬，廣延髦俊，素知憲名，令朱守殷齎書幣延之。 歲餘，釋褐交城令，秩滿，莊宗嗣世，補太原府司錄參軍。 時霸府初開，幕客馬鬱、王緘，燕中名士，盡與之遊。 十二年，莊宗平河朔，念藩邸之舊，征赴行台。 十三年，授監察，賜緋，署魏博推官，自是恒簪筆扈從。 十五年，王師戰胡柳，周德威軍不利，憲與同列奔馬北渡； 梁軍急追，殆將不濟。 至晚渡河，人皆陷水而沒，憲與從子朗履冰而行； 將及岸，冰陷，朗泣，以馬箠引之，憲曰：「吾兒去矣，勿使俱陷。 」朗曰：「忍季父如此，俱死無恨。 」朗偃伏引箠，憲躍身而出。 是夜，莊宗令於軍中求憲，或曰：「與王緘俱歿矣！ 」莊宗垂涕求屍，數日，聞其免也，遣使慰勞。 尋改掌書記、水部郎中，賜金紫，曆魏博觀察判官。 從討張文禮，鎮州平，授魏、博、鎮、冀十郡觀察判官，改考功郎中，兼御史中丞，權鎮州留事。 莊宗即位，詔還魏都，授尚書工部侍郎，充租庸使。 八月，改刑部侍郎，判吏部銓，兼太清宮副使。 莊宗遷洛陽，以憲檢校吏部尚書、興唐尹、東京副留守，知留守事。 憲學識優深，尤精吏道，剖析聽斷，人不敢欺。
When Zhuangzong served as campaigning marshal, he sought out eminent men far and wide. Knowing Xian's reputation, he sent Zhu Shouyin with letters and gifts to invite him. After more than a year he entered office as magistrate of Jiaocheng. When his term ended and Zhuangzong succeeded to power, he was appointed recorder of the Taiyuan prefectural government. The hegemon's headquarters had just been established, and he associated with staff members Ma Yu and Wang Jian, celebrated scholars from the Yan region. In the twelfth year, after Zhuangzong pacified Hebei, he recalled Xian to the field headquarters out of regard for their old ties at the princely residence. In the thirteenth year he was made investigating censor, granted scarlet robes, and appointed pushi of Weibo; from then on he always carried his writing brush in the emperor's retinue. In the fifteenth year the imperial army fought at Huliu. When Zhou Dewei's forces fared badly, Xian and his colleagues galloped north to cross the river; the Liang army pressed hard in pursuit, and they nearly failed to escape. By evening they crossed the river. Many men sank and drowned in the water, but Xian and his nephew Lang walked across on the ice; and as they neared the bank the ice broke. Lang wept and pulled him with a riding crop. Xian said, "My boy, go on—do not let us both go under. " Lang said, "How can I leave my uncle to this? To die together would be no regret. " Lang lay flat and pulled with the crop while Xian sprang free. That night Zhuangzong ordered the army to search for Xian. Someone said, "He has perished together with Wang Jian! " Zhuangzong wept and ordered a search for his body. Several days later, hearing that Xian had survived, he sent an envoy to comfort him. Soon afterward he was made chief secretary and director of the Water Ministry, granted gold and purple insignia, and served successively as administrative aide of the Weibo observation commission. He accompanied the campaign against Zhang Wenli. When Zhenzhou was pacified he was made administrative aide for the ten-prefecture observation commission of Wei, Bo, Zhen, and Ji, then promoted to director of the Ministry of Personnel Evaluation and concurrent vice censor-in-chief, with acting charge of Zhenzhou affairs. When Zhuangzong took the throne, he was recalled to the Wei capital by edict, appointed vice minister of works in the Secretariat, and made commissioner of the grain and corvée tax. In the eighth month he was made vice minister of punishments, placed in charge of personnel selection in the Ministry of Personnel, and concurrently appointed deputy commissioner of the Great Pure Palace. When Zhuangzong moved the capital to Luoyang, Xian was appointed acting minister of personnel, prefect of Xingtang, and deputy eastern capital intendant in charge of the capital's affairs. Xian's learning was deep and his grasp of administration especially keen; in hearing cases and weighing evidence, no one dared deceive him.
3
三年春，車駕幸鄴，時易定王都來朝，宴於行宮，將擊鞠。 初，莊宗行即位之禮，卜鞠場吉，因築壇於其間，至是詔毀之。 憲奏曰：「即位壇是陛下祭接天神受命之所，自風燥雨濡之外，不可輒毀，亦不可修。 魏繁陽之壇，漢汜水之墠，到今猶有兆象。 存而不毀，古之道也。 」即命治之於宮西。 數日，未成。 會憲以公事獲謫，閣門待罪，上怒，戒有司速治行宮之庭，礙事者畢去，竟毀即位壇。 憲私謂郭崇韜曰：「不祥之甚，忽其本也。」
In the spring of the third year the emperor visited Ye. The Prince of Yiding, Wang Du, had come to court, and at the traveling palace a feast was held before a planned game of cuju. Earlier, when Zhuangzong performed his accession rites, divination on the cuju ground had been auspicious, and an altar had been built there. Now an edict ordered it torn down. Xian memorialized: "The accession altar is where Your Majesty received Heaven's mandate through sacrifice. Apart from damage by wind or rain, it must not be destroyed on a whim, nor should it be repaired casually. The altar at Fanyang in Wei and the mound at Sishui under the Han—even now their traces remain. To preserve and not destroy them is the way of antiquity. " He was immediately ordered to restore it west of the palace. Several days passed, and the work was still unfinished. Xian was then demoted on official charges and awaited punishment at the palace gate. The emperor was angry and ordered officials to prepare the traveling palace courtyard at once; everything in the way was cleared away, and the accession altar was destroyed after all. Xian said privately to Guo Chongtao, "This is a grave ill omen—they have forgotten the foundation."
4
秋，崇韜將兵征蜀，以手書告憲曰：「允中避事久矣，餘受命西征，已奏還公黃閣。 」憲報曰：「庖人之代屍祝，所謂非吾事也。 」時樞密承旨段徊當權任事，以憲從龍舊望，不欲憲在朝廷。 會孟知祥鎮蜀川，選北京留守，徊揚言曰：「北門，國家根本，非重德不可輕授； 今之取才，非憲不可。 」趨時者因附徊勢，巧中傷之。 又曰：「憲有相業，然國祚中興，宰相在天子麵前，得失可以改作； 一方之事，製在一人，惟北面事重。 」十一月，授憲銀青光祿大夫、檢校吏部尚書、太原尹、北京留守，知府事。
That autumn, as Chongtao was preparing to lead troops against Shu, he wrote Xian in his own hand: "Yunzhong, you have long kept out of court affairs. I have received orders for the western campaign and have already memorialized to bring you back to the chief ministership. " Xian replied, "For the cook to stand in for the priest at sacrifice—that is what people call 'not my business.' " At that time Duan Huai of the Bureau of Military Affairs held power and managed affairs. Because of Xian's long-standing prestige as a companion of Zhuangzong's rise, he did not want Xian at court. When Meng Zhixiang was posted to Shu and a northern capital intendant was to be chosen, Huai declared publicly, "The northern gate is the foundation of the state. Without great virtue it must not be lightly entrusted; for this appointment no one but Xian will do. " Those who courted favor attached themselves to Huai's power and craftily slandered Xian. He also said, "Xian has the makings of a chief minister, but with the restoration of the dynasty a chief minister stands before the Son of Heaven and his errors can be corrected; affairs of a single region rest in one man's hands, and only the northern quarter is weighty. " In the eleventh month Xian was appointed Grand Master of Splendid Brightness with Silver Seal, acting minister of personnel, prefect of Taiyuan, northern capital intendant, and administrator of the prefecture.
5
四年二月，趙在禮入魏州。 時憲家屬在魏，關東俶擾，在禮善待其家，遣人齎書至太原誘憲。 憲斬其使，書不發函而奏。 既而明宗為兵眾所劫，諸軍離散，地遠不知事實，或謂憲曰：「蜀軍未至，洛陽窘急，總管又失兵權，製在諸軍之手，又聞河朔推戴，事若實然，或可濟否？ 」憲曰：「治亂之機，間不容發，以愚所斷，事未可知。 愚聞藥縱之言，總管德量仁厚，素得士心，餘勿多言，誌此而已。 」四月五日，李存渥自洛陽至，口傳莊宗命，並無書詔，惟云天子授以隻箭，傳之為信。 眾心惑之，時事莫測。 左右獻畫曰：「存渥所乘馬，已戢其飾，復召人謀事，必行陰禍，因欲據城。 寧我負人，宜早為之所，但戮呂、鄭二宦，且係存渥，徐觀其變，事萬全矣。 」憲良久曰：「吾本書生，無軍功而致身及此，一旦自布衣而紆金紫，向來仕宦非出他門，此畫非吾心也。 事苟不濟，以身徇義。」 〈 （《東都事略·張昭傳》：昭勸憲奉表明宗以勸進，憲曰：「吾書生也，天子委以保厘之任，吾豈苟生者乎！ 」昭曰：「此古之大節，公能行之，忠臣也。 」憲既死，論者以昭能成憲之節。）〉 翌日，符彥超誅呂、鄭，軍城大亂，燔剽達曙。 憲初聞有變，出奔沂州。 既而有司糾其委城之罪，四月二十四日，賜死於晉陽之千佛院。 幼子凝隨父走，亦為收者加害。 明宗郊禮大赦，有司請昭雪，從之。 憲沈靜寡欲，喜聚圖書，家書五千卷，視事之餘，手自刊校。 善彈琴，不飲酒，賓僚宴語，但論文嘯詠而已，士友重之。
In the second month of the fourth year Zhao Zaili entered Weizhou. Xian's family were then in Wei. With turmoil erupting east of the pass, Zaili treated them well and sent a man with a letter to Taiyuan to win Xian over. Xian beheaded the envoy, sent the letter to court unopened, and memorialized about the incident. Soon afterward Mingzong was seized by the troops and the armies scattered. Far away in Taiyuan, the facts were unclear. Someone said to Xian, "The Shu army has not arrived; Luoyang is desperate; the commander-in-chief has lost control of the troops, and power rests with the armies. We also hear that Hebei is pressing for enthronement—if that is true, might we succeed? " Xian said, "The turning point between order and chaos allows no delay. By my poor judgment, the outcome is still unknown. I recall what Yao Zong once said: the commander-in-chief is benevolent and magnanimous and has long won the hearts of his men. Say no more—remember only this. " On the fifth day of the fourth month Li Cunwo arrived from Luoyang with Zhuangzong's orders by word of mouth. There was no written edict—only the claim that the Son of Heaven had given a single arrow as token of authority. The men were perplexed and the situation impossible to read. His advisers offered a plan: "The horse Cunwo rides has already had its trappings removed. He has summoned men again to plot—he surely intends treachery and means to seize the city. Better to wrong others than be wronged—act at once. Kill only the two eunuchs Lü and Zheng, detain Cunwo for the moment, watch how events unfold, and all will be secure. " Xian was silent a long while, then said, "I am a scholar by origin. Without military merit I have risen to this rank, and in a day I went from common cloth to gold and purple. My whole career has come through one patron alone—this plan is not in my heart. If it fails, I will give my life for righteousness." [ (Brief Account of the Eastern Capital, Biography of Zhang Zhao: Zhao urged Xian to submit a memorial urging Mingzong to take the throne. Xian said, "I am a scholar. The Son of Heaven entrusted me with guarding the realm—how could I cling to life! " Zhao said, "That is the great integrity of antiquity. If you can do this, you are a loyal minister. " After Xian's death, commentators held that Zhao had helped Xian fulfill his integrity.)〉 The next day Fu Yanchao killed Lü and Zheng. The garrison city fell into chaos, and looting and fires continued until dawn. When Xian first heard of the upheaval he fled to Yizhou. Soon the authorities charged him with abandoning the city. On the twenty-fourth day of the fourth month he was ordered to take his own life at the Thousand Buddha Monastery in Jinyang. His young son Ning fled with him and was also killed by those who captured them. When Mingzong performed the suburban sacrifice and proclaimed a general amnesty, the authorities petitioned to clear Xian's name, and the emperor agreed. Xian was reserved and abstemious, fond of collecting books. His family library held five thousand scrolls, and in the intervals of official business he personally collated them. He played the zither well and did not drink. At gatherings with guests and colleagues he spoke only of literature and poetry, and men of learning held him in high regard.

憲長子守素，仕晉，位至尚書。
Xian's eldest son Shousu served the Jin court and rose to minister.
7
王正言，鄆州人。 父誌，濟陰令。 正言早孤貧，從沙門學，工詩，密州刺史賀德倫令歸俗，署郡職。 德倫鎮青州，表為推官； 移鎮魏州，改觀察判官。 莊宗平定魏博，正言仍舊職任，小心端慎，與物無競。 嘗為同職司空頲所淩，正言降心下之。 頲誅，代為節度判官。 同光初，守戶部尚書、興唐尹。 時孔謙為租庸副使，常畏張憲挺特，不欲其領使，乃白郭崇韜留憲於魏州，請宰相豆盧革判租庸。 未幾，復以盧質代之。 孔謙白云：「錢穀重務，宰相事多，簿籍留滯。 」又云：「盧質判二日，便借官錢，皆不可任。 」意謂崇韜必令己代其任，時物議未允而止，謙沮喪久之。 李紹宏曰：「邦計國本，時號怨府，非張憲不稱職。 」即日征之。 孔謙、段徊白崇韜曰：「邦計雖重，在侍中眼前，但得一人為使即可。 魏博六州戶口，天下之半，王正言操守有餘，智力不足，若朝廷任使，庶幾與人共事； 若專製方隅，未見其可。 張憲才器兼濟，宜以委之。 」崇韜即奏憲留守魏州，征王正言為租庸使。 正言在職，主諾而已，權柄出於孔謙。 正言不耐繁浩，簿領縱橫，觸事遺忘，物論以為不可，即以孔謙代之，正言守禮部尚書。
Wang Zhengyan was a native of Yanzhou. His father Zhi was magistrate of Jiyin. Zhengyan was orphaned young and poor. He studied under Buddhist monks and excelled at poetry. He Delun, prefect of Mizhou, ordered him to return to lay life and gave him a post in the prefecture. When Delun was posted to Qingzhou he recommended Zhengyan as pushi; and when Delun moved his command to Weizhou, Zhengyan became administrative aide of the observation commission. After Zhuangzong pacified Weibo, Zhengyan kept his post. He was careful, upright, and uncontentious. He was once bullied by a colleague of equal rank, Sikong Hui, but Zhengyan humbled himself and yielded. When Hui was executed, Zhengyan replaced him as military commissioner aide. At the beginning of the Tongguang era he served as minister of revenue and prefect of Xingtang. Kong Qian was then deputy commissioner of the grain and corvée tax. He feared Zhang Xian's uncompromising character and did not want him to head the office, so he persuaded Guo Chongtao to keep Xian at Weizhou and asked that chief minister Doulu Ge oversee the grain and corvée tax. Before long Lu Zhi replaced Doulu Ge in the role. Kong Qian told the emperor, "Revenue is a weighty matter, but the chief minister has too many duties and the accounts pile up. " He also said, "Lu Zhi borrowed official funds after only two days in charge—none of them is fit for the post. " He expected Chongtao to appoint him in their place, but public opinion would not allow it and the plan failed. Qian was dejected for a long time. Li Shaohong said, "State revenue is the foundation of the realm—it is called the office of popular complaint. No one but Zhang Xian is fit for it. " That very day Xian was summoned. Kong Qian and Duan Huai told Chongtao, "Revenue is important, but it is under the Vice Director's eye—any competent man will do as commissioner. The six prefectures of Weibo hold half the empire's population. Wang Zhengyan has integrity to spare but lacks ability. In a court appointment he might work under others; but to give him sole control of a region would be unwise. Zhang Xian has both talent and capacity and should be entrusted with the post. " Chongtao at once memorialized to keep Xian as intendant at Weizhou and summoned Wang Zhengyan as commissioner of the grain and corvée tax. In office Zhengyan merely assented to decisions; real power lay with Kong Qian. Zhengyan could not cope with the complexity. Documents piled up and he forgot matters at every turn. Public opinion judged him unfit, and Kong Qian replaced him while Zhengyan remained minister of rites.
8
三年冬，代張憲為興唐尹，留守鄴都。 時武德使史彥瓊，監守鄴都，廩帑出納，兵馬製置，皆出彥瓊，將佐官吏，頤指氣使，正言不能以道禦之，但趑趄聽命。 至是，貝州戍兵亂，入魏州，彥瓊望風敗走，亂兵剽劫坊市。 正言促召書吏寫奏章，家人曰：「賊已殺人縱火，都城已陷，何奏之有。 」是日，正言引諸僚佐謁趙在禮， 〈 （《通鑒》：正言索馬，不能得，乃帥僚佐步出府門謁在禮。）〉 望塵再拜請罪。 在禮曰：「尚書重德，勿自卑屈，餘受國恩，與尚書共事，但思歸之眾，倉卒見迫耳。 」因拜正言，厚加慰撫。 明宗即位，正言求為平盧軍行軍司馬，因以授之，竟卒於任。
In the winter of the third year he replaced Zhang Xian as prefect of Xingtang and remained intendant at Yedu. The martial virtue commissioner Shi Yanqiong then supervised Yedu. Granaries, treasuries, troops, and horses were all under his control, and officers and officials obeyed his every gesture. Zhengyan could not restrain him by proper authority and only shrank back and obeyed. Then the Beizhou garrison mutinied and entered Weizhou. Yanqiong fled at the first alarm while the mutineers looted the wards and markets. Zhengyan urgently summoned clerks to draft a memorial. His family said, "The rebels are already killing and burning—the capital has fallen. What is there to memorialize? " That day Zhengyan led his staff to call on Zhao Zaili, [ (Zizhi Tongjian: Zhengyan sought a horse but could not get one, so he led his staff on foot out the prefectural gate to call on Zaili.)〉 bowing repeatedly in the dust to beg forgiveness. Zaili said, "Minister, your virtue is great—do not demean yourself. I have received the state's grace and serve alongside you. It is only that the men, longing to go home, pressed me in haste. " He then bowed to Zhengyan and comforted him warmly. When Mingzong took the throne, Zhengyan asked to be made campaigning marshal of the Pinglu army and was appointed; he died in office.

Hu Zhuang was the grandson of Zeng, former minister of rites. When the Bian general Yang Shihou was posted to Weizhou, Zhuang, who had old ties with deputy commissioner Li Siye, went to join him and was recommended for appointment as magistrate of Guixiang. When Zhang Yan rebelled, Siye was killed. Zhuang lost his post and lived as a guest in Weizhou. When Zhuangzong first arrived, Zhuang called on him and sought an appointment. Sikong Hui, citing Zhuang's corrupt conduct in office, long refused to approve his transfer. In the thirteenth year, as Zhuangzong returned to Taiyuan, Zhuang waited at the parting pavilion; the usher would not admit him, so he forced his way in and said, "I am a descendant of this dynasty's high ministers and have followed the army here. Your Highness has lately taken up the Tang succession and diligently seeks outstanding men to strengthen your great enterprise. Though I lack talent, I am no worse than those who presented the nine-nine tortoise or brought in favorites like Shu Diao and Tou Xu. Yet for years in exile those in charge have ignored me. I cannot throw myself into the sea or flee abroad—I come today to die before Your Highness! " Zhuangzong said in astonishment, "I knew nothing of this—how could it come to this! " He gave him wine and food, comforted him, and sent him away, telling Guo Chongtao, "Draw up an appointment at once. " That year he was appointed touring officer of the post stations. Before long he was made acting investigating censor, then promoted to touring officer of the military commission and granted scarlet robes and the fish tally; and soon served successively as pushi and acting director of a ministry. Zhuang studied calligraphy without proper training and wrote verse without real literary merit. He had a habit of inscribing walls—at every palace, pavilion, temple, and shrine he visited he wrote his rank and birthplace. People mocked him, but he felt no shame. Staff guests of the four commands all wore gold and purple insignia, but Zhuang alone was ashamed of his silver and azure. In the seventeenth year Zhuangzong left Weizhou for Desheng and on the city tower bade farewell to his staff. When the others had left the table, Zhuang alone remained and presented three poems, aiming to win higher robes and insignia. Zhuangzong raised a great goblet and handed it to Zhuang, saying, "Director, can you drain this? " Zhuang ordinarily drank little, but showed no hesitation and drained it in one draft. Zhuangzong at once removed his purple robe and gave it to him. At the beginning of the Tongguang era he was made attendant censor and accompanied the emperor to Luoyang. Floods had afflicted the realm for years and many officials were impoverished. Zhuang asked to be made deputy commissioner of Xiangzhou. In the fourth year, during the turmoil in Luoyang, military commissioner Liu Xun, from private enmity, exterminated Zhuang's clan and falsely reported that Zhuang had plotted rebellion. Scholars and officials regarded it as a grave injustice.

劉延朗，宋州虞城人也。 末帝鎮河中時，為鄆城馬步都虞候，後納為腹心。 及鎮鳳翔，署為孔目吏。 末帝將圖起義，為捍禦之備，延朗計公私粟帛，以贍其急。 及西師納降，末帝赴洛，皆無所闕焉，末帝甚賞之。 清泰初，除宣徽北院使，俄以劉延皓守鄴，改副樞密使，累官至檢校太傅。 時房皓為樞密使，但高枕閑眠，啟奏除授，一歸延朗，由是得志。 凡藩侯郡牧，自外入者，必先賂延朗，後議進貢，賂厚者先居內地，賂薄者晚出邊藩，故諸將屢有怨訕，末帝不能察之。 及晉高祖入洛，延朗將竄於南山，與從者數輩，過其私第，指而歎曰：「我有錢三十萬貫聚於此，不知為何人所得。 」其愚暗如此。 尋捕而殺之。
Liu Yanlang was a native of Yucheng in Songzhou. When the Last Emperor was posted to Hezhong, Yanlang was chief military inspector of horse and foot at Yancheng and was later taken into his inner circle. When the Last Emperor was posted to Fengxiang, he appointed Yanlang a clerical officer. When the Last Emperor planned his uprising and prepared for defense, Yanlang calculated public and private grain and cloth to meet their urgent needs. When the western army submitted and the Last Emperor went to Luoyang, nothing was wanting, and the Last Emperor greatly commended him. At the beginning of the Qingtai era he was made commissioner of the northern bureau of the Palace Secretariat. Soon afterward, when Liu Yanhao was posted to guard Ye, he was made deputy commissioner of the Bureau of Military Affairs and eventually rose to acting Grand Tutor. At that time Fang Hao was commissioner of the Bureau of Military Affairs but slept at ease and left all memorials for appointments and dismissals to Yanlang, who thereby had his way. Whenever frontier lords and prefects came to court from outside, they had first to bribe Yanlang before their tribute was discussed. Those who bribed heavily were posted first to inner territories; those who bribed lightly were sent late to frontier commands. The generals often complained, but the Last Emperor did not perceive it. When Gaozu of Jin entered Luoyang, Yanlang was about to flee to the southern mountains. With several followers he passed his private residence, pointed at it, and sighed: "I have three hundred thousand strings of cash hoarded here—I do not know who will get it. " His folly was such as this. He was soon captured and killed.
