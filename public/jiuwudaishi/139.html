梁太祖乾化元年，正月丙戌朔，日有蝕之。 時言事諸臣，多引漢高祖末年日蝕於歲首，太祖甚惡之，於是素服避正殿，百官各守本司。 是日，有司奏：「雲初陰晦，事同不蝕。 」百僚奉表稱賀。
In the first year of Qianhua (911), on New Year's Day of the first month, the sun was eclipsed. Memorialists at court repeatedly cited how a solar eclipse at the year's opening had portended trouble in the last years of Han Gaozu's reign. Taizu was profoundly alarmed. He put on mourning dress, withdrew from the main audience hall, and ordered every ministry to maintain vigil at its post. That same day the responsible offices reported: "Thick cloud at first kept the sky dark, so that in effect no eclipse was visible." The entire bureaucracy submitted congratulatory memorials.

長興元年，六月癸巳朔，日蝕。 其日陰冥不見，至夕大雨。 二年，十一月甲申朔，先是，司天奏：「朔日合食二分，伏緣所蝕微少，太陽光影相鑠，伏恐不辨虧闕，請其日不入閣。 百官守司。 」從之。
In the first year of Changxing (930), on the first day of the sixth month, the sun was eclipsed. The sky remained overcast and the eclipse could not be seen; heavy rain fell by evening. In the second year (931), on the first day of the eleventh month, the astronomical bureau had earlier reported: "The eclipse on the new-moon day should cover two-tenths of the sun's disk, but because the obscuration will be slight, sunlight and shadow may merge and the loss may be hard to discern. We request that Your Majesty not hold court in the inner palace that day." All officials should remain at their posts. The request was granted.

晉高祖天福二年，正月乙卯，先是，司天奏：「正月二日，太陽虧蝕，宜避正殿，開諸營門，蓋藏兵器，半月不宜用軍。 」是日太陽虧，十分內食三分，在尾宿十七度。 日出東方，以帶蝕三分，漸生，至卯時復滿。 三年，正月戊申朔，司天先奏，其日日蝕。 至是日不蝕，內外稱賀。 四年，七月庚子朔，時中書門下奏：「謹按舊禮：日有變，天子素服避正殿，太史以所司救日於社，陳五兵、五鼓、五麾，東戟西矛，南弩北楯，中央置鼓，服從其位，百職廢務，素服守司，重列於庭，每等異位，向日而立，明復而止。 今所司法物，鹹不能具，去歲正旦日食，唯謹藏兵仗，皇帝避正殿素食，百官守司。 今且欲依近禮施行。 」從之。 七年，四月甲寅朔，是日百官守司，太陽不蝕，上表稱賀。 八年，四月戊申朔，日有蝕之。
In the second year of Tianfu (937), on the second day of the first month, the astronomical bureau had reported in advance: "On the second day of the first month the sun will be eclipsed. Your Majesty should withdraw from the main hall, open the gates of every camp, put away weapons, and refrain from military operations for half a month." On that day the sun was eclipsed: three-tenths of the disk was obscured, in the seventeenth degree of the Tail lodge. The sun rose in the east with a belt-shaped eclipse covering three-tenths of the disk; it gradually recovered, and by the mao hour was whole again. In the third year (938), on New Year's Day, the astronomical bureau had predicted a solar eclipse for that day. When the day arrived, no eclipse occurred, and court and capital alike offered congratulations. In the fourth year (939), on the first day of the seventh month, the Secretariat and Chancellery submitted: "According to the ancient rites, when the sun shows an anomaly the Son of Heaven puts on white garments and withdraws from the main hall; the Grand Astrologer performs the rite of rescuing the sun at the altar of soil, displaying five weapons, five drums, and five banners—halberds to the east, spears to the west, crossbows to the south, shields to the north, with a drum at the center. Officials take their stations in plain dress; all duties are suspended while each office keeps watch; ranks stand in the courtyard by grade, facing the sun until the light returns, and only then disperse." The ritual implements now in the keeping of the offices cannot all be supplied. At last year's New Year eclipse we could only store away weapons, have the emperor withdraw to the main hall on a vegetarian diet, and keep officials at their posts." We propose to follow the simplified recent practice on this occasion. The proposal was approved. In the seventh year (942), on the first day of the fourth month, officials remained at their posts as ordered; the sun was not eclipsed, and congratulatory memorials were submitted. In the eighth year (943), on the first day of the fourth month, the sun was eclipsed.

梁太祖開平四年，十二月十四日夜，先是，司天奏：「是日月蝕，不宜用兵。 」時王景仁方總大軍北伐，追之不及。 至五年正月二日，果為後唐莊宗大敗於柏鄉。
In the fourth year of Kaiping (910), on the night of the fourteenth day of the twelfth month, the astronomical bureau had reported in advance: "A lunar eclipse is due that night; military operations should be avoided." Wang Jingren was then leading the main army on a northern campaign, and the recall could not overtake him in time. On the second day of the first month of the fifth year (911), he suffered a crushing defeat from Later Tang's Zhuangzong at Boxiang.

In the sixth year of Xiande, on the gengzi day of the sixth month, Mars and the principal star of the Heart lodge shared the same degree, their light beams crossing. For months beforehand Mars had traced hooked paths between the Room and Heart lodges; now it shared a degree with the Heart's principal star, and that night resumed direct motion. ◎星晝見唐同光三年，六月己巳，太白晝見。 天成元年，七月庚申，太白晝見。
Stars Seen by Day. In the third year of Tongguang, on the jisi day of the sixth month, Venus was visible in daylight. In the first year of Tiancheng, on the gengshen day of the seventh month, Venus was visible in daylight.
33
長興二年，五月己亥，歲星晝見。 閏五月己巳，歲星晝見。 八月戊子，太白晝見。 三年，十月壬申，太白晝見。 四年，五月癸卯，太白晝見。 清泰元年，五月己未，太白晝見。
In the second year of Changxing, on the jihai day of the fifth month, Jupiter was visible in daylight. In the intercalary fifth month, on the jisi day, Jupiter was visible in daylight. On the wuzi day of the eighth month, Venus was visible in daylight. In the third year, on the renshen day of the tenth month, Venus was visible in daylight. In the fourth year, on the guimao day of the fifth month, Venus was visible in daylight. In the first year of Qingtai, on the jiwei day of the fifth month, Venus was visible in daylight.
34
漢天福十三年，四月丙子，太白晝見。 乾祐二年，四月壬午，太白晝見，
In the thirteenth year of Tianfu, on the bingzi day of the fourth month, Venus was visible in daylight. In the second year of Qianyou, on the renwu day of the fourth month, Venus was visible in daylight,
35
周廣順二年，二月庚寅，太白經天。 ◎流星
In the second year of Guangshun, on the gengyin day of the second month, Venus crossed the meridian at noon. Shooting Stars
36
梁乾化元年，十一月甲辰，東方有流星如數升器，出畢宿口，曳光三丈餘，有聲如雷。
In the first year of Qianhua, on the jiachen day of the eleventh month, a shooting star the size of a multi-pint vessel appeared in the east, emerging from the mouth of the Net lodge and trailing light for more than thirty feet, with a thunderous roar.
37
唐長興二年，九月丙戌夜，二鼓初，東北方有小流星入北斗魁滅。 至五鼓初，西北方次北有流星，狀如半升器，初小後大，速流如奎滅。 尾跡凝天，屈曲似雲而散，光明燭地。 又東北有流星如大桃，出下臺星，西北速流，至斗柄第三星旁滅。 五鼓後至明，中天及四方有小流星百餘，流註交橫。
In the second year of Changxing, on the night of the bingxu day of the ninth month, at the second watch, a small meteor in the northeast entered the leader of the Northern Dipper and vanished. By the fifth watch, a meteor slightly north of northwest appeared, shaped like a half-pint vessel, growing from small to large as it streaked swiftly past the Striding Legs lodge and vanished. Its trail congealed across the sky, curling like clouds before dispersing, its brilliance lighting the ground. Another meteor the size of a large peach appeared in the northeast, emerging from the Lower Terrace star and streaking northwest until it vanished beside the third star of the Dipper's handle. From after the fifth watch until dawn, more than a hundred small meteors crossed the zenith and the four quarters of the sky, their paths intersecting.
38
應順元年春，二月辛未夜，有大星如五升器，流於東北，有聲如雷。
In the spring of the first year of Yingshun, on the night of the xinwei day of the second month, a great meteor the size of a five-pint vessel streaked toward the northeast with a thunderous roar.
39
清泰元年，九月辛丑夜，五鼓初，有大星如五斗器而南流，尾跡長數丈，亦赤色，移時盤屈如龍形，蹙縮如二鏵，相鬥而散。 又一星稍小，東流，有尾跡，凝成白氣，食頃方散。
In the first year of Qingtai, on the night of the xinchou day of the ninth month, at the fifth watch, a great meteor the size of a five-peck vessel streamed southward, its red trail several yards long. After a time it coiled like a dragon, then contracted as if two plowshares were clashing before dispersing. A somewhat smaller star followed, streaming eastward with a trail that congealed into white vapor and took the space of a meal to dissipate.
40
晉天福三年，三月壬申夜，四鼓後，東方有大流星，狀如三升器，其色白，長尺餘，屈曲流出河鼓星東三尺，流丈餘滅。
In the third year of Tianfu, on the night of the renshen day of the third month, after the fourth watch, a great meteor the size of a three-pint vessel appeared in the east, white and more than a foot long. It twisted as it emerged three feet east of the River Drum star, streaked for more than ten feet, and vanished.

◎雲氣
Cloud Vapors
42
梁開平二年，三月丁丑夜，月有蒼白暈，又有白氣如人形十餘，皆東向，出於暈內。 九月乙酉，平旦，西方有氣如人形甚眾，皆若俯伏之狀，經刻乃散。
In the second year of Kaiping, on the night of the dingchou day of the third month, the moon showed a pale blue halo, and more than ten white vapors shaped like human figures, all facing east, emerged from within the halo. On the yiyou day of the ninth month, at dawn, a multitude of vapors in human shape appeared in the west, all seeming to prostrate themselves; after a quarter-hour they dispersed.
43
唐同光三年，日有背氣，凡十二。 三年，九月丁未夜，遍天陰雲，北方有聲如雷，四面雞雉皆雊，俗謂之「天狗落」。 是歲，日有背氣，凡十三。 是月，司天監奏：「自七月三日陰雲大雨，至九月十八日後方晴，三辰行度災祥，數日不見。 」閏十二月庚午，日有黑氣，似日，交相錯磨，測在室十度。
In the third year of Tongguang, opposing solar vapors appeared twelve times. In the third year, on the night of the dingwei day of the ninth month, cloud covered the entire sky. Thunder sounded in the north, and chickens and pheasants crowed on every side; the people called it "the celestial dog falling." That year opposing solar vapors appeared thirteen times. That month the astronomical directorate reported: "From the third day of the seventh month until after the eighteenth day of the ninth month, cloud and heavy rain hid the sky. For several days the movements of the sun, moon, and stars and their omens could not be observed." On the gengwu day of the intercalary twelfth month, black vapor resembling a second sun crossed and ground against the true sun; observation placed the phenomenon in the tenth degree of the House lodge.
44
天成二年，十二月壬辰，西方有赤氣，如火焰焰，約二千里。 占者云「不出二年，其下當有大兵。」
In the second year of Tiancheng, on the renchen day of the twelfth month, red vapor like blazing flame stretched across the west for roughly two thousand li. Diviners declared: "Within two years great armies will arise in the region below."
45
長興三年，六月，司天監奏：「自月初至月終，每夜陰雲蔽天，不辨星月。 」應順元年，四月九日，白虹貫日，是時閔帝遇害。
In the third year of Changxing, in the sixth month, the astronomical directorate reported: "From the first to the last night of the month, cloud covered the sky each night so that stars and moon could not be distinguished." In the first year of Yingshun, on the ninth day of the fourth month, a white rainbow pierced the sun; at that time Emperor Min was assassinated.
46
晉天福初，高祖將建義於太原，日傍多有五色雲，如蓮芰之狀。 二年，正月丙辰，一鼓初，北方有赤氣，向西至戌亥地，東北至醜地已來向北，闊三丈餘，狀如火光。 赤氣內見紫微宮共北斗諸星，其氣乍明乍暗。 至三點後，後有白氣數條，相次西行，直至三鼓後散。
At the beginning of the Tianfu era, as Gaozu prepared to raise his banner at Taiyuan, many-colored clouds like lotus seedpods gathered beside the sun. In the second year, on the bingchen day of the first month, at the first watch, red vapor in the north stretched west toward the xu and hai quarters and from the northeast through the chou quarter northward, more than thirty feet wide and shaped like firelight. Within the red vapor the Purple Forbidden Palace and the stars of the Northern Dipper could be seen, the vapor flickering bright and dim. After the third hour, several streaks of white vapor followed one another westward until they dispersed after the third watch.
47
漢乾祐二年十二月，日暈三重，上有背氣。
In the twelfth month of the second year of Qianyou, the sun showed a triple halo with opposing vapor above it.
48
周顯德三年，十二月庚午，白虹貫日，氣暈勾環。
In the third year of Xiande, on the gengwu day of the twelfth month, a white rainbow pierced the sun, with hooked and encircling vaporous halos.
