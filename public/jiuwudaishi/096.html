孔崇弼，初仕後唐，自吏部郎中授給事中，時族兄昭序繇給事中改左常侍，兄弟同居門下，時論榮之。 崇弼，天福中遷左散騎常侍。 無他才，但能談笑，戲玩人物，揚眉抵掌，取悅於人。 五年，詔令泛海使於杭越。 先是，浙中贈賄，每歲恒及萬緡，時議者曰：「孔常侍命奇薄，何消盈數，有命即無財，有財即無命。 」明年使還，果海中船壞，空手而歸。 〈 （案：以下殘闕。）〉
Kong Chongbi began his career under Later Tang, rising from director in the Ministry of Personnel to supervising censor. About then his elder clansman Zhaoxu moved from supervising censor to left regular attendant, so the two brothers served together in the Gate and Attendant Office—a pairing that contemporaries regarded as a mark of distinction. During the Tianfu reign period Chongbi was promoted to left irregular cavalier attendant. He possessed no other ability, but he could banter and jest, mock and toy with people, lift his brows and clap his hands together, and win others' favor. In the fifth year of the reign he was ordered by edict to sail as envoy to Hang and Yue. Previously, presents and bribes from Zhejiang had routinely amounted to ten thousand strings of cash a year. People at the time remarked, "Supervisor Kong's fate is peculiarly meager—how could he ever enjoy such a windfall? Where there is life there is no money; where there is money there is no life." " The following year the envoy returned, and as predicted the ship was lost at sea; he came home with nothing. Here an editorial opening bracket appears in the text. 陳保極，閩中人也。 好學，善屬文，後唐天成中擢進士第，秦王從榮聞其名，辟為從事。 從榮素急暴，後怒保極不告出遊宰相門，以馬箠鞭之，尋出為定州推官。 從榮敗，執政知其屈，擢居三署，歷禮部、倉部員外郎。 初，桑維翰登第之歲，保極時在秦王幕下，因戲謂同輩曰：「近知今歲有三個半人及第。 」蓋其年收四人，保極以維翰短陋，故謂之半人也。 天福中，維翰既居相位，保極時在曹郎，慮除官差跌，心不自安，乃乞假南遊，將謀退跡。 既而襄、鄧長吏以行止入奏，維翰乃奏於高祖曰：「保極閩人，多狡，恐逃入淮海。 」即以詔追赴闕，將下臺鍛成其事，同列李崧極言以解之，因令所司就所居鞫之，貶為衛尉寺丞，仍奪金紫。 尋復為倉部員外郎，竟以銜憤而卒。
Chen Baoji was a native of Fujian. He was studious and skilled at composition. In the Tiancheng period of Later Tang he passed the jinshi examination, and when Prince of Qin Congrong heard of him he took him on as a staff officer. Congrong was naturally hot-tempered and violent. Later he flew into a rage because Baoji had gone calling on a chief minister without telling him, and lashed him with a horsewhip; before long Baoji was posted out as investigating officer in Ding prefecture. After Congrong's defeat the authorities recognized that Baoji had been treated unjustly and promoted him into the Three Bureaus, where he served in succession as vice director in the ministries of Rites and of Revenue. Earlier, in the year Sang Weihan passed the jinshi examination, Baoji was serving on the Prince of Qin's staff. He joked to his colleagues, "I've just heard that three and a half men passed this year." " That year four candidates were admitted, but because Weihan was short and plain-looking, Baoji counted him as only half a man. During the Tianfu period, after Weihan had become chief minister, Baoji was serving as a bureau director in the Ministry of Revenue. Fearful that his post might be downgraded, he grew uneasy and asked leave to travel south, planning to retire from public life. Before long the senior officials of Xiang and Deng reported his movements to court. Weihan then memorialized Emperor Gaozu, saying, "Baoji is a Fujianese and notoriously cunning; I fear he may flee to the Huaihai region." " An edict immediately recalled him to the capital. The censorate was about to manufacture a case against him, but his colleague Li Song argued strenuously on his behalf. The authorities were then ordered to try him at his home; he was demoted to assistant director of the Court of Imperial Regalia and stripped of his gold seal and purple robe. He was soon restored as vice director in the Ministry of Revenue, but in the end he died still brooding over the wrong done him.

保極無時才，有傲人之名，而性復鄙吝，所得利祿，未嘗奉身，但蔬食而已。 每與人奕棋，敗則以手亂其局，蓋拒所賭金錢不欲償也。 及卒，室無妻兒，帷囊中貯白金十鋌，為他人所有，時甚嗤之。
Baoji had no real talent for his age and was known for looking down on others; he was also miserly by nature. He never spent his salary and stipends on himself and ate only plain fare. Whenever he played chess and lost, he would sweep the pieces about with his hand—an obvious ploy to avoid paying what he had wagered. When he died he left no wife or children. In a pouch in his curtains were ten ingots of white silver that belonged to someone else, and contemporaries mocked him roundly for it.

王瑜，其先范陽人也。 父欽祚，仕至殿中監，出為義州刺史。 瑜性兇狡，然雋辯驍果，騎射刀筆之長，亦稱於當代。 起家累為從事。 天福中，授左贊善大夫。 會濮郡秋稼豐衍，稅籍不均，命乘使車，按察定計。 既至郡，謂校簿吏胡蘊、惠鶚曰：「余食貧久矣，室無增資，為我致意縣宰，且求假貸。 」由是濮之部內五邑令長共斂錢五十萬，私獻於瑜。 瑜即以書上奏，高祖覽章嘆曰：「廉直清慎有如此者，誠良臣也。 」於是二吏五宰即時停黜，擢瑜為太府少卿。 杜重威之鎮東平也，瑜父欽祚為節度副使，及重威移鎮常山，瑜乃詭計於重威，使奏己為恒州節度副使，竟代其父位。 歲餘，入為刑部郎中。 丙午歲，父欽祚刺舉義州，瑜歸寧至郡。 會契丹據有中夏，何建以秦州歸蜀，瑜說欽祚曰：「若不西走，當屬契丹矣！ 」厲色數諫，其父怒而不從。 因其臥疾涉旬，瑜仗劍而脅之曰：「老懦無謀，欲趨炮烙。 不即為計，則死於刃下。 」父不得已而聽之。 時隴東屯兵扼其川路，將北趣蕃部假途，而因與郡盜酋長趙徽歃血為約，以兄事之。 謂徽曰：「西至成都，余身為相，余父為將，爾當領一大郡，能遂行乎？ 」徽曰：「諾。 」瑜慮為所賣，先致其妻孥，館於郡中。 行有期矣，徽潛召其黨，伺於郊外。 子夜，瑜舉族行，輜重絡繹十有餘里。 徽之所親，循溝澮而遁，至馬峽路隅，舉燧相應，其黨起於伏莽，斷欽祚之首，貫諸長矛。 平生聚蓄金幣萬計，皆為賊所掠，少長百口，殺之殆盡。 瑜尚獨戰千人，矢不虛發，手無射捍，其指流血。 及窘，乃夜竄山谷，落發為僧。 月餘，為樵人所獲，縶送岐州，為侯益所殺，時年三十九。
Wang Yu's family originally came from Fanyang. His father Qinhuo rose to director of the palace domestic service and was then posted out as prefect of Yi. Yu was fierce and treacherous by nature, yet he was eloquent, bold, and resolute, and his prowess in horsemanship, archery, and the written arts also won renown in his day. He began his career with successive appointments as staff officer. During the Tianfu period he was appointed left mentor of the heir apparent. When Pu prefecture enjoyed a bountiful autumn harvest but its tax registers were uneven, he was sent out in the envoy carriage to inspect the district and set the levies aright. On reaching the prefecture he told the registry clerks Hu Yun and Hui E, "I have been poor for a long time and my household has no extra income. Pass word to the district magistrates for me and ask them for loans." " The magistrates of the five districts within Pu then pooled five hundred thousand cash and privately presented it to Yu. Yu immediately submitted a memorial. When Emperor Gaozu read it he exclaimed, "Integrity, uprightness, purity, and caution such as this—truly a fine minister." " The two clerks and five magistrates were dismissed on the spot, and Yu was promoted to vice director of the Court of the Imperial Treasury. When Du Chongwei held Dongping, Yu's father Qinhuo served as his deputy military governor. After Chongwei was transferred to Changshan, Yu tricked him into memorializing for Yu himself as deputy military governor of Heng, and in the end Yu took his father's place. After a little more than a year he was recalled to the capital as director in the Ministry of Justice. In the bingwu year his father Qinhuo was serving as investigating commissioner in Yi prefecture, and Yu went home on leave to visit him there. The Khitan had seized the Central Plains, and He Jian had surrendered Qin to Shu. Yu urged his father, "If we do not flee west, we shall end up under the Khitan!" " He remonstrated again and again with a fierce expression, but his father grew angry and refused to listen. When his father had been bedridden for more than ten days, Yu drew his sword and threatened him: "You feeble old fool with no plan—you mean to march straight into the torturer's fire!" If you do not act now, you die by this blade." " His father had no choice but to yield. Longdong garrison troops then blocked the river routes, so they planned to head north through the tribal lands to borrow a passage. Yu therefore swore a blood oath with the bandit chieftain Zhao Hui of the prefecture and treated him as an elder brother. He told Hui, "When we reach Chengdu in the west, I shall become chief minister and my father a general, and you shall govern a great prefecture. Will you go through with this?" " Hui said, "Agreed." " Fearing betrayal, Yu first sent his wife and children ahead and lodged them in the prefectural seat. When the day of departure was fixed, Hui secretly gathered his men and waited in ambush outside the city. At midnight Yu set out with his entire clan, their baggage trains trailing for more than ten li. Hui's confederates slipped along ditches and canals until they reached a bend on the road at Maxia, where they lit signal fires. His men sprang from ambush in the brush, cut off Qinhuo's head, and spitted it on long spears. The gold and coin he had amassed over a lifetime—tens of thousands in all—were plundered by the bandits. Of more than a hundred people, young and old alike, nearly all were slaughtered. Yu still fought alone against a thousand men, and every arrow found its mark. He had no archer's thumb guard, and his fingers ran with blood. When he was cornered, he fled by night into the mountains, shaved his head, and became a monk. After more than a month woodcutters captured him, bound him, and sent him to Qi prefecture, where Hou Yi had him executed. He was thirty-nine.

始瑜有姑寡居，來歸其家，以前夫遺腹有子，經數年不產，每因事預告人吉兇，無不驗者。 時契丹入中闕，前月餘謂瑜曰：「暴兵將至，宜速去之，茍不去，亂必及矣。 」後瑜果死，此謂「天作孽，猶可違； 自作孽，不可逭」也。
Yu had an aunt who lived as a widow and came to live in his household. She was said to be carrying her late husband's posthumous child, yet for years she bore none. Whenever something happened she would foretell people's fortune or misfortune, and her predictions always came true. When the Khitan entered the central capital, more than a month earlier she had told Yu, "Violent troops are coming—you should flee at once. If you do not leave, calamity will overtake you." " Yu did indeed die afterward. This illustrates the saying, "When Heaven brings calamity, one may still defy it; but when one brings calamity on oneself, there is no escape."

Zhang Jizuo was a son of the former Prince of Qi, Zhang Quanyi. He began as commander of the Henan prefectural guard. After Quanyi's death he was appointed general of the Golden Wu Guard, soon made prefect of Cai, and eventually rose to honorary grand guardian. When Emperor Mingzong performed the suburban sacrifice to Heaven, Jizuo served as provisioning commissioner and was again appointed general of the Western Guard. At the end of the Qingtai reign of Tang he entered mourning for his mother. Early in the Tianfu period, before his mourning term had ended, Zhang Congbin rebelled. Troops were sent to coerce Jizuo, who was taken to Heyang and ordered to serve as regent of the garrison. When Congbin was defeated, he and his two sons were executed by imperial order in the marketplace. Jizuo had long been on friendly terms with Fan Yanguang and once sent him horses as a gift. When the court raised an army to attack Yecheng, patrol troops seized the gift and reported it, and Emperor Gaozu took deep offense. After his defeat, chief minister Sang Weihan—whose father Gong had long served the Prince of Qi—memorialized to clear the family's name, but Emperor Gaozu refused, Here an editorial opening bracket appears in the text. Thereupon the execution of Jizuo's wife and children was halted.) Punishment was then limited to Jizuo's own household and did not extend to the wider clan.

胡饒，大梁人也。 少事本鎮連帥為都吏，歷馬步都虞候。 會唐明宗鎮其地，與部將王建立相善，明宗即位，建立領常山，奏饒為真定少尹。 饒本憸人，既在府幕，無士君子之風。 嘗因事趙郡，有平棘令張鵬者獻策，請建立於境內每縣所管鄉置鄉直一人，令月書縣令出入行止，饒乃導而薦焉。 建立行之彌年，詞訟蜂起，四郡大擾。 天成末，王都構亂，陰使結建立為兄弟之國。 時饒又曾薦梁時右庶子張澄為判官，建立亦狎之。 澄素不知書，每座則以《陰府》、《鬼谷》為己任。 建立時密以王都之盟告之，澄與饒俱贊成其事，會王師圍中山，其事遂寢，而饒之兇戾如此。 清泰初，馮道出鎮同州，饒時為副使，道以重臣，稀於接洽，饒忿之，每乘酒於牙門詬道，道必延入，待以酒肴，致敬而退。 道謂左右曰：「此人為不善，自當有報，吾何怒焉。 」饒後閑居河陽。 天福二年夏，會張從賓作亂，饒謁於麾下，請預其行。 從賓敗，饒以王建立方鎮平盧，走投之，建立延入城，斬之以聞，聞者快焉。
Hu Rao was a native of Daliang. In youth he served the circuit military governor as chief clerk and later held the post of commander of horse and foot. When Emperor Mingzong of Tang governed the region, Rao was on good terms with staff general Wang Jianli. After Mingzong took the throne, Jianli held Changshan and recommended Rao as vice prefect of Zhending. Rao was treacherous by nature, and once he entered the governor's staff he showed none of a gentleman's conduct. Once while on business in Zhao commandery, Zhang Peng, magistrate of Pingji, proposed that Jianli appoint one village inspector in each township under every district, with monthly reports on the magistrates' movements. Rao promoted the scheme. Jianli implemented it for more than a year, lawsuits multiplied, and the four commanderies were thrown into turmoil. At the end of the Tiancheng period Wang Du plotted rebellion and secretly sent envoys to ally with Jianli as brother states. Rao had also recommended Zhang Cheng, former right mentor of the heir under Liang, as administrative aide, and Jianli was equally fond of him. Cheng was illiterate by nature and at every gathering treated the Yin Fu and Guiguzi as his specialty. Jianli secretly told them of Wang Du's alliance, and both Cheng and Rao endorsed it. When the imperial army besieged Zhongshan the plot collapsed, but Rao's savagery was of this sort. Early in the Qingtai period Feng Dao was posted to govern Tong, with Rao as his deputy. As a senior minister Dao rarely received visitors, which angered Rao, who when drunk would curse him at headquarters. Dao always invited him in, served wine and food, paid his respects, and let him leave. Dao told his attendants, "This man does evil; he will bring retribution on himself—why should I be angry?" " Later Rao lived in retirement at Heyang. In the summer of the second Tianfu year Zhang Congbin rebelled. Rao presented himself to Congbin and asked to join the campaign. When Congbin was defeated, Rao fled to Wang Jianli, who was then military governor of Pinglu. Jianli welcomed him into the city, beheaded him, and reported the deed; those who heard of it were delighted.

劉遂清，字得一，青州北海人，梁開封尹鄩之猶子也。 父琪，以鴻臚卿致仕。 遂清少敏惠，初仕梁為保鑾軍使，歷內諸司使，莊宗入汴，不改其職。 明宗即位，加檢校尚書僕射，委以西都監守。 逾歲，以中山王都有不臣之跡，除遂清為易州刺史，俾遏其寇沖，既至郡，大有禦侮之略，境內賴焉。 王都平，加檢校司空，遷棣州刺史。 天成、長興中，歷典淄、興、登三郡，咸有善政。 〈 （《通鑒潞王紀》：帝之起鳳翔也，召興州刺史劉遂清，遲疑不至。 聞帝入洛，乃悉集三泉、西縣、金牛、桑林戍兵以歸，自散關以南，城鎮悉棄之，皆為蜀人所有。 入朝，帝欲治罪，以其能自歸，乃赦之。）〉 高祖即位之二年，授鳳州防禦使，加檢校司徒，會丁母憂，起復，授內客省使、右監門衛大將軍。 六年，駕幸鄴都，轉宣徽北院使兼判三司，加檢校太保。 七年，少帝嗣位，加右領軍衛上將軍，仍賜竭誠翊戴保節功臣。 八年，出領鄭州，加檢校太傅。 開運二年，遷安州防禦使。 未幾，上表稱疾，詔許就便，回至上蔡，終於郵舍，時三年四月也。
Liu Suiqing, courtesy name Deyi, was from Beihai in Qing prefecture and was a nephew by marriage of Zheng, Liang's chief minister of the capital. His father Qi had retired as director of the Court of State Ceremonial. Suiqing was clever from youth. He first served Liang as commissioner of the palace guard, held successive posts as commissioner of the inner bureaus, and when Zhuangzong entered Bian his offices were left unchanged. When Mingzong took the throne he was given the honorary title of vice director of the Masters of Writing and entrusted with guarding the Western Capital. A year later, when Wang Du of Zhongshan showed signs of disloyalty, Suiqing was appointed prefect of Yi to block his raids. On reaching the prefecture he devised strong defenses, and the region came to rely on him. After Wang Du was pacified he was given the honorary title of minister of works and transferred to prefect of Li. During the Tiancheng and Changxing periods he governed in succession the prefectures of Zi, Xing, and Deng, and in each he administered well. Here an editorial opening bracket appears in the text. (The Zizhi Tongjian, Annals of the Prince of Lu: When the emperor rose at Fengxiang he summoned Liu Suiqing, prefect of Xing, but Suiqing hesitated and did not come.) When he heard that the emperor had entered Luoyang, he gathered all garrison troops from Sanquan, Xixian, Jinniu, and Sanglin and withdrew. From San Pass southward he abandoned every city and town, and all fell to Shu. When he came to court the emperor wished to punish him, but because he had returned of his own accord he was pardoned.) In the second year of Emperor Gaozu's reign he was appointed defense commissioner of Feng and given the honorary title of grand master. When his mother died he was recalled from mourning and appointed commissioner of the Inner Guests Bureau and great general of the Right Gate Guard. In the sixth year, when the emperor visited Yedu, he was transferred to commissioner of the northern bureau of the palace secretariat with concurrent charge of the Three Bureaus, and given the honorary title of grand guardian. In the seventh year, when the Young Emperor succeeded, he was made general of the Right Army Guard and granted the title of Meritorious Minister of Loyal Support and Preservation of Integrity. In the eighth year he was posted to govern Zheng and given the honorary title of grand tutor. In the second year of Kaiyun he was transferred to defense commissioner of An. Before long he memorialized that he was ill; an edict allowed him to return at his convenience. On the way back he reached Shangcai and died at a post station, in the fourth month of the third year.

遂清性至孝，牧淄川日，自北海迎其母赴郡，母既及境，遂清奔馳路側，控轡行數十里，父老觀者如堵，當時榮之。 遂清素不知書，但多計畫，判三司日，每給百官俸料，與判官議曰：「斯輩非盡有才能，多世祿之家，宜澄其汙而留其清者。 」或對曰：「昔唐朝渾、郭、顏、段，每一赦出，以一子出身，率為常制，且延賞垂裕，為國美譚，未有因月給而欲沙汰，恐未當也。 」群論由此減之。
Suiqing was profoundly filial by nature. While governing Zichuan he went from Beihai to bring his mother to the prefecture. When she reached the border, he ran along the roadside holding the reins and walked with her carriage for several tens of li. The elders who watched lined the road, and contemporaries praised the deed. Suiqing was illiterate by nature but full of schemes. While in charge of the Three Bureaus, whenever he issued officials' salaries he would tell his aides, "These men are not all capable—many come from hereditary salary families. We should sift out the corrupt and keep the upright." " Someone replied, "Under Tang the Hun, Guo, Yan, and Duan families regularly saw one son enter office with each amnesty—that was established custom. It spread reward and left abundance, a celebrated state policy. No one has ever tried to purge men over monthly salaries; I fear that would be inappropriate." " The proposal was dropped after this discussion.

劉繼勛，衛州人也。 唐天成中，高祖鎮鄴都，繼勛時為客將，高祖愛其端謹，籍其名於帳下，從歷數鎮。 及即位，擢為閣門使，出為淄州刺史，遷澶州防禦使，俄改鄭州，自宣徽北院使拜華州刺史。 歲餘，鎮同州。 始少帝與契丹絕好，繼勛亦預其謀，及契丹主至闕，繼勛自鎮來朝，契丹責之。 時馮道在側，繼勛事急，指道曰：「少帝在鄴，道為首相，與景延廣謀議，遂致南北失歡。 臣位至卑，未嘗措言，今請問道，道細知之。 」契丹主曰：「此老子不是好鬧人，無相牽引，皆爾輩為之。 」繼勛不敢復對。 繼勛時有疾，契丹主因令人候其疾狀，雲有風痹，契丹主曰：「北方地涼，居之此疾可愈。 」乃命鎖繼勛。 尋解之，以疾終於家。 〈 （《通鑒》：契丹主聞趙在禮死，乃釋繼勛，繼勛憂憤而卒。）〉 漢高祖入汴，贈太尉。
Liu Jixun was a native of Wei prefecture. During the Tiancheng period of Tang, when the future Emperor Gaozu held Yedu, Jixun served as a guest general. Gaozu admired his steadiness and care, enrolled him in his personal staff, and took him through several postings. After Gaozu took the throne Jixun was promoted to commissioner of the Gate and Attendant Office, then posted as prefect of Zi, transferred to defense commissioner of Can, soon moved to Zheng, and from commissioner of the northern palace secretariat bureau was appointed prefect of Hua. After a little more than a year he was posted to govern Tong. When the Young Emperor broke with the Khitan, Jixun had taken part in the plot. When the Khitan ruler reached the capital, Jixun came from his post to court, and the Khitan reproached him. Feng Dao stood beside him. In his panic Jixun pointed at Dao and said, "When the Young Emperor was at Ye, Dao was chief minister and plotted with Jing Yanguang, which is what brought North and South to estrangement." My rank was too low for me to speak. Question Dao—he knows every detail." " The Khitan ruler said, "This old man is no troublemaker—do not drag him into it. You people did all of this." " Jixun dared not answer again. Jixun was ill at the time. The Khitan ruler sent someone to inquire after his condition and was told he had rheumatic paralysis. The Khitan ruler said, "The north is cool; if he lives there the illness may clear." " He then ordered Jixun put in chains. He was soon released and died of illness at home. Here an editorial opening bracket appears in the text. (The Zizhi Tongjian: When the Khitan ruler heard that Zhao Zaili was dead, he released Jixun, who died of grief and anger.) When the Gaozu of Han entered Bian, Jixun was posthumously granted grand commandant.

鄭受益， 〈 （《新唐書·宰相世系表》：字謙光。）〉 唐宰相餘慶之曾孫也。 余慶生浣，浣生從讜，兩為太原節度使，再登相位。 從讜兄處誨，為汴州節度使。 家襲清儉，深有士風，中朝禮法，以鄭氏為甲。 處誨生受益。 受益亦以文學致身，累歷臺閣，自尚書郎遷右諫議大夫。 天福七年夏，以張彥澤數為不道，上章請行國典，旬日不報。 又貢表切言，訐直無所忌，執政稍惡之。 俄而以病請告，歸長安。 高祖晏駕，以不赴國哀停任，會赦，拜京兆少尹。 宰相趙瑩出鎮鹹秦，以受益朝班舊僚，眷待甚至。 屬天下率借金谷，乃謂瑩曰：「京兆戶籍登耗，民力虛實，某備知之矣，品而定之，可使平允。 」瑩信之，因使與王人同掌其事。 受益既經廢棄，薄於仕宦，遂阿法射利，冀為生生之資； 又素恃門望，陵轢同幕，內奸外直，群情無相洽者。 及贓汙事發，騰於眾口，瑩不得已，遂按之，其直百萬。 八年冬，賜死於家。 受益數世公臺，一朝自棄，士君子皆惜之。
Zheng Shouyi, Here an editorial opening bracket appears in the text. (New Book of Tang, Genealogical Tables of Chief Ministers: courtesy name Qianguang.) He was the great-grandson of the Tang chief minister Yuqing. Yuqing's son was Huan; Huan's son was Congdan, who twice served as military governor of Taiyuan and twice reached the chief ministership. Congdan's elder brother Chuhui was military governor of Bian. The family maintained purity and frugality and bore a deep scholar-gentleman's air; in court ritual and propriety the Zheng clan ranked first. Chuhui was Shouyi's father. Shouyi likewise rose through scholarship, served repeatedly in the censorate and secretariat, and was promoted from director in the Masters of Writing to right remonstrance adviser. In the summer of the seventh Tianfu year, because Zhang Yanzhe had repeatedly committed outrages, Shouyi memorialized asking that the national law be applied; for ten days there was no response. He submitted another memorial speaking bluntly, impeaching without fear; the authorities gradually turned against him. Soon he asked leave on grounds of illness and returned to Chang'an. When Emperor Gaozu died, Shouyi was suspended for failing to attend the national mourning; after an amnesty he was appointed vice prefect of the capital district. When chief minister Zhao Ying was posted to govern Xianqin, he treated Shouyi, an old court colleague, with exceptional favor. When the realm was levying grain loans everywhere, he told Ying, "I know the capital district's household registers, gains and losses, and the people's real capacity in full. Grade and fix them and the levies can be made fair." " Ying trusted him and had him manage the affair jointly with Wang Ren. Shouyi, already cast aside from office, cared little for public service and bent the law for profit, hoping to secure a livelihood; and he had long relied on his family's standing, bullied colleagues on the staff, and was treacherous within while appearing upright without, so that none on the staff were on good terms with him. When the bribery scandal broke it spread everywhere; Ying had no choice but to investigate, and the sum came to a million cash. In the winter of the eighth year he was ordered to take his own life at home. The Zheng family had served at the highest levels for generations; in a single morning Shouyi threw it all away, and men of cultivation lamented it.

程遜，字浮休，壽春人。 〈 （案：此下有闕文。）〉 召入翰林充學士，自兵部侍郎承旨授太常卿。 天福三年秋，命使吳越， 〈 （《十國春秋》云：禮部尚書程遜為加恩使。）〉 母羸老雙瞽，遜未嘗白執政以辭之。 將行，母以手捫其面，號泣以送之。 仲秋之夕，陰暝如晦，遜嘗為詩曰：「幽室有時聞雁叫，空庭無路見蟾光。 」同僚見之，訝其詩語稍異。 及使回，遭風水而溺焉。

He was summoned to the Hanlin as an academician and promoted from vice minister of War and bearer of edicts to director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. In the autumn of the third Tianfu year he was ordered as envoy to Wuyue, Here an editorial opening bracket appears in the text. His mother was frail, elderly, and blind in both eyes, yet Xun had never asked the authorities to excuse him from the mission on her account. As he was about to leave, his mother felt his face with her hands and wailed as she sent him off. On a mid-autumn evening the sky was dark as night. 李郁，字文緯，唐之宗屬也。 少歷宗寺官，天成、長興中，累遷為宗正卿。 性平允，所歷無愛憎毀譽。 高祖登極，授光祿卿。 一日晝寢，夢食巨棗，覺而有疾，謂其親友曰：「嘗聞『棗』字重『來』，呼魂之象也。 余神氣逼抑，將不免乎！ 」天福五年夏卒。 贈太子太保。 馬重績，字洞微。 少學數術，明太一、五紀、八象、三統大歷，居於太原。 仕晉，拜太子右贊善大夫，遷司天監。 天福三年，重績上言：「歷象，王者所以正一氣之元，宣萬邦之命，而古今所記，考審多差。 《宣明》氣朔正而星度不驗，《崇元》五星得而歲差一日。 以《宣明》之氣朔，合《崇元》之五星，二歷相參，然後符合。 自前世諸歷，皆起天正十一月為歲首，用太古甲子為上元，積歲愈多，差闊愈甚。 臣輒合二歷，創為新法，以唐天寶十四載乙未為上元，雨水正月中氣為氣首。 」詔下司天監趙仁琦、張文皓等考核得失，仁琦等言：「明年庚子正月朔，用重績歷考之，皆合無舛。 」乃下詔班行之，號《調元曆》。 行之數歲輒差，遂不用。 重績又言：「漏刻之法，以中星考晝夜為一百刻，八刻六十分刻之二十為一時，時以四刻十分為正，此自古所用也。 今失其傳，以午正為時始，下侵未四刻十分而為午，由是晝夜昏曉，皆失其正，請依古改正。 」從之。 重績卒年六十四。
Li Yu, courtesy name Wenwei, was a member of the Tang imperial clan. In youth he served in the clan temple offices; during the Tiancheng and Changxing periods he rose in succession to director of the Court of the Imperial Clan. He was even-tempered and fair-minded, and in every post he held he inspired neither favoritism nor slander. When Emperor Gaozu took the throne he was appointed director of the Court of Imperial Entertainments. One day he napped and dreamed of eating an enormous jujube. When he woke he was ill and told his kin and friends, "I have heard that the character for 'jujube' contains the character for 'come'—a sign that the soul is being summoned back." My spirit feels crushed—I suppose I shall not escape death!" " He died in the summer of the fifth Tianfu year. He was posthumously granted grand guardian of the heir apparent. Ma Zhongji, courtesy name Dongwei. From youth he studied numerology and mastered the Taiyi, Five Records, Eight Images, and Santong Great Calendar, and he lived in Taiyuan. He served Jin, was appointed right mentor of the heir apparent, and was then made director of the Directorate of Astronomy. In the third Tianfu year Zhongji memorialized, "Calendars and celestial observations are how kings rectify the origin of cosmic order and proclaim the mandate to the myriad states, yet records from antiquity to the present, when examined closely, contain many errors." The Xuanming calendar's qi and new moons are correct but its star positions fail verification; the Chongyuan calendar tracks the five planets but its year is off by a day. Combine the Xuanming calendar's qi and new moons with the Chongyuan calendar's five planets, and only when the two calendars are cross-referenced do they agree. Calendars since antiquity have all taken the eleventh month as the year's beginning and used the primordial jiazi cycle as the superior origin; the more years accumulate, the wider the error grows. I have combined the two calendars and devised a new method, taking the yiwei year of Tang Tianbao 14 as the superior origin and the mid-qi of the first month of Rain Water as the beginning of qi reckoning. " An edict ordered Zhao Renqi, Zhang Wenhao, and others at the Directorate of Astronomy to evaluate the proposal. They reported, "For the new moon of the first month of next year, the gengzi year, Zhongji's calendar matches in every respect without error." " An edict was then issued to promulgate it under the name Tiaoyuan Calendar. After a few years in use it began to drift, and it was abandoned. Zhongji also said, "The clepsydra method measures day and night as one hundred ke by the culminating star; eight ke plus twenty sixtieth-ke make one double-hour, and the double-hour is reckoned from four ke and ten parts—this is the ancient usage." The tradition has been lost; the current practice takes noon as the start of the double-hour and encroaches four ke and ten into wei to define noon, so that day and night, dusk and dawn, are all wrong. I ask that it be corrected according to the ancient method." " The court agreed. Zhongji died at the age of sixty-four.

陳元，京兆人也。 家世為醫，初事河中王重榮。 乾符中，後唐武皇自太原率師攻王行瑜，路出於蒲中，時元侍湯藥，武皇甚重之。 及還太原，日侍左右。 武皇性剛暴，樂殺人，無敢言者。 元深測其情，每有暴怒，則從容啟諫，免禍者不一，以是晉人深德之，勛貴賂遺盈門。 性好酒樂施，隨得而無私積。 明宗朝，為太原少尹，入為太府卿。 長興中，集平生所驗方七十五首，並修合藥法百件，號曰《要術》，刊石置於太原府衙門之左，以示於眾，病者賴焉。 天福中，以耄期上表求退，以光祿卿致仕，卒於晉陽，年八十餘。
Chen Yuan was a native of the capital district. His family had been physicians for generations; he first served Wang Chongrong of Hezhong. During the Qianfu period the Martial Emperor of Later Tang led troops from Taiyuan against Wang Xingyu, passing through Puzhong. Yuan attended him with medicines, and the Martial Emperor held him in high regard. When the emperor returned to Taiyuan, Yuan attended him daily at his side. The Martial Emperor was fierce and violent by nature, delighted in killing, and none dared remonstrate with him. Yuan read his moods closely; whenever the emperor flew into a rage Yuan would calmly remonstrate, and many were spared death thereby. The people of Jin were deeply grateful, and gifts from nobles filled his gate. He loved wine and delighted in giving away whatever came to him, never hoarding for himself. Under Emperor Mingzong he served as vice prefect of Taiyuan, then entered the capital as director of the Court of the Imperial Treasury. During the Changxing period he compiled seventy-five prescriptions he had tested in his lifetime along with a hundred methods of compounding medicines, titled Essential Methods, had them carved on stone, and set the stele to the left of the Taiyuan prefectural gate for all to see; the sick benefited from them. During the Tianfu period, citing his great age, he memorialized to retire and left office as director of the Court of Imperial Entertainments; he died at Jinyang in his eighties.

史臣曰：夫彰善癉惡，《麟史》之為義也； 瑜不掩瑕，虹玉之為德也。 故自崇弼而下，善者既書之，其不善者亦書之，庶使後之君子見善如不及，見惡如探湯也。 至如重績之曆法，陳元之醫道，亦不可漏其名而弗紀也。
The historiographer writes: To make good manifest and evil hateful is the purpose of the Spring and Autumn Annals; and not to conceal flaws is the virtue of rainbow jade. Therefore from Chongbi onward we have recorded the good and also recorded the bad, so that later gentlemen, seeing good, may strive to match it, and seeing evil, may shrink from it as from scalding water. As for Zhongji's calendrical science and Chen Yuan's medical art, their names too must not be omitted from the record.
