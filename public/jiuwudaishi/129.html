常思，字克恭，太原人也。 父仁嶽，河東牙將，累贈太子太師。 唐莊宗之為晉王也，廣募勝兵，時思以趫悍應募，累從戎役，後為長直都校，歷捧聖軍使。 晉初，遷六軍都虞候。 漢高祖出鎮並門，奏以思從行，尋表為河東牢城都指揮使，以勤幹見稱。 漢國初建，授檢校太保，遙領鄧州。 漢有天下，遷檢校太尉、昭義軍節度使。 乾佑初，李守貞叛於河中，太祖征之，朝廷命思帥部兵以副焉。 既而禦眾無能，勒歸舊藩。 思在上黨凡五年，無令譽可稱，唯以聚斂為務，性又鄙吝，未嘗與賓佐有酒肴之會。 嘗有從事欲求謁見者，思覽刺而怒曰：「彼必是來獵酒也。 」命典客者飲而遣之，其鄙吝也如是。 太祖受命，就加平章事。 初，太祖微時，以季父待思，及即位，遣其妻入覲，太祖拜之如家人之禮，仍呼曰叔母，其恩顧如是。 廣順二年秋，思來朝，加兼侍中，移鎮宋州。 三年夏，詔赴闕，改授平盧軍節度使。 思將赴鎮，奏太祖云：「臣在宋州出鎮，得絲十餘萬兩，謹以上進，請行征督。 」太祖頷之，尋詔本州折券以諭其民。 及到鎮，未幾，染風痹之疾，上表請尋醫，既而舁疾歸洛。 顯德元年春卒，年六十有九。 贈中書令。
Chang Si, style name Kejing, was from Taiyuan. His father Ren Yue had served as a military aide in Hedong and was repeatedly posthumously honored as Grand Preceptor of the Heir Apparent. When Zhuangzong of Tang was still Prince of Jin, he raised troops far and wide; Si enlisted for his speed and boldness, saw action in campaign after campaign, and rose from commander of the Long Straight guard to commissioner of the Sacred Support Army. Early in the Later Tang, he was made Grand Commandant of Inspectors for the Six Armies. When the future Gaozu of Han took up his post at Taiyuan, he had Si accompany him and soon had him appointed to command the Hedong fortified-city garrison, where his industry and efficiency won notice. At the founding of the Later Han, he received the acting rank of Grand Guardian and a nominal commission over Deng Prefecture. Once the Han ruled the empire, he was promoted to Acting Grand Commandant and made military commissioner of the Zhaoyi Army. Early in the Qianyou era, when Li Shouzhen rebelled at Hezhong, Taizu marched against him and the court ordered Si to bring his forces as a subordinate column. He soon proved unable to handle his troops and was ordered back to his old command. For five years at Shangdang, Si earned no good name: he cared only for squeezing revenue, was tight-fisted by nature, and never once treated his staff to a meal or a cup of wine. When a staff officer once asked for an audience, Si read his card and snapped, "He only wants to wheedle wine out of me." He had the usher give the man a drink and show him out—such was his meanness. When Taizu took the throne, Si was immediately made Grand Councilor. In his early days Taizu had treated Si like a younger uncle; after he became emperor he summoned Si's wife to court and greeted her with the deference one shows kin, still calling her "Aunt"—so deep was his regard. In the autumn of Guangshun 2, Si came to court, was made Vice Grand Secretary as well, and was reassigned to Song Prefecture. The next summer he was summoned to court and made military commissioner of the Pinglu Army. Before leaving for his new command, Si reported to Taizu: "When I left Song Prefecture I had over a hundred thousand taels of silk, which I now offer up; please let me continue to collect what is owed." Taizu nodded, and soon ordered the bonds in that prefecture torn up and the people told. Soon after he took up his post he fell ill with wind paralysis, asked leave to seek treatment, and was carried back to Luoyang. He died in the spring of Xiande 1, at the age of sixty-nine. He was posthumously honored as Chief Secretariat Director.

翟光鄴，字化基，濮州鄄城人。 父景珂，倜儻有膽氣。 梁貞明初，唐莊宗始駐軍於河上，景珂率聚邑人守永定驛，固守逾年，後為北軍所攻，景珂戰歿，眾潰。 光鄴時年十歲，為明宗軍所俘，以其穎悟，俾侍左右，字之曰永定。 既冠，沈毅有謀，蒞事寡過。 明宗即位，時深委遇，累遷至皇城使、檢校司空。 長興中，樞密使安重誨得罪，時光鄴與中官孟小僧頗有力焉。 居無何，出為耀州團練使。 清泰初，入為左監門衛大將軍。 晉天福中，歷棣沂二州刺史、西京副留守。 開運初，授宣徽使。 楊光遠叛滅，青州平，除為防禦使，朝廷以兵亂之後，人物雕弊，故命光鄴理之。 光鄴好聚書，重儒者，虛齋論議，惟求理道。 時郡民喪亡十之六七，而招懷撫諭，視之如傷，故期月之間，流亡載輯。 契丹入汴，偽命權知曹州。 李從益假號，以光鄴明宗舊臣，署為樞密使。 漢祖至汴，改左領衛大將軍。 乾祐初，遷右金吾衛大將軍，充街使、檢校太保。 太祖踐阼，復授宣徽使、左千牛衛上將軍、檢校太傅。 數月，兼樞密副使。 會永興李洪信入朝，代知軍府事。 廣順二年十月，卒於長安，時年四十六。
Zhai Guangye, style name Huaji, came from Juancheng in Puzhou. His father Jing Ke was bold and high-spirited. Early in Liang's Zhenming era, when Zhuangzong of Tang first camped on the Yellow River, Jing Ke rallied his neighbors to hold Yongding Post for over a year until the northern army broke in; Jing Ke fell in battle and his men fled. Guangye was only ten when Mingzong's troops captured him; struck by the boy's quick mind, the emperor kept him at his side and gave him the style name Yongding. Once he came of age he proved steady, shrewd, and seldom at fault in office. Under Mingzong he won deep trust and rose through the ranks to Imperial City Commissioner and Acting Minister of Works. During Changxing, when Chief Councillor An Chonghui fell, Guangye and the eunuch Meng Xiaoseng had played no small part. Soon afterward he was posted out as regimental commissioner of Yaozhou. At the start of Qingtai he returned to court as Grand General of the Left Gate Guard. Under Later Jin's Tianfu reign he served as prefect of Di and Yi in turn and as deputy defender of the Western Capital. At the start of Kaiyun he was made Palace Secretariat Commissioner. After Yang Guangyuan's rebellion was crushed and Qingzhou pacified, he was made defense commissioner; with the region laid waste by war, the court sent Guangye to rebuild it. He collected books, honored scholars, and in his spare study debated nothing but principle and right conduct. Six or seven in ten of the people had died, yet he drew in the survivors with gentle care, and within a month the exiles were streaming home. When the Khitans took Bian, they installed him as acting prefect of Caozhou. When Li Congyi claimed the throne, he named Guangye Chief Councillor as a former servant of Mingzong. When the founder of Han reached Bian, he was made Grand General of the Left Leading Guard. Early in Qianyou he became Grand General of the Right Golden Crow Guard, street commissioner, and Acting Grand Guardian. When Taizu took the throne, he again received the posts of Palace Secretariat Commissioner, Senior General of the Left Thousand-Ox Guard, and Acting Grand Mentor. A few months later he was also made Deputy Chief Councillor. When Li Hongxin of Yongxing came to court, he took over the military administration in his stead. He died at Chang'an in the tenth month of Guangshun 2, aged forty-six.

光鄴有器度，慎密敦厚，出於天然，喜慍不形於色。 事繼母以孝聞，兄弟皆雍睦。 雖食祿日久，家無餘財，任金吾日，假官屋數間以蔽風雨，親族累重，糲食才給，人不堪其憂，光鄴處之晏如也。 賓朋至，則貰酒延之，談說終日，略無厭倦，士大夫多之。 及權知京兆，以寬靜為治，前政有煩苛之事，一切停罷，百姓便之。 及病甚，召親隨於臥內，戒之曰：「氣絕之後，以屍歸洛，不得於此停留，慮煩軍府。 」言訖而終。 京兆吏如喪所親，或有以漿酒遙奠者。 樞密使王峻素重光鄴，且欲厚恤其家，為之上請，故自終及葬，所賜賻赗幾數千計。 詔贈太子少師。 光鄴膚革肥皙，善於攝養，故司天監趙延乂有袁、許之術，嘗謂人曰：「翟君外厚而內薄，雖貴而無壽。 」果如其言。
He had true breadth of mind—cautious, discreet, and naturally kind—and never let pleasure or anger show. His filial care for his stepmother was renowned, and he lived in harmony with his brothers. Though he had drawn salary for years, his house held no spare cash; as Golden Crow commissioner he rented a few government rooms against the weather, and with a large clan to feed on coarse grain alone others would have despaired—yet he remained perfectly at ease. When friends came he bought wine on credit and talked the day away without tiring; men of letters held him in high regard. As acting administrator of the Capital District he ruled with leniency and calm, abolishing every harsh practice of his predecessors to the people's relief. As his illness worsened he called his attendants to his bedside and said, "When I am gone, take my body back to Luoyang—do not leave it here and burden the command." He died as soon as he had spoken. The Capital District staff mourned him like a kinsman; some even poured libations from a distance. Chief Councillor Wang Jun, who had long esteemed him, petitioned for a generous settlement of his family, so that from death to burial the court's gifts ran to nearly a thousand items. He was posthumously honored as Junior Mentor of the Heir Apparent. Guangye's complexion was plump and fair and he took good care of himself; Zhao Yanyi of the Directorate of Astronomy, versed in physiognomy in the manner of Yuan and Xu, once said, "Master Zhai looks robust but is hollow within—rank will not buy him long life." Events proved him right.

曹英，字德秀，舊名犯今上御名，故改焉。 本常山鎮定人也。 父全武，事趙王王镕為列校，英因得隸於镕之帳下。 及張文禮之亂，唐莊宗奄有其地，乃錄镕之左右，署為散指揮使。 明宗即位，英侍於仗下，問其祖考，英以實對，明宗曰：「乃朕之舊也。 」擢為本班行首，每加顧遇。 晉天福中，遷弩手軍使。 平張從賓於汜水，以功授本軍都校。 漢初，改奉國軍主，加檢校司徒，兼康州刺史。 乾祐初，李守貞據河中叛，授行營步軍都校。 河中平，遷本軍廂主，領岳州防禦使。 隨太祖在魏，為北面行營步軍都校，從平內難。 國初，以翊戴功授昭武節度使、檢校太傅、侍衛步軍都指揮使。 二年春，總兵討慕容彥超於兗州，梯沖塹壘，頗有力焉。 夏五月，太祖親征，因並兵攻陷其城，及凱旋，領彰信軍節度使，典軍如故。 世宗嗣位，加同平章事，授成德軍節度使。 車駕自太原回，加兼侍中。 顯德元年冬，卒於鎮，時年四十九。 制贈中書令。 英性沈厚，謙恭有禮，雖衽席之際，接對賓客，亦未嘗造次。 及卒，搢紳之士亦皆惜之。
Cao Ying, style name Dexiu, had been given a birth name that tabooed the reigning emperor's name and was renamed accordingly. He was originally from Zhending in Changshan. His father Quanwu had served Zhao's King Wang Rong as a company commander, and Ying entered Rong's service through that connection. When Zhang Wenli rebelled and Zhuangzong of Tang seized the region, he enrolled Rong's former followers and made Ying a roving commander. When Mingzong took the throne, Ying stood guard at court; asked about his family, he answered frankly, and Mingzong said, "Why, he is an old friend of mine." He was made head of his guard unit and shown favor again and again. Under Later Jin's Tianfu reign he was made commissioner of the Crossbowmen Army. For his part in crushing Zhang Congbin at Sishui he was made commander of his army. Early in the Later Han he was made commander of the Supporting-the-State Army, Acting Minister of Education, and concurrent prefect of Kang Prefecture. Early in Qianyou, when Li Shouzhen rebelled at Hezhong, he was made commander of the campaign infantry. After Hezhong fell he became chief of his army's wing and defense commissioner of Yue Prefecture. He followed Taizu at Wei as commander of the northern campaign infantry and helped put down the internal crisis. At the dynasty's founding he was rewarded for helping place Taizu on the throne with the Zhaowu commission, Acting Grand Mentor, and command of the Palace Guard infantry. In the spring of the second year he led the campaign against Murong Yanchao at Yanzhou, where his siege works did much to decide the day. That summer Taizu marched in person; their combined assault took the city, and on the triumphal return Ying was made military commissioner of the Zhangxin Army while keeping his army command. When Shizong succeeded him, Ying was made Associate Grand Councilor and military commissioner of the Chengde Army. When the emperor returned from Taiyuan, he was also made Vice Grand Secretary. He died at his post in the winter of Xiande 1, aged forty-nine. An edict posthumously honored him as Chief Secretariat Director. Ying was grave and steady, humble and courteous; even in private he never treated guests with careless haste. At his death men of rank across the court mourned him.

李彥頵，字德循，太原人也。 本以商賈為業，太祖鎮鄴，置之左右，及即位，歷綾錦副使、搉易使。 世宗嗣位，以彥頵有舊，超授內客省使。 未幾，知相州軍府事，尋改延州兵馬留後。 到鎮，頗以殖貨為意，窺圖剩利，侵漁蕃漢部人，群情大擾。 會世宗南征，蕃部結聚，圍迫州城，彥頵閉壁自守，求援於鄰道，賴救兵至，乃解。 世宗不悅，征赴京師，然猶委曲庇護，竟不之責。 尋為西京水南巡檢使，居無何，命權知泗州軍州事，改滄州兩使留後。 彥頵到任，處置乖方，大為物情所鄙。 顯德六年秋，受代歸闕，遇疾而卒，時年五十有二。
Li Yanyun, style name Dexun, was from Taiyuan. He had begun as a merchant; when Taizu governed Ye he kept him close, and after the enthronement Yanyun rose through posts as deputy brocade commissioner and monopoly commissioner. Shizong, remembering their old tie, skipped the usual steps and made him commissioner of the Inner Guests Bureau. Soon he was administering Xiang Prefecture, then was posted as military regent of Yan Prefecture. At his post he set his mind on piling up wealth, squeezing every margin of profit from tribal and Han subjects alike until the whole region seethed. During Shizong's southern campaign tribesmen gathered and besieged the city; Yanyun shut the gates and held out until neighboring circuits sent relief. Shizong was displeased and recalled him to court, yet still shielded him and never held him to account. He was soon made inspector south of the Western Capital's waterways, then acting administrator of Sizhou, and finally regent of Cang Prefecture. Once installed at Cang he governed so badly that public opinion turned against him. In the autumn of Xiande 6 he was relieved and started back for court, but fell ill and died on the road at fifty-two.

李暉，字順光，瀛州束城人。 弱冠應募於龍驤軍，漢祖領河東，暉請從，因得署為河東牙將。 漢有天下，授檢校司徒、大內皇城使。 未幾，遷宣徽南院使。 乾祐初，拜河陽節度使、檢校太傅。 太祖登極，加同平章事，尋移鎮滄州。 顯德元年，就加兼侍中。 二年秋，以世宗誕慶節來朝，改邠州節度使。 五年，移鎮鳳翔。 歲餘，卒於鎮。 優詔贈中書令。 暉之儀貌不及於常人，而位極將相，年登耳順，袁、許之術，夫何恃哉！ 然性貪鄙，而好小惠以邀虛譽，故在河陽及滄州日，民皆詣闕請立碑以頌其美，識者亦未之許也。
Li Hui, style name Shunguang, came from Dongcheng in Ying Prefecture. As a young man he enlisted in the Dragon Soaring Army; when the founder of Han took Hedong, Hui asked to follow and was made a military aide there. Once the Han ruled the empire he received the acting rank of Minister of Education and the post of Inner Imperial City commissioner. Soon he was made commissioner of the Southern Palace Secretariat Court. Early in Qianyou he was made military commissioner of Heyang and Acting Grand Mentor. When Taizu took the throne he was made Associate Grand Councilor and soon reassigned to Cang Prefecture. In Xiande 1 he was also made Vice Grand Secretary. That autumn he came to court for Shizong's birthday and was reassigned to Bin Prefecture. In the fifth year he was moved to Fengxiang. A little over a year later he died in office. A gracious edict posthumously honored him as Chief Secretariat Director. Hui was no better looking than the next man, yet he rose to the highest civil and military ranks and lived to sixty—what need had he of physiognomy? Yet he was grasping and mean, and bought cheap goodwill with small favors; while he held Heyang and Cang the people even petitioned the throne for commemorative steles—though men of judgment were not fooled.

李建崇，潞州人。 少從軍，善騎射。 初事唐武皇，為鐵林都將，轉突騎、飛騎二軍使。 從莊宗攻常山，阿保機來援，莊宗率親軍千騎，遇於滿城，兵少，為契丹所圍。 時建崇為親將，與契丹格鬥，自午至申，會李嗣昭騎至，契丹乃解去。 同光中，自龍武捧聖都指揮使，出歷襄、秦、徐、雍都指揮使。 建崇性純厚，處身任遇，不能巧宦，以致久滯偏裨。 明宗嘗掌牙兵，與建崇共事，及即位，甚湣之，連授磁、沁二郡。 入晉為申州刺史。 天福七年冬，襄州安從進構逆，率眾寇南陽，時建崇領步騎千餘屯於葉縣，開封尹鄭王遣宣徽使張從恩、皇城使焦繼勛率在京諸軍，會建崇軍拒賊，至湖陽縣之花山，遇從進軍，建崇接戰，大敗之，以功授亳州團練使。 襄陽平，遷安州防禦使。 歷河陽、邢州兵馬留後。 漢初，入為右衛大將軍。 年逾七十，神氣不衰。 建崇始自代北事武皇，至是四十餘年，前後所掌兵，麾下部曲多至節鉞，零落殆盡，惟建崇雖位不及藩屏，而康強自適，以至期耄。 太祖即位，授左監門衛上將軍。 廣順三年春卒。 贈黔南節度使。
Li Jianchong was from Luzhou. He entered the army young and proved adept at mounted archery. He first served Li Keyong as a general of the Iron Forest guard, then commanded the Shock Cavalry and Flying Cavalry armies. When Zhuangzong attacked Changshan and Abaoji marched to relieve it, Zhuangzong led a thousand household troops to Mancheng; outnumbered, they were surrounded by the Khitans. Jianchong, then a household commander, fought the Khitans from midday into the afternoon until Li Sizhao's cavalry arrived and the siege lifted. Under Tongguang he advanced from command of the Dragon Martial Sacred Support Army to successive grand commands at Xiang, Qin, Xu, and Yong. Simple and honest by nature, he took whatever post he was given and never learned court craft, so he long remained a junior officer. Mingzong had once commanded the guard alongside him; on taking the throne he pitied Jianchong and gave him Ci and Qin prefectures in turn. Under Later Jin he became prefect of Shen Prefecture. In the winter of Tianfu 7, when An Congjin of Xiangzhou rebelled and marched on Nanyang, Jianchong held Ye County with a thousand-odd troops; the Prince of Zheng sent Zhang Congen and Jiao Jixun with the capital armies to join him. At Flower Mountain in Huyang County Jianchong met the rebels and crushed them, earning appointment as regimental commissioner of Bozhou. After Xiangyang fell he was made defense commissioner of An Prefecture. He later served as military regent of Heyang and Xingzhou. Early in the Later Han he returned to court as Grand General of the Right Guard. Past seventy, his vigor had not faded. Jianchong had followed Li Keyong from the north forty years earlier; of the men who had served under his command, many had become military commissioners and nearly all were gone—yet he, though never made a frontier commissioner, stayed hale into his eighties. When Taizu took the throne he was made Senior General of the Left Gate Guard. He died in the spring of Guangshun 3. He was posthumously honored as military commissioner of Qiannan.

Xu Qian was from Yanzhou. He began as a military aide in his home prefecture and was stern and unyielding by nature. Early in Later Han Qianyou he was General of the Left Garrison Guard and, with Ma Congbin of the Palace Workshops, oversaw ritual furnishings for the founding emperor's tomb, cutting costs by tens of thousands. Taizu issued a commendatory edict and confirmed him as prefect of Xizhou. Qian was relentless against bandits and punished beyond measure—sometimes nailing robbers to posts and having his men hack them apart. He once executed a man who should not have died; the family appealed to court and the case was referred to Kaifeng. Chen Guan, the Kaifeng prefect and Qian's longtime rival, pressed the case and sought to haul him back for trial; Taizu found the offense pardonable and merely stripped him of his post. 劉仁贍，略通儒術，好兵書，在澤國甚有聲望。 吳主知之，累遷為偽右監門衛將軍，歷黃、袁二州刺史，所至稱治。 洎李景僭襲偽位，俾掌親軍，遷鄂州節度使。 居數年，復以兵柄任之，改壽州節度使。 及王師渡淮，而仁贍固守甚堅。 洎世宗駐蹕於其壘北，數道齊攻，填塹陷壁，晝夜不息，如是者累月。 世宗臨城以諭之，而仁贍但遜詞以謝。 及車駕還京，命李重進總兵守之，復乘間陷我南寨。 自是圍之愈急，城中饑死者甚眾。 三年冬，淮寇復來救援，列寨於紫金山，夾道相屬，累然數十里，垂及壽壁，而重進兵幾不能支，世宗患之，遂復議親征。 車駕至壽春，命今上率師破紫金山之眾，擒其應援使陳承昭以獻。 仁贍聞援兵既敗，計無所出，但扼腕浩嘆而已。 會世宗以紫金山之捷，飛詔以諭之，時仁贍臥疾已亟，因翻然納款，而城內諸軍萬計，皆屏息以聽其命。 及見於行在，世宗撫之甚厚，賜與加等，復令入城養病，尋授天平軍節度使、兼中書令。 制出之日，薨於其家，年五十八。 世宗聞之，遣使吊祭，命內臣監護喪事，追封彭城郡王。 後以其子崇讃為懷州刺史。 仁贍輕財重士，法令嚴肅，重圍之中，其子崇諫犯軍禁，即令斬之，故能以一城之眾，連年拒守。 逮其來降，而其下未敢竊議者，保其後嗣，抑有由焉。
Liu Renshan knew some classics, loved military texts, and was highly regarded in the Yangtze delta. The Wu ruler noticed him and made him Acting Grand General of the Right Gate Guard; as prefect of Huang and Yuan he won praise for good government. When Li Jing seized the Southern Tang throne, he put Renshan in charge of the household armies and made him military commissioner of Ezhou. After some years he was again given command of troops and transferred to military commissioner of Shouzhou. When the Zhou army crossed the Huai, Renshan held Shouzhou with fierce tenacity. Shizong camped north of the walls and assaulted from every side, filling moats and smashing battlements day and night for months on end. Shizong came to the walls to summon him, but Renshan answered only with polite refusal. When the emperor returned to the capital, Li Chongjin was left to besiege the city; Renshan seized an opening and overran the southern Zhou camp. The siege tightened; starvation killed multitudes inside the walls. That winter Southern Tang relief forces camped on Zijin Mountain in a chain of stockades miles long, nearly linking to Shouzhou's walls; Chongjin's army nearly broke, and Shizong resolved to take the field himself. At Shouchun he ordered the future emperor—then Zhao Kuangyin—to rout the Zijin Mountain relief force and present the captured relief commissioner Chen Chengzhao. Hearing the relief army was destroyed, Renshan had no recourse but to wring his hands and sigh. After the Zijin victory Shizong sent a swift summons; Renshan, already gravely ill, at once surrendered, and the city's ten thousand defenders obeyed him in silence. At headquarters Shizong received him with great kindness and lavish gifts, sent him back to the city to convalesce, and soon appointed him military commissioner of Tianping and Grand Counselor. The day the commission was promulgated he died at home, aged fifty-eight. Shizong sent mourners, placed eunuchs in charge of the funeral, and posthumously enfeoffed him as Prince of Pengcheng. Later his son Chongzan was made prefect of Huaizhou. Renshan was generous to scholars and strict in law; during the siege his son Chongjian broke military discipline and was executed on the spot—which is why one city could hold out for years. When he surrendered, his men dared not murmur against him—they knew he had secured their families' futures, and for good reason.

崇贊仕周，累為郡守。 幼子崇諒，後自江南歸於本朝，亦位至省郎。
Chongzan served the Zhou and held several prefectures in succession. The youngest son Chongliang later came home from Jiangnan and rose to a post in the central ministries.
