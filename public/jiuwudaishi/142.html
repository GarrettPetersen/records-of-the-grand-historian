2
梁開平元年夏四月，太祖初受禪，乃立四廟於西京，從近古之制也。
In the fourth month of summer in the first year of Kaiping (907), when Liang Taizu first accepted the abdication, he established four ancestral temples in the Western Capital, in keeping with recent ancient practice.
3
唐同光二年六月，太常禮院奏：「國家興建之初，已於北都置廟，今克復天下，遷都洛陽，卻復本朝宗廟。 按禮無二廟之文，其北都宗廟請廢。 」乃下尚書省集議。 禮部尚書王正言等奏議曰：「伏以都邑之制，宗廟為先。 今卜洛居尊，開基禦宇，事當師古，神必依人。 北都先置宗廟，不宜並設。 況每年朝享，禮有常規，時日既同，神何所據。 竊聞近例，亦有從權。 如神主已修，迎之藏於夾室； 若廟宇已崇，虛之以為恒制。 若齊桓公之廟二主，禮無明文，古者師行，亦無遷於廟主。 昔天後之崇鞏、洛，禮謂非宜； 漢皇之戀豐、滕，事無所法。 況本朝故事，禮院具明，洛邑舊都，嵩高正位，豈宜遠宮闕之居，建祖宗之廟。 事非可久，理在從長。 其北都宗廟，請準太常禮院申奏停廢。 」從之。
In the sixth month of the second year of Tongguang (924), the Directorate of Rites of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices reported: "When the state was first founded, temples were already set up in the Northern Capital. Now that the empire has been recovered and the capital moved to Luoyang, the dynasty's ancestral temples have been restored there as well. According to ritual precedent, there is no authority for maintaining two ancestral temple complexes; we request that the temples in the Northern Capital be abolished. " The matter was referred to the Department of State Affairs for collective deliberation. Minister of Rites Wang Zhengyan and others submitted their opinion: "We respectfully observe that in the organization of a capital, the ancestral temples take precedence. Now that Luoyang has been chosen as the seat of power and the foundation laid to rule the realm, precedent should be followed, and the spirits will surely dwell where the people dwell. The temples previously established in the Northern Capital ought not to be maintained alongside those in the new capital. Moreover, the annual court sacrifices follow fixed ritual schedules; if the dates are identical, which temple would the spirits attend? We understand that recent precedents also allow for expedient measures. If the spirit tablets have already been prepared, they may be brought in and stored in the side chambers; if the temple buildings have already been erected on a grand scale, they may be left standing but unused as a permanent arrangement. Duke Huan of Qi's temple with two spirit-lords has no explicit textual authority in the classics; in antiquity, when armies marched, the temple tablets were not moved either. When Empress Wu honored both Gong and Luoyang with temples, ritualists judged it improper; and when Emperor Gaozu of Han cherished both Feng and Teng, there was no precedent to follow. Moreover, our own dynasty's precedents are fully documented by the Directorate of Rites. Luoyang is the ancient capital and Mount Song stands in its proper place—how could one build the ancestral temples far from the palace residence? Such an arrangement cannot endure; reason favors the lasting solution. We request that the ancestral temples of the Northern Capital be abolished as proposed in the memorial from the Directorate of Rites. " The proposal was approved.
4
天成二年，中書門下又上奏：「伏以兩漢以諸侯王入繼帝統，則必易名上謚，廣孝稱皇，載於諸王故事，孝德皇、孝仁皇、孝元皇是也。 伏乞聖慈，俯從人願，許取皇而薦號，兼上謚以尊名，改置圓陵，仍增兵衛。 」遂詔太常禮院定其儀制焉。 太常博士王丕等引漢桓帝入嗣，尊其祖河間孝王曰孝穆皇帝、父蠡吾侯曰孝崇皇帝為例，請付太常卿定謚。 刑部侍郎、權判太常卿馬縞復議曰：「伏準兩漢故事，以諸侯王宗室入承帝統，則必追尊父祖，修樹園陵，西漢宣帝、東漢光武，孝饗之道，故事具存。 自安帝入嗣，遂有皇太后令，別崇謚法，追曰某皇，所謂孝德、孝穆之類是也。 前代惟孫皓自烏程侯繼嗣，追父和為文皇帝，事出非常，不堪垂訓。 今據禮院狀，漢安帝以下，若據本紀，又不見「帝」字。 伏以謚法「德象天地曰帝」。 伏緣禮院已曾奏聞，難將兩漢故事，便述尊名，請詔百官集議。 」時右僕射李琪等議曰：「伏睹歷代已來，宗廟成制，繼襲無異，沿革或殊。 馬縞所奏，禮有按據，乞下制命，令馬縞虔依典冊，以述尊名。 」時明宗意欲兼加「帝」字，乃下詔曰：「朕聞開國承家，得以制禮作樂，故三皇不相襲，五帝不相沿，隨代創規，於禮無爽。 矧或情關祖禰，事系烝嘗。 且追謚追尊，稱皇與帝，既有減增之字，合陳褒貶之辭。 大約二名俱為尊稱，若三皇之代故不可加帝，五帝之代不可言皇。 爰自秦朝，便兼二號。 至若玄元皇帝，事隔千祀，宗追一源，猶顯冊於鴻名，豈須遵於漢典。 況朕居九五之位，為億兆之尊，不可總二名於眇躬，惜一字於先代，茍隨執議，何表孝誠！ 可委宰臣與百官詳定，集兩班於中書，逐班各陳所見。 」惟李琪等請於祖禰二室先加「帝」字。 宰臣合眾議奏曰：「恭以朝廷之重，宗廟為先，事系承祧，義符致美。 且聖朝追尊之日，即引漢氏舊儀，在漢氏封崇之時，復依何代故事？ 理關凝滯，未協聖謨； 道合變通，方為民則。 且王者功成治定，制禮作樂，正朔服色，尚有改更，尊祖奉先，何妨沿革。 若應州必立別廟，即地遠上都。 今據開元中追尊臯陶為德明皇帝，涼武昭王為興聖皇帝，皆立廟於京都。 臣等商量所議追尊四廟，望依御劄並加皇帝之號，兼請於洛京立廟。 」敕：「宜於應州舊宅立廟，餘依所奏。」 〈 （案《文獻通考》：後唐之所謂七廟者，以沙陀之獻祖國昌、太祖克用、莊宗存勖而上繼唐之高祖、太宗、懿宗、昭宗。 此所謂四廟者，又明宗代北之高、曾、祖、父也。）〉
In the second year of Tiancheng (927), the Secretariat again memorialized the throne: "We respectfully observe that in both Han dynasties, when a feudal prince succeeded to the imperial line, his forebears were invariably given new names and posthumous titles, extending filial devotion by styling them Huang ('Imperial'). This is recorded in the precedents on princely succession—Emperor Xiaode, Emperor Xiaoren, and Emperor Xiaoyuan are examples. We humbly beg Your Majesty, in condescension to the people's wishes, to permit the use of Huang in presenting titles, together with posthumous titles to honor their names, to relocate their tombs to imperial round mounds, and to increase the military guard. " An edict was then issued directing the Directorate of Rites to establish the ceremonial regulations. Academician Wang Pi of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices and others cited the precedent of Emperor Huan of Han's succession, in which his grandfather Prince Xiao of Hejian was honored as Emperor Xiaomu and his father Marquis Liwu as Emperor Xiaochong, and requested that the Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices determine the posthumous titles. Vice Minister of Justice and Acting Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices Ma Gao submitted a further opinion: "We respectfully follow the precedents of both Han dynasties: when a member of the imperial clan succeeded to the throne, he invariably posthumously honored his father and grandfather and constructed park tombs. The filial sacrifices of Emperor Xuan of Western Han and Emperor Guangwu of Eastern Han are fully documented in precedent. From Emperor An's succession onward, there was an empress dowager's order establishing a separate system of posthumous titles, posthumously styling forebears as a certain Huang—Emperor Xiaode, Emperor Xiaomu, and the like. In earlier ages only Sun Hao, succeeding from Marquis of Wucheng, posthumously honored his father He as Emperor Wen—an exceptional case unfit to serve as precedent. According to the memorial from the Directorate of Rites, from Emperor An of Han onward, if one consults the basic annals, the character Di ('emperor') is also absent. We respectfully note that according to the rules of posthumous titles, 'virtue resembling Heaven and Earth is called Di.' The Directorate of Rites has already reported on this matter; it is difficult to apply Han precedents directly in determining honorific titles. We request that Your Majesty summon the officials to collective deliberation. " At that time Right Vice Director Li Qi and others submitted their opinion: "We respectfully observe that through the ages, the established institutions of ancestral temples have been consistent in succession, though modifications in practice may vary. Ma Gao's memorial has solid grounding in ritual precedent; we request that a regulatory order be issued directing Ma Gao to follow the canonical records faithfully in determining the honorific titles. " At that time Emperor Mingzong wished to add the character Di as well and issued an edict: "We have heard that founding a state and continuing a family line permit one to institute rites and compose music. The Three August Ones did not inherit from one another, the Five Thearchs did not continue one another's practices—each age created its own regulations without violating ritual propriety. How much more when one's feelings are bound to one's ancestors and the matter concerns the seasonal sacrifices! Moreover, in posthumous titles and honors, the designations Huang and Di involve characters of praise and censure; the appropriate evaluative language should be employed. Broadly speaking, both titles are honorific designations; in the age of the Three August Ones one could not add Di, and in the age of the Five Thearchs one could not speak of Huang. From the Qin dynasty onward, both titles were used together. As for the August Emperor of the Mysterious Origin (Laozi), though a thousand years intervene and the ancestral line traces to a single source, he still received a resplendent honorific title—why must one follow Han precedent? Moreover, We occupy the throne, the honor of the hundred millions—we cannot combine both titles upon our own person yet withhold a single character for our forebears; if we merely follow rigid deliberation, how would we express our filial devotion! Let this be entrusted to the chief ministers and the hundred officials for detailed determination; assemble both cohorts of officials at the Secretariat, each to state its views in turn. " Only Li Qi and others requested that the character Di be added first to the two chambers of the remote ancestors. The chief ministers, combining the collective deliberation, submitted: "We respectfully consider that in the weight of the court, the ancestral temples come first; the matter concerns continuing the sacrificial line, and the principle accords with achieving the highest expression of filial devotion. When our sacred court posthumously honors its forebears, it cites Han precedent—but when the Han themselves ennobled and honored their ancestors, upon what earlier age's precedents did they rely? Rigid adherence to precedent does not accord with the sacred design; flexible adaptation in keeping with the Way alone becomes the people's standard. When a ruler's merit is accomplished and governance settled, he institutes rites and composes music—even the calendar, regalia, and colors may be changed; in honoring ancestors and serving forebears, what objection can there be to modification? If a separate temple must be established at Ying Prefecture, the location would be far from the capital. In the Kaiyuan era, when Gao Yao was posthumously honored as Emperor Deming and Prince Wuzhao of Liang as Emperor Xingsheng, temples were established for both in the capital. We your subjects, in deliberating the four temples proposed for posthumous honor, request that in accordance with the imperial rescript the title of emperor be added to all, and that temples be established in Luoyang. " Edict: "A temple shall be established at the old residence in Ying Prefecture; the remainder follows the memorial." Editorial bracket opens. (Note from the Wenxian Tongkao: The so-called seven temples of Later Tang comprised the Shatuo ancestors Xianzu Guochang, Taizu Keyong, and Zhuangzong Cunxu, continuing upward to Tang's Gaozu, Taizong, Yizong, and Zhaozong. The so-called four temples mentioned here were the great-great-grandfather, great-grandfather, grandfather, and father from the north, whom Emperor Mingzong honored.)〉

其年八月，太常禮院奏：「莊宗神主以此月十日祔廟，七室之內，合有祧遷。 」中書門下奏議，請祧懿祖一室。 後下百僚集議，禮部尚書蕭頃等奏，請從中書所奏，從之。
In the eighth month of that year, the Directorate of Rites reported: "Zhuangzong's spirit tablet is to be enshrined in the ancestral temple on the tenth day of this month; within the seven chambers, a distancing removal (tia qian) is required. " The Secretariat submitted its opinion, requesting the distancing removal of the Yizu chamber. An order was then issued for collective deliberation by the hundred officials. Minister of Rites Xiao Qing and others memorialized in favor of the Secretariat's proposal, and it was approved.
6
應順元年正月，中書門下奏：「太常以大行山陵畢祔廟。 今太廟見饗七室，高祖、太宗、懿宗、昭宗、獻祖、太祖、莊宗，大行升祔，禮合祧遷獻祖，請下尚書省集議。 」太子少傅盧質等議曰：「臣等以親盡從祧，垂於舊典，疑事無質，素有明文。 頃莊宗皇帝再造寰區。 復隆宗廟，追三祖於先遠，復四室於本朝，式遇祧遷，旋成沿革。 及莊宗升祔，以懿祖從祧，蓋非嗣立之君，所以先遷其室。 光武滅新之後，始有追尊之儀，比只在於南陽，元不歸於太廟，引事且疏於故實，此時須稟於新規。 將來升祔先廟，次合祧遷獻祖，既協隨時之義，又符變體之文。 」從之。 時議以懿祖賜姓於懿宗，以支庶系大宗例，宜以懿祖為始祖，次昭宗可也，不必祖神堯而宗太宗。 若依漢光武，則宜於代州立獻祖而下親廟，其唐廟依舊禮行之可也，而議謚者忘咸通之懿宗，又稱懿祖，父子俱「懿」，於理可乎！ 將朱耶三世與唐室四廟連敘昭穆，非禮之甚也。 議祧者不知受氏於唐懿宗而祧之，今又及獻祖。 以禮論之，始祧昭宗，次祧獻祖可也，而懿祖如唐景皇帝，豈可祧乎？
In the first month of the first year of Yingshun (934), the Secretariat memorialized: "The Court of Imperial Sacrifices reports that upon completion of the late emperor's tomb, his spirit tablet will be enshrined in the ancestral temple. The Grand Temple presently has seven chambers receiving sacrifice: Gaozu, Taizong, Yizong, Zhaozong, Xianzu, Taizu, and Zhuangzong. When the late emperor's tablet is enshrined, ritual requires the distancing removal of Xianzu. We request referral to the Department of State Affairs for collective deliberation. " Junior Tutor to the Heir Apparent Lu Zhi and others submitted their opinion: "We your subjects observe that when close kin is exhausted, distancing removal applies—this is established in the old canon, and doubtful matters have always been resolved by explicit textual authority. Recently Emperor Zhuangzong restored the realm. He restored and elevated the ancestral temples, posthumously honoring three remote ancestors and restoring four chambers for the present dynasty; when distancing removal arose, precedent was immediately modified. When Zhuangzong's tablet was enshrined, Yizu was distanced—presumably because he was not the ruler who had succeeded to the throne, and therefore his chamber was removed first. After Guangwu destroyed the Xin dynasty, posthumous honors were first instituted—but only in Nanyang and not in the Grand Temple. Citing this precedent is loose regarding established fact; at the present time, the new regulations must be followed. When the late emperor's tablet is next enshrined, Xianzu should be distanced next—this accords with adapting to the times and matches the texts on flexible modification. " The proposal was approved. Contemporary commentators held that since Yizu received his surname from Yizong, following the precedent of a collateral line attaching to the great lineage, Yizu should serve as the founding ancestor with Zhaozong next—there was no need to take Emperor Yao as ancestor and honor Taizong as lineage founder. If following Emperor Guangwu of Han, one should establish the close temples from Xianzu downward at Dai Prefecture and let the Tang temples follow the old rites. Yet those deliberating posthumous titles forgot Yizong of the Xiantong era and again styled Yizu—father and son both bearing Yi—is this reasonable! To link three generations of the Zhuoye (Shatuo) clan with four Tang ancestral temples in continuous zhaomu sequence is a grave violation of ritual propriety. Those deliberating distancing removal did not understand that Yizu, having received the surname from Tang's Yizong, was already distanced—and now they propose to distance Xianzu as well. According to ritual propriety, Zhaozong should be distanced first, and Xianzu next would be acceptable—but Yizu is like Tang's Emperor Jing; how could he be distanced?

夫宗廟之制，歷代為難，須考禮經，以求故事。 謹按《尚書·舜典》曰：「正月上日，受終於文祖。 」此是堯之廟也，猶未載其數。 又按《郊祀錄》曰：夏立五廟，商立六廟，周立七廟。 漢初立祖宗廟於郡國，共計一百六十七所。 後漢光武中興後，別立六廟。 魏明帝初立親廟四，後重議依周法立七廟。 晉武帝受禪，初立六廟，後復立七廟。 宋武帝初立六廟，齊朝亦立六廟。 隋文帝受命，初立親廟四，至大業元年，煬帝欲遵周法，議立七廟。 次屬傳禪於唐，武德元年六月四日，始立四廟於長安，至貞觀九年，命有司詳議廟制，遂立七廟，至開元十一年後，創立九廟。 又按《禮記·喪服小記》曰：「王者禘其祖之所自出，以其祖配之，而立四廟。 」鄭玄註云：高祖巳下至禰四世，即親盡也，更立始祖為不遷之廟，共五廟也。 又按《禮記·祭法》及《王制》、《孔子家語》、《春秋穀梁傳》並云：天子七廟，諸侯五廟，大夫三廟，士一廟。 此是降殺以兩之義。 又按《尚書·咸有一德》曰：「七世之廟，可以觀德。 」又按《疑義》云：天子立七廟，或四廟，蓋有其義也。 如四廟者，從禰至高祖已下親盡，故有四廟之理。 又立七廟者，緣自古聖王，祖有功，宗有德，更封立始祖，即於四親廟之外，或祖功宗德，不拘定數，所以有五廟、六廟，或七廟、九廟，欲後代子孫觀其功德，故《尚書》云「七世之廟，可以觀德」矣。 又按周舍論云：「自江左已來，晉、宋、齊、梁相承，多立七廟。 」今臣等參詳，唯立七廟，即並通其理。 伏緣宗廟事大，不敢執以一理定之，故檢七廟、四廟二件之文，俱得其宜，他所論者，並皆勿取。 請下三省集百官詳議。
The institution of ancestral temples has posed difficulties through the ages; one must examine the ritual classics to seek precedents. We respectfully note that the "Canon of Shun" in the Book of Documents states: "On the first day of the first month, he received the conclusion of the mandate at Wenzu (the Cultured Ancestor)." " This was Yao's temple, yet the number of temples is not recorded. We further note that the Record of Suburban Sacrifices states: Xia established five temples, Shang six, and Zhou seven. At the beginning of Han, ancestral temples were established in commanderies and kingdoms, totaling one hundred sixty-seven in all. After Emperor Guangwu of Later Han restored the dynasty, six temples were separately established. Emperor Ming of Wei at first established four close temples, then after renewed deliberation established seven temples following Zhou practice. When Emperor Wu of Jin received the abdication, he first established six temples, then later restored seven. Emperor Wu of Song first established six temples; the Qi dynasty also established six. When Emperor Wen of Sui received the mandate, he first established four close temples; by the first year of Daye (605), Emperor Yang wished to follow Zhou practice and deliberated establishing seven temples. The succession then passed to Tang. On the fourth day of the sixth month of the first year of Wude (618), four temples were first established in Chang'an. By the ninth year of Zhenguan (635), the responsible offices were ordered to deliberate on the temple system in detail, and seven temples were established. By the eleventh year of Kaiyuan (723) and after, nine temples were created. We further note that the "Record of Mourning Garments, Small Treatise" in the Book of Rites states: "The king performs the great di sacrifice to the one from whom his ancestor sprang, matching him with his ancestor, and establishes four temples." " Zheng Xuan's commentary explains: From Gaozu down to Mi is four generations—the exhaustion of close kin. The founding ancestor is then established as the non-distanced temple, making five temples in all. The "Record of Sacrifices," "Royal Regulations," Confucian Family Sayings, and Guliang Commentary to the Spring and Autumn Annals all state: the Son of Heaven seven temples, feudal lords five, grandees three, and officers one. This reflects the principle of descending rank by pairs. The "Common Possession of Pure Virtue" in the Book of Documents states: "The temple of seven generations can display virtue." " The Doubts and Explanations observes that the Son of Heaven may establish seven temples or four—each arrangement has its rationale. As for four temples: from Mi up to Gaozu, close kin is exhausted below—hence the rationale for four temples. As for seven temples: since ancient sage-kings honored ancestors for merit and lineage founders for virtue, additionally enfeoffing and establishing the founding ancestor beyond the four close temples—or honoring ancestral merit and lineage virtue without fixed numbers—there arose five, six, seven, or nine temples, so that later generations might observe their merit and virtue. Hence the Book of Documents says, "The temple of seven generations can display virtue." Zhou She's treatise observes: "From the Eastern Jin onward, Jin, Song, Qi, and Liang in succession mostly established seven temples." " We your subjects, upon comparative examination, hold that establishing seven temples alone comprehends both rationales. The matter of ancestral temples is weighty; we dare not adhere to a single rationale. We have therefore examined texts on both seven temples and four temples and find both appropriate; other arguments should be set aside. We request that the Three Departments convene the hundred officials for detailed deliberation.

I have read the histories of fourteen dynasties, reviewed two thousand years of precedent, and examined the ancestral temples of successive houses. Nowhere do I find the title "Founding Ancestor." Only in the Shang and Zhou dynasties were Ji and Qi honored as Great Ancestors. The Book of Rites states: "The Son of Heaven has seven temples: three zhao, three mu, and the Great Ancestor Temple, making seven in all. " Zheng Xuan annotates: "This is the Zhou system. The seven are the Great Ancestor Hou Ji, Kings Wen and Wu, and four temples for immediate ancestors. " It further states: "The Shang maintained six temples—for Qi and Cheng Tang, together with two zhao and two mu. The Xia established five temples, with no Great Ancestor Temple—only Yu, together with two zhao and two mu. " According to Zheng Xuan's commentary on the Royal Regulations, the Shang and Zhou honored Ji and Qi as Great Ancestors, whereas the Xia had neither a Great Ancestor nor a temple of posthumous ennoblement. From the Shang and Zhou onward, through ten generations of dynastic change, each house designated as Great Ancestor someone meritorious within the temples of immediate ancestors; there is no precedent for posthumously elevating a separate Founding Ancestor. A full citation of every ancient and modern case would make this memorial unwieldy; since the essential point must be established, I shall present only the main outlines. In Han, the great-grandfather, Supreme Emperor Zhijia, had rendered no service to the state and received no temple title; the High Emperor himself became the High Ancestor. In Wei, Duke Cao had assisted Han for nearly thirty years and was first enfeoffed as ruler of Wei; he was therefore made Great Ancestor. In Jin, the Illustrious King, who had served Wei with distinction, was established as High Ancestor; the Brilliant Emperor was first enfeoffed as ruler of Jin and therefore became Great Ancestor. The Liu clan of Song came from humble official origins; though earlier generations were posthumously granted imperial titles, Liu Yu himself became the High Ancestor. In Southern Qi, the High Emperor's father rose only to the rank of Right General; since he had received no enfeoffment in life, he could not be made Great Ancestor, and the High Emperor himself became Great Ancestor. Emperor Wu of Liang's father, Shunzhi, had supported the Qi house, was enfeoffed as marquis, and rose to Commander-in-Chief and Governor of Danyang; though he received no enfeoffment from Liang, he was nonetheless made Great Ancestor. Emperor Wu of Chen's father, Wenzan, held no rank or title in life; on account of the Emperor's achievements, the Liang house posthumously granted him the title of Palace Attendant and enfeoffed him as Duke of Yixing, and when the Emperor took the throne he too was posthumously made Great Ancestor. Emperor Min of Northern Zhou made his father Tai Great Ancestor, for Tai had served as chancellor of Western Wei, managed the founding of the Zhou house, and was first enfeoffed as its ruler. Emperor Wen of Sui's father, Zhong, rendered great service to the Zhou house and was first enfeoffed as ruler of Sui; he therefore became Great Ancestor. Tang's High Ancestor, the Divine and Sagely Emperor, made his grandfather Hu Great Ancestor; Hu had been one of the Eight Pillar Dukes of Zhou and was posthumously enfeoffed as Duke of Tang under the Sui. At the end of Tang, the Zhu house of Liang took the throne and likewise established four temples. The Zhu lord's forebears had held no rank or title; though four temples were posthumously established, no Great Ancestor was designated—the Zhu lord himself became Great Ancestor. Thus in previous dynasties, the posthumous establishment of a Great Ancestor never departed from the established practice within the temples of immediate ancestors.

A true king takes the meritorious as his ancestor and the virtuous as his object of veneration. Under Han and Wei, without merit and virtue one could not be established as an ancestral or venerable figure. When Shang and Zhou received the Mandate, they posthumously honored Ji and Qi as Great Ancestors because of their great service in the age of Tang and Yu. From Qin and Han onward, the rites were not applied in this way; even when honoring the meritorious as ancestor, the designation still had to remain within the temples of immediate ancestors. I shall now briefly review past precedents by way of proof. Qin claimed descent from Zaofu, yet did not make Zaofu its Founding Ancestor. Han claimed descent from Tang Yao and Liu Lei, yet did not make Yao or Lei its Founding Ancestors. Wei claimed descent from Cao Can, yet did not make Can its Founding Ancestor. Jin claimed descent from the Zhao general Sima Ang, yet did not make Ang its Founding Ancestor. Song claimed descent from the Prince Yuan of Chu of Han, yet did not make the Prince Yuan its Founding Ancestor. Both Qi and Liang claimed descent from Xiao He, yet neither made Xiao He its Founding Ancestor. Chen claimed descent from Chen Shi, Prefect of Taqiu, yet did not make Shi its Founding Ancestor. Northern Wei claimed descent from Li Ling, yet did not make Ling its Founding Ancestor. Later Zhou claimed descent from Shennong, yet did not make Shennong its Founding Ancestor. Sui claimed descent from Yang Zhen, yet did not make Yang Zhen its Founding Ancestor. Tang claimed descent from Gao Yao and Laozi, yet did not make Gao Yao or Laozi its Founding Ancestors. Only when Empress Wu, consort of Emperor Gaozong, held court, changed the dynastic name from Tang to Zhou, and re-established seven temples did she posthumously ennoble King Wen of Zhou, Ji Chang, as Founding Ancestor. This was the work of sycophants who did not understand ancient precedent; to rashly establish a temple to Ji was a gross violation, and scholars of the Quetai still ridicule it to this day. Looking from Qin and Han down through Northern Zhou and Sui, I find that in ritual, music, dress, renown, and cultural institutions, none equaled the splendor of the Tang. At the outset of the Wude-era deliberation on ancestral temples, men of talent abounded: Wen, Wei, Yan, and Yu were steeped in ancient and modern learning; Feng, Xiao, Xue, and Du were masters of ritual propriety. In institutions, laws, and regulations, there must have been authoritative models to follow.

The rites for posthumously honoring former kings and former queens originated in the Zhou dynasty. The Records of the Grand Historian and the ritual classics state: "King Wu continued the line of Grand King, King Ji, and King Wen; clad in armor, he won the realm, was honored as Son of Heaven, and received sacrifice in the ancestral temples. The Duke of Zhou completed the virtue of Kings Wen and Wu, posthumously enthroned Grand King and King Ji, and sacrificed to the former lords with the rites due to the Son of Heaven. " It further states: "In the suburban sacrifice, Hou Ji is offered to match Heaven. " On this basis, although King Wu of Zhou sacrificed to seven generations, only four generations received posthumous royal titles. Therefore, from the Eastern Han onward, newly founded dynasties commonly honored four temples, following the Zhou model. Moreover, Shang adopted the rites of Xia, and Han followed the institutions of Qin; there is no need for exhaustive textual research—we should adhere to institutions already established. I request that, following the precedent of Sui and Tang at the founding of their states, four temples be established, and that the forebear of highest title and rank among those four generations be designated Great Ancestor. Respectfully submitted for Your Majesty's consideration.

21
周廣順元年正月，中書門下奏：「太常禮院議，合立太廟室數。 若守文繼體，則魏、晉有七廟之文； 若創業開基，則隋、唐有四廟之議。 聖朝請依近禮，追謚四廟。 伏恐所議未同，請下百官集議。 」太子太傅和凝等議：「請據禮官議，立四親廟。 」從之。 〈 （《五代會要》：和凝議曰：「恭以肇啟洪圖，惟新黃屋。 左宗廟而右社稷，率由舊章； 崇祖禰而辨尊卑，載於前史。 雖質文互變，義趣各殊，或觀損益之規，或系興隆之始。 陛下體元立極，本義祖仁，開變家成國之基，遵奉先思孝之道，合據禮官議，立四親廟，以葉前文。 」從之。）〉 其年四月，中書門下奏：「太常禮院申，七月一日，皇帝御崇元殿，命使奏冊四廟。 準舊儀，服兗冕即座，太尉引冊案入，皇帝降座，引立於御座前南向，中書令奉冊案進，皇帝搢珪捧授，冊使跪受，轉授舁冊官，其進寶授寶儀如冊案。 臣等參詳，至時請皇帝降階授冊。 」從之。
In the first month of the first year of Guangshun (951), the Secretariat memorialized: "The Directorate of Rites has deliberated on the number of chambers to be established in the Grand Temple. If one inherits and continues an existing order, Wei and Jin provide precedent for seven temples; if one founds a state and lays its foundations, Sui and Tang provide precedent for four temples. The sacred court requests following recent practice and posthumously ennobling four temples. We fear the deliberations may not be unanimous and request that all officials be convened for collective deliberation. " Junior Tutor to the Heir Apparent He Ning and others submitted: "We request that in accordance with the ritual officers' deliberation, four temples of immediate ancestors be established. " The proposal was approved. Editorial bracket opens. (Institutional Essentials of the Five Dynasties: He Ning submitted: "We respectfully consider that in inaugurating the great enterprise and renewing the imperial throne, the ancestral temples are placed to the left and the altars of soil and grain to the right—all following established regulations; honoring ancestors and grandfathers while distinguishing rank and precedence—is recorded in former histories. Although substance and ornament change over time and meanings differ, sometimes one observes rules of increase and decrease, sometimes one is bound to the origins of a dynasty's rise. Your Majesty embodies the cosmic origin and establishes the supreme pole, taking righteousness and ancestral benevolence as foundation, opening the basis of transforming the family into a state and following the path of filial devotion to forebears. We should follow the ritual officers' deliberation and establish four temples of immediate ancestors to accord with earlier precedent. " The proposal was approved.)〉 In the fourth month of that year, the Secretariat memorialized: "The Directorate of Rites reports that on the first day of the seventh month, the emperor will attend the Chongyuan Hall and order envoys to present the registers to the four temples. According to the old rites, wearing the nine-tasseled cap and taking the seat, the Grand Marshal leads the register case in; the emperor descends and stands facing south before the imperial seat; the Secretariat Director presents the register case; the emperor inserts the scepter and holds it forth; the register envoy kneels to receive it and transfers it to the bearer of the register; the presentation of the seal follows the same ritual as the register case. We your subjects request that at the appointed time the emperor descend the steps to confer the register in person. " The proposal was approved.
22
三年九月，將有事於南郊，議於東京別建太廟。 時太常禮院言：「準洛京廟室一十五間，分為四室，東西各有夾室，四神門，每方屋一間，各三門，戟二十四，別有齋宮神廚屋宇。 準禮，左宗廟，右社稷，在國城內，請下所司修奉。 」從之。 其月，太常禮院奏：「迎太廟社稷神主到京，其日未審皇帝親出郊外迎奉否。 檢討故事，元無禮例，伏請召三省官集議。 」敕：「宜令尚書省四品以上、中書門下五品已上同參議。 」司徒竇貞固、司空蘇禹珪等議：「按吳主孫休即位，迎祖父神主於吳郡，人祔太廟，前一日出城野次，明日常服奉迎，此其例也。 」遂署狀言車駕出城奉迎為是，請下禮儀使草定儀註。 至十月，禮儀使奏：「太祖神主將至，前一日儀仗出城掌次，於西禦莊東北設神主行廟幄幕，面南。 其日放朝，群臣早出西門，皇帝常服出城詣行宮，群臣起居畢，就次。 神主將至，群臣班定，皇帝立於班前。 神主至，太常卿請皇帝再拜，群臣俱拜。 神主就行廟幄幕座，設常饌，群臣班於神幄前。 侍中就次，請皇帝謁神主。 既至，群臣再拜，皇帝進酒畢再拜，群臣俱拜。 皇帝還幄，群臣先赴太廟門外立班，俟皇帝至起居。 俟神主至，群臣班於廟門外，皇帝立於班前，太常卿請皇帝再拜，群臣俱拜。 皇帝還幄，群臣就次，宮闈令安神主於本室訖，群臣班於廟庭。 太常卿請皇帝於四室奠饗，逐室皇帝再拜，群臣俱拜。 四室祔饗畢，皇帝還宮。 前件儀註，望付中書門下宣下。 」從之。
In the ninth month of the third year, when a suburban sacrifice at the southern altar was planned, deliberation turned to constructing a separate Grand Temple in the Eastern Capital. At that time the Directorate of Rites reported: "The Luoyang temple has fifteen bays divided into four chambers, with side chambers to east and west, four spirit gates, one bay per direction with three gates each, twenty-four halberds, and separately a fasting palace, spirit kitchen, and other buildings. According to the rites, the ancestral temples are to the left and the altars of soil and grain to the right, within the capital walls. We request the responsible offices to construct and maintain them. " The proposal was approved. That month the Directorate of Rites memorialized: "When the spirit tablets of the Grand Temple and the altars of soil and grain are welcomed to the capital, it is not yet determined whether the emperor will personally go beyond the suburbs to welcome them. Examining precedents, there was originally no ritual example. We respectfully request summoning the officials of the Three Departments for collective deliberation. Edict: "Officials of the Department of State Affairs from the fourth rank and above, together with the Secretariat and Chancellery from the fifth rank and above, shall jointly deliberate." " Minister of Education Dou Zheng'gu, Minister of Works Su Yugui, and others submitted: "According to the precedent when Sun Xiu of Wu took the throne, he welcomed his grandfather's spirit tablet from Wu commandery for enshrinement in the Grand Temple; the day before he went beyond the city to camp in the field, and the next day in regular dress welcomed it—this is the applicable example. They then signed a report stating that the imperial carriage going beyond the city to welcome the tablets was correct, and requested the Commissioner of Ritual to draft the ceremonial regulations. By the tenth month the Commissioner of Ritual memorialized: "When the Taizu spirit tablet is about to arrive, on the preceding day the guard of honor goes beyond the city to set up the encampment; northeast of the Western Imperial Estate a traveling temple canopy for the spirit tablet is erected, facing south. On that day court is dismissed; the hundred officials go out early through the western gate; the emperor in regular dress goes beyond the city to the traveling palace; when the hundred officials have finished their salutations, they take their places. When the spirit tablet is about to arrive, the hundred officials form their ranks; the emperor stands before them. When the spirit tablet arrives, the Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices requests the emperor to bow twice; all the hundred officials bow. The spirit tablet is placed on the seat in the traveling temple canopy; regular offerings are set out; the hundred officials rank before the spirit canopy. The Palace Attendant takes his place and requests the emperor to approach the spirit tablet. When he arrives, the hundred officials bow again; when the emperor has finished presenting wine he bows again; all the hundred officials bow. The emperor returns to the canopy; the hundred officials first go to stand in ranks outside the Grand Temple gate, awaiting the emperor's arrival for salutation. When the spirit tablet arrives, the hundred officials rank outside the temple gate; the emperor stands before them; the Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices requests the emperor to bow twice; all bow. The emperor returns to the canopy; the hundred officials take their places; the Director of Palace Affairs settles the spirit tablet in its chamber; the hundred officials rank in the temple courtyard. The Director of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices requests the emperor to offer at the four chambers; at each chamber the emperor bows twice and all the hundred officials bow. When the offerings at the four chambers are completed, the emperor returns to the palace. We request that the foregoing regulations be promulgated by the Secretariat and Chancellery. " The proposal was approved.
23
顯德六年七月，詔以大行皇帝山陵有期，神主將祔太廟，其廟殿室宇合添修否？ 國子司業兼太常博士聶崇義奏議曰：「奉敕，為大行皇帝山陵有期，神主祔廟，恐殿室間數少，合重添修。 今詣廟中相度，若是添修廟殿一間至兩間，並須移動諸神門及角樓宮墻仗舍，及堂殿正面檐栿階道，亦須東省牲立班位，直至齋宮，漸近迫窄。 今重拆廟殿，續更添修，不唯重勞，兼恐未便。 竊見廟殿見虛東西二夾室，況未有祧遷之主，欲請不拆廟殿，更添間數，即便將夾室重安排六室位次。 所有動移神主，若準舊禮，於殿庭權設行廟幕殿，即恐雨水猶多，難於陳設。 伏請權於太廟齋宮內奉安神主，至修奉畢日，庶為宜稱。 又，按《禮記》云：廟成則於中屋刲羊以釁之，夾室則用雞。 又，《大戴禮》及《通典》亦有夾室，察文觀義，乃是備廟之制。 況新主祔廟，諸經有遷易之文，考古沿今，庶合通禮。 伏請遞遷諸室奉安大行皇帝神主，以符禮意。 」敕依典禮。
In the seventh month of the sixth year of Xiande (959), an edict was issued because the late emperor's tomb had a fixed date and his spirit tablet would be enshrined in the Grand Temple, inquiring whether the temple halls and chambers should be expanded or repaired. Associate Director of the Directorate of Education and Academician of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices Nie Chongyi submitted his opinion: "Receiving the edict, because the late emperor's tomb has a fixed date and his spirit tablet will be enshrined, we fear the number of chambers may be insufficient and that additions and repairs may be needed. On inspecting the temple, if one or two chambers of the temple hall are added or repaired, all the spirit gates, corner towers, palace walls, guard houses, and the main hall's front eaves, rafters, and steps would have to be moved, and the eastern side for slaughtering victims and forming ranks, extending to the fasting palace, would gradually become cramped. To demolish and rebuild the temple hall would not only impose heavy labor but may also prove impractical. We observe that the temple hall presently has vacant eastern and western side chambers, and since there is as yet no spirit-lord requiring distancing removal, we request not to demolish the temple hall but to add chambers, immediately rearranging positions for six chambers in the side chambers. As for moving the spirit tablets, if according to the old rites a traveling temple canopy is provisionally set up in the temple courtyard, we fear that with rain still frequent it would be difficult to arrange. We respectfully request provisionally settling the spirit tablets in the Grand Temple's fasting palace until repairs are completed—this would be most appropriate. Moreover, according to the Book of Rites: when the temple is completed, a sheep is slaughtered in the central chamber for the smearing rite; for side chambers a chicken is used. The Elder Dai's Rites and the Tongdian also describe side chambers; examining the texts and observing their meaning, these are institutions for completing the temple complex. Moreover, when a new spirit-lord is enshrined, the classics provide authority for relocation; examining antiquity and following present practice, this should accord with comprehensive ritual propriety. We respectfully request sequentially moving the chambers to settle the late emperor's spirit tablet, in accordance with ritual propriety. Edict: Follow the canonical rites.
