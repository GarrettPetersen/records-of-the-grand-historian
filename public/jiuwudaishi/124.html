王殷，瀛州人。 曾祖昌裔，本州別駕。 祖光，滄州教練使，因家焉。 唐末，幽、滄大亂，殷父鹹珪避地南遷，因投於魏軍。 殷自言生於魏州之開元寺，既長從軍，漸為偏將。 唐同光末，為華州馬步軍副指揮使，因家於華下。 天成中，移授靈武都指揮使，久之代還。 清泰中，張令昭據鄴叛，殷從範延光討之，首冒矢石，率先登城，以功授祁州刺史，尋改原州。 殷性謙謹好禮，事母以孝聞，每與人結交，過從皆先稟於母，母命不從，殷必不往，雖在軍旅，交遊不雜。 及為刺史，政事小有不佳，母察之，立殷於庭，詰責而杖之。 〈 （《歐陽史》：殷為刺史，政事有小失，母責之，殷即取杖授婢仆，自笞於母前。）〉 晉天福中，丁內艱，尋有詔起復，授憲州刺史，殷上章辭曰：「臣為末將，出處無損益於國家。 臣本燕人，值鄉國離亂，少罹偏罰，因母鞠養訓導，方得成人，不忍遽釋苴麻，遠離廬墓，伏願許臣終母喪紀。 」晉高祖嘉而許之。 晉少帝嗣位，會殷服闋，召典禁軍，累遷奉國右廂都指揮使。 漢祖受命，從討杜重威於鄴下，殷與劉詞皆率先力戰，矢中於首，久之，出折鏃於口中，以是漢祖嘉之。 乾祐末，遷侍衛步軍都指揮使，領夔州節度使，會契丹寇邊，遣殷領兵屯澶州。 及李業等作亂，漢隱帝密詔澶帥李洪義遣圖殷，洪義懼不克，反以變告殷，殷與洪義同遣人至鄴，請太祖赴內難。 殷從平京師，授侍衛親軍都指揮使。
Wang Yin was a native of Ying Prefecture. His great-grandfather Changyi had served as vice-prefect of the prefecture. His grandfather Guang was training commissioner of Cang Prefecture, and the family settled there. When Tang was collapsing, You and Cang fell into chaos; Yin's father Xian'gui fled south and enlisted with the Wei army. Yin said he had been born at Kaiyuan Temple in Weizhou; when he came of age he entered military service and gradually rose to subordinate general. Late in the Tang Tongguang era he became deputy commander of Huazhou's combined horse and foot forces and established his household in the Hua region. In the Tiancheng period he was reassigned as overall commander at Lingwu; after a long tour he was relieved and recalled. In the Qingtai era Zhang Lingzhao seized Ye and rebelled; Yin followed Fan Yanguang against him, took the lead under fire, and was first over the wall. For this he was made prefect of Qi Prefecture, then soon transferred to Yuan Prefecture. Yin was modest, careful, and devoted to propriety, and his filial devotion to his mother was well known. Whenever he made friends he asked his mother's leave before visiting them; if she forbade it he would not go. Even in camp his company was selective. Once he became prefect, the least slip in his governance would be noticed by his mother. She would make him stand in the courtyard, rebuke him, and beat him with a staff. (From the History of Ouyang: (The History of Ouyang Xiu records: When Yin was prefect, any small lapse brought his mother's rebuke; he would hand the rod to a servant and flog himself in her presence.)〉 During Jin's Tianfu reign he entered mourning for his mother. Soon an edict recalled him to service and appointed him prefect of Xian, but Yin memorialized to decline: "Your servant is only a junior officer whose presence or absence neither helps nor harms the state. I am a man of Yan. When my homeland was torn apart I barely escaped unjust punishment; only my mother's care and teaching made me a man. I cannot bear to cast off my mourning garb too soon and leave my mother's grave. I beg leave to complete the full mourning period." Emperor Gaozu of Jin praised him and granted his request. When Emperor Shaodi of Jin succeeded, Yin's mourning had ended and he was summoned to command the palace guard, rising eventually to commander of the Fengguo Army's right wing. When the Han founder took power, Yin joined the campaign against Du Chongwei below Ye. He and Liu Ci both fought at the front; an arrow struck Yin's head, and only after a long while did he spit out the broken tip. The Han founder commended him for this. Late in Qianyou he was made commander of the palace guard infantry and concurrently military governor of Kuizhou. When the Khitan raided the frontier he was sent to hold Cangzhou with troops. When Li Ye and others rose in revolt, the Hidden Emperor secretly ordered Cangzhou commander Li Hongyi to plot against Yin. Hongyi feared failure and instead warned Yin. Together they sent envoys to Ye asking Taizu to come and quell the court crisis. Yin followed in pacifying the capital and was appointed commander of the palace guard's imperial army.

太祖即位，授天雄軍節度使，加同平章事，典軍如故。 殷赴鎮，以侍衛司局從，凡河北征鎮有戍兵處，鹹稟殷節制。 又於民間多方聚斂，太祖聞而惡之，因使宣諭曰：「朕離鄴時，帑廩所儲不少，卿與國家同體，隨要取給，何患無財。 」二年夏，太祖征兗還，殷迎謁於路，宴賜而去。 及王峻得罪，太祖遣其子飛龍使承誨往鄴，令口諭峻之過惡，以慰其心。 三年秋，以永壽節上表請覲，太祖雖允其請，且慮殷之不誠，尋遣使止之。 何福進在鎮州，素惡殷之太橫，福進入朝，摭其陰事以奏之，太祖遂疑之。 是年冬，以郊禋有日，殷自鎮入覲，太祖令依舊內外巡警。 殷出入部從不下數百人，又以儀形魁偉，觀者無不聳然。 一日，遽入奏曰：「郊禮在近，兵民大集，臣城外防警，請量給甲仗，以備非常。 」太祖難之。 時中外以太祖嬰疾，步履稍難，多不視朝，俯逼郊禋，殷有震主之勢，頗憂之。 太祖乃力疾坐於滋德殿，殷入起居，即命執之，尋降制流竄，及出都城，遽殺之，眾情乃安。 是歲春末，鄴城寺鐘懸絕而落，又火光出幡竿之上。 殷之入覲也，都人餞於離亭，上馬失鐙，翻墮於地，人訝其不祥，果及於禍。 太祖尋令澶帥鄭仁誨赴鄴，殷次子為衙內指揮使，不出候謁，仁誨誅之，遷其家屬於登州。
When Taizu took the throne, Yin was made military governor of the Tianxiong army, given the added title of Co-Governor, and kept command of the guard as before. Yin went to his post with palace-guard staff in attendance, and every Hebei command that held garrison troops fell under his authority. He also squeezed the people on many fronts. Taizu heard of it with disgust and sent word: "When I left Ye the treasuries were well stocked. You and the state are one body—take what you need whenever you need it. Why worry about money?". That summer in the second year Taizu returned from the Yan campaign. Yin met him on the road, was feasted and rewarded, and then went home. When Wang Jun fell from favor, Taizu sent his son Commissioner Chenghui of Feilong to Ye to tell Yin of Jun's crimes by word of mouth and reassure him. In autumn of the third year he memorialized for an audience on the Yongshou festival. Taizu granted the request but doubted his sincerity and soon sent an envoy to turn him back. He Fujin at Zhenzhou had long resented Yin's arrogance. When Fujin came to court he collected secret reports against Yin, and Taizu grew suspicious. That winter, with the suburban sacrifice approaching, Yin came from his post for audience. Taizu restored him to joint patrol duty inside and outside the capital. Whenever he went abroad his escort numbered at least several hundred men, and his towering frame made every onlooker stand in awe. One day he rushed in and memorialized: "The suburban rites are near and troops and civilians are massing. I am responsible for security outside the walls. I ask for a measured issue of arms and armor against emergencies." Taizu was reluctant to grant it. Inside and outside the court people knew Taizu was chronically ill, walked with difficulty, and often skipped audience. With the sacrifice imminent, Yin's bearing seemed to overshadow the throne, and many were alarmed. Taizu forced himself up and sat in Zide Hall. When Yin came to pay his respects he was seized at once. An edict soon banished him, and once he was beyond the capital he was killed without delay. Public anxiety then subsided. Late that spring a temple bell in Ye had its rope snap and fell, and fire was seen atop a banner pole. When Yin came to court the people of the capital saw him off at the farewell pavilion. He lost a stirrup mounting his horse and fell to the ground. Onlookers took it for an ill omen—and disaster did follow. Taizu soon sent Cangzhou commander Zheng Renhui to Ye. Yin's second son, intramural guard commander, failed to come out to receive him; Renhui executed him and moved the family to Deng Prefecture.

何福進，字善長，太原人。 父神劍，累贈左驍衛大將軍。 福進少從軍，以驍勇聞。 唐同光末，郭從謙以兵圍莊宗於大內，福進時為宿衛軍校，獨出死力拒戰於內，後明宗知而嘉之，擢為捧聖軍校，出為磁州刺史，充北面行營先鋒都校。 清泰中，自彰聖都虞候率本軍從範延光平鄴，以功歷鄭、隴二州防禦使。 開運中，由潁州團練使入拜左驍衛大將軍。 屬契丹陷中原，令中朝文武臣僚凡數十人隨帳北歸，時福進預其行。 行次鎮州，聞北主已斃，其黨尚據鎮陽，遂與李筠、白再榮之儔合謀力戰，盡逐契丹，據有鎮陽。 時漢祖已建號於河東，詔以福進為北面行營馬步都虞候，尋拜曹州防禦使、檢校太保。 太祖出鎮於鄴，將謀北伐，奏以福進自隨。 及太祖入平內難，以輔佐功拜忠武軍節度使，不數月，移領鎮州。 數年之間，北鄙無事。 及聞太祖將有事於南郊，拜章入覲，改天平軍節度使，加同平章事。 未及之任，卒於東京之私第，年六十有六，時顯德元年正月也。 累贈中書令。
He Fujin, styled Shanchang, was a native of Taiyuan. His father Shenjian was repeatedly posthumously promoted to Grand General of the Left Xiaowei Guard. Fujin entered the army in youth and was known for fierce courage. Late in Tongguang, Guo Congqian besieged Emperor Zhuangzong in the inner palace. Fujin was then a palace guard officer and alone fought to the death inside. Emperor Mingzong learned of it and praised him, promoting him to the Pingsheng guard, then sending him out as prefect of Ci and vanguard commander of the northern campaign. In Qingtai, as adjutant of the Zhangsheng army he led his troops with Fan Yanguang to pacify Ye and, for his merit, served in turn as defense commissioner of Zheng and Long prefectures. In Kaiyun he left his post as regimental commissioner of Yingzhou and entered court as Grand General of the Left Xiaowei Guard. When the Khitan overran the Central Plains they took several dozen central-court officials north with the royal camp, and Fujin was among them. At Zhenzhou they heard the Khitan ruler was dead but his followers still held the city. Fujin joined Li Yun, Bai Zairong, and others in a fierce fight that drove the Khitan out and secured Zhenyang. The Han founder had already proclaimed his reign in Hedong and appointed Fujin adjutant of horse and foot for the northern campaign, then soon made him defense commissioner of Cao Prefecture and Honorary Grand Guardian. When Taizu took command at Ye and prepared a northern campaign, he asked that Fujin accompany him. When Taizu quelled the court crisis, Fujin was made military governor of the Zhongwu army for his support; within months he was transferred to Zhenzhou. For several years the northern frontier was quiet. Learning that Taizu would perform the southern suburban sacrifice, he memorialized for audience, was transferred to military governor of the Tianping army, and given the added title of Co-Governor. Before he could take up the new post he died at his home in the Eastern Capital, aged sixty-six, in the first month of Xiande 1. He was posthumously promoted to Director of the Secretariat.

His son Jijun served the dynasty and died while military governor of the Jianwu army.

劉詞，字好謙，元城人。 梁貞明中，事故鄴帥楊師厚，以勇悍聞。 唐莊宗入魏，亦列於麾下，兩河之戰，無不預焉。 同光初，為效節軍使，轉劍直指揮使，尋以忤於權臣，出為汝州小校，凡留滯十餘年。 清泰初，詔諸道選驍果以實禁衛，由是得入典禁軍。 晉初，從侯益收汜水關，佐楊光遠平鄴都，累遷奉國第一軍都虞候。 後從馬全節伐安陸，敗淮賊萬餘眾，晉祖嘉之，授奉國都校，累加檢校司空。 又從杜重威敗安重榮於宗城。 及圍鎮陽，詞自登雲梯，身先士伍，以功加檢校司徒、沁州刺史。 時王師方討襄陽，尋命詞兼行營都虞候，襄陽平，遷本州團練使。 在郡歲餘，臨事之暇，必被甲枕戈而臥，人或問之，詞曰：「我以勇敢而登貴仕，不可一日而忘本也。 若信其溫飽，則筋力有怠，將來何以報國也！ 」及漢有天下，復為奉國右廂都校，遙領閬州防禦使。 從太祖平鄴，加檢校太保。 乾祐初，李守貞叛於河中，太祖征之，朝廷以為侍衛步軍都指揮使，遙領寧江軍節度使，充行營馬步都虞候，命分屯於河西。 二年正月，守貞遣敢死之士數千，夜入其營。 皆怖懼不知所為，惟詞神氣自若，令於軍中曰：「此小盜耳，不足驚也。 」遂免胄橫戈，叱短兵以擊之，賊眾大敗而退。 自是守貞喪膽，不復有奔突之意。 河中平，太祖嘉之，表其功為華州節度使，歲餘，移鎮邢臺。 太祖受命，加同平章事。 三年秋，改鎮河陽。 顯德初，世宗親征劉崇，詞奉命領所部兵隨駕，行及高平南，遇樊愛能等自北退回，且言官軍已敗，止詞不行，詞不聽，疾驅而北。 世宗聞而嘉之，尋命為隨駕都部署，又授河東道行營副部署。 其年夏，車駕還京，授永興軍節度使，加兼侍中，行京兆尹。 二年冬，以疾卒於鎮，年六十有五。 贈中書令，謚曰忠惠。 詞發身軍校，亟歷戎事，常以忠勇自負。 洎領藩鎮，能靖恭為治，無苛政以撓民，謚以忠惠，議者韙之。
Liu Ci, styled Haoqian, was a native of Yuancheng. In Liang's Zhenming era he had served Ye commander Yang Shihou and was known for fierce fighting. When Emperor Zhuangzong of Tang entered Wei, Ci joined his ranks and took part in every major battle between the Yellow and the Bian. Early in Tongguang he was commander of the Xiaojie army, then of the Jianzhi guard. Soon he offended a powerful minister and was demoted to a petty officer at Ru Prefecture, where he languished for more than ten years. Early in Qingtai an edict ordered each circuit to send its best fighters to fill the palace guard, and through this Ci re-entered guard service. Early in Jin he helped Hou Yi recover Sishui Pass and assisted Yang Guangyuan in pacifying Ye, rising to adjutant of the First Fengguo army. He later followed Ma Quanjie against Anlu and routed more than ten thousand Huai rebels. Emperor Zu of Jin praised him, made him colonel of the Fengguo army, and repeatedly promoted him to Honorary Minister of Works. He also followed Du Chongwei in defeating An Chongrong at Zongcheng. At the siege of Zhenyang he climbed the assault ladder himself and led the men from the front. For this he was made Honorary Minister of Education and prefect of Qin Prefecture. While the court army was campaigning against Xiangyang he was made campaign adjutant. When Xiangyang fell he was made regimental commissioner of that prefecture. For more than a year in the prefecture, whenever he had a spare moment he slept in armor with his weapon for a pillow. When asked why, Ci said: "I won high office through courage. I must not forget that for a single day. If I grow soft in comfort my strength will fail—how then could I repay the state!" When Han ruled the realm he again became colonel of the Fengguo army's right wing and held the defense commissionership of Lang Prefecture in absentia. He followed Taizu in pacifying Ye and was made Honorary Grand Guardian. Early in Qianyou Li Shouzhen rebelled in Hezhong. Taizu marched against him and the court made Ci commander of the palace guard infantry, military governor of the Ningjiang army in absentia, and campaign adjutant of horse and foot, with orders to encamp west of the river. In the first month of the second year Shouzhen sent several thousand suicide troops to raid his camp by night. The men panicked, but Ci remained calm and told the army: "These are only petty raiders—nothing to fear. " He doffed his helmet, seized a spear, and led the close fighters in a charge. The enemy broke and fled. After that Shouzhen lost heart and made no more daring sorties. When Hezhong was pacified Taizu praised him and recommended him as military governor of Huazhou. After a year he was transferred to Xingtai. When Taizu took the throne Ci was given the added title of Co-Governor. In autumn of the third year he was transferred to Heyang. Early in Xiande Emperor Shizong personally marched against Liu Chong. Ci led his troops with the imperial train. South of Gaoping he met Fan Aineng and others retreating from the north, claiming the army had been beaten. They tried to hold Ci back, but he would not listen and galloped north at once. Shizong heard of it and praised him, soon making him overall commander with the imperial train and deputy commander of the Hedong campaign. That summer, when the emperor returned to the capital, Ci was made military governor of the Yongxing army, given the concurrent title of Palace Attendant, and commissioned as acting metropolitan magistrate of Jingzhao. In winter of the second year he died of illness at his post, aged sixty-five. He was posthumously made Director of the Secretariat with the posthumous name Loyal and Kind. Ci had risen from a guard officer through many campaigns and always prided himself on loyalty and courage. When he governed a command he ruled with quiet dignity and imposed no harsh policies on the people. Commentators agreed that the posthumous name Loyal and Kind was apt.

史彥超，雲州人也。 性驍獷，有膽氣，累功至龍捷都指揮使。 太祖之赴內難，彥超以本軍從。 國初，與虎捷都指揮使何徵戍晉州，會劉崇與契丹入寇，攻圍州城月餘，是時本州無帥，知州王萬敢不協物情，彥超與何徵協力固拒，累挫敵鋒。 攻擊日急，禦捍有備，軍政甚嚴，居人無擾。 及朝廷遣樞密使王峻總兵為援，敵兵宵遁。 太祖嘉其善守之功，賞賜甚厚。 未幾，授龍捷右廂都指揮使，尋授鄭州防禦使。 劉崇之寇潞州也，車駕親征，以彥超為先鋒都指揮使。 高平之戰，先登陷陣，以功授華州節度使，先鋒如故。 大軍至河東城下，契丹營於忻、代之間，遙應賊勢，詔天雄軍節度使符彥卿率諸將屯忻州以拒之。 彥卿襲契丹於忻口。 彥超以先鋒軍追蕃寇，離大軍稍遠，賊兵伏發，為賊所陷。 世宗痛惜久之，詔贈太師，示加等也，仍命優恤其家焉。
Shi Yanchao was a native of Yun Prefecture. Fierce and unruly by nature, he was bold in spirit and rose through merit to commander of the Longjie army. When Taizu marched to quell the court crisis, Yanchao followed with his own troops. Early in the dynasty he and Huzhe commander He Zheng garrisoned Jinzhou. When Liu Chong and the Khitan invaded they besieged the city for more than a month. The prefecture had no military governor, and prefect Wang Wang'an was unpopular. Yanchao and He Zheng held the city together and repeatedly repulsed the enemy. As the assaults intensified their defenses held firm, military discipline was strict, and the townspeople were left unharmed. When the court sent Grand Councilor Wang Jun with relief troops, the enemy fled overnight. Taizu praised their successful defense and rewarded them generously. Soon he was made commander of the Longjie army's right wing, then defense commissioner of Zheng Prefecture. When Liu Chong attacked Luzhou the emperor marched in person and made Yanchao vanguard commander. At Gaoping he was first over the wall and into the enemy line. For this he was made military governor of Huazhou while keeping his vanguard command. When the main army reached Hedong the Khitan camped between Xin and Dai in support of the enemy. Fu Yanqing, military governor of the Tianxiong army, was ordered to hold Xinzhou with the other generals and block them. Yanqing attacked the Khitan at Xinkou. Yanchao pursued the enemy with the vanguard, straying somewhat from the main force. Ambushers struck and he was cut off. Shizong mourned him deeply. An edict posthumously made him Grand Preceptor—an exceptional honor—and ordered special care for his family.

Bai Yanyu, styled Xiwang, was a native of Taiyuan. He was raised from childhood in the Jin ducal household. At thirteen he followed the Jin founder against Shu and won renown for his swift, fierce fighting. Once Jin held the realm he served in the palace guard and rose to acting Minister of Works. In the Tianfu era, with the Jin founder at Ye, An Chongrong rebelled at Zhenzhou and marched tens of thousands of men on the capital. The Jin founder ordered Du Chongwei to lead the generals against him. Yanyu had not been chosen for the campaign. He wept before the Jin founder and begged to lead the van; his request was granted. At the battle line at Zongcheng Yanyu led his men in the first assault and took dozens of heads. In the heat of battle his sword snapped, and the other generals were won over by his courage. The Jin founder heard of it and at once sent a palace envoy with a treasured sword and fine horse. After Changshan was pacified he was made acting Minister over the Masses and colonel of the horse army's left wing. Later he became prefect of Fenzhou, then defense commissioner of Fuzhou. At the founding of the dynasty he was further made acting Senior Grand Mentor, then relieved and recalled to court. When Taizu marched in person against Yan and Hai he made Yanyu vanguard colonel. After Yanzhou fell Yanyu was made defense commissioner of Qizhou. A year later he was reassigned as defense commissioner of Yanzhou. In two years at Yanzhou his rule won wide praise and the people lived in peace. Hundreds of local citizens went to court asking that a stele be raised to honor his good governance. In the winter of Xiande 2 Shizong made chief minister Li Gu overall commander of the Huainan campaign and ordered Yanyu to serve as vanguard colonel. In the spring of the third year he and Han Lingkun led their troops into Yangzhou first, and the army's fame spread far. Soon he was ordered to hold Sheng Tang with a separate detachment and in repeated engagements routed more than ten thousand Huai rebels. In the summer of the fourth year Shizong returned from Shouchun and appointed Yanyu military governor of Tongzhou. Before he could take up the post he was ordered south again at the head of an army. That winter he died of illness beneath the walls of Haozhou. An edict posthumously made him Grand Commandant.

Tang Jinsi was a native of Qin Prefecture. As a youth he butchered dogs for a living and excelled at wrestling shows. He first served the puppet Shu state as a military colonel. In the Tongguang era Zhuangzong ordered Prince Jiji of Wei to lead an army against Shu. Jinsi was then garrisoning Guzhen with his unit and was the first to surrender the town to Jiji. He was made prefect of Xingzhou and campaign staff officer of Beizhou. When the Khitan attacked his city he fell into their hands. Zhao Yanshou, who had long known his reputation, took him into his service and made him trench-camp commissioner under his command. At the end of the Kaiyun era, with the Khitan holding the Central Plains, Jinsi was made defense commissioner of Bozhou. Shortly after he took office, bandit armies numbering in the tens of thousands besieged the city. Jinsi threw all his strength into the defense. Days later the city fell. Jinsi broke out boldly, sent word to neighboring prefectures, and brought back several hundred relief troops. He drove off the bandits, retook the city, and the people of Bozhou were saved. Early in the Han dynasty he was reassigned as campaign staff officer of Dengzhou. He remained frustrated and unfulfilled until he was relieved and recalled to court. In the Qianyou era Jinsi was made inspector along the Huai River and repeatedly routed Huai rebels. At the time Shi Hongyin abused the law for profit, framing merchants from north and south, executing them, and seizing their wealth while encouraging informers at every turn. Among Jinsi's followers was a servant whose greed knew no limit. Though Jinsi treated him with every courtesy, the man was never satisfied. One day he stormed off, sought out Hongyin, and accused Jinsi of taking heavy bribes from Huainan, hoarding weapons in secret, and plotting to serve as an inside collaborator. Hongyin at once sent his personal clerk with thirty riders to seize Jinsi. The accuser told the arresting officers: "Jinsi is immensely strong—a match for ten men. Kill him on sight, or it will be too late. " When the arresting riders arrived, Jinsi went out to meet them. When some moved to seize him, Jinsi threw his arms around one of them and cried out: "This is injustice! What crime has Jinsi committed? Even if I were guilty, death would not come too late—why will you not let me clear my name? You are all men—how can you bear to act this way! " The commander ordered him released and brought the accuser forward to confront Jinsi face to face, charging that he had taken Huainan bribes. Jinsi said: "My followers and my household are all here. If I had even ten strings of cash put by, that would count as taking bribes. He claims I am hoarding arms and armor. Aside from my official pay and gifts, he says even one other possession would count as secret stores. " The searchers combed his house and found only a single chest of clothes plus muster rolls and ration books, and so they eased their grip on him. Jinsi said: "Sir, just put me in chains and send me to the capital. " Earlier Jinsi had a steward named Wang Zhiquan in the capital. When he heard Jinsi had been framed, he went to Shi Hongzhao and said: "Tang Jinsi has given his loyal heart to the state. I have served him thirty years and know him to be filial to his parents and true to his friends. Framed like this, how can his case be heard? I ask to be thrown into prison myself first, and I beg you to investigate Jinsi properly so he is not destroyed by injustice. " Hongzhao was moved and kept him in custody, sending him wine and food every day. When Jinsi was led away in shackles, people from Ying and Bo followed him all the way to the capital to testify on his behalf. Hongzhao had the accuser interrogated. He confessed fully to the frame-up and was executed at once. Hongzhao then memorialized for Jinsi's release.

The historian writes: From antiquity, ministers whose standing grew weighty were sure to face peril, and those whose merit towered high were hard to preserve. Only the worthy could escape that fate. How much more was this true of Wang Yin, who lacked prudent foresight, under Zhou Taizu, a ruler both formidable and suspicious—how could he hope to escape disaster! From Fujin onward, all were outstanding commanders; to bear imperial banners and serve as frontier pillars was only fitting. Only Yanchao died holding the enemy at bay—can that be called anything but loyalty!
