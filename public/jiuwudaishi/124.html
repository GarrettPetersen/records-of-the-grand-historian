王殷，瀛州人。 曾祖昌裔，本州別駕。 祖光，滄州教練使，因家焉。 唐末，幽、滄大亂，殷父鹹珪避地南遷，因投於魏軍。 殷自言生於魏州之開元寺，既長從軍，漸為偏將。 唐同光末，為華州馬步軍副指揮使，因家於華下。 天成中，移授靈武都指揮使，久之代還。 清泰中，張令昭據鄴叛，殷從範延光討之，首冒矢石，率先登城，以功授祁州刺史，尋改原州。 殷性謙謹好禮，事母以孝聞，每與人結交，過從皆先稟於母，母命不從，殷必不往，雖在軍旅，交遊不雜。 及為刺史，政事小有不佳，母察之，立殷於庭，詰責而杖之。 〈 （《歐陽史》：殷為刺史，政事有小失，母責之，殷即取杖授婢仆，自笞於母前。）〉 晉天福中，丁內艱，尋有詔起復，授憲州刺史，殷上章辭曰：「臣為末將，出處無損益於國家。 臣本燕人，值鄉國離亂，少罹偏罰，因母鞠養訓導，方得成人，不忍遽釋苴麻，遠離廬墓，伏願許臣終母喪紀。 」晉高祖嘉而許之。 晉少帝嗣位，會殷服闋，召典禁軍，累遷奉國右廂都指揮使。 漢祖受命，從討杜重威於鄴下，殷與劉詞皆率先力戰，矢中於首，久之，出折鏃於口中，以是漢祖嘉之。 乾祐末，遷侍衛步軍都指揮使，領夔州節度使，會契丹寇邊，遣殷領兵屯澶州。 及李業等作亂，漢隱帝密詔澶帥李洪義遣圖殷，洪義懼不克，反以變告殷，殷與洪義同遣人至鄴，請太祖赴內難。 殷從平京師，授侍衛親軍都指揮使。
Wang Yin came from Ying Prefecture. His great-grandfather Changyi had served as deputy prefect of that commandery. His grandfather Guang was training commissioner at Cangzhou, where the family then made their home. When the Tang dynasty collapsed into chaos in Youzhou and Cangzhou, Yin's father Xian Gui fled south and enlisted with the Wei forces. Yin claimed to have been born at Kaiyuan Temple in Weizhou; once grown he took up arms and rose by degrees to become a field officer. In the final year of the Tongguang reign he became deputy commander of the cavalry and infantry at Huazhou and established his family there. During the Tiancheng period he was appointed commander at Lingwu; after a long tour he was relieved and recalled. During Qingtai, when Zhang Lingzhao rebelled and seized Ye, Yin marched with Fan Yanguang to suppress him, took the lead under fire, and was first over the wall. For this he was made prefect of Qizhou, then soon transferred to Yuanzhou. Yin was modest, courteous, and devoted to propriety, and his filial care for his mother was renowned. Before accepting any social invitation he always asked his mother's leave; if she refused, he would not go. Even amid military life his circle of friends remained narrow and disciplined. Once he became a prefect, whenever his administration fell even slightly short, his mother would notice, summon him into the courtyard, rebuke him, and have him beaten. [ (According to 《Ouyang Xiu's History》: Whenever Yin erred slightly in office, his mother would scold him; he would hand a cane to a servant and have himself beaten in her presence.)] During the Jin Tianfu period he entered mourning for his mother, but the court soon ordered him recalled and named him prefect of Xianzhou. Yin memorialized to decline, writing: 'I am only a minor commander; whether I serve or withdraw makes no difference to the realm. I am a native of Yan. In the chaos of our homeland I was once unjustly punished as a youth, and it was only through my mother's care and teaching that I grew to manhood. I cannot bring myself to shed my mourning garb and leave her tomb so soon. I beg Your Majesty to let me observe the full period of filial mourning. ' Gaozu of Jin commended his plea and granted it. When Emperor Shao of Jin took the throne, Yin had just completed his mourning. He was called to lead the palace guard and rose in time to command the right wing of the Fengguo army. When the founder of Han took power, Yin joined the campaign against Du Chongwei at Ye. He and Liu Ci both led the charge; an arrow struck him in the head, and only after some time did he spit out the broken tip. The Han founder praised him highly for this. Late in the Qianyou era he became commander of the palace guard infantry and concurrent military governor of Kuizhou. When the Khitans invaded the frontier, he was dispatched to hold Cangzhou with his troops. When Li Ye and his faction rebelled, the Hidden Emperor of Han secretly ordered Li Hongyi, military governor of Cangzhou, to eliminate Yin. Hongyi, fearing failure, instead warned Yin of the plot. Together they sent envoys to Ye asking Taizu to march against the court conspiracy. Yin followed the campaign that pacified the capital and was made commander of the imperial guard.

太祖即位，授天雄軍節度使，加同平章事，典軍如故。 殷赴鎮，以侍衛司局從，凡河北征鎮有戍兵處，鹹稟殷節制。 又於民間多方聚斂，太祖聞而惡之，因使宣諭曰：「朕離鄴時，帑廩所儲不少，卿與國家同體，隨要取給，何患無財。 」二年夏，太祖征兗還，殷迎謁於路，宴賜而去。 及王峻得罪，太祖遣其子飛龍使承誨往鄴，令口諭峻之過惡，以慰其心。 三年秋，以永壽節上表請覲，太祖雖允其請，且慮殷之不誠，尋遣使止之。 何福進在鎮州，素惡殷之太橫，福進入朝，摭其陰事以奏之，太祖遂疑之。 是年冬，以郊禋有日，殷自鎮入覲，太祖令依舊內外巡警。 殷出入部從不下數百人，又以儀形魁偉，觀者無不聳然。 一日，遽入奏曰：「郊禮在近，兵民大集，臣城外防警，請量給甲仗，以備非常。 」太祖難之。 時中外以太祖嬰疾，步履稍難，多不視朝，俯逼郊禋，殷有震主之勢，頗憂之。 太祖乃力疾坐於滋德殿，殷入起居，即命執之，尋降制流竄，及出都城，遽殺之，眾情乃安。 是歲春末，鄴城寺鐘懸絕而落，又火光出幡竿之上。 殷之入覲也，都人餞於離亭，上馬失鐙，翻墮於地，人訝其不祥，果及於禍。 太祖尋令澶帥鄭仁誨赴鄴，殷次子為衙內指揮使，不出候謁，仁誨誅之，遷其家屬於登州。
After Taizu ascended the throne, Yin was appointed military governor of the Tianxiong army and made a co-equal chief councilor while retaining command of the guard. When Yin departed for his post he took his palace guard staff with him, and every garrisoned command in Hebei fell under his authority. He also extorted wealth from the populace on many fronts. When Taizu learned of this he was displeased and sent word: "When I left Ye the treasuries were well stocked. You and the state are one; take what you need—why scrape the people for money?" ' That summer in the second year, as Taizu returned from the Yan campaign, Yin met him on the road, was entertained and rewarded, and then withdrew. When Wang Jun fell from favor, Taizu sent his son Chenghui, the Feilong envoy, to Ye with instructions to recount Jun's offenses aloud and reassure Yin. That autumn in the third year he petitioned to attend court on the Yongshou festival. Taizu granted the request in form but doubted his loyalty and soon sent orders forbidding him to come. He Fujin, stationed at Zhenzhou, had long resented Yin's overbearing conduct. When Fujin came to court he laid out Yin's hidden misdeeds in a memorial, and Taizu began to suspect him. That winter, with the suburban sacrifice approaching, Yin came from his post to attend court, and Taizu restored him to his former duties of internal and external patrol. Whenever he went abroad he was attended by no fewer than several hundred men, and his towering stature made every onlooker stand in awe. One day he rushed in and said: 'The suburban rites are at hand and troops and civilians are massing. I am responsible for security outside the walls. Please issue arms and armor as needed against any emergency. ' Taizu was deeply troubled by the request. Throughout the court it was known that Taizu was ill and walked with difficulty, and he seldom held audience. With the sacrifice imminent and Yin displaying the aura of a man who could overshadow his sovereign, anxiety spread on all sides. Taizu dragged himself to Zide Hall. When Yin came to pay his respects, he had him seized at once, issued an edict of exile—and had him killed as soon as he left the capital. Only then did public alarm subside. Late that spring the temple bell at Ye fell when its ropes snapped, and flames were seen dancing atop a banner pole. When Yin set out for court the people of the capital saw him off at the farewell pavilion; he lost a stirrup mounting his horse and fell to the ground. Onlookers took it for an evil omen—and calamity soon followed. Taizu soon sent Zheng Renhui, governor of Cangzhou, to Ye. Yin's second son, a yamen commander, failed to come out to receive him; Renhui had him executed and relocated the family to Dengzhou.

何福進，字善長，太原人。 父神劍，累贈左驍衛大將軍。 福進少從軍，以驍勇聞。 唐同光末，郭從謙以兵圍莊宗於大內，福進時為宿衛軍校，獨出死力拒戰於內，後明宗知而嘉之，擢為捧聖軍校，出為磁州刺史，充北面行營先鋒都校。 清泰中，自彰聖都虞候率本軍從範延光平鄴，以功歷鄭、隴二州防禦使。 開運中，由潁州團練使入拜左驍衛大將軍。 屬契丹陷中原，令中朝文武臣僚凡數十人隨帳北歸，時福進預其行。 行次鎮州，聞北主已斃，其黨尚據鎮陽，遂與李筠、白再榮之儔合謀力戰，盡逐契丹，據有鎮陽。 時漢祖已建號於河東，詔以福進為北面行營馬步都虞候，尋拜曹州防禦使、檢校太保。 太祖出鎮於鄴，將謀北伐，奏以福進自隨。 及太祖入平內難，以輔佐功拜忠武軍節度使，不數月，移領鎮州。 數年之間，北鄙無事。 及聞太祖將有事於南郊，拜章入覲，改天平軍節度使，加同平章事。 未及之任，卒於東京之私第，年六十有六，時顯德元年正月也。 累贈中書令。
He Fujin, whose style was Shanchang, came from Taiyuan. His father Shenjian was posthumously honored as Grand General of the Left Xiaowei Guard. Fujin took up arms young and won renown for bold fighting. At the close of the Tongguang era Guo Congqian besieged Zhuangzong in the inner palace. Fujin, then a night-guard officer, fought alone with desperate valor inside the walls. Mingzong learned of this and commended him, promoting him in the Fengsheng guard, appointing him prefect of Cizhou, and naming him vanguard commander of the northern expedition. During Qingtai he led his Zhangsheng troops with Fan Yanguang to pacify Ye and, for his service, served in turn as defense commissioner of Zhengzhou and Longzhou. During Kaiyun he came to court from his post as regiment trainer of Yingzhou and was appointed Grand General of the Left Xiaowei Guard. When the Khitans overran the Central Plains they compelled several dozen court officials to march north with their camp, and Fujin was among them. Halting at Zhenzhou they learned the Khitan emperor was dead though his followers still held Zhenyang. Fujin joined Li Jun, Bai Zairong, and others in a fierce rising that drove the Khitans out and took the city. By then the Han founder had proclaimed his reign in Hedong. Fujin was named deputy commander of the northern campaign's cavalry and infantry, then made defense commissioner of Caozhou and Acting Grand Guardian. When Taizu took up his post at Ye and prepared a northern campaign, he asked that Fujin accompany him. After Taizu quelled the court crisis, Fujin was made military governor of the Zhongwu army; within months he was transferred to Zhenzhou. For several years the northern frontier remained quiet under his rule. Learning that Taizu would perform the suburban sacrifice, he petitioned for an audience, was transferred to the Tianping army, and made a co-equal chief councilor. Before he could take up the new post he died at his home in the Eastern Capital, aged sixty-six, in the first month of the first year of Xiande. He was posthumously honored as Director of the Secretariat.

His son Jijun served the dynasty as military governor of the Jianwu army and died in that post.

劉詞，字好謙，元城人。 梁貞明中，事故鄴帥楊師厚，以勇悍聞。 唐莊宗入魏，亦列於麾下，兩河之戰，無不預焉。 同光初，為效節軍使，轉劍直指揮使，尋以忤於權臣，出為汝州小校，凡留滯十餘年。 清泰初，詔諸道選驍果以實禁衛，由是得入典禁軍。 晉初，從侯益收汜水關，佐楊光遠平鄴都，累遷奉國第一軍都虞候。 後從馬全節伐安陸，敗淮賊萬餘眾，晉祖嘉之，授奉國都校，累加檢校司空。 又從杜重威敗安重榮於宗城。 及圍鎮陽，詞自登雲梯，身先士伍，以功加檢校司徒、沁州刺史。 時王師方討襄陽，尋命詞兼行營都虞候，襄陽平，遷本州團練使。 在郡歲餘，臨事之暇，必被甲枕戈而臥，人或問之，詞曰：「我以勇敢而登貴仕，不可一日而忘本也。 若信其溫飽，則筋力有怠，將來何以報國也！ 」及漢有天下，復為奉國右廂都校，遙領閬州防禦使。 從太祖平鄴，加檢校太保。 乾祐初，李守貞叛於河中，太祖征之，朝廷以為侍衛步軍都指揮使，遙領寧江軍節度使，充行營馬步都虞候，命分屯於河西。 二年正月，守貞遣敢死之士數千，夜入其營。 皆怖懼不知所為，惟詞神氣自若，令於軍中曰：「此小盜耳，不足驚也。 」遂免胄橫戈，叱短兵以擊之，賊眾大敗而退。 自是守貞喪膽，不復有奔突之意。 河中平，太祖嘉之，表其功為華州節度使，歲餘，移鎮邢臺。 太祖受命，加同平章事。 三年秋，改鎮河陽。 顯德初，世宗親征劉崇，詞奉命領所部兵隨駕，行及高平南，遇樊愛能等自北退回，且言官軍已敗，止詞不行，詞不聽，疾驅而北。 世宗聞而嘉之，尋命為隨駕都部署，又授河東道行營副部署。 其年夏，車駕還京，授永興軍節度使，加兼侍中，行京兆尹。 二年冬，以疾卒於鎮，年六十有五。 贈中書令，謚曰忠惠。 詞發身軍校，亟歷戎事，常以忠勇自負。 洎領藩鎮，能靖恭為治，無苛政以撓民，謚以忠惠，議者韙之。
Liu Ci, whose style was Haoqian, came from Yuancheng. During the Liang Zhenming era he served under Yang Shihou, the former governor of Ye, and won fame for fierce fighting. When Zhuangzong of Tang entered Wei, Ci joined his ranks and took part in nearly every battle fought between the Yellow and Bian rivers. Early in Tongguang he commanded the Xiaojie army, then the Jianzhi guard, but after offending a powerful minister was demoted to a junior post at Ruzhou and languished there for more than a decade. Early in Qingtai the throne ordered each circuit to send its bravest men to fill the palace guard, and through this Ci reentered imperial service. Early in Jin he followed Hou Yi to recover Sishui Pass, aided Yang Guangyuan in pacifying Ye, and rose to deputy commander of the first Fengguo army. He later joined Ma Quanjie against Anlu and routed more than ten thousand Huai rebels. The Jin emperor commended him, made him commander of the Fengguo army, and promoted him to Acting Minister of Works. He also followed Du Chongwei in the defeat of An Chongrong at Zongcheng. During the siege of Zhenyang he climbed the assault ladder himself and led the charge, earning promotion to Acting Minister of Education and appointment as prefect of Qinzhou. While the imperial army was campaigning against Xiangyang, Ci was named deputy commander of the expedition; when the city fell he became regiment trainer of Xiangyang. After more than a year in office, whenever he had a spare moment he would sleep in armor with his weapon for a pillow. When asked why, Ci said: 'I won high rank through courage; I must not forget that for a single day. If I grow soft with comfort, my strength will fail—how then could I repay the state in the days ahead! ' When Han ruled the realm he again commanded the right wing of the Fengguo army and held the defense commission of Langzhou in absentia. He followed Taizu in pacifying Ye and was promoted to Acting Grand Guardian. Early in Qianyou, when Li Shouzhen rebelled at Hezhong, Taizu marched against him. The court named Ci commander of the palace guard infantry, concurrent military governor of the Ningjiang army, and deputy commander of the expedition, with orders to encamp west of the Yellow River. In the first month of the second year Shouzhen sent several thousand suicide troops in a night assault on his camp. The camp panicked, but Ci alone remained composed. He called out to the troops: 'These are mere raiders—nothing to fear. ' He doffed his helmet, seized his halberd, and drove the skirmishers forward; the enemy broke and fled. From that day Shouzhen lost heart and attempted no further sorties. When Hezhong fell, Taizu commended him and had him made military governor of Huazhou; a year later he was transferred to Xingtai. When Taizu took the throne, Ci was made a co-equal chief councilor. That autumn in the third year he was transferred to Heyang. Early in Xiande, when Shizong marched in person against Liu Chong, Ci led his troops with the imperial train. South of Gaoping he met Fan Aineng and others fleeing south, who claimed the army had been routed and begged him to turn back. Ci refused and pressed north at full speed. Shizong heard of this and commended him, soon naming him chief deployer of the imperial train and deputy deployer of the Hedong expedition. That summer, when the emperor returned to the capital, Ci was made military governor of the Yongxing army, appointed Palace Attendant, and entrusted with the duties of Jingzhao governor. He died of illness at his post that winter in the second year, aged sixty-five. He was posthumously honored as Director of the Secretariat with the posthumous name Loyal and Kind. Ci had risen from the ranks, seen constant warfare, and prided himself on loyalty and valor. Once he held a frontier command he governed with quiet dignity and imposed no harsh levies on the people; commentators agreed that the posthumous name Loyal and Kind was well deserved.

史彥超，雲州人也。 性驍獷，有膽氣，累功至龍捷都指揮使。 太祖之赴內難，彥超以本軍從。 國初，與虎捷都指揮使何徵戍晉州，會劉崇與契丹入寇，攻圍州城月餘，是時本州無帥，知州王萬敢不協物情，彥超與何徵協力固拒，累挫敵鋒。 攻擊日急，禦捍有備，軍政甚嚴，居人無擾。 及朝廷遣樞密使王峻總兵為援，敵兵宵遁。 太祖嘉其善守之功，賞賜甚厚。 未幾，授龍捷右廂都指揮使，尋授鄭州防禦使。 劉崇之寇潞州也，車駕親征，以彥超為先鋒都指揮使。 高平之戰，先登陷陣，以功授華州節度使，先鋒如故。 大軍至河東城下，契丹營於忻、代之間，遙應賊勢，詔天雄軍節度使符彥卿率諸將屯忻州以拒之。 彥卿襲契丹於忻口。 彥超以先鋒軍追蕃寇，離大軍稍遠，賊兵伏發，為賊所陷。 世宗痛惜久之，詔贈太師，示加等也，仍命優恤其家焉。
Shi Yanchao came from Yunzhou. Fierce and domineering by nature, bold in spirit, he rose through repeated merit to command the Longjie army. When Taizu marched to quell the court crisis, Yanchao followed with his troops. Early in the dynasty he and He Zheng of the Hujie guard garrisoned Jinzhou. When Liu Chong and the Khitans invaded they besieged the city for more than a month. The circuit had no military governor, and the prefect Wang Wan, though brave, had alienated local opinion. Yanchao and He Zheng held the city together and repeatedly repulsed the enemy. As the assaults intensified daily, their defenses held firm, military discipline remained strict, and the civilian population was left unmolested. When the court sent Privy Councilor Wang Jun with a relief army, the enemy withdrew under cover of night. Taizu commended their stout defense and rewarded them handsomely. Soon he was named commander of the right wing of the Longjie army, then defense commissioner of Zhengzhou. When Liu Chong attacked Luzhou the emperor marched in person and appointed Yanchao vanguard commander. At Gaoping he was first over the wall and into the enemy line; for this he was made military governor of Huazhou while retaining command of the vanguard. When the main army reached the walls of Hedong, the Khitans camped between Xinzhou and Daizhou in support of the enemy. The throne ordered Fu Yanqing, military governor of the Tianxiong army, to lead the generals to Xinzhou to block them. Yanqing attacked the Khitans at Xinkou. Yanchao pursued the enemy with the vanguard, outrunning the main force, and fell into an ambush. Shizong mourned him deeply, posthumously honoring him as Grand Preceptor—the highest rank—and ordering special care for his family.

Wang Lingwen, whose style was Shunzhi, came from Hejian in Yingzhou. His father Di had been prefect of Dezhou and was posthumously honored as Grand Preceptor of the Heir Apparent. Lingwen won early fame for martial valor, first entering service under Zhuangzong of Tang and rising to become an officer of the hall-direct guard. When Mingzong was supreme commander he once fought the Khitans at Shanggu. Mingzong's mount bolted under fire and the enemy closed in; Lingwen gave him his own horse and fought on foot, loosing arrow after arrow until the enemy drew back. After Mingzong ascended the throne, Lingwen rose to command the Shenwu-Zhangsheng army. Early in Jin he moved from prefect of Zizhou to regiment trainer of Mingzhou. When An Chongrong rebelled at Zhenzhou, the Jin emperor named Lingwen commander of the expedition's cavalry. With Du Chongwei he routed the rebels at Zongcheng, was made defense commissioner of Bozhou, and soon military governor of the Yongqing army. When the Khitans invaded, Lingwen was summoned to court; they then seized Beizhou, and his family fell into Khitan hands. Emperor Shao of Jin took pity on him and made him military governor of the Wusheng army. Soon he was transferred to Yanzhou, then to Lingwu. When Han ruled the realm he again governed the Yongqing army, then was transferred to Anzhou. At the founding of the dynasty he was made Acting Grand Commandant and co-equal chief councilor. When Shizong succeeded the throne, Lingwen was transferred to the Zhen'an army, then relinquished his command and returned to court. He died of illness that summer in the third year of Xiande, aged sixty-two; the throne posthumously honored him as Palace Attendant.

At the founding of Han he was made military governor of Hezhong, commissioner for pacifying the southwest, and Acting Grand Tutor. Bai Yanyu, whose style was Xiwang, came from Taiyuan. He was raised from childhood in the Jin princely household; at thirteen he joined the Jin founder's campaign against Shu and won fame for his fierce agility. After Jin ruled the realm he held successive commands in the palace guard and rose to Acting Minister of Works. During Tianfu, while the Jin founder was at Ye, An Chongrong rebelled at Zhenzhou and marched tens of thousands toward the capital. The founder ordered Du Chongwei to lead the generals against him. Yanyu had not been assigned to the campaign; he wept before the founder and begged to lead the charge, and was granted leave. At the battle of Zongcheng he led his men in the first assault and took dozens of heads; when the fighting grew fierce his sword snapped, and the other generals were won to admiration. The Jin founder heard of this and at once sent a palace envoy with a treasured sword and fine horse as reward. After Changshan was pacified he was made Acting Minister of Education and commander of the left wing of the cavalry. He later served as prefect of Fenzhou, then as defense commissioner of Fuzhou. At the dynasty's founding he was made Acting Grand Guardian; soon he was relieved and recalled to court. When Taizu campaigned in person against Yanhai, Yanyu was named vanguard commander; after Yanzhou fell he was made defense commissioner of Qizhou. A year later he was transferred to Yanzhou as defense commissioner. During two years at Yanzhou his rule won wide praise and the people lived in peace; several hundred residents petitioned the throne to erect a monument celebrating his virtuous administration. That winter in the second year of Xiande, Shizong named Chief Minister Li Gu overall deployer of the Huainan army and appointed Yanyu vanguard commander. That spring he and Han Lingkun led their troops into Yangzhou ahead of the main force, and the army's prestige soared. He was then ordered to encamp a separate division at Shengtang and in repeated engagements routed more than ten thousand Huai rebels. That summer Shizong returned from Shouchun and appointed Yanyu military governor of Tongzhou, but before he could take up the post he was ordered south again at the head of an army. He died of illness that winter beneath the walls of Haozhou. The throne posthumously honored him as Grand Commandant.

唐景思，秦州人也。 幼以屠狗為業，善角觝戲。 初事偽蜀為軍校。 唐同光中，莊宗命魏王繼岌帥師伐蜀，時景思以所部戍於固鎮，首以其城降於繼岌，乃授興州刺史，為貝州行軍司馬。 屬契丹攻其城，因陷於幕庭，趙延壽素知其名，令隸於帳下，署為所部壕寨使。 開運末，契丹據中原，以景思為亳州防禦使。 領事之日，會草寇數萬攻圍其城，景思悉力以拒之。 後數日城陷，景思挺身而出，使人告於鄰郡，得援軍數百，逐其草寇，復有其城，亳民賴是以濟。 漢初，改授鄧州行軍司馬，常郁郁不得志，後受代歸闕。 乾祐中，命景思為沿淮巡檢使，屢挫淮賊。 時史宏肇淫刑黷貨，多織羅南北富商殺之，奪其財，大開告密之門。 景思部下有仆夫，希求無厭，雖委曲待之，不滿其心，一日拂衣而去，見宏肇，言景思受淮南厚賂，私貯器械，欲為內應。 宏肇即令親吏殿三十騎往收之，告者謂收吏曰：「景思多力，十夫之敵也，見便殺之，不然則無及矣。 」收騎至，景思迎接。 有欲擒之者，景思以兩手抱之，大呼曰：「冤哉！ 景思何罪？ 設若有罪，死亦非晚，何不容披雪？ 公等皆丈夫，安忍如此！ 」都將命釋之，引告者面證景思，言受淮南賂。 景思曰：「我從人家人並在此，若有十緡貯積，亦是受賂。 言我貯甲仗，除官賜外，有一事亦是私貯。 」使者搜索其家，惟衣一笥，軍籍糧簿而已，乃寬之。 景思曰：「使但械系送我入京。 」先是，景思別有紀綱王知權者，在京，聞景思被誣，乃見史宏肇曰：「唐景思赤心為國，某服事三十年，孝於父母，義於朋友，被此誣罔，何以伸陳。 某請先下獄，願公追劾景思，免至冤橫。 」宏肇湣之，令在獄，日與酒食。 景思既桎梏就路，潁、亳之人隨至京師，眾保證之。 宏肇乃令鞫告事者，具伏誣陷，即斬之，遂奏釋景思。
Tang Jinsi came from Qinzhou. In youth he worked as a dog butcher and excelled at wrestling. He first served the puppet Shu state as an army officer. During Tongguang, Zhuangzong sent Prince Jiji of Wei against Shu. Jinsi was then garrisoning Guzhen and was the first to surrender the town to Jiji; he was made prefect of Xingzhou and campaign marshal of Beizhou. When the Khitans attacked his city he fell into their camp. Zhao Yanshou, who had long known his reputation, took him into his service and appointed him trench-camp commissioner of his command. Late in Kaiyun, when the Khitans held the Central Plains, Jinsi was made defense commissioner of Bozhou. Soon after he took office, tens of thousands of bandits besieged the city, and Jinsi resisted with all his strength. Days later the city fell, but Jinsi sallied forth in person, sent word to neighboring prefectures, gathered several hundred relief troops, drove off the bandits, and recovered the city—the people of Bozhou were saved by his effort. Early in Han he was reassigned as campaign marshal of Dengzhou, but remained discontented; later he was relieved and recalled to court. During Qianyou he was named inspector along the Huai and repeatedly defeated Huai rebels. At that time Shi Hongzhao abused the law for profit, entrapping wealthy merchants from north and south, executing them and seizing their goods, while encouraging a flood of secret denunciations. A servant in Jinsi's household was insatiably greedy; though Jinsi treated him generously, the man was never satisfied. One day he stormed off to Hongzhao and accused Jinsi of taking heavy bribes from Huainan, hoarding weapons, and plotting to act as an enemy within. Hongzhao at once sent thirty horsemen under a trusted officer to seize him. The accuser told the arresting party: 'Jinsi is immensely strong—the equal of ten men. Kill him on sight, or you will never take him alive. ' When the horsemen arrived, Jinsi came out to meet them. As some moved to seize him, Jinsi seized one man in both arms and cried out: 'This is injustice! What crime have I committed? If I am guilty, death can wait—why not let the truth be heard? You are all men of honor—how can you bear to do this! ' The commander ordered him released and brought the accuser face to face with Jinsi, who repeated the charge of Huainan bribes. Jinsi said: 'My servants and household are all here. Search them—if you find so much as ten strings of cash laid by, count it as proof of bribery. You say I hoarded arms and armor—aside from what the court bestowed, there is one thing I did keep privately. ' The envoys searched his home and found only a single chest of clothing, military rolls, and grain ledgers, and so eased their suspicion. Jinsi said: 'Sir, put me in chains and take me to the capital. ' Earlier, Jinsi had a steward named Wang Zhiquan in the capital. When he heard Jinsi had been slandered, he went to Shi Hongzhao and said: 'Tang Jinsi has served the state with a loyal heart. I have served him thirty years; he is filial to his parents and faithful to his friends. How can such a false charge stand? I ask to be imprisoned first, and beg Your Excellency to investigate Jinsi thoroughly, that he may not suffer unjust destruction. ' Hongzhao took pity on him and had him held in prison, with wine and food brought each day. Once Jinsi was shackled and on the road to the capital, people from Yingzhou and Bozhou followed all the way to the capital, and crowds came forward to attest to his innocence. Hongzhao then had the accuser interrogated; he confessed fully to the false charge and was beheaded at once. Hongzhao then memorialized for Jinsi's release.

The historiographer writes: From antiquity, ministers whose prestige grew heavy were always imperiled, and those whose merit towered high were hard to preserve—only the truly worthy could escape such a fate. Wang Yin, lord of Ye, lacked the foresight of a sage, and Taizu of Zhou was a ruler both formidable and suspicious—how could he hope to escape disaster?
