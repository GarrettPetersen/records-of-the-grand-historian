When the Later Liang founder founded his dynasty, the realm was still reeling from Huang Chao's rebellion. From a single stronghold at Yimen he tightened frontier defenses abroad and reclaimed wasteland at home, promoted farming and silk production, and kept taxes low. Soldiers bore the hardships of war, yet commoners paid levies willingly; within roughly twenty years he had built a hegemonic power. When the last Liang emperor and Li Cunxu faced off along the Yellow River, Henan commoners were worn down by supply transport but did not yet flee en masse—because levies remained tolerable and families still had reason to cling to their farms. After Zhuangzong overthrew the Liang, he put the clerk Kong Qian in charge of transport and corvée taxes, enforcing harsh exactions on the populace while heaping revenue on the court. Private wealth was drained dry, yet military rations still fell short. War piled on top of famine, and within three or four years the Tang founder's regime collapsed—for no other reason than crushing taxes and corvée that left the empire disillusioned. [ (Editorial note: The passage above appears in Hong Mai's Third Brush Notes as a quotation from the Old History; by its sense it belongs to the preface of this Finance treatise and is placed here at the chapter opening.)]
4
唐同光三年二月，敕：「魏府小菉豆稅，每畝減放三升。 城內店宅園囿，比來無稅，頃因偽命，遂有配征。 後來以所征物色，添助軍裝衣賜，將令通濟，宜示矜蠲。 今據緊慢去處，於見輸稅絲上，每兩作三等，酌量納錢，收市軍裝衣賜，其絲仍與除放。 」其年閏十二月，吏部尚書李琪上言：「請賦稅不以折納為事，一切以本色輸官，又不以紐配為名，止以正稅加納。 」敕曰：「本朝征科，惟配有兩稅，至於折納，當不施為。 宜依李琪所論，應逐稅合納錢物斛斗鹽錢等，宜令租庸司指揮，並準元征本色輸納，不得改更，若合有移改，即須具事由聞奏。」
In the second month of Tongguang 3 (925), an edict ordered: "Reduce the Weifu green-bean levy by three sheng per mu. Shops, houses, and gardens inside the city had long been untaxed, but a spurious order had recently imposed assessments on them. The proceeds had been earmarked to supplement army uniforms and grants, but to keep commerce viable the levy should be lifted as a gesture of relief. Henceforth, depending on local conditions, each liang of silk already due in tax shall be split into three grades and commuted to cash at assessed rates to fund uniforms and grants, while the silk obligation itself is waived. " In the intercalary twelfth month of that year, Li Qi, Minister of Personnel, memorialized: "Stop using commutation for tax payments; deliver all dues in kind to the government, and abolish surcharges disguised as paired allotments—charge only the standard tax." " The throne replied: "Our collections permit only the paired allotments tied to the two-tax system; commutation must not be used. Follow Li Qi's proposal: the Transport and Corvée Office shall order that every tax item—cash, grain, salt dues, and the rest—be paid in the originally assessed kind without substitution; any change requires a memorial stating the reasons."
5
長興二年六月，敕：「委諸道觀察使，屬縣於每村定有力人戶充村長。 與村人議，有力人戶出剩田苗，補貧下不迨，肯者即具狀征收，有辭者即排段檢括。 自今年起為定額。 有經災沴及逐年逋處，不在此限。 」三年十二月，三司奏請：「諸道上供稅物，充兵士衣賜不足。 其天下所納斛斗及錢，除支贍外，請依時折納綾羅絹帛。 」從之。
In the sixth month of Changxing 2 (931), an edict directed circuit commissioners to appoint a leading household in each village as village head. They were to consult villagers so wealthy households could contribute surplus harvest assessments to cover shortfalls among the poor; volunteers would register for collection, while holdouts would be surveyed parcel by parcel. From that year forward the arrangement became a fixed quota. Areas hit by disaster or chronic arrears were exempt. " In the twelfth month of year 3, the Three Departments reported that circuit tribute was insufficient for soldiers' clothing grants. They requested that grain and cash collected empire-wide, after meeting current expenses, be commuted seasonally into silks and textiles. The request was approved.
6
晉天福四年正月，敕：「應諸道節度刺史，不得擅加賦役及於縣邑別立監征。 所納田租，委人戶自量自既。」
In the first month of Jin Tianfu 4 (939), an edict forbade military commissioners and prefects from imposing extra levies or setting up separate collection offices in counties. Field rent was to be measured and delivered by the paying households themselves."
7
周顯德三年十月，宣三司指揮諸道州府，今後夏稅，以六月一日起征，秋稅至十月一日起征，永為定制。 五年七月，賜諸道《均田圖》。 十月，命左散騎常侍艾穎等三十四人，下諸州檢定民租。 周顯德六年春，諸道使臣回，總計檢到戶二百三十萬九千八百一十二。
In the tenth month of Zhou Xiande 3 (956), the Three Departments ordered all circuits that summer tax would be collected from the first day of the sixth month and autumn tax from the first day of the tenth month, permanently. In the seventh month of year 5, each circuit received a copy of the Equal-Field Diagram. In the tenth month, thirty-four officials led by Ai Ying, Left Regular Attendant, were sent to the prefectures to audit household rents. In the spring of Xiande 6 (959), after envoys returned from the circuits, the audit registered 2,309,812 households.
8
唐同光二年，度支奏請榜示府州縣鎮，軍民商旅凡有買賣，並須使八十陌錢。
In Tongguang 2 (924), the Revenue Office ordered placards in every administrative seat requiring all buyers and sellers, military or civilian, to use cash counted at eighty per hundred full value.
9
唐同光二年二月，詔曰：「錢者，古之泉布，蓋取其流行天下，布散人間，無積滯則交易通，多貯藏則士農困，故西漢興改幣之制，立告緡之條，所以權蓄賈而防大奸也。 宜令所司散下州府，常須檢察，不得令富室分外收貯見錢，又工人銷鑄為銅器，兼沿邊州鎮設法鈐轄，勿令商人般載出境。 」三月，知唐州晏駢安奏：「市肆間點檢錢帛，內有錫镴小錢，揀得不少，皆是江南綱商挾帶而來。 」詔曰：「帛布之幣，雜以鉛錫，惟是江湖之外，盜鑄尤多，市肆之間，公行無畏，因是綱商挾帶，舟楫往來，換易好錢，藏貯富室，實為蠹弊，須有條流。 宜令京城、諸道，於坊市行使錢內，點檢雜惡鉛錫錢，並宜禁斷。 沿江州縣，每有舟船到岸，嚴加覺察，不許將雜鉛錫惡錢往來換易好錢，如有私載，並行收納。」
A Tongguang 2 edict explained: "Coin is the ancient currency called 'spring and cloth' because it should circulate widely. When it does not stagnate, trade thrives; when the wealthy hoard it, farmers and gentry suffer. That is why the Western Han reformed coinage and required reporting of hoards—to curb merchants' stockpiling and major fraud." Responsible offices were to circulate the order to all prefectures, forbid wealthy households from hoarding cash, stop artisans from melting coin into copperware, and tighten frontier controls so merchants could not export currency. " In the third month, Yan Pian'an, prefect of Tangzhou, reported that market inspections of currency had turned up many tin-lead small coins, all smuggled in by Jiangnan merchant convoys. " The court replied that debased cloth coin mixed with lead and tin flourished chiefly south of the Yangtze, where counterfeiters operated openly in markets. Convoys shuttled the bad money in, traded it for good coin, and wealthy houses hoarded it—a serious abuse requiring new rules. The capital and every circuit were to inspect currency in urban markets and ban all adulterated lead-tin coin. Riverine prefectures were to scrutinize every arriving vessel and forbid exchanging debased coin for good money; any private shipment was to be confiscated.
10
天成元年八月，中書門下奏：「訪聞近日諸道州府所賣銅器價貴，多是銷熔見錢，以邀厚利。 」乃下詔曰：「宜令遍行曉告，如元舊系銅器及碎銅，即許鑄造器物。 仍生銅器物每斤價定二百文，熟銅器物每斤四百文，如違省價，買賣之人依盜鑄錢律文科斷。」
In the eighth month of Tiancheng 1 (926), the Secretariat reported that copperware prices had risen across the circuits because artisans were melting circulating cash for profit. An edict followed ordering public notice that only registered old copperware and scrap copper might be cast into new vessels. Raw copperware was capped at two hundred cash per jin and wrought copperware at four hundred; anyone selling below those prices would be punished under the illicit-coining statute."
11
清泰二年十二月，詔御史臺曉告中外，禁用鉛錢，如違犯，準條流處分。
In the twelfth month of Qingtai 2 (935), the Censorate was ordered to announce empire-wide prohibition of lead coin, with violations punished under statute.
12
晉天福二年，詔：「禁一切銅器，其銅鏡今後官鑄造，於東京置場貨賣，許人收買，於諸處興販去。」
In Jin Tianfu 2 (937), all private copperware was banned; mirrors would be minted by the state and sold at an Eastern Capital depot for resale elsewhere.
13
周廣順元年三月，敕：「銅法，今後官中更不禁斷，一任興販，所在一色即不得瀉破為銅器貨賣，如有犯者，有人糾告捉獲，所犯人不計多少斤兩，並處死。 其地分所由節級，決脊杖十七放，鄰保人決臀杖十七放，其告事人給與賞錢一百貫文。」
In the third month of Guangshun 1 (951), copper trade was legalized, but melting coin into copperware for sale remained a capital crime on report and arrest, regardless of quantity. Local officers received seventeen strokes on the back, neighbor guarantors seventeen on the buttocks, and informants a reward of one hundred strings of cash."

江南因唐舊制，饒州置永平監，歲鑄錢，池州永寧監、建州永豐監，並歲鑄錢，杭州置保興監鑄錢。
Jiangnan followed Tang precedent: Raozhou's Yongping mint, Chizhou's Yongning mint, and Jianzhou's Yongfeng mint coined annually, while Hangzhou's Baoxing mint was also established.

A Tongguang 2 edict declared that in state finance salt revenue ranked first—and the two salt pools were especially lucrative. War had scattered the population and wrecked the salt offices, so scheduled revenues had collapsed. Restoration required firm regulations and pragmatic policies that could actually succeed. Prince Li Jilin of Ji, military commissioner of Hedong, was appointed monopoly salt commissioner for the Anyi and Jie pools and authorized to draft comprehensive regulations immediately." " In Tiancheng 1, silkworm-salt distribution to households was limited to a single allotment each February, with payment due on the summer-tax schedule. " On the seventh day of the fifth month of Changxing 4, the salt transport commissioner submitted unified salt regulations: prefectures selling granular salt were to maintain state monopoly and barter depots. 晉天福中，河南、河北諸州，除俵散蠶鹽征錢外，每年末鹽界分場務，約糶錢一十七萬貫有餘。 言事者稱，雖得此錢，百姓多犯鹽法，請將上件食鹽錢於諸道州府計戶，每戶一貫至二百，為五等配之，然後任人逐便興販，既不虧官，又益百姓。 朝廷行之，諸處場務亦且仍舊。 俄而鹽貨頓賤，去出鹽遠處州縣，每斤不過二十文，近處不過一十文，掌事者又難驟改其法，奏請重制鹽場稅，蓋欲絕其興販，歸利於官也。
During mid-Tianfu, Henan and Hebei monopoly offices for powdered salt, apart from silkworm-salt allotments, yielded over 170,000 strings annually. Critics argued that despite the revenue, salt violations were rampant; they proposed distributing the table-salt levy across households in five grades from one string down to two hundred cash, then allowing free trade—benefiting both state and people. The court adopted the plan while leaving local offices temporarily unchanged. Salt prices soon collapsed—to twenty cash per jin far from production centers and ten nearby. Officials, unable to reverse course quickly, petitioned to restore salt-yard taxes to choke private trade and reclaim revenue.

The Changxing 1 (930) amnesty cut the autumn leaven levy from five to three cash per mu. Leaven taxes on autumn seedlings in the capital and every circuit were remitted starting that summer. The state would brew leaven itself, ordering each prefecture to halve the old price and sell it exclusively within urban markets. Urban residents could not brew privately, but rural families needing leaven for home use were free to make their own. When the edict was promulgated, the populace rejoiced.

周顯德四年七月，詔曰：「諸道州府曲務，今後一依往例，官中禁法賣曲，逐處先置都務，候敕到日，並仰停罷。 據見在曲數，準備貨買，兼據年計合，使曲數依時蹋造，候人戶將到價錢，據數計曲，不得賒賣抑配與人。」
In the seventh month of Zhou Xiande 4 (957), an edict declared that circuit leaven monopolies would revert to the old state monopoly: local chief offices set up earlier were to shut down when the order arrived. Stock on hand was to be readied for sale, annual requirements scheduled for timely production, and leaven issued only after full cash payment in measured amounts—no credit sales or forced allocations.
