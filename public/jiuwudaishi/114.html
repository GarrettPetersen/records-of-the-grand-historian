1
世宗睿武孝文皇帝，諱榮，太祖之養子，蓋聖穆皇后之侄也。 本姓柴氏，父守禮，太子少保致仕。 〈 （《隆平集》：柴翁者，嘗獨居室，人以為司冥事。 一日，笑不止，妻問其故，不答。 翁嗜飲，妻醉之以酒，乃曰：「上帝有命，郭郎為天子。 」考柴翁即守禮之父，史佚其名。）〉 帝以唐天祐十八年，歲在辛巳，九月二十四日丙午，生於邢州之別墅。 年未童冠，因侍聖穆皇后，在太祖左右。 時太祖無子，家道淪落，然以帝謹厚，故以庶事委之。 帝悉心經度，資用獲濟，太祖甚憐之，乃養為己子。 漢初，太祖以佐命功為樞密副使，帝始授左監門衛將軍。 〈 （《國老談苑》云：周世宗在漢為諸衛將軍，嘗遊畿甸，謁縣令，忘其姓名，令方聚邑客蒱博，勿得見，世宗頗銜之。 及即位，令因部夫犯贓數百匹，宰相範質以具獄上奏，世宗曰：「親民之官，贓狀狼籍，法當處死。 」質奏曰：「受所監臨財物有罪，上贓雖多，法不至死。 」世宗怒，厲聲曰：「法者自古帝王之所制，本以防奸，朕立法殺贓吏，非酷刑也。 」質曰：「陛下殺之則可，若付有司，臣不敢署敕。 」遂貸其命。）〉 二年，太祖鎮鄴，改天雄軍牙內都指揮使，領貴州刺史、檢校右僕射。 三年冬，太祖入平內難，留帝守鄴城。
Emperor Shizong, posthumously titled Sagely Martial and Filial, bore the personal name Rong. He was Emperor Taizu's adopted son and a nephew of Empress Shengmu. He was born into the Chai clan. His father Shouli had retired from office as Grand Master of Remonstrance for the Heir Apparent. [Note: (From the 《Longping ji》: An old man of the Chai clan once lived alone in his chamber, and people believed he had dealings with the realm of the dead. One day he laughed without cease. When his wife asked why, he would not tell her. The old man loved wine. His wife plied him with drink until he was drunk, and then he said, "Heaven has decreed that Lord Guo shall become emperor." " It is established that this old man Chai was Shouli's father, though the history does not record his personal name.)] He was born on the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month of 921, the eighteenth year of Tang Tianyou, cyclical year xinsi, at a country estate in Xing Prefecture. While still a boy, he attended Empress Shengmu and remained close at Emperor Taizu's side. Taizu then had no sons of his own, and the family's fortunes had fallen on hard times. Because Rong was prudent and steadfast, Taizu entrusted him with the household's day-to-day affairs. He managed everything with scrupulous care and kept the household supplied. Taizu grew deeply fond of him and adopted him as his own son. When the Later Han was founded, Taizu was made Vice Commissioner of the Bureau of Military Affairs for his service in establishing the dynasty. Rong received his first appointment as Left Gate Guard General. [Note: (The 《Guolao tanyuan》 records that while Shizong was still a guard general under the Later Han, he once traveled in the capital region and called on a county magistrate, whose name is not recorded. The magistrate was hosting local guests at dice and refused to see him, and Shizong took the slight hard. After he took the throne, that same magistrate was found to have embezzled several hundred bolts of silk through corvée laborers. Chief Minister Fan Zhi submitted the completed case for decision. Shizong said, "An official charged with caring for the people whose corruption is this blatant deserves death under the law." " Fan Zhi replied, "Taking property from those under one's supervision is indeed a crime, but even when the amount is large, the statute does not prescribe death." " Shizong grew angry and said sharply, "Law has been made by rulers since antiquity precisely to guard against wickedness. When I legislate the execution of corrupt officials, that is not cruel punishment." " Fan Zhi said, "Your Majesty may execute him if you wish, but if the case is handed to the judicial offices, I will not sign the edict." " In the end his life was spared.)] In the second year, when Taizu took command at Ye, Rong was made Commander-in-Chief of the Inner Guards of the Tianxiong Army and concurrently served as Prefect of Gui Prefecture and Acting Right Vice Director of the Imperial Secretariat. That winter Taizu marched to the capital to quell internal strife and left Rong to hold Ye.
2
廣順元年正月，太祖踐祚，帝懇求入覲，忽夢至河而不得渡，尋授澶州節度使、檢校太保，封太原郡侯。 帝在鎮，為政清肅，盜不犯境。 先是，澶之裏巷湫隘，公署毀圯，帝即廣其街肆，增其廨宇，吏民賴之。 〈 （《宋史·王贊傳》：周世宗鎮澶淵，每旬決囚，贊引律令，辨析中理。 問之，知其嘗事學問，即署右職。）〉 二年正月，兗州慕容彥超反，帝累表請征行，太祖嘉之。 及曹英等東討，數月無功，太祖欲親征，召群臣議其事，宰臣馮道奏以方當盛夏，車駕不宜沖冒。 太祖曰：「寇不可玩。 如朕不可行，當使澶州兒子擊賊，方辦吾事。 」時樞密王峻意不欲帝將兵，故太祖親征。 六月，兗州平。 十二月，加檢校太傅、同平章事。 三年正月，帝入覲。 三月，授開封尹兼功德使，封晉王。
In the first month of Guangshun 1, year 951, Taizu took the throne. Rong urgently petitioned for an audience, then dreamed that he came to a river but could not cross. Soon after he was appointed Military Commissioner of Cao Prefecture and Acting Grand Guardian, and enfeoffed as Marquis of Taiyuan. In his commandery his rule was clear and strict, and bandits did not trouble his territory. Cao's lanes had been cramped and its government offices dilapidated. He widened the market streets, rebuilt the official quarters, and officials and commoners alike benefited. [Note: (From the 《Song shi》, biography of Wang Zan: When Shizong held Cao, he heard criminal cases every ten days. Wang Zan cited statutes and analyzed each case with sound reasoning. On inquiry Shizong learned that he had pursued scholarship, and at once gave him a senior appointment.)] In the first month of the second year Murong Yanchao of Yan Prefecture rebelled. Rong repeatedly petitioned to lead the campaign, and Taizu praised his zeal. When Cao Ying and others had campaigned in the east for months without success, Taizu wished to take the field himself. He summoned the ministers to discuss the matter. Chief Minister Feng Dao argued that it was high summer and the imperial carriage should not brave the heat. Taizu said, "Rebels must not be trifled with." If I cannot go myself, then let my boy at Cao strike the rebels. That will settle the matter." Wang Jun, Commissioner of the Bureau of Military Affairs, did not want Rong to command troops, so Taizu took the field himself. In the sixth month Yan Prefecture was pacified. In the twelfth month he was further appointed Acting Grand Tutor and Associate Director of the Imperial Secretariat. In the first month of the third year he came to court for an audience. In the third month he was made Prefect of Kaifeng and Commissioner of Meritorious Deeds, and enfeoffed as Prince of Jin.
3
二月庚戌，潞州奏，河東劉崇與契丹大將軍楊袞，舉兵南指。 壬戌，宰臣馮道率百僚上表請御殿，凡三上，允之。 丁卯，以中書令馮道充山陵使，太常卿田敏充禮儀使，兵部尚書張昭充鹵簿使，御史中丞張煦充儀仗使，開封少尹、權判府事王敏充橋道使。 河東賊將張暉率前鋒自團柏谷入寇，帝召群臣議親征。 宰臣馮道等奏，以劉崇自平陽奔遁之後，勢弱氣奪，未有復振之理，竊慮聲言自來，以誤於我，陛下纂嗣之初，先帝山陵有日，人心易搖，不宜輕舉，命將禦寇，深以為便。 帝曰：「劉崇幸我大喪，聞我新立，自謂良便，必發狂謀，謂天下可取，謂神器可圖，此際必來，斷無疑耳！ 」馮道等以帝銳於親征，因固諍之。 帝曰：「昔唐太宗之創業，靡不親征，朕何憚焉！ 」道曰：「陛下未可便學太宗。 」帝又曰：「劉崇烏合之眾，茍遇王師，必如山壓卵耳。 」道曰：「不知陛下作得山否？ 」帝不悅而罷。 詔諸道募山林亡命之徒有勇力者送於闕下，仍目之為強人。 帝以趫捷勇猛之士多出於群盜中，故令所在招納，有應命者，即貸其罪，以禁衛處之。 至有朝行殺奪，暮升軍籍，讎人遇之不敢仰視。 帝意亦患之，其後頗有不獲宥者。
On the gengxu day of the second month Lu Prefecture reported that Liu Chong of Hedong and the Khitan Grand General Yang Gun had raised troops and were marching south. On the renxu day Chief Minister Feng Dao led the hundred officials in submitting a memorial asking him to hold court. They submitted three times before he assented. On the dingmao day Feng Dao was made Commissioner for the Imperial Tomb, Tian Min Commissioner for Ritual, Zhang Zhao Commissioner for the Imperial Train, Zhang Xu Commissioner for Ceremonial Arms, and Wang Min, Junior Prefect of Kaifeng and acting administrator, Commissioner for Bridges and Roads. The Hedong rebel general Zhang Hui led the vanguard through Tuanbai Valley. The emperor summoned the ministers to discuss a personal campaign. Chief Minister Feng Dao and others argued that since Liu Chong had fled Pingyang in panic, his forces were spent and demoralized and could not recover. They feared the reports might be misleading. With Your Majesty newly enthroned and the former emperor's funeral imminent, morale was fragile and a rash move was unwise; sending generals to repel the enemy would be far safer. The emperor said, "Liu Chong will seize on our mourning and on my recent accession. He will think the moment perfect, hatch reckless schemes, and imagine he can seize the realm and the throne. He is certain to come now—there is no doubt of it!" " Feng Dao and the others, seeing how determined he was to take the field, remonstrated all the more firmly. The emperor said, "When Emperor Taizong of Tang built his empire, he campaigned in person at every turn. What have I to fear!" " Feng Dao said, "Your Majesty cannot yet simply take Taizong as your model." " The emperor added, "Liu Chong's rabble, if they meet the imperial army, will be crushed like a mountain falling on an egg." " Feng Dao replied, "I wonder whether Your Majesty can be that mountain?" " The emperor was displeased and ended the discussion. An edict ordered every circuit to recruit fugitives and outlaws from the hills who had strength and courage and send them to court. They were called "strong men." He believed that the swiftest and fiercest fighters often came from outlaw bands, so he ordered recruitment everywhere. Those who answered had their crimes pardoned and were placed in the palace guard. Some robbed and killed in the morning and were enrolled in the army by evening. Their enemies did not dare meet their eyes. The emperor himself grew troubled by this, and afterward many were not pardoned.
4
三月丁丑，潞州奏，河東劉崇入寇，兵馬監押穆令均部下兵士為賊軍所襲，官軍不利。 詔天雄軍節度使符彥卿領兵自磁州固鎮路赴潞州，以澶州節度使郭崇副之。 詔河中節度使王彥超領兵取晉州路東向邀擊，以陜府節度使韓通為副。 命宣徽使向訓、馬軍都指揮使樊愛能、步軍都指揮使何徽、滑州節度使白重贊、鄭州防禦使史彥超、前耀州團練使符彥能等，領兵先赴澤州。 辛巳，制：「大赦天下，常赦所不原者，咸赦除之。 諸貶降責授官，量與升陟敘用，應配流徒役人，並放逐便。 諸道州府所欠去年夏秋租稅並放。 內外見任、前文武職官並與加恩，父母在者並與恩澤，亡沒者與封贈，其母妻未敘者，特與敘封」云。 前涇州節度使史匡懿卒。
On the dingchou day of the third month Lu Prefecture reported that Liu Chong of Hedong had invaded. Troops under Military Supervisor Mu Lingjun were ambushed and the government forces suffered defeat. Fu Yanqing, Military Commissioner of the Tianxiong Army, was ordered to lead troops from Cizhou along the Guzhen route toward Lu, with Guo Chong of Cao as his deputy. Wang Yanchao of Hezhong was ordered to advance along the Jin Prefecture route and intercept from the east, with Han Tong of Shaanfu as deputy. Xiang Xun, Fan Aineng, He Hui, Bai Chongzan, Shi Yanchao, Fu Yanneng, and others were ordered to advance first to Ze Prefecture. On the xinsi day an ordinance proclaimed a great amnesty for the realm, pardoning even offenses normally excluded from routine amnesties. Demoted and reassigned officials were to be promoted and reemployed as appropriate. All those in exile, penal servitude, or corvée were to be released and sent home. Unpaid summer and autumn rent and tax from the previous year owed by every circuit, prefecture, and district were remitted. All serving and former civil and military officials received added favors. Living parents received grants of favor; the deceased received posthumous titles; mothers and wives not yet ennobled received special enfeoffment," the ordinance stated. The former Military Commissioner of Jing Prefecture, Shi Kuangyi, died.
5
癸未，詔以劉崇入寇，車駕取今月十一日親征。 甲申，以樞密使鄭仁誨為東京留守。 乙酉，車駕發京師。 壬辰，至澤州。 癸巳，王師與河東劉崇、契丹楊袞大戰於高平，賊軍敗績。 初，車駕行次河陽，聞劉崇自潞而南，即倍程而進。 是月十八日至澤州，既晡，帝御戎服，觀兵於東北郊，距州十五里，夜宿於村舍。 十九日，先鋒與賊軍相遇，賊陣於高平縣南之高原。 有賊中來者，云：「劉崇自將騎三萬，並契丹萬餘騎，嚴陣以待官軍。 」帝促兵以擊之，崇東西列陣，頗亦嚴整。 乃令侍衛馬步軍都虞候李重進、滑州節度使白重贊將左，居陣之西廂； 侍衛馬軍都指揮使樊愛能、步軍都指揮使何徽將右，居陣之東廂； 宣徽使向訓、鄭州防禦使史彥超，以精騎當其中； 殿前都指揮使張永德以禁兵衛蹕。 帝介馬觀戰。 兩軍交鋒，未幾，樊愛能、何徽望賊而遁，東廂騎軍亂，步軍解甲投賊，帝乃自率親騎，臨陣督戰。 〈 （《隆平集·馬仁瑀傳》：從世宗親征劉崇，王師不利，仁瑀謂眾曰：「主辱臣死！ 」因躍馬大呼，引弓連斃將卒數十，士氣始振。）〉 今上馳騎於陣前，先犯其鋒，戰士皆奮命爭先，賊軍大敗。 日暮，賊萬餘人阻澗而陣，會劉詞領兵至，與大軍迫之，賊軍又潰，臨陣斬賊大將張暉及偽樞密使王延嗣。 諸將分兵追襲，僵屍棄甲填滿山谷。 初夜，官軍至高平，降賊軍數千人，所獲輜重、兵器、駝馬、偽乘輿器服等不可勝紀。 其夕，殺降軍二千餘人，我軍之降敵者亦皆就戮。 初，兩軍之未整也，風自東北起，不便於我，及與賊軍相遇，風勢陡回，人情相悅。 戰之前夕，有大星如日，流行數丈，墜於賊營之上。 及戰，北人望見官軍之上，有雲氣如龍虎之狀，則天之助順，亶其然乎！ 是日，危急之勢頃刻莫保，賴帝英武果敢，親臨寇敵，不然則社稷幾若綴旒矣。 是夕，帝宿於野次。 甲午，次高平縣。 詔賜河東降軍二千餘人各絹二匹，並給其衣裝，鄉兵各給絹一匹，放還本部。 是日大雨。 戊戌，車駕至潞州。 河南府上言，前青州節度使常思卒。 己亥，侍衛馬軍都指揮使、夔州節度使樊愛能，侍衛步軍都指揮使、壽州節度使何徽等並諸將校七十餘人，並伏誅。 高平之役，兩軍既成列，賊騎來挑戰，愛能望風而退，何徽以徒兵陣於後，為奔騎所突，即時潰亂，二將南走。 帝遣近臣宣諭止遏，莫肯從命，皆揚言曰：「官軍大敗，餘眾已解甲矣。 」至暮，以官軍克捷，方稍稍而回。 帝至潞州，錄其奔遁者，自軍使以上及監押使臣並斬之，由是驕將墮兵，無不知懼。 帝以何徽有平陽守禦之功，欲貸其罪，竟不可，與愛能俱殺之，皆給槥車歸葬。 〈 （《東都事略》：世宗謂張永德曰：「樊愛能及偏裨七十餘人，吾欲盡按軍法，何如？ 」對曰：「必欲開拓疆宇，威加四海，安可已也！ 」世宗善其言，悉誅愛能等以徇，軍聲始振。）〉
On the guiwei day an edict announced that because Liu Chong had invaded, the emperor would take the field in person on the eleventh of the month. On the jiashen day Zheng Renhui, Commissioner of the Bureau of Military Affairs, was made Military Governor of the Eastern Capital. On the yiyou day the emperor departed the capital. On the renchen day he reached Ze Prefecture. On the guisi day the imperial army fought Liu Chong of Hedong and the Khitan Yang Gun in a great battle at Gaoping. The rebel army was routed. When the emperor halted at Heyang and heard that Liu Chong was marching south from Lu, he immediately pressed forward at double speed. He reached Ze on the eighteenth. In late afternoon he put on military dress and reviewed the troops fifteen li northeast of the city, then spent the night in a farmhouse. On the nineteenth the vanguard met the rebels, who drew up on the high ground south of Gaoping County. A man came from the rebel camp and reported, "Liu Chong commands thirty thousand cavalry in person, with more than ten thousand Khitan horsemen besides, all drawn up in tight formation awaiting the imperial army." " The emperor pressed the attack. Liu Chong deployed his forces in east-west lines, likewise in tight order. He ordered Chief Commandant Li Chongjin and Bai Chongzan of Hua to command the left wing on the western flank; Fan Aineng and He Hui to command the right wing on the eastern flank; Xiang Xun and Shi Yanchao of Zheng to hold elite cavalry in the center; Zhang Yongde to guard the imperial procession with palace troops. The emperor watched from horseback in armor. Soon after the armies clashed, Fan Aineng and He Hui fled at sight of the enemy. The eastern cavalry collapsed and the infantry threw down their armor and surrendered. The emperor then led his personal guard to the front to direct the fighting himself. [Note: (From the 《Longping ji》, biography of Ma Renyu: On Shizong's campaign against Liu Chong, when the imperial army faltered, Renyu cried to the troops, "When the lord is shamed, the minister must die!" " He spurred his horse forward, shouting, and with his bow killed dozens of enemy officers and men in succession until the army's spirit revived.)] The present sovereign charged to the front and struck the enemy vanguard first. Every soldier fought to outdo the rest, and the rebel army was utterly defeated. At dusk more than ten thousand rebels held a stream and made a stand. Liu Ci arrived with reinforcements and, with the main army, pressed them until they broke again. The rebel generals Zhang Hui and the false Commissioner Wang Yansi were beheaded on the field. The generals split their forces in pursuit. Corpses and discarded armor filled the valleys. In the first watch the army reached Gaoping. Several thousand rebels surrendered. Baggage, weapons, camels, horses, and counterfeit imperial regalia were captured beyond count. That evening more than two thousand surrendered rebels were put to death, and enemy soldiers who had surrendered to the imperial side were also executed. Before the armies were fully drawn up, a wind rose from the northeast to our disadvantage. When the enemy was met, the wind suddenly shifted, and morale lifted throughout the ranks. On the eve of battle a great star bright as the sun streaked across the sky and fell on the rebel camp. During the fight northerners saw cloud shapes like dragons and tigers above the imperial army. Heaven truly favors the righteous cause—was there ever any doubt! That day disaster hung by a thread. Only the emperor's martial courage in facing the enemy in person saved the dynasty from hanging by a thread. That night the emperor camped in the open field. On the jiawu day he halted at Gaoping County. An edict granted each of the more than two thousand surrendered Hedong troops two bolts of silk and restored their clothing. Local militiamen each received one bolt and were sent home. That day brought heavy rain. On the wuxu day the emperor reached Lu Prefecture. Henan Prefecture reported that the former Military Commissioner of Qing Prefecture, Chang Si, had died. On the jihai day Fan Aineng, Cavalry Commander-in-Chief and Military Commissioner of Kui, He Hui, Infantry Commander-in-Chief and Military Commissioner of Shou, and more than seventy other generals and officers were all executed. At Gaoping, once the armies were drawn up, rebel cavalry came to challenge. Fan Aineng fled at the first sign of trouble. He Hui's infantry in the rear was overrun by fleeing horsemen and broke at once. Both generals fled south. The emperor sent close attendants to order them to stop, but they would not obey. All shouted, "The imperial army is routed—the rest have already thrown down their arms." " Only at dusk, when the imperial army had won, did they slowly straggle back. At Lu Prefecture he recorded every fugitive from army commanders and supervisory commissioners upward and had them beheaded. After that no arrogant general or lax soldier failed to fear the consequences. Because He Hui had merit in defending Pingyang, the emperor wished to spare him, but in the end could not. He was executed together with Fan Aineng, and both were given coffin carts for burial at home. [Note: (From the 《Outline of the Eastern Capital》: Shizong said to Zhang Yongde, "Fan Aineng and more than seventy junior officers—I want to punish them all under military law. What do you think? " Yongde replied, "If you mean to expand the realm and impose your authority on all under heaven, how can you hold back now!" " Shizong approved and had Fan Aineng and the rest executed as a public example. Only then did army discipline begin to recover.)]

On the first day of the sixth month, the guimao day, an edict ordered withdrawal; the emperor departed Taiyuan. At that time troops and levies had been massed in great numbers, and tens of thousands of laborers from east of the mountains and from Huai, Meng, Pu, and Shaan had been conscripted for an urgent assault expected to take the city within days. Then seasonal heavy rains fell, the soldiers were exhausted, and the force at Xinkou had faltered; the emperor thereupon decided to withdraw. The withdrawal was hurried and confused; ranks broke down and discipline collapsed; unruly men spread alarming rumors; some supplies were lost on the march; and beneath the rebel walls several hundred thousand units of grain and fodder were burned and abandoned. [Note: (The 《Examination of Variations in the Comprehensive Mirror》 cites the 《Records of What Was Seen at Jinyang》: On the first day of the sixth month Zhou troops turned south to withdraw. From the 《History of Song》, biography of Yao Yuanfu: When an edict ordered withdrawal, Yuanfu submitted a memorial saying, "Advancing the army is very easy; withdrawing the army is very difficult." " Shizong said, "I leave it entirely to you." The troops were formed into square columns and marched south, with Yuanfu and his own command guarding the rear. Chong indeed sent troops in pursuit; Yuanfu struck and drove them off.)] On the yisi day the emperor reached Lu Prefecture. On the guichou day the emperor departed Lu Prefecture. On the yichou day the emperor visited Xinzheng County. On the bingyin day the emperor personally paid homage at Songling, performed the sacrificial rites, and withdrew. The tomb garrison and nearby households received silk grants in varying amounts. [Note: (From the 《Institutional Essentials of the Five Dynasties》: In the second month of the first year of Xiande, when the emperor returned from the campaign against Taiyuan, he personally paid homage at Songling and, gazing at the tomb, wailed in grief. When he reached the tomb site, he prostrated himself and wept in sorrow, moving those around him; when he had bowed twice, he performed the sacrificial rites and withdrew.)] On the gengwu day the emperor returned from Hedong.

On the gengchen day the emperor visited Nanzhuang. On the xinsi day Gao Baorong, Military Commissioner of Jingnan and Prince of Nanping, was additionally made Guardian Director of the Imperial Secretariat; Li Yixing, Military Commissioner of Xia and Prince of Xiping, Guardian Grand Guardian; Wu Xingde, Military Governor of the Western Capital, Wang Yan of Xu, and Hou Zhang of Deng were all additionally made Directors of the Imperial Secretariat. On the guimao day Wang Jinkui of Hunan was additionally made Director of the Imperial Secretariat; Guo Xun, Military Commissioner of the Tiande Army, Zhe Congruan of Bin, and Li Hongyi of An were all additionally made Palace Attendants; Sun Fangjian, former Military Commissioner of Hua, was made Military Commissioner of Tong and additionally made Director of the Imperial Secretariat concurrently; Wang Renhao, former Military Commissioner of the Yongxing Army, was made Military Commissioner of Hezhong and additionally made Honorary Grand Marshal. On the yiyou day Li Hui of Cang, Wang Rao of Bei, and Cao Ying of Zhen were all additionally made Palace Attendants concurrently; Zhang Duo of Jing, Wang Jin of Xiang, and Yuan Zhi of Yan were all additionally made Honorary Grand Marshal. On the renchen day the hundred officials submitted a memorial requesting that the emperor's birthday, the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, be established as the Tianqing Festival. The request was granted. On the guisi day Fan Zhi, Left Vice Director concurrently Vice Director of the Chancellery, Director of the Imperial Secretariat, and Supervisor of the Compilation of National History, was made Guardian Grand Tutor concurrently Vice Director of the Chancellery, Director of the Imperial Secretariat, and Grand Academician of the Hongwen Hall; [Note: (The 《Guolao tanyuan》 records that Taizu of Zhou once sent Shizong to call on Fan Zhi. At the time Shizong was still a prince; his carriage was so tall the gate could not take it, so he dismounted at once and walked in. After he succeeded to the throne, he said to Fan Zhi with easy familiarity, "Is this your old residence? How very small the gate tower is! " He thereupon had a mansion built for him.)] Li Gu, Left Vice Director concurrently Vice Director of the Secretariat, Director of the Imperial Secretariat, Grand Academician of the Jixian Hall, and Administrator of the Three Offices, was made Guardian Grand Tutor concurrently Vice Director of the Chancellery, Director of the Imperial Secretariat, and Supervisor of the Compilation of National History; Wang Pu, Vice Director of the Secretariat and Director of the Imperial Secretariat, was made Vice Director of the Secretariat concurrently Minister of Rites, Director of the Imperial Secretariat, and Grand Academician of the Jixian Hall; Jing Fan, Academician of the Bureau of Military Affairs and Vice Director of the Ministry of Works, was made Vice Director of the Secretariat, Director of the Imperial Secretariat, and Administrator of the Three Offices; Zheng Renhui, Commissioner of Military Affairs, Honorary Grand Guardian, and Associate Director of the Imperial Secretariat, was additionally made Palace Attendant concurrently; Feng Jiye of Lingwu, Sun Xingyou of Ding, and Tian Jingxian of Xing were all additionally made Honorary Grand Tutor; Yang Tingzhang of Jin was additionally made Honorary Grand Guardian; Zhao Shangjiao, Supervisor of the Household of the Heir Apparent, was made Guest of the Heir Apparent. On the yiwei day Wei Renpu, Deputy Commissioner of Military Affairs and Major General of the Right Gate Guard Command, was made Commissioner of Military Affairs and Honorary Grand Guardian. [Note: (From the 《Outline of the Eastern Capital》: Critics objected that Renpu had not risen through the civil service examinations. Shizong said, "What matters is talent! " He thereupon appointed him.)] On the bingshen day Liu Wensou, Drafting Officer of the Secretariat, Compiler of the History Office, and Administrator of the Office, was made Vice Director of the Ministry of Rites; his administration of the office remained as before. On the dingyou day Wang Jin, Military Commissioner of Xiang, died.
11
八月壬申朔，以宣徽北院使吳延祚為右監門衛大將軍充職，以樞密院直學士、尚書右丞邊歸讜為尚書左丞充職。 甲辰，幸南莊，賜從臣射。 乙巳，以吏部侍郎顏衎為工部尚書致仕。 丙午，同州節度使孫方諫卒。 己酉，前澤州刺史李彥崇責授右司禦副率。 高平之役，帝與賊軍相遇，即令彥崇領兵守江豬嶺，以遏寇之歸路，彥崇初見王師已卻，即時而退，及劉崇兵敗，果由茲嶺而遁，故有是責。 壬子，以金州防禦使王暉為同州留後。 癸丑，以吳越國內外都指揮使吳延福為寧國軍節度使、檢校太尉，從錢俶之請也。 以太子少師宋彥筠為太子太師致仕。 甲寅，以兵部郎中兼太常博士尹拙為國子祭酒。 丙辰，皇姑故福慶長公主追封燕國大長公主，李重進之母也。 丁巳，以戶部郎中致仕景初為太僕卿致仕，宰臣範之父也。 己巳，宜停華州鎮國軍，依舊為郡。 庚午，以給事中劉悅、康澄並為右散騎常侍。 辛未，以左散騎常侍裴巽為御史中丞，以御史中丞張煦為兵部侍郎，集賢殿學士、判院事司徒詡為吏部侍郎，以左散騎常侍薛沖乂為工部侍郎。
On the first day of the eighth month, the renshen day, Wu Yanzuo, Northern Court Palace Commissioner, was made Major General of the Right Gate Guard Command to fill the post; Bian Guidan, Direct Academician of the Bureau of Military Affairs and Right Vice Director of the Ministry of Personnel, was made Left Vice Director of the Ministry of Personnel to fill the post. On the jiachen day the emperor visited Nanzhuang and granted the accompanying officials an archery contest. On the yisi day Yan Kan, Vice Director of the Ministry of Personnel, was made Minister of Works and retired. On the bingwu day Sun Fangjian, Military Commissioner of Tong, died. On the jiyou day Li Yanchong, former Prefect of Ze, was demoted and appointed Deputy Commandant of the Right Imperial Guard. During the Gaoping campaign, when the emperor met the rebel army, he at once ordered Yanchong to lead troops to hold Jiangzhu Ridge and block the enemy's retreat. Yanchong, seeing the imperial army withdraw at first, retreated at once; when Liu Chong's army was defeated, it fled by that very ridge—hence this punishment. On the renzi day Wang Hui, Defender of Jin, was made Acting Commander of Tong. On the guichou day Wu Yanfu, Overall Commander-in-Chief of Wuyue, was made Military Commissioner of the Ningguo Army and Honorary Grand Marshal, at Qian Chu's request. Song Yanjun, Supervisor of the Heir Apparent, was made Grand Preceptor of the Heir Apparent and retired. On the jiayin day Yin Zhuo, Bureau Director of the Ministry of War concurrently Erudite of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, was made Director of the National University. On the bingchen day the late Princess Fuchang, the emperor's paternal aunt, was posthumously enfeoffed as Grand Princess of Yan; she was Li Chongjin's mother. On the dingsi day Jing Chu, retired Bureau Director of the Ministry of Revenue, was made Minister of Imperial Studs and retired; he was Chief Minister Fan's father. On the jisi day it was ordered to abolish the Zhenguo Army at Hua and restore the prefecture as a commandery as before. On the gengwu day Liu Yue, Supervising Censor, and Kang Cheng were both made Right Regular Attendant of the Cavalry. On the xinwei day Pei Xun, Left Regular Attendant of the Cavalry, was made Vice Censor-in-Chief; Zhang Xu, Vice Censor-in-Chief, Vice Director of the Ministry of War; Situ Xu, Academician of the Jixian Hall and Administrator of the Academy, Vice Director of the Ministry of Personnel; Xue Chongyi, Left Regular Attendant of the Cavalry, Vice Director of the Ministry of Works.
12
九月壬申朔，以東京舊宅為皇建禪院。 甲戌，以武安軍節度副使、知潭州軍府事周行逢為鄂州節度使，知潭州軍府事，加檢校太尉。 丙戌，右屯衛將軍薛訓除名，流沙門島，坐監雍兵倉，縱吏卒掊斂也。 己亥，以右僕射致仕韓昭裔、左僕射致仕楊凝式並為太子太保致仕，以太子太傅致仕李肅為太子太師致仕。 辛丑，斬宋州巡檢供奉官、副都知竹奉璘於寧陵縣，坐盜掠商船不捕獲也。
On the first day of the ninth month, the renshen day, the old residence in the Eastern Capital was made the Huangjian Chan Monastery. On the jiaxu day Zhou Xingfeng, Deputy Military Commissioner of the Wu'an Army and Administrator of Tan, was made Military Commissioner of E while continuing to administer Tan, and additionally made Honorary Grand Marshal. On the bingxu day Xue Xun, General of the Right Encampment Guard, was stripped of rank and exiled to Shamen Island for supervising the Yong military granary and allowing clerks and soldiers to extort. On the jihai day Han Zhaoyi, retired Right Vice Director, and Yang Ningshi, retired Left Vice Director, were both made Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent and retired; Li Su, retired Grand Mentor of the Heir Apparent, was made Grand Preceptor of the Heir Apparent and retired. On the xinchou day Zhu Fenglin, Patrol Inspector of Song, Palace Attendant, and Deputy Supervisor, was beheaded at Ningling County for plundering merchant ships while serving in that post.
13
冬十月甲辰，左羽林大將軍孟漢卿賜死，坐監納厚取耗餘也。 丙午，以安州節度使李洪義為青州節度使，以貝州節度使王饒為相州節度使，以徐州節度使王晏為西京留守，以西京留守武行德為徐州節度使。 戊申，以龍捷左廂都指揮使、泗州防禦使韓令坤為洋州節度使，充侍衛馬軍都指揮使； 以虎捷右廂都指揮使、永州防禦使李繼勛為利州節度使，充侍衛步軍都指揮使。 己酉，太子太保致仕楊凝式卒。 詔安、貝二州依舊為防禦州，其軍額並停。 壬子，以今上為永州防禦使，依前殿前都虞候。 戊午，監修國史李穀等上言曰：「竊以自古王者鹹建史官，君臣獻替之謀皆須備載，家國安危之道得以直書。 歷代已來，其名不一。 人君言動，則起居註創於累朝； 輔相經綸，則時政記興於前代。 然後采其事實，編作史書。 蓋緣聞見之間須有來處，記錄之際得以審詳。 今之左右起居郎，即古之左右史也。 唐文宗朝，命其官執筆，立於殿階螭頭之下，以紀政事。 後則明宗朝，命端明殿及樞密直學士，皆輪修日曆，旋送史官，以備纂修。 及近朝，此事皆廢，史官惟憑百司報狀，館司但取兩省制書，此外雖有訪聞，例非端的。 伏自先皇帝創開昌運，及皇帝陛下纘嗣丕基，其聖德武功、神謀睿略，而皆萬幾宥密，丹禁深嚴，非外臣之所知，豈庶僚之可訪。 此後欲望以諮詢之事、裁制之規，別命近臣旋具抄錄，每當修撰日曆，即令封付史臣，庶國事無漏略之文，職業免疏遺之咎。 」從之。 因命樞密直學士，起今後於樞密使處，逐月抄錄事件，送付史館。 己未，供奉官郝光庭棄市，坐在葉縣巡檢日，挾私斷殺平人也。 是日大閱，帝親臨之。 帝自高平之役，睹諸軍未甚嚴整，遂有退卻，至是命今上一概簡閱，選武藝超絕者，署為殿前諸班，因是有散員、散指揮使、內殿直、散都頭、鐵騎、控鶴之號。 復命總戎者，自龍捷、虎捷以降，一一選之，老弱羸小者去之，諸軍士伍，無不精當。 由是兵甲之盛，近代無比，且減冗食之費焉。 〈 （《五代會要》：顯德元年，上謂侍臣曰：「侍衛兵士老少相半，強懦不分，蓋徇人情，不能選練。 今春朕在高平，與劉崇及蕃軍相遇，臨敵有指使不前者，茍非朕親當堅陣，幾至喪敗。 況百戶農夫，未能贍一甲士，且兵在精不在眾，宜令一一點選，精銳者升在上軍，怯懦者任從安便，庶期可用，又不虛費。 」先是，上按於高平，觀其退縮，慨然有懲革之志。 又以驍勇之士，多為外諸侯所占，如是召募天下豪傑，不以草澤為阻，在於闕下，躬親試閱，選武藝超絕及有身首者，分署為殿前諸班。）〉
On the jiachen day in the tenth month of winter Meng Hanqing, Major General of the Left Forest Guard, was granted death for supervising tax collection and taking excessive surplus allowances. On the bingwu day Li Hongyi, Military Commissioner of An, was made Military Commissioner of Qing; Wang Rao of Bei, Military Commissioner of Xiang; Wang Yan of Xu, Military Governor of the Western Capital; Wu Xingde, Military Governor of the Western Capital, Military Commissioner of Xu. On the wushen day Han Lingkun, Commander of the Left Wing of the Dragon Swift Army and Defender of Si, was made Military Commissioner of Yang and appointed Commander of the Palace Guard cavalry; Li Jixun, Commander of the Right Wing of the Tiger Swift Army and Defender of Yong, was made Military Commissioner of Li and appointed Commander of the Palace Guard infantry. On the jiyou day Yang Ningshi, retired Grand Guardian of the Heir Apparent, died. An edict ordered that An and Bei be restored as defensive prefectures as before, and their army designations were all abolished. On the renzi day the present sovereign was made Defender of Yong, retaining his previous post as Front Palace Deputy Commander. On the wuwu day Li Gu, Supervisor of the Compilation of National History, and others submitted a memorial stating, "We presume that since antiquity kings and lords have all established historiographers; the counsel exchanged between ruler and minister must all be fully recorded, so that the paths of state and family's safety and peril can be written plainly. Through successive ages the names have not been uniform. For a ruler's words and actions, the Diary of Activity and Repose was instituted across many dynasties; For a chancellor's statecraft, the Record of Current Administration arose in earlier ages. Only then were facts gathered and compiled into historical works. This was because what was heard and seen had to have a source, and recording could then be thorough and precise. Today's Left and Right Attendants for the Diary of Activity and Repose are the ancient Left and Right Scribes. Under Tang Emperor Wenzong, these officials were ordered to hold the brush and stand below the ch'i-head of the palace steps to record affairs of state. Later, under Emperor Mingzong, Direct Academicians of the Duanming Hall and the Bureau of Military Affairs took turns compiling the daily calendar and promptly sending it to the historiographers for compilation. In recent courts this practice has been abandoned; historiographers rely only on reports from the hundred offices, and the archive office takes only edicts from the two secretariats. Beyond that, though there may be inquiries and hearsay, as a rule it is not reliable. Humbly, since the late emperor opened a splendid destiny and Your Majesty succeeded to the great foundation, his sagely virtue and martial achievement, divine stratagems and wise designs—all are matters of myriad affairs conducted in the inner sanctum, the forbidden precinct deeply strict. They are not what outer ministers can know—how could lesser officials be consulted? Hereafter we wish that matters of consultation and rules of decision may be separately assigned to nearby ministers to copy down promptly; whenever the daily calendar is compiled, have these sealed and delivered to the historiographers, so that state affairs may lack no omitted passages and our office may escape the fault of negligence. " His request was granted. Thereupon Direct Academicians of the Bureau of Military Affairs were ordered that from then on, at the Commissioner of Military Affairs' office, they should each month copy down events and send them to the History Office. On the jiwei day Hao Guangting, Palace Attendant, was executed in the marketplace for killing a commoner in a private vendetta while serving as patrol inspector in Ye County. That day a grand review was held, and the emperor personally attended. Since the Gaoping campaign, the emperor had seen that the armies were not very disciplined and had retreated; at this time he ordered the present sovereign to review them comprehensively, selecting those of surpassing martial skill and appointing them to the various Front Palace companies—hence the designations Irregular Attendants, Irregular Commanders, Inner Hall Direct, Irregular Squad Chiefs, Iron Cavalry, and Cranes Controlling. He also ordered the commanders-in-chief, from the Dragon Swift and Tiger Swift armies downward, to select one by one and remove the old, weak, and frail, until every rank and file in the armies was fit and proper. Hence the army's strength was unmatched in recent times, and superfluous consumption was reduced as well. [Note: (From the 《Institutional Essentials of the Five Dynasties》: In the first year of Xiande, the emperor said to attending ministers, "Palace guard soldiers are half old and half young, strong and weak undifferentiated—presumably indulging human sentiment, unable to select and drill. This spring I was at Gaoping and met Liu Chong and the barbarian army. Facing the enemy, some commanders would not advance—had I not personally held the firm line, we would nearly have suffered defeat. Moreover, a hundred farming households cannot support one armored soldier; troops lie in quality, not numbers. They should be selected one by one—the elite promoted to the upper army, the timid allowed to take convenient posts, so that we may expect usefulness and not waste resources in vain. " Earlier, when the emperor reviewed troops at Gaoping and saw them withdraw, he resolved to reform and punish. Also, because valiant warriors were largely taken by outer feudatories, he recruited heroes throughout the realm without regard to humble origins; at the capital he personally tested and reviewed them, selecting those of surpassing martial skill and stature and assigning them to the various Front Palace companies.)]
14
十一月戊寅，以太子賓客石光贊為兵部尚書致仕。 壬午，鎮州節度使曹英卒。 乙酉，以澶州節度使郭崇為鎮州節度使。 乙未，以荊南節度副使、歸州刺史高保勖為寧江軍節度使、檢校太尉，充荊南節度行軍司馬。 戊戌，詔宰臣李穀監築河堤。 先是，鄆州界河決，數州之地洪流為患，故命穀治之。 役丁夫六萬人，三十日而罷。
On the wuyin day in the eleventh month Shi Guangzan, Guest of the Heir Apparent, was made Minister of War and retired. On the renwu day Cao Ying, Military Commissioner of Zhen, died. On the yiyou day Guo Chong, Military Commissioner of Cao, was made Military Commissioner of Zhen. On the yiwei day Gao Baoxu, Deputy Military Commissioner of Jingnan and Prefect of Gui, was made Military Commissioner of the Ningjiang Army and Honorary Grand Marshal, and appointed Acting Chief Administrator of Jingnan. On the wuxu day an edict ordered Chief Minister Li Gu to supervise construction of the river embankment. Earlier the Yellow River had burst its banks in the border of Yun, and several prefectures suffered from flooding—therefore Gu was ordered to remedy it. Sixty thousand corvée laborers were mobilized, and the work was finished in thirty days.
15
十二月己酉，太子太師侯益以本官致仕。
On the jiyou day in the twelfth month Hou Yi, Grand Preceptor of the Heir Apparent, retired in his present office.
