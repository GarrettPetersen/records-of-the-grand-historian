史宏肇，字化元，鄭州滎澤人也。 父潘，本田家。 宏肇少遊俠無行，拳勇健步，日行二百里，走及奔馬。 梁末，每七戶出一兵，宏肇在籍中，後隸本州開道都，選入禁軍。 嘗在晉祖麾下，遂留為親從，及踐阼，用為控鶴小校。 高祖鎮太原，奏請從行，升為牙校，後置武節左右指揮，以宏肇為都將，遙領雷州刺史。 高祖建號之初，代州王暉叛，以城歸契丹，宏肇征之，一鼓而拔，尋授許州節度使，充侍衛步軍都指揮使。 會王守恩以上黨求附，契丹主命大將耿崇美率眾登太行，欲取上黨，高祖命宏肇以軍應援。 軍至潞州，契丹退去，翟令奇以澤州迎降。 會河陽武行德遣人迎宏肇，遂率眾南下，與行德合。 故高祖由蒲、陜赴洛如歸，宏肇前鋒之功也。
Shi Hongzhao, whose style was Huayuan, came from Xingze in Zhengzhou. His father Pan had originally been registered as a farming household. In his youth Hongzhao lived as an unruly bravo, famed for his fists and his legs: he could cover two hundred li in a day and keep pace with a running horse. When the Later Liang was failing, every seven households had to supply one soldier; Hongzhao was conscripted, later assigned to the prefectural Kaodao command, and then chosen for the imperial guard. He had served under the Jin founder and stayed on as a personal attendant; when that ruler acceded, Hongzhao was appointed a junior officer in the Cranes Controlling Guard. When Gaozu governed Taiyuan he asked that Hongzhao march with him and raised him to aide-de-camp; later, when the Left and Right Commands of Martial Valor were formed, Hongzhao became their commander-in-chief and was given Leizhou in absentia. Soon after Gaozu founded the dynasty, Wang Hui of Daizhou rebelled and handed his city to the Khitan; Hongzhao attacked, took it at the first blow, and was soon made military governor of Xuzhou and commander of the palace guard infantry. When Wang Shou'en offered Shangdang in submission, the Khitan ruler sent the general Geng Chongmei over the Taihang to take it, and Gaozu ordered Hongzhao to march in relief. At Luzhou the Khitan pulled back, and Zhai Lingqi surrendered Zezhou to the Han forces. Wu Xingde of Heyang then sent envoys to welcome Hongzhao, who marched south and joined forces with him. That Gaozu could travel from Pu and Shaan to Luoyang as if he were merely going home was largely Hongzhao's work as the vanguard.

宏肇嚴毅寡言，部轄軍眾，有過無舍，兵士所至，秋毫不犯。 部下有指揮使，嘗因指使少不從命，宏肇立撾殺之，將吏股栗，以至平定兩京，無敢幹忤。 從駕征鄴回，加同平章事，充侍衛親軍都指揮使，兼鎮宋州。 高祖大漸，與樞密使楊邠、周太祖、蘇逢吉等同受顧命。 隱帝嗣位，加檢校太師、兼侍中。 居無何，河中、永興、鳳翔連橫謀叛，關輔大擾，朝廷日有征發，群情憂揣，亦有不逞之徒，妄構虛語，流布京師。 宏肇都轄禁軍，警衛都邑，專行刑殺，略無顧避，無賴之輩，望風匿跡，路有遺棄，人不敢取。 然而不問罪之輕重，理之所在，但雲有犯，便處極刑，枉濫之家，莫敢上訴。 巡司軍吏，因緣為奸，嫁禍脅人，不可勝紀。 〈 （《宋史·邊歸讜傳》：史宏肇怙權專殺，閭裏告訐成風，歸讜言曰：「邇來有匿名書及言風聞事，構害良善，有傷風化，遂使貪吏得以報復私怨，讒夫得以肆其虛誕。 請明行條制，禁遏誣妄，凡顯有披論，具陳姓名。 其匿名書及風聞事者，並見止絕。 」論者韙之。）〉 時太白晝見，民有仰觀者，為坊正所拘，立斷其腰領。 又有醉民抵忤一軍士，則誣以訛言棄市。 其他斷舌、決口、斫筋、折足者，僅無虛日。 故相李崧為部曲誣告，族戮於市，取其幼女為婢。 自是仕宦之家畜仆隸者，皆以姑息為意，而舊勛故將失勢之後，為廝養輩之所脅制者，往往有之。 軍司孔目吏解暉，性狡而酷，凡有推劾，隨意鍛煉。 人有抵軍禁者，被其苦楚，無不自誣以求死所，都人遇之，莫敢仰視。 有燕人何福殷者，以商販為業。 嘗以十四萬市得玉枕，遣家僮及商人李進賣於淮南，易茗而回。 家僮無行，隱福殷貨財數十萬，福殷責其償，不伏，遂杖之。 未幾，家僮詣宏肇上變，言契丹主之入汴也，趙延壽遣福殷賫玉枕陰遺淮南，以致誠意。 宏肇即日遣捕福殷等系之。 解暉希旨，榜掠備至，福殷自誣，連罪者數輩，並棄市。 妻女為宏肇帳下分取之，其家財籍沒。
Hongzhao was severe, taciturn, and relentless with discipline: his men took nothing from the people they passed. Once a subordinate commander balked at a minor order and Hongzhao had him beaten to death on the spot; from that day his officers shook with fear, and through the pacification of the two capitals no one dared cross him. After the Ye campaign he was made co-equal Grand Counsellor, commander of the palace guard, and concurrent governor of Songzhou. As Gaozu lay dying, Hongzhao received the final charge along with Yang Bin, Zhou Taizu, Su Fengji, and the rest. When Emperor Yin came to the throne, Hongzhao was given the honorary rank of Grand Preceptor and made Palace Attendant as well. Before long Hezhong, Yongxing, and Fengxiang were in league to rebel; the Guanzhong heartland was in turmoil, levies followed one after another, and fear spread through the city along with wild rumors invented by malcontents. As head of the palace guard he policed the capital and killed without scruple; thugs vanished at his approach, and even goods left in the street went untouched. He paid no heed to whether a charge was grave or petty or even true: any report of wrongdoing brought death, and families unjustly condemned dared not appeal. Patrolmen and army clerks used the climate to frame rivals and extort the innocent beyond counting. Editorial bracket opening a comment block. (The Song History biography of Bian Guiyan records that under Shi Hongzhao's arbitrary killings, informers flourished in every lane. Guiyan urged the throne: "Anonymous letters and rumor-mongering lately frame honest men and poison public life, letting greedy officials settle private scores and letting slanderers run wild. Issue clear rules against false denunciation: every open charge must name its author. Anonymous letters and hearsay accusations should be stopped altogether." Commentators approved his proposal.) Editorial bracket closing the comment block. When Venus was seen by day, a man who looked up at the sky was seized by his ward officer and beheaded on the spot. A drunk who jostled a soldier was accused of spreading sedition and put to death in the market. Tongues cut out, mouths split, tendons severed, feet broken—hardly a day passed without such punishments. The former chief minister Li Song was framed by his own retainers; his whole clan was slaughtered in the market, and his young daughter was made a servant girl. After that, official households indulged their servants for fear of denunciation, and many once-great generals, fallen from power, found themselves bullied by the very men who waited on them. The army clerk Xie Hui was sly and pitiless; in every case he tortured confessions as he saw fit. Anyone caught in his grip confessed to anything to escape further torture; in the capital people lowered their eyes when he passed. A merchant of Yan named He Fuyin traded for his living. He once paid one hundred forty thousand cash for a jade pillow and sent his servant and a trader named Li Jin to sell it in Huainan for tea. The servant was worthless and embezzled hundreds of thousands from him; when Fuyin demanded repayment the man refused and was beaten. Soon the servant denounced his master to Hongzhao, claiming that when the Khitan took Bian, Zhao Yanshou had sent Fuyin with the jade pillow as a secret gift to Huainan. Hongzhao had Fuyin and the others arrested that very day. Xie Hui tortured them to please his master; Fuyin confessed under the lash, several others were implicated, and all were executed in the market. His wife and daughters were divided among Hongzhao's retainers, and his estate was confiscated.

宏肇不喜賓客，嘗言：「文人難耐，輕我輩，謂我輩為卒，可恨可恨！ 」宏肇所領睢陽，其屬府公利，委親吏楊乙就府檢校，貪戾兇橫，負勢生事，吏民畏之，副戎已下，望風展敬。 聚劍刻剝，無所不至，月率萬緡，以輸宏肇，一境之內，嫉之如仇。 〈 （《東都事略·薛居正傳》：史宏肇領侍衛親軍，威震人主，殘忍自恣，人莫敢忤其意。 其部下吏告民犯鹽禁，法當死。 居正疑其不實，召詰之，乃其吏以私憾而誣之也。 逮捕吏鞫之，具伏，以吏抵法。 宏肇雖怒甚，竟亦無以屈也。）〉 周太祖平河中班師，推功於眾，以宏肇有翊衛鎮重之功，言之於隱帝，即授兼中書令。 隱帝自關西賊平之後，昵近小人，太后親族，頗行幹托，宏肇與楊邠甚不平之。 太后有故人子求補軍職，宏肇怒而斬之。 帝始聽樂，賜教坊使玉帶，諸伶官錦袍，往謝宏肇，宏肇讓之曰：「健兒為國戍邊，忍寒冒暑，未能遍有沾賜，爾輩何功，敢當此賜！ 」盡取袍帶還官，其兇戾如此。
Hongzhao disliked entertaining guests and once said, "Scholars are unbearable—they despise men like us and call us common soldiers. Detestable, detestable! " In Suiyang, which Hongzhao governed, he put his crony Yang Yi in charge of the prefectural accounts. Yi was greedy, brutal, and fond of trouble; officials and commoners alike feared him, and even deputy commanders bowed when he appeared. He squeezed the region by every trick and sent Hongzhao ten thousand strings of cash a month; the whole circuit hated him as they would a foe. Editorial bracket opening a comment block. (The Outline History of the Eastern Capital biography of Xue Juzheng notes that Shi Hongzhao, commanding the palace guard, awed even the emperor, ruled by cruelty, and brooked no opposition. One of his clerks reported a salt smuggler who by law deserved death. Juzheng doubted the charge, questioned the man, and found the clerk had framed him out of spite. He arrested the clerk, obtained a full confession, and punished the accuser instead. Hongzhao was furious but could not make Juzheng yield.) Editorial bracket closing the comment block. After Zhou Taizu pacified Hezhong he credited the army but told Emperor Yin that Hongzhao deserved praise for guarding the throne; Hongzhao was then made concurrent Director of the Secretariat. After the western rebels were crushed, Emperor Yin favored petty courtiers and the empress dowager's relatives, who pressed their requests freely; Hongzhao and Yang Bin were deeply offended. When the empress dowager's protégé sought a post in the army, Hongzhao had him beheaded in a rage. When the emperor first took up music he gave jade belts to the directors of the Music Bureau and brocade robes to the players; they came to thank Hongzhao, who scolded them: "Soldiers on the frontier endure cold and heat and still go unrewarded—what have you done to deserve this? " He seized the gifts and returned them to the treasury—so brutal was his temper.

周太祖有鎮鄴之命，宏肇欲兼領機樞之任，蘇逢吉異其議，宏肇忿之。 翌日，因竇貞固飲會，貴臣悉集，宏肇厲色舉爵屬周太祖曰：「昨晨廷論，一何同異！ 今日與弟飲此。 」楊邠、蘇逢吉亦舉大爵曰：「此國家之事也，何足介意！ 」俱飲釂。 宏肇又厲聲言曰：「安朝廷，定禍亂，直須長槍大劍，至如毛錐子，焉足用哉！ 」三司使王章曰：「雖有長槍大劍，若無毛錐子，贍軍財賦，自何而集？ 」宏肇默然，少頃而罷。 未幾，三司使王章於其第張酒樂，時宏肇與宰相、樞密使及內客省使閻晉卿等俱會。 酒酣，為手勢令，宏肇不熟其事，而閻晉卿坐次宏肇，屢教之。 蘇逢吉戲宏肇曰：「近坐有姓閻人，何憂罰爵！ 」宏肇妻閻氏，本酒妓也，宏肇謂逢吉譏之，大怒，以醜語詬逢吉。 逢吉不校，宏肇欲毆逢吉，逢吉策馬而去，宏肇遽起索劍，意欲追逢吉。 楊邠曰：「蘇公是宰相，公若害之，致天子何地，公細思之！ 」邠泣下。 宏肇索馬急馳而去，邠慮有非常，連鑣而進，送至第而還。 自是將相不協如水火矣。 隱帝遣王峻將酒樂於公子亭以和之，竟不能解。 其後李業、郭允明、後贊、聶文進居中用事，不悅執政。 又見隱帝年漸長，厭為大臣所制，嘗有忿言，業等乃乘間譖宏肇等，隱帝稍以為信。 業等乃言宏肇等專權震主，終必為亂，隱帝益恐。 嘗一夕，聞作坊鍛甲之聲，疑外有兵仗卒至，達旦不寐。 自是與業等密謀禁中，欲誅宏肇等。 議定，入白太后，太后曰：「此事豈可輕發耶！ 更問宰臣等。 」李業在側，曰：「先皇帝言，朝廷大事，莫共措大商量。 」太后又言之，隱帝怒曰：「閨門之內，焉知國家之事！ 」拂衣而出。 內客省使閻晉卿潛知其事，乃詣宏肇私第，將欲告之，宏肇以他事拒之不見。 乾祐三年冬十一月十三日，宏肇入朝，與樞密使楊邠、三司使王章同坐於廣政殿東廡下，俄有甲士數十人自內而出，害宏肇等於閣，夷其族。 先是，宏肇第數有異，嘗一日，於階砌隙中有煙氣蓬勃而出。 禍前二日昧爽，有星落於宏肇前三數步，如迸火而散，俄而被誅。 周太祖踐阼，追封鄭王，以禮葬，官為立碑。
When Zhou Taizu was sent to govern Ye, Hongzhao wanted to keep the military secretariat as well; Su Fengji disagreed, and Hongzhao bore a grudge. The next day at a banquet given by Dou Zhengu, Hongzhao glared and raised his cup to Zhou Taizu: "Yesterday's debate in court—what a quarrel that was! Today younger brother, drink with me." " Yang Bin and Su Fengji raised their cups as well: "These are affairs of state—why take offense? " All drained their cups. Hongzhao shouted again, "To steady the court and end chaos you need long spears and great swords—what use are writing brushes! " Wang Zhang, director of the Three Agencies, replied, "Even with spears and swords, without brushes where would the money to feed the army come from? " Hongzhao fell silent, and soon the party ended. Soon Wang Zhang gave a feast at his house; Hongzhao came with the chief ministers, the military commissioners, and Yan Jinqing of the Inner Guests Bureau. As the drinking deepened they played hand-signal forfeits; Hongzhao did not know the game, and Yan Jinqing beside him kept coaching him. Su Fengji teased him: "You have a Yan sitting right beside you—why fear a forfeit cup? " Hongzhao's wife was surnamed Yan and had once been a tavern singer; he took Fengji's joke as a slur on her and cursed him foully. Fengji would not answer back; Hongzhao tried to strike him, but Fengji rode off, and Hongzhao leapt up for a sword to give chase. Yang Bin cried, "Su is chief minister—if you kill him, what becomes of the emperor? Think again! " He wept as he spoke. Hongzhao galloped home; fearing worse, Bin rode after him and did not turn back until he had seen him inside his gate. From then on the generals and ministers were at odds like fire and water. Emperor Yin sent Wang Jun with wine and music to the Prince's Son Pavilion to patch things up, but the breach could not be healed. Later Li Ye, Guo Yunming, Hou Zan, and Nie Wenjin held sway in the palace and resented the chief ministers. They saw too that Emperor Yin was maturing and chafed under ministerial control; when he spoke bitterly, Ye and his circle slandered Hongzhao and the others until the emperor began to believe them. They told him Hongzhao's faction monopolized power, awed the throne, and would surely rebel; the emperor grew more afraid. One night he heard armor being hammered in the workshops, imagined troops had come upon him, and lay awake until dawn. He then plotted secretly in the palace with Ye and the others to kill Hongzhao and his allies. When the plan was set he told the empress dowager, who said, "Such a thing must not be undertaken lightly! Consult the chief ministers again." " Li Ye at her side said, "The late emperor warned never to discuss great affairs of state with those coarse fellows." " When she repeated her warning, Emperor Yin flared up: "What do women behind the curtain know of statecraft! " He stormed out. Yan Jinqing of the Inner Guests Bureau learned of the plot and went to warn Hongzhao at his home, but Hongzhao refused to see him. On the thirteenth day of the eleventh month of Qianyou 3, Hongzhao attended court and sat with Yang Bin and Wang Zhang under the eastern veranda of the Hall of Broad Governance; suddenly dozens of armored men emerged from within, killed Hongzhao and his allies in the pavilion, and exterminated their families. Before his fall his house had shown omens more than once; one day smoke poured from a crack in the courtyard steps. Two days before his death, at dawn, a meteor fell a few paces before him and burst like flame; soon he was killed. After Zhou Taizu came to the throne he posthumously made Hongzhao Prince of Zheng, buried him with full honors, and had a stele set up.

宏肇子德充，乾祐中，授檢校司空，領忠州刺史。 粗讀書，親儒者，常不悅父之所為。 貢院嘗錄一學科於省門叫噪，申中書門下，宰相蘇逢吉令送侍衛司，請痛笞刺面。 德充聞之，白父曰：「書生無禮，有府縣御史臺，非軍務治也。 公卿如此，蓋欲彰大人之過。 」宏肇深以為然，即破械放之。 後之識者尤嘉德充之為人焉。
Hongzhao's son Dechong, in the Qianyou era, was made Honorary Minister of Works and prefect of Zhongzhou. He had some learning, favored scholars, and often disapproved of his father's ways. Once an examinee who had passed one subject made a disturbance at the provincial gate; Su Fengji ordered him handed to the palace guard for a severe beating and facial tattoo. Dechong told his father, "The man was rude, but prefects, magistrates, and the censorate exist for that—this is not a soldier's affair. If ministers treat him so harshly, they only want to expose your excesses." " Hongzhao saw the point, broke his bonds, and set him free. Later observers especially admired Dechong's character.

楊邠，魏州冠氏人也。 少以吏給事使府，後唐租庸使孔謙，即其妻之世父也。 謙領度支，補勾押官，歷孟、華、鄆三州糧料使。 高祖為鄴都留守，用為左都押衙，高祖鎮太原，益加親委。 漢國建，遷檢校太保、權樞密使。 汴、洛平，正拜樞密使、檢校太傅。 及高祖大漸，與蘇逢吉、史宏肇等同受顧命，輔立嗣君。 隱帝即位，宰臣李濤上章，請出邠與周太祖為藩鎮，邠等泣訴於太后，由是罷濤而相邠，加中書侍郎兼吏部尚書、同平章事，仍兼樞密使。 時中書除吏太多，訛謬者眾。 及邠居相位，帝一以委之，凡南衙奏事，中書除命，先委邠斟酌，如不出邠意，至於一簿一掾，亦不聽從。 邠雖長於吏事，不識大體，常言：「為國家者，但得帑藏豐盈，甲兵強盛，至於文章禮樂，並是虛事，何足介意也。 」平河中，邠加右僕射。 邠既專國政，觸事苛細，條理煩碎。 前資官不得於外方居止，自京師至諸州府，行人往來，並須給公憑。 所由司求請公憑者，朝夕填咽，旬日之間，民情大擾，行路擁塞，邠乃止其事。 時史宏肇恣行慘酷，殺戮日眾，都人士庶，相目於路，邠但稱宏肇之善。 太后弟武德使李業求為宣徽使，隱帝與太后重違之，私訪於邠，邠以朝廷內使，遷拜有序，不可超居，遂止。 隱帝所愛耿夫人，欲立為後，邠亦以為太速。 夫人卒，隱帝欲以後禮葬，邠又止之，隱帝意不悅，左右有承間進甘言者，隱帝益怒之。 〈 （案：此下疑有闕文。）〉 邠繕甲兵，實帑廩，俾國用不闕，邊鄙粗寧，亦其功也。 〈 （《宣和書譜》云：邠末年留意縉紳，延客門下，知經史有用，乃課吏傳寫。）〉
Yang Bin came from Guanshi in Weizhou. As a youth he clerked in a military commissioner's office; Kong Qian, Later Tang's grain-and-corvée commissioner, was his wife's uncle. When Qian ran the revenue bureau, Bin became a checking clerk and served as grain commissioner in Meng, Hua, and Yan. When Gaozu was governor of the Ye capital he made Bin his left chief aide; when Gaozu moved to Taiyuan he relied on him still more. When the Han dynasty was founded he was made Honorary Grand Guardian and acting military commissioner. After Bian and Luoyang were secured he was formally made military commissioner and Honorary Grand Tutor. As Gaozu lay dying he received the final charge along with Su Fengji and Shi Hongzhao to help install the heir. Wang Zhang came from Nanle in Daming prefecture. As a youth he clerked in a military commissioner's office. Liu Zhu came from Shaanzhou. In his youth he served Liang's Prince Shao, Zhu Youhui, as an aide-de-camp. During Jin's Tianfu era, when Gaozu commanded the palace guard, he knew Zhu of old and had him appointed to the inner service. When Gaozu went to govern Taiyuan he made Zhu his left chief aide-de-camp. Zhu was cruel and bloodthirsty; Gaozu saw his own ruthless decisiveness in him and relied on him heavily. At the dynasty's founding he became military governor of Yongxing, helped pacify Bian and Luoyang, was moved to Qingzhou, and was made co-equal Grand Counsellor. When Emperor Yin came to the throne he was given the honorary rank of Grand Preceptor and made Palace Attendant as well. His laws were harsh and absolute: any offense, great or small, was never forgiven. At court the slightest slip brought an order to drag the offender headfirst for hundreds of paces; few survived the ordeal with whole skin. When he flogged a man he used two clubs at once, called the "paired-club beating"; or he gave as many strokes as the victim's age, called the "year-for-year beating." He taxed at will—three thousand cash per mu of autumn grain, two thousand for summer—and his subjects shrank from him in the roads. During Qianyou, when locusts ravaged Zi and Qing, he ordered a thorough hunt that left the crops untouched. Coastal towns had long hosted Two-Zhe trade offices that extorted the people, enforced their own punishments, and seized subjects of the throne; local governors took their bribes and dared not stop them. Zhu forbade his region to collect debts for Wu-Yue or seize people on their behalf; the Zhe merchants fell silent and none dared defy him. Fearing Zhu's brutal temper, the court kept Guo Qiong—returning from Haizhou through Qingzhou—and sent Murong Yanchao to replace him; Zhu yielded immediately. Editorial bracket opening a comment block. (The Longping Collection biography of Guo Qiong records that Liu Zhu, holding Pinglu, pleaded illness and stayed away; the emperor suspected revolt and sent Qiong to camp at Qingzhou. Zhu planned to kill him and set armed men under the feast canopy; Qiong showed no fear, and Zhu did not dare strike. Qiong explained the consequences of staying or going, and Zhu hurried to court.) Editorial bracket closing the comment block. On leaving his post he left several rooms of illicit salt mixed with filth, stuffed the wells, and buried them under earth. Yanchao reported the salt; Zhu lingered at court as a gentleman-in-attendance and often passed Hongzhao's and Bin's mansions with clenched fists. When Li Ye's faction killed Hongzhao and the rest, Zhu rejoiced and told them, "You fellows have done the master's dirty work at last. " He was then made acting prefect of Kaifeng and slaughtered Zhou Taizu's relatives and Wang Jun's household. When Zhou Taizu entered the capital he was arrested. Zhu told his wife, "I am as good as dead—you will end someone's servant girl! " His wife replied, "Given what you have done, that is only fitting." " Taizu sent a messenger to reproach him: "We once served the Han together—had you no memory of friendship? You butchered whole families on the emperor's order, yet added cruelty of your own—how pitiless! You too have a wife and children—do you never think of them? " Zhu could only plead for death. He reported to the empress dowager, executed Zhu and one son, and spared his wife. After Taizu took the throne he granted Zhu's wife an estate and a house in Shaanzhou. Editorial bracket opening a comment block. (The Supplemental Texts to the History of the Five Dynasties says that under Emperor Yin, Zhu as Kaifeng prefect slaughtered more than a dozen of Zhou Taizu's kin—men, women, and children—with appalling cruelty when Taizu marched from Ye. After the emperor was killed, Taizu, acting on the empress dowager's order, imprisoned Zhu and sent a messenger to condemn him. Zhu answered, "I only destroyed a rebel clan for the Han—I know nothing beyond that. " Taizu in fury had him killed.) Editorial bracket closing the comment block.

史臣曰：臣觀漢之亡也，豈系於天命哉！ 蓋委用不得其人，聽斷不符於理故也。 且如宏肇之淫刑，楊邠之秕政，李業、晉卿之設計，文進、允明之狂且，雖使成王為君，周公作相，亦不能保宗社之安，延歲月之命，況隱帝、逢吉之徒，其能免乎！ 《易》曰：「大君有命，開國承家，小人勿用，必亂邦也。 」當乾祐之末也，何斯言之驗歟！ 惟劉銖之忍酷，又安能逭於一死乎！
The historian writes: In my view, did the fall of Later Han truly hang on Heaven's mandate? It came rather from putting power in the wrong hands and deciding affairs without reason. Hongzhao's savage punishments, Yang Bin's hollow governance, the intrigues of Li Ye and Jinqing, the reckless folly of Wenjin and Yunming—even with King Cheng on the throne and the Duke of Zhou as minister the altars could not have stood nor the dynasty endured; how then could Emperor Yin, Su Fengji, and their like have been spared? The Book of Changes says, "The great ruler enfeoffs states and establishes houses, but petty men must not be used—they will surely bring the realm to ruin." " At the close of Qianyou, how fully those words were proved! As for Liu Zhu's cruelty, how could he have escaped death?
