王峻，字秀峰，相州安陽人也。 父豐，本郡樂營使。 峻幼慧黠善歌，梁貞明初，張筠鎮相州，憐峻敏惠，遂畜之。 及莊宗入魏州，筠棄鎮南渡，以峻自隨。 時租庸使趙巖訪筠於其第，筠召峻聲歌以侑酒，巖悅，筠因以贈之，頗得親愛。 梁亡，趙氏族滅，峻流落無依，寄食於符離陳氏之家，久之彌窘，乃事三司使張延朗，所給甚薄。 清泰末，延朗誅，漢祖盡得延朗之資產仆從，而峻在籍中，從歷數鎮，常為典客。 漢祖踐阼，授客省使，奉使荊南，留於襄、漢為監軍，入為內客省使。 及趙思綰作亂於永興，漢隱帝命郭從義討之，以峻為兵馬都監。 從義與峻不協，甚如水火。 未幾，改宣徽北院使。 賊平，加檢校太傅，轉南院使。
Wang Jun, whose courtesy name was Xiufeng, came from Anyang in Xiang Prefecture. His father Feng held the post of director of the prefectural music corps. As a boy Wang Jun was bright, quick-witted, and gifted at singing. Early in the Liang reign era Zhenming, when Zhang Jun was posted as military governor of Xiang Prefecture, he took a liking to the boy's cleverness and kept him in his service. When Emperor Zhuangzong marched into Weizhou, Zhang Jun gave up his command and fled south across the river, bringing Wang Jun along. At that time Zhao Yan, commissioner for rent and corvée labor, visited Zhang Jun at his mansion. Jun had Wang Jun sing to enliven the drinking, and Yan was delighted. Jun then gave Wang Jun to him as a gift, and Yan came to treat him with real fondness. After the Liang fell and the Zhao clan was wiped out, Wang Jun was left adrift with nowhere to turn. He lived on charity at a Chen household in Fuli; as months passed his plight grew worse, and he finally entered the service of Zhang Yanlang, commissioner of the Three Departments, who paid him very little. Late in the Qingtai reign, Zhang Yanlang was put to death. The Han founder Liu Zhiyuan seized all of Yanlang's property and retainers; Wang Jun was listed among them. He served in one commandery after another, usually as master of ceremonies for visiting guests. After Liu Zhiyuan took the throne, Wang Jun was made envoy of the Guest Reception Bureau, dispatched on embassy to Jingnan, then left behind as army supervisor at Xiangyang and Hanzhong, and later recalled to the capital as commissioner of the Inner Guest Reception Bureau. When Zhao Sizong rose in rebellion at Yongxing, Emperor Yin of Later Han ordered Guo Congyi to campaign against him and appointed Wang Jun superintendent of army and horse affairs. Guo Congyi and Wang Jun could not work together; their relations were as hostile as fire and water. Before long he was reassigned as commissioner of the Northern Court of the Palace Secretariat. After the rebellion was crushed he received the honorary title of Grand Preceptor and was moved to commissioner of the Southern Court.
2
太祖鎮鄴，兼北面兵馬，峻為監軍，留駐鄴城。 隱帝蕭墻變起，峻亦為群小所構，舉家見害。 從太祖赴闕，綢繆帷幄，贊成大事，峻居首焉。 京師平定，受漢太后令，充樞密使。 太祖北征，至澶州，為諸軍擁迫，峻與王殷在京聞變，乃遣侍衛馬軍都指揮使郭崇往宋州，前申州刺史馬鐸往許州，以防他變，二州安然，亦峻之謀也。 太祖踐阼，加平章事，尋兼右僕射、門下侍郎平章事，監修國史。 時朝廷初建，四方多故，峻夙夜奉事，知無不為，每侍太祖商榷軍事，未嘗不移時而退，甚有裨益。 然為性輕躁，舉措率易，以天下之事為己任，每有啟請，多自任情。 太祖從而順之，則忻然而退； 稍未允可，則應聲而慍，不遜之語隨事輒發。 太祖素知其為人，且以佐命之故，每優容之。 峻年長於太祖二歲，太祖雖登大位，時以兄呼之，有時呼表字，不忘布衣之契也。 峻以此益自負焉。
When Guo Wei was posted at Ye, he also commanded the northern frontier armies; Wang Jun served as army supervisor and stayed garrisoned in Ye. When the Hidden Emperor's palace coup broke out, Wang Jun was likewise framed by petty schemers, and his entire family was slaughtered. When he followed Guo Wei to the capital, he worked closely in the command tent and helped bring the great enterprise to success; Wang Jun ranked first among those who did so. After the capital was secured, he received orders from the Han empress dowager and was appointed commissioner of the Bureau of Military Affairs. On Guo Wei's northern campaign, when he reached Cangzhou and was pressed forward by his troops, Wang Jun and Wang Yin in the capital learned of the upheaval. They sent Palace Guard cavalry commander Guo Chong to Songzhou and former Shenzhou inspector Ma Duo to Xuzhou to forestall other trouble; both prefectures stayed calm—another stratagem of Wang Jun's. When Guo Wei took the throne, Wang Jun was made Grand Councilor; soon afterward he also served as Right Vice Director of the Department of State Affairs, Gate Department Attendant and Grand Councilor, and supervised compilation of the national history. The dynasty was newly established and troubles pressed on every side. Wang Jun attended the emperor day and night, undertaking whatever task he could. Whenever he stood by Guo Wei to discuss military affairs he never left before an hour had passed, and his counsel proved highly useful. Yet he was by nature impulsive and rash, acting with little deliberation, treating the affairs of the empire as his personal burden. When he petitioned the throne he usually did as he pleased. If Guo Wei agreed, Wang Jun would withdraw in satisfaction; but if the emperor hesitated even slightly, he would flare up at once, and impertinent words spilled out on every occasion. Guo Wei had long known what sort of man he was, and because Wang Jun had helped him seize the throne, he always indulged him. Wang Jun was two years older than Guo Wei. Even after Guo Wei became emperor he would sometimes call him elder brother and sometimes use his courtesy name, never forgetting their friendship from humbler days. This only made Wang Jun all the more conceited.
3
廣順元年冬，劉崇與契丹圍晉州，峻請行應援，太祖用為行營都部署，以徐州節度使王彥超為副。 詔諸軍並取峻節度，許峻以便宜從事，軍行資用仰給於官，隨行將吏得自選擇。 將發之前，召宴於滋德殿，太祖出女樂以寵之。 奉辭之日，恩賜優厚，不拘常制。 及發，太祖幸西莊，親臨宴餞，別賜御馬玉帶，執手而別。 峻至陜駐留數夕，劉崇攻晉州甚急，太祖憂其不可支，議親征，取澤州路入，與峻會合，先令諭峻。 峻遣驛騎馳奏，請車駕不行幸。 時已降御劄，行有日矣，會峻奏至，乃止。 峻軍既過絳郡，距平陽一舍，賊軍燔營，狼狽而遁。 峻入晉州，或請追賊，必有大利，峻猶豫久之，翌日方遣騎軍襲賊，信宿而還。 向使峻極力追躡，則並、汾之孽，無噍類矣。 峻亦深恥無功，因計度增修平陽故城而回。 時永興軍節度使李洪信，漢室之密戚也，自太祖踐阼，恒有憂沮之意，而本城軍不滿千，峻出征至陜州，以救援晉州為辭，抽起數百人，及劉崇北遁，又遣禁兵千餘人，屯於京兆，洪信懼，遂請入朝。 峻軍回，太祖厚加優賜。 時慕容彥超叛於兗州，已遣侍衛步軍都指揮使曹英、客省使向訓率兵攻之。 峻意欲自將兵討賊，累言於太祖曰：「慕容劇賊，曹英不易與之敵耳。 」太祖默然。 未幾親征，命峻為隨駕一行都部署，破賊之日，峻督軍在城南，其眾先登，頗有得色。 從駕還京，未幾貢表乞解樞機，即時退歸私第。
In winter of the first Guangshun year, Liu Chong of Hedong and the Khitans besieged Jinzhou. Wang Jun volunteered to lead the relief force; Guo Wei appointed him supreme commander of the field army and named Xuzhou military governor Wang Yanchao his deputy. An edict placed every army under Wang Jun's command, allowed him discretionary authority on campaign, ordered that marching expenses be drawn from the treasury, and let him pick his own staff of officers and clerks. Before he marched, Guo Wei feasted him in the Zide Hall and sent out palace musicians to show him special favor. On the day he took leave, the rewards were lavish and broke with usual precedent. As he departed, Guo Wei went to the Western Villa to give him a personal farewell banquet, also granting an imperial horse and jade belt, and took his hand at parting. Wang Jun reached Shanzhou and lingered several nights. Liu Chong's assault on Jinzhou grew fierce; Guo Wei feared the city could not stand and debated leading the campaign in person, entering by way of Zezhou to join Wang Jun, and first sent orders to notify him. Wang Jun dispatched a courier at full gallop with a memorial asking that the emperor not take the field in person. An imperial rescript had already gone out and the departure was set; but when Wang Jun's memorial arrived, the emperor stopped. After Wang Jun's army passed Jiangzhou, a day's march from Pingyang, the enemy burned their camp and fled in disarray. Wang Jun entered Jinzhou. Some urged him to pursue the fleeing foe for a great prize; he hesitated a long while, and only on the morrow sent cavalry after them, returning two days later. Had Wang Jun pressed the pursuit with all his might, the rebels of Bing and Fen would have been wiped out to the last man. Ashamed of accomplishing so little, Wang Jun spent his efforts enlarging the old walls of Pingyang, then marched back. Li Hongxin, military governor of Yongxing, was a close relative of the Han imperial clan. Since Guo Wei took the throne he had been uneasy and downcast, and his capital garrison held fewer than a thousand men. When Wang Jun marched to Shanzhou on the pretext of relieving Jinzhou, he drafted several hundred troops from Hongxin's command; after Liu Chong retreated north he also stationed more than a thousand imperial guards at Chang'an. Terrified, Hongxin petitioned to come to court. When Wang Jun's army came back, Guo Wei showered him with favors. Murong Yanchao had rebelled at Yanzhou. Palace Guard infantry commander Cao Ying and guest-reception envoy Xiang Xun were already leading troops against him. Wang Jun wanted to lead the campaign himself and told Guo Wei again and again, "Murong Yanchao is a ferocious rebel; Cao Ying will not easily stand against him." Guo Wei said nothing. Soon Guo Wei took the field in person and named Wang Jun supreme commander of the accompanying column. On the day the city fell, Wang Jun directed the force south of the wall; his men were the first over the ramparts and wore a look of triumph. After returning to the capital with the emperor, he soon submitted a memorial asking to leave the Bureau of Military Affairs and withdrew at once to his private house.
4
峻貪權利，多機數，好施小惠，喜人附己。 太祖登極之初，務存謙抑，潛龍將佐未甚進用，其後鄭仁誨、李重進、向訓等稍遷要職，峻心忌之，至是求退，蓋偵太祖之意也。 未陳請之前，多發外諸侯書以求保證，旬浹之內，諸道馳騎進納峻書，聞者驚駭其事。 峻連貢三章，中使宣諭無虛日，太祖嚴駕將幸其第，峻聞之，即馳馬入見，太祖慰勞久之，復令視事。 峻又於本院之東，別建公署，廓廡廳事，高廣華侈。 及土木之功畢，請太祖臨幸，恩賜甚厚。 其後內園新起小殿，峻視之，奏曰：「宮室已多，何用於此？ 」太祖曰：「樞密院舍宇不少，公更自興造何也？ 」峻慚默而退。 時峻以前事趙巖，頗承寵愛，至是欲希贈官立碑。 或謂峻曰：「趙巖以諂佞事君，破壞梁室，至今言者無不切齒，茍如所欲，必貽物議。 」乃止。 巖侄崇勛，居於陳郡，峻為求官田宅以賜之，太祖亦從之。 三年春，修利河堤，大興土功，峻受詔檢校。 既而世宗自澶州入覲，峻素憚世宗之聰明英果，聞其赴闕，即自河次歸朝。 居無何，邀求兼領青州，太祖不得已而授之。 既受命，求暫赴任，奏借左藏綾絹萬匹，從之。
Wang Jun craved power and profit, trafficked in intrigue, liked to hand out petty favors, and delighted in gathering followers. When Guo Wei first took the throne he cultivated modesty and did not promote his old comrades from before the throne. Later Zheng Renhui, Li Chongjin, Xiang Xun, and others gradually rose to key posts. Wang Jun resented this. His request to retire was chiefly a probe of the emperor's intentions. Before he filed his petition he wrote widely to regional governors asking them to vouch for him. Within a fortnight couriers from every circuit raced in with Wang Jun's letters, and the court was alarmed by the spectacle. Wang Jun sent up three memorials in a row; imperial envoys came every day with reassuring words. Guo Wei was preparing to visit his house in full ceremonial array. Hearing this, Wang Jun rode at once to audience; the emperor comforted him at length and ordered him back to office. Wang Jun also erected a new official compound east of the Bureau of Military Affairs, with broad corridors and reception halls tall, spacious, and sumptuous. When construction was finished he invited Guo Wei to visit in person, and the gifts showered on him were lavish. Later a small hall was raised in the inner park. Wang Jun saw it and memorialized, "The palace already has more rooms than it needs—why build this?" Guo Wei replied, "The Bureau of Military Affairs has plenty of offices—why did you put up still more for yourself?" Wang Jun withdrew, abashed and speechless. Wang Jun had once served Zhao Yan, who had favored him; now he hoped to win posthumous honors and a memorial stele for Yan. Someone warned him, "Zhao Yan won favor through flattery and helped destroy the Liang dynasty. People still curse his name. If you have your way you will invite a storm of criticism." He dropped the idea. Yan's nephew Chongxun lived in Chen prefecture; Wang Jun asked that official fields and a residence be granted him, and Guo Wei agreed. In spring of the third year, work began on repairing the Benefit River dikes on a large scale; Wang Jun was ordered to supervise it. Before long Emperor Shizong came from Cangzhou to audience. Wang Jun had always been wary of his intelligence and force of character; hearing he was on his way to the capital, Wang Jun left the river project and hurried back. Soon he demanded concurrent appointment as military governor of Qingzhou; Guo Wei, unable to refuse, granted it. After accepting the post he asked permission to take up office for a time and memorialized to borrow ten thousand bolts of silk from the left treasury; the request was granted.
5
是歲，戶部侍郎趙上交權知貢舉，上交嘗詣峻，峻言及一童子，上交不達其旨，榜出之日，童子不第，峻銜之。 及貢院申中書門下，取日過堂，峻知印，判定過日。 及上交引新及第人至中書，峻在政事堂厲聲曰：「今歲選士不公，當須覆試。 」諸相曰：「但緣已行指揮行過，臨事不欲改移，況未敕下，覆試非晚。 」峻愈怒，詬責上交，聲聞於外。 少頃，竟令引過。 及罷，上交詣本廳謝峻，峻又延之飲酌從容。 翌日，峻奏上交知舉不公，請致之於法，太祖頷之而已。 又奏請以顏愆、陳觀代範質、李穀為相。 太祖曰：「進退宰輔，未可倉卒，待徐思之。 」峻論列其事，奏對不遜。 太祖未食，日將亭午，諍之不已。 太祖曰：「節假之內，未欲便行，已俟假開，即依所奏。 」峻退至中書。 是月，吏部選人過門下，峻當其事，頗疑選部不公，其擬官選人落下者三十餘人。 次日寒食時節，臣僚各歸私第。 午時，宣召宰臣、樞密使，及入，幽峻於別所。 太祖見馮道已下，泣曰：「峻淩朕頗甚，無禮太過，擬欲盡去左右臣僚，翦朕羽翼。 朕兒在外，專意阻隔，暫令到闕，即懷怨望。 豈有既總樞機，又兼宰相，堅求重鎮，尋亦授之，任其襟懷，尚未厭足，如此無君，誰能甘忍！ 」即召翰林學士徐臺符等草制。 其日，退朝宣制，貶授商州司馬，差供奉官蔣光遠援送赴商州。 未幾，死於貶所，時廣順三年三月也。 〈 （《通鑒》：峻至商州，得腹疾，帝猶湣之，命其妻往視之，未幾而卒。）〉
That year Zhao Shangjiao, vice minister of revenue, acted as chief examiner. He had once visited Wang Jun, who mentioned a certain young candidate; Shangjiao did not catch his meaning. When the results were posted the youth failed, and Wang Jun took offense. When the examination office reported to the Secretariat to schedule the palace audience, Wang Jun applied the seal and set the date. When Shangjiao brought the new jinshi to the Secretariat, Wang Jun shouted in the Hall of Administration, "This year's examination was unfair and must be retested." The chief ministers said, "The directive has already gone out; we do not want to alter things at the last moment. Besides, the edict has not yet been promulgated—a retest can wait." Wang Jun grew angrier still, berated Shangjiao, and his voice carried outside the hall. In the end he still ordered the graduates presented. When the audience ended, Shangjiao went to Wang Jun's office to apologize, and Wang Jun received him again for a relaxed drink. The next day Wang Jun memorialized that Shangjiao had run the examination unfairly and asked that he be punished; Guo Wei only nodded. He also asked that Yan Qian and Chen Guan replace Fan Zhi and Li Gu as chief ministers. Guo Wei said, "Promoting and dismissing chief ministers cannot be rushed; let us think on it awhile." Wang Jun argued the point at length in audience, his replies lacking all deference. Guo Wei had not yet eaten; the sun was nearly at noon, and Wang Jun would not stop arguing. Guo Wei said, "During the holiday I do not wish to act hastily. When the holiday ends I will do as you ask." Wang Jun withdrew to the Secretariat. That month civil-service candidates passed review at the Gate Department under Wang Jun's authority. He suspected the selection bureau of bias and struck more than thirty proposed appointees from the list. The next day was Cold Food; the officials went home. At noon Guo Wei summoned the chief ministers and the military-affairs commissioner. When they arrived, Wang Jun was held apart. Facing Feng Dao and the others, Guo Wei wept and said, "Wang Jun has bullied me terribly, with insolence beyond bearing. He means to drive away every minister at my side and cut off my wings." My son is kept away outside the capital; Wang Jun deliberately blocks him. When I briefly let him come to court he is already full of grievance. He already commands the Bureau of Military Affairs, is also chief minister, demanded a major frontier post and got it, and still his appetite is not sated. Who could bear such disloyalty! He immediately summoned Hanlin academician Xu Taifu and others to draft the demotion edict. That day, after court, the edict was read: Wang Jun was demoted to military adjutant of Shangzhou, and palace supply officer Jiang Guangyuan was sent to escort him thither. Before long he died in exile, in the third month of Guangshun's third year. 〈Note: (《Zizhi Tongjian》: When Wang Jun reached Shangzhou he fell ill with abdominal disease. The emperor still pitied him and sent his wife to visit him; before long he died.)〉

初，峻降制除青州，有司制造旌節，以備迎授。 前一夕，其旄有聲甚異，聞者駭之。 主者曰：「昔安重誨授河中節，亦有此異焉。 」又所居堂陛，忽然隱起如堆。 又夢被官府追攝入司簿院，既寤，心惡之，以是尤加狂躁。 峻才疏位重，輕躁寡謀，聽人穿鼻，既國權在手，而射利者曲為指畫，乃啖餌虎臣，離間親舊，加以善則稱己，無禮於君，欲求無罪，其可得乎！ 〈 （《五代史闕文》：廣順初，河東劉崇引契丹攻晉州。 遣王峻率師赴援，峻頓兵於陜。 周祖親征，遣使諭之。 峻見使受宣訖，謂使曰：「與某馳還，附奏陛下，言晉州城堅，未易可破，劉崇兵鋒方銳，不可與力爭，所以駐兵者，待其氣衰耳，非臣怯也。 陛下新即位，不宜輕舉。 今朝中受聖知者，惟李蒨、範質而已，陛下若車駕出汜水，則慕容彥超以賊軍入汴，大事去矣。 」使還具奏，周祖自以手提其耳曰：「幾敗吾事。」）〉
Earlier, when the edict demoting Wang Jun assigned him to Qingzhou, the proper offices had begun making banners of command for his installation. On the night before, his command banners gave off a very strange noise, and those who heard it were frightened. The custodian said, "When An Chonghui received the Bingzhong commission there was this same omen." The steps of his hall also suddenly bulged upward like a mound. He dreamed officials seized him and dragged him into the Bureau of Registers. When he woke he was filled with dread and grew all the more frantic. Wang Jun's talent did not match his rank. Impulsive and scheming little, he let others lead him about. Once he held state power, profiteers bent plans to their ends, baiting fierce ministers and driving wedges between the emperor and his old companions. He took credit for every good turn and showed his sovereign no courtesy. How could he expect to escape guilt! 〈Note: (《Supplement to the History of the Five Dynasties》: Early in Guangshun, Liu Chong of Hedong brought Khitan forces against Jinzhou. Wang Jun was dispatched to relieve the city and camped his army at Shanzhou. Guo Wei took the field in person and sent an envoy to instruct him. When Wang Jun received the envoy and heard the imperial message, he told him, "Ride back with me and report to His Majesty: Jinzhou's walls are strong and will not fall easily; Liu Chong's troops are at their sharpest and cannot be met head-on. I am holding my army here only until their ardor fades—it is not cowardice." Your Majesty has only just taken the throne; you should not take risks lightly. Of those at court who enjoy your full trust today, only Li Qian and Fan Zhi remain. If Your Majesty's carriage goes beyond Sishui, Murong Yanchao will lead rebel troops into Bian—the whole enterprise will be lost." When the envoy returned and reported everything, Guo Wei seized his own ear and said, "I nearly ruined everything.")〉

慕容彥超， 〈 （案：此下有闕文。）〉 為兗州節度使，彥超即漢高祖之同產弟也。 嘗冒姓閻氏，體黑麻面，故謂之閻昆侖。 彥超鎮兗州，漢隱帝欲殺周太祖，召彥超，方食，釋匕箸而就道。 周兵犯京師，隱帝出勞軍，太后使彥超衛帝，彥超曰：「北兵何能為，當於陣上唱坐使歸營。 」彥超敗，奔兗，隱帝遇弒。 周太祖時，彥超進呈鄆州節度使高行周來書，其書意即行周毀讟太祖結連彥超之意，帝覽之，笑曰：「此必是彥超之詐也。 」試令驗之，果然。 其鄆州印元有缺，文不相接，其為印即無缺處，帝尋令賫書示諭行周，行周上表謝恩。 先是，填星初至角、亢，占者曰：角，鄭分，兗州屬焉。 彥超即率軍府賓佐，步出州西門三十里致祭，迎於開元寺，塑像以事之，謂之「菩薩」，日至祈禱，又令民家豎黃幡以禳之。 及城陷，彥超方在土星院燃香，急乃馳去。 〈 （《五代史補》：慕容彥超素有鉤距。 兗州有盜者，詐為大官從人，跨驢於衢中，市羅十餘匹，價值既定，引物主詣一宅門，以驢付之，曰：「此本宅使，汝且在此，吾為汝上白於主以請值。 」物主許之。 既而聲跡悄然，物主怒其不出，叩門呼之，則空宅也。 於是連叫「賊」，巡司至，疑其詐，兼以驢收之詣府。 彥超憫之，且曰：「勿憂，吾為汝擒此賊。 」乃留物主府中，復戒廄卒高系其驢，通宵不與水草，然後密召親信者，牽於通衢中放之，且曰：「此盜者之驢耳，自昨日不與水草，其饑渴者甚矣，放之必奔歸家，但可躡蹤而觀之，盜無不獲也。 」親信者如其言隨之，其驢果入一小巷，轉數曲，忽有兒戲於門側，視其驢，連呼曰：「驢歸，驢歸。 」盜者聞之，欣然出視，遂擒之。 高祖登極，改乾祐為廣順。 是年，兗州慕容彥超反。 高祖親征，城將破，忽夜夢一人，狀貌甚偉異，被王者之服，謂高祖曰：「陛下明日當得城。 」及覺，天猶未曉。 高祖私謂征兆如此，可不預備乎！ 於是躬督將士，戮力急攻，至午而城陷。 車駕將入，有司請由生方鳴鞘而進，遂取別巷，轉數曲，見一處門墻甚高大，問之，雲夫子廟。 高祖意豁然，謂近臣曰：「寡人所夢，得非夫子乎？ 不然，何取路於此也。 」因下馬觀之，方升堂，睹其聖像，一如夢中所見者，於是大喜，叩首再拜。 近臣或諫，以為天子不合拜異世陪臣。 高祖曰：「夫子聖人也，百王取則，而又夢告寡人，得非夫子幽贊所及耶？ 安得不拜！ 」仍以廟側數十家為灑掃戶，命孔氏襲文宣王者長為本縣。 慕容彥超之被圍也，乘城而望，見高祖親臨矢石，其勢不可當，退而憂之，因勉其麾下曰：「汝等宜為吾盡命，吾庫中金銀如山積，若全此城，吾盡以為賜，汝等勿患富貴。 」頃之，有卒私言曰：「我知侍中銀皆鐵胎，得之何用？ 」於是諸軍聞之，稍稍解體，未幾城陷。 及高祖之入也，有司閱其庫藏，其間銀鐵胎者果十有七八。 初，彥超常令人開質庫，有以鐵胎銀質錢者，經年後，庫吏始覺，遂言之於彥超。 初甚怒，頃之謂吏曰：「此易致耳，汝宜偽竇刂庫墻，凡金銀器用暨縑帛等，速皆藏匿，仍亂撤其餘以為賊踐，然後申明，吾當擒此輩矣。 」庫吏如其教，於是彥超下令曰：「吾為使長典百姓，而又不謹，遭賊竇刂去，其過深矣。 今恐百姓疑彥超隱其物，宜令三日內各投狀，明言質物色，自當陪償之，不爾者有過。 」百姓以為然，於是投狀相繼，翌日鐵胎銀主果出。 於是擒之，置之深屋中，使教部曲輩晝夜造，用廣府庫，此銀是也。）〉
Murong Yanchao, 〈Note: (Editorial note: Text is missing below.)〉 was military governor of Yanzhou; Yanchao was the Han founder's younger brother by the same mother. He had once borne the surname Yan; dark-skinned and pockmarked, he was nicknamed "Yan Kunlun." While Yanchao governed Yanzhou, Emperor Yin of Later Han plotted to kill Guo Wei and summoned him. Yanchao was in the middle of a meal but laid down his utensils and set out at once. When Zhou forces threatened the capital, the Hidden Emperor went out to review the troops. The empress dowager ordered Yanchao to protect him. Yanchao said, "What can those northern soldiers do? I'll stand on the battlefield and sing them back to camp." Yanchao was beaten and fled to Yanzhou; the Hidden Emperor was murdered. During Guo Wei's reign, Yanchao submitted a letter purportedly from Yanzhou governor Gao Xingzhou, implying that Xingzhou had slandered Guo Wei and was conspiring with Yanchao. Guo Wei read it and laughed, "This must be Yanchao's trick." An investigation proved it at once. The genuine Yanzhou seal had a flaw where the characters did not meet; the forged seal had no such gap. Guo Wei soon sent the letter to Gao Xingzhou to show him what had been done, and Xingzhou submitted a memorial of thanks. Earlier, when the Piled Star entered the Horn and Neck lodges, diviners said, "Horn is Zheng's celestial allotment, and Yanzhou falls within it." Yanchao led his staff out the west gate thirty li to make offerings, welcomed the omen at Kaiyuan Temple, and set up an image to worship it as the "Bodhisattva." He prayed daily and ordered households to raise yellow banners to ward off ill fortune. When the city fell, Yanchao was still burning incense at the Saturn Courtyard and fled in panic. 〈Note: (《Supplement to the History of the Five Dynasties》: Murong Yanchao was always full of stratagems. At Yanzhou a thief posed as a great official's attendant, riding a donkey through the streets to buy more than ten bolts of silk. When the price was agreed he led the seller to a mansion gate, handed him the donkey, and said, "This is our master's house. Wait here while I report upstairs and get your payment." The seller agreed. Soon all went quiet. Angry that no one appeared, the seller knocked and shouted at the gate, only to find an empty house. He began shouting "Thief!" The patrol came, suspected him of fraud, and took the donkey to the prefectural office. Yanchao took pity on him and said, "Do not worry; I will catch this thief for you." He kept the seller at his headquarters, ordered the stable hands to tie the donkey high and withhold fodder and water all night, then secretly told a trusted man to lead it into the main street and release it, saying, "This is the thief's donkey. Without food or water since yesterday it must be desperate; release it and it will run home. Follow its tracks and you cannot fail to catch the thief." The man did as told. The donkey entered a small lane, wound through several turns, and a child playing by a gate saw it and cried, "The donkey's back! The donkey's back!" The thief heard him, came out gladly to look, and was captured. When Guo Wei took the throne he changed the reign title from Qianyou to Guangshun. That year Murong Yanchao rebelled at Yanzhou. Guo Wei campaigned in person. As the city was about to fall he dreamed of a man of striking appearance in kingly robes who said, "Tomorrow Your Majesty will take the city." When he woke, dawn had not yet broken. Guo Wei said to himself, "With an omen like this, how can I fail to prepare!" He personally directed his officers in a furious assault, and by noon the city fell. As the imperial carriage was about to enter, officials asked that it approach from the auspicious direction with the whip sounded. He took a side lane instead, wound through several turns, and came upon a gate and wall of great height. Inquiring, he learned it was the Confucian temple. Guo Wei's mind cleared at once. He told his close ministers, "Could the figure in my dream have been Confucius?" If not, why should our route have brought us here?" He dismounted to look. As he ascended the hall he saw the sage's image, exactly as in his dream. Overjoyed, he bowed twice. Some close ministers objected that the Son of Heaven ought not bow to a minister of another age. Guo Wei said, "Confucius is a sage whom a hundred kings take as their model. He also warned me in a dream. Could this not be his hidden blessing?" How could I not bow!" He made several dozen households beside the temple temple sweepers and appointed the Kong clan heir to the title Duke of Wenxuan as elder of the county. While Murong Yanchao was under siege he climbed the wall and saw Guo Wei himself in the hail of arrows and stones. The assault was irresistible. He withdrew in alarm and urged his men, "Fight for me with all you have. Gold and silver in my treasury are heaped like mountains. Save this city and I will give it all to you; you need not fear for wealth or rank." Soon a soldier whispered, "I know the commander's silver is all iron at the core. What's the use of that?" When the troops heard this they slowly lost heart, and before long the city fell. When Guo Wei entered, officials inspected the treasury. Seven or eight tenths of the silver proved to be iron at the core. Yanchao often had pawnshops opened in his domain. Some customers pawned iron-core silver. More than a year passed before the clerks noticed and reported it to him. At first he was furious; then he told the clerk, "This is easy to fix. Pretend thieves broke through the pawnshop wall. Hide all gold and silver goods and silks at once, scatter the rest as if thieves trampled them, then report the burglary. I will catch these people." The clerk did as instructed. Yanchao then proclaimed, "As commissioner I am charged with the people, yet I was careless and thieves broke in. The fault is grave." I fear the people may think I am hiding their goods. Within three days each of you must submit a statement describing your pawned items in detail. I will compensate you myself. Anyone who does not will be at fault." The people believed him, and statements poured in. The next day the owners of iron-core silver appeared. They were seized, shut in a deep chamber, and made to teach their gangs to forge day and night to fill the prefectural treasury. That was the silver in question.)〉

Yan Honglu was the son of Yan Bao, military governor of Xingzhou under Later Tang. Bao has a biography in the Tang History. Honglu served Emperor Mingzong of Tang and Emperor Gaozu of Later Jin, rising through a series of posts. His family came from the Lu region. When he went home on sick leave, Murong Yanchao on first taking Yanzhou treated him with exceptional honor. When Yanchao plotted rebellion he made Honglu's son Xijun deputy military commissioner of Zhenning, stationed on Shizong's staff, whom Yanchao hated. Learning that the court was sending troops against him, he rebuked Honglu at once: "You taught your son to oppose me at court. Do you mean to destroy my whole clan!" For this he suffered death.

Cui Zhoudu's father Guangbiao passed the jinshi examination in the top class. When Lu Zhi governed Henghai he recruited him as branch commissioner. Zhoudu was a man of letters, beginning as magistrate of Changlu and entering court as investigating censor and right remonstrator. Wishing to arrange his family's burial in Qi Prefecture, he begged for an outside post and was made judge on the staff of the Taining military commissioner. His nature was stern, and having once served as a remonstrator he could not sit by while the rebel governor broke the law. He remonstrated with Yanchao to the utmost and for that met this fate.
