霍峻字仲邈，南郡枝江人也。 兄篤於鄉里合部曲數百人，篤卒，荊州牧劉表令峻攝其眾。 表卒，峻率眾歸先主，先主以峻為中郎將。 先主自葭萌南還襲劉璋，留峻守葭萌城。 張魯遣將楊帛誘峻，求共守城，峻曰：「小人頭可得，城不可得。 」帛乃退去。 後璋將扶禁、向存等帥萬餘人由閬水上，攻圍攻峻，且一年，不能下。 峻城中兵才數百人，伺其怠隙，選精銳出擊，大破之，即斬存首。 先主定蜀，嘉峻之功，乃分廣漢為梓潼郡，以峻為梓潼太守、裨將軍。 在官三年，年四十卒，還葬成都。 先主甚悼惜，乃詔諸葛亮曰：「峻既佳士，加有功於國，欲行爵。 」遂親率群僚臨會弔祭，因留宿墓上，當時榮之。
Huo Jun, whose courtesy name was Zhongmiao, came from Zhijiang in Nan Commandery. His older brother Huo Du had raised a private following of several hundred men in their native place. After Du died, Liu Biao, as shepherd of Jing Province, put Jun in charge of that force. After Liu Biao died, Jun brought his men over to the First Lord, who made him a middle-ranking general. The First Lord marched south from Jiameng to surprise Liu Zhang and left Jun to hold Jiameng. Zhang Lu sent his general Yang Bo to win Jun over with an offer to hold the city jointly. Jun replied, "You may have my head, but you will not have the city." Yang Bo withdrew. Later Liu Zhang's generals Fu Jin and Xiang Cun led over ten thousand men up the Lang River to lay siege to Jun; the siege lasted the better part of a year without success. Jun had only a few hundred defenders. He waited for a gap in their vigilance, picked his best troops for a sortie, shattered their army, and took Xiang Cun's head. Once the First Lord had settled Shu, he singled out Jun's achievement, split off Zitong from Guanghan as a new commandery, and named Jun its administrator and a general of the second rank. He served three years in office and died at forty; his body was brought back to Chengdu for burial. The First Lord grieved deeply and told Zhuge Liang, "Jun was an excellent man who also served the realm with distinction; I intend to grant him a noble rank." He then led the full court to the funeral rites himself and spent the night by the grave—a mark of honor people still spoke of.

子弋，字紹先，行主末年為太子舍人。 後主踐阼，除謁者。 丞相諸葛亮北駐漢中，請為記室，使與子喬共周旋游處。 亮卒，為黃門侍郎。 後主立太子璿，以弋為中庶子，璿好騎射，出入無度，弋援引古義，盡言規諫，甚得切磋之體。 後為參軍庲降屯副貳都督，又轉護軍，統事如前。 時永昌郡夷獠恃險不賓，數為寇害，乃以弋領永昌太守，率偏軍討之，遂斬其豪帥，破壞邑落，郡界寧靜。 遷監軍、翊軍將軍，領建寧太守，還統南郡事。 景耀六年，進號安南將軍。 是歲，蜀並於魏，弋與巴東領軍襄陽羅憲各保全一方，舉以內附，鹹因仍前任，寵待有加。 〈《汉晋春秋》曰：霍弋闻魏军来，弋欲赴成都，后主以备敌既定，不听。 及成都不守，弋素服号哭，大临三日。 诸将咸劝宜速降，弋曰：“今道路隔塞，未详主之安危，大故去就，不可苟也。 若主上与魏和，见遇以礼，则保境而降，不晚也。 若万一危辱，吾将以死拒之，何论迟速邪！ ”得后主东迁之问，始率六郡将守上表曰：“臣闻人生於三，事之如一，惟难所在，则致其命。 今臣国败主附，守死无所，是以委质，不敢有贰。 ”晋文王善之，又拜南中都督，委以本任。 后遣将兵救援吕兴，平交阯、日南、九真三郡，功封列侯，进号崇赏焉。 弋孙彪，晋越巂太守。
His son Huo Yi, courtesy name Shaoxian, became an attendant to the heir apparent in the closing years of the First Lord's reign. When the Later Lord took the throne, Yi was appointed usher at court. While Chancellor Zhuge Liang was stationed north at Hanzhong, he asked Yi to serve as his secretary and had him live and travel with Liang's son Zhuge Qiao. After Liang's death he was promoted to gentleman at the Yellow Gates. When the Later Lord installed Liu Xuan as heir apparent, he named Yi junior tutor. The heir loved riding and shooting and roamed without limit; Yi cited the classics and remonstrated at length in the best tradition of candid counsel. He later served as deputy to the commander of the Lajiang garrison, then became colonel of the guards while keeping his former responsibilities. The hill peoples of Yongchang had long used the rugged country to defy authority and raid the frontier. Yi was therefore made concurrent administrator of Yongchang, led a detached column against them, executed their headmen, razed their stockades, and restored calm to the commandery. He rose to army supervisor and general who supports the army, with concurrent appointment as administrator of Jianning, then again took charge of affairs in Nan Commandery. In the sixth year of the Jingyao era his rank was raised to general who pacifies the south. That year Shu fell to Wei. Yi and Luo Xian of Xiangyang, who commanded the Badong guard, each held his territory intact and then submitted to Wei. They were all allowed to keep their former posts and received still greater marks of favor. The Han-Jin Annals records that when Huo Yi learned Wei was advancing, he wanted to rush to Chengdu, but the Later Lord, believing the defense was already arranged, refused permission. When Chengdu fell, Yi put on mourning, wept openly, and kept the grand mourning for three days. His officers all pressed him to yield at once. Yi said, "Communications are cut; I do not yet know whether our sovereign is safe or in danger. A decision of this weight cannot be taken lightly. If our lord has come to terms with Wei and is treated with proper respect, we can still hold the frontier and surrender then—it would not be too late. But if he has suffered outrage or ruin, I will meet the invader with my life. What is there to say about haste or delay?" When news arrived that the Later Lord had been moved eastward, he led the officers of six commanderies in a memorial: "I have read that a man owes his life to three bonds and serves them as one; wherever crisis falls, he should give his utmost. My country is lost and my sovereign a guest in another court; I have no ground left to die for, so I offer my allegiance and dare not hold a divided heart." Sima Zhao, king of Jin, approved the memorial and again named Yi commander of the southern interior, leaving him in his former role. He later dispatched troops to aid Lu Xing, reduced Jiaozhi, Rinan, and Jiuzhen, and for that service was enfeoffed a full marquis with a further honorary generalship. His grandson Huo Biao became administrator of Yuexi under the Jin.

《襄阳记》曰：罗宪字令则。 父蒙，避乱於蜀，官至广汉太守。 宪少以才学知名，年十三能属文。 后主立太子，为太子舍人，迁庶子、尚书吏部郎，以宣信校尉再使於吴，吴人称美焉。 时黄皓预政，众多附之，宪独不与同，皓恚，左迁巴东太守。 时右大将军阎宇都督巴东，为领军，后主拜宪为宇副贰。 魏之伐蜀，召宇西还，留宇二千人，令宪守永安城。 寻闻成都败，城中扰动，江边长吏皆弃城走，宪斩称成都乱者一人，百姓乃定。 得后主委质问至，乃帅所统临于都亭三日。 吴闻蜀败，起兵西上，外讬救援，内欲袭宪。 宪曰：“本朝倾覆，吴为脣齿，不恤我难而徼其利，背盟违约。 且汉已亡，吴何得久，宁能为吴降虏乎！ ”保城缮甲，告誓将士，厉以节义，莫不用命。 吴闻锺、邓败，百城无主，有兼蜀之志，而巴东固守，兵不得过，使步协率众而西。 宪临江拒射，不能御，遣参军杨宗突围北出，告急安东将军陈骞，又送文武印绶、任子诣晋王。 协攻城，宪出与战，大破其军。 孙休怒，复遣陆抗等帅众三万人增宪之围。 被攻凡六月日而救援不到，城中疾病大半。 或说宪奔走之计，宪曰：“夫为人主，百姓所仰，危不能安，急而弃之，君子不为也，毕命於此矣。 ”陈骞言於晋王，遣荆州刺史胡烈救宪，抗等引退。 晋王即委前任，拜宪凌江将军，封万年亭侯。 会武陵四县举众叛吴，以宪为武陵太守巴东监军。 泰始元年改封西鄂县侯。 宪遣妻子居洛阳，武帝以子袭为给事中。 三年冬，入朝，进位冠军将军、假节。 四年三月，从帝宴于华林园，诏问蜀大臣子弟，后问先辈宜时叙用者，宪荐蜀郡常忌、杜轸、寿良、巴西陈寿、南郡高轨、南阳吕雅、许国、江夏费恭、琅邪诸葛京、汝南陈裕，即皆叙用，咸显於世。 宪还，袭取吴之巫城，因上伐吴之策。 宪方亮严正，待士不倦，轻财好施，不治产业。 六年薨，赠安南将军，谥曰烈侯。 子袭，以凌江将军领部曲，早卒，追赠广汉太守。 袭子徽，顺阳内史，永嘉五年为王如所杀。 此作“献”，名与本传不同，未详孰是也。〉
The Xiangyang Record gives Luo Xian's courtesy name as Lingze. His father Luo Meng had fled the wars into Shu and risen to administrator of Guanghan. From boyhood Xian was known for ability and scholarship; at thirteen he could already write polished essays. After the Later Lord named an heir apparent, Xian served as attendant to the heir, then rose to junior tutor and director of personnel in the Masters of Writing. Twice he went to Wu as trust-dispensing colonel and won admiration there. When Huang Hao meddled in state affairs, most officials curried favor with him; Xian alone held aloof. Hao nursed a grudge and had him demoted to administrator of Badong. The great general on the right, Yan Yu, was army supervisor of Badong and commanding general; the Later Lord made Xian his second-in-command. When Wei invaded Shu, Yan Yu was recalled westward, leaving two thousand men under Xian with orders to hold Yongan. Word soon came that Chengdu had fallen; the town panicked, and riverfront magistrates bolted. Xian executed one man who spread false tales of chaos in the capital, and the populace steadied. When the Later Lord's letter of explanation reached him, he led his command to the roadside pavilion and mourned for three days. Wu learned of Shu's collapse and marched west, claiming to help while actually planning to seize Xian. Xian declared, "Our court has fallen. Wu ought to stand with us as lip to tooth, yet instead of pitying our plight they angle for gain— they have broken faith and cast aside the treaty. Han is gone; how long can Wu last? Am I to become a prisoner who bends the knee to Wu?" He locked the gates, refitted the armor, swore the garrison in, and appealed to duty and honor until every man stood ready. When Wu heard that Zhong Hui and Deng Ai had destroyed each other and a hundred towns were leaderless, it coveted Shu for itself. Badong blocked the route, so Wu sent Bu Xie west with a large force. Xian tried to hold the line with bowshot but could not stem the tide. He sent his aide Yang Zong to break through northward and beg relief from Chen Qian, general who secures the east, and forwarded his seals of office and a son as hostage to the king of Jin. Bu Xie stormed the walls; Xian sallied forth and shattered his army. Sun Xiu flew into a rage and dispatched Lu Kang and others with thirty thousand men to tighten the siege around Xian. The siege ran half a year without relief, and disease carried off more than half the defenders. Some counseled retreat. Xian answered, "A leader is what the people lean on. To flee danger and abandon them in crisis is not what a gentleman does. I will die here." Chen Qian pleaded with the king of Jin, who sent Hu Lie, inspector of Jing Province, to relieve the city; Lu Kang and his colleagues withdrew. The king of Jin at once confirmed his old appointment, named him general who crosses the river, and enfeoffed him as marquis of Wannian village. When four counties in Wuling rose against Wu, Xian was appointed administrator of Wuling and army supervisor of Badong. In the first year of Taishi his fief was moved to marquis of Xie county. He sent his family to live in Luoyang; Emperor Wu named his son Luo Xi gentleman-in-attendance. In the third year of Taishi, winter, he attended court and was promoted to general-champion with credential. At a spring banquet in Hualin Park in the fourth year, the emperor asked after the sons and brothers of Shu officials, then who among the older generation deserved prompt employment. Xian nominated Chang Ji, Du Zhen, and Shou Liang of Shu, Chen Shou of Ba, Gao Gui of Nan, Lu Ya, Xu Guo, Fei Gong of Jiangxia, Zhuge Jing of Langye, and Chen Yu of Runan. They were all given posts at once and rose to prominence. On his return he seized Wu's border town of Wu and submitted a plan for conquering Wu. Xian was upright, capable, and strict, never weary of looking after his men, generous with money yet indifferent to personal property. He died in the sixth year of Taishi and was posthumously titled general who pacifies the south with the posthumous epithet Lie (Ardent) and a marquis rank. His son Luo Xi, general who crosses the river in command of household troops, died young and was posthumously honored as administrator of Guanghan. Xi's son Luo Hui served as interior chief of Shunyang and was killed by the bandit Wang Ru in the fifth year of Yongjia. The text here reads "present" for the name, which disagrees with this biography; it is unclear which form is right.

王連字文儀，南陽人也。 劉璋時入蜀為梓潼令。 先主起事葭萌，進軍來南，連閉城不降，先主義之，不強逼也。 及成都既平，以連為什邡令，轉在廣都，所居有績。 遷司鹽校尉，較鹽鐵之利，利入甚多，有裨國用，於是簡取良才以為官屬，若呂乂、杜祺、劉干，終皆至大官，自連所拔也。 遷蜀郡太守、興業將軍，領鹽府如故。 建興元年，拜屯騎校尉，領丞相長史，封平陽亭侯。 時南方諸郡不賓，諸葛亮將自征之，連諫以為「此不毛之地，疫癘之鄉，不宜以一國之望，冒險而行」。 亮慮諸將才不及己，意欲必往而連言輒懇至，故停留者久之。 會連卒。 子山嗣，官至江陽太守。
Wang Lian, courtesy name Wenyi, was a native of Nanyang. Under Liu Zhang he had crossed into Shu and served as magistrate of Zitong. When the First Lord rose at Jiameng and marched south, Lian barred the gates and refused to yield. The First Lord respected his integrity and did not press the siege. After Chengdu fell he was appointed magistrate of Shifang, then transferred to Guangdu, and in every post he left a record of good government. He rose to colonel director of the salt and iron monopolies, reorganized their revenues, and greatly increased the income that fed the treasury. He picked able men for his staff— Lu Yi, Du Qi, Liu Gan among them— who all rose to high rank on his recommendation. He was promoted to administrator of Shu commandery and general who establishes enterprise while still overseeing the salt administration. In the first year of Jianxing he became colonel of the agile cavalry and chief clerk to the chancellor, with a marquisate at Pingyang village. The southern commanderies were still in revolt, and Zhuge Liang meant to lead the expedition himself. Lian urged against it: "That country is barren malarial ground; the fate of the realm should not be wagered on such a march." Liang doubted any general could match his own ability and was determined to go, yet Lian's pleas were so urgent that the campaign was postponed for a long while. Then Wang Lian died. His son Wang Shan succeeded him and rose to administrator of Jiangyang.

向朗字巨達，襄陽宜城人也。 〈《襄阳记》曰：朗少师事司马德操，与徐元直、韩德高、庞士元皆亲善。〉 荊州牧劉表以為臨沮長。 表卒，歸先主。 先主定江南，使朗督秭歸、夷道、巫 （山） 、夷陵四縣軍民事。 蜀既平，以朗為巴西太守，頃之轉任，又徙房陵。 后主践阼，为步兵校尉，代王连领丞相长史。 丞相亮南征，朗留统后事。 五年，隨亮漢中。 朗素與馬謖善，謖逃亡，朗知情不舉，亮恨之，免官還成都。 數年，為光祿勳，亮卒後徒左將軍，追論舊功，封顯明亭侯，位特進。 初，朗少時雖涉獵文學，然不治素檢，以吏能稱。 自去長史，優遊無事垂三十年， 〈臣松之案：朗坐马谡免长史，则建兴六年中也。 朗至延熙十年卒，整二十年耳，此云“三十”，字之误也。〉 乃更潛心典籍，孜孜不倦。 年逾八十，猶手自校書，刊定謬誤，積聚篇卷，於時最多。 開門接賓，誘納後進，但講論古義，不干時事，以是見稱。 上自執政，下及童冠，皆敬重焉。 延熙十年卒。 〈襄阳记曰：朗遗言戒子曰：“传称师克在和不在众，此言天地和则万物生，君臣和则国家平，九族和则动得所求，静得所安，是以圣人守和，以存以亡也。 吾，楚国之小子耳，而早丧所天，为二兄所诱养，使其性行不随禄利以堕。 今但贫耳； 贫非人患，惟和为贵，汝其勉之！ ”〉 子條嗣，景耀中為御史中丞。 〈襄阳记曰：条字文豹，亦博学多识，入晋为江阳太守、南中军司马。〉
Xiang Lang, courtesy name Jufa, came from Yicheng in Xiangyang. The Xiangyang Record notes that in youth he studied under Sima Hui and was close to Xu Shu, Han Song, and Pang Tong. Liu Biao, as shepherd of Jing Province, made him magistrate of Linju. After Liu Biao died he joined the First Lord. After the First Lord secured the south bank of the Yangzi, he put Lang in overall charge of Zigui, Yidao, and Wu (the text inserts the character “mountain,” yielding “Wushan”) and Yiling—four counties in all, with charge of their military and civil affairs. Once Shu was pacified he became administrator of Ba, served briefly elsewhere, and was then posted to Fangling. When the Later Lord took the throne, Lang was made colonel of the footmen and succeeded Wang Lian as chief clerk to the chancellor. While Chancellor Liang marched south, Lang remained behind to manage the rear administration. In the fifth year of Jianxing he accompanied Liang to Hanzhong. Lang had long been friendly with Ma Su; when Su deserted after his defeat, Lang knew but said nothing. Liang was furious, dismissed him, and sent him home to Chengdu. Years later he became minister of the household, and after Liang's death was shifted to general of the left. His earlier service was remembered and he was enfeoffed marquis of the Xianming village with the rank of special advancement. In his youth he had read widely but cared little for conventional restraint; people valued him chiefly as an administrator. After losing the chief clerkship he lived at ease for almost thirty years, Pei Songzhi remarks: Lang lost the chief clerk's post over Ma Su in the sixth year of Jianxing. He died in the tenth year of Yanxi— only twenty years later— so the "thirty" here is a scribal mistake. He then buried himself in the classics with tireless devotion. Past eighty he still collated texts with his own hand, emending mistakes, and amassed more rolls than anyone of his day. He welcomed students and nurtured the young, confining his teaching to antiquity and never meddling in politics— for which he was widely admired. From high ministers down to lads in scholars' caps, everyone held him in esteem. He died in the tenth year of Yanxi. The Xiangyang Record preserves his deathbed advice to his sons: "The canon says victory lies in unity, not in numbers. When heaven and earth are at peace the myriad things thrive; when lord and subject are at peace the realm is steady; when the wider family is at peace, you find what you seek in action and rest in repose. That is why the sage cherishes harmony as the root of survival or ruin. I am only a boy from Chu who lost his father too soon and was raised by two older brothers who guided me, so that my character never slid into greed for rank and gain. Today I have nothing but poverty; poverty itself is no disaster; harmony is what counts— strive for that above all! Here the quotation and the annotation block come to a close. His son Xiang Tiao succeeded him and, during the Jingyao years, became palace secretary. The Xiangyang Record adds that Tiao's courtesy name was Wenbao, that he was as erudite as his father, and that under the Jin he rose to administrator of Jiangyang and army marshal of the southern interior.

張裔字君嗣，蜀郡成都人也。 治《公羊春秋》，博涉《史》、《漢》。 汝南許文休入蜀，謂裔干裡敏捷，是中夏鐘元常之倫也。 劉璋時，舉孝廉，為魚復長，還州署從事，領帳下司馬。 張飛自荊州由墊江入，璋授裔兵，拒張飛於德陽陌下，軍敗，還成都。 為璋奉使詣先主，先主許以禮其君而安其人也，裔還，城門乃開。 先主以裔巴郡太守，還為司金中郎將，典作農戰之器。 先是，益州郡殺太守正昂，耆率雍闓恩信著於南土，使命周旋，遠通孫權。 乃以裔為益州太守，逕往至郡。 闓遂趑趄不賓，假鬼教曰：「張府君如瓠壺，外雖澤而內實粗，不足殺，令縛與吳。 」於是遂送裔於權。
Zhang Yi, courtesy name Junsu, came from Chengdu in Shu commandery. He specialized in 《the Gongyang commentary on the Spring and Autumn Annals》 and ranged widely through 《the Records of the Historian》 and 《the Book of Han》. When Xu Shao of Runan came into Shu, he pronounced Yi as sharp and resourceful as anyone within a thousand li— the equal of Zhong Yao of the heartland. Under Liu Zhang he earned recommendation as filial and incorrupt, served as magistrate of Yufu, then came back to headquarters as an adjutant commanding the marshal of the personal guard. Zhang Fei advanced from Jing Province via Dianjiang. Liu Zhang armed Yi and sent him to block Zhang Fei on the Deyang plain; Yi's force was routed and he fell back to Chengdu. Liu Zhang then dispatched Yi as envoy to the First Lord, who pledged to honor Liu Zhang by rite and to spare the people. Yi brought back that promise, and only then did the gates open. The First Lord named Yi administrator of Ba, then recalled him to the post of colonel director of metals, responsible for forging tools for both tillage and campaigning. Earlier the people of Yi Province commandery had slain their prefect Zheng Ang. Still hoping to win Da as an ally beyond the border, Liang finally wrote: "During the last southern expedition the year closed (before I could) [before I could] return I met Li Hong at Hanyang. His news moved me deeply, for I know the purpose you once avowed— surely you do not mean to cling to hollow titles while we remain sundered!" Alas, my friend, Liu Feng's bullying of you was what soured the First Lord's faith in treating men generously. Hong also said Wang Chong invented rumors, yet you read my heart and refused to believe him. Trusting the plain words of my earlier memorial and the friendship we once shared, I gaze eastward and send this note. Once the letter reached him, he and Liang traded several messages, and he began plotting to revolt from Wei. Wei sent Sima Yi against him; Da was caught and killed at once. Liang never marched to his aid, for he knew Da lacked genuine loyalty. Under Jiang Wan's administration Shi was named remonstrance counsellor and ended his days in private life.
18
王沖者，廣漢人也。 為牙門將，統屬江州李嚴。 為嚴所疾，懼罪降魏。 魏以沖為樂陵太守。 〈孙盛蜀世谱曰：诗子立，晋散骑常侍。 自后益州诸费有名位者，多是诗之后也。〉
Wang Chong came from Guanghan. He served as camp-gate general under Li Yan, the Jiangzhou commander. Yan hated him; dreading a reckoning, Chong fled to Wei. Wei made him prefect of Leling. Sun Sheng's Shu genealogy records that Shi's son Fei Li rose to supernumerary palace attendant and cavalier attendant-in-ordinary under the Jin. Most of the prominent Fei clansmen in Yi Province thereafter traced their line to Shi.

评曰：霍峻孤城不倾，王连固节不移，向朗好学不倦，张裔肤敏应机，杨洪乃心忠公，费诗率意而言，皆有可纪焉。 以先主之广济，诸葛之准绳，诗吐直言，犹用陵迟，况庸后乎哉！
The historian concludes: Huo Jun held a single citadel unbroken; Wang Lian kept his integrity unbent; Xiang Lang never tired of study; Zhang Yi was alert and resourceful; Yang Hong gave his whole heart to the state; Fei Shi spoke his mind without flattery— each deserves to be remembered. Even under a ruler as magnanimous as the First Lord and a minister as exacting as Zhuge Liang, blunt counsel earned Fei Shi only demotion— what hope, then, for lesser lords who followed?
