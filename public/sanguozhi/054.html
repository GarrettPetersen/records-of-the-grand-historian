周瑜字公瑾，廬江舒人也。 從祖父景，景子忠，皆為漢太尉。 〈謝承《後漢書》曰：景字仲鄉，少以廉能見稱，以明學察孝廉，辟公府。 後為豫州刺史，辟汝南陳蕃為別駕，潁川李膺、荀緄、杜密、沛國硃寓為從事，皆天下英俊之士也。 稍遷至尚書令，遂登太尉。 張璠《漢紀》曰：景父榮，章、和世為尚書令。 初景歷位牧守，好善愛士，每歲舉孝廉，延請入，上後堂，與家人宴會，如此者數四。 及贈送既備，又選用其子弟，常稱曰：「移臣作子，於政何有？ 」先是，司徒韓縯為河內太守，在公無私，所舉一辭而已，後亦不及其門戶，曰：「我舉若可矣，不令恩偏稱一家也。 」當時論者，或兩譏焉。〉 父異，洛陽令。 瑜長壯有姿貌。 初，孫堅與義兵討董卓，徙家於舒。 堅子策與瑜同年，獨相友善，瑜推道南大宅以舍策，升堂拜母，有無通共。 瑜從父尚為丹楊太守，瑜往省之。 會策將東渡，到歷陽，馳書報瑜，瑜將兵迎策。 策大喜曰：「吾得卿。 諧也。 」遂從攻橫江、當利，皆拔之。 乃渡江擊秣陵，破笮融、薛禮。 轉下湖孰、江乘，進入曲阿。 劉繇奔走，而策之眾已數萬矣。 因謂瑜曰：「吾以此眾取吳會平山越已足。 卿還鎮丹楊。 」瑜還。 頃之，袁術遣從弟胤代尚為太守，而瑜與尚俱還壽春。 術欲以瑜為將，瑜觀術終無所成，故求為居巢長，欲假塗東歸，術聽之。 遂自居巢還吳。 是歲，建安三年也。
Zhou Yu, courtesy Gongjin, came from Shu county in Lujiang commandery. His grandfather’s cousin Zhou Jing and Jing’s son Zhou Zhong each rose to Han grand commandant. Xie Cheng’s *Later Han History* records Zhou Jing, courtesy Zhongxiang, famed young for honesty and competence, nominated flourishing talent on scholarly merit, then summoned to the capital ministries. As governor of Yu he hired Chen Fan of Runan as chief clerk and Li Ying, Xun Gun, Du Mi, and Zhu Yu as staff advisers—each among the finest men of the age. He climbed step by step to director of the secretariat and then grand commandant. Zhang Fan’s *Han Annals* adds that Zhou Jing’s father Rong had held the same director’s post under Emperors Zhang and He. Zhou Jing, while serving as regional inspector and governor, loved nurturing talent: every year he nominated flourishing candidates, brought them to his rear hall, and feasted them with his family—again and again. After showering them with gifts he promoted their sons as well, remarking that turning clients into foster sons did the administration no harm. Minister Han Yan of Henei had taken the opposite tack—purely public recommendations, no follow-up visits—declaring he would not let patronage pool in one clan. Contemporaries argued both approaches had their faults.〉 Zhou Yu’s father Zhou Yi served as magistrate of Luoyang. Zhou Yu was a tall, powerfully built man of striking appearance. When Sun Jian marched west against Dong Zhuo with the coalition army, he resettled his household at Shu. Sun Ce, born the same year as Zhou Yu, became his closest friend; Zhou gave him the family mansion south of the lane, paid respects to Lady Wu in the hall, and pooled every resource with him. While Zhou Shang governed Danyang, Zhou Yu went to visit his uncle. Just as Sun Ce prepared to cross the river east, he wrote from Liyang; Zhou Yu marched out to meet him. Sun Ce exclaimed, “Now that I have you— —the thing is settled.” Together they stormed Hengjiang and Dangli and carried both forts. They crossed the Yangzi, stormed Moling, and broke Ze Rong and Xue Li. They swept down through Hushu and Jiangcheng into Qu’a. Liu Yao broke and ran while Sun Ce’s army swelled past ten thousand. Sun Ce then told Zhou Yu, “This army is enough to seize Wu and Gui commanderies and subdue the hill tribes. You go back and hold Danyang for me.” Zhou Yu obeyed and withdrew to Danyang. Soon Yuan Shu replaced Zhou Shang with his cousin Yuan Yin, so Zhou Yu and his uncle both reported to Shouchun. Yuan Shu wanted Zhou Yu as a general, but Zhou saw no future in him and asked instead for the minor post at Juchao, planning to slip back east through that assignment—and Yuan Shu agreed. From Juchao he made his way home to Wu. The year was Jian’an 3 (198 CE).

When Sun Ce turned toward Jingzhou he named Zhou Yu central protector of the army with acting authority over Jiangxia and brought him on the storming of Wan, which fell. Among the captives were the Qiao sisters, famed as the realm’s great beauties. Sun Ce married the elder sister; Zhou Yu wed the younger. Pei Songzhi argues that the Baqiu where Zhou Yu camped is the county of that name in the west, not the Baqiu on the lower river associated with later campaigns.〉

五年，策薨。 權統事。 瑜將兵赴喪，遂留吳，以中護軍與長史張昭共掌眾事。 〈《江表傳》曰：曹公新破袁紹，兵威日盛，建安七年，下書責權質任子。 權召群臣會議，張昭、秦松等猶豫不能決，權意不欲遣質，乃獨將瑜詣母前定議，瑜曰：「昔楚國初封於荊山之側，不滿百里之地，繼嗣賢能，廣土開境，立基於郢，遂據荊楊，至於南海，傳業延祚，九百餘年。 今將軍承父兄餘資，兼六郡之眾，兵精糧多，將士用命，鑄山為銅，煮海為鹽，境內富饒，人不思亂，汎舟舉帆，朝發夕到，士風勁勇，所向無敵，有何偪迫，而欲送質？ 質一入，不得不與曹氏相首尾，與相首尾，則命召不得不往，便見制於人也。 極不過一侯印，僕從十餘人，車數乘，馬數匹，豈與南面稱孤同哉？ 不如勿遣，徐觀其變。 若曹氏能率義以正天下，將軍事之未晚。 若圖為暴亂，兵猶火也，不戢將自焚。 將軍韜勇抗威，以待天命，何送質之有！ 」權母曰：「公瑾議是也。 公瑾與伯符同年，小一月耳，我視之如子也，汝其兄事之。 遂不送質。〉
In the fifth year of Jian’an Sun Ce died. Sun Quan assumed leadership of the house. Zhou Yu marched in for the obsequies and stayed at the capital as central protector of the army, sharing civil and military authority with chief clerk Zhang Zhao. The *Jiangbiao zhuan* relates that after Cao Cao’s victory over Yuan Shao his prestige soared; in Jian’an 7 he demanded a hostage from Sun Quan. Sun Quan called council; Zhang Zhao and Qin Song wavered while Quan himself refused the demand. Taking Zhou Yu to his mother’s chamber, he heard Zhou argue that tiny Chu had grown across nine centuries by refusing servitude— —whereas you command six commanderies, rich arsenals, loyal soldiers, salt and copper wealth, fearless marines—why bend the knee and send a hostage? A hostage would chain you to Cao Cao’s summons forever. At best you would win a hollow marquis title and a handful of followers—nothing beside ruling your own southland as “this orphan.” Better to wait and watch how the north shifts. If Cao Cao proves a true restorer of Han, you can still submit later. If he turns tyrant, his armies will consume him like unchecked fire. Stand firm, husband your strength, and await Heaven’s verdict—no hostage is needed!” Lady Wu ruled, “Zhou Yu speaks rightly. He is almost your brother’s age; I count him as a son—serve him as an elder brother.” The hostage was never sent.〉

Sun Quan cried, “That old rogue has meant to steal the throne for years—blocked only by the two Yuans, Lü Bu, Liu Biao, and me.” Those rivals are gone; only he and I remain—and we cannot share one sky.” Your counsel to fight matches my own heart—Heaven sent you to me.” The *Jiangbiao zhuan* adds that Sun Quan slashed his desk, swearing death to anyone else who mentioned surrender. That night Zhou Yu asked a private audience: “They panic over Cao’s boast of eight hundred thousand men without counting noses—foolish.” In truth he has perhaps 150,000 northerners, already tired, plus maybe 70,000 Jingzhou conscripts who do not trust him. Sick veterans herding wavering provincials make a noisy but brittle host. Fifty thousand good men can master him—do not fear.” Sun Quan clasped his shoulder: “Zhou Yu, you read my mind. Zhang Zhao and the rest think only of their households; only you and Lu Su stand with me—Heaven gave me you both.” He could not raise fifty thousand overnight but had thirty thousand ready with full train—Zhou Yu, Lu Su, and Cheng Pu would sail first while more men and supplies followed. Win if you can; if fortune turns, fall back on me—I will face Cao Cao myself.” Pei Songzhi notes that the first voice for defying Cao Cao was actually Lu Su’s. Liu Bei, shattered by Cao Cao, meant to retreat south across the Yangzi. At Dangyang he joined Lu Su in strategy, moved up to Xiakou, and sent Zhuge Liang to Sun Quan. Sun Quan dispatched Zhou Yu, Cheng Pu, and allies with Liu Bei to meet Cao Cao at Red Cliffs. Cao’s troops were already sickly; the first clash sent them reeling to the north bank. Zhou Yu held the southern shore. Huang Gai warned Zhou Yu: the enemy outnumbered them and a long stalemate would be ruinous. Yet Cao’s fleet was chained stem to stern—fire ships could scatter it.” They loaded dozens of war junks with kindling, straw, and lamp oil. They draped the hulls, flew command flags, and sent a letter feigning defection. The *Jiangbiao zhuan* preserves Huang Gai’s forged letter of surrender to Cao Cao. It pleaded that six southern commanderies could not forever hold off a million northern soldiers. Every Wu officer knew the odds—only Zhou Yu and Lu Su were painted as stubborn fools.” Surrender, the letter claimed, was the sober choice. Zhou Yu’s line, it said, could be broken at a stroke. Huang Gai promised to turn the battle the moment the fleets met.” Cao Cao interviewed the envoy in secret, muttering that he feared a ruse. If Huang Gai meant it, Cao promised rewards above any other defector.”〉 Fast skiffs trailed the fire ships, ready to row the crews clear once the blaze caught. Cao’s men lined the rail, craning to watch “Huang Gai come over.” Huang Gai cut the cables and the whole line burst into flame. A gale whipped the fire into the northern shore camps. Moments later, Smoke blotted out the sky; men and horses burned or drowned by thousands; Cao’s host fled toward Nan commandery. The *Jiangbiao zhuan* adds detail: ten fast boats stacked with oiled reeds under red awnings, dragon pennants snapping. A southeast wind drove the decoy squadron midstream; Huang Gai’s torch was the signal for a thunderous cry of “Surrender!” Cao’s men poured from their tents to watch the show. Two li from Cao’s lines the torches went up; wind and flame turned the chained fleet into an arrow of fire that consumed shore batteries as well. 以蒙為南郡太守，封孱陵候， 〈《江表傳》曰：權於公安大會，呂蒙以疾辭，權笑曰：「禽羽之功，子明謀也，今大功已捷，慶賞未行，豈邑邑邪？ 」乃增給步騎鼓吹，敕選虎威將軍官屬，並南郡、廬江二郡威儀。 拜畢還營，兵馬導從，前後鼓吹，光燿於路。〉 賜錢一億，黃金五百斤。 蒙固辭金錢，權不許。 封爵未下。 會蒙疾發，權時在公安，迎置內殿。 所以治護者萬方，募封內有能愈蒙疾者，賜千金。 時有針加，權為之慘戚，欲數見其顏色，又恐勞動，常穿壁瞻之，見小能下食則喜，顧左右言笑，不然則咄唶，夜不能寐。 病中瘳，為下赦令，群臣畢賀。 後更增篤，權自臨視，命道士於星辰下為之請命。 年四十二，遂卒於內殿。 時權哀痛甚，為之降損。 蒙未死時，所得金寶諸賜盡付府藏，敕主者命絕之日皆上還，喪事務約。 權聞之，益以悲感。
He named Lü Meng governor of Nan commandery and marquis of Chanling— The *Jiangbiao zhuan* says Lü Meng skipped the victory feast until Sun Quan coaxed him. Sun Quan added guards of honor, musicians, and full staff for a tiger general across two commanderies. Lü Meng returned to camp in full pomp.〉 Sun Quan paid him a hundred million cash and five hundred jin of gold. Lü Meng refused the treasure; Sun Quan would not hear it. The formal patent had not yet arrived. Lü Meng fell ill at Gong'an; Sun Quan brought him into the palace. He offered a thousand jin for a cure. Sun Quan watched through a wall, rejoiced at every bite of food, and lay sleepless when Lü Meng failed. A brief rally brought a general amnesty and court rejoicing. When he worsened Sun Quan attended him and had Daoists pray under the stars. He died in the inner hall at forty-two. Sun Quan mourned him with exceptional rites. Before death he returned every gift to the treasury and asked a plain funeral. Sun Quan wept the more for that humility.
37
蒙少不修書傳，每陳大事，常口占為箋疏。 常以部曲事為江夏太守蔡遺所白，蒙無恨意。 及豫章太守顧邵卒，權問所用，蒙因薦遺奉職佳吏，權笑曰：「君欲為祁奚耶？ 」於是用之。 甘寧粗暴好殺，既常失蒙意，又時違權令，權怒之，蒙輒陳請：「天下未定，鬥將如寧難得，宜容忍之。 」權遂厚寧，卒得其用。 蒙子霸襲爵，與守塚三百家，復田五十頃。 霸卒，兄琮襲候。 琮卒，弟睦嗣。
Young Lü Meng had dictated his state papers to scribes. Cai Yi once impeached him over troop affairs; Lü Meng bore no grudge. When a post opened he recommended his old critic Cai Yi—Sun Quan compared him to the noble Qi Xi. Sun Quan hired Cai Yi. When Sun Quan raged at Gan Ning, Lü Meng begged patience for so rare a fighter. Sun Quan indulged Gan Ning and reaped his service. His son Lü Ba inherited the title with three hundred tomb guards and fifty qing of land. When Lü Ba died, his brother Lü Cong took the marquisate. When Lü Cong died, Lü Mu succeeded.

孫權與陸遜論周瑜、魯肅及蒙曰：
Sun Quan said to Lu Xun of Zhou Yu, Lu Su, and Lü Meng—
39
公瑾雄烈，膽略兼人，遂破孟德，開拓荊州，邈焉難繼，君今繼之。 公瑾昔要子敬來東，致達於孤，孤與宴語，便及大略帝王之業，此一快也。 後孟德因獲劉琮之勢，張言方率數十萬眾水步俱下。 孤普請諸將，諮問所宜，無適先對，至子佈、文表，俱言宜遣使修檄迎之，子敬即駁言不可，勸孤急呼公瑾，付任以眾，逆而擊之，此二快也。 且其決計策意，出張、蘇遠矣。 後雖勸吾借玄德地，是其一短，不足以損其二長也。 周公不求備於一人，故孤忘其短而貴其長，常以比方鄧禹也。 又子明少時，孤謂不辭劇易，果敢有膽而已。 及身長大，學問開益，籌略奇至，可以次於公瑾，但言議英發不及之耳。 圖取關羽，勝於子敬。 子敬答孤書云：『帝王之起，皆有驅除，羽不足忌。 』此子敬內不能辦，外為大言耳，孤亦恕，不苟責也。 然其作軍屯營，不失令行禁止，部界無廢負，路無拾遺，其法亦美也。
Zhou Yu was a blazing strategist who broke Cao Cao and carved out Jingzhou—a standard almost impossible to match; you now walk in his footsteps. When Zhou Yu brought Lu Su east and introduced him, our first banquet already ranged the great theme of empire—that was my first delight in him. Then Cao Cao rode Liu Cong's surrender and boasted of a half-million host marching downriver. I polled my commanders; Zhang Zhao and Qin Song would have welcomed Cao Cao—until Lu Su alone said no and made me recall Zhou Yu for the battle. That was my second joy. His strategic clarity towered above the Zhang Zhao faction and the other waverers. His later advice to cede ground to Liu Bei was a flaw—but it cannotcels his two great services. Like the Duke of Zhou, I do not demand perfection; I forgave that lapse and honored his strengths, and often compared him to Deng Yu of Han. Young Lü Meng I took for a mere daredevil who feared no odds. Grown and lettered, his plans rivaled Zhou Yu's—second only in eloquence on the tongue. In the design to capture Guan Yu he outdid Lu Su. Lu Su once wrote that every founding age sweeps rivals aside and that Guan Yu was nothing to fear. That was bluster masking doubt—I indulged him and did not press the point. Yet his camps ran tight—orders absolute, districts orderly, not a coin taken from the road. That discipline was itself a gift.

【評】
Appraisal
41
評曰：曹公乘漢相之資，挾天子而掃群桀，新蕩荊城，仗威東夏，於時議者莫不疑貳。 周瑜、魯肅建獨斷之明出眾人之表，實奇才也。 呂蒙勇而有謀，斷識軍計，譎郝普，禽關羽，最其妙者。 初雖輕果妄殺，終於克己，有國士之量，豈徒武將而已乎！ 孫權之論，優劣允當，故載錄焉。
The historian's verdict: Cao Cao as chancellor, holding the emperor, sweeping the lords, fresh from crushing Jingzhou and thundering at the east—left every counselor wavering. Zhou Yu and Lu Su alone saw the clear course and stood above the panic—true prodigies. Lü Meng combined courage with guile—tricking Hao Pu, caging Guan Yu—the peak of his art. He began as a rash killer but ended in self-mastery—worthy of the title state knight, not a mere brawler. Sun Quan's judgments on the three ring true, so the text preserves them here.
