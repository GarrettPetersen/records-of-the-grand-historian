1
列傳第三十一徐湛之江湛王僧綽
Biography 31: Xu Zhanzhi, Jiang Zhan, and Wang Sengchuo
2
徐湛之，字孝源，東海郯人。 司徒羨之兄孫，吳郡太守佩之弟子也。 祖欽之，秘書監。 父逵之，尚高祖長女會稽公主，為振威將軍、彭城、沛二郡太守。 高祖諸子並幼，以逵之姻戚，將大任之，欲先令立功。 及討司馬休之，使統軍為前鋒，配以精兵利器，事克，當即授荊州。 休之遣魯宗之子軌擊破之，於陣見害。 追贈中書侍郎。
Xu Zhanzhi, styled Xiaoyuan, came from Tan in Donghai commandery. He was a grandson of Grand Tutor Xianzhi's elder brother and a nephew of Wu commandery Administrator Peizhi. His grandfather Qinzh had served as Director of the Secretariat. His father Kuizhi married Emperor Gaozu's eldest daughter, Princess Kuaiji, and held the posts of General Who Quells Might and administrator of Pengcheng and Pei commanderies. Gaozu's sons were all still young; because Kuizhi was a kinsman by marriage, Gaozu meant to entrust him with great responsibility and wanted him to earn merit in battle first. When Gaozu campaigned against Sima Xiuzhi, he put Kuizhi in command of the vanguard with picked troops and sharp weapons; once victory was won, he was to receive Jing province at once. Xiuzhi sent Zongzi Gui of Lu to rout him, and Kuizhi was killed in the fighting. He was posthumously made Vice Director of the Secretariat.
3
湛之幼孤，為高祖所愛，常與江夏王義恭寢食不離於側。 永初三年，詔曰：「永興公主一門嫡長，早罹辛苦。 外孫湛之，特所鍾愛。 且致節之胤，情實兼常。 可封枝江縣侯，食邑五百戶。 」年數歲，與弟淳之共車行，牛奔車壞，左右馳來赴之。 湛之先令取弟，眾咸歎其幼而有識。 及長，頗涉大義，善自位待。 事祖母及母，並以孝謹聞。
Zhanzhi lost his father young and was cherished by Gaozu; he often ate and slept beside Prince of Jiangxia Yigong and rarely left his side. In the third year of Yongchu an edict said: "Princess Yongxing, the eldest legitimate daughter of her line, suffered hardship early in life. Her grandson Zhanzhi is especially beloved. Moreover, as a descendant who upholds integrity, his disposition is truly out of the ordinary. Let him be enfeoffed as Marquis of Zhijiang county with a fief of five hundred households." When he was only a few years old he rode in a carriage with his younger brother Chunzhi; the ox bolted, the carriage broke, and attendants rushed to their aid. Zhanzhi first told them to rescue his brother; everyone marveled that even as a child he showed such sense. As he grew up he steeped himself in moral principle and knew how to keep his place with restraint. In serving his grandmother and his mother alike he won renown for filial devotion and circumspection.
4
元嘉二年，除著作佐郎，員外散騎侍郎，並不就。 六年，東宮始建，起家補太子洗馬，轉國子博士，遷奮威將軍、南彭城、沛二郡太守，徙黃門侍郎。 祖母年老，辭以朝直，不拜。 復授二郡，加輔國將軍，遷秘書監，領右軍將軍，轉侍中，加驍騎將軍。 復為秘書監，加散騎常侍，驍騎如故。
In the second year of Yuanjia he was named Assistant Gentleman of the Writing Office and supernumerary Attendant Cavalier-in-Ordinary, but declined both appointments. In the sixth year, when the Eastern Palace was first set up, he began service as Senior Libationer to the Heir Apparent, became Erudite of the National University, was promoted to General Who Exerts Might and administrator of Nan Pengcheng and Pei commanderies, and moved to Gentleman at the Yellow Gates. His grandmother was elderly; he pleaded palace attendance duties and refused the appointment. He was again given the two commanderies with the added title General Who Assists the State, became Director of the Secretariat while serving concurrently as General of the Right Army, was made Palace Attendant, and received the further title General of Valiant Cavalry. He again became Director of the Secretariat with the added title Regular Attendant Cavalier, retaining General of Valiant Cavalry as before.
5
會稽公主身居長嫡，為太祖所禮，家事大小，必咨而後行。 西征謝晦，使公主留止台內，總攝六宮。 忽有不得意，輒號哭，上甚憚之。 初，高祖微時，貧陋過甚，嘗自往新洲伐荻，有納布衫襖等衣，皆敬皇后手自作； 高祖既貴，以此衣付公主，曰：「後世若有驕奢不節者，可以此衣示之。 」湛之為大將軍彭城王義康所愛，與劉湛等頗相附協。 及劉湛得罪，事連湛之，太祖大怒，將致大辟。 湛之憂懼無計，以告公主。 公主即日入宮，既見太祖，因號哭下床，不復施臣妾之禮。 以錦囊盛高祖納衣，擲地以示上曰：「汝家本貧賤，此是我母為汝父作此納衣。 今日有一頓飽食，便欲殘害我兒子！ 」上亦號哭，湛之由此得全也。 遷中護軍，未拜，又遷太子詹事，尋加侍中。
Princess Kuaiji, as the eldest legitimate daughter, was honored by Taizu; no matter in the household, great or small, was undertaken until she had been consulted. On the western campaign against Xie Hui, he had the princess remain within the palace precincts to oversee the six palaces in general. Whenever something displeased her she would break into loud weeping; the emperor was deeply wary of this. Earlier, when Gaozu was still obscure, his poverty had been extreme; he once went himself to Xinzhou to cut reeds, and had cotton jacket, padded coat, and other garments—all made by Empress Dowager Jing with her own hands; After Gaozu rose to eminence he gave these garments to the princess, saying, "If later generations produce someone arrogant, extravagant, and unrestrained, you may show them these clothes." Zhanzhi won the favor of Grand General Prince of Pengcheng Yikang and associated rather closely with Liu Tan and others. When Liu Tan fell from favor the affair implicated Zhanzhi as well; Taizu was furious and was about to impose the death penalty. Zhanzhi, anxious and at his wits' end, told the princess. That very day the princess entered the palace; once she had seen Taizu she wailed and threw herself from the couch, no longer observing the ceremonies owed a subject and wife. She packed Gaozu's cotton garments in a brocade pouch and flung them to the ground before the emperor, saying, "Your family was originally poor and lowly—this is the cotton garment my mother made for your father." Now that you have had your fill for one meal, you would destroy my son!" The emperor wept as well, and Zhanzhi was thereby spared. He was transferred to General of the Palace Guard but did not accept; he was then made Supervisor of the Heir Apparent's Household and soon afterward added as Palace Attendant.
6
湛之善於尺牘，音辭流暢。 貴戚豪家，產業甚厚。 室宇園池，貴游莫及。 伎樂之妙，冠絕一時。 門生千餘人，皆三吳富人之子，姿質端妍，衣服鮮麗。 每出入行遊，途巷盈滿，泥雨日，悉以後車載之。 太祖嫌其侈縱，每以為言。 時安成公何勖，無忌之子也，臨汝公孟靈休，昶之子也，並各奢豪，與湛之共以肴膳、器服、車馬相尚。 京邑為之語曰：「安成食，臨汝飾。 」湛之二事之美，兼于何、孟。 勖官至侍中，追諡荒公。 靈休善彈棋，官至秘書監。
Zhanzhi excelled at correspondence; his tone and phrasing flowed with ease. As a noble kinsman of a great house, his estates were very substantial. His halls, gardens, and ponds were unmatched among the titled gentry. The excellence of his performers and music was unmatched in the age. He had more than a thousand retainers, all sons of wealthy families of the three Wu regions, handsome in bearing and dressed in bright, fresh clothing. Whenever he went out on excursion the lanes were packed; on muddy rainy days he had them all carried in the rear carriage. Taizu disliked his extravagance and license and often spoke of it. At that time Duke of Ancheng He Xu, son of Wuji, and Duke of Linru Meng Lingxiu, son of Chang, were each sumptuous and proud; with Zhanzhi they vied in food, vessels and dress, carriages and horses. The capital said of them: "Ancheng for eating, Linru for ornament." Zhanzhi's excellence in both matters surpassed He and Meng together. Xu rose to Palace Attendant and was posthumously given the derogatory title Duke Huang. Lingxiu was skilled at pitch-pot and rose to Director of the Secretariat.
7
湛之遷冠軍將軍、丹陽尹，進號征虜將軍，加散騎常侍，以公主憂不拜。 過葬，復授前職，湛之表啟固辭，又詣廷尉受罪； 上詔獄官勿得受，然後就命。 固辭常侍，許之。 二十二年，范曄等謀逆，湛之始與之同，後發其事，所陳多不盡，為曄等款辭所連，乃詣廷尉歸罪，上慰遣令還郡。 湛之上表曰：
Zhanzhi was transferred to General Who Crowned Champions and Governor of Danyang, advanced in rank to General Who Subdues the Barbarians, and given Regular Attendant Cavalier; because of mourning for the princess he did not accept office. After the funeral he was offered his former posts again; Zhanzhi memorialized firmly declining and also went to the Minister of Justice to submit to punishment; the emperor ordered the prison officials not to accept his surrender, and only then did he take office. He firmly declined Regular Attendant Cavalier, and this was granted. In the twenty-second year Fan Ye and others plotted rebellion; Zhanzhi had at first gone along with them, then exposed the plot; what he reported was largely incomplete, and he was implicated by Ye's confessions; he therefore went to the Minister of Justice to admit guilt, and the emperor consoled him and sent him back to his commandery. Zhanzhi submitted a memorial:
8
賊臣范曄、孔熙先等，連結謀逆，法靜尼宣分往還，與大將軍臣義康共相脣齒，備于鞠對。 伏尋仲承祖始達熙先等意，便極言奸狀。 而臣兒女近情，不識大體，上聞之初，不務指斥，紙翰所載，尤復漫略者，實以凶計既表，逆事歸露； 又仰緣聖慈，不欲窮盡，故言勢依違，未敢縷陳。 情旨無隱，已昭天鑒。 及群凶收禽，各有所列，曄等口辭，多見誣謗； 承祖醜言，紛紜特甚。 乃雲臣與義康宿有密契，在省之言，期以為定，潛通姦意，報示天文。 末雲熙先縣指必同，以誑於曄，或以智勇見稱，或以愚懦為目。 既美其信懷可履，復駭其動止必啟。 凡諸詭妄，還自違伐，多舉事端，不究源統，齎傳之信，無有主名，所征之人，又已死沒，首尾乖互，自為矛楯。 即臣誘引之辭，以為始謀之證，銜臣糾告，並見怨咎，縱肆狂言，必規禍陷。 伏自探省，亦復有由。
The traitorous ministers Fan Ye, Kong Xixian, and others conspired in rebellion; the nun Fajing and Xixian's emissaries traveled back and forth, and with Grand General Yikang they were as lip to teeth—all fully set forth in the interrogation record. Reflecting on how Zhong Chengzu, once he grasped Xixian's intentions, at once set forth the treachery in full detail, I, because my children are close at hand, did not grasp the larger picture: at the first report I did not press to denounce them, and what my letters recorded was especially cursory—truly because once the wicked design was laid bare and the rebellion revealed, and again because I relied on your sacred compassion and did not wish to pursue the matter to the end, my words were evasive and I did not dare set out every strand. My true intent, hiding nothing, has already been clear to Heaven's mirror. When the ringleaders were seized each gave his own account; Ye's oral statements largely slandered me; Chengzu's vile words were especially voluminous and confused. They even said I had long shared a secret pact with Yikang, that words exchanged inside the Secretariat fixed the appointed time, that I secretly conveyed treasonous intent and reported celestial signs. In the end they said Xixian's pointing finger would surely agree, to deceive Ye—or that I was praised as wise and brave, or held up as stupid and craven. They would extol my faith and disposition as reliable, then again alarm themselves that my movements must betray the plot. All such calumny contradicts itself: it raises many incidents but does not trace them to their source; the credentials it cites have no principal name; the persons it summons are already dead; head and tail clash and become mutual spears. They take my words of enticement as proof of initial conspiracy, resent my denunciation, and all alike heap blame; they give free rein to mad speech, surely intending ruin. Reflecting inward on myself, there is likewise cause.
9
昔義康南出之始，敕臣入相伴慰，晨夕覲對，經逾旬日。 逆圖成謀，雖無顯然，懟容異意，頗形言旨。 遺臣利刃，期以際會，臣苦相諫譬，深加拒塞。 以為怨憤所至，不足為慮，便以關啟，懼成虛妄，思量反覆，實經愚心，非為納受，曲相蔽匿。 又令申情范曄，釋中間之憾，致懷蕭思話，恨婚意未申，謂此僥倖，亦不宣達。
When Yikang was first sent south into exile, I was ordered to accompany him in consolation; morning and evening we met face to face for more than ten days. Though the rebellious design was not overt, resentment and a different bearing quite appeared in his words and looks. He gave me a sharp blade, expecting we would meet at the decisive moment; I bitterly admonished and urged him, and he deeply refused to listen. Thinking this was no more than the depth of personal hatred and not worth heed, I closed the matter; fearing it would prove groundless, I turned it over in my mind—truly a foolish heart, not an acceptance of the plot, still less a deliberate cover on my part. He also had me convey feelings to Fan Ye to resolve ill will between them, and win over Xiao Sihua—resenting that marriage ties had not been fulfilled—and said such chances should not be passed up; I also did not report these.
10
陛下敦惜天倫，彰于四海，籓禁優簡，親理咸通； 又昔蒙眷顧，不容自絕，音翰信命，時相往來。 或言少意多，旨深文淺，辭色之間，往往難測。 臣每懼異聞，皆略而不答。 惟心無邪悖，故不稍以自嫌。 慺慺丹實，具如此啟。 至於法靜所傳，及熙先等謀，知實不早，見關之日，便即以聞。 雖晨光幽燭，曲昭窮款，裁以正義，無所逃刑。 束骸北闕，請罪司寇，乾施含宥，未加治考，中旨頻降，制使還往，仰荷恩私，哀惶失守。
Your Majesty nurtures and cherishes heavenly kinship, making it manifest throughout the realm; restrictions on the princes are generous and simplified, and personal dealings with kinsmen are all permitted; Moreover I was once shown favor that I could not break off on my own; letters and entrusted messages were exchanged from time to time. Sometimes a little was said but much was meant, or the purport was deep but the wording shallow, and between tone and color one could often not be sure. I always feared questionable words and generally answered briefly or not at all. Only because my heart harbored no wicked rebellion did I not much distrust myself on that account. My earnest loyal truth is fully set forth in this report. As for Fajing's transmissions and Xixian's plots, I truly did not know early; on the day I was implicated I reported at once. Though dawn light illumines the dark and bends to expose my innermost sincerity, measured by righteous law there is no escaping punishment. I would bind my bones at the northern gate and ask punishment from the Minister of Crime; your gracious pardon has not yet applied correction; repeated imperial orders have sent me back and forth; I bear your private kindness and grieve in bewildered loss of composure.
11
臣殃積罪深，丁罹酷罰，久應屏棄，永謝人理。 況奸謀所染，忠孝頓闕，智防愚淺，暗於禍萌，士類未明其心，群庶謂之同惡，朝野側目，眾議沸騰，專信仇隙之辭，不復稍相申體。 臣雖駑下，情非木石。 豈不知醜點難嬰，伏劍為易。 而靦然視息，忍此餘生，實非苟吝微命，假延漏刻。 誠以負戾灰滅，貽惡方來，貪及視息，少自披訴； 冀幽誠丹款，儻或昭然，雖復身膏草土，九泉無恨。 顯居官次，垢穢朝班，厚顏何地，可以自處。 乞蒙隳放，伏待鈇钅質。
My calamity and accumulated guilt run deep; I have suddenly suffered harsh punishment and should long since have been cast aside, forever cut off from human fellowship. How much more when stained by treasonous conspiracy—loyalty and filial piety suddenly fail; wit guards against foolish shallowness, blind to the sprouting of disaster; gentlemen have not made my heart clear, the multitude call me of one wickedness with them; court and countryside fix their eyes on me, public talk seethes; they trust only words borne of enmity and no longer grant the slightest fair hearing. Though I am mean and low, I am not wood and stone by nature. Do I not know that disgrace is hard to bear and that falling on my sword would be easier? Yet I calmly draw breath and endure this remnant of life; truly it is not that I meanly cling to a slight existence or borrow extra moments before the water clock runs dry. It is sincerely because extinction of my guilt would leave malice for those who come after; greedy for the breath still in me, I venture a little self-revelation; hoping my hidden sincerity and loyal candor might perchance be clear, so that though my flesh be greased on meadow soil, the nine springs would hold no regret. To sit lofty in official rank, a stain defiling the court assembly—where in the world can such a thick face find footing? I beg to be cast down and dismissed, to lie prostrate awaiting the axe.
12
上優詔不許。 二十四年，服闋，轉中書令，領太子詹事。 出為前軍將軍、南兗州刺史。 善於為政，威惠並行。 廣陵城舊有高樓，湛之更加修整，南望鐘山。 城北有陂澤，水物豐盛。 湛之更起風亭、月觀，吹台、琴室，果竹繁茂，花藥成行，招集文士，盡遊玩之適，一時之盛也。 時有沙門釋惠休，善屬文，辭采綺豔，湛之與之甚厚。 世祖命使還俗。 本姓湯，位至揚州從事史。 二十六年，復入為丹陽尹，領太子詹事，將軍如故。 二十七年，索虜至瓜步，湛之領兵置佐，與皇太子分守石頭。 二十八年春，魯爽兄弟率部曲歸順，爽等，魯軌子也。 湛之以為廟算遠圖，特所獎納，不敢苟申私怨。 乞屏居田里，不許。
The emperor issued a gracious edict and would not allow it. In the twenty-fourth year, when mourning ended, he was transferred to Director of the Secretariat, holding concurrently Supervisor of the Heir Apparent's Household. He went out as General of the Forward Army and Governor of Southern Yanzhou. He was skilled in administration; authority and kindness went together. The old city of Guangling had a tall tower, which Zhanzhi further refurbished so that it looked south toward Zhongshan. North of the city were marshes and ponds, rich in aquatic creatures. Zhanzhi further raised the Wind Pavilion, Moon Terrace, Flute Tower, and Zither Hall; fruit and bamboo grew thick, flowering herbs lined the paths; he gathered literati and exhausted every delight of wandering—a splendor for the age. At that time there was the monk Huìxiū, skilled in composition, his diction brilliantly ornate; Zhanzhi was on very close terms with him. Emperor Xiaowu ordered him returned to lay life. His original surname was Tang; he rose to Clerk of Yangzhou. In the twenty-sixth year he returned to the capital as Governor of Danyang and Supervisor of the Heir Apparent's Household, retaining his general's rank. In the twenty-seventh year the Northern Wei reached Guabu; Zhanzhi led troops and staff officers and, with the Crown Prince, jointly held Shitou. In the spring of the twenty-eighth year Lu Shuang and his brothers led their followers in submission; Shuang and his brothers were sons of Lu Gui. Zhanzhi regarded this as the court's far-reaching strategy and especially commended it, not daring to press a private grudge. He asked to retire to the countryside; the request was denied.
13
轉尚書僕射，領護軍將軍。 時尚書令何尚之以湛之國戚，任遇隆重，欲以朝政推之。 凡諸辭訴，一不料省。 湛之亦以《職官記》及令文，尚書令敷奏出內，事無不總，令缺則僕射總任。 又以事歸尚之，互相推委。 御史中丞袁淑並奏免官，詔曰：「令僕治務所寄，不共求體當，而互相推委，糾之是也。 然故事殘舛，所以致茲疑執，特無所問，時詳正之。 」乃使湛之與尚之並受辭訴。 尚之雖為令，而朝事悉歸湛之。
He became Vice Director of the Secretariat while serving concurrently as General Who Guards the Army. At that time Director He Shangzhi, because Zhanzhi was a kinsman by marriage, favored him heavily and wished to hand court affairs over to him. He did not examine a single petition or suit. Zhanzhi also cited the Record of Offices and ordinance texts: the Director presents memorials from within and oversees everything; when the directorate is vacant the vice director assumes overall responsibility. He again turned affairs back to Shangzhi, and the two pushed responsibility onto each other. Censor-in-Chief Yuan Shu jointly memorialized to dismiss them; an edict said: "The director and vice director are entrusted with governing affairs; instead of jointly seeking what is fitting they push responsibility onto each other—censure is correct." Yet precedent is fragmentary and confused, which produced this dispute; they are specially not questioned, and the matter is to be clarified in due course." Zhanzhi and Shangzhi were then both ordered to receive petitions and suits. Though Shangzhi held the directorate, all court affairs went to Zhanzhi.
14
初，劉湛伏誅，殷景仁卒，太祖委任沈演之、庾炳之、范曄等，後又有江湛、何瑀之。 曄誅，炳之免，演之、瑀之並卒，至是江湛為吏部尚書，與湛之並居權要，世謂之江、徐焉。
Earlier, after Liu Tan was executed and Yin Jingren died, Taizu entrusted Shen Yanzi, Yu Bingzhi, Fan Ye, and others; later Jiang Zhan and He Yuzhi were added. After Ye was executed, Bingzhi dismissed, and Yanzi and Yuzhi both died, Jiang Zhan became Director of the Ministry of Personnel and with Zhanzhi jointly held crucial power—the age called them Jiang and Xu.
15
上每有疾，湛之輒入侍醫藥。 二凶巫蠱事發，上欲廢劭，賜浚死。 而世祖不見寵，故累出外蕃，不得停京輦。 南平王鑠、建平王宏並為上所愛，而鑠妃即湛妹，勸上立之。 元嘉末，征鑠自壽陽入朝，既至，又失旨，欲立宏，嫌其非次，是以議久不決。 與湛之屏人共言論，或連日累夕。 每夜常使湛之自秉燭，繞壁檢行，慮有竊聽者。 劭入弑之旦，其夕，上與湛之屏人語，至曉猶未滅燭。 湛之驚起趣北戶，未及開，見害。 時年四十四。 世祖即位，追贈司空，加散騎常侍，本官如故，諡曰忠烈公。 又詔曰：「徐湛之、江湛、王僧綽門戶荼酷，遺孤流寓，言念既往，感痛兼深。 可令歸居本宅，厚加恤賜。 」於是三家長給廩。
Whenever the emperor fell ill, Zhanzhi at once entered to attend him with medicine. When the witchcraft affair of the two villains broke out, the emperor wished to depose Shao and order Jun to die. But Emperor Xiaowu was not favored and was repeatedly sent to border principalities, unable to remain in the capital. Prince of Nanping Shuo and Prince of Jianping Hong were both loved by the emperor; Shuo's consort was Zhanzhi's younger sister and urged the emperor to name Shuo heir. At the end of Yuanjia, Shuo was summoned from Shouyang to court; once he arrived he again lost favor; the emperor wished to name Hong heir but disliked his irregular order of succession, and deliberation dragged on. With Zhanzhi he discussed matters in private, sometimes for days on end through the night. Each night he often had Zhanzhi hold the candle himself and walk the walls on inspection, fearing eavesdroppers. On the dawn when Shao entered to kill his father, that very evening the emperor talked with Zhanzhi in private until dawn, the candles still burning. Zhanzhi started up in alarm and hurried to the north door; before he could open it he was killed. He was forty-four. When Emperor Xiaowu took the throne, he was posthumously made Minister of Works with the added title Regular Attendant Cavalier, his former offices unchanged, and given the posthumous title Duke Zhonglie. Another edict said: "The households of Xu Zhanzhi, Jiang Zhan, and Wang Sengchuo have suffered bitter ruin; their orphaned survivors wander abroad; to speak of the past is grief upon grief." Let them return to their original residences and receive generous relief and gifts." Thereupon the three families received long-term grain rations.
16
三子：聿之、謙之，為元凶所殺。 恆之嗣侯，尚太祖第十五女南陽公主，蚤卒，無子。 聿之子孝嗣紹封，齊受禪，國除。
He had three sons: Yuzhi and Qianzhi were killed by the Prince of Evil Origin. Hengzhi inherited the marquisate, married Taizu's fifteenth daughter Princess Nanyang, died young, and left no sons. Yuzhi's son Xiaosi succeeded to the enfeoffment; when Qi received the abdication the fief was abolished.
17
江湛，字徽淵，濟陽考城人，湘州刺史夷子也。 居喪以孝聞。 愛好文義，喜彈棋鼓琴，兼明算術。 初為著作佐郎，遷彭城王義康司徒行參軍，南譙王義宣左軍功曹。 復為義康司徒主簿，太子中舍人。 司空檀道濟為子求湛妹婚，不許。 義康有命，又不從。 時人重其立志。 義康欲引與日夕，湛固求外出，乃以為武陵內史，還為司徒從事中郎，遷太子中庶子，尚書吏部郎。 隨王誕為北中郎將、南徐州刺史，以湛為長史、南東海太守，政事委之。
Jiang Zhan, styled Huiyuan, came from Kaocheng in Jiyang commandery and was the son of Xiangzhou Inspector Yi. He won renown for filial devotion during mourning. He loved letters and moral discourse, delighted in pitch-pot and the zither, and was also skilled in calculation. He began as Assistant Gentleman of the Writing Office, then became Acting Army Staff Officer to Prince of Pengcheng Yikang as Grand Tutor and Merit Officer of the Left Army to Prince of Nanqiao Yixuan. He again served as Chief Clerk to Yikang as Grand Tutor and as Attendant to the Heir Apparent. Minister of Works Tan Daoji sought Zhan's younger sister in marriage for his son; Zhan refused. Yikang gave an order; again he did not comply. Men of the time respected his firm resolve. Yikang wished to keep him at his side day and night; Zhan firmly sought an outside post and was made Interior Administrator of Wuling; he returned as Attendant of the Secretariat Staff, became Senior Attendant to the Heir Apparent, and moved to Gentleman of the Ministry of Personnel. When Prince of Sui Dan was North General of the Household and Governor of Southern Xuzhou, Zhan was made Chief Clerk and Administrator of Southern Donghai, and administration was entrusted to him.
18
元嘉二十五年，徵為侍中，任以機密，領本州大中正，遷左衛將軍。 時改選學職，以太尉江夏王義恭領國子祭酒，湛及侍中何攸之領博士。 二十七年，轉吏部尚書。 家甚貧約，不營財利，餉饋盈門，一無所受，無兼衣餘食。 嘗為上所召，值浣衣，稱疾經日，衣成然後赴。 牛餓，馭人求草，湛良久曰：「可與飲。 」在選職，頗有刻核之譏，而公平無私，不受請謁，論者以此稱焉。
In the twenty-fifth year of Yuanjia he was summoned as Palace Attendant, entrusted with confidential matters, held concurrently as Senior Rectifier of his native commandery, and became General of the Left Guard. At that time academic offices were reformed; Grand Marshal Prince of Jiangxia Yigong held concurrently Rector of the National University, and Zhan with Palace Attendant He Youzhi served concurrently as Erudites. In the twenty-seventh year he became Director of the Ministry of Personnel. His household was very poor; he did not pursue wealth, gifts filled his gate yet he accepted none, and he had no spare garments or leftover food. Once when summoned by the emperor he was washing clothes; he pleaded illness for a full day and went only after the clothes were finished. When an ox was hungry and the driver sought fodder, Zhan said after a long pause, "Give it drink." In his selection post he drew censure for severity, yet he was fair and unbiased, accepted no solicitations, and critics praised him on that account.
19
上大舉北代，舉朝為不可，唯湛贊成之。 索虜至瓜步，領軍將軍劉遵考率軍出江上，以湛兼領軍，軍事處分，一以委焉。 虜遣使求婚，上召太子劭以下集議，眾並謂宜許，湛曰：「戎狄無信，許之無益。 」劭怒，謂湛曰：「今三王在厄，詎宜苟執異議。 」聲色甚厲。 坐散俱出，劭使班劍及左右推之，殆將側倒。 劭又謂上曰：「北伐敗辱，數州淪破，獨有斬江湛，可以謝天下。 」上曰：「北伐自我意，江湛但不異耳。 」劭後燕集，未嘗命湛。 常謂上曰：「江湛佞人，不宜親也。 」上乃為劭長子偉之娉湛第三女，欲以和之。
When the emperor launched a great northern campaign the whole court opposed it; only Zhan endorsed it. When the Northern Wei reached Guabu, General Who Commands the Army Liu Zunkao led troops onto the river; Zhan was given concurrent command of the army, and all military disposition was entrusted to him. When the barbarians sent envoys to seek marriage, the emperor summoned Crown Prince Shao and the court to deliberate; all said it should be granted; Zhan said, "The barbarians are faithless—granting the request would do no good." Shao was angry and said to Zhan, "The three princes are in peril now—how can you stubbornly dissent?" His voice and expression were very fierce. When the session ended and all went out, Shao had his armed attendants and guards push him, nearly tipping him over. Shao also told the emperor, "The northern campaign ended in defeat and disgrace and several provinces were lost—only by executing Jiang Zhan can we answer to the realm." The emperor said, "The northern campaign was my own idea; Jiang Zhan merely did not dissent—that is all." Afterward at Shao's banquets Zhan was never invited. He often told the emperor, "Jiang Zhan is a flatterer and should not be kept close." The emperor then betrothed Shao's eldest son Weizhi to Zhan's third daughter, hoping to reconcile them.
20
上將廢劭，使湛具詔草。 劭之入弑也，湛直上省，聞叫噪之聲，乃匿傍小屋中。 劭遣收之，舍吏紿云：「不在此。 」兵士即殺舍吏，乃得湛。 湛據窗受害，意色不撓。 時年四十六。 湛五子恁、恕、憼、愻、法壽，皆見殺。 初，湛家數見怪異，未敗少日，所眠床忽有數升血。 世祖即位，追贈左光祿大夫、開府儀同三司，加散騎常侍，本官如故，諡曰忠簡公。 長子恁，尚太祖第九女淮陽長公主，為著作佐郎。
When the emperor was about to depose Shao, he had Zhan draft the edict. When Shao entered to kill his father, Zhan went straight to the Secretariat; hearing shouts and uproar, he hid in a small room nearby. Shao sent men to seize him; the lodging clerk lied, saying, "He is not here." The soldiers at once killed the lodging clerk and thus seized Zhan. Zhan braced himself at the window as he was struck; his expression did not flinch. He was forty-six. Zhan's five sons Ren, Shu, Cong, Xun, and Fashou were all killed. Earlier Zhan's household had repeatedly seen strange omens; a few days before his fall, several sheng of blood suddenly appeared on his bed. When Emperor Xiaowu took the throne, he was posthumously made Left Grand Master of Splendid Happiness with ceremonial equal to the Three Excellencies and Opening of a Government Office, with the added title Regular Attendant Cavalier, his former offices unchanged, and given the posthumous title Duke Zhongjian. His eldest son Ren married Taizu's ninth daughter Princess Huaiyang and served as Assistant Gentleman of the Writing Office.
21
王僧綽，琅邪臨沂人，左光祿大夫曇首子也。 幼有大成之度，弱年眾以國器許之。 好學有理思，練悉朝典。 年十三，太祖引見，下拜便流涕哽咽，上亦悲不自勝。 襲封豫章縣侯，尚太祖長女東陽獻公主。 初為江夏王義恭司徒參軍，轉始興王文學，秘書丞，司徒左長史，太子中庶子。 元嘉二十六年，徙尚書吏部郎，參掌大選。 究識流品，諳悉人物，拔才舉能，咸得其分。 二十八年，遷侍中，任以機密。 僧綽沈深有局度，不以才能高人。 先是，父曇首與王華並為太祖所任，華子嗣人才既劣，位遇亦輕。 僧綽嘗謂中書侍郎蔡興宗曰：「弟名位應與新建齊，超至今日，蓋由姻戚所致也。 」新建者，嗣之封也。 及為侍中，時年二十九。 始興王浚嘗問其年，僧綽自嫌蚤達，逡巡良久乃答，其謙虛自退若此。
Wang Sengchuo came from Linyi in Langye commandery and was the son of Left Grand Master of Splendid Happiness Tanshou. As a child he showed the measure of great accomplishment; in his youth all acknowledged him as a pillar of the state. He loved learning and thought with principle, and was thoroughly versed in court regulations. At thirteen Taizu received him in audience; he bowed and at once wept with choked sobs, and the emperor too was overcome with grief. He inherited the marquisate of Yuzhang county and married Taizu's eldest daughter Princess Dongyang Xian. He began as Army Staff Officer to Prince of Jiangxia Yikang as Grand Tutor, then became Literary Companion to Prince of Shixing, Secretary Director, Left Chief Clerk to the Grand Tutor, and Senior Attendant to the Heir Apparent. In the twenty-sixth year of Yuanjia he became Gentleman of the Ministry of Personnel and took part in governing the great selection of officials. He thoroughly knew grades of talent, was well acquainted with persons, and in promoting the able assigned each to his proper place. In the twenty-eighth year he became Palace Attendant and was entrusted with confidential matters. Sengchuo was deep, steady, and broad in vision; he did not set himself above others by talent. Earlier his father Tanshou and Wang Hua were both entrusted by Taizu; Hua's son Si had inferior talent and lighter rank and favor. Sengchuo once told Vice Director of the Secretariat Cai Xingzong, "My younger brother's name and rank ought to stand level with Xinjian; that I have risen above today is surely due to marriage kinship." Xinjian was Si's enfeoffment. When he became Palace Attendant he was twenty-nine. Prince of Shixing Jun once asked his age; Sengchuo, self-conscious of having risen early, hesitated a long while before answering—such was his modest self-restraint.
22
元嘉末，太祖頗以後事為念，以其年少，方欲大相付託，朝政小大，皆與參焉。 從兄徽，清介士也，懼其太盛，勸令損抑。 僧綽乃求吳郡及廣州，上並不許。 會二凶巫蠱事泄，上獨先召僧綽具言之。 及將廢立，使尋求前朝舊典。 劭於東宮夜饗將士，僧綽密以啟聞，上又令撰漢魏以來廢諸王故事。 撰畢，送與江湛、徐湛之。 湛之欲立隨王誕，江湛欲立南平王鑠，太祖欲立建平王宏，議久不決。 延妃即湛之女，鑠妃即湛妹。 太祖謂僧綽曰：「諸人各為身計，便無與國家同憂者。 」僧綽曰：「建立之事，仰由聖懷。 臣謂唯宜速斷，不可稽緩。 當斷不斷，反受其亂。 願以義割恩，略小不忍。 不爾，便應坦懷如初，無煩疑論。 淮南云：'以石投水，吳越之善沒取之。 '事機雖密，易致宣廣，不可使難生慮表，取笑千載。 」上曰：「卿可謂能斷大事。 此事重，不可不殷勤三思。 且庶人始亡，人將謂我無復慈愛之道。 」僧綽曰：「臣恐千載之後，言陛下唯能裁弟，不能裁兒。 」上默然。 江湛同侍坐，出閣，謂僧綽曰：「卿向言，將不大傷切直。 」僧綽曰：「弟亦恨君不直。」
At the end of Yuanjia Taizu thought much about affairs after his time; because Sengchuo was young, he was about to entrust him heavily; matters of court policy, large and small, were all referred to him. His elder cousin by the paternal line Hui was a man of pure integrity; fearing Sengchuo had grown too great, he urged him to restrain himself. Sengchuo then sought Wu commandery and Guang province; the emperor granted neither. When the witchcraft affair of the two villains leaked out, the emperor first summoned Sengchuo alone and told him everything. When about to depose one heir and establish another, he had him search out precedents of former dynasties. When Shao feasted his officers at night in the Eastern Palace, Sengchuo secretly reported it; the emperor also had him compile precedents since Han and Wei for deposing princes. When the compilation was finished it was sent to Jiang Zhan and Xu Zhanzhi. Zhanzhi wished to name Prince of Sui Dan heir; Jiang Zhan wished to name Prince of Nanping Shuo; Taizu wished to name Prince of Jianping Hong; deliberation dragged on. Dan's consort was Zhanzhi's daughter; Shuo's consort was Zhanzhi's younger sister. Taizu told Sengchuo, "Each of these men schemes for himself; there is no one who shares the state's anxiety." Sengchuo said, "The matter of establishing an heir depends on your sacred intent." I hold that you should decide swiftly and not delay. When one should decide and does not, one instead suffers disorder. I wish you would sever affection by righteousness and set aside small unwillingness to endure. Otherwise you should open your heart as before and have no need for suspicious debate. The Huainanzi says: "Cast a stone into water, and the skilled divers of Wu and Yue will retrieve it." Though the affair is secret, it can easily spread; you must not let trouble show on the surface and become a laughingstock for a thousand years." The emperor said, "You may truly be said able to decide great affairs. This matter is weighty and must be considered thrice with earnest care. Moreover the heir has only just been cast down; men will say I no longer have the way of kindness and love. Sengchuo said, "I fear that after a thousand years men will say Your Majesty could judge younger brothers but not sons." The emperor fell silent. Jiang Zhan had sat in attendance; leaving the pavilion he told Sengchuo, "What you just said will wound rather deeply with blunt directness." Sengchuo said, "I too regret that you were not direct."
23
及劭弑逆，江湛在尚書上省，聞變，歎曰：「不用僧綽言，以至於此。 」劭既立，轉為吏部尚書，委以事任，事在《二凶傳》。 頃之，劭料檢太祖巾箱及江湛家書疏，得僧綽所啟饗士並廢諸王事，乃收害焉，時年三十一。 因此陷北第諸王侯，以為與僧綽有異志，並殺僧綽門客太學博士賈匪之、奉朝請司馬文穎、建平國常侍司馬仲秀等。 世祖即位，追贈散騎常侍、金紫光祿大夫，諡曰湣侯。
When Shao committed regicide, Jiang Zhan was in the upper offices of the Secretariat; hearing the upheaval he sighed and said, "Had we taken Sengchuo's advice, it would not have come to this." After Shao was established he became Director of the Ministry of Personnel and was entrusted with affairs; the matter is recorded in the Biography of the Two Villains. Before long Shao examined Taizu's scarf-box and Jiang Zhan's household papers, found Sengchuo's reports on feasting officers and deposing the princes, and had him seized and killed; he was thirty-one. On this account he implicated the princes and marquises of the Northern Residence, holding that they shared Sengchuo's divergent intent, and also killed Sengchuo's clients Erudite Jia Feizhi, Court Attendant Sima Wenying, Regular Attendant Sima Zhongxiu, and others. When Emperor Xiaowu took the throne, he was posthumously made Regular Attendant Cavalier and Grand Master of Splendid Happiness with the Golden Seal, with the posthumous title Marquis Min.
24
初，太社西空地一區，吳時丁奉宅，孫晧流徙其家。 江左初為周顗、蘇峻宅，其後為袁悅宅，又為章武王司馬秀宅，皆以凶終。 後給臧燾，亦頗遇喪禍，故世稱為凶地。 僧綽常以正達自居，謂宅無吉凶，請以為第。 始就造築，未及居而敗。
Earlier, a vacant plot west of the Grand Altar of Earth had been Ding Feng's residence in Wu times; Sun Hao exiled his household from it. In the Eastern Jin it first served Zhou Yi and Su Jun; later it was Yuan Yue's residence, then Prince of Zhangwu Sima Xiu's—all ended in violent death. Later it was given to Zang Can, who also met considerable mourning calamity; the age therefore called it an ill-omened place. Sengchuo often set himself up as upright and penetrating, held that dwellings have no good or ill fortune, and asked to take it as his residence. He had just begun construction and, before he could move in, met ruin.
25
子儉嗣，升明末，為齊國尚書右僕射。
His son Jian inherited; at the end of the Shengming era he served as Right Vice Director of the Secretariat in the State of Qi.
26
史臣曰：甚矣，宋氏之家難也，仇釁所鐘，親地兼極，雖復傾天滅道，跡非嫌路，而災隙內兆，邪蠱外興，天性既離，愛敬同盡，探雀請熊，非無前釁，猜防之道，有未足乎。 世祖弱年輕躁，夙無朝寵，累任邊外，未嘗居中。 當璧之重，將由愛立，臣主回疑，事無蚤斷。 若使守器以長，命不待賢，則密禍自銷，危機可免。 聖哲之訓，豈欺我哉！ 昔山濤舉羊祜為太子太傅，蓋欲以後事委之，而羊公短世。 僧綽綢繆主心，將任以國重，而宮車晏駕。 二臣並以道德謙沖，名高兩代。 胙未中年，功謝成日，惜矣哉！
The historiographer says: How severe were the Song household calamities! Enmity and grievance converged, kinship and territory both reached their extreme; though heaven was overturned and the Way extinguished, the traces were not on a path of suspicion alone—yet disaster fissures sprouted within, wicked witchcraft rose without, natural affection was sundered and love and respect alike exhausted; seeking the sparrow and inviting the bear—there were prior provocations; were the ways of suspicion and guard not yet sufficient? Emperor Xiaowu in his youth was light and rash, long without court favor, repeatedly posted to the outer borders, never stationed in the center. The weight of holding the jade disk was to be settled through affection; lord and minister turned in doubt, and the affair had no early decision. If the heir had been kept by seniority and fate had not awaited the worthy, secret calamity would have extinguished itself and crisis could have been avoided. The teaching of the sage and wise—would it deceive us? Formerly Shan Tao recommended Yang Hu as Grand Tutor to the Heir Apparent, intending to entrust later affairs to him, but Yang Hu died young. Sengchuo bound close to the ruler's heart and was about to be entrusted with the weight of the state, but the emperor died before he could take office. Both ministers were alike for moral virtue and modest restraint, their names lofty across two generations. Their blessings had not reached mid-years; their merit faded before completion—how regrettable!
