列傳第二十三王華王曇首殷景仁沈演之
Biographies 23: Wang Hua, Wang Tanshou, Yin Jingren, and Shen Yanzhi
2
王華，字子陵，琅邪臨沂人，太保弘從祖弟也。 祖薈，衛將軍，會稽內史。 父，廞，太子中庶子，司徒左長史。 居在吳，晉隆安初，王恭起兵討王國寶，時廞丁母憂在家，恭檄令起兵，廞即聚眾應之，以女為貞烈將軍，以女人為官屬。 國寶既死，恭檄廞罷兵。 廞起兵之際，多所誅戮，至是不復得已，因舉兵以討恭為名。 恭遣劉牢之擊廞，廞敗走，不知所在。 長子泰為恭所殺。 華時年十三，在軍中，與廞相失，隨沙門釋曇永逃竄。 時牢之搜檢覓華甚急，曇永使華提衣襆隨後，津邏咸疑焉。 華行遲，永呵罵云：「奴子怠懈，行不及我！ 」以杖捶華數十，眾乃不疑，由此得免。 遇赦還吳。
Wang Hua, whose courtesy name was Ziling, came from Linyi in Langya. He was a younger cousin of Grand Protector Wang Hong on the paternal side. His grandfather Wang Hui had served as General of the Guard and Interior Minister of Kuaiji. His father Wang Yin had held the posts of Grand Master of the Crown Prince's Household and Left Chief Clerk of the Minister of Education. The family was living in Wu when, at the start of the Jin dynasty's Long'an era, Wang Gong took up arms against Wang Guobao. Wang Yin was then at home in mourning for his mother, but when Gong's summons arrived calling on him to mobilize, he immediately gathered a force to answer it. He made his daughter General of Chaste Resolve and staffed his command with women as officials. Once Wang Guobao had been killed, Wang Gong sent a summons ordering Wang Yin to stand his troops down. When Wang Yin had first mobilized, he had executed many people. By now he could no longer simply disband, and so he raised troops again, this time under the banner of punishing Wang Gong. Wang Gong dispatched Liu Laozhi against Wang Yin. Yin was defeated and fled, and his whereabouts became unknown. His eldest son Wang Tai was killed on Wang Gong's orders. Wang Hua was then only thirteen and was with the army. Separated from his father, he fled into hiding with the monk Shi Tan Yong. Liu Laozhi was then searching for Wang Hua with great urgency. Tan Yong had the boy carry a bundle of clothes and walk behind him, and the guards at every ferry crossing looked on with suspicion. When Wang Hua lagged behind, Tan Yong shouted abuse at him: "You worthless, lazy wretch—you can't even keep pace with me! " He then struck Wang Hua with a staff several dozen times. The onlookers no longer suspected them, and in this way the boy escaped capture. When an amnesty was declared, he returned to Wu.
3
少有志行，以父存亡不測，布衣蔬食不交遊，如此十餘年，為時人所稱美。 高祖欲收其才用，乃發廞喪問，使華制服。 服闋，高祖北伐長安，領鎮西將軍、北徐州刺史，辟華為州主簿，仍轉鎮西主簿，治中從事史，歷職著稱。 太祖鎮江陵，以為西中郎主簿，遷諮議參軍，領錄事。 太祖進號鎮西，復隨府轉。 太祖未親政，政事悉委司馬張邵。 華性尚物，不欲人在己前； 邵性豪，每行來常引夾轂，華出入乘牽車，從者不過二三以矯之。 嘗於城內相逢，華陽不知是邵，謂左右：「此鹵簿甚盛，必是殿下出行。 」乃下牽車，立於道側； 及邵至，乃驚。 邵白服登城，為華所糾，坐被徵； 華代為司馬、南郡太守，行府州事。
From youth he showed ambition and moral resolve. With his father's fate still unknown, he dressed plainly, ate sparingly, and kept to himself for more than ten years—a conduct widely admired at the time. Emperor Gaozu wished to draw on his talents, and so he formally announced Wang Yin's death and had Wang Hua enter mourning. When his mourning ended, Emperor Gaozu marched north against Chang'an while holding the titles General Who Guards the West and Inspector of Northern Xuzhou. He recruited Wang Hua as provincial chief clerk, then transferred him to chief clerk of the Guard-the-West headquarters, and afterward to registrar and attendant clerk. In every post he distinguished himself. When Emperor Taizu took up his post at Jiangling, he appointed Wang Hua chief clerk of the Western Palace Guard, then promoted him to advisory adjutant in charge of records. When Emperor Taizu was promoted to Guard-the-West, Wang Hua transferred with the headquarters once again. Emperor Taizu had not yet assumed personal rule, and he entrusted all government affairs to Marshal Zhang Shao. Wang Hua was by nature fond of outward display and could not bear to let anyone outrank him in splendor. Zhang Shao was extravagant by nature and whenever he went abroad usually had paired carriages in his train. Wang Hua, by contrast, traveled in a hand-drawn cart with no more than two or three attendants—a deliberate counter to Shao's display. Once, meeting inside the city, Wang Hua failed to recognize Zhang Shao in the bright light and said to his attendants, "That guard of honor is so grand—it must be His Highness going abroad. " He stepped down from his cart and stood aside on the road. When Zhang Shao drew near, Wang Hua was startled to see who it was. Zhang Shao once went up on the city wall dressed in plain white garments. Wang Hua impeached him for it, and Shao was recalled on that charge. Wang Hua replaced him as marshal and administrator of Nan Commandery, acting in the affairs of both headquarters and province.
4
先是，會稽孔甯子為太祖鎮西諮議參軍，以文義見賞，至是為黃門侍郎，領步兵校尉。 甯子先為高祖太尉主簿，陳損益曰：「隆化之道，莫先於官得其才； 枚卜之方，莫若人慎其舉。 雖復因革不同，損益有物，求賢審官，未之或改。 師錫僉曰，煥乎欽明之誥，拔茅徵吉，著於幽《賁》之爻。 晉師有成，瓜衍作賞，楚乘無入，蔿賈不賀。 今舊命惟新，幽人引領，《韶》之盡美，已備於振綱； 《武》之未盡，或存於理目。 雖九官之職，未可備舉，親民之選，尤宜在先。 愚欲使天朝四品官，外及守牧，各舉一人堪為二千石長吏者，以付選官，隨缺敘用，得賢受賞，失舉任罰。 夫惟帝之難，豈庸識所易，然舉爾所知，非求多人，因百官之明，孰與一識之見，執咎在己，豈容徇物之私。 今非以選曹所銓，果於乖謬，眾職所舉，必也惟良，蓋宜使求賢辟其廣塗，考績取其少殿。 若才實拔群，進宜尚德，治阿之宰，不必計年，免徒之守，豈限資秩。 自此以還，故當才均以資，資均以地。 宰蒞之官，誠曰吏職，然監觀民瘼，翼化宣風，則隱厚之求，急於刀筆，能事之功，接於德心，以此論才，行之年歲，豈惟政無秕蠹，民庇手足而已，將使公路日清，私請漸塞。 士多心競，仁必由己，處士砥自求之節，仕子藏交馳之情。 甯子庸微，不識治體，冒昧陳愚，退懼違謬。」
Earlier, Kong Ningzi of Kuaiji had served as advisory adjutant on Emperor Taizu's Guard-the-West staff and won praise for his literary learning. By this time he had become Gentleman Attendant at the Yellow Gate and concurrently Colonel of Foot Soldiers. Kong Ningzi had earlier served as chief clerk to Emperor Gaozu when he held the post of Grand Commandant. In a memorial on reforms he wrote, "In the work of elevating civilization, nothing comes before placing the right talent in office. In the method of selection, nothing surpasses having men exercise care in whom they recommend. Institutions may change with the times and reforms may differ in detail, but the pursuit of worthy men and careful appointment of officials has never changed. The Teacher's Gift and unanimous assent shine forth in the radiant edicts of reverent brilliance; pulling up the rushes to seek the auspicious is recorded in the lines of the obscure Hexagram Adornment. When the army of Jin achieved victory, rewards were granted at Guayan; when the army of Chu entered without resistance, Wei Jia offered no congratulations. Now old appointments are being renewed, and reclusive men look on with eager expectation. The full perfection of the Sha music is already complete in the setting of the governing framework. What remains incomplete in the Wu music may still lie in matters of principle and regulation. Although not every one of the nine offices can be filled at once, the selection of officials who serve close to the people should come first. I propose that every fourth-rank court official, together with every prefect and governor in the provinces, recommend one man fit to serve as a two-thousand-bushel chief administrator. The selection office should receive these names and appoint them as vacancies arise. Those who recommend worthy men should be rewarded; those who fail in their recommendations should be punished. The difficulty of being the sole ruler is not something ordinary understanding can easily grasp. Yet to recommend those you know is not to seek a multitude of candidates. What insight can match that of officials who truly know the men they recommend? When blame rests on oneself, how can one indulge private favoritism? This is not to say that the Selection Bureau's assessments are bound to err, nor that recommendations from all offices will necessarily be good. Rather, one should open broad paths in seeking the worthy and, in evaluating performance, judge by the fewest lapses. When talent and achievement stand far above the rest, promotion should honor virtue. A magistrate of Zhi'e need not be judged by years of service; a warden of Miantu should not be limited by rank and seniority. Below that level, talent and seniority should be balanced against one another, and seniority balanced against locality. Magistrates who oversee districts are indeed administrative posts, yet in observing the people's afflictions and aiding moral transformation, the need for depth of character and generosity is more urgent than skill with the brush. Effective service must connect with the heart of virtue. To judge talent on this basis, together with years of conduct, would not only keep government free of corruption and shelter the people like one's own limbs, but would also daily clarify the public road and gradually choke off private solicitations. When scholars compete in their hearts, benevolence must begin with oneself. Recluses will sharpen the integrity of self-reliance, and officeholders will conceal the eagerness that drives them to currying favor. I, Ningzi, am lowly and insignificant and do not understand the principles of governance. Presumptuously I offer this foolish view and withdraw in fear of error."
5
甯子與華並有富貴之願，自羨之等秉權，日夜構之於太祖。 甯子嘗東歸，至金昌亭，左右欲泊船，甯子命去之，曰：「此弑君亭，不可泊也。 」華每閒居諷詠，常誦王粲《登樓賦》曰：「冀王道之一平，假高衢而騁力。 」出入逢羨之等，每切齒憤吒，歎曰：「當見太平時不？ 」元嘉二年，甯子病卒。 三年，誅羨之等，華遷護軍，侍中如故。
Kong Ningzi and Wang Hua both harbored ambitions for wealth and rank. From the time Xu Xianzhi and his faction held power, they worked day and night to undermine the two men before Emperor Taizu. Kong Ningzi once returned east and came to Jinchang Pavilion. His attendants wished to moor the boat, but Ningzi ordered them away, saying, "This is the Regicide Pavilion—it cannot be moored here. " In his leisure at home Wang Hua often recited Wang Can's "Rhapsody on Climbing a Tower," chanting the lines: "I hope for the royal way to be unified and level, and to borrow the high road to exert my strength. " Whenever he encountered Xu Xianzhi and his faction going out or coming in, he would gnash his teeth in rage and sigh, "Will I ever live to see a time of peace? " In the second year of Yuanjia, Kong Ningzi died of illness. In the third year, Xu Xianzhi and his faction were executed. Wang Hua was transferred to General of the Guard while retaining his post as Gentleman Attendant.
6
子定侯嗣，官至左衛將軍，卒。 子長嗣，太宗泰始二年，坐罵母奪爵，以長弟終紹封。 後廢帝元徽三年，終上表乞以封還長，許之。 齊受禪，國除。 華從父弟鴻，五兵尚書，會稽太守。
His son Wang Ding succeeded to the marquisate, rose to the post of General of the Left Guard, and then died. His son Wang Chang succeeded to the title. In the second year of Emperor Taizong's Taishi era, he was stripped of his rank for reviling his mother, and the fief passed to Chang's younger brother Wang Zhong. In the third year of Yuanhui under the Deposed Emperor, Wang Zhong submitted a memorial asking that the fief be returned to Wang Chang. The request was granted. When Qi received the abdication, the fief was abolished. Wang Hua's younger male cousin on the paternal side, Wang Hong, served as Minister of the Five Offices of War and Administrator of Kuaiji.
7
王曇首，琅邪臨沂人，太保弘少弟也。 幼有業尚，除著作郎，不就。 兄弟分財，曇首唯取圖書而已。 辟琅邪王大司馬屬，從府公脩復洛陽園陵。 與從弟球俱詣高祖，時謝晦在坐，高祖曰：「此君並膏粱盛德，乃能屈志戎旅。 」曇首答曰：「既從神武之師，自使懦夫有立志。 」晦曰：「仁者果有勇。 」高祖悅。 行至彭城，高祖大會戲馬台，豫坐者皆賦詩； 曇首文先成，高祖覽讀，因問弘曰：「卿弟何如卿？ 」弘答曰：「若但如民，門戶何寄。 」高祖大笑。 曇首有識局智度，喜慍不見於色，閨門之內，雍雍如也。 手不執金玉，婦女不得為飾玩，自非祿賜所及，一毫不受於人。
Wang Tanshou came from Linyi in Langya and was the youngest brother of Grand Protector Wang Hong. From youth he showed scholarly ambition. He was appointed Gentleman Author but declined the post. When the brothers divided the family property, Wang Tanshou took only books and records. He was recruited as an aide to the Grand Marshal of Langya Wang and followed his lord in restoring the gardens and tombs at Luoyang. Together with his younger male cousin Wang Qiu he went to see Emperor Gaozu. Xie Hui was present at the audience. Emperor Gaozu said, "These gentlemen are both of noble lineage and lofty virtue, yet they are willing to bend their wills to military service. " Wang Tanshou replied, "Having followed the army of divine martial prowess, even a coward would be given firm resolve. " Xie Hui said, "The benevolent man indeed has courage. " Emperor Gaozu was pleased. When they reached Pengcheng, Emperor Gaozu held a great gathering at the Play-Horses Terrace, and all who were seated there composed poems. Wang Tanshou's poem was finished first. Emperor Gaozu read it through and then asked Wang Hong, "How does your younger brother compare with you? " Wang Hong replied, "If he were merely my equal, on whom would the family rely? " Emperor Gaozu laughed heartily. Wang Tanshou possessed discernment, breadth of vision, and wisdom. Joy and anger never showed on his face, and within his household all was harmonious. He would not handle gold or jade, and the women of his household were forbidden ornamental trinkets. Apart from salary and gifts from the throne, he would not accept the slightest thing from anyone.
8
太祖為冠軍、徐州刺史，留鎮彭城，以曇首為府功曹。 太祖鎮江陵，自功曹為長史，隨府轉鎮西長史。 高祖甚知之，謂太祖曰：「王曇首，沈毅有器度，宰相才也。 汝每事咨之。 」景平中，有龍見西方，半天騰上，廕五彩雲，京都遠近聚觀，太史奏曰：「西方有天子氣。 」太祖入奉大統，上及議者皆疑不敢下，曇首與到彥之、從兄華固勸，上猶未許。 曇首又固陳，並言天人符應，上乃下。 率府州文武嚴兵自衛，臺所遣百官眾力，不得近部伍，中兵參軍朱容子抱刀在平乘戶外，不解帶者數旬。 既下在道，有黃龍出負上所乘舟，左右皆失色，上謂曇首曰：「此乃夏禹所以受天命，我何堪之。 」及即位，又謂曇首曰：「非宋昌獨見，無以致此。 」以曇首為侍中，尋領右衛將軍，領驍騎將軍。 以朱容子為右軍將軍。 誅徐羨之等，平謝晦，曇首及華之力也。
When Emperor Taizu was Champion General and Inspector of Xuzhou, he remained stationed at Pengcheng and appointed Wang Tanshou chief clerk of his headquarters. When Emperor Taizu took up his post at Jiangling, Wang Tanshou rose from chief clerk to chief administrator, then transferred with the headquarters to become chief administrator of Guard-the-West. Emperor Gaozu knew him well and said to Emperor Taizu, "Wang Tanshou is steady, resolute, and possessed of breadth of character. He has the talent of a chief minister. Consult him in every matter. " During the Jingping era a dragon appeared in the west, soaring halfway up the sky beneath five-colored clouds. People throughout the capital gathered to watch. The Grand Astrologer memorialized, "In the west there is the aura of the Son of Heaven. " Emperor Taizu entered the capital to receive the great succession. The emperor and his advisers all hesitated and dared not proceed. Wang Tanshou, together with Dao Yanzhi and his older male cousin Wang Hua, firmly urged him on, but the emperor still would not consent. Wang Tanshou pressed his case again, citing the correspondence between Heaven and man, and only then did the emperor proceed. He led the civil and military officials of headquarters and province in strict self-defense with armed troops. The officials and forces sent from the capital were not allowed near his ranks. The middle army adjutant Zhu Rongzi stood outside the Pingcheng gate with sword in hand and did not ungird his blade for several tens of days. After he had set out on the road, a yellow dragon appeared and bore up the boat on which the emperor was riding. His attendants all paled in alarm. The emperor said to Wang Tanshou, "This is how Yu of Xia received the Mandate of Heaven—how could I be worthy of it? " When he took the throne, he again said to Wang Tanshou, "Had it not been for Song Chang's lone insight, this could not have been achieved. " He appointed Wang Tanshou Gentleman Attendant, and soon afterward made him concurrently General of the Right Guard and General of Valiant Cavalry. He appointed Zhu Rongzi General of the Right Army. The execution of Xu Xianzhi and his faction and the pacification of Xie Hui were due to the efforts of Wang Tanshou and Wang Hua.
9
元嘉四年，車駕出北堂，嘗使三更竟開廣莫門，南臺云：「應須白虎幡，銀字棨。 」不肯開門。 尚書左丞羊玄保奏免御史中丞傅隆以下，曇首繼啟曰：「既無墨敕，又闕幡棨，雖稱上旨，不異單刺。 元嘉元年、二年，雖有再開門例，此乃前事之違。 今之守舊，未為非禮。 但既據舊史，應有疑卻本末，曾無此狀，猶宜反咎其不請白虎幡、銀字棨，致門不時開，由尚書相承之失，亦合糾正。 」上特無所問，更立科條。 遷太子詹事，侍中如故。
In the fourth year of Yuanjia the imperial carriage went out through the North Hall. An order was once sent at the end of the third watch to open the Broad Mo Gate. The Southern Office replied, "The white tiger banner and the silver-character signal staff are required. " They refused to open the gate. Left Assistant Director of the Secretariat Yang Xuanbao memorialized to dismiss Censor-in-Chief Fu Long and those below him. Wang Tanshou followed with a memorial saying, "Since there was neither an ink edict nor the banner and staff, although the order claimed to carry the emperor's intent, it was no different from a private note. In the first and second years of Yuanjia there were precedents for opening the gate a second time, but those were departures from proper practice. The present adherence to established rules is not contrary to propriety. Yet since they relied on established records, there should have been a full account of the grounds for hesitation and refusal. Since there was no such record, blame should instead fall on them for failing to request the white tiger banner and silver-character staff, which caused the gate not to open in time. This was a failure handed down through the Secretariat and should also be corrected. " The emperor made no further inquiries and instead established new regulations. He was transferred to Grand Tutor of the Crown Prince while retaining his post as Gentleman Attendant.
10
晦平後，上欲封曇首等，會宴集，舉酒勸之，因拊御床曰：「此坐非卿兄弟，無復今日。 」時封詔已成，出以示曇首，曇首曰：「近日之事，釁難將成，賴陛下英明速斷，故罪人斯戮。 臣等雖得仰憑天光，效其毫露，豈可因國之災，以為身幸。 陛下雖欲私臣，當如直史何？ 」上不能奪，故封事遂寢。
After Xie Hui was pacified, the emperor wished to ennoble Wang Tanshou and the others. At a banquet he raised his cup to urge them on and, patting the imperial couch, said, "Had it not been for you brothers in this seat, there would be no today. " The enfeoffment edict had already been completed. The emperor produced it and showed it to Wang Tanshou. Tanshou said, "In recent affairs disaster was about to take shape. It was thanks to Your Majesty's brilliant and swift decision that the guilty were punished. Although we your ministers were able to rely on Heaven's radiance and offer our tiny contribution, how could we turn the state's disaster into personal good fortune? Even if Your Majesty wished to favor us ministers, what of the upright historiographers? " The emperor could not override him, and so the matter of enfeoffment was dropped.
11
時兄弘錄尚書事，又為揚州刺史，曇首為上所親委，任兼兩宮。 彭城王義康與弘並錄，意常怏怏，又欲得揚州，形於辭旨。 以曇首居中，分其權任，愈不悅。 曇首固乞吳郡，太祖曰：「豈有欲建大廈而遺其棟樑者哉？ 賢兄比屢稱疾，固辭州任，將來若相申許者，此處非卿而誰？ 亦何吳郡之有。 」時弘久疾，屢遜位，不許。 義康謂賓客曰：「王公久疾不起，神州詎合臥治。 」曇首勸弘減府兵力之半以配義康，義康乃悅。
At the time his older brother Wang Hong recorded the affairs of the Masters of Writing and also served as Inspector of Yangzhou. Wang Tanshou was personally entrusted by the emperor and held responsibilities spanning both palaces. Prince of Pengcheng Liu Yikang recorded affairs together with Wang Hong and was often discontented. He also wished to obtain Yangzhou, and this showed plainly in his words. Because Wang Tanshou stood at the center and divided his power and responsibilities, Yikang became all the more displeased. Wang Tanshou firmly begged to be sent to Wu Commandery. Emperor Taizu said, "How could one wish to build a great hall yet leave aside its pillars and beams? Your worthy older brother has repeatedly claimed illness and firmly declined the provincial post. If in the future he should be granted his request, who but you should hold this place? What need is there for Wu Commandery? " At the time Wang Hong had long been ill and repeatedly asked to resign, but each request was refused. Liu Yikang said to his guests, "Lord Wang has long been ill and cannot rise. How can the heartland be governed from one's sickbed? " Wang Tanshou urged Wang Hong to cut the headquarters' military strength in half and assign it to Liu Yikang. Only then was Yikang pleased.
12

" The emperor said, "It is simply that my own house is declining. 13

Yin Jingren came from Changping in Chen Commandery. His great-grandfather Yin Rong had served as Grand Master of Splendid Happiness under the Jin dynasty. His grandfather Yin Mao had held the posts of Regular Attendant of the Dispersed Cavalry, Special Advance, and Left Grand Master of Splendid Happiness. His father Yin Daoyu died young. From youth Yin Jingren showed the capacity for great achievement. Minister of Education Wang Mi recognized it and gave him his daughter in marriage. He first served as rear army adjutant under Liu Yi and as acting adjutant to Emperor Gaozu when he held the post of Grand Commandant. He proposed that all officials be required to recommend talent, with promotion and demotion determined by whether those recommended proved able or not. He was transferred to Gentleman of the Secretariat on the Song Terrace, middle army adjutant to the Heir Apparent, then chief clerk, and afterward chief clerk to Swift Cavalry General Daolian. He went out to serve as Administrator of Hengyang, then entered court as groom of the Heir Apparent of Song, and was afterward transferred to Gentleman Attendant of the Central Secretariat. Yin Jingren did not pursue learning for the sake of literary composition. He was quick-witted and possessed thoughtful insight. He did not discourse on abstruse doctrine, yet he deeply understood fundamental principles. As for state codes, court ritual, old regulations, and recorded annotations, there was none he did not compile and record. Those who understood him knew that he harbored ambitions to serve the age. Emperor Gaozu knew him well and transferred him to Grand Master of the Crown Prince's Household.
14
太祖所生章太后早亡，上奉太后所生蘇氏甚謹。 六年，蘇氏卒，車駕親往臨哭，下詔曰：「朕夙罹偏罰，情事兼常，每思有以光隆懿戚，少申罔極之懷。 而禮文遺逸，取正無所，監之前代，用否又殊，故惟疑累年，在心未遂。 蘇夫人奄至傾殂，情禮莫寄，追思遠恨，與事而深，日月有期，將卜窀穸，便欲粗依《春秋》以貴之義，式遵二漢推恩之典。 但動藉史筆，傳之後昆，稱心而行，或容未允。 可時共詳論，以求其中。 執筆永懷，益增感塞。 」景仁議曰：「至德之感，靈啟厥祥，文母俔天，實熙皇祚。 主上聿遵先典，號極徽崇，以貴之義，禮盡於此。 蘇夫人階緣戚屬，情以事深，寒泉之思，實感聖懷，明詔爰發，詢求厥中。 謹尋漢氏推恩加爵，於時承秦之弊，儒術蔑如，自君作故，罔或前典，懼非盛明所宜軌蹈。 晉監二代，朝政之所因，君舉必書，哲王之所慎。 體至公者，懸爵賞於無私； 奉天統者，每屈情以申制。 所以作孚萬國，貽則後昆。 臣豫蒙博逮，謹露庸短。 」上從之。
Empress Dowager Zhang, who had borne Emperor Taizu, died early. The emperor served Lady Su, who had borne the empress dowager, with great reverence. In the sixth year Lady Su died. The emperor went in person to mourn at her deathbed. An edict was issued, saying, "From early on I have suffered partial loss, and my feelings are doubly ordinary. I have always wished to glorify my honored kin and give some expression to boundless grief. Yet the ritual texts have been lost and scattered, and there is nowhere to seek proper standards. Looking to earlier ages, what was adopted or rejected differs again. Therefore I have hesitated for years, and what is in my heart remains unfulfilled. Lady Su has suddenly fallen and died. Feeling and ritual have nowhere to lodge. Recalling distant regrets, my sorrow grows with the event. The days and months have their term, and burial is soon to be divined. I wish roughly to follow the Spring and Autumn Annals in the principle of honoring by rank, and to model myself on the Han dynasties' precedent of extending grace. Yet every action rests on the historiographer's brush and is transmitted to later generations. To act as the heart desires may perhaps not be fully acceptable. Discuss this together in detail now to seek the proper middle course. Holding the brush, I cherish this memory forever, and my feeling of being moved and overwhelmed only increases. " Yin Jingren submitted his opinion, saying, "The response of supreme virtue moves the spirits and opens auspicious signs. The civil mother matches Heaven and truly glorifies the imperial fortune. Your Majesty reverently follows the former canon, and in title reaches the utmost honor and elevation. In the principle of honoring by rank, ritual is complete in this. Lady Su stands in the steps of kinship as a kinswoman. Feeling runs deep because of the matter. The thought of the cold spring truly moves the sacred heart. A bright edict has been issued seeking the proper middle course. Carefully examining the Han practice of extending grace and adding noble rank: at that time they inherited the defects of Qin, and Confucian learning was scorned. The ruler made new precedents with no former canon to follow. I fear this is not a path our flourishing clarity should tread. Jin observed the two earlier dynasties and took its court governance from them. Every action of the ruler must be recorded—this is what the wise kings were careful about. Embodying utmost fairness means suspending rank and reward upon impartiality. Upholding the mandate of Heaven means often bending personal feeling to assert regulation. In this way one inspires trust throughout the myriad states and leaves a model for later generations. Your minister has undeservedly received broad instruction and humbly offers this mediocre view. " The emperor followed his advice.
15
丁母憂，葬竟，起為領軍將軍，固辭。 上使綱紀代拜，遣中書舍人周赳輿載還府。 九年，服闋，遷尚書僕射。 太子詹事劉湛代為領軍，與景仁素善，皆被遇於高祖，俱以宰相許之。 湛尚居外任，會王弘、華、曇首相係亡，景仁引湛還朝，共參政事。 湛既入，以景仁位遇本不逾己，而一旦居前，意甚憤憤。 知太祖信仗景仁，不可移奪，乃深結司徒彭城王義康，欲倚宰相之重以傾之。
Upon completing mourning for his mother and after the burial, he was recalled to serve as General of the Guard. He firmly declined. The emperor had the household steward perform the bow in his place and sent Palace Secretary Attendant Zhou Jiu in a carriage to bring him back to his headquarters. In the ninth year, when his mourning ended, he was transferred to Vice Director of the Masters of Writing. Grand Tutor of the Crown Prince Liu Zhan replaced him as General of the Guard. Zhan and Yin Jingren had long been on good terms. Both had been favored by Emperor Gaozu, and both were regarded as future chief ministers. Liu Zhan was still serving in an outside post. When Wang Hong, Wang Hua, and Wang Tanshou died one after another, Yin Jingren brought Zhan back to court to participate jointly in government affairs. Once Liu Zhan had entered court, he felt that Yin Jingren's rank and treatment had originally not exceeded his own, yet Jingren had suddenly moved ahead of him. He was deeply resentful. Knowing that Emperor Taizu trusted and relied on Yin Jingren and that this could not be shifted, he formed a deep alliance with Minister of Education Prince of Pengcheng Liu Yikang, wishing to lean on the weight of a chief minister to bring Jingren down.
16
十二年，景仁復遷中書令，護軍、僕射如故。 尋復以僕射領吏部，護軍如故。 湛愈忿怒。 義康納湛言，毀景仁於太祖； 太祖遇之益隆。 景仁對親舊歎曰：「引之令入，入便噬人。 」乃稱疾解職，表疏累上，不見許，使停家養病。 發詔遣黃門侍郎省疾。 湛議遣人若劫盜者於外殺之，以為太祖雖知，當有以，終不能傷至親之愛。 上微聞之，遷景仁於西掖門外晉鄱陽主第，以為護軍府，密邇宮禁，故其計不行。
In the twelfth year Yin Jingren was again transferred to Director of the Central Secretariat, while retaining his posts as Protector of the Army and Vice Director. Soon afterward he again served as Vice Director while heading the Ministry of Personnel, and remained Protector of the Army as before. Liu Zhan grew all the more angry. Liu Yikang accepted Liu Zhan's words and slandered Yin Jingren before Emperor Taizu. Emperor Taizu treated Yin Jingren with ever greater favor. Yin Jingren said to relatives and old friends with a sigh, "I brought him in, and once inside he bites people. " He then claimed illness and resigned his post. Memorial after memorial was submitted, but none was granted. He was ordered to remain at home and convalesce. An edict was issued sending a Gentleman Attendant at the Yellow Gate to inquire after his illness. Liu Zhan proposed sending men disguised as robbers to kill him outside, reasoning that even if Emperor Taizu knew, he would have his reasons and in the end could not wound the love between the closest of kin. The emperor faintly heard of this and transferred Yin Jingren to the residence of the Jin Princess of Poyang outside the Western Side Gate, making it his Protector-of-the-Army headquarters. It was close to the palace precincts, and so their plan could not be carried out.
17
景仁臥疾者五年，雖不見上，而密表去來，日中以十數； 朝政大小，必以問焉，影跡周密，莫有窺其際者。 收湛之日，景仁使拂拭衣冠，寢疾既久，左右皆不曉其意。 其夜，上出華林園延賢堂召景仁，猶稱腳疾，小床輿以就坐，誅討處分，一皆委之。
Yin Jingren lay ill for five years. Although he did not see the emperor, secret memorials went back and forth, numbering in the tens each day at midday. Great and small matters of court governance were invariably referred to him. The traces were closely guarded, and no one could peer into their inner workings. On the day Liu Zhan was arrested, Yin Jingren had his robes and cap brushed clean. He had long been bedridden with illness, and those beside him did not understand his meaning. That night the emperor went out to the Hall for Extending Worthies in the Hualin Garden and summoned Yin Jingren. Jingren still claimed illness in his feet and was brought in a small bed-cart to take his seat. All decisions on punishment and disposition were entirely entrusted to him.

The chief clerk performed the bow in his place. When the bow was finished, he immediately felt that his mind and feelings had gone awry. Emperor Taizu said that holding the provincial post was unfavorable and had him return to lodge at the Vice Director's lower office. He had held the province for little more than a month in all before he died. Some said he had seen Liu Zhan haunting him. He was then fifty-one years old. Posthumously he was granted Gentleman Attendant and Minister of Works, while his original posts remained as before. His posthumous title was Duke Wen of Cheng.

His knowledge was comprehensive, his vision far-reaching, and his service to the state utterly sincere. Our long intimacy fills me with grief redoubled. He was the people's hope and the state's pillar. To encounter such a man is rare. My regret and sighing run so deep they cannot cease. Do you feel the same? He is gone—what can be done! His fine achievements and loyal counsel were understood early and shone beforehand. His benevolent governance and abundant reputation truly remain with the people. Gazing recently upon his mound and tomb, I am moved by the past and stirred to mourning. An envoy may be sent to perform sacrifice."

Daojin's son Yin Heng, during Emperor Taizong's reign, served as Gentleman Attendant and Director of the Department of Revenue. 沈演之，字台真，吳興武康人也。 高祖充，晉車騎將軍，吳國內史。 曾祖勁，冠軍陳祐長史，戍金墉城，為鮮卑慕容恪所陷，不屈節，見殺，追贈東陽太守。 祖赤黔，廷尉卿。 父叔任，少有幹質，初為揚州主簿，高祖太尉參軍，吳、山陰令，治皆有聲。 朱齡石伐蜀，為齡石建威府司馬，加建威將軍。 平蜀之功，亞于元帥，即本號為西夷校尉、巴西梓潼郡太守，戍涪城。 東軍既反，二郡強宗侯勱、羅奧聚眾作亂，四面雲合，遂至萬餘人，攻城急。 叔任東兵不滿五百，推布腹心，眾莫不為用，出擊大破之，逆黨皆平。 高祖討司馬休之，齡石遣叔任率軍來會。 時高祖領鎮西將軍，命為司馬。 及軍還，以為揚州別駕從事史。 以平蜀全涪之功，封寧新縣男，食邑四百四十戶。 出為建威將軍、益州刺史，以疾還都。 義熙十四年，卒，時年五十。 長子融之，蚤卒。
Shen Yanzhi, whose courtesy name was Taizhen, came from Wukang in Wuxing. His ancestor Shen Chong had served as General of Chariots and Cavalry under the Jin dynasty and Interior Minister of Wu. His great-grandfather Shen Jin served as chief administrator to Champion Chen You, garrisoning the city of Jinyong. He was captured by the Xianbei Murong Ke, refused to submit, and was killed. Posthumously he was granted Administrator of Dongyang. His grandfather Shen Chiqian served as Commandant of Justice. His father Shen Shuren in youth showed capacity and substance. He first served as chief clerk of Yangzhou, adjutant to Emperor Gaozu when he held the post of Grand Commandant, and magistrate of Wu and Shanyin. In each post his governance won renown. When Zhu Lingshi campaigned against Shu, Shen Shuren served as marshal on Lingshi's Establishing Might headquarters and was additionally made General Who Establishes Might. In the achievement of pacifying Shu his merit was second only to the commander-in-chief. On the basis of his original title he was made Colonel of the Western Yi, Administrator of the commanderies of Baxi and Zitong, and garrisoned Fucheng. Once the eastern army had rebelled, the powerful clans of the two commanderies, Hou Mo and Luo Ao, gathered followers and raised rebellion. From all four sides they converged like clouds until they reached more than ten thousand men, pressing the siege urgently. Shen Shuren's eastern troops numbered fewer than five hundred. He extended trust with open heart, and none among the men failed to serve him. Going out he attacked and inflicted a great defeat. The rebel faction was entirely pacified. When Emperor Gaozu campaigned against Sima Xiuzhi, Zhu Lingshi sent Shen Shuren to lead troops to join him. At the time Emperor Gaozu held the title General Who Guards the West and appointed Shen Shuren marshal. When the army returned, Shen Shuren was made attendant clerk and assistant director of Yangzhou. For the achievement of pacifying Shu and preserving Fucheng intact, he was enfeoffed as Baron of Ningxin with a fief of four hundred forty households. He went out to serve as General Who Establishes Might and Inspector of Yizhou, then returned to the capital because of illness. In the fourteenth year of Yixi he died at the age of fifty. His eldest son Shen Rongzhi died young.

演之年十一，尚書僕射劉柳見而知之，曰：「此童終為令器。 」家世為將，而演之折節好學，讀《老子》日百遍，以義理業尚知名。 襲父別爵吉陽縣五等侯。 郡命主簿，州辟從事史，西曹主簿，舉秀才，嘉興令，有能名。 入為司徒祭酒，南譙王義宣左軍主簿，錢唐令，復有政績。 復為司徒主簿。 丁母憂。 起為武康令，固辭不免，到縣百許日，稱疾去官。 服闋，除司徒左西掾，州治中從事史。
When Shen Yanzhi was eleven, Vice Director of the Masters of Writing Liu Liu saw him and recognized his talent, saying, "This boy will in the end become a fine vessel. " His family for generations had been generals, but Shen Yanzhi restrained himself and loved learning. He read the Laozi a hundred times each day and became renowned for his pursuit of principle and reason. He inherited his father's separate noble rank as Fifth-Rank Marquis of Jiyang County. The commandery appointed him chief clerk. The province recruited him as attendant clerk, then as chief clerk of the western section. He was recommended as a Filial and Incorrupt candidate, became Magistrate of Jiaxing, and won a reputation for ability. He entered court as Libationer of the Minister of Education, then served as left army chief clerk to Prince of Nanqiao Liu Yixuan and as Magistrate of Qiantang, again achieving administrative merit. He again served as chief clerk to the Minister of Education. He entered mourning for his mother. He was recalled to serve as Magistrate of Wukang. He firmly declined but could not avoid it. After reaching the county for about a hundred days, he claimed illness and resigned his post. When his mourning ended, he was appointed left western aide of the Minister of Education and registrar and attendant clerk of the province.
23
元嘉十二年，東諸郡大水，民人饑饉，吳義興及吳郡之錢唐，升米三百。 以演之及尚書祠部郎江邃並兼散騎常侍，巡行拯恤，許以便宜從事。 演之乃開倉廩以賑饑民，民有生子者，口賜米一斗，刑獄有疑枉，悉制遣之，百姓蒙賴。 轉別駕從事史，領本郡中正，深為義康所待，故在府州前後十餘年。 後劉湛、劉斌等結黨，欲排廢尚書僕射殷景仁，演之雅仗正義，與湛等不同，湛因此讒之於義康。 嘗因論事不合旨，義康變色曰：「自今而後，我不復相信！ 」演之與景仁素善，盡心於朝庭，太祖甚嘉之，以為尚書吏部郎。
In the twelfth year of Yuanjia the eastern commanderies suffered great floods. The people were starving. In Yixing of Wu and Qiantang in Wu Commandery, a sheng of rice cost three hundred cash. Shen Yanzhi and Gentleman of the Ministry of Rites Jiang Sui were both additionally made Regular Attendants of the Dispersed Cavalry to tour the region and provide relief, with permission to act as circumstances required. Shen Yanzhi then opened the granaries to relieve the starving people. For each person who gave birth he granted one dou of rice per mouth. Where criminal cases held doubtful wrongs, he ordered them all released. The common people relied on him. He was transferred to assistant director and attendant clerk, and headed the provincial rectifier of his native commandery. He was deeply favored by Liu Yikang, and so remained at headquarters and province for more than ten years in all. Later Liu Zhan, Liu Bin, and others formed a faction wishing to drive out and disgrace Vice Director Yin Jingren. Shen Yanzhi by nature relied on righteousness and differed from Zhan and the others. Zhan therefore slandered him before Liu Yikang. Once, because his discussion of affairs did not accord with Liu Yikang's intent, Yikang changed color and said, "From this day forward I will no longer trust you! " Shen Yanzhi and Yin Jingren had long been on good terms. He devoted himself wholeheartedly to the court. Emperor Taizu greatly praised him and appointed him Director of Personnel in the Masters of Writing.
24
十七年，義康出籓，誅湛等，以演之為右衛將軍。 景仁尋卒，乃以後軍長史范曄為左衛將軍，與演之對掌禁旅，同參機密。 二十年，遷侍中，右衛將軍如故。 太祖謂之曰：「侍中領衛，望實優顯，此蓋宰相便坐，卿其勉之。 」上欲伐林邑，朝臣不同，唯廣州刺史陸徽與演之贊成上意。 及平，賜群臣黃金、生口、銅器等物，演之所得偏多。 上謂之曰：「廟堂之謀，卿參其力，平此遠夷，未足多建茅土。 廓清京都，鳴鸞東岱，不憂河山不開也。 」二十一年，詔曰：「總司戎政，翼贊東朝，惟允之舉，匪賢莫授。 侍中領右衛將軍演之，清業貞審，器思沈濟。 右衛將軍曄，才應通敏，理懷清要。 並美彰出內，誠亮在公，能克懋厥猷，樹績所蒞。 演之可中領軍，曄可太子詹事。 」曄懷逆謀，演之覺其有異，言之太祖，曄尋事發伏誅。 遷領國子祭酒，本州大中正，轉吏部尚書，領太子右衛率。 雖未為宰相，任寄不異也。
In the seventeenth year Liu Yikang went out to his princely fief. Zhan and the others were executed, and Shen Yanzhi was made General of the Right Guard. Yin Jingren soon died. Fan Ye, chief administrator of the rear army, was then made General of the Left Guard to share command of the palace guard with Shen Yanzhi and jointly participate in confidential affairs. In the twentieth year he was transferred to Gentleman Attendant, while remaining General of the Right Guard as before. Emperor Taizu said to him, "Gentleman Attendant commanding the guard—your reputation and substance are eminent and distinguished. This is essentially the chief minister's seat of convenience. You should exert yourself. " The emperor wished to campaign against Linyi. The court ministers were not of one mind. Only Inspector of Guangzhou Lu Hui and Shen Yanzhi supported the emperor's intent. When it was pacified, the ministers were granted gold, captives, bronze vessels, and other goods. Shen Yanzhi received a comparatively larger share. The emperor said to him, "In the planning within the temple hall you contributed your strength. Pacifying this distant barbarian land is not enough to warrant extensive enfeoffment. When the capital is cleared and purified and the imperial carriage sounds its bells at Mount Dai in the east, you need not worry that the rivers and mountains will not be opened. " In the twenty-first year an edict said, "To oversee military affairs overall and assist the Eastern Palace—such trustworthy appointments must be given only to the worthy. Gentleman Attendant and General of the Right Guard Shen Yanzhi has a pure career, integrity, and carefulness, with depth of thought and steady resolve. General of the Right Guard Fan Ye has talent that responds with quick intelligence and principles held with clarity and refinement. Both have beauty displayed within and without, sincerity and brightness in public service, and the ability to exert themselves in their plans and establish achievements in the posts they oversee. Shen Yanzhi may become Central Army Commander; Fan Ye may become Grand Tutor of the Crown Prince. " Fan Ye harbored treasonous designs. Shen Yanzhi noticed that something was amiss and reported it to Emperor Taizu. Ye soon had his plot exposed and was executed. He was transferred to head the Imperial Academy, became grand rectifier of his native province, was then transferred to Director of the Ministry of Personnel, and concurrently served as Commander of the Crown Prince's Right Guard. Although he had not become chief minister, the trust placed in him was no different.
25
素有心氣，疾病歷年，上使臥疾治事。 性好舉才，申濟屈滯，而謙約自持，上賜女伎，不受。 二十六年，車駕拜京陵，演之以疾不從。 上還宮，召見，自勉到坐，出至尚書下省，暴卒，時年五十三。 太祖痛惜之，追贈散騎常侍、金紫光祿大夫，諡曰貞侯。
He had long suffered from heart and qi ailments. His illness lasted for years, and the emperor had him conduct affairs while lying ill. By nature he loved to recommend talent and bring relief to those blocked and stagnant. Yet he maintained himself with humility and restraint. The emperor granted him female performers, but he did not accept them. In the twenty-sixth year the imperial carriage went to pay respects at the capital tombs. Shen Yanzhi did not follow because of illness. When the emperor returned to the palace he summoned Shen Yanzhi for an audience. Yanzhi exerted himself to reach his seat. After leaving and arriving at the lower office of the Masters of Writing, he died suddenly. He was then fifty-three years old. Emperor Taizu grieved deeply for him. Posthumously he was granted Regular Attendant of the Dispersed Cavalry and Grand Master of Splendid Happiness with the Golden Seal and Purple Cord. His posthumous title was Marquis Zhen.
26
演之昔與同使江邃字玄遠，濟陽考城人。 頗有文義。 官至司徒記室參軍，撰《文釋》，傳於世。 演之子睦，至黃門郎，通直散騎常侍。 世祖大明初，坐要引上左右俞欣之訪評殿省內事，又與弟西陽王文學勃忿鬩不睦，坐徙始興郡，勃免官禁錮。
Shen Yanzhi's former fellow envoy Jiang Sui, whose courtesy name was Xuan Yuan, came from Kaocheng in Jiyang. He possessed considerable literary learning. He rose to the post of recording secretary adjutant of the Minister of Education and compiled Literary Explanations, which was transmitted in the world. Shen Yanzhi's son Shen Mu rose to Gentleman at the Yellow Gate and Regular Attendant of the Dispersed Cavalry on Direct Duty. At the beginning of Daming under Emperor Shizu, Shen Mu was punished for summoning the emperor's attendant Yu Xinzhi to inquire into and comment on affairs within the palace offices. He also quarreled bitterly with his younger brother Shen Bo, literary instructor to Prince of Xiyang Wang. On these charges he was banished to Shixing Commandery, and Bo was dismissed from office and placed under restriction.
27
勃弟統，大明中為著作佐郎。 先是，五省官所給幹僮，不得雜役，太祖世，坐以免官者，前後百人。 統輕役過差，有司奏免。 世祖詔曰：「自頃幹僮，多不祗給，主可量聽行杖。 」得行幹杖，自此始也。
Bo's younger brother Shen Tong, during the Daming era, served as Assistant Gentleman Author. Earlier, the official attendants granted to officials of the five secretariats were not to be used for miscellaneous labor. During Emperor Taizu's reign, those punished by dismissal from office for this numbered a hundred people before and after. Shen Tong used attendants for light labor beyond proper limits. The relevant offices memorialized for his dismissal. Emperor Shizu issued an edict, saying, "Recently official attendants have often failed to serve properly. Their masters may, as appropriate, be permitted to apply the staff. " The permission to apply the staff to official attendants began from this point.
28
演之兄融之子暢之，襲寧新縣男。 大明中，為海陵王休茂北中郎諮議參軍，為休茂所殺，追贈黃門郎。 子曄嗣，齊受禪，國除。
Changzhi, son of Shen Yanzhi's older brother Shen Rongzhi, inherited the barony of Ningxin County. During the Daming era he served as advisory adjutant to the northern palace guard of Prince of Hailing Liu Xiumao and was killed by Xiumao. Posthumously he was granted Gentleman at the Yellow Gate. His son Ye succeeded. When Qi received the abdication, the fief was abolished.
29
史臣曰：元嘉初，誅滅宰相，蓋王華、孔甯子之力也。 彼群公義雖往結，恩實今疏，而任即曩權，意非昔主，居上六之窮爻，當來寵之要轍，顛覆所基，非待他釁，況於廢殺之重，其隙易乘乎！ 夫殺人而取其璧，不知在己興累； 傾物而移其寵，不忌自我難持。 若二子永年，亦未知來禍所止也。 有能戒彼而悟此，則所望於來哲。
The historian says: At the beginning of Yuanjia, the execution and destruction of the chief ministers was largely due to the efforts of Wang Hua and Kong Ningzi. Those lords in righteousness had ties from the past, but in favor they were in truth now estranged. Their posts were the same as former power, yet their intent was not that of the former lord. Occupying the exhausted line of the upper sixth, they stood at the crucial track of coming favor. The foundation of their overthrow did not wait for another pretext—how much more so when the weight of deposition and killing made the gap easy to exploit! To kill a man and take his jade disk is not to know that one thereby raises trouble for oneself. To overturn others and transfer their favor is not to fear that one will find it hard to sustain oneself. Had those two men lived out their full years, one still would not have known where later calamity would stop. If there are those who can take warning from them and understand this, that is what is hoped for from sages to come.
