Preface
3
昔八卦兆而天人之理著，九疇序而帝王之應明。 雖可以知從德獲自天之祐，違道陷神聽之罪，然未詳舉徵效，備考幽明，雖時列鼎雉庭穀之異，然而未究者眾矣。 至於鑑悟後王，多有所闕。 故仲尼作春秋，具書祥眚，以驗行事。 是則九疇陳其義於前，春秋列其效於後也。 逮至伏生創紀大傳，五行之體始詳； 劉向廣演洪範，休咎之文益備。 故班固斟酌經、傳，詳紀條流，誠以一王之典，不可獨闕故也。 夫天道雖無聲無臭，然而應若影響，天人之驗，理不可誣。 司馬彪纂集光武以來，以究漢事； 王沈魏書志篇闕，凡厥災異，但編帝紀而已。 自黃初以降，二百餘年，覽其災妖，以考之事，常若重規沓矩，不謬前說。 又高堂隆、郭景純等，據經立辭，終皆顯應。 闕而不序，史體將虧。 今自司馬彪以後，皆撰次論序，斯亦班固遠采春秋，舉遠明近之例也。 又按言之不從，有介蟲之孽，劉歆以為毛蟲； 視之不明，有蠃蟲之孽，劉歆以為羽蟲。 按月令，夏蟲羽，秋蟲毛，宜如歆說，是以舊史從之。 五行精微，非末學所究。 凡已經前議者，並即其言以釋之； 未有舊說者，推準事理，以俟來哲。
In antiquity the Eight Trigrams appeared and the principle of Heaven and man was made manifest; the Nine Categories were ordered and the responses of emperors and kings were clarified. Although one may know that following virtue wins Heaven's blessing and violating the Way falls into the crime of divine hearing, the omens and effects have not been fully set forth and a complete examination of the hidden and manifest remains wanting — though the times list such anomalies as tripods, pheasants, and courtyard grain, what has not been investigated remains numerous. As for illuminating and awakening later kings, much remains lacking. Therefore Confucius composed the Spring and Autumn Annals, fully recording auspices and calamities to verify conduct. Thus the Nine Categories set forth their meaning beforehand, and the Spring and Autumn Annals listed their effects afterward. When Fu Sheng composed the Great Commentary, the substance of the Five Elements first became detailed; Liu Xiang broadly expounded the Great Plan, and the texts on blessing and calamity became more complete. Therefore Ban Gu weighed the classics and commentaries and recorded each category and branch in detail — truly because the statutes of a single dynasty cannot be allowed to stand alone incomplete. The Way of Heaven, though without sound and without odor, responds like shadow and echo; the verification of Heaven and man — its principle cannot be deceived. Sima Biao compiled and gathered from Guangwu onward to investigate Han affairs; Wang Shen's Book of Wei lacked treatise chapters; all such disasters and anomalies were only compiled in the imperial annals. From Huangchu downward, over two hundred years, surveying its disasters and prodigies and examining them against events, they constantly accord like overlapping compasses and stacked carpenter's squares, not contradicting prior explanations. Further, Gaotang Long, Guo Jingchun, and others, grounding their words in the classics, in the end all found clear fulfillment. To leave them out without ordering them — the form of history would be damaged. Now from Sima Biao onward, all have been composed and arranged with discussion and prefaces — this too is Ban Gu's example of drawing remotely from the Spring and Autumn Annals to illuminate the near. Again, when speech is not followed, there is the calamity of shell-bearing creatures; Liu Xin took them to be hairy creatures; when vision is not clear, there is the calamity of naked creatures; Liu Xin took them to be feathered creatures. According to the Monthly Ordinances, summer creatures are feathered and autumn creatures are hairy — Liu Xin's explanation is appropriate, and therefore the earlier histories followed it. The subtleties of the Five Elements are not what shallow learning can exhaust. For all that have already been discussed by earlier commentators, I take their words to explain them; where no prior explanation exists, I infer by analogy from principle and fact, awaiting later sages.
4
五行傳曰：「田獵不宿，飲食不享，出入不節，奪民農時，及有姦謀，則木不曲直，謂木失其性而為災也。 」又曰：「貌之不恭，是謂不肅。 厥咎狂，厥罰恒雨，厥極惡。 時則有服妖，時則有龜孽，時則有雞禍，時則有下體生上之痾，時則有青眚、青祥。 惟金沴木。 」班固曰：「蓋工匠為輪矢者多傷敗，及木為變怪。 」皆為不曲直也。
The Five Elements Commentary says: "When hunts roll on without pause, feasts ignore sacrificial propriety, movement ignores measure, corvées steal the farming calendar, and treachery festers — then wood forfeits its office of bending and straightening; this means wood has lost its nature and become a disaster. " It also says: "When bearing lacks respect, this is called lacking solemnity. Its fault is madness, its punishment constant rain, its extreme wickedness. At times there are costume prodigies; at times turtle calamities; at times chicken disasters; at times ailments of the lower body growing on the upper; at times green internal misfortune and green auguries. Only metal encroaches upon wood. " Ban Gu said: "Generally when craftsmen make wheels and arrows many are damaged and ruined, and when wood becomes strange anomalies — " all of these are wood losing its bending and straightening."
5
木不曲直
Wood Loses Its Straightness and Suppleness
6
魏文帝黃初六年正月，雨，木冰。 接劉歆說，木不曲直也。 劉向曰：「冰者陰之盛，木者少陽，貴臣象也。 此人將有害，則陰氣脅木，木先寒，故得雨而冰也。 」是年六月，利成郡兵蔡方等殺太守徐質，據郡反，多所脅略，并聚亡命。 遣二校尉與青州刺史共討平之。 太守，古之諸侯，貴臣有害之應也。 一說以木冰為甲兵之象。 是歲，既討蔡方，又八月，天子自將以舟師征吳，戎卒十餘萬，連旍數百里，臨江觀兵。
In the first month of the sixth year of Huangchu under Emperor Wen of Wei, rain fell and encased the trees in ice. Following Liu Xin's explanation, this was wood losing its straightness and suppleness. Liu Xiang said: "Ice is the flourishing of Yin; wood is lesser Yang; it is the image of honored ministers. When such an official faces mortal peril, Yin first presses upon wood's Yang; the timber turns chill before the freezing rain seals it." That sixth month the Licheng garrison under Cai Fang murdered Prefect Xu Zhi and seized the commandery in revolt, coercing and plundering widely and gathering fugitives. Two commandants were dispatched together with the Inspector of Qing Province to campaign against them and pacify them. A commandery governor ranked as a feudal lord of old — precisely the ministerial token Liu Xiang had named. Another explanation takes glazed branches as the image of armor and weapons. The same year saw Cai Fang crushed; then in the eighth month the emperor personally led a river fleet against Wu — more than a hundred thousand men and banners unbroken for hundreds of li massed along the bank.
7
晉元帝太興三年二月辛未，雨，木冰。 後二年，周顗、戴淵、刁協、劉隗皆遇害，與春秋同事，是其應也。 一曰，是後王敦攻京師，又其象也。
On the xinwei day of the second month in the third year of Taixing under Emperor Yuan of Jin came freezing rain and ice-rimed trees. Two years later Zhou Yi, Dai Yuan, Diao Xie, and Liu Kui were all killed — the same sort of affair as in the Spring and Autumn Annals; this was its response. One account says: afterward Wang Dun attacked the capital — this too was its portent.
8
晉穆帝永和八年正月乙巳，雨，木冰。 是年，殷浩北伐，明年，軍敗，十年，廢黜。 又曰，荀羨、殷浩北伐，桓溫入關之象也。
On the yisi day of the first month in the eighth year of Yonghe under Emperor Mu of Jin, rain fell and trees froze over. Yin Hao marched north that year; his army collapsed the next, and within ten years he was stripped of rank. Commentators also tie the sign to Xun Xian's and Yin Hao's northern expeditions — the prelude to Huan Wen's thrust into Guanzhong.
9
晉孝武帝太元十四年十二月乙巳，雨，木冰。 明年二月，王恭為北蕃； 八月，庾楷為西蕃； 九月，王國寶為中書令，尋加領軍將軍； 十七年殷仲堪為荊州。 雖邪正異規，而終同摧滅，是其應也。 一曰，苻堅雖敗，關、河未一，丁零鮮卑，侵略司、兗，竇揚勝扇逼梁、雍，兵役不已，又其象也。
On the yisi day of the twelfth month in the fourteenth year of Taiyuan under Emperor Xiaowu of Jin, freezing rain glazed the trees. The following spring Wang Gong took charge of the northern marches; by the eighth month Yu Kai held the western frontier; in the ninth month Wang Guobao entered the Palace Secretariat, soon adding command of the capital guards; within seventeen years Yin Zhongkan gained Jing Province. Rival camps clashed in principle yet every faction was annihilated alike — this was its response. One account says: although Fu Jian was defeated, the passes and rivers were not yet unified; the Dingling and Xianbei invaded Si and Yan; Dou Yangsheng fanned rebellion and pressed Liang and Yong; military corvées did not cease — this too was its portent.
10
吳孫亮建興二年，諸葛恪征淮南，行後，所坐聽事棟中折。 恪妄興徵役，奪民農時，作為邪謀，傷國財力，故木失其性，致毀折也。 及旋師而誅滅，於周易又為棟橈之凶也。
In the second year of Jianxing under Sun Liang of Wu, after Zhuge Ke marched against Huainan the central beam of his audience hall snapped. Zhuge Ke had stirred needless wars, seized the farming calendar, and spun treasonous schemes that drained the realm — wood therefore abandoned its nature and shattered his hall. His retreat ended in execution and clan extinction — the Zhou yi calls such collapse the ill omen of the sagging ridgepole.
11
晉武帝太康五年五月，宣帝廟地陷梁折。 八年正月，太廟殿又陷，改作廟，築基及泉。 其年九月，遂更營新廟，遠致名材，雜以銅柱。 陳勰為匠，作者六萬人。 十年四月，乃成。 十一月庚寅，梁又折。 按地陷者，分離之象，梁折者，木不曲直也。 孫盛曰：于時後宮殿有孽火，又廟梁無故自折。 先是帝多不豫，益惡之。 明年，帝崩，而王室頻亂，遂亡天下。
In the fifth month of the fifth year of Taikang under Emperor Wu of Jin, the floor of Emperor Xuan's temple subsided and its beams gave way. The first month of the eighth year brought another collapse of the imperial ancestral hall; builders dug a new foundation to the water table. That autumn they raised a new shrine elsewhere, importing prized lumber and bronze columns. Chen Xie directed sixty thousand laborers. The structure stood finished in the tenth year's fourth month. Yet on gengyin in the eleventh month the beams snapped again. Heaven's lesson read partition into the sinking soil and wood's failed office into the splintered beams. Sun Sheng said: At that time there was calamitous fire in the rear palace halls, and the temple beams again broke without cause. Before this the emperor had often been unwell and grew all the more troubled by it. The emperor died the next year, and the royal house repeatedly fell into chaos and in the end lost the realm.
12
晉惠帝太安二年，成都王穎使陸機率眾向京師，擊長沙王乂。 軍始引而牙竿折，俄而戰敗，機被誅。 穎尋奔潰，卒賜死。 初，河間王顒謀先誅長沙，廢太子，立穎。 長沙知之，誅其黨卞粹等，故穎來伐。 機又以穎得遐邇心，將為漢之代王，遂委質於穎，為犯從之將。 此皆姦謀之罰，木不曲直也。
In the second year of Tai'an under Emperor Hui of Jin, Prince Chengdu Sima Ying sent Lu Ji against Prince Changsha Sima Yi at the capital. The host had barely marched when the standard mast splintered — soon Lu Ji fell defeated and was executed. Ying's army melted away, and he himself was ultimately compelled to suicide. Earlier Prince Hejian Sima Yong had plotted first to execute Prince Changsha, depose the crown prince, and install Ying. Prince Changsha learned of it and executed his faction, Bian Cui and others — therefore Ying came to attack. Lu Ji also, because Ying had won the hearts of near and far and would become a replacement king for Han, then submitted to Ying and became a general in the ranks of the accomplice. Heaven punished the conspiracy by stripping wood of its bend-and-straight virtue.
13
王敦在武昌，鈴下儀仗生華如蓮花狀，五六日而萎落。 此木失其性而為變也。 干寶曰：「鈴閤，尊貴者之儀； 鈴下，主威儀之官。 今狂花生於枯木，又在鈴閤之間，言威儀之富，榮華之盛，皆如狂花之發，不可久也。 」其後終以逆命，沒又加戮，是其應也。 一說此花孽也，於周易為「枯楊生華」。
When Wang Dun was at Wuchang, halberds and paraphernalia under the guard sprouted blossoms like lotus flowers; after five or six days they withered and fell. Wood had plainly forfeited its proper nature and become a transformation. Gan Bao said: "The bell pavilion is the ritual of the honored; those under the bell are officials who maintain awe-inspiring bearing. Now freak blossoms grow on dead timber beside the headquarters bell — this speaks of the fullness of awe-inspiring ritual and the flourishing of glory and splendor, all flashing as briefly as those unnatural flowers." Wang Dun later died in rebellion and suffered posthumous mutilation for defying the throne — this was its response. Another reading classes it among floral prodigies — the Zhou yi image of fresh blossoms on a withered poplar.
14
桓玄始篡，龍旂竿折。 玄田獵出入，不絕昏夜，飲食恣奢，土水妨農，又多姦謀，故木失其性也。 夫旂所以擬三辰，章著明也。 旂竿之折，高明去矣。 在位八十日而敗。
As Huan Xuan seized the throne, the great dragon standard broke. He hunted without restraint day and night, feasted extravagantly, and pressed construction works that tore peasants from their fields while intrigue multiplied — wood therefore lost its nature. The banner displays the Three Lights of sovereignty and manifests brightness. A shattered mast meant manifest legitimacy had departed. He held office eighty days and was defeated.
15
宋明帝泰始二年五月丙午，南琅邪臨沂黃城山道士盛道度堂屋一柱自然，夜光照室內。 此木失其性也。 或云木腐自光。
On bingwu day in the fifth month of the second year of Taishi under Emperor Ming of Song, at Huangcheng Mountain in Linyi, Nanlangya, one pillar of the Daoist Sheng Daodu's hall spontaneously glowed, its light illuminating the chamber at night. Wood had plainly lost its nature. Some say rotted wood naturally glows.
16
廢帝昇明元年，吳興餘杭舍亭禾蕈樹生李實。 禾蕈樹，民間所謂胡頹樹。
In the first year of Shengming under the Deposed Emperor, at Sheting in Yuhang, Wuxing, a hetong tree bore plum fruit. The hetong tree is what the common people call the hutui tree.
17
貌不恭
Bearing Lacks Respect
18
魏文帝居諒闇之始，便數出遊獵體，貌不重，風尚通脫。 故戴凌以直諫抵罪，鮑勛以迕旨極刑。 天下化之，咸賤守節，此貌之不恭也。 是以享國不永，後祚短促。 春秋魯君居喪不哀，在慼而有嘉容，穆叔謂之不度，後終出奔。 蓋同事也。
At the beginning of Emperor Wen of Wei's period of mourning for his father, he repeatedly went out hunting and sporting; his bearing lacked weight; the prevailing fashion favored easy unconventionality. Therefore Dai Ling suffered punishment for direct remonstrance, and Bao Xun received the extreme penalty for opposing the imperial will. All under Heaven were transformed by it and alike despised maintaining propriety — this was bearing lacking respect. Therefore his enjoyment of the realm was not long-lasting and the later fortune was brief. In the Spring and Autumn Annals, the lord of Lu during mourning was not mournful; while in grief he yet had a pleasing countenance; Mu Shu called this lacking measure, and afterward he finally fled into exile. Generally it was the same sort of affair.
19
魏尚書鄧颺，行步弛縱，筋不束體，坐起傾倚，若無手足。 此貌之不恭也。 管輅謂之鬼躁。 鬼躁者，凶終之徵。 後卒誅死。
Deng Yang of Wei, Master of Writing, walked loose-limbed and reckless — sinews not binding the body; sitting and rising he leaned and slouched as if without hands or feet. This was bearing lacking respect. Guan Lu called it ghost restlessness. Ghost restlessness is a sign of a violent end. Later he was executed and died.
20
晉惠帝元康中，貴遊子弟相與為散髮倮身之飲，對弄婢妾。 逆之者傷好，非之者負譏。 希世之士，恥不與焉。 蓋胡、翟侵中國之萌也。 豈徒伊川之民，一被髮而祭者乎。
During the Yuankang era under Emperor Hui of Jin, sons of noble families held drinking parties with hair loose and bodies bare, sporting with servant girls and concubines. Those who resisted lost friends; those who disapproved bore ridicule. Men who craved renown were ashamed not to join in. This was surely the first sprouting of Hu and Di encroachment upon the Central Plains. Was it not merely like the people of the Yi River, who once let down their hair and offered sacrifice?
21
晉惠帝元康中，賈謐親貴，數入二宮，與儲君遊戲，无降下心。 又嘗同弈棊爭道，成都王穎厲色曰：「皇太子，國之儲貳。 賈謐何敢無禮！ 」謐猶不悛，故及於禍。
During Yuankang under Emperor Hui of Jin, Jia Mi stood close to power; he repeatedly entered the two palaces to play with the crown prince, showing none of a subject's deference. Once when they played go together and disputed the board, Prince Chengdu Sima Ying said sternly: "The crown prince is the heir to the realm. How dare Jia Mi act without propriety!" Mi still would not repent, and therefore met disaster.
22
齊王冏既誅趙倫，因留輔政，坐拜百官，符敕臺府，淫醟專驕，不一朝覲。 此狂恣不肅之容也。 天下莫不高其功，而慮其亡也。 冏終弗改，遂至夷滅。
Prince Qi Sima Jiong, having executed Zhao Lun, stayed on to govern; he accepted bows while seated, issued orders to the central offices, drowned himself in wine and pride, and never once attended court. This was the bearing of reckless insolence without solemnity. All under Heaven praised his achievement yet feared his downfall. Jiong never reformed, and was utterly destroyed.
23
太元中，人不復著帩頭。 頭者，元首，帩者，令髮不垂，助元首為儀飾者也。 今忽廢之，若人君獨立無輔，以至危亡也。 其後桓玄篡位。
During the Taiyuan era, people ceased wearing the zhatou headwrap. The head is the sovereign part; the zhatou keeps the hair from falling loose and assists the head as ritual adornment. Now it was suddenly cast aside — as if the ruler stood alone without ministers, leading to peril and ruin. Afterward Huan Xuan usurped the throne.
24
舊為屐者，齒皆達楄上，名曰「露卯」。 太元中，忽不徹，名曰「陰卯」。 其後多陰謀，遂致大亂。
Formerly when making wooden clogs, the pegs reached the upper frame — they were called "exposed mortise." During Taiyuan they suddenly stopped driving the pegs through, calling them "hidden mortise." Afterward conspiracies multiplied and the realm fell into great chaos.
25
晉安帝義熙七年，晉朝拜授劉毅世子。 毅以王命之重，當設饗宴親，請吏佐臨視。 至日，國僚不重白，默拜於廄中。 王人將反命，毅方知，大以為恨，免郎中令劉敬叔官。 識者怪焉。 此墮略嘉禮，不肅之妖也。
In the seventh year of Yixi under Emperor An of Jin, the Jin court invested Liu Yi's heir. Liu Yi, considering the weight of the royal mandate, ought to have held a feast for his kinsmen and invited his officers to witness. On the day, his state retainers failed to announce the rite properly and performed the obeisance in silence in the stable. When the court envoy was about to report back, Yi only then learned of it; he resented this deeply and stripped Gentleman of the Palace Liu Jingshu of his post. Those who understood were astonished. This was a prodigy of neglecting excellent ritual and lacking solemnity.
26
陳郡謝靈運有逸才，每出入，自扶接者常數人。 民間謠曰「四人挈衣裙，三人捉坐席」是也。 此蓋不肅之咎，後坐誅。
Xie Lingyun of Chen Commandery had exceptional talent; whenever he went out or in, several men supported him by the arms. A folk song ran, "Four men carry gown and skirt, three men hold the seat" — this was it. This was surely the fault of lacking solemnity; afterward he was executed for it.
27
宋明帝泰始中，幸臣阮佃夫勢傾朝廷，室宇豪麗，車服鮮明，乘車常偏向一邊，違正立執綏之體。 時人多慕效。 此亦貌不恭之失也。 時偏左之化行，方正之道廢矣。
During the Taishi era under Emperor Ming of Song, the favorite Ruan Tianfu's power tilted the court; his halls were sumptuous, his carriages and vestments brilliant; when he rode he constantly leaned to one side, violating the upright posture of standing square and holding the reins. Many at the time admired and copied him. This too was bearing lacking respect. The fashion of leaning left spread; the way of uprightness and squareness was abandoned.
28
後廢帝常單騎遊遨，出入市里營寺，未嘗御輦。 終以殞滅。
The Deposed Emperor often rode alone on excursions, passing through markets, wards, and monasteries, never using the imperial carriage. In the end he met destruction.
29
恒雨
Constant Rain
30
魏明帝太和元年秋，數大雨，多暴雷電，非常，至殺鳥雀。 案楊阜上蔬，此桓雨之罰也。 時帝居喪不哀，出入弋獵無度，奢侈繁興，奪民農時，故木失其性而恒雨為災也。
In autumn of the first year of Taihe under Emperor Ming of Wei, heavy rains fell again and again with violent thunder and lightning — something extraordinary, even killing birds. According to Yang Fu's memorial, this was the punishment of constant rain. At the time the emperor was not mournful during mourning; he hunted without restraint; extravagance flourished; he seized the people's farming seasons — therefore wood lost its nature and constant rain became disaster.
31
太和四年八月，大雨霖三十餘日，伊、洛、河、漢皆溢，歲以凶饑。
In the eighth month of the fourth year of Taihe came incessant rain for more than thirty days; the Yi, Luo, Yellow, and Han rivers all overflowed, and the year brought famine.
32
孫亮太平二年二月甲寅，大雨震電； 乙卯，雪，大寒。 案劉歆說，此時當雨而不當大，大雨，恒雨之罰也。 於始震電之明日而雪大寒，又恒寒之罰也。 劉向以為既已震電，則雪不當復降，皆失時之異也。 天戒若曰，為君失時，賊臣將起。 先震電而後雪者，陰見間隙，起而勝陽。 逆殺之禍將及也。 亮不悟，尋見廢。 此與春秋魯隱同也。
In the second year of Taiping under Sun Liang of Wu, on jiayin of the second month, heavy rain and thunder; on yimao, snow and severe cold. According to Liu Xin's explanation, at this season there should have been rain but not so much — heavy rain is the punishment of constant rain. That snow and severe cold came the day after the first thunder was also the punishment of constant cold. Liu Xiang held that once thunder had sounded, snow ought not to fall again — all were anomalies out of season. Heaven's warning seemed to say: the ruler has lost the season; a rebel minister will rise. Thunder first and snow afterward means Yin saw an opening and rose to overcome Yang. The calamity of treacherous killing was about to arrive. Liang did not understand; soon he was deposed. This was the same as Duke Yin of Lu in the Spring and Autumn Annals.
33
晉武帝泰始六年六月，大雨霖，甲辰，河、洛、沁水同時並溢，流四千九百餘家，殺二百餘人，沒秋稼千三百六十餘頃。 晉武太康五年七月，任城、梁國暴雨，害豆麥。 太康五年九月，南安霖雨暴雪，折樹木，害秋稼； 魏郡、淮南、平原雨水，傷秋稼。 是秋，魏郡、西平郡九縣霖雨暴水，霜傷秋稼。
In the sixth month of the sixth year of Taishi under Emperor Wu of Jin came incessant rain; on jiachen the Yellow, Luo, and Qin rivers overflowed together, sweeping away more than four thousand nine hundred households, killing more than two hundred people, and drowning more than thirteen hundred sixty qing of autumn grain. In the seventh month of the fifth year of Taikang under Emperor Wu of Jin, torrential rain in Rencheng and Liang struck beans and wheat. In the ninth month of the fifth year of Taikang, incessant rain and sudden snow in Nan'an broke trees and harmed autumn grain; in Wei, Huainan, and Pingyuan, rain and flood harmed autumn grain. That autumn, incessant rain and sudden flood in nine counties of Wei and Xiping, with frost, harmed autumn grain.
34
晉惠帝永寧元年十月，義陽、南陽、東海霖雨，淹害秋麥。
In the tenth month of the first year of Yongning under Emperor Hui of Jin, incessant rain in Yiyang, Nanyang, and Donghai flooded and harmed autumn wheat.
35
晉成帝咸康元年八月乙丑，荊州之長沙攸、醴陵、武陵之龍陽三縣，雨水浮漂屋室，殺人，傷損秋稼。
On yichou of the eighth month in the first year of Xiankang under Emperor Cheng of Jin, in the three counties of Changsha, You, and Longyang in Wuling of Jing Province, floodwaters floated houses, killed people, and harmed autumn grain.

宋文帝元嘉二十一年六月，京邑連雨百餘日，大水。
In the sixth month of the twenty-first year of Yuanjia under Emperor Wen of Song, the capital saw rain for more than a hundred consecutive days and great flood.

孝武帝大明元年正月，京邑雨水。
In the first month of the first year of Daming under Emperor Xiaowu of Song, the capital saw rain and flood.

In the seventh month of the fifth year of Daming, the capital saw rain and flood.

In the sixth month of the second year of Taishi under Emperor Ming of Song, the capital saw rain and flood.

順帝昇明三年四月乙亥，吳郡桐廬縣暴風雷電，揚砂折木，水平地二丈，流漂居民。
On yihai of the fourth month in the third year of Shengming under Emperor Shun of Song, in Tonglu County of Wu Commandery came violent wind, thunder, and lightning; sand was whipped up and trees broken; water stood two zhang above level ground and swept away inhabitants.

服妖
Costume Prodigies
43
魏武帝以天下凶荒，資財乏匱，始擬古皮弁，裁縑帛為白帢，以易舊服。 傅玄曰：「白乃軍容，非國容也。 」干寶以為縞素，凶喪之象，帢，毀辱之言也。 蓋革代之後，攻殺之妖也。 初為白帢，橫縫其前以別後，名之曰「顏」，俗傳行之。 至晉永嘉之間，稍去其縫，名「無顏帢」。 而婦人束髮，其緩彌甚，紒之堅不能自立，髮被于額，目出而已。 無顏者，愧之言也； 覆額者，慚之貌； 其緩彌甚，言天下忘禮與義，放縱情性，及其終極，至乎大恥也。 永嘉之後，二帝不反，天下愧焉。 魏明帝著繡帽，被縹紈半袖，嘗以見直臣楊阜。 阜諫曰：「此於禮何法服邪？ 」帝默然。 近服妖也。 縹，非禮之色，褻服不貳。 今之人主，親御非法之章，所謂自作孽不可禳也。 帝既不享永年，身沒而祿去王室，後嗣不終，遂亡天下。
Because the realm was stricken with famine and resources were exhausted, Emperor Wu of Wei first modeled the ancient leather cap and cut silk cloth into a white cap to replace the old dress. Fu Xuan said: "White is military bearing, not the bearing of the state." Gan Bao took plain white silk to be the image of mourning and calamity; the cap was a word of disgrace and ruin. This was surely the prodigy of attack and slaughter after a change of dynasty. At first the white cap had a horizontal seam in front to distinguish back from front, called "face"; the custom spread it widely. By the Yongjia era of Jin they gradually removed the seam, calling it the "faceless cap." Meanwhile women bound their hair ever more loosely; though tied tight it could not stand upright; hair spread over the forehead, with only the eyes showing. "Faceless" is a word of shame; covering the forehead is the appearance of embarrassment; that the binding grew ever looser speaks of the realm forgetting ritual and righteousness, indulging feeling and nature, and at the extreme reaching great shame. After Yongjia the two emperors did not return; all under Heaven was shamed. Emperor Ming of Wei wore an embroidered cap and a light silk half-sleeve, and once appeared thus before the upright minister Yang Fu. Fu remonstrated: "By what rule of ritual is this dress?" The emperor fell silent. This was a recent costume prodigy. Light blue is not a color of ritual; intimate dress should not be duplicated. Now the ruler personally wore unlawful adornment — what is called bringing calamity upon oneself with no rite to avert it. The emperor did not enjoy long years; after his death stipends departed the royal house, later heirs did not endure, and the realm was lost.
44
魏明帝景初元年，發銅鑄為巨人二，號曰「翁仲」。 置之司馬門外。 案古長人見，為國亡； 長狄見臨洮，為秦亡之禍。 始皇不悟，反以為嘉祥，鑄銅人以象之。 魏法亡國之器，而於義竟無取焉。 蓋服妖也。
In the first year of Jingchu under Emperor Ming of Wei, copper was cast into two giant figures called "Wengzhong." They were placed outside the Sima Gate. According to antiquity, when a giant man appeared, the state perished; when the Long Di appeared at Lintao, it was the calamity of Qin's destruction. The First Emperor did not understand, but instead took it as an auspice and cast bronze men to imitate it. Wei's law held such things to be instruments of a state's destruction, yet in righteousness they were utterly without merit. This too was a costume prodigy.
45
魏尚書何晏，好服婦人之服。 傅玄曰：「此服妖也。 」夫衣裳之制，所以定上下，殊內外也。 大雅云：「玄袞赤舄，鉤膺鏤鍚。 」歌其文也。 小雅云：「有嚴有翼，共武之服。 」詠其武也。 若內外不殊，王制失敍，服妖既作，身隨之亡。 末嬉冠男子之冠，桀亡天下； 何晏服婦人之服，亦亡其家。 其咎均也。
He Yan, Master of Writing of Wei, delighted in wearing women's dress. Fu Xuan said: "This is a costume prodigy." The system of garments and robes is what fixes high and low and distinguishes inner from outer. The Greater Odes say: "Dark robes and red shoes, hooked breastplate and carved bell." They sing its ornament. The Lesser Odes say: "Stern and supportive, together in martial dress." They chant its martial bearing. If inner and outer are not distinguished, the royal system loses order; once costume prodigies appear, the person follows them to destruction. Mo Xi wore a man's cap and Jie lost the realm; He Yan wore women's dress, and his house perished as well. Their faults were equal.
46
吳婦人之修容者，急束其髮，而劘角過于耳。 蓋其俗自操束大急，而廉隅失中之謂也。 故吳之風俗，相驅以急，言論彈射，以刻薄相尚。 居三年之喪者，往往有致毀以死。 諸葛患之，著正交論，雖不可以經訓整亂，蓋亦救時之作也。 孫休後，衣服之制，上長下短，又積領五六而裳居一二。 干寶曰：「上饒奢，下儉逼，上有餘下不足之妖也。 」至孫晧，果奢暴恣情於上，而百姓彫困於下，卒以亡國。 是其應也。
Wu women who adorned themselves bound their hair very tight, shaving the temples so the corners passed behind the ears. This signified a custom of excessive self-restraint and loss of the balanced mean. Therefore Wu's customs drove one another toward urgency; speech turned sharp and projectile; men took harshness and stinginess as honor. Those observing the three-year mourning often destroyed their bodies unto death. Zhuge Ke grieved at this and wrote the Correcting Associations treatise; though it could not set the disorder in order by the classic teachings, it was nonetheless a work to save the times. After Sun Xiu, clothing regulations had upper garments long and lower garments short; the collar accumulated five or six folds while the skirt occupied only one or two. Gan Bao said: "Extravagance above and tight economy below — a prodigy of surplus above and insufficiency below." When Sun Hao came to power, he was indeed extravagant and unrestrained above while the people were carved and impoverished below, and in the end the state perished. This was the fulfillment.
47
晉興後，衣服上儉下豐，著衣者皆厭䙅蓋裙。 君衰弱，臣放縱，下掩上之象也。 陵遲至元康末，婦人出兩襠，加乎脛之上，此內出外也。 為車乘者，苟貴輕細，又數變易其形，皆以白篾為純，古喪車之遺象。 乘者，君子之器，蓋君子立心無恒，事不崇實也。 干寶曰：「及晉之禍，天子失柄，權制寵臣，下掩上之應也。 永嘉末，六宮才人，流徙戎、翟，內出外之應也。 及天下亂擾，宰輔方伯，多負其任，又數改易，不崇實之應也。」
After Jin rose, upper garments were economical and lower garments abundant; those who dressed all trailed long skirt hems. The ruler weakened, ministers grew unrestrained — an image of the lower covering the upper. By the end of the Yuankang era, women wore two dang panels over the shins — this was the inner going outside. Those who rode in carts valued light and fine carriages and repeatedly changed their forms, all using white bamboo strips as ornament — the legacy image of ancient mourning carts. The cart is the implement of the gentleman; it signifies that gentlemen set their hearts without constancy and do not exalt what is solid. Gan Bao said: "At the Jin calamity, the Son of Heaven lost the handle; power rested with favored ministers — the response of the lower covering the upper." At the end of Yongjia, the palace beauties of the six palaces were scattered among the Rong and Di — the response of the inner going outside. When all under Heaven was in turmoil, chief ministers and regional governors mostly failed in their duties and were repeatedly changed — the response of not exalting what is solid."
48
晉武帝泰始後，中國相尚用胡牀、貊盤，及為羌煑、貊炙。 貴人富室，必置其器，吉享嘉會，皆此為先。 太康中，天下又以氈為絈頭及絡帶、衿口。 百姓相戲曰，中國必為胡所破也。 氈產於胡，而天下以為絈頭、帶身、衿口，胡既三制之矣，能无敗乎。 干寶曰：「元康中，氐、羌反，至于永嘉，劉淵、石勒遂有中都。 自後四夷迭據華土，是其應也。」
After Taishi under Emperor Wu of Jin, Central China took to using Hu couches, Mo trays, and making Qiang stews and Mo roasts. The noble and wealthy invariably kept these implements; at auspicious feasts and grand gatherings, these came first. In the Taikang era, all under Heaven also used felt for ge'er headwraps, cord belts, and collar openings. The common people jested that Central China would surely be destroyed by the Hu. Felt was produced by the Hu, yet all under Heaven used it for headwraps, belts, and collar openings — the Hu had already imposed three regulations upon them; how could defeat not follow? Gan Bao said: "In Yuankang, the Di and Qiang rebelled; by Yongjia, Liu Yuan and Shi Le held the central capital." Afterward the four barbarians successively occupied Central China — this was the fulfillment."
49
晉武帝太康後，天下為家者，移婦人於東方，空萊北庭，以為園囿。 干寶曰：「夫王朝南向，正陽也； 后北宮，位太陰也； 世子居東宮，位少陽也。 今居內於東，是與外俱南面也。 亢陽无陰，婦人失位而干少陽之象也。 賈后讒戮愍懷，俄而禍敗亦及。」
After Taikang under Emperor Wu of Jin, households that built homes moved women to the east side, left the north courtyard empty, and made it a garden. Gan Bao said: "The royal court faces south — that is the position of orthodox yang; the empress's Northern Palace is the position of great yin; the heir apparent dwells in the Eastern Palace, the position of lesser yang. Now to dwell within in the east is to face south together with the outer; excess of yang without yin — women lose their proper place and encroach upon lesser yang." Empress Jia slandered and killed Crown Prince Minhuai, and soon calamity and ruin reached her as well."
50
昔初作履者，婦人圓頭，男子方頭。 圓者，順從之義，所以別男女也。 晉太康初，婦人皆履方頭，此去其圓從，與男無別也。
In antiquity when shoes were first made, women had round toes and men had square toes. Round signifies yielding compliance — thus to distinguish male from female. In the early Taikang period of Jin, women all wore square-toed shoes — this abandons the round and compliant and makes no distinction from men.
51
太康之中，天下為晉世寧之舞，手接杯槃反覆之，歌曰：「晉世寧，舞杯槃。 」夫樂生人心，所以觀事。 故記曰：「總干山立，武王之事也； 發揚蹈厲，太公之志也； 武亂皆坐，周、召之治也。 」又曰：「其治民勞者，舞行綴遠； 其治民逸者，舞行綴近。 今接杯槃於手上而反覆之，至危也。 杯槃者，酒食之器也，而名曰晉世寧者，言晉世之士，偷苟於酒食之間，而其知不及遠，晉世之寧，猶杯槃之在手也。」
In the midst of Taikang, all under Heaven performed the Jin-era-at-peace dance, tossing cups and platters in the hands and flipping them, singing: "Jin era at peace, dance of cups and platters." Music arises from the human heart — by it one observes affairs. Therefore the Record says: "Gathering the shield and standing like a mountain — that was King Wu's deed; expanding and stamping with force — that was Grand Duke Wang's intent; when the martial dance ended all sat — that was the governance of the Duke of Zhou and Duke of Shao." It also says: "Where the people are ruled with labor, the dance steps are long; where the people are ruled with ease, the dance steps are short. Now to toss cups and platters in the hands and flip them — this is utmost peril. Cups and platters are implements of wine and food, yet they are named "Jin era at peace" — meaning that Jin gentlemen are slothful amid wine and food, their vision cannot reach far; the peace of the Jin age is like cups and platters held in the hand."
52
晉惠帝元康中，婦人之飾有五兵佩，又以金、銀、瑇瑁之屬為斧、鉞、戈、戟，以當笄□。 干寶曰：「男女之別，國之大節，故服物異等，贄幣不同。 今婦人而以兵器為飾，又妖之大也。 遂有賈后之事，終以兵亡天下。」
In Yuankang under Emperor Hui of Jin, women's adornments included the Five Weapons pendant; they also made axes, battle-axes, halberds, and spears from gold, silver, tortoise shell and the like, using them in place of hairpins. Gan Bao said: "The distinction between man and woman is a great statute of the state; therefore garments and goods differ in rank and tribute offerings differ. Now for women to use weapons as adornment — this is also a great prodigy. Then came the affairs of Empress Jia, and in the end the realm was lost through arms."
53
元康中，婦人結髮者，既成，以繒急束其環，名曰擷子紒。 始自中宮，天下化之。 其後賈后果害太子。
In Yuankang, women who bound their hair, once finished, used silk to bind the loop very tight — called the "plucking-child knot." It began in the inner palace and all under Heaven followed. Afterward Empress Jia indeed harmed the crown prince.
54
元康中，天下始相倣為㮧杖，以柱掖其後，稍施其錞，住則植之。 夫木，東方之行，金之臣也。 扶者，杖體之器，㮧其頭者，尤便用也。 必傍柱掖者，傍救之象也。 王室多故，而元帝以蕃臣樹德東方，維持天下，柱掖之應也。 至社稷無主，海內歸之，遂承天命，建都江外，獨立之應也。
In Yuankang, all under Heaven began to imitate making sharp-headed staves, using them to prop under the armpits, gradually adding bronze ferrules; when standing they planted them in the ground. Wood is the element of the east, subject of metal. The staff is an implement for supporting the body; sharpening the head makes it especially convenient to use. That they must lean on a post under the armpit is the image of leaning on aid from the side. The royal house had many troubles, and Emperor Yuan, as a frontier prince, established virtue in the east and maintained all under Heaven — the response of post-propping. When the altars had no master and the realm within the seas turned to him, he received Heaven's mandate and established the capital beyond the river — the response of standing independent.
55
元康末至太安間，江、淮之域，有敗編自聚于道，多者或至四五十量。 干寶嘗使人散而去之，或投林草，或投坑谷。 明日視之，悉復如故。 民或云見狸銜而聚之，亦未察也。 寶說曰：「夫編者，人之賤服，最處于下，而當勞辱，下民之象也。 敗者，疲斃之象也。 道者，地理四方，所以交通王命所由往來也。 故今敗編聚於道者，象下民罷病，將相聚為亂，絕四方而壅王命之象也。 在位者莫察。 太安中，發壬午兵，百姓嗟怨。 江夏男子張昌遂首亂荊楚，從之者如流。 於是兵革歲起，天下因之，遂大破壞。 此近服妖也。」
From the end of Yuankang to the Taian era, in the Jiang-Huai region tattered hemp sandals gathered of themselves upon the roads, sometimes as many as forty or fifty bundles. Gan Bao once sent men to scatter and remove them, casting them into woods and grass or into pits and valleys. The next day when he looked, all were as before. Some among the people said they saw raccoon dogs carrying them to gather — but none examined it closely. Bao explained: "Sandals are the humble dress of men, worn lowest and subject to toil and insult — the image of the lower people. Tattered ones are the image of exhaustion and death. Roads are the geography of the four directions, the paths by which the royal commands travel back and forth. Therefore tattered sandals gathering upon the roads now images the lower people worn and sick, about to gather in rebellion and cut off the four directions and block the royal commands." Those in office did not perceive it. In the Taian era, the renwu campaign was launched and the people groaned in resentment. A man of Jiangxia named Zhang Chang then raised the first rebellion in Jing and Chu; those who followed flowed like a stream. Thereafter arms rose year after year; all under Heaven followed suit, and great ruin ensued. This is a recent costume prodigy."
56
晉孝懷永嘉以來，士大夫竟服生箋單衣。 遠識者怪之，竊指擿曰：「此則古者繐衰之布，諸侯大夫所以服天子也。 今無故畢服之，殆有應乎？ 」其後愍、懷晏駕，不獲厥所。
From Yongjia onward under Emperor Minhuai of Jin, gentlemen competed to wear raw hemp single garments. Men of far sight found it strange and privately pointed and said: "This is the cloth of the ancient suicai mourning garment, which feudal lords and great officers wore when serving the Son of Heaven." Now to wear it entirely without cause — perhaps there will be a fulfillment? Afterward Emperors Min and Huai passed away without attaining their proper end.
57
晉元帝太興以來，兵士以絳囊縛紒。 紒在首，莫上焉。 周易乾為首，坤為囊。 坤，臣道也。 晉金行，赤火色，金之賊也。 以朱囊縛紒，臣道上侵之象也。 到永昌元年，大將軍王敦舉兵內攻，六軍散潰。
From Taixing onward under Emperor Yuan of Jin, soldiers bound their hair knots with crimson bags. The knot is upon the head — nothing is above it. In the Book of Changes, Qian is the head and Kun is the bag. Kun is the way of the minister. Jin belongs to the metal phase; red is the color of fire, the conqueror of metal. To bind the hair knot with a crimson bag is the image of the minister's way encroaching upward. In the first year of Yongchang, the Grand General Wang Dun raised troops and attacked inward; the six armies scattered and collapsed.
58
舊為羽扇，柄刻木，象其骨形，羽用十，取全數也。 晉中興初，王敦南征，始改為長柄下出，可捉，而減其羽用八。 識者尤之曰：「夫羽扇，翼之名也。 創為長柄者，執其柄制羽翼也。 以十改八者，將以未備奪已備也。 」是時為衣者，又上短，帶至于掖； 著帽者，以帶縛項。 下逼上，上無地也。 下袴者，直幅為口無殺，下大失裁也。 尋有兵亂，三年而再攻京師。
Formerly the feather fan had a carved wooden handle, imitating the bone's form; the feathers numbered ten, taking the complete number. In the early Eastern Jin restoration, when Wang Dun marched south, he first changed it to a long handle projecting downward that could be gripped, and reduced the feathers to eight. Men of insight blamed it, saying: "The feather fan is named for the wing. To make a long handle is to grasp the handle and control the wings. To change ten to eight is to take from the prepared and give to the unprepared." At that time those who made clothes also had short upper garments with belts reaching to the armpits; those who wore caps bound the neck with belts. The lower pressing the upper — the upper has no room. Those who wore lower trousers made the leg opening from straight panels without taper — the lower greatly lost proper cut. Soon there was military turmoil; within three years the capital was attacked twice.
59
晉海西初嗣位，迎官忘設豹尾。 識者以為不終之象，近服妖也。
When Emperor Haixi of Jin first succeeded, at the welcoming ceremony he forgot to set the leopard-tail banner. Men of insight took it as an image of not completing one's term — a recent costume prodigy.
60
晉司馬道子於府北園內為酒鑪列肆，使姬人酤鬻酒肴，如裨販者，數遊其中，身自買易，因醉寓寢，動連日夜。 漢靈帝嘗若此。 干寶以為：「君將失位，降在皁隸之象也。 」道子卒見廢徙，以庶人終。
Sima Daozi of Jin set up wine furnaces and rows of shops in the garden north of his mansion, having his concubines sell wine and food like petty merchants; he often wandered among them, buying and selling himself, and from drunkenness lodged there for days and nights on end. Emperor Ling of Han once did likewise. Gan Bao held that: "The ruler is about to lose his position — the image of descending among menials." Daozi in the end was deposed and banished, ending as a commoner.
61
桓玄篡立，殿上施絳綾帳，鏤黃金為顏，四角金龍，銜五色羽葆流蘇。 羣下竊相謂曰：「頗類輀車。 」此服妖也。
When Huan Xuan usurped the throne, red silk canopies were hung in the hall, with faces carved in gold; at the four corners were golden dragons holding streamers of five-colored feathers. The assembled ministers whispered to one another: "It very much resembles a bier carriage." This was a costume prodigy.
62
晉末皆冠小冠，而衣裳博大，風流相倣，輿臺成俗。 識者曰：「此禪代之象也。 」永初以後，冠還大云。
By the end of Jin everyone wore small caps and flowing, ample robes; one imitated another in elegant display until even carriage men and platform attendants had made it the custom. Those who read the signs said: "This is the image of abdication and dynastic change." After Yongchu, so they say, caps grew large again.
63
宋文帝元嘉六年，民間婦人結髮者，三分髮，抽其鬟直向上，謂之「飛天紒」。 始自東府，流被民庶。 時司徒彭城王義康居東府，其後卒以陵上徙廢。
In the sixth year of Yuanjia under Emperor Wen of Song, women who bound their hair began dividing it in three parts and drawing the topknot straight upward — a style called the "Flying Heaven knot." It began at the Eastern Mansion and spread to the common people. At the time Grand Preceptor Prince Pengcheng Liu Yikang lived in the Eastern Mansion; afterward he was banished and deposed for lording it over his superiors.
64
孝武帝世，豫州刺史劉德願善御車，世祖嘗使之御畫輪，幸太宰江夏王義恭第。 德願挾牛杖催世祖云：「日暮宜歸！ 」又求益僦車。 世祖甚歡。 此事與漢靈帝西園蓄私錢同也。
During Emperor Xiaowu's reign, Liu Deyuan, Inspector of Yuzhou, was a skilled charioteer; the Emperor once had him drive a painted-wheel carriage on a visit to Grand Preceptor Prince Jiangxia Liu Yigong's mansion. Deyuan, ox-goad staff in hand, urged the Emperor: "The sun is setting — you should go home!" He also asked for more hired carriages. The Emperor was delighted. This was the same as Emperor Ling of Han hoarding private funds in the Western Garden.
65
孝武世，幸臣戴法興權亞人主，造圓頭履，世人莫不效之。 其時圓進之俗大行，方格之風盡矣。
During Emperor Xiaowu's reign, the favored minister Dai Faxing wielded authority second only to the throne; he devised round-toed shoes, and the whole world copied them. Round-toed fashion swept the realm, and the old propriety of square-cut rule vanished entirely.
66
明帝初，司徒建安王休仁統軍赭圻，制烏紗帽，反抽帽裙，民間謂之「司徒狀」，京邑翕然相尚。 休仁後果以疑逼致禍。
Early in Mingdi's reign, Grand Preceptor Prince Jian'an Liu Xiuren, commanding troops at Zheqi, devised a black gauze cap with the brim turned up backward; the people called it the "Steward's style," and the capital eagerly followed suit. Xiuren afterward did meet calamity, driven to ruin by suspicion and coercion.
67
龜孽
Turtle Calamities
68
晉惠帝永熙初，衛瓘家人炊飯，墮地，盡化為螺，出足起行。 螺，龜類，近龜孽也。 干寶曰：「螺被甲，兵象也。 於周易為離，離為戈兵。 」明年，瓘誅。
In the early Yongxi era under Emperor Hui of Jin, when Wei Guan's household cooked rice, the grains fell to the ground and all became snails that sprouted feet and walked. Snails belong to the turtle kind — a turtle calamity close at hand. Gan Bao said: "The snail wears a shell — the image of arms." In the Book of Changes this corresponds to Li; Li signifies spears and halberds. The next year Wei was put to death.
69
雞禍
Chicken Calamities
70
魏明帝景初二年，廷尉府中有雌雞變為雄，不鳴不將。 干寶曰：「是歲，晉宣帝平遼東，百姓始有與能之議，此其象也。 」然晉三后並以人臣終，不鳴不將，又天意也。
In the second year of Jingchu under Emperor Ming of Wei, a hen in the Court of Justice transformed into a rooster that neither crowed nor led the flock. Gan Bao said: "That year Jin's Grand Guardian pacified Liaodong, and the people first began to speak of yielding power to the capable — this was the omen." Yet the three Jin empresses all died as subjects; neither crowing nor leading the flock — again Heaven's intent.
71
晉惠帝元康六年，陳國有雞生雄雞無翅，既大，墜坑而死。 王隱曰：「雄，胤嗣象，坑地事為母象，賈后誣殺愍懷，殆其應也。」
In the sixth year of Yuankang under Emperor Hui of Jin, in Chen commandery a hen bore a wingless rooster; when it grew large, it fell into a pit and died. Wang Yin said: "The rooster signifies heirs; a pit in the earth signifies the mother — Empress Jia's false killing of Crown Prince Minhuai was perhaps the fulfillment."
72
晉惠帝太安中，周玘家有雌雞逃承霤中，六七日而下，奮翼鳴將，獨毛羽不變。 其後有陳敏之事。 敏雖控制江表，終無綱紀文章，殆其象也。 卒為玘所滅。 雞禍見玘家，又天意也。
During the Taian era under Emperor Hui of Jin, at Zhou Qi's house a hen fled into the rain gutter; six or seven days later it came down, flapping its wings and crowing like a leader, though its feathers alone never changed. Afterward came the affair of Chen Min. Though Min held the lands south of the Yangtze, in the end he lacked proper governance and civil order — perhaps the omen's meaning. In the end Zhou Qi destroyed him. The chicken calamity appeared in Qi's house — Heaven's intent again.
73
晉元帝太興中，王敦鎮武昌，有雌雞化為雄。 天戒若曰：「雌化為雄，臣陵其上。 」其後王敦再攻京師。
During the Taixing era under Emperor Yuan of Jin, while Wang Dun held Wuchang, a hen turned into a rooster. Heaven's warning seemed to say: "Female turns male — a minister towers over his lord." Afterward Wang Dun twice attacked the capital.
74
晉孝武太元十三年四月，廣陵高平閻嵩家雄雞，生無右翅； 彭城到象之家雞，無右足。 京房易傳曰：「君用婦人言，則雞生妖。」
In the fourth month of the thirteenth year of Taixuan under Emperor Wu of Jin, at Yan Song's house in Gaoping, Guangling, a rooster was born without its right wing; at Dao Xiang's house in Pengcheng, a chicken without its right foot. Jing Fang's Commentary on the Changes says: "When a ruler heeds women's counsel, chickens bear prodigies."
75
晉安帝隆安元年八月，琅邪王道子家青雌雞化為赤雄，不鳴不將。 後有桓玄之事，具如其象。
In the eighth month of the first year of Long'an under Emperor An of Jin, at Prince Langye Sima Daozi's house a green hen turned into a red rooster that neither crowed nor led the flock. Afterward came Huan Xuan's rebellion, matching the omen in every detail.
76
隆安四年，荊州有雞生角，角尋墮落。 是時桓玄始擅西夏，狂慢不肅，故有雞禍。 角，兵象； 尋墮落者，暫起不終之妖也。
In the fourth year of Long'an, in Jing province a chicken sprouted horns; the horns soon dropped off. At that time Huan Xuan first seized the western Xia regions, arrogant and insolent — hence the chicken calamity. Horns signify weapons; their quick fall — a prodigy of brief ascent without lasting success.
77
晉安帝元興二年，衡陽有雌雞化為雄，八十日而冠萎。 衡陽，桓玄楚國封略也。 後篡位八十日而敗，徐廣以為玄之象也。
In the second year of Yuanxing under Emperor An of Jin, in Hengyang a hen turned into a rooster; after eighty days its comb withered. Hengyang lay within the territory of Huan Xuan's fief of Chu. Later he seized the throne and fell after eighty days — Xu Guang took this as the image of Huan Xuan.
78
宋文帝元嘉十二年，華林園雌雞漸化為雄。 後孝武即位，皇太后令行于外，亦猶漢宣帝時，雌雞為雄，至哀帝時，元后與政也。
In the twelfth year of Yuanjia under Emperor Wen of Song, a hen in Hualin Garden gradually turned into a rooster. Afterward Emperor Xiaowu took the throne, and the empress dowager's orders went out into the world — as when, under Emperor Xuandi, a hen became a rooster, until under Emperor Aidi the Grand Empress Dowager Wang held power.
79
明帝泰始中，吳興東遷沈法符家雞有四距。
During the Taishi era under Emperor Ming, at Shen Fafu's house in Dongqian, Wuxing, a chicken bore four spurs.
80
青眚青祥
Green Blight and Green Omens
81
晉武帝咸寧元年八月丁酉，大風折太社樹，有青氣出焉。 此青祥也。 占曰：「東莞當有帝者。 」明年，元帝生。 是時帝大父武王封東莞，由是徙封琅邪。 孫盛以為中興之表。 晉室之亂，武帝子孫無孑遺，社樹折之應，又恒風之罰也。
On the day dingyou in the eighth month of the first year of Xianning under Emperor Wu of Jin, a fierce wind snapped the tree at the Grand Altar of Earth, and green vapor rose from it. This was a green omen. The omen read: "Dongguan will produce an emperor." The next year Emperor Yuan was born. At that time the future emperor's great-grandfather, the Prince of Wu, held Dongguan as his fief; from this the family was later enfeoffed at Langye. Sun Sheng took it as a sign of dynastic renewal. In the chaos of the Jin house, none of Emperor Wu's descendants survived — the broken altar tree fulfilled, and again the punishment of unceasing wind.
82
晉惠帝元康中，洛陽南山有䖟作聲曰：「韓屍屍。 」識者曰：「韓氏將死也。 言屍屍者，盡死意也。 」其後韓謐誅而韓族殲焉。 此青祥也。
During Yuankang under Emperor Hui of Jin, on the southern hills of Luoyang a gnat cried: "Han shi shi." Those who read the signs said: "The Han clan is about to perish." Shi shi meant total extinction. Afterward Han Mi was executed and the Han clan wiped out. This too was a green omen.
83
金沴木
Metal Encroaching upon Wood
84
魏文帝初七年正月，幸許昌。 許昌城南門無故自崩，帝心惡之，遂不入，還洛陽。 此金沴木，木動也。 五月，宮車晏駕。 京房易傳曰：「上下咸悖，厥妖城門壞。」
In the first month of the seventh year of Huangchu, Emperor Wen of Wei visited Xuchang. Xuchang's south gate collapsed without cause; troubled in heart, the emperor would not enter and returned to Luoyang. This was metal encroaching upon wood — wood had been set in motion. In the fifth month the imperial carriage halted forever. Jing Fang's Commentary on the Changes says: "When high and low alike turn against order, the prodigy is a broken city gate."
85
晉元帝太興二年六月，吳郡米廩無故自壞。 是歲大饑，死者數千。
In the sixth month of the second year of Taixing under Emperor Yuan of Jin, Wu commandery's granary collapsed without cause. That year famine struck; several thousand perished.
86
晉明帝太寧元年，周筵自歸王敦，既立宅宇，而所起五間六架，一時躍出墮地，餘桁猶亘柱頭。 此金沴木也。 明年五月，錢鳳謀亂，遂族滅筵，而湖熟尋亦為墟矣。
In the first year of Taining under Emperor Ming of Jin, Zhou Yan went over to Wang Dun; after he built a house, the five bays and six frames he had raised sprang out at once and crashed to earth, though the remaining rafters still bridged the pillar tops. This was metal encroaching upon wood. In the fifth month of the following year Qian Feng plotted rebellion; Zhou Yan's clan was wiped out, and Hushu soon lay in ruins.
87
晉安帝元興元年正月丙子，司馬元顯將西討桓玄，建牙揚州南門，其東者難立，良久乃正。 近沴妖也。 尋為桓玄所禽。
On the day bingzi in the first month of the first year of Yuanxing under Emperor An of Jin, as Sima Yuanxian prepared to march west against Huan Xuan, battle flags were raised at Yang province's south gate; the eastern standard was hard to set upright and stood straight only after a long delay. A prodigy of encroachment close at hand. Soon Huan Xuan captured him.
88
元興三年五月，樂賢堂壞。 天意若曰，安帝嚚眊，不及有樂賢之心，故此堂見沴也。
In the fifth month of the third year of Yuanxing the Hall of Delighting in Worthies collapsed. Heaven's intent seemed to say: Emperor An was dull and dim-sighted and had no heart to delight in worthies — so this hall met encroachment.
89
晉安帝義熙九年五月乙酉，國子聖堂壞。
On the day yiyou in the fifth month of the ninth year of Yixi under Emperor An of Jin, the Imperial Academy's Sage Hall collapsed.
90
宋文帝元嘉十七年，劉斌為吳郡，郡堂屋西頭鴟尾無故落地，治之未畢，東頭鴟尾復落。 頃之，斌誅。
In the seventeenth year of Yuanjia under Emperor Wen of Song, while Liu Bin was prefect of Wu commandery, the owl-tail finial at the west end of the prefectural hall dropped to the ground for no reason; before repairs were finished, the east-end finial fell as well. Before long Bin was executed.
