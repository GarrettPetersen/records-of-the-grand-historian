1
廢帝諱昱，字德融，小字慧震，明帝長子也。 大明七年正月辛丑，生於衞尉府。 太宗諸子在孕，皆以周易筮之，即以所得之卦為小字，故帝字慧震，其餘皇子亦如之。 泰始二年，立為皇太子。 三年，始制太子改名昱。 安車乘象輅。 六年，出東宮。 又制太子元正朝賀，服袞冕九章衣。
Emperor Fei II, whose personal name was Yu, courtesy name Derong, and childhood name Huizhen, was Emperor Ming's eldest son. He was born on xinchou day in the first month of the seventh year of Daming at the Commandant's residence. While Taizong's sons were still in the womb, each was divined with the Book of Changes, and the hexagram obtained became his childhood name; hence the emperor's childhood name Huizhen, and the other princes followed the same practice. In the second year of Taishi he was named crown prince. In the third year regulations were first issued requiring the crown prince to take the name Yu. He was granted use of the peace chariot and the elephant-drawn imperial carriage. In the sixth year he took up residence in the Eastern Palace. Regulations were also issued that at the crown prince's New Year's court audience he would wear the nine-emblem robe with round cap and tassels.
2
泰豫元年四月己亥，太宗崩。 庚子，太子即皇帝位，大赦天下。 尚書令袁粲、護軍將軍褚淵共輔朝政。 乙巳，以護軍將軍張永為右光祿大夫，撫軍將軍安成王為揚州刺史。 己酉，特進、右光祿大夫劉遵考改為左光祿大夫。
On jihai day in the fourth month of the first year of Taiyu Taizong died. On gengzi day the crown prince ascended the throne and proclaimed a general amnesty throughout the realm. Director of the Masters of Writing Yuan Can and General of the Guard Chu Yuan jointly assisted in governing the court. On yisi day General of the Guard Zhang Yong was made right grandee for splendid virtue, and General Who Tranquilizes the Army Prince of Ancheng was appointed inspector of Yangzhou. On jiyou day Special Advanced Grandee and Right Grandee for Splendid Virtue Liu Zun-kao was transferred to left grandee for splendid virtue.
3
五月丁巳，以吳興太守張岱為益州刺史。 戊辰，緣江戍兵老疾者，悉聽還。 班劍依舊入殿。
On dingsi day in the fifth month Administrator of Wuxing Zhang Dai was appointed inspector of Yizhou. On wuchen day garrison troops stationed along the river who were aged or infirm were all permitted to return home. Officials granted the privilege of bearing swords in court entered the hall as before.
4
六月壬辰，詔曰：「夫興王經制，實先民隱，方求廣教，刑於四維。 朕以煢眇，夙膺寶歷，永言民政，未接聽覽，眷言乃顧，無忘鑒寐。 可遣大使分行四方，觀採風謠，問其疾苦。 令有咈民，法不便俗者，悉各條奏。 若守宰威恩可紀，廉勤允著，依事騰聞。 如獄訟誣枉，職事紕繆，惰公存私，害民利己者，無或隱昧。 廣納芻輿之議，博求獻藝之規。 巡省之道，務令精洽，深簡行識，俾若朕親覽焉。 」又詔曰：「夫寢夢期賢，往誥垂美，物色求良，前書稱盛。 朕以沖昧，嗣膺寶業，思仰述聖猷，勉弘政道，興言多士，常想得人。 可普下牧守，廣加搜採。 其有孝友聞族，義讓光閭，或匿名屠釣，隱身耕牧，足以整厲澆風，扶益淳化者，凡厥一善，咸無遺逸。 虛輪佇帛，俟聞嘉薦。 」京師雨水，詔賑卹二縣貧民。 乙巳，尊皇后曰皇太后，立皇后江氏。
On renchen day in the sixth month an edict declared: "The canonical institutions of a founding king truly spring from the people's hidden needs; we now seek to spread instruction broadly and make it law in the four quarters. I, young and alone, have from early youth received the precious succession; though I have long spoken of civil affairs, I have not yet heard and reviewed them myself; turning my thoughts again and again, I forget neither waking nor sleeping to take them as my mirror. Let envoys be dispatched to travel separately through the four directions, observe and gather popular songs and rumors, and inquire into the people's hardships. Wherever laws displease the people or fail to suit local custom, let each case be listed and reported separately. If prefects and magistrates have authority and grace worthy of record, or show manifest probity and diligence, let reports on them be sent according to the facts. If lawsuits involve false accusations, official duties are mishandled, public service is shirked for private gain, or the people are harmed for personal profit, let nothing be concealed. Let counsel from the humblest sources be broadly received and plans for presenting talent widely sought. The path of inspection must be made thorough and precise; carefully select men of capacity and learning, so that it is as if I personally reviewed matters. " Again an edict declared: "From dreams one anticipates the worthy; former injunctions have left fine examples; in seeking out excellence, earlier writings spoke of a flourishing age. I, young and untested, have succeeded to the precious enterprise; I wish to follow and proclaim the sage's design, strive to broaden the path of governance, and whenever I speak of the many scholars, I constantly think of finding the right men. Let this be sent down broadly to governors and prefects to search widely and gather them in. Those whose filial piety and brotherly love are renowned in their clans, whose righteousness and forbearance shine throughout their hamlets, or who hide their names while butchering or fishing and conceal themselves in farming and herding—anyone who can stiffen diluted customs and support pure transformation—every such virtue, let none be overlooked. Empty carriages stand ready with silk, awaiting word of worthy recommendations. " Rain caused flooding in the capital region, and an edict ordered relief for the poor of two counties. On yisi day the empress was honored as empress dowager and Lady Jiang was installed as empress.
5
秋七月戊辰，崇拜帝所生陳貴妃為皇太妃。
On wuchen day in the seventh month of autumn the emperor's birth-mother, Offered Consort Chen, was honored as grand imperial consort.
6
閏月丁亥，罷宋安郡還屬廣興。 己丑，割南豫州南汝陰郡屬西豫州，西豫州廬江郡屬豫州。 甲辰，以新除征西將軍、開府儀同三司、荊州刺史蔡興宗為中書監、光祿大夫，安西將軍、郢州刺史沈攸之為鎮西將軍、荊州刺史，南徐州刺史劉秉為平西將軍、郢州刺史，新除太常建平王景素為鎮軍將軍、南徐州刺史。
On dinghai day in the intercalary month Song'an commandery was abolished and restored to Guangxing. On jichou day Nanruyin commandery of South Yuzhou was transferred to West Yuzhou, and Lujiang commandery of West Yuzhou was transferred to Yuzhou. On jiachen day newly appointed General Who Campaigns West, bearer of the staff with equal protocol to the Three Ducal Ministers, and inspector of Jingzhou Cai Xingzong was made supervisor of the secretariat and grandee for splendid virtue; General Who Pacifies the West and inspector of Yingzhou Shen Youzhi was made general who tranquilizes the west and inspector of Jingzhou; inspector of South Xuzhou Liu Bing was made general who pacifies the west and inspector of Yingzhou; and newly appointed director of ceremonies Prince of Jianping Jingsu was made general who tranquilizes the army and inspector of South Xuzhou.
7
冬十月辛卯，[1]撫軍將軍劉韞有罪免官。 辛未，護軍將軍褚淵母憂去職。
On xinmao day in the tenth month of winter, [1] General Who Tranquilizes the Army Liu Yun, having committed an offense, was dismissed from office. On xinwei day General of the Guard Chu Yuan left office to observe mourning for his mother.
8
十一月己亥，新除平西將軍、郢州刺史劉秉為尚書左僕射。 辛丑，護軍將軍褚淵還攝本任。 芮芮國、高麗國遣使獻方物。
On jihai day in the eleventh month newly appointed General Who Pacifies the West and inspector of Yingzhou Liu Bing was made left vice-director of the Masters of Writing. On xinchou day General of the Guard Chu Yuan returned to hold his former post in an acting capacity. The states of Rouran and Goguryeo sent envoys bearing tribute.
9
十二月，索虜寇義陽。 丁巳，司州刺史王瞻擊破之。
In the twelfth month the Northern Wei raided Yiyang. On dingsi day Inspector of Sizhou Wang Zhan defeated them.
10
元徽元年春正月戊寅朔，改元，大赦天下。 壬寅，詔曰：「夫緩法昭恩，裁風茂典，蠲憲貸眚，訓俗彝義。 朕臨馭宸樞，夤制氓宇，式存寬簡，思孚矜惠。 今開元肆宥，萬品惟新，凡茲流斥，宜均弘洗。 自元年以前貽罪徙放者，悉聽還本。」
On wuyin day, the first day of the first month of spring in the first year of Yuanhui, the era name was changed and a general amnesty was proclaimed throughout the realm. On renyin day an edict declared: "Clemency in law displays grace; curtailing excess is a flourishing standard; remitting punishment and pardoning offenses teaches the people constant righteousness. I preside over the imperial pivot and extend governance across the realm; I uphold leniency and simplicity and seek to inspire compassionate kindness. Now, as the new era opens with broad forgiveness and the myriad ranks are renewed, all those cast out and banished should equally receive expansive pardon. All who before this inaugural year suffered punishment and exile are permitted to return home."
11
二月乙亥，以晉熙王燮為郢州刺史。
On yihai day in the second month Prince of Jinxi Xie was appointed inspector of Yingzhou.
12
三月丙申，以撫軍長史何恢為廣州刺史。 婆利國遣使獻方物。 戊戌，以前淮南太守劉靈遺為南豫州刺史。
On bingshen day in the third month Chief Clerk of the Army Who Tranquilizes the Army He Hui was appointed inspector of Guangzhou. The state of Poli sent envoys bearing tribute. On wuxu day former administrator of Huainan Liu Lingyi was appointed inspector of South Yuzhou.
13
夏五月辛卯，以輔師將軍李安民為司州刺史。 丙申，河南王遣使獻方物。
On xinmao day in the fifth month of summer General Who Assists the Army Li Anmin was appointed inspector of Sizhou. On bingshen day the King of Henan sent envoys bearing tribute.
14
六月壬子，以越州刺史陳伯紹為交州刺史。 [2]乙卯，特進、左光祿大夫劉遵考卒。 壽陽大水，己未，遣殿中將軍賑卹慰勞。 丙寅，以左軍將軍孟次陽為兗州刺史。
On renzi day in the sixth month Inspector of Yuezhou Chen Bozhao was appointed inspector of Jiaozhou. [2] On yimao day Special Advanced Grandee and Left Grandee for Splendid Virtue Liu Zun-kao died. Shouyang suffered severe flooding; on jiwei day palace guard generals were dispatched to provide relief and offer consolation. On bingyin day General of the Left Army Meng Ciyang was appointed inspector of Yanzhou.
15
秋七月丁丑，散騎常侍顧長康、長水校尉何翌之表上所撰諫林，上自虞、舜，下及晉武，凡十二卷。
On dingchou day in the seventh month of autumn Attendant-in-Ordinary Gu Changkang and Commandant of the Long Water He Yizhi submitted the Admonition Forest they had compiled, from Yu and Shun down to Emperor Wu of Jin, twelve volumes in all.
16
八月辛亥，詔曰：「分方正俗，著自虞冊，川谷異制，煥乎姬典。 故井遂有辨，閭伍無雜，用能七教克宣，八政斯序。 雖綿代殊軌，沿革異儀，或民懷遷俗，或國尚興徙，漢陽列燕、代之豪，關西熾齊、楚之族，並通籍新邑，即居成舊。 洎金行委御，禮樂南移，中州黎庶，襁負揚、越。 聖武造運，道一閎區，貽長世之規，申土斷之制。 而夷險相因，盈晦遞襲，歲饉凋流，戎役惰散，違鄉寓境，漸至繁積。 宜式遵鴻軌，以為永憲，庶阜俗昌民，反風定保。 夷胥山之險，澄瀚海之波，括河圖於九服，振玉軔於五都矣。 」祕書丞王儉表上所撰七志三十卷。 京師旱。 甲寅，詔曰：「比亢序騫度，留熏燿晷，有傷秋稼，方貽民瘼。 朕以眇疚，未弘政道，囹圄尚繁，枉滯猶積，夕厲晨矜，每惻于懷。 尚書令可與執法以下，就訊眾獄，使寃訟洗遂，困弊昭蘇。 頒下州郡，咸令無壅。 」癸亥，鎮軍將軍、南徐州刺史建平王景素進號鎮北將軍。 庚午，陳留王曹銑薨。
On xinhai day in the eighth month an edict declared: "Dividing territory and rectifying customs was set forth in the records of Yu; differing institutions for valleys and rivers shine forth in the Zhou canon. Thus wells and paths were distinct and hamlet groups unmixed, enabling the Seven Teachings to be fully spread and the Eight Affairs duly ordered. Though across long ages tracks differed and institutions changed, whether peoples cherished migration or states favored resettlement—at Hanyang stood the magnates of Yan and Dai, and west of the Pass flourished the clans of Qi and Chu—all registering in new settlements until residence there became old custom. When the Jin line yielded the throne and rites and music moved south, the common people of the central provinces carried infants on their backs to the Yang and Yue regions. When sacred martial prowess forged the destiny and the Way unified the broad realm, a plan for ages was bequeathed and the land-allocation system was enforced. Yet ease and hardship followed one another, fullness and decline succeeded in turn; famine years scattered the people and military service slackened and dispersed them; living apart from home districts in foreign lands gradually accumulated until the problem grew vast. It is fitting to take the great track as model and make it an eternal statute, so that customs may flourish, the people prosper, and wayward trends be stilled to secure stability. The peril of Mount Xushan would be levelled, the waves of the vast sea cleared; the River Chart would be gathered within the nine domains and jade shafts would resound in the five capitals. " Secretary-in-Chief of the Palace Library Wang Jian submitted the Seven Records he had compiled, thirty volumes in all. The capital region suffered drought. On jiayin day an edict declared: "Recently the drought star has crossed its allotted degree and lingering heat scorches the days; autumn crops are harmed and the people's distress is at hand. I, slight in stature and troubled in conscience, have not yet expanded the path of governance; prisons remain crowded and wrongful detentions still accumulate; evening admonishment and morning compassion weigh on my heart. The director of the Masters of Writing together with judicial officials downward should go in person to examine the various prisons, so that wrongful lawsuits are cleared and the exhausted revived. Let this be sent down to the provinces and commanderies; all are to ensure that nothing is obstructed. " On guihai day General Who Tranquilizes the Army and inspector of South Xuzhou Prince of Jianping Jingsu was promoted to general who tranquilizes the north. On gengwu day Prince of Chenliu Cao Xuan died.
17
九月壬午，詔曰：「國賦氓稅，蓋有恒品，往屬戎難，務先軍實，徵課之宜，或乖昔准。 湘、江二州，糧運偏積，調役既繁，庶徒彌擾。 因循權政，容有未革，民單力弊，歲月愈甚。 永言矜歎，情兼宵寐。 可遣使到所，明加詳察。 其輸違舊令，役非公限者，並即蠲改，具條以聞。 」丁亥，立衡陽王嶷子伯玉為南平王。
On renwu day in the ninth month an edict declared: "State levies and commoners' taxes have fixed standards; in recent times, owing to military hardship, military supplies came first and the proper measures for collection sometimes departed from former norms. In Xiang and Jiang provinces grain transport piled up unevenly; corvée and levies were already heavy, and the common people were ever more disturbed. Following expedient policies, some abuses may not yet have been reformed; the people grow ever weaker and hardship worsens month by month. Long do I sigh in compassion, my feelings mingling with sleepless nights. Let envoys be dispatched to the places concerned to examine matters clearly in detail. Whatever deliveries violate old regulations or corvée falls outside public limits, let all be immediately remitted and corrected, with detailed lists reported." On dinghai day Boyu, son of Prince of Hengyang Yi, was installed as Prince of Nanping.
18
冬十月壬子，以撫軍司馬王玄載為梁、南秦二州刺史。 癸酉，割南兗州之鍾離、豫州之馬頭，又分秦郡、梁郡、歷陽置新昌郡，立徐州。
On renzi day in the tenth month of winter Chief Clerk of the Army Who Tranquilizes the Army Wang Xuanzai was appointed inspector of Liang and South Qin provinces. On guiyou day Zhongli of South Yanzhou and Matou of Yuzhou were transferred, and Qin, Liang, and Liyang commanderies were separated to establish Xinchang commandery and create Xuzhou.
19
十一月丙子，以散騎常侍垣閎為徐州刺史。 丁丑，尚書令袁粲母喪去職。
On bingzi day Attendant-in-Ordinary Yuan Hong was appointed inspector of Xuzhou. On dingchou day Director of the Masters of Writing Yuan Can left office to observe mourning for his mother.
20
十二月癸卯朔，日有蝕之。 乙巳，司空、江州刺史桂陽王休範進位太尉，尚書令袁粲還攝本任，加號衞將軍。 癸亥，立前建安王世子伯融為始安縣王。 丙寅，河南王遣使獻方物。
On guimao day, the first day of the twelfth month, there was a solar eclipse. On yisi day Minister of Works and inspector of Jiangzhou Prince of Guiyang Xiufan was promoted to grand commandant; Director of the Masters of Writing Yuan Can returned to hold his former post in an acting capacity and was given the additional title general of the guard. On guihai day Boyu, former heir of Prince of Jian'an, was installed as prince of Shi'an county. On bingyin day the King of Henan sent envoys bearing tribute.
21
二年春正月庚子，以右光祿大夫張永為征北將軍、南兗州刺史。
On gengzi day in the first month of spring in the second year Right Grandee for Splendid Virtue Zhang Yong was made general who campaigns north and inspector of South Yanzhou.
22
二月己巳，加護軍將軍褚淵中軍將軍。
On jisi day in the second month General of the Guard Chu Yuan was also made general of the central army.
23
三月癸酉，以左衞將軍王寬為南豫州刺史。
On guiyou day in the third month General of the Left Guard Wang Kuan was appointed inspector of South Yuzhou.
24
夏四月癸亥，詔曰：「頃列爵敍勳，銓榮酬義，條流積廣，又各淹闕。 歲往事留，理至逋壅，在所參差，多違甄飭。 賞未均洽，每疚厥心。 可悉依舊准，並下注職。」
On guihai day in the fourth month of summer an edict declared: "Recently, when ranks and fiefs were conferred and merit was recorded, the categories piled up ever wider and each case remained long unsettled. Years pass while matters linger; reason itself comes to obstruction and delay; locally the treatment is uneven and in many cases proper scrutiny is violated. Rewards are not evenly distributed, and this weighs on my heart. Let all be handled according to the old standards and reported to the responsible offices."
25
五月壬午，[3]太尉、江州刺史桂陽王休範舉兵反。 庚寅，內外戒嚴。 加中領軍劉勔鎮軍將軍，加右衞將軍齊王平南將軍，前鋒南討，出屯新亭。 征北將軍張永屯白下，前南兗州刺史沈懷明戍石頭，衞將軍袁粲、中軍將軍褚淵入衞殿省。 壬辰，賊奄至，攻新亭壘。 齊王拒擊，大破之。 越騎校尉張敬兒斬休範。 賊黨杜黑蠡、丁文豪分軍向朱雀航，[4]劉勔拒賊敗績，力戰死之。 右軍將軍王道隆奔走遇害。 張永潰於白下，沈懷明自石頭奔散。 甲午，[5]撫軍典籤茅恬開東府納賊，[6]賊入屯中堂。 羽林監陳顯達擊大破之。 丙申，張敬兒等破賊於宣陽門、莊嚴寺、小市，進平東府城，梟擒羣賊。 賞賜封爵各有差。 丁酉，詔京邑二縣埋藏所殺賊，并戰亡者，復同京城。 是日解嚴，大赦天下，文武賜位一等。 戊戌，原除江州逋債，其有課非常調、役為民蠹者，悉皆蠲停。 詔曰：「頃國賦多騫，公儲罕給。 近治戎雖淺，而軍費已多，廩藏虛罄，難用馭遠。 宜矯革淫長，務在節儉。 其供奉服御，悉就減撤，雕文靡麗，廢而勿修。 凡諸游費，一皆禁斷，外可詳為科格。 」荊州刺史沈攸之、南徐州刺史建平王景素、郢州刺史晉熙王燮、湘州刺史王僧虔、雍州刺史張興世並舉義兵赴京師。 己亥，以第七皇弟友為江州刺史。 芮芮國遣使獻方物。
On renwu day in the fifth month, [3] Grand Commandant and inspector of Jiangzhou Prince of Guiyang Xiufan raised troops in rebellion. On gengyin day martial law was proclaimed inside and outside the capital. Central army commander Liu Xu was also made general who tranquilizes the army; General of the Right Guard Prince of Qi was also made general who pacifies the south; as vanguard they marched south to attack and encamped at Xinting. General Who Campaigns North Zhang Yong encamped at Baixia; former inspector of South Yanzhou Shen Huaiming garrisoned Shitou; General of the Guard Yuan Can and General of the Central Army Chu Yuan entered to guard the palace offices. On renchen day the rebels suddenly arrived and attacked the fortifications at Xinting. The Prince of Qi repelled them and won a crushing victory. Zhang Jing'er, cavalry commandant of Yue, beheaded Xiufan. Rebel partisans Du Heili and Ding Wenhao divided their forces and marched on Zhuque ford; [4] Liu Mian resisted the rebels, was defeated, and died fighting to the last. General of the Right Army Wang Daolong fled but was overtaken and killed. Zhang Yong was routed at Baixia, and Shen Huaiming fled in disarray from Shitou. On jiawu day, [5] Chief Clerk of the Army Who Tranquilizes Mao Tian opened the Eastern Directorate to the rebels; [6] the rebels entered and encamped in the Central Hall. Director of the Feathered Forest Guard Chen Xianda attacked and routed them completely. On bingshen day Zhang Jing'er and others defeated the rebels at Xuanyang Gate, Zhuangyan Temple, and Xiaoshi, then advanced to pacify the Eastern Directorate city, beheading and capturing the rebel bands. Rewards, gifts, and enfeoffments were distributed according to merit. On dingyou day an edict ordered the two counties of the capital district to bury the slain rebels and those who had fallen in battle, under the same arrangements as in the capital. That same day martial law was lifted, a general amnesty was proclaimed throughout the realm, and civil and military officials were each promoted one rank. On wuxu day Jiangzhou's overdue debts were entirely remitted, and all irregular levies and corvée that had become a plague on the people were wholly abolished. It is fitting to rectify excess and profligacy and devote ourselves to frugality. Let all provisions for imperial service and attire be reduced and withdrawn; ornamental and sumptuous embellishments are to be abandoned and not restored. All idle expenditures are altogether forbidden; the relevant offices may draw up detailed regulations." 天府虛散，垂三十年。 江、荊諸州，稅調本少，自頃以來，軍募多乏。 其穀帛所入，折供文武。 豫、兗、司、徐，開口待哺，西北戎將，裸身求衣。 委輸京都，蓋為寡薄。 天府所資，唯有淮、海。 民荒財單，不及曩日。 而國度弘費，[10]四倍元嘉。 二衞臺坊人力，五不餘一； 都水材官朽散，十不兩存。 備豫都庫，材竹俱盡； 東西二𡍒，塼瓦雙匱。 敕令給賜，悉仰交市。 尚書省舍，日就傾頹，第宅府署，類多穿毀。 視不遑救，知不暇及。 尋所入定調，用恒不周，既無儲畜，理至空盡。 積弊累耗，鍾於今日。 昔歲奉敕，課以揚、徐眾逋，凡入米穀六十萬斛，錢五千餘萬，布絹五萬匹，雜物在外，賴此相贍，故得推移。 即今所懸轉多，興用漸廣，深懼供奉頓闕，軍器輟功，將士飢怨，百官騫祿。 署府謝雕麗之器，土木停緹紫之容，國戚無以贍，勳求無以給。 如愚管所慮，不月則歲矣。
The imperial treasury has stood empty and depleted for nearly thirty years. In Jiang and Jing provinces taxes and levies were originally meager; since recent times military recruitment has often lacked funds. The grain and silk that came in was entirely diverted to supply civil and military officials. Yuzhou, Yanzhou, Sizhou, and Xuzhou waited with open mouths to be fed; frontier generals in the northwest went bare-bodied seeking clothing. What was forwarded to the capital was, in sum, scant and thin. The treasury's reliance was solely upon the Huai region and the coast. The people were destitute and resources depleted, falling short of former days. Yet state expenditures were vast, [10] four times those of the Yuanjia era. Manpower in the Two Guards, platform offices, and workshops—fewer than one in five remained; The Director of Waters' material officers saw stores rot and scatter; not two in ten survived. The reserve capital arsenals had timber and bamboo utterly exhausted; East and west kilns had both brick and tile doubly depleted. Imperial orders for grants and gifts all depended upon market purchase. Buildings of the Masters of Writing Secretariat daily drew toward collapse; mansions and government offices were in large part breached and ruined. To look was to have no leisure to rescue; to know was to have no time to reach. Soon the fixed levies taken in were perpetually insufficient; with no reserves accumulated, reason itself led to total emptiness. Abuses piled up and waste accumulated, converging upon this day. Last year, by imperial order, overdue payments were collected from the masses of Yang and Xu provinces; altogether there came in six hundred thousand hu of grain, more than fifty million in coin, fifty thousand bolts of cloth and silk, and miscellaneous goods besides; relying on this mutual support, affairs could be managed onward. But now what hangs in arrears grows ever greater and expenditures steadily widen; I deeply fear that imperial provisions will abruptly fail, armament projects will halt, soldiers and officers will hunger and seethe with resentment, and the hundred officials will see their salaries fall short. Government offices would go without carved and splendid vessels; construction in earth and timber would cease its purple-trimmed splendor; imperial kin would have nothing to live on and those seeking rewards nothing to be given. As my feeble counsel apprehends, the crisis is not months but a year away.

To the general of the guard, general-in-chief, supervisor of the secretariat, and the eight chief ministers: Yu, as the legitimate eldest son, succeeded to the throne, and we hoped his understanding would deepen day by day and the altars of state find their support in him. Who would have thought that from childhood to adulthood he would prove utterly vicious and perverse, violating every small good and committing every great evil. Instruction and guidance were offered again and again, yet we always concealed his faults; his savage perversity could not be moved, and day by day it grew worse. He cast off cap and crown and long wore military dress; dogs and horses were his companions, hawks and falcons his delight. In black boots he walked the palace halls, hawking gear at the side of the imperial screen. He even rode alone to distant suburbs and slept alone in deep wilderness, wielding spear and halberd with his own hand and personally dismembering men; bare blades were his playthings, and killing his constant occupation. He abandoned the guard of crossed halberds and cast aside the rites befitting Heaven's chosen, hurried through market gates, sang drunkenly in taverns, roamed at night and forgot to return, feasted and slept in barracks, seized men's wives and daughters, and plundered their goods—things unrecorded in the law codes, unheard of since antiquity. Shen Bo was a Confucian scholar, Sun Chaozhi a meritorious minister, and the brothers Youwen had all shared in the dynasty's achievements. The four were guiltless, yet in a single morning were slain together. With flying arrows and clashing swords not even infants were spared; he tore open livers and bowels for sport and cast corpses into the river for laughter. Again he squandered without limit until the treasury was emptied; he levied taxes across the passes and rivers to fill private hoards, while the common people wailed and had nowhere to live. I and his birth-mother repeatedly exhorted him with righteous teaching, whereupon he plotted poison to unleash his savage rage. Deep anxiety consumed my days; I feared I would not survive till morning. From the ancient tyrants Xin and Gui down to You and Li of Zhou—compared with this, they do not reach one ten-thousandth. The people's resentment had grown deep and Heaven's wrath long accumulated; the seven ancestral temples stood on the brink of ruin, and the four seas lost their breath.
59

Deposing the benighted and enthroning the enlightened is a fine model of former ages; how much more when one extinguishes righteousness and turns against the Way, abandoned by Heaven and men alike—guilt as deep as at Muye, reason severed as at Tong Palace. Therefore I secretly ordered General Xiao to move with enlightened strategy; the hidden and the manifest joined in one design, and universal peace was restored. General-in-Chief of Agile Cavalry Prince of Ancheng is descended from Taizong; Heaven endowed him with deep wisdom, his bearing is composed and far-reaching, and his virtue shines even in humble retirement. His rank in the clan is lofty and his kinship flourishing; the imperial chronology returns to him, the hundred millions bind their hearts to him, and all living beings look to him. It is fitting that he brightly serve the ancestors and preside over the myriad states. Let the old rites be followed and enacted in due season. I, the survivor, look back in grief; my words end in boundless sorrow.
60
太后又令曰：「昱窮凶極暴，自取灰滅，雖曰罪招，能無傷悼。 棄同品庶，顧所不忍。 可特追封蒼梧郡王。 」葬丹陽秣陵縣郊壇西。
The empress dowager issued another edict: "Yu was utterly vicious and savage and brought ruin upon himself; though his crimes invited this end, how can one not mourn? To cast him out like the common multitude is more than the heart can bear. Let him be specially posthumously enfeoffed as prince of Cangwu commandery. " He was buried west of the suburban altar in Moling county, Danyang.
61
初昱在東宮，年五六歲時，始就書學，而惰業好嬉戲，主帥不能禁。 好緣漆帳竿，去地丈餘，如此者半食久，乃下。 年漸長，喜怒乖節，左右有失旨者，輒手加撲打。 徒跣蹲踞，以此為常。 主帥以白太宗，上輒敕昱所生，嚴加捶訓。 及嗣位，內畏太后，外憚諸大臣，猶未得肆志。 自加元服，變態轉興，內外稍無以制。 三年秋冬間，便好出遊行，太妃每乘青篾車，隨相檢攝。 昱漸自放恣，太妃不復能禁。 單將左右，棄部伍，或十里、二十里，或入市里，或往營署，日暮乃歸。 四年春夏，此行彌數。 自京城剋定，意志轉驕，於是無日不出。 與左右人解僧智、張五兒恒相馳逐，夜出，開承明門，夕去晨反，晨出暮歸。 從者並執鋋矛，行人男女，及犬馬牛驢，值無免者。 民間擾懼，晝日不敢開門，道上行人殆絕。 常著小袴褶，未嘗服衣冠。 或有忤意，輒加以虐刑。 有白棓數十枚，各有名號，鍼椎鑿鋸之徒，不離左右。 嘗以鐵椎椎人陰破，左右人見之有斂眉者，昱大怒，令此人袒胛正立，以矛刺胛洞過。 於耀靈殿上養驢數十頭，所自乘馬，養於御牀側。 先是民間訛言，謂太宗不男，陳太妃本李道兒妾，道路之言，或云道兒子也。 昱每出入去來，常自稱劉統，或自號李將軍。 與右衞翼輦營女子私通，每從之遊，持數千錢，供酒肉之費。 阮佃夫腹心人張羊為佃夫所委信。 佃夫敗，叛走，後捕得，昱自於承明門以車轢殺之。 杜延載、沈勃、杜幼文、孫超，皆躬運矛鋋，手自臠割。 執幼文兄叔文於玄武湖北，昱馳馬執矟，自往刺之。 制露車一乘，其上施篷，乘以出入，從者不過數十人。 羽儀追之恒不及； 又各慮禍，亦不敢追尋，唯整部伍，別在一處瞻望而已。 凡諸鄙事，過目則能，鍛鍊金銀，裁衣作帽，莫不精絕。 未嘗吹篪，執管便韻。 天性好殺，以此為歡，一日無事，輒慘慘不樂。 內外百司，人不自保，殿省憂遑，夕不及旦。
When Yu was in the Eastern Palace, at five or six he first began book learning, yet he was idle in his studies and fond of play; his tutors could not restrain him. He loved to climb the lacquered curtain poles more than ten feet above the ground and would stay there for half a meal before coming down. As he grew older his joy and anger lost all measure; whenever those around him displeased him he would beat them with his own hand. Barefoot and squatting—this became his habit. The tutors reported this to Taizong, and the emperor would order Yu's birth-mother to beat and instruct him severely. When he succeeded to the throne he feared the empress dowager within and the chief ministers without, and still could not fully indulge his will. After he received the cap-and-gown of adulthood his transformation grew ever sharper, and gradually none within or without could restrain him. Between autumn and winter in the third year he took to going out on excursions; the imperial consort mother would ride in a green bamboo carriage and follow to keep watch. Yu gradually gave himself over to license, and the imperial consort mother could no longer restrain him. Alone with a few attendants he abandoned his escort, sometimes for ten or twenty li, sometimes entering market wards, sometimes going to barracks, and returned only at dusk. In spring and summer of the fourth year such outings became ever more frequent. Once the capital had been secured his will turned ever prouder, and from then on not a day passed without his going out. With his attendants Jie Sengzhi and Zhang Wu'er he constantly raced in pursuit; at night they opened Chenming Gate, leaving at dusk and returning at dawn, or leaving at dawn and returning at dusk. Those who followed all carried halberds and spears; men and women among travelers, and dogs, horses, oxen, and donkeys—none who met them escaped harm. The people were alarmed and afraid; by day they dared not open their doors, and travelers on the roads nearly vanished. He constantly wore short trousers and jackets and never put on cap and gown. Whenever anyone displeased him he would inflict cruel punishment. He kept several dozen white clubs, each with its own name; needles, awls, chisels, and saws never left his side. Once he smashed a man's groin with an iron awl; when an attendant who saw it frowned, Yu flew into a rage, ordered the man to bare his back and stand upright, and drove a spear through his shoulder blade. On the terrace of Yaoling Hall he kept several dozen donkeys; the horse he himself rode was kept beside the imperial couch. Earlier a false rumor among the people said Taizong was not a man; the Chen imperial consort mother had originally been a concubine of Li Dao'er, and street talk sometimes said Yu was Dao'er's son. Whenever Yu went out or came in he often called himself Liu Tong, or styled himself General Li. He had secret relations with a woman of the Right Guard Wing-Litter Guard and went out with her whenever she traveled, carrying several thousand coins to pay for wine and meat. Zhang Yang, a trusted man of Ruan Tianfu, was one in whom Tianfu placed full confidence. When Tianfu fell, Yang fled in rebellion; later he was captured, and Yu personally had him crushed to death with a cart at Chenming Gate. Du Yanzai, Shen Bo, Du Youwen, and Sun Chao—all personally wielded spear and halberd and dismembered men with their own hands. When Youwen's elder brother Shuwen was seized north of Xuanwu Lake, Yu galloped there with a spear in hand and personally ran him through. He had a single open carriage made with an awning erected upon it and used it when going out; those who followed numbered no more than several dozen. The imperial guard in pursuit could never catch up; each also feared disaster and dared not pursue, but only kept ranks whole and watched from another place. Of all vulgar crafts, whatever he saw he could do; forging and refining gold and silver, cutting clothes and making caps—nothing was not exquisitely done. He had never blown the chi, yet when he took up the pipe the tones came in tune. By nature he loved killing; this was his delight, and on a day when nothing happened he grew gloomy and unhappy. Throughout the hundred offices within and without, no man could be sure of himself; the palace departments were anxious and alarmed, and evening did not reach morning before new fear arose.
62
齊王順天人之心，潛圖廢立，與直閤將軍王敬則謀之。 七月七日，昱乘露車，從二百許人，無復鹵簿羽儀，往青園尼寺，晚至新安寺就曇度道人飲酒。 醉，夕扶還於仁壽殿東阿氈幄中臥。 時昱出入無恒，省內諸閤，夜皆不閉。 且羣下畏相逢值，無敢出者。 宿衞並逃避，內外無相禁攝。 王敬則先結昱左右楊玉夫、楊萬年、呂欣之、湯成之、陳奉伯、張石留、羅僧智、鍾千載、嚴道福、雷道賜、戴昭祖、許啟、戚元寶、盛道泰、鍾千秋、王天寶、公上延孫、俞成、錢道寶、馬敬之、陳寶直、吳璩之、劉印魯、唐天寶、俞孫等二十五人，謀共取昱。 其夕，敬則出外，玉夫見昱醉熟無所知，乃與萬年同入氈幄內，以昱防身刀斬之。 奉伯提昱首，依常行法，稱敕開承明門出，以首與敬則，馳至領軍府，以首呈齊王。 王乃戎服，率左右數十人，稱行還，開承明門入。 昱他夕每開門，門者震懾不敢視，至是弗之疑。 齊王既入，曉，乃奉太后令奉迎安成王。
Prince of Qi, following the people's hearts, secretly plotted deposition and enthronement and consulted with General of the Direct Retinue Wang Jingze. On the seventh day of the seventh month Yu rode in his open carriage with about two hundred men, no longer with imperial guards or ceremonial escort, and went to Qingyuan Nunnery; in the evening he reached Xinan Temple and drank with the Daoist priest Tandu. Drunk, he was helped back in the evening to lie in the felt tent east of Renshou Hall. At that time Yu came and went without fixed pattern; the various gates within the palace were left unclosed at night. Moreover the officials below feared meeting him by chance, and none dared go out. The night guards all fled; within and without there was no one to restrain or supervise. Wang Jingze had first won over twenty-five of Yu's attendants—Yang Yufu, Yang Wannian, Lü Xinzhi, Tang Chengzhi, Chen Fengbo, Zhang Shiliu, Luo Sengzhi, Zhong Qianzai, Yan Daofu, Lei Daoci, Dai Zhaozu, Xu Qi, Qi Yuanbao, Sheng Daotai, Zhong Qianqiu, Wang Tianbao, Gongshang Yansun, Yu Cheng, Qian Daobao, Ma Jingzhi, Chen Baozhi, Wu Quzhi, Liu Yinlu, Tang Tianbao, Yu Sun, and others—to plot together to seize Yu. That evening Jingze went outside; Yufu saw that Yu was drunk and unconscious, and together with Wannian entered the felt tent and cut him down with Yu's own side-arm. Fengbo lifted Yu's head; according to the usual procedure he declared an edict and opened Chenming Gate to go out, gave the head to Jingze, and galloped to the general-in-chief's headquarters to present the head to Prince of Qi. The prince then put on military dress, led several dozen attendants, declared that he was returning from an outing, and entered through Chenming Gate. On other nights whenever Yu opened the gate the gatekeepers were terrified and dared not look up; this time they suspected nothing. Once Prince of Qi had entered, at dawn he then received the empress dowager's order to welcome Prince of Ancheng.
63
史臣曰：喪國亡家之主，雖適末同途，發軫或異也。 前廢帝卑遊褻幸，皆龍駕帝飾，傳警清路； 蒼梧王則藏璽懷紱，魚服忘反，危冠短服，匹馬孤征。 至於殞身覆祚，其理若一。 姬、夏之隆，質文異尚，亡國之道，其亦然乎。
The historian says: Lords who lose their states and ruin their houses, though they meet the same end, may set out from different beginnings. The Former Deposed Emperor debased himself in wandering and intimate favor—all with the dragon carriage and imperial regalia, with guards clearing the road. Prince of Cangwu hid the seal and wore the cord, forgot he was a fish in imperial dress, wore a perilous crown and short garments, and rode alone on a single horse. When it came to perishing and overturning the succession, the principle was one. In the flourishing ages of Zhou and Xia, substance and ornament were differently prized—yet the way to lose a state, is it not likewise?

"Inspector of Yuezhou Chen Bozhao was made inspector of Jiaozhou": Zhang Senkai's collation notes say: "According to the Annals of Emperor Ming, in the fifth year of Taishi Bozhao was inspector of Jiaozhou. In the seventh year Yuezhou was established. The Monograph on Provinces and Commanderies in the History of the Southern Qi says that in the second year of Yuanhui Chen Bozhao was inspector of Yuezhou—this must refer to the present passage.
