列傳第五十二良吏
Biography 52: Good Officials
2
高祖起自匹庶，知民事艱難，及登庸作宰，留心吏職，而王略外舉，未遑內務。 奉師之費，日耗千金，播茲寬簡，雖所未暇，而絀華屏欲，以儉抑身，左右無幸謁之私，閨房無文綺之飾，故能戎車歲駕，邦甸不憂。 太祖幼而寬仁，入纂大業，及難興陝方，六戎薄伐，命將動師，經略司、兗，費由府實，役不及民。 自此區宇宴安，方內無事，三十年間，氓庶蕃息，奉上供徭，止於歲賦，晨出莫歸，自事而已。 守宰之職，以六期為斷，雖沒世不徙，未及曩時，而民有所係，吏無苟得。 家給人足，即事雖難，轉死溝渠，于時可免。 凡百戶之鄉，有市之邑，歌謠舞蹈，觸處成群，蓋宋世之極盛也。 暨元嘉二十七年，北狄南侵，戎役大起，傾資掃蓄，猶有未供，於是深賦厚斂，天下騷動。 自茲至於孝建，兵連不息，以區區之江東，地方不至數千里，戶不盈百萬，薦之以師旅，因之以凶荒，宋氏之盛，自此衰矣。
Emperor Gaozu arose from humble ranks and understood how hard ordinary life could be. Once he held high office as chief minister, he kept an eye on the duties of local administrators; but with his royal strategy turned outward, he had little room left for domestic administration. Campaign costs ran to a thousand gold a day, so he had no chance to put his lenient, simple policies fully into practice; still he cut back splendor and checked his appetites, living thriftily himself, forbidding favoritism among attendants and luxuries in the inner apartments—so year after year the armies went to war without plunging the realm into distress. Emperor Taizu had been gentle and generous since youth. After he took over the great enterprise, when troubles flared in the Shaan region and the northern tribes pressed the borders, he sent generals to campaign in the Si and Yan circuit; costs were borne by the treasury, and the people were not pressed into labor. Thereafter the realm lay at peace and the interior knew no disturbance. For thirty years the common people flourished; what they owed the court was limited to the annual tax. They left at dawn, returned at dusk, and minded their own affairs. Prefects and magistrates served six-year terms. Though some held office for life without transfer—a practice not as strict as in earlier times—the people still had officials they could depend on, and clerks could not grab gains at will. Households were supplied and people had enough. Even when times were hard, the starving dead in ditches and canals—a scourge that could then be avoided. In hamlets of a hundred households and in market towns, song and dance broke out everywhere in groups—such was the height of Song prosperity. By the twenty-seventh year of Yuanjia, northern enemies invaded from the south and military service surged. Treasuries and stores were emptied and still could not meet demand; heavy taxes followed, and the realm was thrown into turmoil. From then until the Xiaojian era, war never ceased. In the small Jiangdong domain—less than a few thousand li across, with fewer than a million households—armies and famine piled on one another, and Song greatness began to wane.
3
晉世諸帝，多處內房，朝宴所臨，東西二堂而已。 孝武末年，清暑方構，高祖受命，無所改作，所居唯稱西殿，不制嘉名； 太祖因之，亦有合殿之稱。 及世祖承統，制度奢廣，犬馬餘菽粟，土木衣綈繡，追陋前規，更造正光、玉燭、紫極諸殿。 雕欒綺節，珠窗網戶，嬖女幸臣，賜傾府藏，竭四海不供其欲，單民命未快其心。 太宗繼阼，彌篤浮侈，恩不恤下，以至橫流。 蒞民之官，遷變歲屬，灶不得黔，席未暇暖，蒲、密之化，事未易階。 豈徒吏不及古，民偽于昔，蓋由為上所擾，致治莫從。 今采其風跡粗著者，以為《良吏篇》云。
Jin emperors mostly stayed within the inner quarters; for court banquets they used only the eastern and western halls. In Emperor Xiaowu's last years the Clear Summer Pavilion was under construction. When Gaozu took the throne he changed nothing: his residence was simply called the Western Hall, without a grand ceremonial name; Taizu followed suit and also used the name United Hall. When Emperor Shizu came to the throne, institutions grew lavish and vast: dogs and horses had grain to spare, builders dressed timber in silk brocade. Casting aside the frugal precedents, he erected the Zhengguang, Yuzhu, Ziji, and other halls anew. Carved beams bore brocade tracery; pearl windows and latticed doors gleamed. Favored women and court favorites drew gifts that drained the treasury; all within the four seas could not satisfy their wants, and a single commoner's life was not enough to please them. Emperor Taizong succeeded him and pursued extravagance all the more, showing no concern for those below until abuses overflowed everywhere. Magistrates who governed the people were rotated every year: the stove never blackened, the mat never warmed. The civilizing reforms of Pu and Mi were not something one could reach in a single step. It was not merely that officials fell short of antiquity or the people grew more deceitful than before; the rulers above kept disrupting them, so good government had no chance to take hold. Here I collect those whose reputations stand out clearly enough to form this chapter on Good Officials.
4
王鎮之，字伯重，琅邪臨沂人，征士弘之兄也。 曾祖暠，晉驃騎將軍。 祖耆之，中書郎。 父隨之，上虞令。 鎮之初為琅邪王衛軍行參軍，出補剡、上虞令，並有能名。 內史謝輶請為山陰令，復有殊績。 遷衛軍參軍，本國郎中令，加寧朔將軍。 桓玄輔晉，以為大將軍錄事參軍。 時三吳饑荒，遣鎮之銜命賑恤，而會稽內史王愉不奉符旨，鎮之依事糾奏。 愉子綏，玄之外甥，當時貴盛，鎮之為所排抑，以母老求補安成太守。 及玄敗，玄將苻宏寇亂郡境，鎮之拒戰彌年，子弟五人，並臨陣見殺。 母憂去職，在官清潔，妻子無以自給，乃棄家致喪還上虞舊基。 畢，為子標之求安復令，隨子之官。 服闋，為征西道規司馬、南平太守。 徐道覆逼江陵，加鎮之建威將軍，統檀道濟、到彥之等討道覆，以不經將帥，固辭，不見聽。 既而前軍失利，白衣領職，尋復本官。 以討道覆功，封華容縣五等男，徵廷尉。 晉穆帝何皇后山陵，領將作大匠。 遷御史中丞，秉正不撓，百僚憚之。
Wang Zhenzhi, courtesy name Bozhong, came from Linyi in Langye and was the elder brother of the recluse Wang Hongzhi. His great-grandfather Wang Hao served Jin as General of Agile Cavalry. His grandfather Wang Qizhi was a Secretariat Gentleman. His father Wang Suizhi was magistrate of Shangyu. Zhenzhi first served as acting aide in the guard of the Prince of Langye, then took outside appointments as magistrate of Shan and Shangyu, winning a name for competence in both posts. Interior Minister Xie Zhou asked that he be made magistrate of Shanyin, where he again produced exceptional results. He was promoted to army aide of the guard general, grandee master of his native commandery, and additionally made General Who Pacifies the North. When Huan Xuan dominated Jin, Zhenzhi was appointed chief clerk aide to the grand general. When famine struck the Three Wu region, Zhenzhi was sent with imperial orders to provide relief. Kuaiji interior minister Wang Yu refused to obey the edict, and Zhenzhi impeached him on the facts. Wang Yu's son Sui was Huan Xuan's nephew and then at the height of power; Zhenzhi was sidelined by them and, citing his mother's age, asked to be made prefect of Ancheng. After Huan Xuan's fall, his general Fu Hong ravaged the commandery. Zhenzhi held out in battle for a year; five of his sons and younger brothers were killed in action. He left office to mourn his mother. His official service had been so scrupulously clean that his wife and children could not support themselves; he gave up his household goods and brought her coffin back to his old home in Shangyu. When the mourning was over, he secured the magistracy of Anfu for his son Biaozhi and went with him to his post. After mourning he became western campaign aide to Liu Daogui and prefect of Nanping. When Xu Daofu threatened Jiangling, Zhenzhi was made General Who Establishes Might and placed over Tan Daoji, Dao Yanzhi, and others to suppress Daofu. He firmly declined on the ground that he had never served as a field commander, but his plea was rejected. When the vanguard was defeated he was briefly reduced to commoner status, then soon restored to his former rank. For his part in defeating Daofu he was enfeoffed as fifth-rank baron of Huarong and summoned to be minister of justice. He oversaw construction of the tomb mound for Empress He, consort of Emperor Mudi of Jin. He was promoted to imperial censor-in-chief, upright and unyielding, and the whole bureaucracy stood in awe of him.
5
出為使持節、都督交、廣二州諸軍事、建威將軍、平越中郎將、廣州刺史。 高祖謂人曰：「王鎮之少著清績，必將繼美吳隱之。 嶺南之弊，非此不康也。 」在鎮不受俸祿，蕭然無所營。 去官之日，不異始至。 高祖初建相國府，以為諮議參軍，領錄事。 善於吏職，嚴而不殘。 遷宋臺祠部尚書。 高祖踐阼，鎮之以腳患自陳，出為輔國將軍、琅邪太守，遷宣訓衛尉，領本州大中正。 永初三年，卒官，時年六十六。 弟弘之，在《隱逸傳》。
He was sent out bearing the staff of authority as commander of military affairs in Jiao and Guang, General Who Establishes Might, General of the Household Who Pacifies Yue, and inspector of Guang Province. Gaozu told others, "Wang Zhenzhi has shown a clean record from early on; he is sure to follow in the footsteps of Wu Yinshi. The ills of the Lingnan region will not be cured without him." In his post he refused salary and stipends and lived sparely, with no private ventures. When he left office he was no richer than when he had arrived. When Gaozu first set up the chancellor's office, Zhenzhi was made advisory aide and head of records. He excelled at administrative duty—strict but not harsh. He was promoted to minister of rites of the Song terrace. After Gaozu took the throne, Zhenzhi cited a foot ailment and left the capital as General Who Assists the State and prefect of Langye, then became commandant of the garrison for instruction and grand rectifier of his native province. In the third year of Yongchu he died in office at the age of sixty-six. His younger brother Hongzhi is treated in the chapter on recluses.
6
杜慧度，交趾朱鸘人也。 本屬京兆。 曾祖元，為寧浦太守，遂居交趾。 父瑗，字道言，仕州府為日南、九德、交趾太守。 初，九真太守李遜父子勇壯有權力，威制交土，聞刺史騰遯之當至，分遣二子斷遏水陸津要。 瑗收眾斬遜，州境獲寧。 除龍驤將軍。 遯之在州十餘年，與林邑累相攻伐。 遯之將北還，林邑王范胡達攻破日南、九德、九真三郡，遂圍州城。 時遯之去已遠，瑗與第三子玄之悉力固守，多設權策，累戰，大破之。 追討於九真、日南。 連捷，故胡達走還林邑。 乃以瑗為龍驤將軍、交州刺史。 義旗進號冠軍將軍。 盧循竊據廣州，遣使通好，瑗斬之。 義熙六年，年八十四，卒，追贈右將軍，本官如故。
Du Huidu was a native of Zhu'er in Jiaozhi. His family originally came from Jingzhao. His great-grandfather Du Yuan served as prefect of Ningpu and then settled in Jiaozhi. His father Du Yuan, courtesy name Daoyan, served the regional government as prefect of Rinan, Jiude, and Jiaozhi in turn. Earlier, Li Xun, prefect of Jiuzhen, and his sons were bold and powerful and dominated the Jiao region. Learning that Inspector Teng Dunzhi was on his way, they sent two sons to seize the key passes by land and water. Yuan gathered forces, executed Xun, and restored peace in the province. He was appointed General of Soaring Dragon. Dunzhi spent more than ten years in the province, fighting repeatedly with Linyi. As Dunzhi prepared to return north, the king of Linyi, Fan Huda, overran Rinan, Jiude, and Jiuzhen and then besieged the provincial capital. Dunzhi was already far away. Yuan and his third son Xuanzhi defended with all their strength, devised many stratagems, fought battle after battle, and routed the enemy. They pursued the foe through Jiuzhen and Rinan. Victory followed victory until Huda fled back to Linyi. Yuan was then appointed General of Soaring Dragon and inspector of Jiao Province. When the righteous banner rose, his rank was raised to General Who Champions the State. Lu Xun had seized Guangzhou and sent envoys seeking friendship; Yuan had them executed. In the sixth year of Yixi he died at eighty-four and was posthumously made General of the Right with his former titles preserved.
7
慧度，瑗第五子也。 初為州主簿，流民督護，遷九真太守。 瑗卒，府州綱佐以交土接寇，不宜曠職，共推慧度行州府事，辭不就。 七年，除使持節、督交州諸軍事、廣武將軍、交州刺史。 詔書未至，其年春，盧循襲破合浦，徑向交州。 慧度乃率文武六千人距循於石碕，交戰，禽循長史孫建之。 循雖敗，餘黨猶有三千人，皆習練兵事。 李子遜李弈、李脫等奔竄石碕，盤結俚、獠，各有部曲。 循知弈等與杜氏有怨，遣使招之，弈等引諸俚帥眾五六千人，受循節度。 六月庚子，循晨造南津，命三軍入城乃食。 慧度悉出宗族私財，以充勸賞。 弟交趾太守慧期、九真太守章民並督率水步軍，慧度自登高艦，合戰，放火箭雉尾炬，步軍夾兩岸射之。 循眾艦俱然，一時散潰，循中箭赴水死。 斬循及父嘏，並循二子，親屬錄事參軍阮靜、中兵參軍羅農夫、李脫等，傳首京邑。 封慧度龍編縣侯，食邑千戶。
Huidu was Yuan's fifth son. He began as provincial chief clerk and protector of displaced populations, then became prefect of Jiuzhen. When Yuan died, the leading officials of the province argued that with enemies on the border the post could not stay empty and jointly urged Huidu to run provincial affairs; he refused. In the seventh year he was appointed bearer of the staff, commander of military affairs in Jiao, General of Expansive Might, and inspector of Jiao. Before the edict arrived, that spring Lu Xun stormed Hepu and marched straight on Jiao. Huidu then led six thousand officials and troops to meet Xun at Shiji, fought him, and captured his chief clerk Sun Jianzhi. Though Xun was beaten, three thousand seasoned fighters remained in his ranks. Li Zixun, Li Yi, Li Tuo, and others fled to Shiji, where they entrenched themselves among the Li and Liao peoples, each with his own band. Xun knew the Li brothers bore a grudge against the Du family and sent envoys to recruit them. They brought Li chieftains and five or six thousand men under Xun's command. On the gengzi day of the sixth month Xun reached Nanjin at dawn and ordered his three armies not to eat until they had entered the city. Huidu poured out his clan's private wealth to pay battle rewards. His brother Huigi, prefect of Jiaozhi, and Zhang Min, prefect of Jiuzhen, led the land and river forces while Huidu boarded a tall warship. In the combined battle they loosed fire arrows and pheasant-tail torches while infantry on both banks rained shots on the enemy. All of Xun's ships burst into flame at once and his force broke apart. Xun took an arrow and died in the water. They beheaded Xun, his father Gu, his two sons, and such associates as recorder aide Ruan Jing, central army aide Luo Nongfu, and Li Tuo, and sent the heads to the capital. Huidu was enfeoffed as marquis of Longbian with a fief of one thousand households.
8
高祖踐阼，進號輔國將軍。 其年，率文武萬人南討林邑，所殺過半，前後被抄略，悉得還本。 林邑乞降，輸生口、大象、金銀、古貝等，乃釋之。 遣長史江悠奉表獻捷。 慧度布衣蔬食，儉約質素，能彈琴，頗好《莊》、《老》。 禁斷淫祀，崇脩學校。 歲荒民饑，則以私祿賑給。 為政纖密，有如治家，由是威惠沾洽，姦盜不起，乃至城門不夜閉，道不拾遺。 少帝景平元年，卒，時年五十，追贈左將軍。
When Gaozu took the throne, Huidu was promoted to General Who Assists the State. That year he led ten thousand officials and troops south against Linyi, killing more than half the enemy and recovering all who had been carried off in earlier raids. Linyi sued for peace, offering captives, elephants, gold, silver, cowrie shells, and other goods, and he let them go. He sent chief clerk Jiang You to the court with a memorial reporting victory. Huidu dressed in plain cloth and ate simple food, living frugally and without ornament. He played the zither and was fond of Zhuangzi and Laozi. He banned improper cults and promoted the schools. In years of famine he used his private salary to feed the people. He governed with the meticulous care of a household head, so that his authority and kindness spread together, crime vanished, city gates stood unbarred at night, and no one picked up lost goods in the streets. In the first year of Jingping under Emperor Shaodi he died at fifty and was posthumously made General of the Left.
9
以慧度長子員外散騎侍郎弘文為振威將軍、刺史。 初，高祖北征關、洛，慧度板弘文為鷹揚將軍，流民督護，配兵三千，北係大軍。 行至廣州，關、洛已平，乃歸。 統府板弘文行九真太守。 及繼父為刺史，亦以寬和得眾，襲爵龍編侯。 太祖元嘉四年，以廷尉王徽為交州刺史，弘文就徴。 會得重疾，牽以就路，親舊見其患篤，勸表待病癒。 弘文曰：「吾世荷皇恩，杖節三世，常欲投軀帝庭，以報所荷。 況親被徵命，而可宴然者乎！ 如其顛沛，此乃命也。 」弘文母既年老，見弘文輿疾就路，不忍分別，相與俱行。 到廣州，遂卒。 臨死，遣弟弘猷詣京，朝廷甚哀之。
Huidu's eldest son Hongwen, an outside attendant cavalier, was made General Who Quells Might and inspector. Earlier, when Gaozu marched north against Guan and Luoyang, Huidu commissioned Hongwen as General of the Soaring Hawk and protector of displaced populations with three thousand men to join the main army in the north. He reached Guangzhou only to learn that Guan and Luoyang were already pacified, and turned back. The provincial headquarters commissioned him acting prefect of Jiuzhen. When he succeeded his father as inspector he likewise won the people through lenience and harmony and inherited the marquisate of Longbian. In the fourth year of Yuanjia, Emperor Taizu appointed Minister of Justice Wang Hui inspector of Jiao, and Hongwen was summoned to yield the post. He fell gravely ill but was dragged onto the road anyway. Kin and friends, seeing how serious his illness was, urged him to petition and wait until he recovered. Hongwen said, "Our family has borne the emperor's grace for generations. For three generations we have held the staff of office, and I have long wished to offer my life at court in repayment." How much more now that I have been summoned in person—how could I rest at ease!" If I come to ruin on the way, that will be fate." Hongwen's mother was already old. Seeing her son carried off ill on the road, she could not bear to part from him and went along with him. When he reached Guangzhou, he died there. On his deathbed he sent his younger brother Hongyou to the capital, and the court deeply grieved for him.
10
徐豁，字萬同，東莞姑幕人也，中散大夫廣兄子。 父邈，晉太子左衛率。 豁晉安帝隆安末為太學博士。 桓玄輔政，為中外都督，豁議：「致敬唯內外武官，太宰、司徒，並非軍職，則琅邪王不應加敬。 」玄諷中丞免豁官。 玄敗，以為秘書郎，尚書倉部郎，右軍何無忌功曹，仍為鎮南參軍； 又祠部，永世令，建武司馬，中軍參軍，尚書左丞。 永初初，為徐羨之鎮軍司馬，尚書左丞，山陰令。 歷二丞三邑，精練明理，為一世所推。
Xu Huo, courtesy name Wantong, came from Gumu in Dongguan commandery and was a nephew of Xu Guang, palace attendant-in-ordinary. His father Miao had been left commandant of the crown prince's guard under Jin. At the end of the Long'an reign of Emperor An of Jin, Huo served as an erudite of the imperial academy. When Huan Xuan held the regency as grand commander of inner and outer forces, Huo argued: "Salutations should be required only of inner and outer military officials. The grand tutor and grand marshal are not military posts, so the Prince of Langye ought not be made to render such salutes. " Huan Xuan induced the censor-in-chief to dismiss Huo from office. After Huan Xuan's defeat, Huo was appointed secretary gentleman, director of the storage bureau in the ministry of works, administrative aide to He Wuji of the right army, and then military aide to the army who guards the south; he also served in sacrificial affairs, as magistrate of Yongshi, army aide to the army who establishes might, military aide to the central army, and left assistant director in the ministry of works. At the beginning of the Yongchu era he became army aide to the army-in-chief under Xu Xianzhi, left assistant director in the ministry of works, and magistrate of Shanyin. Having served twice as assistant director and three times as magistrate, he was skilled, experienced, and clear in judgment, and his generation held him in high regard.

元嘉初，為始興太守。 三年，遣大使巡行四方，並使郡縣各言損益。 豁因此表陳三事，其一曰：「郡大田，武吏年滿十六，便課米六十斛，十五以下至十三，皆課米三十斛，一戶內隨丁多少，悉皆輸米。 且十三歲兒，未堪田作，或是單迥，無相兼通，年及應輸，便自逃逸，既遏接蠻、俚，去就益易。 或乃斷截支體，產子不養，戶口歲減，實此之由。 謂宜更量課限，使得存立。 今若減其米課，雖有交損，考之將來，理有深益。 」其二曰：「郡領銀民三百餘戶，鑿坑采砂，皆二三丈。 功役既苦，不顧崩壓，一歲之中，每有死者。 官司檢切，猶致逋違，老少相隨，永絕農業； 千有餘口，皆資他食，豈唯一夫不耕，或受其饑而已。 所以歲有不稔，便致甚困。 尋臺邸用米，不異於銀，謂宜准銀課米，即事為便。 」其三曰：「中宿縣俚民課銀，一子丁輸南稱半兩。 尋此縣自不出銀，又俚民皆巢居鳥語，不閑貨易之宜，每至買銀，為損已甚。 又稱兩受入，易生姦巧，山俚愚怯，不辨自申，官所課甚輕，民以所輸為劇。 今若聽計丁課米，公私兼利。」
At the beginning of the Yuanjia era he was made prefect of Shixing. In the third year the court sent grand commissioners to tour the realm and ordered every commandery and county to report what policies should be cut back or expanded. Huo therefore submitted a memorial on three matters. The first read: "In this commandery's chief fields, military conscripts who reach sixteen are assessed sixty hu of grain; from fifteen down to thirteen, each is assessed thirty hu. Every household must deliver grain according to however many males it contains. Thirteen-year-olds cannot yet work the fields. Some households stand alone with no one to share the burden; when the year of assessment arrives they simply flee. Because this region borders Man and Li territory, leaving and returning is all too easy. Some even mutilate their limbs or abandon newborn children. The population shrinks year by year, and this is the real reason. The assessment limits should be recalibrated so that people can survive. If the grain levy were reduced now, there would be an immediate shortfall, but judged by the future, the long-term gain would be great. " The second read: "More than three hundred households of silver miners in this commandery dig pits two or three zhang deep to extract ore. The work is so grueling that they ignore the risk of collapse, and every year people die in the pits. Even under strict official pressure they still fail to meet quotas. Old and young alike are drawn into the mines and abandon farming forever; more than a thousand mouths depend on others for food. The harm is not simply that one man fails to plow while others go hungry. That is why any lean year brings severe distress. Since the capital offices use grain no less than silver, the silver levy should be converted to a grain levy on the same standard. That would be the practical course. " The third read: "In Zhongsu County the Li people are assessed silver: each adult male pays half a liang by the southern weight standard. That county produces no silver of its own, and the Li live in tree nests and speak their own languages, knowing nothing of trade. Whenever they must buy silver to pay the tax, the cost to them is crushing. Weighing silver by the liang at collection also invites fraud. The mountain Li are simple and timid and cannot plead their own case. The official assessment seems light, yet what the people must actually deliver feels unbearably heavy. If they were allowed to pay grain assessed by head count instead, both the state and the people would benefit."
12
在郡著績，太祖嘉之。 下詔曰：「始興太守豁，潔己退食，恪居在官，政事脩理，惠澤沾被。 近嶺南荒弊，郡境尤甚，拯恤有方，濟厥饑饉，雖古之良守，蔑以尚焉。 宜蒙褒賁，以旌清績，可賜絹二百匹，穀千斛。 」五年，以為持節、督廣、交二州諸軍事、寧遠將軍、平越中郎將、廣州刺史。 未拜，卒，時年五十一。 太祖又下詔曰：「豁廉清勤恪，著稱所司，故擢授南服，申其才志。 不幸喪殞，朕甚悼之。 可賜錢十萬，布百匹，以營葬事。」
He distinguished himself in the commandery, and Emperor Wu praised him. An edict was issued: "Shixing Prefect Huo keeps himself pure, eats frugally, maintains reverent conduct in office, governs well, and spreads his kindness far and wide. Lingnan has lately been desolate and impoverished, and his commandery especially so, yet he found effective ways to relieve and succor the people through famine. Even the finest prefects of antiquity could scarcely match him. He should receive commendation and reward to mark his clean record. Let him be granted two hundred bolts of silk and one thousand hu of grain." In the fifth year he was appointed bearer of the staff, commander of military affairs in Guang and Jiao provinces, General of Distant Peace, General of the Household Who Pacifies Yue, and inspector of Guang Province. Before he could take up the appointment he died, at the age of fifty-one. The emperor again issued an edict: "Huo was renowned in every office for integrity, purity, diligence, and devotion. That is why he was promoted to the south, to give full scope to his talent and ambition. He has died untimely, and I deeply mourn him. Let him be granted one hundred thousand cash and one hundred bolts of cloth for his funeral arrangements."

陸徽，字休猷，吳郡吳人也。 郡辟命主簿，仍除衛軍、車騎二府參軍，揚州主簿，王弘衛將軍主簿，除尚書都官郎，出補建康令。 清平無私，為太祖所善，遷司徒左西掾。 元嘉十四年，為始興太守。 明年，仍除使持節、交、廣二州諸軍事、綏遠將軍、平越中郎將、廣州刺史。 清名亞王鎮之，為士民所愛詠。 上表薦士曰：「臣聞陵雪褒潁，貞柯必振； 尊風賞流，清原斯挹。 是以衣囊揮譽於西京，折轅延高於東帝。 伏見廣州別駕從事史朱萬嗣，年五十三，字少豫，理業沖夷，秉操純白，行稱私庭，能著官政。 雖氏非世祿，宦無通資，而隨牒南服，位極僚首，九綜州綱，三端府職，頻掌蕃機，屢績符守。 年暨知命，廉尚愈高，冰心與貪流爭激，霜情與晚節彌茂。 歷宰金山，家無寶鏤之飾； 連組珠海，室靡璫珥之珍。 確然守志，不求聞達，實足以澄革汙吏，洗鏡貪氓。 臣謬忝司牧，任專萬里，雖情祗慎擢，才闕豪露，敢罄愚陋，舉其所知。 如得提名禮闈，抗跡朝省，摶嶺表之清風，負冰宇之潔望，則恩融一臣，而施光萬物。 敢緣天澤云行，時德雨施，每甄外州，榮加遠國。 是以獻其瞽言，希垂聽覽。」
Lu Hui, courtesy name Xiuyou, came from Wu in Wu commandery. The commandery recruited him as chief clerk. He was then made military aide in the guard army and cavalry general's offices, chief clerk of Yang Province, and chief clerk to Wang Hong as general of the guard. He was promoted to director of punishments in the ministry of works and sent out to serve as magistrate of Jiankang. Pure, fair, and impartial, he won the emperor's favor and was transferred to left western aide on the grand marshal's staff. In the fourteenth year of Yuanjia he was made prefect of Shixing. The following year he was again appointed bearer of the staff, commander of military affairs in Jiao and Guang, General Who Pacifies the Distance, General of the Household Who Pacifies Yue, and inspector of Guang Province. His reputation for integrity ranked just below Wang Zhenzhi's, and gentry and common people alike loved to praise him. He submitted a memorial recommending a scholar, saying: "I have heard that when snow buries the peaks, the fir and cypress of Song and Ying are praised, and the upright trunk will surely stand firm; when lofty winds honor the stream, the clear spring is drawn from in turn. Thus Zhu Maichen won renown with nothing but robe and purse in the Western Capital, and a broken carriage axle drew in a lofty man for the Eastern Emperor. I humbly observe Zhu Wansyi, assistant administrator and attendant clerk of Guangzhou, aged fifty-three, courtesy name Shaoyu. His learning is serene and modest, his conduct pure, his private life exemplary, and his ability evident in public office. Although his family holds no hereditary stipend and his career had no ready patronage, he followed his appointments to the south and rose to the head of the staff. Nine times he has mastered a commandery's affairs, thrice he has held key posts in a prefecture, repeatedly handled frontier business, and repeatedly distinguished himself as inspector and prefect. Now past fifty, his integrity grows ever higher. His heart of ice contends fiercely with the stream of greed, and his frosty resolve and late-season virtue flourish all the more. Though he governed Jinshan, his home had no jewel-inlaid ornament; though he served in office after office along the Pearl River, his rooms held no jeweled ear pendants. He steadfastly keeps to his principles and seeks no fame or advancement. Truly he is fit to clarify and reform corrupt officials and hold up a mirror to greedy commoners. Your servant unworthily holds the post of regional governor, charged with ten thousand li. Though I am reverently cautious in recommending men and lack the talent to display them boldly, I dare offer all my poor knowledge and recommend one I know. If he could be nominated at the ritual gate and take his place in the court ministries, spreading the pure breeze of the Lingnan region and bearing a reputation for icy integrity, then grace would reach one minister while its light would shine on all things. I venture to rely on Your Majesty's grace moving like clouds and virtue falling like rain, which each time selects men from outer commanderies and adds honor to distant lands. Therefore I offer these blind words of mine, hoping they may receive your attention."
14
二十一年，徵以為南平王鑠冠軍司馬、長沙內史，行湘州府事。 母憂去職。 張尋、趙廣為亂於益州，兵寇之餘，政荒民擾。 二十三年，乃追徽為持節、督益、寧二州諸軍事、寧朔將軍、益州刺史。 隱恤有方，威惠兼著，寇盜靜息，民物殷阜，蜀土安說，至今稱之。 二十九年，卒，時年六十二。 身亡之日，家無餘財。 太祖甚痛惜之，詔曰：「徽厲志廉潔，歷任恪勤，奉公盡誠，克己無倦。 褒榮未申，不幸夙殞，言念在懷，以為傷恨。 可贈輔國將軍，本官如故。 」賜錢十萬，米二百斛。 諡曰簡子。 子睿，正員外郎。 弟展，臧質車騎長史、尋陽太守，質敗，從誅。
In the twenty-first year he was summoned to serve as champion army aide to the Prince of Nanping, Shuo, interior minister of Changsha, and provisional head of the Xiangzhou prefecture office. He left office to mourn his mother. Zhang Xun and Zhao Guang raised rebellion in Yizhou. After the ravages of war, government collapsed and the people were in turmoil. In the twenty-third year he was urgently recalled as bearer of the staff, commander of military affairs in Yi and Ning provinces, General Who Pacifies the North, and inspector of Yizhou. He had effective methods of discreet relief. Both authority and kindness were manifest, bandits and robbers subsided, people and goods prospered, and Shu was peaceful and content. To this day he is still praised. In the twenty-ninth year he died, at the age of sixty-two. On the day he died his household had no surplus wealth. The emperor deeply grieved for him and issued an edict: "Hui set his heart on integrity and purity. In every post he was diligent and devoted, served the public with full sincerity, and restrained himself without weariness. Commendation and honors had not yet been granted when he died untimely. The thought fills me with grief and regret. Let him be posthumously made General Who Assists the State, with his former rank retained." He was granted one hundred thousand cash and two hundred hu of grain. He was given the posthumous title Jianzi, "The Simple." His son Rui served as a regular outside attendant. His younger brother Zhan was chief clerk to Zang Zhi as cavalry general and prefect of Xunyang. When Zang Zhi was defeated, Zhan was executed with him.
15
阮長之，字茂景，陳留尉氏人也。 祖思曠，金紫光祿大夫。 父普，驃騎諮議參軍。 長之年十五喪父，有孝性，哀感傍人。 服除，蔬食者猶積載。 閒居篤學，未嘗有惰容。 初為諸府參軍，除員外散騎侍郎。 母老，求補襄垣令，督郵無禮，鞭之，去職。 尋補廬陵王義真車騎行正參軍，平越長史，東莞太守。 入為尚書殿中郎，出為武昌太守。 時王弘為江州，雅相知重，引為車騎從事中郎。 入為太子中舍人，中書侍郎，以母老，固辭朝直，補彭城王義康平北諮議參軍。 元嘉九年，遷臨川內史，以南土卑濕，母年老，非所宜，辭不就。 十一年，復除臨海太守。 至郡少時而母亡，葬畢，不勝憂，十四年，卒，時年五十九。
Ruan Changzhi, courtesy name Maojing, came from Weishi in Chenliu commandery. His grandfather Sikuan had been grand master of splendid happiness with the golden seal and purple ribbon. His father Pu had been advisory aide to the fast cavalry general. Changzhi lost his father at fifteen. Deeply filial by nature, his grief moved all who saw him. Even after mourning ended he continued to eat plain food for years. Living quietly at home he devoted himself to study and never showed a lazy face. He first served as military aide in various offices and was appointed outside attendant cavalier. When his mother grew old he sought appointment as magistrate of Xiangyuan. A postal inspector treated him disrespectfully, so he flogged the man and resigned. He was soon appointed acting regular military aide to the cavalry general under the Prince of Luling, Yizhen, chief clerk who pacifies Yue, and prefect of Dongguan. He entered the capital as director in the palace secretariat and went out again as prefect of Wuchang. At the time Wang Hong was in charge of Jiang Province and held him in high esteem, bringing him in as attendant gentleman on the cavalry general's staff. He entered the capital as palace attendant to the heir apparent and gentleman of the secretariat, but because his mother was old he firmly declined palace duty and was appointed advisory aide who pacifies the north under the Prince of Pengcheng, Yikang. In the ninth year of Yuanjia he was transferred to interior minister of Linchuan, but because the south is low and damp and his mother was old, he declined the post as unsuitable. In the eleventh year he was again appointed prefect of Linhai. Not long after he reached the commandery his mother died. When the burial was over he could not bear his grief, and in the fourteenth year he died, at the age of fifty-nine.

時郡縣田祿，芒種為斷，此前去官者，則一年秩祿皆入前人； 此後去官者，則一年秩祿皆入後人。 始以元嘉末改此科，計月分祿。 長之去武昌郡，代人未至，以芒種前一日解印綬。 初發京師，親故或以器物贈別，得便緘錄，後歸，悉以還之。 在中書省直，夜往鄰省，誤著履出閣，依事自列門下； 門下以闇夜人不知，不受列。 長之固遣送之，曰：「一生不侮闇室。 」前後所蒞官，皆有風政，為後人所思。 宋世言善治者，咸稱之。 子師門，原鄉令。
At the time, field-revenue salaries in commanderies and counties were reckoned at Grain-in-Ear. For an official who left before that date, the full year's salary went to his predecessor; for one who left after it, the full year's salary went to his successor. Only at the end of the Yuanjia era was this rule changed, and salaries were apportioned by month. When Changzhi left Wuchang, his replacement had not yet arrived. He surrendered his seal and cord on the day before Grain-in-Ear. When he first set out from the capital, relatives and friends sometimes gave him parting gifts. He sealed and recorded each item when he could, and on his return he restored them all. While on duty in the secretariat he went one night to a neighboring office and mistakenly walked out through the gate wearing shoes. As regulations required, he reported himself to the gate office; but the gate office, saying that no one had seen him in the dark, refused to accept the report. Changzhi insisted on submitting it again, saying: "All my life I have never cheated even a dark room. " In every office he held, before and after, he governed with exemplary integrity, and later generations remember him. Among those in the Song who spoke of good governance, all praised him. His son Shimen served as magistrate of Yuanxiang.

In the twelfth year of Yuanjia he was transferred to Linhai, and in both posts was praised for simplicity and frugality. He gave away all his salary and emoluments to relatives and friends, while his wife and children often went hungry and cold. When someone urged him to cultivate fields, Bingzhi said sternly: "A household that lives on official salary — how can it compete with farmers for profit! When he died, his wife and children were left cold and hungry. Conduct so unlike the common run is truly worthy of mourning. Let his household be granted five hundred hu of grain."
21
時有北地傅僧祐、潁川陳瑉、高平張祐，並以吏才見知。 僧祐事在《臧燾傳》。 瑉為吳令，善發姦伏，境內以為神明。 祐祖父湛，晉孝武世，以才學為中書侍郎，光祿勳。 祐歷臨安、武康、錢塘令，並著能名，宋世言長吏者，以三人為首。 元嘉中，高平太守潘詞，有清節。 子亮為昌慮令，亦著廉名，大明中，為徐州刺史劉道隆所表。 世祖世，吳郡陸法真歷官有清節，嘗為劉秀之安北錄事參軍。 泰山羊希與安北諮議參軍孫詵書曰：「足下同僚似有陸錄事者，此生東南名地，又張玄外孫，持身至清，雅有志節。 年高官下，秉操不衰，計當日夕相與申意。 」太宗初，為南海太守，卒官。
At that time Fu Sengyou of Beidi, Chen Min of Yingchuan, and Zhang You of Gaoping were all recognized for administrative talent. Sengyou is treated in the biography of Zang Can. Min served as magistrate of Wu and excelled at exposing hidden crimes; the commandery regarded him as almost supernatural. You's grandfather Zhan, in the reign of Jin Emperor Xiaowu, rose by learning to secretariat gentleman and grand master for splendid happiness. You served in turn as magistrate of Lin'an, Wukang, and Qiantang, winning a name for competence in each. When Song officials spoke of outstanding local administrators, these three headed the list. During Yuanjia, Gaoping prefect Pan Ci maintained a spotless record. His son Liang was magistrate of Changlu and likewise won a name for integrity; in the Daming era he was recommended by Xu inspector Liu Daolong. Under Emperor Shizu, Lu Fazhen of Wu commandery held a series of offices with a clean reputation and once served as recorder aide to Liu Xiuzhi, northern pacification general. Yang Xi of Taishan wrote to northern pacification advisory aide Sun Shen: "Among your colleagues there seems to be Recorder Lu. He comes from a distinguished southeastern family and is a grandson of Zhang Xuan. He conducts himself with exceptional purity and has genuine integrity." Though advanced in years he still holds a modest post without letting his conduct fade. I expect you will soon make your regard for him known. " Early in Taizong's reign he became prefect of Nanhai and died in office.
22
太宗世，琅邪王悅，亦蒞官清正見知。 悅字少明，晉右將軍羲之曾孫也。 父靖之，官至司徒左長史。 靖之為劉穆之所厚，就穆之求侍中，如此非一。 穆之曰：「卿若不求，久自得也。 」遂不果。 悅泰始中，為黃門郎，御史中丞。 上以其廉介，賜良田五頃。 遷尚書吏部郎，侍中，在門下，盡其心力。 五年，卒官，追贈太常。 初，悅為侍中，檢校御府、太官、太醫諸署，得姦巧甚多。 及悅死，眾咸謂諸署詋詛之，上乃收典掌者十餘人，桎梏云送淮陰，密令渡瓜步江，投之中流。
In Taizong's era Wang Yue of Langye was likewise known for governing with pure integrity. Yue, courtesy name Shaoming, was a great-great-grandson of Jin general of the right Wang Xizhi. His father Jingzhi rose to left chief clerk under the minister of education. Jingzhi enjoyed Liu Muzhi's favor and repeatedly asked him for the post of palace attendant. Muzhi said, "If you did not ask, you would have received it in time on your own." " In the end he never received it. During Taishi, Yue served as yellow gate gentleman and imperial censor-in-chief. The emperor granted him five qing of fine farmland for his integrity. He was promoted to gentleman of the ministry of personnel and palace attendant, and in the gate department gave his full energy. In the fifth year he died in office and was posthumously made minister of ceremonies. Earlier, when Yue was palace attendant, he inspected the imperial storehouse, grand provisioner, imperial physician, and other offices and uncovered many abuses. When Yue died, many believed the offices had cursed him. The emperor then arrested more than ten officials in charge, shackled them as if sending them to Huaiyin, secretly had them ferried across the Guabu River, and cast them into midstream.
23
史臣曰：夫善政之於民，猶良工之於埴也，用功寡而成器多。 漢世戶口殷盛，刑務簡闊，郡縣治民，無所橫擾，勸賞威刑，事多專斷，尺一詔書，希經邦邑，龔、黃之化，易以有成。 降及晚代，情偽繁起，民減昔時，務多前世，立績垂風，艱易百倍。 若以上古之化，治此世之民，今吏之良，撫前代之俗，則武城弦歌，將有未暇； 淮陽臥治，如或可勉。 未必今才陋古，蓋化有淳薄也。
The historian remarks: Good government toward the people is like a skilled potter with clay: little effort yields many vessels. In Han times the population was abundant, penal matters were simple, and counties governed without arbitrary interference. Rewards and punishments were often decided on the spot, imperial edicts rarely reached local towns, and the civilizing models of Gong Yu and Huang Ba were easy to realize. In later ages deceit multiplied, the population shrank, and official burdens grew. To leave a lasting achievement became a hundred times harder. If the civilizing ways of highest antiquity were applied to the people of this age, or if today's good officials had to govern the customs of earlier generations, then the zither-playing governance of Wucheng would find no leisure; only the recumbent governance of Huaiyang might barely be within reach. It is not that talent today is inferior to antiquity; rather, the moral climate has grown thinner.
