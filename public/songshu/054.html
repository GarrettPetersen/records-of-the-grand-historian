孔靖
Kong Jing
3
孔靖字季恭，會稽山陰人也。 名與高祖祖諱同，故稱字。 祖愉，晉車騎將軍。 父誾，散騎常侍。
Kong Jing, styled Jigong, came from Shanyin in Kuaiji commandery. Because his personal name matched the ancestral taboo of the High Founder's grandfather, he was addressed by his style rather than his given name. His grandfather Yu served the Jin as Chariot-and-Cavalry General. His father Yin held the post of attendant at the chamberlain for attendants.
4
季恭始察郡孝廉，功曹史，著作佐郎，太子舍人，鎮軍司馬，司徒左西掾。 未拜，遭母憂。 隆安五年，於喪中被起建威將軍、山陰令，不就。 高祖東征孫恩，屢至會稽，季恭曲意禮接，贍給甚厚。 高祖後討孫恩，時桓玄篡形已著，欲於山陰建義討之。 季恭以為山陰去京邑路遠，且玄未居極位，不如待其篡逆事彰，釁成惡稔，徐於京口圖之，不憂不剋。 高祖亦謂為然。 虞嘯父為征東將軍、會稽內史，季恭初求為府司馬，不得。 及帝定桓玄，以季恭為內史，使齎封板拜授，正與季恭相值，季恭便回舟夜還。 至即叩扉告嘯父，并令掃拂別齋，即便入郡。 嘯父本為桓玄所授，聞玄敗，震懼，開門請罪。 季恭慰勉，使且安所住，明旦乃移。 季恭到任，務存治實，敕止浮華，翦罰遊惰，由是寇盜衰止，境內肅清。
Jigong began as a commandery nominee for filial conduct and incorruptibility, then rose through posts as clerical officer, assistant gentleman in the Secretariat, attendant in the crown prince's household, marshal of the Pacification Army, and western clerk under the minister over the masses. Before he assumed office, his mother died and he entered mourning. In Long'an year 5, even during mourning he was called up as General Who Establishes Might and magistrate of Shanyin, but he declined. When Gaozu marched east against Sun En, he often passed through Kuaiji; Jigong went out of his way to honor him and provisioned his forces lavishly. On a later campaign against Sun En, Huan Xuan's plot to seize the throne was already plain, and Gaozu meant to launch a righteous rising from Shanyin to overthrow him. Jigong argued that Shanyin lay too far from the capital, and that since Xuan had not yet taken the throne outright, they should wait until his treason was unmistakable and his crimes fully ripe, then strike gradually from Jingkou—success would be assured. Gaozu agreed. Yu Xiaofu held the posts of General Who Pacifies the East and interior administrator of Kuaiji; Jigong had earlier sought a marshal's appointment on his staff but was refused. After the throne had suppressed Huan Xuan, Jigong was named interior administrator and an envoy was sent with the sealed patent of appointment; he met the envoy on the road, turned his boat about, and reached home that same night. As soon as he arrived he knocked at Xiaofu's door to announce himself, had a side chamber cleaned for his use, and entered the administrative city without delay. Xiaofu had owed his appointment to Huan Xuan; when he heard of Xuan's fall he was terrified and came out to submit and beg pardon. Jigong reassured him and told him to stay where he was until the next day before moving out. Once in office Jigong pursued practical administration, curbed ostentation, and punished loafers and the idle; banditry dwindled away and the commandery grew calm and clean.
5
徵為右衞將軍，加給事中，不拜。 尋除侍中，領本國中正，徙琅邪王大司馬司馬。 尋出為吳興太守，加冠軍。 先是，吳興頻喪太守，云項羽神為卞山王，居郡聽事，二千石至，常避之，季恭居聽事，竟無害也。 遷尚書右僕射，固讓。 義熙八年，復督五郡諸軍、征虜、會稽內史。 修飾學校，督課誦習。 十年，復為尚書右僕射，加散騎常侍，又讓不拜。 頃之，除領軍將軍，加散騎常侍，本州大中正。 十二年，致仕，拜金紫光錄大夫，常侍如故。 是歲，高祖北伐，季恭求從，以為太尉軍諮祭酒、後將軍。 從平關、洛。 高祖為相國，又隨府遷。 宋臺初建，令書以為尚書令，加散騎常侍，又讓不受，乃拜侍中、特進、左光祿大夫。 辭事東歸，高祖餞之戲馬臺，百僚咸賦詩以述其美。 及受命，加開府儀同三司，辭讓累年，終以不受。 永初三年，薨，時年七十六。 追贈侍中、左光祿大夫、開府儀同三司。
He was called up as General of the Right Guard with an additional post as supervising attendant, but declined. He was soon made palace attendant and chief rectifier of his home commandery, then transferred to serve as army marshal on the staff of the Prince of Langye, grand marshal. Shortly afterward he was sent out as governor of Wuxing with the additional rank of champion general. Before this, Wuxing had lost one governor after another; people said the god of Xiang Yu, as King of Mount Bian, haunted the commandery hall and that incoming governors always steered clear—yet Jigong took up residence there and came to no harm. He was promoted to right vice director of the Masters of Writing but firmly declined the post. In Yixi year 8 he again took command of the armies of five commanderies as General Who Captures Barbarians and interior administrator of Kuaiji. He restored the schools and supervised students in their recitation and study. In year 10 he was again named right vice director of the Masters of Writing with an additional post as attendant at the chamberlain for attendants, and once more declined. Soon afterward he was made General of the Palace Guard, with additional rank as attendant at the chamberlain for attendants and as grand rectifier of his native province. In year 12 he retired and was honored as recorder grandee with the golden seal and purple ribbon, while retaining his attendant title. That year Gaozu marched north; Jigong asked to go with the expedition and was named staff adviser and libationer on the grand marshal's army staff and rear general. He took part in the conquest of the passes and Luoyang. When Gaozu became chancellor of state, Jigong moved with his staff once more. When the Song regime was first set up, he was ordered to become director of the Masters of Writing with an additional post as attendant at the chamberlain for attendants; he declined again, and was then appointed palace attendant, special advance, and left household grandee. He resigned and went home to the east; Gaozu feasted him at the Terrace of the Sportive Horse, and the whole bureaucracy wrote poems in praise of him. After Gaozu took the throne, he was offered open office with ritual parity to the three dukes; Jigong declined for years and never accepted. He died in Yongchu year 3, at the age of seventy-six. He was posthumously honored as palace attendant, left household grandee, and holder of open office with parity to the three dukes.

=子山士，歷顯位，侍中，會稽太守，坐小弟駕部郎道穰逼略良家子女，白衣領郡。 元嘉二十七年，卒官。

11
=弟靈符，元嘉末，為南譙王義宣司空長史、南郡太守，尚書吏部郎。 世祖大明初，自侍中為輔國將軍、郢州刺史。 入為丹陽尹。 山陰縣土境褊狹，民多田少，靈符表徙無貲之家於餘姚、鄞、鄮三縣界，墾起湖田。 上使公卿博議，太宰江夏王義恭議曰：「夫訓農修本，有國所同，土著之民，習翫日久，如京師無田，不聞徙居他縣。 尋山陰豪族富室，頃畝不少，貧者肆力，非為無處，耕起空荒，無救災歉。 又緣湖居民，魚鴨為業，及有居肆，理無樂徙。 」尚書令柳元景、右僕射劉秀之、尚書王瓚之、顧凱之、顏師伯、嗣湘東王彧議曰：「富戶溫房，無假遷業； 窮身寒室，必應徙居。 葺宇疏臯，產粒無待，資公則公未易充，課私則私卒難具。 生計既完，畬功自息，宜募亡叛通卹及與樂田者，其往經創，須粗修立，然後徙居。 」侍中沈懷文、王景文、黃門侍郎劉敳、郄顒議曰：「百姓雖不親農，不無資生之路，若驅以就田，則坐相違奪。 且鄞等三縣，去治並遠，既安之民，忽徙他邑，新垣未立，舊居已毀，去留兩困，無以自資。 謂宜適任民情，從其所樂，開宥逋亡，且令就業，若審成腴壤，然後議遷。 」太常王玄謨議曰：「小民貧匱，遠就荒疇，去舊即新，糧種俱闕，習之既難，勸之未易。 謂宜微加資給，使得肆勤，明力田之賞，申怠惰之罰。 」光祿勳王昇之議曰：「遠廢之疇，方翦荊棘，率課窮乏，其事彌難，資徙粗立，徐行無晚。 」上違議，從其徙民，並成良業。
His younger brother Lingfu, late in Yuanjia, served the Prince of Nanqiao Yixuan as chief clerk to the minister of works and governor of Nan commandery, and as a gentleman in the ministry of personnel. Early in the Daming era under Emperor Xiaowu, he left the palace attendant post to become General Who Assists the State and inspector of Ying province. He was brought in as intendant of Danyang. Shanyin county was cramped for land and its people had too little farmland; Lingfu proposed relocating impoverished households to the margins of Yuyao, Yin, and Mao counties and reclaiming lake bottom for fields. The emperor ordered a full debate among the chief ministers. The grand mentor, Prince of Jiangxia Yigong, argued: "Teaching agriculture and strengthening the rural foundation is common to every state; people rooted in their native soil have long settled habits—even in the capital, one never hears of the landless being moved to other counties." "Besides, Shanyin's great families already hold plenty of land; the poor can still find work—it is not that they have nowhere to turn. Reclaiming empty ground will not cure years of disaster and shortage." "And those who live along the lakes earn their living from fish and fowl, or run shops—they cannot reasonably be expected to welcome relocation." Director Liu Yuanjing, right vice director Liu Xiuzhi, masters of writing Wang Zanzhi, Gu Kaizhi, Yan Shibo, and the heir Prince of Xiangdong Yu argued: "Rich households in comfortable homes have no reason to change trades;" "but the destitute in cold hovels ought to be relocated." "With patched roofs over marginal land, harvests cannot be counted on; public funds will not easily suffice, and private households cannot quickly be made whole." "Once livelihoods are secure, reclamation will take care of itself. Recruit fugitives, absconders, and men willing to till the soil; the lands they are sent to have been ravaged and must be roughly put in order before anyone is moved there." Palace attendants Shen Huaiwen and Wang Jingwen and yellow gate attendants Liu Kan and Xi Yan argued: "Even when the people do not farm themselves, they still have ways to live; drive them onto the land and you simply strip them of their livelihood." "Yin and the other counties lie far from the prefectural seat; people long settled are suddenly uprooted—new homes are not yet built while old ones are already lost; whether they go or stay they are trapped, with no way to support themselves." "We say policy should fit the people's wishes and let them do what they prefer: pardon fugitives and let them find work for now; only after the new land is truly fertile should relocation be debated." Minister of Ceremonies Wang Xuanmo argued: "Common people are poor; send them far into wasteland, tear them from the old and thrust them into the new—food and seed are both lacking; the habit is hard to form and persuasion is not easy." "Give them modest support so they can labor freely, make rewards for diligent farming clear, and enforce penalties on the lazy." Household grandee Wang Sheng argued: "Distant abandoned fields are only now being cleared of thorns; pressing the poor into service makes the task harder still. Establish support for the migrants first; there is no harm in moving slowly." The emperor rejected the opposing views, adopted Lingfu's relocation plan, and the migrants all established successful livelihoods.
12
靈符自丹陽出為會稽太守，尋加豫章王子尚撫軍長史。 靈符家本豐，產業甚廣，又於永興立墅，周回三十三里，水陸地二百六十五頃，含帶二山，又有果園九處。 為有司所糾，詔原之，而靈符答對不實，坐以免官。 後復舊官，又為尋陽王子房右軍長史，太守如故。 慤實有材幹，不存華飾，每所蒞官，政績修理。 前廢帝景和中，犯忤近臣，為所讒搆，遣鞭殺之。 二子湛之、淵之，於都賜死。 太宗即位，追贈靈符金紫光祿大夫。
Lingfu left the capital post to become governor of Kuaiji and was soon also named chief clerk on the pacification army staff of the Prince of Yuzhang, Zishang. Lingfu's family was already rich with vast estates; he also built a manor at Yongxing thirty-three li around, holding 265 qing of land and water, including two hills and nine orchards. The authorities impeached him; an edict pardoned the offense, but because Lingfu's answers were false he was dismissed from office. He later regained his offices and also served as chief clerk of the right army for the Prince of Xunyang, Zifang, while keeping the governorship. Earnest and capable, he shunned ostentation; wherever he served, his administration was sound and orderly. In the Jinghe era under the Deposed Emperor he offended a court favorite, was framed by him, and was ordered beaten to death. His two sons Zhanzhi and Yuanzhi were ordered to take their own lives in the capital. After Emperor Xiaowen came to the throne, Lingfu was posthumously honored as household grandee with the golden seal and purple ribbon.
13
淵之大明中為尚書比部郎。 時安陸應城縣民張江陵與妻吳共罵母黃令死，黃忿恨自經死，值赦。 律文，子賊殺傷毆父母，梟首，罵詈，棄市，謀殺夫之父母，亦棄市。 值赦，免刑補冶。 江陵罵母，母以之自裁，重於傷毆。 若同殺科，則疑重，用毆傷及罵科，則疑輕。 制唯有打母，遇赦猶梟首，無罵母致死值赦之科。 淵之議曰：「夫題里逆心，而仁者不入，名且惡之，況乃人事。 故毆傷呪詛，法所不原，詈之致盡，則理無可宥。 罰有從輕，蓋疑失善，求之文旨，非此之謂。 江陵雖值赦恩，故合梟首。 婦本以義，愛非天屬，黃之所恨，情不在吳，原死補冶，有允正法。 」詔如淵之議，吳免棄市。
Yuanzhi served in the Daming era as a gentleman in the comparative section of the Masters of Writing. At that time Zhang Jiangling of Yingcheng in Anlu and his wife Wu together abused his mother Huang until she wished for death; in fury she hanged herself. An amnesty was in effect. The code states that a child who kills, injures, or beats parents is beheaded and the head exposed; cursing and reviling is punished by execution in the market; plotting to kill a husband's parents is likewise market execution. Under amnesty the death penalty was commuted to service in the state ironworks. Jiangling cursed his mother until she killed herself—a graver wrong than mere injury or beating. To apply the homicide statute might be too harsh; to apply the statutes on beating or cursing might be too lenient. The code explicitly covers beating one's mother—even under amnesty the head is still exposed—but has no provision for cursing one's mother to death under amnesty. Yuanzhi argued: "When a village posts a man's name for treachery, the humane will not enter his door; even the name is loathed—how much more so when it comes to human conduct." Beating, injury, and cursing are never pardoned by law; when cursing drives a parent to death, reason offers no excuse. Leniency in punishment reflects doubt in favor of the accused; read against the spirit of the code, this case does not qualify. Even with the amnesty, Jiangling should still be beheaded and his head exposed. A daughter-in-law is tied by duty, not by blood; Huang's grievance was not aimed at Wu. Commuting her death to service in the ironworks fits the law. The emperor approved Yuanzhi's opinion; Wu was spared market execution.

羊玄保
Yang Xuanbao
15
羊玄保，太山南城人也。 祖楷，尚書都官郎。 父綏，中書侍郎。
Yang Xuanbao came from Nancheng in Taishan commandery. His grandfather Kai was a gentleman in the capital offices section of the Masters of Writing. His father Sui was attendant gentleman of the Secretariat.
16
玄保起家楚臺太常博士，遭母憂，服闕，右將軍何無忌、前將軍諸葛長民俱板為參軍，並不就。 除臨安令。 劉穆之舉為高祖鎮軍參軍，庫部郎，永世令。 復為高祖太尉參軍，轉主簿，丹陽丞。 少帝景平二年，入為尚書右丞，轉左丞，司徒右長史。 府公王弘甚知重之，謂左長史庾登之、吏部尚書王准之曰：「卿二賢明美朗識，會悟多通，然弘懿之望，故當共推羊也。 」頃之，入為黃門侍郎。
Xuanbao began as an erudite of the chamberlain for ceremonials on the Chu regime staff; after his mother's death and the end of mourning, Generals He Wuji and Zhuge Changmin both offered him staff posts, but he declined both. He was appointed magistrate of Lin'an. Liu Muzhi recommended him as a staff officer on Gaozu's pacification army, as gentleman of the storehouse section, and as magistrate of Yongshi. He again served on Gaozu's grand marshal staff, then became chief clerk and assistant intendant of Danyang. In Jingping year 2 under Emperor Shao he entered the Masters of Writing as right assistant director, then left assistant director, and chief clerk on the right under the minister over the masses. Wang Hong, head of the office, thought highly of him and told chief clerk Yu Dengzhi and minister of personnel Wang Zhunzhi: "You are both able and quick-witted, but in breadth and steadiness of character we should together advance Yang." Soon afterward he was brought in as yellow gate attendant.
17
善弈棊，棊品第三，太祖與賭郡戲，勝，以補宣城太守。 先是，劉式之為宣城，立吏民亡叛制，一人不禽，符伍里吏送州作部，若獲者賞位二階。 玄保以為非宜，陳之曰：「臣伏尋亡叛之由，皆出於窮逼，未有足以推存而樂為此者也。 今立殊制，於事為苦。 臣聞苦節不可貞，懼致流弊。 昔龔遂譬民於亂繩，緩之然後可理，黃覇以寬和為用，不以嚴刻為先。 臣愚以謂單身逃役，便為盡戶。 今一人不測，坐者甚多，既憚重負，各為身計，牽挽逃竄，必致繁滋。 又能禽獲叛身，類非謹惜，既無堪能，坐陵勞吏，名器虛假，所妨實多，將階級不足供賞，服勤無以自勸。 又尋此制，施一邦而已，若其是邪，則應與天下為一，若其非邪，亦不宜獨行一郡。 民離憂患，其弊將甚。 臣忝守所職，懼難遵用，致率管穴，冒以陳聞。 」由此此制得停。
He was skilled at go and ranked third in the realm; Taizu wagered a commandery in a match, and when Xuanbao won he was appointed governor of Xuancheng. Earlier, when Liu Shizhi governed Xuancheng, he instituted rules on fugitives and rebels: if even one was not caught, the local group and village clerk had to send a substitute to provincial labor; capture brought a two-rank reward. Xuanbao judged this unwise and memorialized: "I have considered the causes of flight and rebellion: they all spring from desperate poverty; no one with a decent livelihood chooses such a course." "To impose a special rule now only adds hardship." "I have heard that harsh measures cannot hold; I fear they will breed further abuses." "Gong Sui compared the people to tangled rope—only when loosened could it be straightened; Huang Ba governed through leniency and harmony rather than severity." "I believe that when one man flees labor service, the whole household is punished." "When one man vanishes without warning, many are punished; fearing the burden, everyone looks out for himself and drags others into flight—the abuses will only multiply." "Those who catch fugitives are rarely conscientious men; lacking real talent, they bully the clerks. Honors become empty, the harm is real, ranks will not cover the rewards promised, and honest effort wins no encouragement." "This rule applies to only one commandery; if it is sound, it should bind the whole realm; if it is unsound, it should not run alone in one prefecture." "The people, driven from their livelihoods, will suffer all the more." "Your servant holds his post in shame, fears he cannot enforce this rule, and ventures this humble report from his limited view." On this basis the regulation was suspended.
18
玄保在郡一年，為廷尉。 數月，遷尚書吏部郎，御史中丞，衡陽王義季右軍長史、南東海太守，加輔國將軍。 入為都官尚書、左衞將軍，加給事中，丹陽尹，會稽太守。 又徙吳郡太守，加秩中二千石。 太祖以玄保廉素寡欲，故頻授名郡。 為政雖無幹績，而去後常見思。 不營財利，處家儉薄。 太祖嘗曰：「人仕宦非唯須才，然亦須運命，每有好官缺，我未嘗不先憶羊玄保。」
After a year as governor he became minister of justice. Within months he became gentleman of the ministry of personnel, censor-in-chief, chief clerk of the right army for the Prince of Hengyang Yiji and governor of Southern Donghai, with the additional rank of General Who Assists the State. He was brought to court as minister of the capital offices and General of the Left Guard, with additional supervising attendant, intendant of Danyang, and governor of Kuaiji. He was then moved to governor of Wu commandery with salary at the middle two-thousand-bushel grade. Taizu kept assigning him famous commanderies because Xuanbao was frugal, plain, and free of greed. His administration showed no striking achievements, yet after he left people often missed him. He did not chase profit, and kept his household on a spare footing. Taizu once said: "Office requires talent, but luck as well; whenever a good post opens, Yang Xuanbao is always the first man I think of."

元凶弒立，為吏部尚書，領國子祭酒，尋加光祿大夫。 及世祖入討，朝野多南奔，劭集羣僚，橫刀怒曰：「卿等便可去矣！ 」眾戰懼莫敢言，玄保容色不異，徐曰：「臣以死奉朝。 」劭乃解。 世祖即位，以為散騎常侍，領崇憲衞尉。 尋遷金紫光祿大夫。 又以謹敬見知，賜賚甚厚。 大明初，進位光祿大夫。 五年，遷散騎常侍，特進。 玄保自少至老，謹於祭奠，四時珍新，未得祠薦者，口不妄嘗。 八年，卒，時年九十四。 諡曰定子。
When the arch-rebel Liu Shao murdered his way to the throne, Xuanbao became minister of personnel and director of the imperial university, and soon received the additional title of household grandee. When Emperor Xiaowu marched east to punish him, much of court and country fled south. Shao assembled the officials, drew his sword, and snarled: "You may all go now!" Everyone shrank in terror and none dared speak; Xuanbao's face did not change as he said calmly: "I will serve the dynasty with my life." Shao then backed down. After Xiaowu took the throne, he was made attendant at the chamberlain for attendants and superintendent of the palace enclosure guard. He was soon promoted to household grandee with the golden seal and purple ribbon. His reverent manner won favor, and imperial gifts to him were lavish. Early in the Daming era he was promoted to household grandee. In year 5 he became attendant at the chamberlain for attendants with special advance. From youth to old age Xuanbao was scrupulous in ancestral rites; seasonal delicacies not yet offered to the spirits never passed his lips. He died in year 8, at the age of ninety-four. He was posthumously titled Dingzi, "the Settled."
20
子戎，有才氣，而輕薄少行檢，玄保嘗云：「此兒必亡我家。 」官至通直郎。 與王僧達謗議時政，賜死。 死後世祖引見玄保，玄保謝曰：「臣無日磾之明，以此上負。 」上美其言。 戎二弟，太祖並賜名，曰咸，曰粲。 謂玄保曰：「欲令卿二子有林下正始餘風。」
His son Rong had talent but was frivolous and ill-disciplined; Xuanbao once said: "This boy will destroy our family." He rose to attendant for direct communication. He joined Wang Sengda in criticizing current policy and was ordered to take his own life. Afterward Xiaowu received Xuanbao in audience; Xuanbao apologized: "I lacked Ridimi's discernment as a father, and failed Your Majesty thereby." The emperor praised his reply. Taizu personally named Rong's two younger brothers Xian and Can. He told Xuanbao: "I want your two sons to keep something of the reclusive, Zhengshi-era spirit."
21
玄保既善棊，而何尚之亦雅好棊。 吳郡褚胤，年七歲，入高品。 及長，冠絕當時。 胤父榮期與臧質同逆，胤應從誅，何尚之請曰：「胤弈棊之妙，超古冠今。 魏犨犯令，以才獲免。 父戮子宥，其例甚多。 特乞與其微命，使異術不絕。 」不許。 時人痛惜之。
Xuanbao was skilled at go, and He Shangzhi was also a devoted player. Chu Yin of Wu commandery was ranked in the top class at the age of seven. As an adult he had no peer in his day. Yin's father Rongqi had rebelled with Zang Zhi, and Yin should have died with him. He Shangzhi pleaded: "Yin's mastery of go surpasses every player before or since." Wei Chou broke the law yet was spared for his ability. There are many precedents for executing the father while sparing the son. I beg only his life, so that this rare art may not die out. The plea was denied. Contemporaries mourned his loss.

兄子希
Brother's son: Xi
24
=玄保兄子希字泰聞，少有才氣。 大明初，為尚書左丞。 時揚州刺史西陽王子尚上言：「山湖之禁，雖有舊科，民俗相因，替而不奉，熂山封水，保為家利。 自頃以來，頹弛日甚，富強者兼嶺而占，貧弱者薪蘇無託，至漁採之地，亦又如茲。 斯實害治之深弊，為政所宜去絕，損益舊條，更申恒制。 」有司撿壬辰詔書：「占山護澤，強盜律論，贓一丈以上，皆棄市。 」希以「壬辰之制，其禁嚴刻，事既難遵，理與時㢮。 而占山封水，漸染復滋，更相因仍，便成先業，一朝頓去，易致嗟怨。 今更刊革，立制五條。 凡是山澤，先常熂爈種養竹木雜果為林㼐，及陂湖江海魚梁鰌鮆場，常加功修作者，聽不追奪。 官品第一、第二，聽占山三頃； 第三、第四品，二頃五十畝； 第五、第六品，二頃； 第七、第八品，一頃五十畝； 第九品及百姓，一頃。 皆依定格，條上貲簿。 若先已占山，不得更占； 先占闕少，依限占足。 若非前條舊業，一不得禁。 有犯者，水土一尺以上，並計贓，依常盜律論。 停除咸康二年壬辰之科。 」從之。
Xuanbao's nephew Xi, styled Taiming, showed talent from an early age. Early in the Daming era he became left assistant director of the Masters of Writing. The inspector of Yang province, the Prince of Xiyang Zishang, memorialized: "Though old laws restrict mountains and lakes, custom has worn them away; people seize hills and waters and treat them as private profit." "Lately the abuse has grown worse: the powerful monopolize whole ranges, the poor cannot gather fuel, and even fishing and foraging grounds are seized." "This is a grave harm to good government and should be abolished; revise the old rules and proclaim a permanent statute." The authorities cited the renchen-year edict: "Seizing mountains and guarding marshes—under the bandit statute, any seizure of one zhang or more is punished by market execution." Xi argued: "The renchen rules are harsh and hard to enforce; in principle they no longer fit the times." "Yet encroachment on hills and waters has spread for generations until it counts as inherited property; to abolish it overnight would breed widespread resentment." "Revise the law now and establish five articles." "All hills and waters long cleared for groves, orchards, fish weirs, and fisheries that have been actively maintained may be left in the holders' hands." "Officials of the first and second ranks may hold three qing of mountain land;" "the third and fourth ranks, two qing fifty mu;" "the fifth and sixth ranks, two qing;" "the seventh and eighth ranks, one qing fifty mu;" "the ninth rank and commoners, one qing." "All holdings must follow these fixed quotas and be entered on the property registers." "Those who already hold mountain land may not take more;" "if their prior holding falls short, they may fill out the quota within the limit." "Apart from established holdings under the preceding articles, no further seizure is permitted." "Violators who seize a chi or more of land or water are to be charged as thieves under the ordinary theft statute." "Suspend the renchen statute of Xiankang year 2." The emperor approved.
25
益州刺史劉瑀，先為右衞將軍，與府司馬何季穆共事不平。 季穆為尚書令建平王宏所親待，屢毀瑀於宏。 會瑀出為益州，奪士人妻為妾，宏使羊希彈之，瑀坐免官，瑀恨希切齒。 有門生謝元伯往來希間，瑀令訪訊被免之由。 希曰：「此奏非我意。 」瑀即日到宏門奉牋陳謝，云聞之羊希。 希坐漏泄免官。
Liu Yu, inspector of Yi province, had earlier served as General of the Right Guard and clashed with his headquarters marshal He Jimu. Jimu was a favorite of the director of the Masters of Writing, the Prince of Pingping Hong, and repeatedly slandered Liu Yu to him. When Liu Yu took up Yi province he seized a scholar's wife as a concubine; Hong had Yang Xi impeach him, and Liu Yu was dismissed; Liu Yu hated Xi bitterly. His student Xie Yuanbo frequented Xi's household; Liu Yu had him inquire why he had been dismissed. Xi said: "That memorial did not represent my wishes." That same day Liu Yu went to Hong's gate with a written apology, saying he had heard this from Yang Xi. Xi was dismissed for leaking the exchange.
26
大明末，為始安王子真征虜司馬，黃門郎，御史中丞。 泰始三年，出為寧朔將軍、廣州刺史。 希初請女夫鎮北中兵參軍蕭惠徽為長史，帶南海太守，太宗不許。 又請為東莞太守。 希既到鎮，長史、南海太守陸法真喪官，希又請惠徽補任。 詔曰：「希卑門寒士，累世無聞，輕薄多釁，備彰歷職。 徒以清刻一介，擢授嶺南，干上逞欲，求訴不已，可降號橫野將軍。」
Late in Daming he served as pacification-army marshal for the Prince of Shi'an Zizhen, yellow gate attendant, and censor-in-chief. In Taishi year 3 he was sent out as General Who Pacifies the North and inspector of Guang province. Xi first asked to appoint his son-in-law Xiao Huihui, a staff officer on the northern pacification army, as chief clerk and concurrent governor of Nanhai; Taizong refused. He then asked for him as governor of Dongguan. After Xi reached his post, chief clerk and Nanhai governor Lu Fazhen died; Xi again asked that Huihui fill the post. An edict said: "Xi comes from a humble house unknown for generations, is frivolous and full of offenses, as his whole career shows." "He was promoted to the south only for a show of severity; he importunes the throne and indulges his desires without end—reduce his rank to General of the Wild Expanse."
27
初，李萬周、劉嗣祖籍略廣州，事在鄧琬傳。 太宗以萬周為步兵校尉，加寧朔將軍，權行廣州事。 希既至，而萬周等並有異圖，希誅之。 希以沛郡劉思道行晉康太守，領軍伐俚。 思道違節度，失利，希遣收之。 思道不受命，率所領攻州，希遣平越長史鄒琰於朝亭拒戰，軍敗見殺。 思道進攻州城，司馬鄒嗣之拒之西門，戰敗又死。 希踰城走，思道獲而殺之。 府參軍鄒曼率數十人襲思道，已得入城，力不敵，又敗。 東莞太守蕭惠徽率郡文武千餘人攻思道，戰敗，又見殺。 時龍驤將軍陳伯紹率軍伐俚，還擊思道，定之。 贈希輔國將軍，惠徽中書郎，嗣之越騎校尉。
The earlier plundering of Guang province by Li Wanzhou and Liu Sizu is recorded in Deng Wan's biography. Taizong made Wanzhou colonel of footsoldiers with additional rank as General Who Pacifies the North and acting authority over Guang province. When Xi arrived, Wanzhou and his associates were plotting treason; Xi had them executed. Xi put Liu Sidao of Pei in charge as acting governor of Jinkang and sent the army against the Li people. Sidao disobeyed orders, was defeated, and Xi sent men to arrest him. Sidao refused obedience, led his men against the provincial seat, and Xi sent chief clerk Zou Yan to resist at Chaoting; Yan's force was defeated and he was killed. Sidao attacked the provincial city; army marshal Zou Sizhi held the west gate, was defeated, and died. Xi fled over the wall; Sidao caught and killed him. Staff officer Zou Man led a few dozen men in a raid on Sidao; they broke into the city but were overpowered and defeated. Dongguan governor Xiao Huihui led more than a thousand local officials and troops against Sidao; they were defeated and he was killed. General of the Dragon Valiant Chen Bozhao, then campaigning against the Li, turned back, attacked Sidao, and put down the revolt. Xi was posthumously honored as General Who Assists the State; Huihui as attendant of the Secretariat; and Sizhi as colonel of the valiant horse.

希子崇字伯遠，尚書主客郎。 丁母憂，哀毀過禮。 及聞廣州亂，即日便徒跣出新亭，不能步涉，頓伏江渚。 門義以小船致之，於是進路。 父葬畢，不勝哀，卒。
Xi's son Chong, styled Boyuan, was gentleman of the host and guest section in the Masters of Writing. In mourning for his mother he grieved beyond what the rites required. When he heard of the rebellion in Guang province, he went barefoot that same day from Xinting; unable to ford the river, he collapsed on the bank. A household retainer ferried him across in a small boat, and he went on. After his father's burial he succumbed to grief and died.

沈曇慶
Shen Tanqing
30
沈曇慶，吳興武康人，侍中懷文從父兄也。 父發，員外散騎侍郎，早卒，吳興太守王韶之為之誄焉。
Shen Tanqing came from Wukang in Wuxing and was a paternal cousin of palace attendant Shen Huaiwen. His father Fa was an extraordinary attendant gentleman of the chamberlain for attendants and died young; Wang Shaozhi, governor of Wuxing, wrote his dirge.
31
曇慶初辟主簿，州從事，西曹主簿，長沙王義欣後軍鎮軍主簿。 遭母憂，哀毀致稱，本縣令諸葛闡之公解言上。 服釋，復為主簿。 義欣又請為鎮軍記室參軍。 出為餘杭令，遷司徒主簿，江夏王義恭太尉錄事參軍，尚書右丞。 時歲有水旱，曇慶議立常平倉以救民急，太祖納其言，而事不行。 領本邑中正，少府，揚州治中從事史，始興王濬衞軍長史。 元凶弒立，世祖入討，劭遣曇慶還東募人，安東將軍隨王誕收付永興縣獄，久之，被原。
Tanqing began as chief clerk, commandery staff officer, western bureau chief clerk, and chief clerk on the rear army and pacification army staff of the Prince of Changsha, Yixin. When his mother died his grief beyond the rites won renown; his home county magistrate Zhuge Chan reported this in a public appraisal to the throne. After mourning he again served as chief clerk. Yixin again asked him to serve as recorder on the pacification army staff. He was sent out as magistrate of Yuhang, then became chief clerk under the minister over the masses, recorder on the grand marshal staff of the Prince of Jiangxia Yigong, and right assistant director of the Masters of Writing. In years of flood and drought Tanqing proposed ever-normal granaries to relieve the people; Taizu accepted the idea, but it was never implemented. He served as chief rectifier of his home district, privy treasurer, chief clerk of Yang province, and chief clerk of the palace guard for the Prince of Shixing, Jun. When Liu Shao murdered his way to the throne and Xiaowu marched east, Shao sent Tanqing home to recruit troops; the Prince of Sui Dan, General Who Pacifies the East, jailed him in Yongxing county; he was pardoned only after a long detention.
32
世祖踐阼，除東海王褘撫軍長史，入為尚書吏部郎，江夏王義恭大司馬長史，南東海太守，左衞將軍。 大明元年，督徐兗二州及梁郡諸軍事、輔國將軍、徐州刺史。 時殿中員外將軍裴景仁助戍彭城，本傖人，多悉戎荒事。 曇慶使撰秦記十卷，敍苻氏僭偽本末，其書傳於世。 明年，復徵為左衞將軍，加給事中，領本州大中正。 三年，遷祠部尚書。 其年，卒。 時年五十七。 追贈本官。 曇慶謹實清正，所莅有稱績。 常謂子弟曰：「吾處世無才能，政圖作大老子耳。 」世以長者稱之。
After Xiaowu took the throne, Tanqing became chief clerk on the pacification army staff of the Prince of Donghai Hui, then entered the Masters of Writing as gentleman of the ministry of personnel, chief clerk on the grand marshal staff of the Prince of Jiangxia Yigong, governor of Southern Donghai, and General of the Left Guard. In Daming year 1 he took command of the armies of Xu and Yan provinces and Liang commandery as General Who Assists the State and inspector of Xu province. At that time extraordinary palace general Pei Jingren was helping defend Pengcheng; a man of tribal origin, he knew the frontier well. Tanqing had him compile ten juan of Records of Qin on the rise and fall of the Fu regime; the work circulated widely. The following year he was recalled as General of the Left Guard with additional supervising attendant and as grand rectifier of his home province. In year 3 he was made minister of the imperial rites. He died that same year. He was fifty-seven years old. He was posthumously honored with his last office. Tanqing was earnest, upright, and incorruptible, and wherever he served he earned a good reputation. He often told his sons: "I have no real talent in public life; I only hope to grow into a respected elder." His contemporaries called him a man of the elder sort.

史評
Historian's Appraisal
34
史臣曰：江南之為國盛矣，雖南包象浦，西括邛山，至於外奉貢賦，內充府實，止於荊、揚二州。 自漢氏以來，民戶彫秏，荊楚四戰之地，五達之郊，井邑殘亡，萬不餘一也。 自義熙十一年司馬休之外奔，至于元嘉末，三十有九載，兵車勿用，民不外勞，役寬務簡，氓庶繁息，至餘糧栖畝，戶不夜扃，蓋東西之極盛也。 既揚部分析，境極江南，考之漢域，惟丹陽會稽而已。 自晉氏遷流，迄於太元之世，百許年中，無風塵之警，區域之內，晏如也。 及孫恩寇亂，殲亡事極，自此以至大明之季，年踰六紀，民戶繁育，將曩時一矣。 地廣野豐，民勤本業，一歲或稔，則數郡忘飢。 會土帶海傍湖，良疇亦數十萬頃，膏腴上地，畝直一金，鄠、杜之間，不能比也。 荊城跨南楚之富，揚部有全吳之沃，魚鹽𣏌梓之利，充仞八方，絲綿布帛之饒，覆衣天下。 而田家作苦，役難利薄，亘歲從務，無或一日非農，而經稅橫賦之資，養生送死之具，莫不咸出於此。 穰歲糶賤，糶賤則稼苦； 饑年糴貴，糴貴則商倍。 常平之議，行於漢世。 元嘉十三年，東土潦浸，民命棘矣。 太祖省費減用，開倉廩以振之，病而不凶，蓋此力也。 大明之末，積旱成災，雖敝同往困，而救非昔主，所以病未半古，死已倍之，并命比室，口減過半。 若常平之計，興於中年，遂切扶患，或不至是。 若籠以平價，則官苦民優，議屈當時，蓋由於此。
The historian writes: The realm south of the Yangzi was prosperous indeed; though it reached Elephant Harbor in the south and the Qiong mountains in the west, the tribute that filled the treasuries came chiefly from Jing and Yang provinces. Since Han times the population had been worn down; Jing and Chu were battlegrounds at every crossroads, and towns and villages had been destroyed until scarcely one in ten thousand remained. From Yixi year 11, when Sima Xiuzhi fled, to the end of Yuanjia—thirty-nine years—the armies rested, the people were not pressed by outside labor, taxes were light and administration simple, and the population grew until surplus grain lay in the fields and doors were left unbarred at night: this was perhaps the greatest prosperity the realm had known. After the Yang region was reassessed, the southern frontier amounted in Han terms to little more than Danyang and Kuaiji. From the Jin migration to the Taixuan era, for roughly a century there was no threat of war within the region, and the land was at peace. Sun En's rebellion brought slaughter to its peak; yet from then until the end of Daming—more than sixty years—the population recovered until it nearly matched the old levels. The land was broad and fertile, the people diligent in farming; one good harvest could keep several commanderies free of hunger. Kuaiji lay between sea and lake with hundreds of thousands of qing of fine fields; its richest land sold for a gold per mu—surpassing even the estates around Chang'an. Jing held the wealth of southern Chu; Yang held all the fertility of Wu—fish, salt, timber, and timber products supplied the empire, and silks and cloths clothed the realm. Yet farmers toiled bitterly; labor was heavy and profit thin; they worked the fields year round, and every tax, every cost of living and of burial, came from that labor alone. In bumper years grain was cheap, and cheap grain made farming bitter; in famine years grain was dear, and dear grain doubled the merchants' profits. The ever-normal granary policy had been practiced in Han times. In Yuanjia year 13 the eastern provinces were flooded and the people's plight was desperate. Taizu cut spending, opened the granaries, and relieved the distress; there was suffering but not catastrophe, thanks largely to that relief. At the end of Daming drought became disaster; the ruin matched old famines, but relief was nothing like before—sickness had not reached half the scale of antiquity, yet deaths had already doubled; whole households died together, and the population fell by more than half. Had the ever-normal granary system been established in mid-reign and truly succored the afflicted, the catastrophe might not have reached this pitch. Fixed fair prices would have burdened the state and favored the people; the proposal was defeated at the time largely for that reason.
