列傳第十五臧燾徐廣傅隆
Biography 15: Zang Dao, Xu Guang, and Fu Long
2
臧燾，字德仁，東莞莒人，武敬皇后兄也。 少好學，善《三禮》，貧約自立，操行為鄉里所稱。 晉孝武帝太元中，衛將軍謝安始立國學，徐、兗二州刺史謝玄舉燾為助教。
Zang Dao, courtesy name Deren, came from Ju in Dongguan and was the elder brother of Empress Dowager Wu Jing. From youth he was devoted to study, mastered the Three Rites, and though poor lived self-reliantly; his character won praise throughout his neighborhood. During the Taikang reign of Emperor Xiaowu of Jin, when Defender-in-Chief Xie An founded the National Academy, Xie Xuan, governor of Xu and Yan, nominated Dao as an assistant instructor.
3
孝武帝追崇庶祖母宣太后，議者或謂宜配食中宗。 燾議曰：「《陽秋》，之義，母以子貴，故仲子、成風，咸稱夫人。 《經》雲'考仲子之宮'。 若配食惠廟，則宮無緣別築。 前漢孝文、孝昭太后，並系子為號，祭於寢園，不配于高祖、孝武之廟。 後漢和帝之母曰恭懷皇所，安帝祖母曰敬隱皇后，順帝之母曰恭湣皇后，雖不系子為號，亦祭於陵寢。 不配章、安二帝。 此則二漢雖有太后、皇后之異，至於並不配食，義同《陽秋》。 唯光武追廢呂後，故以薄後配高祖廟。 又衛後既廢，霍光追尊李夫人為皇后，配孝武廟，此非母以子貴之例，直以高、武二廟無配故耳。 夫漢立寢於陵，自是晉制所異。 謂宜遠准《陽秋》考宮之義，近摹二漢不配之典。 尊號既正，則罔極之情申，別建寢廟，則嚴禰之義顯，系子為稱，兼明母貴之所由，一舉而允三義，固哲王之高致也。 」議者從之。
When Emperor Xiaowu raised posthumous honors for his father's concubine mother, Empress Dowager Xuan, some advisers argued that she ought to share sacrifices in Emperor Zhongzong's temple. Dao argued: "According to the Spring and Autumn Annals, a mother gains rank through her son—hence Zhongzi and Cheng Feng were both addressed as ladies." The Classic records that he 'visited the shrine of Zhongzi.' Were she to share the temple of Emperor Hui, there would be no occasion to erect a separate shrine. Under the Former Han, the mothers of Emperors Wen and Zhao took titles from their sons, received sacrifice in tomb-garden shrines, and were not enshrined with Gaozu or Emperor Wu. In Later Han, Emperor He's mother was styled Empress Gonghuai, Emperor An's grandmother Empress Jingyin, and Emperor Shun's mother Empress Gongmin; even without titles tied to their sons, all were honored at the imperial tombs. None of them shared the temples of Emperors Zhang and An. Thus across both Han dynasties, whatever the difference between empress dowager and empress, none received shared temple sacrifice—a practice in keeping with the Spring and Autumn Annals. Only Emperor Guangwu, having posthumously deposed Empress Lü, installed Empress Bo in Gaozu's temple. When Empress Wei was deposed, Huo Guang posthumously elevated Lady Li to empress and enshrined her with Emperor Wu—a special case, not mother-through-son ennoblement, but because Gaozu's and Wu's temples lacked a consort. Han practice of erecting tomb shrines already differed from Jin ritual. He urged taking the Spring and Autumn's shrine visit as the distant precedent and the two Han dynasties' refusal of shared sacrifice as the immediate model. With the proper honorific, filial devotion finds its fullest expression; a separate tomb shrine upholds solemn ancestral worship; a title drawn from the son shows why the mother was ennobled—three rites satisfied at once is the mark of a sage king. " The court adopted his proposal.
4
頃之，去官。 以母老家貧，與弟熹俱棄人事，躬耕自業，約己養親者十餘載。 父母喪亡，居喪六年，以毀瘠著稱。 服闋，除臨沂令。 義旗建，為太學博士，參右將軍何無忌軍事，隨府轉鎮南參軍。
Soon afterward he resigned his post. With an elderly mother and a poor household, he and his younger brother Xi withdrew from public life, farmed with their own hands, and for more than a decade lived frugally to support their parents. After his parents' deaths he mourned six years and won fame for wasting away in filial grief. When mourning ended, he was named magistrate of Linyi. When Liu Yu raised the righteous banner, he became a National Academy erudite, joined General of the Right He Wuji's staff, and later became Pacification of the South staff officer when the command transferred.
5
高祖鎮京口，與燾書曰：「頃學尚廢弛，後進頹業，衡門之內，清風輟響。 良由戎車屢警，禮樂中息，浮夫恣志，情與事染，豈可不敷崇墳籍，敦厲風尚。 此境人士，子侄如林，明發搜訪，想聞令軌。 然荊玉含寶，要俟開瑩，幽蘭懷馨，事資扇發，獨習寡悟，義著周典。 今經師不遠，而赴業無聞，非唯志學者鮮，或是勸誘未至邪。 想複弘之。 」參高祖中軍軍事，入補尚書度支郎，改掌祠部。 襲封高陵亭侯。
While stationed at Jingkou, the Gaozu wrote to Dao: "Learning has lately fallen into neglect; the younger generation's studies have collapsed, and no clear moral wind stirs behind humble gates." War has sounded the alarm again and again; rites and music have stalled; drifting men indulge every whim as feeling and circumstance entangle them—how can we not exalt the classics and stiffen public morals? This region teems with promising sons and nephews; at daybreak I shall seek them out, eager to hear of their exemplary conduct. Yet jade from Jing hides its luster until cut and polished; orchids in shade keep their fragrance until someone fans it abroad—solitary study rarely enlightens, as the Zhou classics teach. Classic masters are close at hand, yet no one seems to be enrolling—is it only that few wish to study, or that encouragement has not yet reached them? I trust you will carry this forward once more. " He joined the Gaozu's Central Army staff, entered the ministry as Revenue Section clerk, then took charge of the Sacrifices Section. He inherited the title Marquis of Gaoling district.
6
時太廟鴟尾災，燾謂著作郎徐廣曰：「昔孔子在齊，聞魯廟災，曰必桓、僖也。 今征西、京兆四府君，宜在毀落，而猶列廟饗，此其征乎？ 」乃上議曰：「臣聞國之大事，在祀與戎，將營宮室，宗廟為首。 古先哲王，莫不致肅恭之誠心，盡崇嚴乎祖考，然後能流淳化于四海，通幽感於神明。 固宜詳廢興於古典，循情禮以求中者也。 禮，天子七廟，三昭三穆，與太祖而七。 自考廟以至祖考五廟，皆月祭之，遠廟為祧，有二祧，享嘗乃止。 去祧為壇，去壇為墠，有禱然後祭之。 此宗廟之次，親疏之序也。 鄭玄以為祧者文王、武王之廟，王肅以為五世六世之祖。 尋去祧之言，則祧非文、武之廟矣。 文、武周之祖宗，何雲去祧為壇乎？ 明遠廟為祧者，無服之祖也。 又遠廟則有享嘗之禮，去祧則有壇墠之殊，明世遠者，其義彌疏也。 若祧是文、武之廟，宜同月祭于太祖，雖推後稷以配天，由功德之所始，非尊崇之義每有差降也。 又禮有以多貴者，故傳稱德厚者流光，德薄者流卑。 又雲自上以下，降殺以兩，禮也。 此則尊卑等級之典，上下殊異之文。 而雲天子諸侯俱祭五廟，何哉？ 又王祭嫡殤，下及來孫，而上祀之禮，不過高祖。 推隆恩於下流，替誠敬於尊屬，亦非聖人制禮之意也。 是以泰始建廟，從王氏議，以禮父為士，子為天子諸侯，祭以天子諸侯，其屍服以士服。 故上及征西，以備六世之數，宣皇雖為太祖，尚在子孫之位，至於殷祭之日，未申東向之禮，所謂子雖齊聖，不先父食者矣。 今京兆以上既遷，太祖始得居正，議者以昭穆未足，欲屈太祖于卑坐，臣以為非禮典之旨。 所與太祖而七，自是昭穆既足，太祖在六世之外，非為須滿七廟，乃得居太祖也。 議者又以四府君神主宜永同于殷祫，臣又以為不然。 傳所謂毀廟之主，陳乎太祖，謂太祖以下先君之主也。 故《白虎通》雲'禘祫祭遷廟者，以其繼君之體，持其統而不絕也。 」豈如四府君在太祖之前乎。 非繼統之主，無靈命之瑞，非王業之基，昔以世近而及，今則情禮已遠，而當長饗殷祫，永虛太祖之位，求之禮籍，未見其可。 昔永和之初，大議斯禮，于時虞喜、范宣並以淵儒碩學，鹹謂四府君神主，無緣永存於百世。 或欲瘞之兩階，或欲藏之石室，或欲為之改築，雖所秉小異，而大歸是同。 若宣皇既居群廟之上，而四主禘祫不已，則大晉殷祭，長無太祖之位矣。 夫理貴有中，不必過厚； 禮與世遷，豈可順而不斷！ 故臣子之情雖篤，而靈厲之諡彌彰； 追遠之懷雖切，而遷毀之禮為用。 豈不有心于加厚，顧禮制不可逾爾。 石室則藏於廟北，改築則未知所處，虞主所以依神，神移則有瘞埋之禮。 四主若饗祀宜廢，亦神之所不依也，准傍事例，宜同虞主之瘞埋。 然經典難詳，群言紛錯，非臣卑淺所能折中。 」時學者多從燾議，竟未施行。
When fire struck the temple's ridge ornaments, Dao told Master of Writings Xu Guang: "Confucius once heard from Qi that Lu's temple had burned and said it must concern Dukes Huan and Xi." Today the four lords of Zhengxi and Jingzhao ought long ago to have been removed, yet they still receive temple sacrifice—is this not the sign?" " He then memorialized the throne: "I have heard that a state's greatest concerns are sacrifice and war, and that when palaces are planned, the ancestral temple comes first." Sage kings of old devoted reverent hearts and fullest honor to their forebears; only then could pure teaching spread across the realm and the hidden world touch the spirits. One ought to weigh abolition and establishment against the ancient classics and follow feeling and ritual to the mean. Ritual assigns the Son of Heaven seven temples—three zhao, three mu, and the Grand Ancestor, seven in all. From the father temple through five generations to the great-great-grandfather, all receive monthly sacrifice; distant temples become tiao, and once there are two tiao, only seasonal offerings remain. Beyond the tiao stands an altar; beyond the altar an open mound; sacrifice there only when prayer is required. Such is the order of ancestral temples and the gradation of kinship near and far. Zheng Xuan identified the tiao as the temples of Kings Wen and Wu; Wang Su as ancestors of the fifth and sixth generations. On the phrase 'beyond the tiao,' the tiao cannot mean the temples of Wen and Wu. Wen and Wu were Zhou's founding ancestors—how could ritual speak of setting them 'beyond the tiao' at a mere altar? Thus the tiao are distant ancestors beyond the mourning-garment limit. Distant temples still receive seasonal offerings; beyond the tiao lie altar and mound—rites grow sparser as generations recede. Were the tiao Wen and Wu's temples, they would share monthly sacrifice with the Grand Ancestor; Hou Ji was paired with Heaven because merit began with him—not because honor itself admits endless gradation. Ritual also holds that abundance confers honor; tradition says thick virtue casts light far, thin virtue casts it low. It also says honor diminishes by steps of two from above downward—that is ritual. Such is the canon of rank and the rule of distinction between high and low. Yet how can Son of Heaven and feudal lords alike sacrifice at only five temples? A king may sacrifice to legitimate heirs who died young, even remote descendants, yet upward sacrifice stops at the high ancestor. Heaping favor on distant descendants while displacing reverence owed to honored forebears was never the sages' intent in ritual. When temples were founded in the Taishi era, the court followed the Wang proposal: ritually the father ranked as a common officer though the son was emperor; sacrifice used imperial rites, but the impersonator wore a commoner's robe. Sacrifice therefore reached Lord Zhengxi to fill six generations; though Emperor Xuan was Grand Ancestor, he still ranked as a descendant, and on the great seasonal day he received no east-facing rite—'though the son equals a sage, he does not eat before his father.' Now that the Jingzhao line has been removed, the Grand Ancestor can take the central seat; some argue zhao and mu are incomplete and would demote him—your servant finds that contrary to ritual. 'Seven with the Grand Ancestor' means zhao and mu are already sufficient; the Grand Ancestor stands beyond the six generations—not that seven temples must be filled before he takes his place. Others argued the four lords' tablets should forever share the great seasonal sacrifice—your servant again disagreed. When tradition says demolished temples' tablets are set before the Grand Ancestor, it means former lords below him—not above. The Baihu tong explains: 'Great seasonal sacrifice to removed temples honors those who embodied succession and kept the line unbroken.' " How could the four lords rank before the Grand Ancestor? They did not inherit the throne, bore no mandate from Heaven, and laid no foundation for the dynasty; they were honored once because the generation was near, but feeling and ritual have long since receded—yet they would forever share the great sacrifice and leave the Grand Ancestor's seat empty; the ritual books offer no warrant. In early Yonghe the court debated this at length; Yu Xi and Fan Xuan, leading scholars both, agreed the four lords' tablets could not endure for a hundred generations. Some proposed burying them between the steps, others storing them in a stone chamber, others rebuilding shrines—minor differences, but the conclusion was the same. If Emperor Xuan already towers above the other temples while the four lords forever share the great sacrifice, Jin's seasonal rites would forever lack a seat for the Grand Ancestor. Ritual values the mean and need not run to excess; ritual shifts with the times—how can one follow custom without also knowing when to end it? Thus though a subject's devotion may run deep, posthumous titles of spiritual austerity grow all the clearer; though longing for the distant dead may be keen, the rites of transfer and demolition must prevail. One may wish to heap further honor, yet ritual cannot be overstepped. A stone chamber would lie north of the temple; rebuilding would leave their site uncertain; the temporary tablet lodges the spirit, and when the spirit departs, burial rites apply. If sacrifice to the four lords should cease, their spirits have no further dwelling; by precedent they should be buried like temporary tablets. Yet the classics are obscure and opinions tangle—more than your servant's shallow learning can reconcile. " Many scholars then sided with Dao, but in the end his proposal was never enacted.
7
遷通直郎，高祖鎮軍、車騎、中軍、太尉諮議參軍。 高祖北伐關、洛，大司馬琅邪王同行，除大司馬從事中郎，總留府事。 義熙十四年，除侍中。 元熙元年，以腳疾去職。 高祖受命，征拜太常，雖外戚貴顯，而彌自沖約，茅屋蔬餐，不改其舊。 所得奉祿，與親戚共之。 永初三年，致仕，拜光祿大夫，加金章紫綬。 其年卒，時年七十。 少帝追贈左光祿大夫，加散騎常侍。
Promoted to Palace Attendant of Direct Communication, he served the Gaozu as advisory staff officer under the Pacifying Army, Chariots and Cavalry, Central Army, and Grand Marshal commands. On the Gaozu's northern campaign toward Guan and Luo, the Prince of Langye marched as Grand Marshal; Dao was named attendant gentleman of the Grand Marshal and directed all affairs left behind at headquarters. In the fourteenth year of Yixi he was appointed Palace Attendant. In the first year of Yuanxi he resigned because of a foot ailment. When the Gaozu took the throne he was summoned as Minister of Ceremonies; though now a powerful imperial in-law, he grew only more modest, living still in his thatched hut on plain vegetables. He shared his salary with his kinsmen. In the third year of Yongchu he retired, receiving the title Grand Master of Splendid Happiness with golden seal and purple cord. He died the same year, at seventy. The Deposed Emperor posthumously honored him as Left Grand Master of Splendid Happiness with the additional title Regular Attendant of the Scattered Cavalry.
8
長子邃，護軍司馬，宜都太守。 少子綽，太子中舍人，新安太守。 邃長子諶之，尚書都官郎，烏程令。 諶之弟凝之，學涉有當世才具，與司空徐湛之為異常之交。 年少時與北地傅僧祐俱以通家子始為太祖所引見，時上與何尚之論鑄錢事，凝之便幹其語，上因回與論之。 僧祐引凝之衣令止，凝之大言謂僧祐曰：「明主難再遇，便應正盡所懷。 」上與往復十餘反，凝之詞韻銓序，兼有理證，上甚賞焉。 曆隨王誕後軍記室錄事，欲以為青州，其事不果。 遷尚書右丞，以徐湛之党，為元兇所殺。 子夤，尚書主客郎，沈攸之征西功曹，為攸之盡節，事在《攸之傳》。 凝之弟潭之，亦有美譽。 太宗世，曆尚書吏部郎，禦史中丞。 後廢帝元徽中，為左民尚書，卒官。 潭之弟澄之，太子左積弩將軍。 元嘉二十七年，領軍於盱眙，為索虜所破，見殺，追贈通直郎。 綽子煥，順帝升明中，為武昌太守。 沈攸之攻郢城，煥棄郡赴之； 攸之敗，伏誅。
His eldest son Sui served as staff officer of the Protector Army and governor of Yidu. His younger son Chuo was attendant in the Crown Prince's household and governor of Xin'an. Sui's eldest son Chenzhi served as a clerk in the Ministry of Works' Punishments Section and as magistrate of Wucheng. Chenzhi's younger brother Ningzhi was widely learned and gifted for his generation; he formed an unusually close bond with Minister of Works Xu Tanzhi. In youth he and Fu Sengyou of Beidi, as sons of allied families, were first presented to the Gaozu; while the emperor debated coinage with He Shangzhi, Ningzhi broke in, and the emperor turned to argue with him. Sengyou tugged his robe to silence him; Ningzhi said loudly: "A wise ruler is rarely met twice—we should speak our minds plainly now." " They exchanged more than ten rounds; Ningzhi spoke with measured eloquence and sound argument, and the emperor was deeply impressed. He served as recorder on Prince Dan of Sui's Rear Army staff; a plan to appoint him governor of Qingzhou came to nothing. Promoted to Right Assistant Director of the Ministry of Works, he was killed as a partisan of Xu Tanzhi in the prince's revolt. His son Yin served as Guests Section clerk and Pacification of the West staff officer under Shen Youzhi, dying in loyal service to him, as told in Youzhi's biography. Ningzhi's younger brother Tanzhi likewise enjoyed a fine reputation. Under Emperor Taizong he served as director of the Personnel Section and as Imperial Censor. Under the Later Deposed Emperor, in the Yuanhui era, he served as Minister of the Left for the People and died in office. Tanzhi's younger brother Chengzhi was Left Commander of Stacked Crossbows in the Crown Prince's guard. In the twenty-seventh year of Yuanjia he commanded at Xuyi, was routed by the northern enemy, and killed; posthumously he received the title Palace Attendant of Direct Communication. Chuo's son Huan served as governor of Wuchang in the Shengming era of Emperor Shun. When Shen Youzhi besieged Yingcheng, Huan abandoned his post and rushed to join him; when Youzhi fell, he was executed.
9
傅僧祐，祖父弘仁，高祖外弟也。 以中表曆顯官，征虜將軍、南譙太守，太常卿。 子邵，員外散騎侍郎，妻燾女也，生僧祐，有吏才，再為山陰令，甚有能名，末世令長莫及。 亦以徐湛之党，為元兇所殺。
Fu Sengyou's grandfather Hongren was a maternal younger brother of the Gaozu. Through ties of maternal kinship he rose to high office, serving as General Who Pacifies the Barbarians, governor of Nanqiao, and Minister of Ceremonies. His son Shao was an attendant gentleman of the Scattered Cavalry; he married Dao's daughter and fathered Sengyou, a capable administrator who twice governed Shanyin and won such a reputation that no magistrate of the late dynasty could rival him. He too was killed as a partisan of Xu Tanzhi in the prince's revolt.
10
徐廣，字野民，東莞姑幕人也。 父藻，都水使者。 兄邈，太子前衛率。 家世好學，至廣尤精，百家數術，無不研覽。 謝玄為州，辟廣從事西曹。 又譙王司馬恬鎮北參軍。 晉孝武帝以廣博學，除為秘書郎，校書秘閣，增置職僚。 轉員外散騎侍郎，領校書如故。 隆安中，尚書令王珣舉為祠部郎。
Xu Guang, courtesy name Yemin, came from Gumu in Dongguan. His father Zao served as Commissioner of Waterways. His elder brother Miao commanded the Crown Prince's Forward Guard. His family had long prized learning; Guang surpassed them all, mastering every school of thought and art of calculation the classics record. When Xie Xuan governed the province, he recruited Guang as Western Bureau staff officer. He also served Prince Qiao, Sima Tian, as Pacification of the North staff officer. Emperor Xiaowu of Jin, impressed by Guang's erudition, named him Secretary Gentleman to collate texts in the Secret Archive and expanded the staff. Promoted to attendant gentleman of the Scattered Cavalry, he continued to oversee collation as before. During Longan, Minister of Works Wang Xun recommended him as Sacrifices Section clerk.
11
時會稽王世子元顯錄尚書，欲使百僚致敬，台內使廣立議，由是內外並執下官禮，廣常為愧恨焉。 元顯引為中軍參軍，遷領軍長史。 桓玄輔政，以為大將軍文學祭酒。
When Kuaiji's heir Yuan Xian took charge of the ministry and demanded homage from all officials, Guang was ordered to draft the protocol; court and provinces alike then observed inferior-to-superior rites—a source of lasting shame and resentment for Guang. Yuan Xian recruited him as Central Army staff officer, then made him chief clerk of the Commandant Army. Under Huan Xuan's regency he became libationer of letters on the Grand Marshal's staff.
12
義熙初，高祖使撰車服儀注，乃除鎮軍諮議參軍，領記室。 封樂成縣五等侯。 轉員外散騎常侍，領著作郎。 二年，尚書奏曰：「臣聞左史述言，右官書事，《乘》、《志》顯于晉、鄭，《陽秋》著乎魯史。 自皇代有造，中興晉祀，道風帝典，煥乎史策。 而太和以降，世曆三朝，玄風聖跡，倏為疇古。 臣等參詳，宜敕著作郎徐廣撰成國史。 」詔曰：「先朝至德光被，未著方策，宜流風緬代，永貽將來者也。 便敕撰集。」
In early Yixi the Gaozu commissioned him to draft regulations for carriages, dress, and court ceremony, naming him advisory staff officer of the Pacifying Army and head of the secretariat. He received the fifth-rank marquisate of Lecheng district. Promoted to regular attendant of the Scattered Cavalry, he headed the Bureau of Writings. In the second year the ministry memorialized: "We have heard that the left historiographer records speech and the right officer records events; the Cheng and Zhi made their mark in Jin and Zheng, and the Spring and Autumn Annals were written in Lu's chronicle." Since the dynasty was founded and Jin ritual was restored, the Way, imperial custom, and sage precedent have shone bright in the annals. Yet from the Taihe reign three dynasties have passed, and the subtle ethos and traces of sages have already turned to antiquity. We recommend that Master of Writings Xu Guang be ordered to compile the national history. " The edict replied: "The former court's supreme virtue shines abroad yet remains unwritten; its influence should flow down the ages and be left as a lasting gift to posterity." Begin the compilation at once."
13
六年，遷散騎常侍，又領徐州大中正，轉正員常侍。 時有風雹為災，廣獻書高祖曰：「風雹變未必為災，古之聖賢輒懼而修己，所以興政化而隆德教也。 嘗忝服事，宿眷未忘，思竭塵露，率誠于習。 明公初建義旗，匡複宗社，神武應運，信宿平夷。 且恭儉謙約，虛心匪懈，來蘇之化，功用若神。 頃事故既多，刑德並用，戰功殷積，報敘難盡，萬機繁湊，固應難速，且小細煩密，群下多懼。 又谷帛豐賤，而民情不勸； 禁司互設，而劫盜多有，誠由俗弊未易整，而望深未易炳。 追思義熙之始，如有不同，何者？ 好安願逸，萬物之大趣，習舊駭新，凡識所不免。 要當俯順群情，抑揚隨俗，則朝野歡泰，具瞻允康矣。 言無可采，願矜其愚款之志。 」又轉大司農，領著作郎皆如故。 十二年，《晉紀》成，凡四十六卷，表上之。 遷秘書監。
In the sixth year he became regular attendant of the Scattered Cavalry, also served as great impartial judge of Xuzhou, and then took a regular roster post as attendant. When wind and hail brought disaster, Guang wrote the Gaozu: "Such omens need not spell calamity; sage kings of old took them as warnings to refine themselves—thereby advancing government and elevating moral teaching." I once had the honor to serve you and have not forgotten that old favor; I offer what little strength I have in sincere counsel drawn from long habit. When you first raised the righteous banner and restored the altars of state, your divine martial prowess answered Heaven's mandate, and within a day and night the realm was pacified. Moreover you are respectful, frugal, and modest, never slack in self-cultivation; the renewal you bring works almost like a divine gift. Lately affairs have multiplied; punishment and grace are applied together; battle honors pile up faster than rewards can be granted; the myriad tasks are dense—it is naturally hard to move quickly, and petty regulations leave many below in fear. Grain and cloth are plentiful and cheap, yet the people are not stirred to industry; prohibitory offices multiply yet banditry remains common—truly because deep-rooted custom is hard to reform and high expectations are hard to make clear at once. Looking back to the start of Yixi, if things differ now, wherein lies the change? Love of ease is the bent of all living things; shock at the new while clinging to the old is what no mind entirely escapes. You must bend to popular feeling and moderate policy to custom; then court and countryside will rejoice and all will find true peace. My words may be unworthy of adoption; I beg you to pity my sincere but foolish intent." " He was made Minister of the Grand Granary while continuing to head the Bureau of Writings. In the twelfth year he completed the Jin Annals in forty-six scrolls and presented them to the throne. He was promoted to Director of the Secretariat.
14
初，桓玄篡位，安帝出宮，廣陪列悲慟，哀動左右。 及高祖受禪，恭帝遜位，廣又哀感，涕泗交流。 謝晦見之，謂之曰：「徐公將無小過？ 」廣收淚答曰：「身與君不同。 君佐命興王，逢千載嘉運； 身世荷晉德，實眷戀故主。 」因更歔欷。
When Huan Xuan usurped the throne and Emperor An was driven from the palace, Guang wept among the attendants until all beside him were moved. When the Gaozu received the abdication and Emperor Gong yielded the throne, Guang wept again until tears and mucus streamed down his face. Xie Hui saw this and said, "Master Xu, are you not carrying this a bit too far?" " Guang wiped away his tears and replied, "You and I are not in the same position." You aided the Mandate and helped raise a new dynasty, meeting a once-in-a-thousand-years fortune; my life has received Jin's grace, and I truly still cherish my former lord." " He sobbed again.
15
傅隆，字伯祚，北地靈州人也。 高祖咸，晉司隸校尉。 曾祖晞，司徒屬。 父祖早亡。 隆少孤，又無近屬，單貧有學行，不好交遊。 義熙初，年四十，始為孟昶建威將軍，員外散騎侍郎。 坐辭兼，免。 複為會稽征虜參軍。 家在上虞，及東歸，便有終焉之志。 曆佐三軍，首尾八年。 除給事中。 尚書僕射、丹陽尹徐羨之置建威府，以為錄事參軍，尋轉尚書祠部郎、丹陽丞，入為尚書左丞。 以族弟亮為僕射，緦服不得相臨，徙太子率更令，廬陵王義真車騎諮議參軍，出補山陰令。 太祖元嘉初，除司徒右長史，遷禦史中丞。 當官而行，甚得司直之體。 轉司徒左長史。
Fu Long, courtesy name Bozuo, came from Lingzhou in Beidi. His great-grandfather Xian served as Metropolitan Commandant under Jin. His great-grandfather Xi was an aide in the Ministry of Education. His father and grandfather died while he was young. Orphaned young and without close kin, Long was poor but learned and upright, and cared little for society. In early Yixi, at forty, he first entered service under Meng Chang as attendant gentleman of the Scattered Cavalry on the Pacifying Army staff. Dismissed for declining a concurrent post. He again served as Pacification of the Barbarians staff officer in Kuaiji. His family lived in Shangyu; on returning east he resolved to end his days there. He served on three armies' staffs over eight years in all. He was appointed Supervising Censor. Vice Minister Xu Xianzhi, also governor of Danyang, made him recorder on the Pacifying Army staff; he soon became Sacrifices Section clerk and Danyang assistant magistrate, then Left Assistant Director of the ministry. When his kinsman Liang became vice minister, mourning protocol barred them from serving together; Long was transferred to the Crown Prince's Astronomy Office, then to Prince Yizhen of Luling's staff, and finally went out as magistrate of Shanyin. In early Yuanjia under Emperor Taizu he became Right Chief Clerk of the Ministry of Education, then Imperial Censor. He enforced the law as his office required and fully embodied impartial integrity. He was transferred to Left Chief Clerk of the Ministry of Education.
16
時會稽剡縣民黃初妻趙打息載妻王死亡，遇赦，王有父母及息男稱、息女葉，依法徙趙二千裏外。 隆議之曰：「原夫禮律之興，蓋本之自然，求之情理，非從天墮，非從地出也。 父子至親，分形同氣，稱之於載，即載之于趙，雖雲三世，為體猶一，未有能分之者也。 稱雖創巨痛深，固無仇祖之義。 若稱可以殺趙，趙當何以處載？ 將父子孫祖，互相殘戮，懼非先王明罰，咎繇立法之本旨也。 向使石厚之子、日磾之孫，砥鋒挺鍔，不與二祖同戴天日，則石碏、秺侯何得流名百代，以為美談者哉！ 舊令雲，'殺人父母，徙之二千裏外'。 不施父子孫祖明矣。 趙當避王期功千里外耳。 令亦雲，'凡流徙者，同籍親近欲相隨者，聽之'。 此又大通情體，因親以教愛者也。 趙既流移，載為人子，何得不從； 載從而稱不行，豈名教所許？ 如此，稱、趙竟不可分。 趙雖內愧終身，稱當沈痛沒齒，孫祖之義，自不得永絕，事理固然也。 」從之。
In Yan district of Kuaiji, Huang Chu's wife Zhao beat to death the wife Wang of her stepson Zai; though an amnesty followed, Wang's parents and her children Cheng and Ye remained; by law Zhao was to be exiled two thousand li. Long ruled on the case: "Ritual and law arise from what is natural and are grounded in reason—they did not fall from Heaven or spring from Earth." Father and son are the closest kin, sharing flesh and breath; Cheng stands to Zai as Zai stands to Zhao—though three generations are named, they are one body and cannot be split. Though Cheng's wound is grievous, there is no principle by which he may avenge a grandmother. If Cheng may kill Zhao, what may Zhao do to Zai? Father, son, grandson, and grandmother slaughtering one another—this cannot be what the sage kings intended in clarifying punishments or what Gao Yao intended in framing law. Had the son of Shi Hou and the grandson of Rizhu sharpened blades and refused to live under the same Heaven as their forebears, how could Shi Que and the Marquis of Di be praised for a hundred generations! The old ordinance says: 'Whoever kills another's parents is exiled two thousand li.' That it does not apply across father, son, grandson, and grandmother is clear. Zhao need only keep a thousand li from Wang's parents and close kin in mourning. The ordinance also says: 'When anyone is exiled, close kin of the same registry who wish to accompany him may do so.' This too accords with human feeling, teaching love through kinship. Zhao is exiled; as her son, how can Zai fail to follow; if Zai follows but Cheng does not, can ritual teaching permit it? Thus Cheng and Zhao cannot in the end be separated. Zhao may bear lifelong shame, and Cheng lifelong grief, but the bond of grandson and grandmother cannot be severed forever—that is the logic of the case." " The court adopted his ruling.
17
又出為義興太守，在郡有能名。 征拜左民尚書，坐正直受節假，對人未至，委出，白衣領職。 尋轉太常。 十四年，太祖以新撰《禮論》付隆使下意，隆上表曰：「臣以下愚，不涉師訓，孤陋閭閻，面牆靡識，謬蒙詢逮，愧懼流汗。 原夫禮者，三千之本，人倫之至道。 故用之家國，君臣以之尊，父子以之親； 用之婚冠，少長以之仁愛，夫妻以之義順； 用之鄉人，友朋以之三益，賓主以之敬讓。 所謂極乎天，播乎地，窮高遠，測深厚，莫尚於禮也。 其樂之五聲，《易》之八象，《詩》之《風雅》，《書》之《典誥》，《春秋》之微婉勸懲，無不本乎禮而後立也。 其源遠，其流廣，其體大，其義精，非夫睿哲大賢，孰能明乎此哉。 況遭暴秦焚亡，百不存一。 漢興，始徵召故老，搜集殘文，其體例紕繆，首尾脫落，難可詳論。 幸高堂生頗識舊義，諸儒各為章句之說，既明不獨達，所見不同，或師資相傳，共枝別幹。 故聞人、二戴，俱事後蒼，俄已分異； 盧植、鄭玄，偕學馬融，人各名象。 又後之學者，未逮曩時，而問難星繁，充斥兼兩，摛文列錦，煥爛可觀。 然而五服之本或差，哀敬之制舛雜，國典未一于四海，家法參駁於縉紳，誠宜考詳遠慮，以定皇代之盛禮者也。 伏惟陛下欽明玄聖，同規唐、虞，疇咨四嶽，興言《三禮》，而伯夷未登，微臣竊位，所以大懼負乘，形神交惡者，無忘夙夜矣。 而複猥充搏采之數，與聞爰發之求，實無以仰酬聖旨萬分之一。 不敢廢默，謹率管穴所見五十二事上呈。 蚩鄙茫浪，伏用竦赧。」
He went out again as governor of Yixing and won a reputation for capable administration. Summoned as Minister of the Left for the People, he was demoted to plain-robed service for leaving his post during approved leave before an expected visitor arrived. Soon he became Minister of Ceremonies. In the fourteenth year the Taizu asked Long to review the newly compiled Treatise on Rites; Long memorialized: "I am low and unlearned, without teachers' guidance, crude and isolated, ignorant as one facing a wall—yet I have been questioned and sweat with shame and fear." Ritual is the root of the three thousand norms and the highest Way of human relations. In state and family, lord and minister gain honor through it, father and son gain closeness through it; in marriage and capping, young and old gain affection through it, husband and wife gain righteous harmony through it; among neighbors, friends gain the three benefits through it, guest and host gain respect and yielding through it. Reaching to Heaven, spreading over Earth, exhausting height and distance, sounding depth and thickness—nothing surpasses ritual. Music's five tones, the Changes' eight images, the Odes' Airs and Eulogies, the Documents' Canons and Charges, the Spring and Autumn's subtle warnings—all rest on ritual before they can stand. Its source is distant, its flow broad, its body vast, its meaning refined—who but a sage of penetrating wisdom can fully grasp it? Then came violent Qin's burning, and scarcely one part in a hundred survived. When Han arose, old scholars were summoned and remnant texts gathered, but categories were tangled and beginnings and ends lost—hard to discuss in full. Fortunately Gaotang knew the old meanings fairly well; each scholar wrote chapter-and-verse glosses—understanding was not uniform, views diverged, and schools split like branches from one trunk. Wenren and the two Dai all studied under Hou Cang, yet soon diverged; Lu Zhi and Zheng Xuan both studied under Ma Rong, each with his own terminology. Later scholars did not match the ancients, yet disputations multiplied, pages overflowed, and prose spread like brocade—brilliant to behold. Yet the roots of mourning garments sometimes differ, grief and respect are inconsistently regulated, the national canon is not unified, and family law conflicts among the gentry—it is truly time to examine all with far-reaching care and fix the imperial age's full ritual canon. Your Majesty is reverent, luminous, and sage, matching Tang and Yu, consulting the four peaks, and raising the Three Rites—yet Bo Yi has not taken his post while I hold office unworthily; fearing the overloaded cart, body and spirit alike distressed, I dare not forget this day or night. Yet I am unworthily counted among those who gather materials and heard the inquiry at its start—I truly cannot repay your command even one part in ten thousand." I dare not remain silent and respectfully submit fifty-two observations from my narrow learning. Ignorant and crude, I prostrate myself in fear and shame."
18
明年，致仕，拜光祿大夫。 歸老在家，手不釋卷，博學多通，特精《三禮》。 謹于奉公，常手抄書籍。 二十八年，卒，時年八十三。
The following year he retired and received the title Grand Master of Splendid Happiness. In retirement he never put down his books; erudite and widely learned, he was especially master of the Three Rites. Diligent in public service, he often copied books by hand. In the twenty-eighth year he died, at eighty-three.
19
史臣曰：選賢於野，則治身業弘； 求士子朝，則飾智風起。 《六經》奧遠，方軌之正路； 百家淺末，捷至之偏道。 漢世登士，閭黨為先，崇本務學，不尚浮詭，然後可以俯拾青組，顧蔑籝金。 於是人厲從師之志，家競專門之術，藝重當時，所居一旦成市，黌舍暫啟，著錄或至萬人。 是故仕以學成，身由義立。 自魏氏膺命，主愛雕蟲，家棄章句，人重異術。 又選賢進士，不本鄉閭，銓衡之寄，任歸台閣。 以一人之耳目，究山川之險情，賢否臆斷，萬不值一。 由是仕憑藉譽，學非為己，崇詭遇之巧速，鄙稅駕之遲難，士自此委笥植《經》，各從所務，早往晏退，以取世資。 庠序黌校之士，傳經聚徒之業，自黃初至於晉末，百餘年中，儒教盡矣。 高祖受命，議創國學，宮車早晏，道未及行。 迄於元嘉，甫獲克就，雅風盛烈，未及曩時，而濟濟焉，頗有前王之遺典。 天子鸞旗警蹕，清道而臨學館，儲後冕旒黼黻，北面而禮先師，後生所不嘗聞，黃發未之前睹，亦一代之盛也。 臧燾、徐廣、傅隆、裴松之、何承天、雷次宗，並服膺聖哲，不為雅俗推移，立名於世，宜矣。 潁川庾蔚之、雁門周野王、汝南周王子、河內向琰、會稽賀道養，皆托志經書，見稱於後學。 蔚之略解《禮記》，並注賀循《喪服》，行於世雲。
The historiographer remarks: When worthies are chosen in the countryside, self-cultivation flourishes; when scholars are sought at court, the fashion of ornamented cleverness rises. The Six Classics are profound and far-reaching—the true highway; the hundred schools shallow and marginal—a shortcut's side path. Under Han, office began in the village community; men honored fundamentals and devoted themselves to study, not empty cleverness—only then could they gain office and scorn mere wealth. Men sharpened their will to follow teachers; families competed in specialized arts; craft was prized—where a master lived became a market overnight, and when the schoolhouse opened, enrollment might reach ten thousand. Thus office came through completed learning, and the person through established righteousness. Once Wei received the Mandate, rulers loved ornamental prose, families abandoned classical glosses, and men prized strange arts. Choosing worthies and advancing scholars no longer began in the village; weighing and selection rested with the central ministries. With one man's eyes and ears to judge every corner of the realm, worth and unworth were guessed—hardly one judgment in ten thousand was right. Office then relied on borrowed reputation; learning was not for oneself; men prized clever chance and despised slow thoroughness; scholars shelved their classics, each pursued his trade, hurried out and home late, and sought worldly gain. School scholars and the work of transmitting classics and gathering disciples—from Huangchu to Jin's end, more than a century—Confucian teaching was spent. When the Gaozu took the throne, a National Academy was proposed, but he died early and the plan was never carried out. By Yuanjia it was at last achieved; elegant learning flourished, though short of former ages, yet in abundance—with much of the former kings' ritual legacy restored. The emperor came to the academy with banners flying and roads cleared; the heir in full regalia faced north to honor the former master—unheard by the young, unseen by the old: a splendor of the age. Zang Dao, Xu Guang, Fu Long, Pei Songzhi, He Chengtian, and Lei Cizong all embraced the sages and were not swayed by fashion; that they won lasting names was only fitting. Yu Weizhi of Yingchuan, Zhou Yewang of Yanmen, Zhou Wangzi of Runan, Xiang Yan of Henei, and He Daoyang of Kuaiji all devoted themselves to the classics and won praise from later scholars. Weizhi had a partial grasp of the Record of Rites and annotated He Xun's Mourning Garments, both of which circulated widely.
