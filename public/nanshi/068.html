2
趙知禮蔡景曆宗元饒韓子高華皎劉師知謝岐毛喜沈君理陸山才
Zhao Zhili, Cai Jingli, Zong Yuanrao, Han Zigao, Hua Jiao, Liu Shizhi, Xie Qi, Mao Xi, Shen Junli, and Lu Shancai
3
趙知禮字齊旦，天水隴西人也。 父孝穆，梁候官令。 知禮涉獵文史，善書翰。 陳武帝之討元景仲也，或薦之，引為書記。 知禮為文贍速，每占授軍書，下筆便就，率皆稱旨。 由是恒侍左右，深被委任，當時計畫，莫不預焉。 武帝征侯景，至白茅灣，上表于梁元帝及與王僧辯論軍事，其文並知禮所制。 及景平，授中書侍郎，封始平縣子。 陳受命，位散騎常侍、太府卿，權知領軍事。
Zhao Zhili, whose courtesy name was Qidan, came from Longxi in Tianshui. His father Xiaomu had served as magistrate of Houguan under the Liang. Zhili read widely in literature and history and was accomplished at writing and calligraphy. When Chen Wu-di was campaigning against Yuan Jingzhong, someone recommended Zhili, and he was taken on as a secretary. Zhili wrote with fluency and speed; whenever he was told to draft military dispatches, he would finish at a stroke, and his work invariably pleased his superiors. He was henceforth always at the emperor's side, deeply trusted, and involved in every major plan of the day. When the emperor marched against Hou Jing and reached Baimao Bay, the memorial to Emperor Yuan of Liang and the exchanges on military affairs with Wang Senbian were both written by Zhili. After Hou Jing was defeated, he was made Attendant-in-Ordinary of the Secretariat and enfeoffed as Viscount of Shiping. When the Chen dynasty was founded, he served as Regular Attendant of Scattered Cavalry and Grand Steward, with provisional charge of military administration.
4
天嘉元年，進爵為伯。 王琳平，授吳州刺史。 知禮沈靜有謀謨，每軍國大事，文帝輒令璽書問之。 再遷右將軍，領前軍將軍。 卒，贈侍中，諡曰忠。 子元恭嗣。
In the first year of Tiāncái (560), he was promoted to baron. After Wang Lin was defeated, he was appointed governor of Wu prefecture. Zhili was reserved and thoughtful; on every major military or political matter, Emperor Wen would consult him by sealed edict. He was later promoted to General of the Right and concurrently commanded the vanguard. When he died, he was posthumously made Attendant-in-Ordinary and given the posthumous name Loyal. His son Yuangong succeeded him.
5
蔡景曆字茂世，濟陽考城人也。 祖點，梁尚書左戶侍郎。 父大同，輕車岳陽王記室參軍。 景曆少俊爽，有孝行，家貧好學，善尺牘，工草隸。 為海陽令，政有能名。 在侯景中，與南康嗣王會理通，謀匡復，事泄被執，賊党王偉保護之，獲免，因客遊京口。
Cai Jingli, courtesy name Maoshi, was from Kaocheng in Jiyang. His grandfather Dian had been Attendant-in-Ordinary of the Left Household under the Liang. His father Datong had served as recorder on the staff of the Prince of Yueyang, Light Chariots. As a youth Jingli was bright and spirited, known for filial piety; though his family was poor he loved learning, wrote excellent letters, and was accomplished in cursive and clerical calligraphy. As magistrate of Haiyang he earned a reputation for capable government. During Hou Jing's rebellion he was in contact with Huili, heir of Nankang, and plotted to restore the dynasty; when the plot was exposed he was arrested, but the rebel Wang Wei protected him and he escaped execution, afterward living as a guest in Jingkou.
6
侯景平，陳武帝鎮朱方，素聞其名，以書要之。 景曆對使人答書，筆不停綴，文無所改。 帝得書，甚加欽賞，即日授征北府中記室參軍，仍領記室。
After Hou Jing was defeated, Chen Wu-di, then stationed at Zhufang, had long heard of him and invited him by letter. Jingli composed his reply to the envoy without pausing his brush, and not a word needed changing. The emperor was deeply impressed and that same day appointed him army recorder in the northern campaign headquarters, with continuing charge of the secretariat.
7
衡陽獻王昌為吳興太守，帝以鄉里父老，尊卑有數，恐昌年少接對乖禮，乃遣景曆輔之。 承聖中，還掌記室。 武帝將討王僧辯，獨與侯安都等數人謀之，景曆弗之知。 部分既畢，召令草檄，景曆援筆立成，辭義感激，事皆稱旨。 及受禪，遷秘書監、中書通事舍人，掌詔誥。
When Prince Xian of Hengyang, Chang, became governor of Wuxing, the emperor feared that the young prince would mishandle protocol among the local gentry and sent Jingli to advise him. During the Chengsheng reign he again headed the recorder's office. When Emperor Wu prepared to move against Wang Senbian, he confided only in Hou Andu and a few others; Jingli was not told. Once the dispositions were set, he was called on to draft the proclamation; Jingli wrote it at a stroke in stirring language that fully satisfied the emperor. After the abdication he was made Director of the Secretariat and Attending Clerk for Comprehensive Affairs, with charge of imperial edicts.
8
永定二年，坐妻弟受周寶安餉馬，為御史中丞沈炯所劾，降為中書侍郎，舍人如故。
In the second year of Yongding (558), his wife's younger brother was found to have accepted horses from Zhou Bao'an; impeached by Censor-in-Chief Shen Jiong, Jingli was demoted to Attendant-in-Ordinary of the Secretariat but kept his clerk's duties.
9
三年，武帝崩。 時外有強寇，文帝鎮南皖，朝無重臣，宣後呼景曆及江大權、杜棱定議，秘不發喪，疾召文帝。 景曆躬共宦者及內人密營斂服，時既暑熱，須營梓宮，恐斤斧之聲聞外，乃以蠟為秘器，文詔依舊宣行。
In the third year the emperor died. Powerful enemies threatened from without, Emperor Wen was posted at Southern Wan, and no senior minister remained at court; Empress Xuan called in Jingli, Jiang Daquan, and Du Leng, concealed the death, and urgently summoned Wen. Jingli worked with eunuchs and palace women to prepare the burial privately; because summer heat required a wooden coffin whose hammering might be heard outside, they used a wax inner coffin while edicts continued to issue as usual.
10
華皎反，以景曆為武勝將軍、吳明徹軍司。 皎平，明徹於軍中輒戮安成內史楊文通，又受降人馬仗有不分明，景曆又坐不能匡正被收。 久之獲宥。
When Hua Jiao rebelled, Jingli was appointed General Who Establishes Martial Victory and staff officer to Wu Mingche. After Jiao's defeat, Mingche summarily executed Yang Wentong, administrator of Ancheng, in camp and took horses and arms from surrendering troops in questionable fashion; Jingli was arrested for failing to restrain him. After some time he was pardoned.
11
宣帝即位，累遷通直散騎常侍、中書通事舍人，掌詔誥，仍復封邑。
Under Emperor Xuan he rose to Regular Attendant of Scattered Cavalry, Unimpeded Service, and Attending Clerk, again directing edicts, and his fief was restored.
12
太建五年，都督吳明徹北侵，所向克捷，大破周梁士彥于呂梁，方進圍彭城。 時宣帝銳意河南，以為指麾可定，景曆稱師老將驕，不宜過窮遠略。 帝惡其沮眾，大怒，猶以朝廷舊臣，不加深罪，出為豫章內史。 未行，為飛章所劾，以在省之日，贓汙狼籍，帝令有司案問，景曆但承其半。 於是御史中丞宗元饒奏免景曆所居官，徙居會稽。
In the fifth year of Taijian (573), Wu Mingche marched north as supreme commander, winning every engagement and crushing Zhou general Liang Shiyan at Lüliang before moving to besiege Pengcheng. Emperor Xuan was bent on recovering the south bank of the Yellow River and believed victory was at hand; Jingli warned that the army was tired and the generals overconfident, and that the campaign should not be pushed too far. The emperor, angered at this discouragement of the troops, refrained from harsher punishment only because Jingli was a longtime minister and instead sent him out as administrator of Yuzhang. Before he could leave, an urgent memorial accused him of widespread corruption during his time at court; ordered to answer the charges, Jingli admitted only half. Censor-in-Chief Zong Yuanrao then memorialized for his dismissal and exile to Kuaiji.
13
及吳明徹敗，帝追憶景曆前言，即日追還，以為征南鄱陽王諮議。 數日，遷員外散騎常侍，兼御史中丞，復本爵封，入守度支尚書。 舊式拜官在午後，景曆拜日，適逢輿駕幸玄武觀，在位皆侍宴，帝恐景曆不預，特令早拜，其見重如此。
When Wu Mingche was defeated, the emperor remembered Jingli's warning and recalled him the same day as adviser to the Prince of Poyang on the southern campaign. Within days he was made Extraordinary Attendant of Scattered Cavalry and concurrent Censor-in-Chief, with his rank and fief restored, and appointed Minister of Revenue. Investiture normally took place in the afternoon, but on Jingli's appointment day the emperor was feasting at the Xuanwu Observatory with the whole court; fearing Jingli would miss the ceremony, he ordered an early investiture—such was the regard in which he was held.
14
卒官，贈太常卿，諡曰敬。 十三年，改葬，重贈中領軍。 禎明元年，配享武帝廟庭。 二年，車駕親幸其宅，重贈景曆侍中、中撫將軍，諡曰忠敬，給鼓吹一部，於墓所立碑。
He died in office and was posthumously made Minister of Ceremonies with the posthumous name Reverent. In the thirteenth year, at his reburial, he was posthumously made General of the Central Army. In the first year of Zhenming (587) he was granted a place in sacrifices at Emperor Wu's temple. The next year the emperor visited his home in person and posthumously made him Attendant-in-Ordinary and General of the Central Pacification Army, with the posthumous name Loyal and Reverent, granted martial music, and ordered a stele at his tomb.
15
景曆屬文，不尚雕靡，而長於敘事，應機敏速，為當時所稱。 有文集三十卷。 子征嗣。
His prose avoided ornament and excelled in narrative; quick and apt in every situation, he was widely admired. His collected works ran to thirty juan. His son Zheng succeeded him.
16
江大權字伯謀，濟陽考城人，位少府，封四會縣伯。 太建二年，卒於通直散騎常侍。
Jiang Daquan, courtesy name Bomou, was from Kaocheng in Jiyang; he served as Minister of the Palace Storehouses and was enfeoffed as Baron of Sihui. He died in the second year of Taijian (570) while serving as Regular Attendant of Scattered Cavalry, Unimpeded Service.
17
征字希祥，幼聰敏，精識強記。 年六歲，詣梁吏部尚書河南褚翔，嗟其穎悟。 七歲丁母憂，居喪如成人禮。 繼母劉氏，性悍忌，視之不以道，征供侍益謹，初無怨色。 征本名覽，景曆以其有王祥之性，更名字焉。
Zheng, courtesy name Xixiang, was clever as a child, with a sharp mind and remarkable memory. At six he visited Chu Xiang of Henan, Liang Minister of Personnel, who marveled at his precocity. At seven, when his mother died, he observed mourning with the gravity of an adult. His stepmother Lady Liu was harsh and jealous and treated him badly, yet Zheng served her only more carefully and never showed resentment. His original name was Lan; Jingli, seeing in him the filial spirit of Wang Xiang, gave him the name Zheng.
18
陳武帝為南徐州，召補迎主簿，尋授太學博士。 太建中，累遷太子中舍人，兼東宮領直，襲封新豐侯。 至德中，位太子中庶子、中書舍人，掌詔誥。 尋授左戶尚書，與僕射江總知撰五禮事。 後主器其才幹，任寄日重。 遷吏部尚書，每十日一往東宮，于皇太子前論述古今得喪及當時政務。 又敕以廷尉寺獄，事無大小，取征議決。 俄敕遣徵收募兵士，自為部曲，征善撫恤，得物情，旬月之間，眾近一萬。 位望既重，兼聲位熏灼，物議咸忌憚之。 尋徙中書令。 中書清簡無事，或云征有怨言，後主聞之大怒，收奪人馬，將誅之，左右致諫，獲免。
When Chen Wu-di was governor of Southern Xuzhou, Zheng was summoned as registrar for reception and soon made Erudite of the Imperial University. During Taijian he rose to Attendant of the Heir Apparent and head of eastern palace duty, inheriting the marquisate of Xinfeng. Under Zhide he served as Junior Mentor of the Heir Apparent and Attending Clerk of the Secretariat, directing imperial edicts. He was soon made Minister of the Left Household and, with Vice Director Jiang Zong, oversaw compilation of the Five Rites. The later emperor valued his ability and relied on him ever more heavily. Promoted to Minister of Personnel, he visited the eastern palace every ten days to lecture the crown prince on history and current affairs. He was also ordered that all cases in the Court of Judicial Review, large or small, be decided on his advice. Soon he was ordered to raise troops under his own command; Zheng treated them well, won their loyalty, and within a month had nearly ten thousand men. His rank and influence were now immense, his fame dazzling, and opinion turned against him. He was soon made Director of the Secretariat. The secretariat had little to do; rumor said Zheng grumbled at his idleness; the later emperor flew into a rage, stripped him of men and horses, and would have executed him but for the remonstrance of his attendants.
19
禎明三年，隋軍濟江，後主以征有幹用，令權知中領軍事。 征日夜勤苦，備盡心力，後主嘉焉，謂曰：「事寧有以相報」。 及決戰于鍾山南岡，敕征守宮城西北大營，尋令督眾軍戰事。 陳亡，隨例入長安。
In the third year of Zhenming (589), when Sui forces crossed the Yangzi, the later emperor, trusting Zheng's ability, put him in provisional charge of the central army. Zheng labored day and night with all his strength; the emperor praised him, saying, "When this is over I will repay you." At the decisive battle on the southern slope of Zhong Mountain he was ordered to hold the great camp northwest of the palace, then to direct all field operations. When Chen fell, he was taken to Chang'an like the other captives.
20
征美容儀，有口辯，多所詳究。 至於士流官宦，陳宗戚屬，及當朝制度，憲章儀軌，戶口風俗，山川土地，問無不對。 然性頗便佞進取，不能以退素自業。 初拜吏部尚書，啟後主借鼓吹，後主謂所司曰：「鼓吹軍樂，有功乃授，蔡征不自量揆，紊我朝章。 然其父景曆既有締構之功，宜且如啟，拜訖即追還。 」征不修廉隅，皆此類也。
Zheng was handsome, eloquent, and widely learned. On the gentry, Chen imperial kin, court institutions, ritual codes, population registers, local customs, and geography, he could answer any question. Yet he was ambitious and ingratiating and could not content himself with a modest life. On first becoming Minister of Personnel he asked the emperor for martial music; the emperor told his officers, "Martial music is a military honor granted only for merit. Cai Zheng overreaches and disrupts our regulations. Yet his father Jingli helped found the dynasty; grant it for now, but recall the music as soon as the appointment ceremony ends." Zheng lacked integrity in matters like these.
21
隋文帝聞其敏贍，召見顧問，言輒會旨。 然累年不調，久之，除太常丞。 曆尚書戶部儀曹郎，轉給事郎，卒。 子翼，位司徒屬。 入隋，為東宮學士。
Emperor Wen of Sui, hearing of his brilliance, summoned him for counsel; his answers always pleased the emperor. Yet for years he received no substantive post; at last he was made Assistant Director of the Imperial Ancestral Temple. He served as Attendant of the Household Department, Rites Section, then as Attendant for Submission of Memorials, and died. His son Yi served as attendant of the Minister of Education. Under the Sui he became a scholar of the eastern palace.
22
宗元饒，南郡江陵人也。 少好學，以孝聞。 仕梁為征南府外兵參軍。 及司徒王僧辯幕府初建，元饒與沛國劉師知同為主簿。 陳武帝受禪，稍遷廷尉卿、尚書左丞。 宣帝初，軍國務廣，事無巨細，一以咨之，台省號為稱職。
Zong Yuanrao was from Jiangling in Nan commandery. He loved learning as a youth and was known for filial piety. Under the Liang he served as external army recorder on the southern campaign staff. When Wang Senbian first formed his staff, Yuanrao and Liu Shizhi of Pei both served as chief clerks. After the Chen founding he rose to Minister of Justice and Left Assistant of the Masters of Writing. Early in Emperor Xuan's reign, with military and civil affairs pressing, every matter large or small was referred to him, and the bureaucracy was said to be functioning well.
23
遷御史中丞，知五禮事。 時合州刺史陳褒贓汙狼籍，遣使就渚斂魚，又令人於六郡乞米，百姓甚苦之，元饒劾奏免之。 吳興太守武陵王伯禮、豫章內史南康嗣王方泰等，驕蹇放橫，元饒案奏，皆見削黜。 元饒性公平，善持法，諳曉故事，明練政體，吏有犯法，政不便時，及於名教不足者，隨事糾正，多所裨益。 遷南康內史，以秩米三千餘斛助人租課，存問高年，拯救乏絕，百姓甚賴焉。 以課最入朝，詔加散騎常侍。 後為吏部尚書，卒。
He was made Censor-in-Chief with charge of the Five Rites. Governor Chen Bao of He prefecture was notoriously corrupt, extorting fish at the shore and sending men to beg grain across six commanderies; Yuanrao impeached him and had him removed. Prince Boli of Wuling, governor of Wuxing, and Fangtai, heir of Nankang and administrator of Yuzhang, among others, were arrogant and abusive; Yuanrao prosecuted them all and they were stripped of office. Fair and firm in law, versed in precedent and clear on administration, he corrected illegal officials, impractical policies, and breaches of ritual wherever he found them, to great public benefit. As administrator of Nankang he gave more than three thousand hu of his salary grain to help people pay taxes, comforted the elderly, and relieved the destitute; the people depended on him greatly. Summoned to court for outstanding administration, he was made Regular Attendant of Scattered Cavalry by edict. He later served as Minister of Personnel and died in office.
24
韓子高，會稽山陰人也。 家本微賤。 侯景之亂，寓都下。 景平，陳文帝出守吳興，子高年十六，為總角，容貌美麗，狀似婦人，於淮渚附部伍寄載欲還鄉里，文帝見而問曰：「能事我乎？ 」子高許諾。 子高本名蠻子，帝改名之。 性恭謹，恒執備身刀及傳酒炙。 帝性急，子高恒會意旨。 稍長，習騎射，頗有膽決，願為將帥。 及平杜龕，配以士卒。 文帝甚愛之，未嘗離左右。
Han Zigao was from Shanyin in Kuaiji. His family was of humble origin. During Hou Jing's rebellion he lived in the capital. After Hou Jing was defeated, Chen Wen-di went to govern Wuxing; Zigao was sixteen, still wearing the youth's topknot, strikingly beautiful and almost feminine in appearance. At a Huai River crossing he joined a convoy bound for home, and when the emperor saw him he asked, "Will you serve me?" Zigao agreed. His original name was Manzi; the emperor gave him the name Zigao. Respectful and attentive, he always carried the emperor's sidearm and served wine and food. The emperor was quick-tempered, but Zigao always anticipated his wishes. As he grew older he learned riding and archery, showed real courage, and aspired to command troops. After Du Kan was defeated, he was given a command of troops. Emperor Wen favored him deeply and kept him constantly at his side.
25
帝嘗夢騎馬登山，路危欲墮，子高推捧而升。
The emperor once dreamed he was riding uphill on a dangerous path about to fall, and Zigao lifted him safely to the summit.
26
文帝之討張彪也，沈泰等先降，帝據有州城，周文育鎮北郭香岩寺，張彪自剡縣夜還襲城，文帝自北門出，倉卒闇夕，軍人擾亂，唯子高在側。 文帝乃遣子高自亂兵中往見文育，反命酬答，於闇中又往慰勞眾軍。 文帝散兵稍集，子高引入文育營，因共立柵。 明日敗彪，彪奔松山，浙東平。 文帝乃分麾下多配子高，子高亦輕財禮士，歸之者甚眾。
When Emperor Wen campaigned against Zhang Biao, Shen Tai and others surrendered first and Wen held the city while Zhou Wenyü garrisoned Xiangyan Temple in the northern suburb. Zhang Biao struck by night from Shan county; the emperor fled through the north gate in the dark confusion with only Zigao beside him. The emperor sent Zigao through the chaos to Zhou Wenyü and back with his answer, then out again in the dark to rally the troops. As scattered forces reassembled, Zigao brought the emperor into Wenyü's camp and they threw up defenses together. The next day they defeated Biao, who fled to Song Mountain, and eastern Zhe was pacified. The emperor gave Zigao many of his own followers; generous and respectful of talent, Zigao attracted a large following.
27
文帝嗣位，除右軍將軍，封文招縣子。 及王琳平，子高所統益多，將士依附之，其有所論進，帝皆任使焉。 天嘉六年，為右衛將軍。 文帝不豫，入侍醫藥。
When Wen succeeded to the throne, Zigao was made General of the Right Army and enfeoffed as Viscount of Wenzhao. After Wang Lin was defeated, Zigao's command grew; officers and men flocked to him, and the emperor adopted every man he recommended. In the sixth year of Tiāncái he was made General of the Right Guard. When Emperor Wen fell ill, he attended him at his bedside.
28

When Emperor Xuan became regent, Zigao's military power made him uneasy; he frequented the ministries and sought a post in Hengzhou, Guangzhou, or another frontier command. In the eighth month of the first year of Guangda, former magistrate Lu Fang and one of Zigao's commanders accused him of treason. Emperor Xuan, at the Masters of Writing, summoned the court to discuss the crown prince; Zigao attended, was arrested, and sent to the Court of Judicial Review. That night he was executed along with Dao Zhongju. His father Yanqing and his brothers and sons were all pardoned.
29

When Wang Lin marched east, Jiao followed Hou Jin in resisting him. After Lin's defeat he administered Jiang province. He later joined Wu Mingche's campaign against Zhou Di; after Di's defeat he was promoted to marquis and made supervisor and governor of Xiang province. Rising from a minor clerk, Jiao was skilled at amassing wealth; campaigning in the hill country he sent many bronze drums and captives to the capital. When the deposed emperor acceded, Jiao was re-enfeoffed as Duke of Chong'an.
30

After Han Zigao's execution Jiao grew anxious; in the first year of Guangda he secretly asked for Guangzhou to test the court's intentions. Emperor Xuan pretended to agree, but no edict was issued. Jiao also invited Zhou forces and proclaimed allegiance to Liang Ming-di; his army grew formidable. The court named Wu Mingche governor of Xiang province, intending a surprise attack with light forces. Fearing Jiao would move first, they sent Mingche ahead with thirty thousand men in swift vessels straight for Yingzhou, and Pacification Army Grand General Chunyu Liang with fifty thousand in large ships behind.
31

Liang Ming-di sent a fleet to support Jiao; Zhou Wu-di posted Duke of Wei Yuwen Zhi at Lushan and sent Pillar of State Yuan Ding of Western Changhu to besiege Yingzhou. Liang Ming-di made Jiao Minister of Works; the governors and administrators Dai Sengshuo of Ba, Ren Mannu of Hengyang, Pan Zhiqian of Baling, Zhang Zhaoyu of Yueyang, Cao Xuan of Guiyang, and Qian Ming of Xiangdong all submitted to him. Cao Qing of Changsha and others who had already been under Jiao joined him as well. Fearing upstream officials would rally to Jiao, the emperor offered a special pardon in Xiang and Ba provinces, inviting rebel leaders and garrison commanders to surrender.
32

Jiao loaded his great ships with fuel and tried to burn the Chen fleet, but the wind shifted and his own ships caught fire; utterly defeated, he fled with Dai Sengshuo to Jiangling. Yuan Ding and his men, unable to cross by ship, marched on Baling, found it already in Chen hands, surrendered, and were sent to Jiankang. Jiao died at Jiangling; his followers were executed, though Ren Mannu, Zhang Zhaoyu, Cao Xuan, and Liu Guangye were spared.

劉師知，沛國相人也。 家本素族。 祖奚之，齊淮南太守，以善政聞。 父景彥，梁司農卿。
Liu Shizhi was from Xiang in Pei commandery. His family was of modest lineage. His grandfather Xizhi, governor of Huainan under Qi, was known for good administration. His father Jingyan served as Minister of Agriculture under the Liang.
34

His original name was Shizhi, changed to avoid the taboo of Emperor Jing of Liang. A devoted scholar with practical talent, he read widely, wrote well, mastered court ritual, and knew institutional precedent in detail. At the beginning of Shaotai, when Chen Wu-di became regent, Shizhi was made Attending Clerk of the Secretariat with charge of edicts. After the wars much court ritual had been lost; Shizhi drew up most of the protocols for Wu-di's chancellorship, the Nine Bestowals, and the abdication.
35
梁敬帝在內殿，師知常侍左右。 及將加害，師知詐帝令出，帝覺，遶床走曰：「師知賣我，陳霸先反。 我本不須作天子，何意見殺。 」師知執帝衣，行事者加刃焉。 既而報陳武帝曰：「事已了。 」武帝曰：「卿乃忠於我，後莫復爾。 」師知不對。 武帝受命，仍兼舍人。 性疏簡，與物多忤，雖位宦不遷，而任遇甚重，其所獻替，皆有弘益。
When Liang Emperor Jing was confined in the inner palace, Shizhi was constantly at his side. When the moment came to kill him, Shizhi tricked the emperor into leaving the chamber. The emperor realized what was happening and cried as he ran around the bed, "Shizhi has betrayed me! Chen Baxian is in rebellion! I never wanted to be emperor—why must you kill me?" Shizhi held the emperor fast while the assassins struck. He then reported to Chen Wu-di, "It is done." The emperor said, "You have served me faithfully—but never do such a thing again." Shizhi made no answer. When Wu-di took the throne, Shizhi retained his clerkship. Plain and often at odds with others, he advanced little in rank yet was deeply trusted, and his counsel consistently benefited the state.
36

When Emperor Wu died, the court completed the six-day mourning observance and debated whether attendants at the late emperor's spirit seat should wear auspicious or mourning dress. Erudite Shen Wena argued for auspicious dress; Shizhi replied, "Once mourning garments are completed, full funeral rites apply. When Crown Prince Zhaoming of Liang died, attendants at the completion of mourning all wore full sackcloth except those in armor—this precedent should govern. I hold that on the sixth day, attendants at the spirit seat must wear full mourning with hemp bands." Attending Clerks Cai Jingli, Jiang Dezao, and Xie Qi sided with Shizhi. The two views deadlocked, and the matter was referred to Left Assistant Xu Ling. Ling replied, "In the auspicious section of the tomb procession, from ministers down through guards, musicians, canopy bearers, and chariot attendants, all wear auspicious dress. How can attendants alone wear mourning? If ministers and clerks all wore mourning, how would they differ from the cortège of the inner coffin? If regalia were auspicious but performers in mourning, could one bear the imperial canopy in sackcloth or mount the jade chariot in mourning robes?" He sided with the erudite. Xie Qi argued, "The spirit seat goes to the ancestral temple and the inner coffin to the tomb, as the Left Assistant said. But the tomb procession has both auspicious and mourning sections: followers of the spirit carriage keep auspicious dress, while followers of the inner coffin wear coarse sackcloth, even in the rites for common scholars. That is tomb ritual, not the completion of mourning garments. Here the inner coffin and spirit screen stand together on the western steps with no procession—only the completion of mourning garments—yet from clerks to princes throughout the realm all must wear full mourning. When Crown Prince Zhaoming died, that was the precedent. Can every scholar and commoner wear heavy mourning while the closest officials, from Attendants-in-Ordinary to the Martial Guard, wear jade and blue sashes as on any ordinary day?" The Left Assistant treats this as tomb ritual, but I believe completing mourning garments is a different matter." Ling replied again, "Old and ill, I lie near death and cannot debate at length. Ancient disputants often made lasting enemies—Fu Xuan suffered in Jin, Wang Shang in Han. I seal my lips thrice and bow to your judgment. If by chance I survive, I may yet argue the point again with the assembled worthies." Wena held to his view; unable to decide, the court recorded both opinions and memorialized the emperor, who ruled with Shizhi.

In the first year of Tiānkāng, when Emperor Wen fell ill, Shizhi and Vice Director Dao Zhongju attended him at his bedside. When the emperor died, Shizhi was named in the final testament. When Emperor Xuan became regent, Shizhi and Zhongju forged an edict through Clerk Yin Buniu ordering him back to the eastern palace; exposed, Shizhi was executed in the northern prison.

Emperor Wen had ordered Shizhi to compile the court diary from autumn of the second year of Yongding through the first year of Tiāncái, ten juan in all.
39

Xie Qi was from Shanyin in Kuaiji. His father Da was Erudite of the Imperial University under the Liang.
40

His younger brother Qiao was a devoted scholar and recognized authority on the classics.

Mao Xi, courtesy name Bowu, was from Yangwu in Xingyang. His grandfather Cheng was Attendant-in-Ordinary of Scattered Cavalry under the Liang. His father Qizhong was Marshal of the Central Guard.
42

43

When Emperor Wen died, the young deposed emperor reigned and Emperor Xuan served as regent. Vice Director Dao Zhongju forged an empress dowager's order sending Xuan back to the eastern palace; the court was terrified and none dared speak. Xi rode in at once and told Emperor Xuan, "This order cannot truly come from the empress dowager. The fate of the dynasty hangs on your decision—think carefully." Events unfolded exactly as he had foreseen.
44

General of the Right Guard Han Zigao was plotting with Zhongju before the plot was exposed. Xi told Emperor Xuan, "You should assign men and horses to Zigao and supply iron and charcoal for armor—" Emperor Xuan said, "I mean to arrest Zigao—why arm him further?" Xi said, "The imperial tomb is barely finished and enemies still threaten the borders. Zigao served the previous reign and claims loyalty—reassure him so he does not grow suspicious; then one bold man can bring him down." Emperor Xuan followed his advice.
45

After recovering the Huai region, Xi proposed border-security measures that Emperor Xuan adopted the same day. The emperor also planned to march on Pengcheng and Bian and asked Xi, who replied, "The Huai region is newly pacified and the frontier people are unsettled; Zhou has just absorbed Qi and is too strong to challenge. Secure the people and hold the borders—that is the path to lasting peace." The emperor refused. Wu Mingche was eventually captured by Zhou.
46

47

After Mingche's defeat the emperor deeply regretted ignoring him and told Yuan Xian, "Had I listened to Xi, we would not be here." From then on he was trusted all the more, and Xi spoke without holding back. The crown prince loved wine and often held all-night revels with his favorites; Xi reported this to Emperor Xuan, and the prince bore a grudge; after accession he gradually distanced Xi. After being wounded by the Prince of Shixing and recovering, he held a feast for Jiang Zong and others with music and poetry, and when drunk summoned Xi. The imperial tomb had only just been completed and less than a year had passed; Xi was displeased and wished to remonstrate, but the later emperor was already drunk. Xi claimed a heart ailment, collapsed on the steps, and was carried from the palace offices. When the later emperor sobered, he told Jiang Zong, "I regret calling Mao Xi. I want to hand him to the princes of Poyang to take revenge—would that do?" Shen replied, "He will never serve the court again. Your Majesty's will be done." Fu Zai objected, "If you allow revenge, what becomes of the late emperor's honor?" The later emperor said, "Give him a small prefecture and keep him from court affairs."

53
君高字季高，少知名，性剛直，有吏能。 位衛尉卿，平越中郎將、都督、廣州刺史，甚得人和。 卒，諡祁子。
Jungau, courtesy name Jigao, was known from youth for his upright character and administrative ability. He served as Minister of the Guards, Middle General Who Pacifies the South, supervisor, and governor of Guang province, and won wide loyalty. He died and was given the posthumous name Reverent.
54
君公自梁元帝敗後，常在江陵。 禎明中，與蕭瓛、蕭岩叛隋歸陳，後主擢為太子詹事。 君公博學有才辯，善談論，後主深器之。 陳亡入隋，文帝以其叛亡，命斬于建康。
After Emperor Yuan of Liang's defeat, Jungong remained at Jiangling. In Zhenming he defected from Sui to Chen with Xiao Huan and Xiao Yan, and the later emperor made him Mentor of the Heir Apparent. Learned and eloquent, skilled in debate, he was deeply valued by the later emperor. When Chen fell he entered Sui service, but Emperor Wen ordered him executed at Jiankang for his defection.
55
君理第五叔邁，亦方正有幹局，位通直散騎常侍，侍東宮。
Junli's fifth uncle Mai was likewise upright and capable; he served as Regular Attendant of Scattered Cavalry, Unimpeded Service, attending the eastern palace.
56
陸山才字孔章，吳郡吳人也。 祖翁寶，梁尚書水部郎。 父泛，中散大夫。
Lu Shancai, courtesy name Kongzhang, was from Wu in Wu commandery. His grandfather Wengbao was Attendant of the Water Section under the Liang Masters of Writing. His father Fan was Grand Master of Leisurely Dissemination.
57
山才倜儻，好尚文史，范陽張纘、纘弟綰並欽重之。
Bold and free-spirited, he loved literature and history; Zhang Zuan of Fanyang and his brother Wan both held him in high regard.
58
紹泰中，都督周文育出鎮南豫州，不知書疏，以山才為長史，政事悉以委之。 文育南討，克蕭勃，禽歐陽頠，計畫多出山才。 後文育重鎮豫章金口，山才復為鎮南長史、豫章太守。
In Shaotai, when Zhou Wenyü was posted to Southern Yuzhou and could not manage documents himself, he made Shancai chief of staff and entrusted all affairs to him. In Wenyü's southern campaign against Xiao Bo and Ouyang Yi, most of the strategy came from Shancai. When Wenyü again garrisoned Jinkou in Yuzhang, Shancai again served as southern staff chief and governor of Yuzhang.
59
文育為熊曇朗所害，曇朗囚山才等，送于王琳。 未至，而侯安都敗琳將常眾愛，由是山才獲反。 累遷度支尚書，坐侍宴與蔡景曆言語過差，為有司所奏，免官。 尋授散騎常侍，遷西陽、武昌二郡太守。 卒，諡曰簡子。
Wenyü was killed by Xiong Tanlang, who imprisoned Shancai and sent him to Wang Lin. Before they reached Lin, Hou Andu defeated Lin's general Chang Zhong'ai, and Shancai was able to return. He rose to Minister of Revenue but was dismissed after speaking improperly to Cai Jingli at a court banquet. He was soon made Regular Attendant of Scattered Cavalry and governor of Xiyang and Wuchang. He died and was given the posthumous name Simple.
60
論曰：趙知禮、蔡景曆屬陳武經綸之日，居文房書記之任，此乃宋、齊之初傅亮、王儉之職。 若乃校其才用，理不同年，而卒能膺務濟時，蓋其遇也。 希祥勞臣之子，才名自致，跡涉便佞，貞介所羞。 元饒始終任遇，無虧公道，名位自卒，其殆優乎。 子高權重為戮，亦其宜也。 華皎經綸雲始，既蹈元功，殷憂之辰，自同勁草，雖致奔敗，未足為非。 師知送往多闕，見忌新主，謀人之義，可無慎哉； 然晚遇誅夷，非其過也。 毛喜逢時遇主，好謀而成，見廢昏朝，不致公輔，惜矣。 沈、陸所以見重，固亦雅望之所致焉。
The historian comments: Zhao Zhili and Cai Jingli served during Chen Wu-di's founding, in the secretarial posts once held by Fu Liang and Wang Jian under Song and Qi. Their talents were not of the same order, yet both served the times well—such was their fortune. Xixiang, son of a meritorious minister, won fame by his own gifts, yet his career smacked of flattery—shameful to the upright. Yuanrao served throughout without compromising justice and died in honor—perhaps the finest of them. Zigao's execution for excessive power was only fitting. Hua Jiao shared in the founding and stood firm in hard times; though he ended in defeat, he was not without honor. Shizhi's role in the late emperor's death earned the new ruler's hatred; in such matters of conspiracy, caution is everything; yet his late execution was hardly his fault alone. Mao Xi met a worthy ruler and succeeded by counsel, yet was cast aside by a dissolute court and never reached the highest office—a pity. Shen and Lu were valued above all for their cultivated reputations.
