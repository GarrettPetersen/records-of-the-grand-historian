龔穎，遂寧人也。 少好學，益州刺史。 毛璩辟為勸學從事。 璩為譙縱所殺，故佐吏並逃亡，穎號哭奔赴，殯送以禮。 縱後設宴延穎，不獲已而至。 樂奏，穎流涕起曰：「北面事人，亡不能死，何忍舉觴聞樂，蹈跡逆亂乎。」 縱大將譙道福引出將斬之，道福母即穎姑也，跣出救之得免。 及縱僭號，備禮征又不至，乃脅以兵刃，執志終無回改，至於蜀平，遂不屈節。 其後刺史至，輒加辟引。 曆府參軍，州別駕從事史。 宋文帝元嘉二十四年，刺史陸徽表穎節義，遂不被朝命，終於家。
Gong Ying was a native of Suining. From youth he loved learning and was connected with the Inspector of Yizhou. Mao Qiu recruited him as an aide charged with promoting scholarship. When Qiu was murdered by the rebel Qiao Zong, his former subordinates all fled, but Ying wept aloud, hurried to the scene, and laid him out and buried him with full propriety. Zong later gave a feast and summoned Ying, who came only because he could not refuse. When the music began, Ying rose in tears and said, "I served my lord as a subject, yet when he died I could not die with him—how can I bear to lift a cup and listen to music while walking in the footsteps of treason Zong's chief general Qiao Daofu had him dragged out to be beheaded, but Daofu's mother was Ying's aunt; she ran out barefoot and rescued him, and he was spared. When Zong declared himself emperor and summoned him with full honors, Ying still would not come; threatened with weapons, he held firm to the end, and even after Shu was pacified he never compromised his loyalty. Thereafter each new provincial inspector would try to recruit him. He held posts as a prefectural staff officer, provincial chief clerk, and attendant secretary. In Yuanjia 24 of Emperor Wen of Song, Inspector Lu Hui submitted a memorial praising Ying's loyalty, but Ying never accepted an imperial post and died at home.
4
劉瑜，曆陽曆陽人也。 七歲喪父，事母至孝。 年五十二，又喪母，三年不進鹽酪，號泣晝夜不絕聲，勤身力以營葬事。 服除，二十餘年，布衣蔬食，言輒流涕，常居墓側，未嘗暫違。 宋文帝元嘉初卒。
Liu Yu was a native of Liyang in Liyang commandery. He lost his father at seven and thereafter served his mother with the utmost devotion. At fifty-two he lost his mother as well; for three years he ate no salt or dairy, wailed day and night without stopping, and strained his own strength to manage the funeral. After the mourning period, for more than twenty years he wore plain clothes and ate simple fare, wept whenever he spoke, and lived beside the grave without ever leaving it, even for a moment. He died early in the Yuanjia era of Emperor Wen of Song.
5
又元嘉七年，南豫州舉所統西陽縣人董陽三世同居，外無異門，內無異煙。 詔榜門曰：「篤行董氏之閭」，蠲一門租布。
In Yuanjia 7 as well, South Yuzhou reported that Dong Yang of Xiyang County had three generations living under one roof, with no separate outer gates and no separate hearths inside. An edict ordered a placard posted on their gate reading "The Lane of the Dong Family's Steadfast Conduct," and exempted the entire household from rent and cloth taxes.
6
賈恩，會稽諸暨人也。 少有志行。 元嘉三年母亡，居喪過禮。 未葬，為鄰火所逼，恩及妻桓氏號哭奔救，鄰近赴助，棺櫬得免，恩及桓俱燒死。 有司奏改其裏為孝義裏，蠲租布三世。 追贈恩天水郡顯親左尉。
Jia En was a native of Zhuji in Kuaiji. From youth he was known for moral resolve. In Yuanjia 3 his mother died, and he observed mourning with greater rigor than the rites required. Before the burial, a fire from a neighbor's house threatened the coffin; En and his wife Lady Huan ran wailing to save it; neighbors rushed to help and the coffin was spared, but En and Lady Huan both perished in the flames. The authorities memorialized to rename his neighborhood the Lane of Filial Piety and Righteousness and to exempt three generations from rent and cloth levies. Jia En was posthumously granted the title of Left Commandant of Xianqin in Tianshui commandery.
7
郭世通，會稽永興人也。 年十四喪父，居喪殆不勝哀。 家貧，傭力以養繼母。 婦生一男，夫妻恐廢侍養，乃垂泣瘞之。 母亡，負土成墳。 親戚或共賻助，微有所受，葬畢，傭賃還先直。 服除後，思慕終身如喪者，未嘗釋衣幍。 仁孝之風，行於鄉黨。 鄰村小大莫有呼其名者。 嘗與人共于山陰市貨物，誤得一千錢，當時不覺，分背方悟，追還本主。 錢主驚歎，以半直與之，世通委之而去。 元嘉四年，大使巡行天下，散騎常侍袁愉表其淳行，文帝嘉之，敕榜表門閭，蠲其租調，改所居獨楓裏為孝行焉。 太守孟顗察孝廉，不就。
Guo Shitong was a native of Yongxing in Kuaiji. He lost his father at fourteen and grieved so deeply in mourning that he nearly could not endure it. The household was poor, so he hired himself out as a laborer to support his stepmother. When his wife bore a son, the couple feared the child would keep them from caring for his mother, so they wept and secretly buried the infant. When his mother died, he carried earth on his back to raise the burial mound himself. Relatives sometimes pooled funeral gifts; he accepted only a small amount, and after the burial he worked for hire to repay every advance he had taken. After the mourning period he grieved for the rest of his life as though still in mourning and never removed his mourning headcloth. His reputation for benevolence and filial devotion spread through the countryside. In neighboring villages, young and old alike never addressed him by his personal name out of respect. Once while trading in the Shanyin market he mistakenly received an extra thousand cash without noticing; only after they parted did he realize, and he ran after the owner to return it. The owner was astonished and offered him half the sum as a reward, but Shitong laid it down and walked away. In Yuanjia 4 an imperial envoy toured the empire; Attendant Cavalier Yuan Yu submitted a memorial praising his pure conduct; Emperor Wen was pleased, ordered a placard posted at his gate, exempted his taxes, and renamed his neighborhood from Lone Maple Lane to the Lane of Filial Conduct. Prefect Meng Yi nominated him as a Filial and Incorrupt candidate, but he declined the office.
8
子原平字長恭，又稟至行，養親必以己力，傭賃以給供養。 性甚巧，每為人作正，取散夫價。 主人設食，原平自以家貧，父母不辦有肴味，唯飧鹽飯而已。 若家或無食，則虛中竟日，義不獨飽。 須日暮作畢，受直歸家，於裏糴買，然後舉爨。
His son Yuanping, styled Changgong, inherited the same exemplary conduct; he always supported his parents with his own labor, hiring himself out to provide for them. He was naturally very skillful; whenever he built a main hall for someone he charged only day-labor rates. When his employer offered a meal, Yuanping, knowing his parents at home could afford no delicacies, ate only plain rice with salt. If there was no food at home, he would go hungry all day rather than eat his fill while his parents had nothing. He waited until dusk when the day's work was done, took his wages home, bought grain in the village, and only then cooked the evening meal.
9
父篤疾彌年，原平衣不解帶，口不嚐鹽菜者，跨積寒暑，又未嘗睡臥。 父亡，哭踴慟絕，數日方蘇。 以為奉終之義，情禮自畢，塋壙凶功，不欲假人。 本雖巧而不解作墓，乃訪邑中有營墓者，助人運力，經時展勤，久乃閑練。 又自賣十夫以供眾費，窀穸之事，儉而當禮。 性無術學，因心自然。 葬畢，詣所買主執役無懈，與諸奴分務，讓逸取勞。 主人不忍使，每遣之。 原平服勤未嘗暫替，傭賃養母，有餘聚以自贖。 既學構塚，尤善其事，每至吉歲，求者盈門。 原平所起必自貧始，既取賤價，又以夫日助之。 及父喪終，自起兩間小屋以為祠堂，每至節歲，常于此數日中哀思，絕飲粥。 父服除後，不復食肉。 高陽許瑤之罷建安郡丞還家，以綿一斤遺之，不受。 瑤之乃自往，曰：「今歲過寒，而建安綿好，以此奉尊上下耳。」 原平乃拜而受之。
When his father lay gravely ill for a full year, Yuanping never loosened his belt, ate no salted food, endured summer and winter alike, and never lay down to sleep. When his father died, he wailed and stamped until he collapsed; several days passed before he regained consciousness. He felt that the emotional and ritual obligations of mourning he could fulfill himself, but for the grave and funeral labor he would not rely on others. Though skilled by nature, he did not know tomb-building, so he found grave-builders in the district, helped them carry earth, and through long practice became expert. He also hired himself out for ten days' labor to cover the funeral costs, and the burial was frugal yet fully in keeping with the rites. He had no formal training in the craft; everything came naturally from the heart. After the burial he went to the man who had bought his labor and worked without slackening, sharing tasks with the household slaves and always taking the harder work for himself. The master could not bear to work him so hard and kept sending him home. Yuanping labored without a day's rest, hired himself out to support his mother, and saved whatever remained to buy back his pledged labor. Once he had mastered tomb-building he excelled at it, and in every propitious year clients crowded his door. Yuanping always took the poorest clients first, charged the lowest prices, and added his own labor days at no extra cost. After his father's mourning ended, he built two small rooms as a memorial shrine; at each festival he spent several days there in grief, taking no food at all. After his father's mourning period he never ate meat again. Xu Yaozhi of Gaoyang, after leaving his post as assistant prefect of Jian'an and returning home, sent him a pound of cotton, but he refused it. Yaozhi then came in person and said, "This year is bitterly cold, and Jian'an cotton is excellent—I bring this only for your honored parents Only then did Yuanping bow and accept it.

及母終，毀瘠彌甚，僅乃免喪。 墓前有數十畝田，不屬原平，每至農月，耕者恒裸袒。 原平不欲使人慢其墳墓，乃貿家資，貴買此田，三農之月，輒束帶垂泣，躬自耕墾。
When his mother died, his grief left him so wasted that he barely survived the mourning period. Several tens of mu of fields before the grave did not belong to Yuanping, and each planting season the laborers worked stripped to the waist. Yuanping could not bear to see the grave treated with disrespect, so he sold his household goods and bought the land at a high price; during the three farming seasons he would gird himself, weep, and till the soil with his own hands.
11
每出賣物，裁求半價，邑人皆共識悉，輒加本價與之，彼此相讓，要使微賤，然後取直。 宅上種竹，夜有盜其筍者，原平遇見之，盜者奔走墜溝。 原平乃於所植竹處溝上立小橋令通，又采筍置籬外，鄰里慚愧，無復取者。
Whenever he sold anything he asked only half the going price; the townspeople all knew him and would pay the full price instead, each insisting on yielding until the sum was very small, and only then would he accept payment. He had bamboo planted at his home; one night a thief came for the shoots; Yuanping caught him, and the thief fled and fell into a ditch. Yuanping built a small bridge over the ditch by the bamboo so the thief could cross safely, and left picked shoots outside the fence; the neighbors were ashamed and never stole again.
12
宋文帝崩，原平號慟，日食麥鉡一枚，如此五日。 人曰：「誰非王臣，何獨如此？」 原平泣而答曰：「吾家見異先朝，蒙褒贊之賞，不能報恩，私心感動耳。」
When Emperor Wen of Song died, Yuanping wailed in grief and ate only one wheat cake a day for five days. Someone said, "Who is not a subject of the throne—why grieve alone like this Yuanping wept and replied, "My family was singled out by the previous reign and received imperial praise and reward; I could not repay that kindness—this is only a private stirring of gratitude."
13
又以種瓜為業，大明七年大旱，瓜瀆不復通船。 縣令劉僧秀湣其窮老，下瀆水與之。 原平曰：「普天大旱，百姓俱困，豈可減溉田之水，以通運瓜之船。」 乃步從他道往錢唐貨賣。 每行來見人牽埭未過，輒迅烜助之。 己自引船，不假旁人。 若自船已度，後人未及，常停住須待，以此為常。 嘗于縣南郭鳳埭助人引船，遇有鬥者為吏所錄，鬥者逃散，唯原平獨住，吏執以送縣。 縣令新到，未相諳悉，將加嚴罰，原平解衣就罪，義無一言。 左右大小咸稽顙請救，然後得免。 由來不謁官長，自此乃始修敬。 太守蔡興宗臨郡，深加貴異，以私米饋原平及山陰朱百年妻各百斛。 原平誓死不受，百年妻亦固辭。
He also made his living growing melons; in Daming 7 a severe drought struck, and the Melon Channel could no longer carry boats. Magistrate Liu Senxiu pitied his poverty and old age and released irrigation water for him. Yuanping said, "The whole empire is in drought and the people are all suffering—how can I divert irrigation water from the fields just to float boats carrying my melons He then walked by another route to Qiantang to sell his melons. Whenever he traveled and saw someone struggling to haul a boat over a dam, he would hurry to help. He always pulled his own boat and never asked others to do it for him. If his own boat had crossed but those behind had not, he always stopped and waited for them; this became his constant practice. Once at Feng Dam south of the county seat he was helping haul a boat when brawlers were seized by clerks; the brawlers fled, but Yuanping alone remained, and the clerks arrested him and took him to the magistrate. The magistrate had just arrived and did not know him; about to impose a harsh penalty, Yuanping stripped for punishment and on principle said not a word in his own defense. Everyone around him, young and old alike, kowtowed and pleaded for his release, and only then was he spared. He had never visited officials before, but from that day he began to pay his respects. When Prefect Cai Xingzong took office he held Yuanping in high esteem and sent a hundred hu of grain from his private stores to Yuanping and to the widow of Zhu Bainian of Shanyin. Yuanping vowed he would rather die than accept, and Zhu's widow likewise firmly refused.
14
會稽郡貴重望計及望孝，盛族出身，不減秘、著。 明帝泰始七年，興宗欲舉山陰孔仲智子為望計，原平次息為望孝。 仲智會土高門，原平一邦至行，欲以相敵。 會明帝別敕用人，故二選並寢。 興宗征還都，表其殊行，舉為太學博士。 會興宗薨，事不行。 卒於家。 三子一弟，並有門行。
In Kuaiji commandery the prestigious offices of wangji and wangxiao were reserved for eminent clans whose standing rivaled the Mi and Zhu families. In Taishi 7 of Emperor Ming, Xingzong wished to nominate Kong Zhongzhi's son of Shanyin as wangji and Yuanping's second son as wangxiao. Zhongzhi came from a great house in the capital region, while Yuanping's family represented the highest conduct in the commandery; Xingzong wished to pair them as equals. But Emperor Ming issued a separate edict on appointments, so both nominations were set aside. When Xingzong was recalled to the capital he submitted a memorial praising Yuanping's extraordinary conduct and recommended him as Erudite of the Imperial Academy. But Xingzong died before the appointment could be made. He died at home. His three sons and one younger brother all upheld the family's reputation for virtue.
15
嚴世期，會稽山陰人也。 性好施，同里張邁等三人妻各產子，歲饑，欲棄而不舉。 世期分食解衣以贍其乏，三子並得成長。 同縣俞陽妻莊年九十，莊女蘭七十，並老病無所依，世期飴之二十年，死並殯葬。 宗親嚴弘、鄉人潘伯等十五人，荒年並餓死，露骸不收。 世期買棺殯埋，存育孩幼。 宋元嘉四年，有司奏榜門曰：「義行嚴氏之門」。 復其身徭役，蠲租稅十年。
Yan Shiqi was a native of Shanyin in Kuaiji. He was generous by nature; the wives of Zhang Mai and two other men in his lane each gave birth during a famine year and wished to abandon their infants. Shiqi shared his food and gave them his clothes to relieve their want, and all three children grew to adulthood. In the same county, Zhuang, wife of Yu Yang, was ninety and her daughter Lan was seventy; both were old and ill with no one to care for them; Shiqi supported them for twenty years and buried them when they died. Fifteen kinsmen and fellow villagers including Yan Hong and Pan Bo starved to death in the famine year, their bodies left unburied. Shiqi bought coffins and buried them, and took in and raised the surviving children. In Yuanjia 4 of Song the authorities memorialized to post a placard on his gate reading "The Gate of the Yan Family's Righteous Conduct." His personal labor service was remitted and his rent and taxes were exempted for ten years.

吳逵，吳興烏程人也。 經荒饑饉，系以疾疫，父母兄嫂及群從小功之親男女死者十三人，逵時病困，鄰里以葦席裹之，埋於村側。 既而親屬皆盡，唯逵夫妻獲全。 家徒四壁立，冬無被褲，晝則傭賃，夜則伐木燒磚，妻亦同逵此誠，無有懈倦。 逵夜行遇猛獸，猛獸輒下道避之。 期年中成七墓，葬十三棺，鄰里嘉之。 葬日，悉出赴助，送終之事，亦儉而周禮。 逵時逆取鄰人夫直，葬畢，眾悉以放之，逵一無所受，皆傭力報答焉。 太守張崇之三加禮命，太守王韶之擢補功曹史。 逵以門寒，固辭不就。 舉為孝廉。
Wu Kui was a native of Wucheng in Wuxing. After famine came pestilence; his parents, sister-in-law, and collateral kin within the lesser mourning grade—thirteen men and women in all—died; Kui himself lay gravely ill, and neighbors wrapped the bodies in reed mats and buried them beside the village. Before long all his relatives perished; only Kui and his wife were left alive. Their home was stripped bare; in winter they had no bedding; by day they hired themselves out, by night they felled timber and fired bricks, and his wife shared Kui's devotion without ever tiring. When Kui traveled at night and met wild beasts, the beasts always stepped off the path to avoid him. Within a year he built seven tombs and buried thirteen coffins, and the neighbors praised him. On the burial day everyone came to help; the funeral was frugal yet fully in keeping with the rites. Kui had borrowed neighbors' labor in advance; when the burial was finished they all wished to forgive his debt, but he accepted nothing and repaid every obligation with his own work. Prefect Zhang Chongzhi summoned him three times with honors; Prefect Wang Shaozhi promoted him to clerk for recording merit. Kui, considering his family too humble, firmly declined office. He was nominated as a Filial and Incorrupt candidate.
17
潘綜，吳興烏程人也。 孫恩之亂，祅黨攻破村邑，綜與父驃共走避賊。 驃年老行遲，賊轉逼驃。 驃語綜：「我不能去，汝走可脫，幸勿俱死。」 驃困乏坐地，綜迎賊叩頭曰：「父年老，乞賜生命。」 賊至，驃亦請賊曰：「兒年少自能走，今為老子不去。 老子不惜死，乞活此兒。」 賊因斫驃，綜抱父于腹下，賊斫綜頭面凡四創，綜當時悶絕。 有一賊從傍來相謂曰：「卿欲舉大事，此兒以死救父，云何可殺？ 殺孝子不祥。」 賊乃止，父子並得免。 鄉人秘書監丘系祖、廷尉沈赤黔以綜異行，薦補左戶令史，除遂昌長。 歲滿還家，太守王韶之臨郡，發教列上州台，陳其行跡。 及將行，設祖道，贈以四言詩。 元嘉四年，有司奏改其裏為純孝裏，蠲租布三世。
Pan Zong was a native of Wucheng in Wuxing. During Sun En's rebellion, the cultists raided villages; Zong fled with his father Biao to escape the rebels. Biao was elderly and walked slowly, and the rebels closed in on him. Biao told Zong, "I cannot go on—you run and can escape; do not let us both die Biao, exhausted, sat down; Zong met the rebels and kowtowed, saying, "My father is old—please spare his life" When the rebels arrived, Biao pleaded as well, "The boy is young and could have run away; now he stays for this old man I do not begrudge death—please let this boy live" The rebels then struck at Biao; Zong shielded his father against his chest; they cut Zong's head and face four times, and he fainted on the spot. One rebel said to another, "You mean to accomplish a great undertaking—this boy would die to save his father; how can you kill him To kill a filial son brings ill fortune. The rebels then stopped, and father and son both survived. His fellow townsman Qiu Xizu, Director of the Secretariat, and Shen Chiqian, Minister of Justice, because of Zong's extraordinary conduct recommended him as Left Household Clerk and appointed him magistrate of Suichang. When his term ended he returned home; Prefect Wang Shaozhi took office, issued an instruction reporting his conduct to the provincial and central authorities. When Zong was about to leave, the prefect held a farewell feast and presented him with a poem in four-character lines. In Yuanjia 4 the authorities memorialized to rename his neighborhood the Lane of Pure Filial Piety and to exempt three generations from rent and cloth levies.

He Ziping was a native of Qian in Lujiang. His great-grandfather Kai was Attendant-in-Ordinary of Jin. His grandfather You was consulting staff officer to Prince Daozi of Kuaiji, Rapid Cavalry General. His father Zixian was prefect of Jian'an.

Ziping's family had long lived in Kuaiji; from youth he showed moral resolve and served his mother with the utmost devotion. Yangzhou recruited him as attendant secretary; his monthly salary was paid in white rice, which he always sold for millet and wheat in the market. Someone said, "The profit is slight—why go to the trouble Ziping said, "My elders are in the east and cannot obtain rice—how could I enjoy white grain alone" Whenever anyone gave him fresh delicacies, if they could not be sent home to his mother he refused them. His mother had been a concubine; the household register was inaccurate; she was not yet old enough to require support by the true count, but the register showed the age fulfilled, so he resigned and returned home. At the time General of the Garrison Army Gu Kanzhi was the province's chief official and told him, "Your mother is not truly eighty yet—relatives know this; the province has a modest salary available—I will petition to keep you Ziping said, "The government rightly trusts the household register; once the register age is reached one must go to support one's parent—how can one grasp at office and profit" He then returned home and devoted all his strength to supporting her.

In Yuanjia 30 the crown prince committed regicide; Prince Dan of Sui marched to suppress the rebellion and appointed Ziping as a marching staff officer. Ziping held that the traitor had violated all moral principle and therefore resigned the office he had accepted; when peace was restored he left of his own accord. Later he was appointed magistrate of Haiyu in Wu commandery; his county salary supported only his mother and did not extend to his wife and children. People suspected him of stinginess; Ziping said, "I sought salary to support my parent, not for myself The questioner withdrew in shame. When his mother died he resigned office; his grief exceeded the rites; whenever he wailed and stamped he collapsed and only revived after a time. Late in Daming the eastern lands suffered famine, followed by warfare; for eight years he could not arrange the burial. Day and night he wailed, always as on the first day of mourning. In winter he wore no padded clothes; in summer he avoided cool places; each day he made gruel from a few he of rice and ate no salt or vegetables. His dwelling was ruined and gave no shelter from wind and sun; his nephew Boxing wished to repair it; Ziping refused, saying, "My grief is not yet fulfilled—I am but a criminal under heaven; how should I have a roof over my head Cai Xingzong, prefect of Kuaiji, greatly admired him and arranged the tomb mound for him.

Cui Huaishun was a native of Dongwucheng in Qinghe. His father Xieli was prefect of Lu commandery; during the Yuanjia era of Song he was captured by Wei. Huaishun and his wife Lady Fang were deeply devoted; when he learned his father was a captive, that very day he sent his wife away, wore mourning clothes and ate plain food, and each year wept facing north. Xieli later served Wei and wrote forbidding Huaishun to act thus. Huaishun received the letter and wept all the more. Huaishun's uncle Mo was prefect of Xingyang and also went over to Wei; Mo's son, though he adapted to life there, did not abandon marriage and office. In Daming of Song, Huaishun's clansman Yuan Sun, Inspector of Jizhou, went north as envoy to Wei; the Wei people asked him, "Cui Xieli and Mo both submitted when overcome—yet their sons and nephews take different paths; where does righteousness lie Yuan Sun said, "Wang Zun drove out the steed and Wang Yang turned the carriage back—wishing to fulfill both loyalty and filial piety, both as subject and as son."

Early in Taishi, when the Huai north bank fell to Wei, Huaishun returned north; reaching the Wei capital, Xieli had already died; Huaishun collapsed and revived, then carried the coffin back to Qingzhou. Barefoot through ice and snow in bitter cold, his hands and feet were unharmed; contemporaries attributed it to filial devotion moving heaven. After the mourning period, because his younger brother was in the south, he fled back at the beginning of Jianyuan of Qi, but his brother had already died. Huaishun was orphaned and poor; the clan pitied him and each day collected a dou of rice for him. He died during the Yongming era.

Wang Xuzhi, styled Wenjing, was a native of Shiyang in Lujiang. He lost his mother at thirteen and his father at thirty-three; for twenty-five years he ate no salt or dairy. Ill and bedridden, suddenly a man came to inquire after him and said, "Your illness will soon improve In a moment he vanished; the illness indeed soon improved. A bayberry in the courtyard bore three fruits in deep winter; each night his dwelling shone like a candle; an orange tree on the grave bore fruit twice in one winter—all were attributed to filial devotion. During Yongming of Qi an edict posted at his gate exempted taxes for three generations.

Ruiming prayed day and night; in the cold his tears froze into icicles like chopsticks, and blood from his forehead kowtows froze without dripping. 樂頤之字文德，南陽涅陽人也，世居南郡，少而言行和謹。 仕為京府參軍，父在郢病亡。 頤之忽悲戀涕泣，因請假還，中路果得父凶問，便徒跣號啕，出陶家後渚，遇商人附載西上，水漿不入口數日。 嘗遇病，與母隔壁，忍病不言，齧被至碎，恐母之哀己也。 湘州刺史王僧虔引為主簿，以同僚非人，棄官去。 吏部郎庾杲之嘗往候，頤之為設食，唯枯魚菜葅杲之曰：「我不能食此。」 母聞之，自出常膳魚羹數種。 杲之曰：「卿過於茅季偉，我非郭林宗。」 仕至郢州中從事。
Yue Yizhi, styled Wende, was a native of Niyang in Nanyang whose family lived in Nan commandery; from youth his conduct was harmonious and careful. He served as a capital staff officer; his father died of illness in Ying. Yizhi suddenly grieved and wept, took leave and returned; midway he received news of his father's death, went barefoot wailing, left by Taojia ford, boarded a merchant boat west, and took no food or water for days. Once ill in a room next to his mother's, he bore it in silence, biting the quilt to pieces lest she grieve for him. Wang Sengqian, inspector of Xiangzhou, recruited him as chief clerk; finding a colleague unworthy he resigned. Geng Guozhi of the Ministry of Personnel once visited; Yizhi served only dried fish and pickles; Guozhi said, "I cannot eat this. His mother heard and brought out her usual fare of several fish soups. Guozhi said, "You surpass Mao Jiwei—I am not Guo Linzong. He rose to attendant secretary in Yingzhou.
89
弟預字文介，亦至孝。 父臨亡，執手以托郢州行事王奐。 預悲感悶絕，吐血數升，遂發病。 官至驃騎錄事參軍。
His younger brother Yu, styled Wenjie, was also utterly filial. When their father was dying he took Yu's hand and entrusted him to Wang Huan, acting officer of Yingzhou. Yu grieved and fainted, spat blood, and fell ill. He rose to recording secretary of the Rapid Cavalry.
90
隆昌末，預謂丹陽尹徐孝嗣曰：「外傳藉藉，似有伊、周之事。 君蒙武帝殊常之恩，荷託付之重，恐不得同人此事。 人笑褚公，至今齒冷，無為效尤。」 孝嗣故吏吳興沈升之亦說之曰：「升之與君俱有項領之功，今一言而二功俱解，豈願聞之乎。 君受恩二祖，而更參惟新之政，以君為反復人，事成則無處逃咎矣。 升之草萊百姓，言出禍已隨之，孰與超然謝病，高枕家園，則與松柏比操，風霜等烈，豈不美邪。」 孝嗣並改容謝之。
At the end of Longchang Yu told Xu Xiaosi of Danyang, "Rumors spread—as if an Yi Yin and Duke of Zhou affair were afoot. You received extraordinary grace from Emperor Wu and a heavy trust—I fear you cannot join this affair. People still mock Lord Chu—do not imitate him. His former clerk Shen Shengzhi also said, "We both have merit at stake; one word and both achievements vanish—will you hear it You received grace from two emperors yet join the new regime—as a turncoat, when it succeeds you cannot escape blame. I am a common man—words bring calamity; better to retire ill, rest at home, match pine and cypress in integrity—is that not better" Xiaosi changed expression and thanked them both.
91
預建武中為永世令，人懷其德，卒官。 時有一媼年可六七十，擔檞蔌葉造市貨之，聞預亡大泣，棄溪中，曰：「失樂令，我輩孤獨老姥政應就死耳。」 市人亦皆泣，其惠化如此。
In Jianwu Yu was magistrate of Yongshi; people cherished his virtue; he died in office. An old woman of sixty or seventy carried mulberry leaves to market; hearing Yu had died she wept, threw them in the stream, and said, "Losing Magistrate Yue, we old women ought to die. Market people wept as well; such was his benevolent influence.
92
江泌字士清，濟陽考城人也。 父亮之，員外郎。 泌少貧，晝日斫屧為業，夜讀書隨月光，光斜則握卷升屋，睡極墮地則更登。 性行仁義，衣弊虱多，綿裹置壁上，恐虱饑死，乃復置衣中。 數日間，終身無復虱。 母亡後，以生闕供養，遇鮭不忍食。 菜不食心，以其有生意，唯食老葉而已。 母墓為野火所燒，依「新宮災，三日哭」。 淚盡系之以血。
Jiang Bi, styled Shiqing, was a native of Kaocheng in Jiyang. His father Liangzhi was an outside officer. Bi was poor in youth; by day he cut sandals; at night he read by moonlight—when light slanted he climbed the roof with his scroll; when he fell asleep and tumbled down he climbed up again. His clothes were ragged with lice; he wrapped them in cotton on the wall, fearing lice would starve, then put them back in his clothes. Within days he had no lice for life. After his mother died, lacking offerings in her lifetime, he could not bear to eat salmon. He did not eat vegetable hearts because they had life; he ate only old leaves. His mother's tomb was burned by wildfire; following the rite of three days' weeping when the new palace burns— when tears were exhausted he wept blood.
93
曆仕南中郎行參軍，所給募吏去役，得時病，莫有舍之者。 吏扶杖投泌，泌自隱恤。 吏死，泌為買棺。 無僮役，兄弟共輿埋之。 後領國子助教，乘牽車至染烏頭，見一老公步行，下車載之，躬自步去染。 武帝以為南康王子琳侍讀。
He served as marching staff officer; a conscript clerk left service and fell ill; none would shelter him. The clerk came leaning on a staff; Bi sheltered and cared for him. When the clerk died Bi bought a coffin. With no servants the brothers carried the bier and buried him. Later as National University assistant instructor he rode an ox cart to Wutou dye works, saw an old man walking, gave him the cart and walked himself. Emperor Wu made him attendant reader to Prince Lin of Nankang.
94
建武中，明帝害諸王，後泌憂念子琳，訪志公道人，問其禍福。 志公覆香爐灰示之曰：「都盡無餘。」 及子琳被害，泌往哭之，淚盡續以血，親視殯葬畢乃去。 泌尋卒。 族人兗州中從事泌，黃門郎悆子也，與泌同名，世謂泌為「孝泌」以別之。
In Jianwu Emperor Ming killed the princes; Bi later worried for Prince Lin and asked the monk Zhigong about his fate. Zhigong overturned censer ash and said, "All exhausted, nothing left. When Prince Lin was killed Bi wailed; when tears ended he wept blood; he watched the burial before leaving. Bi soon died. Clansman Bi, attendant secretary in Yanzhou and son of Gentleman Yu, shared the name; the world called this one Filial Bi to distinguish them.
95
庾道湣，潁川鄢陵人，晉司空冰之玄孫也。 有孝行，頗能屬文。 少出孤悴，時人莫知。 其所生母流漂交州，道湣尚在繈褓。 及長知之，求為廣州綏寧府佐。 至南而去交州尚遠，乃自負擔冒嶮，僅得自達。 及至交州，尋求母雖經年，日夜悲泣。 嘗入村，日暮雨驟，乃寄止一家。 旦有一嫗負薪外還，而道湣心動，因訪之，乃其母也。 於是行伏號泣，遠近赴之，莫不揮淚。
Yu Daomin was a native of Yanling in Yingchuan, great-great-grandson of Minister Bing of Jin. He had filial conduct and could compose literary pieces. Orphaned young and unknown, people of the time did not know him. His birth mother was swept to Jiaozhou; Daomin was still in swaddling clothes. When grown he knew and sought a post in Guangzhou's Suining prefecture. Reaching the south he was still far from Jiaozhou; he bore his own load through danger and barely arrived. Upon reaching Jiaozhou he sought his mother for years, weeping day and night. Once entering a village at dusk a storm came and he lodged in a household. At dawn an old woman returned with firewood; Daomin's heart stirred; he inquired—it was his mother. He lay prostrate wailing; people near and far came and all wept.
96
道湣尤精相板，宋明帝時，山陽王休佑屢以言語忤顏，見道湣，托以己板為他物，令道湣占之。 道湣曰：「此乃甚貴，然使人多愆忤。」 休佑以褚彥回詳密，求換其板。 他日，彥回侍明帝，自稱下官。 帝多忌，甚不悅。 休佑具以狀言，帝乃意解。
Daomin was skilled at reading fate boards; Prince Xiuyou of Shanyang repeatedly offended Emperor Ming; he showed Daomin another's board disguised as his own. Daomin said, "This is very noble, yet it brings many offenses and disobedience. Xiuyou, because Chu Yanhui was careful, sought to exchange boards with him. Another day Yanhui attended Emperor Ming and called himself a subordinate official. The emperor was suspicious and very displeased. Xiuyou fully explained; the emperor's mind was eased.
97
道湣仕齊，位射聲校尉。 族孫沙彌亦以孝行著。
Daomin served Qi and reached Colonel Who Shoots Sound. Clansman Shamini was also famed for filial conduct.
98
沙彌，晉司空冰之六世孫也。 父佩玉，仕宋位長沙內史，升明中，坐沈攸之事誅。 時沙彌始生。 及年五歲，所生母為制采衣，輒不肯服。 母問其故，流涕對曰：「家門禍酷，用是何為？」 及長，終身布衣蔬食。 為中軍田曹行參軍。 嫡母劉氏寢疾，沙彌晨昏侍側，衣不解帶。 或應針灸，輒以身先試。 及母亡，水漿不入口累日。 初進大麥薄飲，經十旬方為薄粥。 終喪不食鹽酢，冬日不衣綿纊，夏日不解衰絰。 不出廬戶，晝夜號慟，鄰人不忍聞。 所坐薦，淚沾為爛。 墓在新林，忽生旅松百許株，枝葉鬱茂，有異常松。 劉好噉甘蔗，沙彌遂不食焉。 宗人都官尚書詠表言其狀，應純孝之舉，梁武帝召見嘉之，以補歙令。 還除輕車邵陵王參軍事，隨府會稽，復丁所生母憂，喪還都，濟浙江，中流遇風，舫將覆沒。 沙彌抱柩號哭，俄而風靜，咸以為孝感所致。 後卒于長城令。 子持。
Shamini was sixth-generation descendant of Minister Bing of Jin. His father Peiyu served Song as interior secretary of Changsha; in Shengming he was executed in Shen Youzhi's affair. At the time Shamini had just been born. At five his birth mother made him colored clothes; he refused to wear them. His mother asked why; he answered weeping, "Our house has suffered cruel calamity—what is this for When grown he wore plain cloth and ate simple fare for life. He was marching staff officer in the Central Army's Field Bureau. His legal mother Lady Liu lay gravely ill; Shamini attended day and night and never loosened his belt. When acupuncture was needed he tried it on himself first. When his mother died he took no water or broth for many days. At first he took only thin barley drink; only after a hundred days did he take thin gruel. Throughout mourning he ate no salt or vinegar; in winter he wore no cotton; in summer he did not remove mourning garb. He did not leave the mourning hut, wailing day and night; neighbors could not bear to hear. The mat he sat on was rotted by his tears. The tomb was at Xinlin; suddenly a hundred lodge pines grew, lush and unlike ordinary pines. Lady Liu loved sugarcane; Shamini then never ate it. Clansman Yong memorialized him for the pure filial piety recommendation; Emperor Wu of Liang summoned him, praised him, and appointed him magistrate of She. Later staff officer to the Prince of Shaoling; in Kuaiji he mourned his birth mother again; returning across the Zhe midstream a storm nearly capsized the boat. Shamini embraced the coffin wailing; soon the wind stilled; all attributed it to filial devotion. He later died as magistrate of Changcheng. His son Chi.
99
持字元德，少孤，性至孝，父憂，居喪過禮。 篤志好學，仕梁為尚書左戶郎，後兼建康監。 陳文帝為吳興太守，以為郡丞，兼掌書翰。 天嘉初，為尚書左丞，封崇德縣子。 拜封之日，請令史為客，受其餉遺，文帝怒之，因坐免。 後為臨安令，坐杖殺人免封。 還為給事黃門侍郎，曆鹽官令，秘書監，知國史事。 又為少府卿，遷太中大夫，領步兵校尉，卒。 持善字書，每屬辭，好為奇字，文士亦以此譏之。 有集十卷。
Chi, styled Yuande, was orphaned young and utterly filial; in his father's mourning he exceeded the rites. Devoted and scholarly, he served Liang as Left Household Gentleman and later superintendent of Jiankang. When Emperor Wen of Chen was prefect of Wuxing he made him commandery aide and charged him with documents. Early in Tianjia he was Left Assistant Minister and enfeoffed as Viscount of Chongde. On the day of enfeoffment he invited a clerk as guest and accepted a gift; Emperor Wen was angry and dismissed him. Later magistrate of Lin'an; he lost his title for beating a man to death. He returned as Attendant Gentleman at the Yellow Gate, served as salt monopoly magistrate, Director of the Secretariat, and oversaw national history. Also Minister of Palace Supplies, promoted to Grand Master of Palace Service and Colonel of Foot Soldiers; he died. Chi was skilled in calligraphy; in composition he loved strange characters, which literary men mocked. He had a collection of ten scrolls.
