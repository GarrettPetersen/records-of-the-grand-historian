○外戚
Imperial Affines
3
曆觀前代外戚之家，乘母后之權以取高位厚秩者多矣，然而鮮有克終之美，必罹顛覆之患，何哉？ 皆由乎無德而尊，不知紀極，忽于滿盈之戒，罔念高危之咎，故鬼瞰其室，憂必及之。 夫其誠著艱難，功宣社稷，不以謙沖自牧，未免顛蹶之禍，而況道不足以濟時，仁不足以利物，自矜於己，以富貴驕人者乎？ 此呂、霍、上官、閻、梁、竇、鄧所以繼踵而亡滅者也。 昔文皇潛躍之際，獻後便相推轂，煬帝大橫方兆，蕭妃密勿經綸，是以恩禮綢繆，始終不易。 然內外親戚，莫預朝權，昆弟在位，亦無殊寵。 至於居擅玉堂，家稱金穴，暉光戚裡，重灼四方，將三司以比儀，命五侯而同拜者，終始一代，寂無聞焉。 考之前王，可謂矯其弊矣。 故雖時經擾攘，無有陷於不義，市朝遷貿，而皆得以保全。 比夫憑藉寵私，階緣恩澤，乘其非據，旋就顛隕者，豈可同日而言哉！ 此所謂愛之以禮，能改覆車。 輒敘其事，為《外戚傳》雲。
A survey of past dynasties shows that many families of imperial affines used the empress dowager's authority to win high office and lavish stipends, yet few enjoyed a happy end and nearly all met ruin—why is this? It is because they were exalted without virtue, knew no bounds, ignored the warning against excess, and forgot the peril of standing too high—so that, as the saying goes, when ghosts gaze upon a house, grief is sure to follow. Even men whose loyalty was proved in adversity and whose service to the state was real, if they did not govern themselves with humility, could not escape disaster—how much less those whose conduct could not save the times, whose kindness could not benefit others, yet who prided themselves on wealth and rank? This is why the houses of Lü, Huo, Shangguan, Yan, Liang, Dou, and Deng fell one after another. When Emperor Wen was still making his rise, Empress Xian already helped drive his cause forward; when Emperor Yang's great mandate was only beginning to show, Consort Xiao took a close hand in affairs of state. For this reason imperial favor and ceremony remained constant from first to last. Yet neither the maternal nor the paternal kin shared in court power, and brothers who held office received no extraordinary favor. As for the abuses of old—monopolizing the halls of state, households famed as treasure vaults, kin whose splendor dazzled the realm, treating the Three Excellencies as equals and commanding the Five Marquises to bow as one—through the entire Sui dynasty one hears nothing of the kind. Compared with earlier rulers, the Sui emperors may be said to have corrected this abuse. Thus even when the realm was in turmoil, none of the affinal kin were drawn into disloyalty; though power changed hands in court and market, they all survived. How can they be mentioned in the same breath as those who leaned on private favor, climbed by imperial grace, seized what was not theirs, and were soon overthrown! This is what is meant by loving kin with proper restraint and learning from the wreck of the overturned cart. I therefore set out these affairs in the 《Biographies of Imperial Affines》.

○高祖外家呂氏
The Lü Clan, Gaozu's Maternal Kin
5
高祖外家呂氏，其族蓋微，平齊之後，求訪不知所在。 至開皇初，濟南郡上言，有男子呂永吉，自稱有姑字苦桃，為楊忠妻。 勘驗知是舅子，始追贈外祖雙周為上柱國、太尉、八州諸軍事、青州刺史，封齊郡公，諡曰敬，外祖母姚氏為齊敬公夫人。 詔並改葬，于齊州立廟，置守塚十家。 以永吉襲爵，留在京師。 大業中，授上党郡太守，性識庸劣，職務不理。 後去官，不知所終。
Gaozu's mother's family, the Lü clan, was of humble standing; after the conquest of Qi, inquiries failed to locate them. At the beginning of Kaihuang, Jinan commandery reported that a man named Lü Yongji claimed an aunt, styled Kutao, who had been the wife of Yang Zhong. Investigation confirmed him as a maternal nephew. Gaozu then posthumously honored his maternal grandfather Shuang Zhou as Supreme Pillar of State, Grand Commandant, commander of military affairs in eight provinces, and Governor of Qingzhou, enfeoffing him as Duke of Qi with the posthumous title Jing; his maternal grandmother, the Yao clan, was titled Lady of Duke Jing of Qi. An edict ordered their remains reinterred, a temple built in Qi province, and ten households assigned to tend the graves. Yongji inherited the title and remained at court. During Daye he was appointed governor of Shangdang commandery, but his mind was dull and he neglected his office. He later left office, and nothing more is known of him.
6
永吉從父道貴，性尤頑騃，言詞鄙陋。 初自鄉里征入長安，上見之悲泣。 道貴略無戚容，但連呼高祖名，雲：「種末定不可偷，大似苦桃姊。 」是後數犯忌諱，動致違忤，上甚耽之。 乃命高熲厚加供給，不許接對朝士。 拜上儀同三司，出為濟南太守，令即之任，斷其入朝。 道貴還至本郡，高自崇重，每與人言，自稱皇舅。 數將儀衛出入閭裡，從故人游宴，官民鹹苦之。 後郡廢，終於家，子孫無聞焉。
Yongji's uncle Daogui was even more obstinate and slow-witted, and his speech was coarse. When he was first summoned from the countryside to Chang'an, the emperor wept at the sight of him. Daogui showed little sign of emotion, but kept calling the emperor by his personal name and said, "The breed isn't settled yet—you can't steal that. You're just like Elder Sister Kutao." Thereafter he repeatedly violated taboo and gave offense at every turn, until the emperor could bear him no longer. The emperor ordered Gao Jiong to provide for him generously but forbade him to associate with officials at court. He was appointed Senior Palace Attendant of the Third Rank and sent out as governor of Jinan with orders to take up his post immediately and never return to court. Back in his home commandery, Daogui held himself in high regard and told everyone he met that he was the emperor's maternal uncle. He often paraded with guards through the streets, caroused with old friends, and officials and commoners alike found him unbearable. When the commandery was later abolished, he died at home, and nothing is known of his descendants.

獨孤羅弟陀
Dugu Luo and His Younger Brother Tuo
8
獨孤羅，字羅仁，雲中人也。 父信，初仕魏為荊州刺史。 武帝之入關也，信棄父母妻子西歸長安，曆職顯貴，羅由是遂為高氏所囚。 信後仕周為大司馬。 及信為宇文護所誅，羅始見釋，寓居中山，孤貧無以自給。 齊將獨孤永業以宗族之故，見而哀之，為買田宅，遺以資畜。 初，信入關之後，複娶二妻，郭氏生子六人，善、穆、藏、順、陀、整，崔氏生獻皇后。 及齊亡，高祖為定州總管，獻皇后遣人尋羅，得之，相見悲不自勝，侍御者皆泣。 於是厚遺車馬財物。 未幾，周武帝以羅功臣子，久淪異域，征拜楚安郡太守。 以疾去官，歸於京師。 諸弟見羅少長貧賤，每輕侮之，不以兄禮事也。 然性長者，亦不與諸弟校競長短，後由是重之。 及高祖為丞相，拜儀同，常置左右。 既受禪，下詔追贈羅父信官爵曰：「褒德累行，往代通規，追遠慎終，前王盛典。 故柱國信，風宇高曠，獨秀生民，睿哲居宗，清猷映世。 宏謀長策，道著於弼諧，緯義經仁，事深於拯濟。 方當宣風廊廟，亮采臺階，而運屬艱危，功高弗賞，眷言令範，事切於心。 今景運初開，椒闈肅建，載懷塗山之義，無忘褒、紀之典。 可贈太師、上柱國、冀定等十州刺史、趙國公，邑萬戶。 」其諸弟以羅母沒齊，先無夫人之號，不當承襲。 上以問後，後曰：「羅誠嫡長，不可誣也。 」於是襲爵趙國公。 以其弟善為河內郡公，穆為金泉縣公，藏為武平縣公，陀為武喜縣公，整為千牛備身。 擢拜羅為左領左右將軍，尋遷左衛將軍，前後賞賜不可勝計。 久而出為涼州總管，進位上柱國。 仁壽中，征拜左武衛大將軍。 煬帝嗣位，改封蜀國公。 未幾，卒官，諡曰恭。
Dugu Luo, styled Luoren, was a native of Yunzhong. His father Xin had first served Northern Wei as governor of Jing province. When Emperor Wu of Northern Zhou entered the passes, Xin left his parents, wife, and children behind and went west to Chang'an, rising to high office; Luo was therefore left behind and imprisoned by the Gao clan of Northern Qi. Xin later served the Zhou court as Grand Marshal. After Xin was executed by Yuwen Hu, Luo was at last released. He lived in Zhongshan, alone and destitute, with no means of support. The Northern Qi general Dugu Yongye, a kinsman, took pity on him, bought him land and a house, and gave him livestock and supplies. After Xin entered the passes he had taken two more wives: Lady Guo bore six sons—Shan, Mu, Zang, Shun, Tuo, and Zheng—and Lady Cui bore the future Empress Xian. When Northern Qi fell, Gaozu was regional inspector of Dingzhou. Empress Xian sent men to find Luo; when they met, her grief was overwhelming and all who attended wept. She then sent him generous gifts of carriages, horses, and goods. Soon afterward Emperor Wu of Zhou, noting that Luo was the son of a meritorious minister long stranded in enemy territory, summoned him and appointed him governor of Chu'an commandery. Illness forced him to resign and return to the capital. His younger brothers, knowing he had grown up poor and obscure, often slighted him and did not treat him with the respect owed an elder brother. But he was by nature forbearing and did not contend with his brothers; for this they later came to respect him. When Gaozu became chancellor, Luo was appointed Palace Attendant and kept constantly at his side. After Gaozu took the throne, he issued an edict posthumously honoring Luo's father Xin, which read: "To praise accumulated virtue is the constant practice of former ages; to honor the dead and complete the rites is the great ceremony of ancient kings." "The former Pillar of State Xin bore himself with lofty grace, stood apart among men, held sagely wisdom at the clan's center, and whose pure designs illuminated his age." "His grand designs and far-reaching policies made his Way manifest in aiding the throne; in weaving righteousness and threading benevolence, his service went deep in rescuing the realm." "Just as he was to spread his influence in the halls of state and display his brilliance on the steps of power, fate turned harsh; his merit went unrewarded—yet memory of his fine example still moves the heart." "Now, as a new reign begins and the inner palace is duly established, mindful of the example of Mount Tu and unforgetting the rites owed to worthy forebears." "—let him be posthumously honored as Grand Preceptor, Supreme Pillar of State, governor of ten provinces including Ji and Ding, and Duke of Zhao, with a fief of ten thousand households." " His younger brothers argued that because Luo's mother had died in Qi without ever receiving the title of Lady, he ought not inherit the dukedom. The emperor asked the empress, who replied, "Luo is truly the eldest legitimate son—that cannot be denied." " Luo therefore inherited the title Duke of Zhao. His brother Shan was made Duke of Henei, Mu Duke of Jinquan county, Zang Duke of Wuping county, Tuo Duke of Wuxi county, and Zheng a guard of the Thousand-Ox. Luo was promoted to General of the Left Retinue, soon transferred to General of the Left Guard, and showered with rewards beyond counting. After some time he was sent out as regional inspector of Liangzhou and advanced to Supreme Pillar of State. During Renshou he was recalled and appointed Grand General of the Left Martial Guard. When Emperor Yang succeeded to the throne, Luo's title was changed to Duke of Shu. He soon died in office and was given the posthumous title Gong.
9
子纂嗣，仕至河陽郡尉。 纂弟武都，大業末，亦為河陽郡尉。 庶長子開遠，宇文化及之弑逆也，裴虔通率賊入成象殿，宿衛兵士皆從逆，開遠時為千牛，與獨孤盛力戰於閤下，為賊所執，賊義而舍之。 善後官至柱國。 卒，子覽嗣，仕至左候衛將軍，大業末卒。
His son Zuan inherited the title and rose to commandant of Heyang commandery. Zuan's younger brother Wudu likewise became commandant of Heyang commandery at the end of Daye. His eldest son by a concubine, Kaiyuan, was serving as a guard of the Thousand-Ox when Yuwen Huaji murdered the emperor. Pei Qiantong led the rebels into the Chengxian Hall, and the palace guards joined the revolt. Kaiyuan and Dugu Sheng fought fiercely below the gate-tower, were captured, but the rebels, respecting their courage, let them go. Shan later rose to Pillar of State. After his death his son Lan inherited and rose to General of the Left Garrison Guard; he died at the end of Daye.
10
獨孤陀，字黎邪。 仕周胥附上士，坐父徙蜀郡十餘年。 宇文護被誅，始歸長安。 高祖受禪，拜上開府、右領左右將軍。 久之，出為郢州刺史，進位上大將軍，累轉延州刺史。 好左道。 其妻母先事貓鬼，因轉入其家。 上微聞而不之信也。 會獻皇后及楊素妻鄭氏俱有疾，召醫者視之，皆曰：「此貓鬼疾也。 」上以陀後之異母弟，陀妻楊素之異母妹，由是意陀所為，陰令其兄穆以情喻之。 上又避左右諷陀，陀言無有。 上不悅，左轉遷州刺史。 出怨言。 上令左僕射高熲、納言蘇威、大理正皇甫孝緒、大理丞楊遠等雜治之。 陀婢徐阿尼言，本從陀母家來，常事貓鬼。 每以子日夜祀之。 言子者鼠也。 其貓鬼每殺人者，所死家財物潛移於畜貓鬼家。 陀嘗從家中素酒，其妻曰：「無錢可酤。 」陀因謂阿尼曰：「可令貓鬼向越公家，使我足錢也。 」阿尼便咒之歸。 數日，貓鬼向素家。 十一年，上初從并州還，陀於園中謂阿尼曰：「可令貓鬼向皇后所，使多賜吾物。 」阿尼複咒之，遂入宮中。 楊遠乃于門下外省遣阿尼呼貓鬼。 阿尼於是夜中置香粥一盆，以匙扣而呼之曰：「貓女可來，無住宮中。 」久之，阿尼色正青，若被牽曳者，雲貓鬼已至。 上以其事下公卿，奇章公牛弘曰：「妖由人興，殺其人可以絕矣。 」上令以犢車載陀夫妻，將賜死於其家。 陀弟司勳侍中整詣闕求哀，於是免陀死，除名為民，以其妻楊氏為尼。 先是，有人訟其母為人貓鬼所殺者，上以為妖妄，怒而遣之。 及此，詔誅被訟行貓鬼家。 經未幾而卒。 煬帝即位，追念舅氏，聽以禮葬，乃下詔曰：「外氏衰禍，獨孤陀不幸早世，遷卜有期。 言念渭陽之情，追懷傷切，宜加禮命，允備哀榮。 可贈正議大夫。 」帝意猶不已，複下詔曰：「舅氏之尊，戚屬斯重，而降年弗永，凋落相繼。 緬惟先往，宜崇徽秩。 複贈銀青光祿大夫。 」有二子：延福、延壽。
Dugu Tuo, styled Lixie. He served the Zhou court as a senior attendant of the palace retinue and, because of his father's case, was banished to Shu commandery for more than ten years. After Yuwen Hu was executed, he was at last allowed to return to Chang'an. When Gaozu took the throne, Tuo was appointed Senior Opening Grandee and General of the Right Retinue. After some time he was sent out as inspector of Ying province, advanced to Grand General, and later transferred to inspector of Yan province. He was devoted to heterodox cult practices. His wife's mother had first practiced the cat-demon cult, and the rite passed into his household. The emperor heard rumors but did not believe them. When Empress Xian and Yang Su's wife Lady Zheng both fell ill, physicians were summoned and all declared, "This is cat-demon sickness." " The emperor, noting that Tuo was the empress's younger half-brother and that Tuo's wife was Yang Su's younger half-sister, suspected Tuo and secretly had his brother Mu reason with him. The emperor also spoke to him privately, hinting at the charge, but Tuo denied everything. Displeased, the emperor demoted him to inspector of Qian province. He began to voice complaints. The emperor ordered Left Vice Director Gao Jiong, Supporter of Speech Su Wei, Chief of the Court of Judicial Review Huangfu Xiaoxu, Vice Chief Yang Yuan, and others to investigate jointly. Tuo's maid Xu Ani testified that she had come from his mother's household and had long served the cat-demon cult. Every night at the hour of zi she performed the rite. The word zi here refers to the rat. Whenever the cat-demon killed someone, the victim's wealth was secretly transferred to the household that kept the demon. Tuo once asked for wine at home; his wife replied, "We have no money to buy any." " Tuo then told Ani, "Send the cat-demon to the Duke of Yue's house so we have enough money." " Ani immediately chanted the demon home. Within days the cat-demon was directed at Yang Su's household. In the eleventh year, soon after the emperor returned from Bingzhou, Tuo told Ani in the garden, "Send the cat-demon to the empress's quarters so she bestows more gifts on me." " Ani chanted again, and the demon entered the palace. Yang Yuan then had Ani summon the cat-demon at the outer office of the Gates Department. At midnight Ani set out a basin of fragrant porridge, tapped it with a spoon, and called, "Cat-girl, come—but do not remain in the palace." " After a long while Ani's face turned ashen, as if she were being pulled, and she said the cat-demon had arrived. The emperor referred the case to the high ministers. Duke Niuzhang Niu Hong said, "Such evil arises through men—execute the man and the matter ends." " The emperor ordered Tuo and his wife placed on a cart and sent home to be executed. Tuo's younger brother Zheng, Palace Attendant of the Ministry of Personnel, pleaded at court for mercy. Tuo's death sentence was remitted; he was stripped of rank and made a commoner, and his wife Lady Yang was forced to become a nun. Earlier, a man had sued, claiming his mother was killed by another household's cat-demon; the emperor judged it superstitious nonsense, grew angry, and dismissed the case. Now an edict ordered the household that had been sued for practicing the cat-demon to be executed. Before long Tuo died. When Emperor Yang took the throne, mindful of his mother's kin, he permitted a proper burial and issued an edict: "Our maternal clan has fallen on hard times; Dugu Tuo met an untimely death, and the date for reinterment is now set." Mindful of the bond between uncle and nephew, my grief cuts deep; additional honors are fitting, that mourning may be complete and dignified. Let him be posthumously granted the title Regular Counselor. " The emperor's grief was not yet spent, and he issued another edict: "Maternal uncles hold a place of honor, and affinal kin weigh heavily in the heart—yet their years are cut short, and one after another they fall away." Recalling those who have gone before, his posthumous rank should be elevated. He was again posthumously granted Silver-Gleaming Glory Grand Master of the Palace. " He left two sons: Yanfu and Yanshou.

Tuo's younger brother Zheng rose to governor of Youzhou; he died early in the Daye era and was posthumously granted Gold-Purple Glory Grand Master and enfeoffed as Marquis of Pingxiang.

The Sons of Xiao Kui: Xiao Cong; Xiao Cong's Younger Brother Xiao Huan
13
蕭巋，字仁遠，梁昭明太子統之孫也。 父詧，初封岳陽王，鎮襄陽。 侯景之亂，其兄河東王譽與其叔父湘東王繹不協，為繹所害。 及繹嗣位，詧稱籓於西魏，乞師請討繹。 周太祖以詧為梁主，遣柱國於謹等率騎五萬襲繹，滅之。 詧遂都江陵，有荊郡、其西平州延袤三百里之地，稱皇帝于其國，車服節文一同王者。 仍置江陵總管，以兵戍之。 詧薨，巋嗣位，年號天保。 巋俊辯有才學，兼好內典。 周武帝平齊之後，巋來賀，帝享之甚歡。 親彈琵琶，令巋起舞，巋曰：「陛下親禦五糸玄，臣敢不同百獸！ 」高祖受禪，恩禮彌厚，遣使賜金五百兩，銀千兩，布帛萬匹，馬五百匹。 巋來朝，上甚敬焉，詔巋位在王公之上。 巋被服端麗，進退閒雅，天子矚目，百僚傾慕。 賞賜以億計。 月餘歸籓，帝親餞于滻水之上。 後備禮納其女為晉王妃，又欲以其子瑒尚蘭陵公主。 由是漸見親待。 獻皇后言于上曰：「梁主通家，腹心所寄，何勞猜防也。 」上然之，於是罷江陵總管，巋專制其國。 歲餘，巋又來朝，賜縑萬匹，珍玩稱是。 及還，上親執手曰：「梁主久滯荊楚，未復舊都，故鄉之念，良軫懷抱。 朕當振旅長江，相送旋反耳。 」巋拜謝而去。 其年五月，寢疾，臨終上表曰：「臣以庸暗，曲荷天慈，寵冠外籓，恩逾連山，爰及子女，尚主婚王。 每願躬擐甲胄，身先士卒，掃蕩逋寇，上報明時。 而攝生乖舛，遽罹屙疾，屬纊在辰，顧陰待謝。 長違聖世，感戀嗚咽，遺嗣孤藐，特乞降慈。 伏願聖躬與山嶽同固，皇基等天日俱永，臣雖九泉，實無遣恨。 」並獻所服金裝劍，上覽而嗟悼焉。 巋在位二十三年，年四十四薨，梁之臣子諡曰孝明皇帝，廟號世宗。 子琮嗣。 巋著《孝經》、《周易義記》及《大小乘幽微》十四卷，行於世。
Xiao Kui, styled Renyuan, was a grandson of the Liang Crown Prince Zhaoming, Xiao Tong. His father Xiao Cha was first enfeoffed Prince of Yueyang and posted to garrison Xiangyang. During Hou Jing's rebellion, Kui's elder brother Prince Yu of Hedong fell out with their uncle Prince Xiao Yi of Xiangdong, and Yu was killed by Yi. When Yi took the throne, Cha submitted as a vassal to Western Wei, begged for troops, and asked permission to attack Yi. The Zhou Grand Ancestor installed Cha as ruler of Liang and sent Pillar of State Yu Jin with fifty thousand cavalry to strike Yi and destroy him. Cha then moved his capital to Jiangling, holding Jing commandery and Xiping prefecture to the west—some three hundred li of territory—and proclaimed himself emperor in his realm, with carriage, dress, and ceremonial regalia matching those of a sovereign. Zhou still posted an area commander at Jiangling to hold the region with troops. When Cha died, Kui succeeded him and took the era name Tianbao. Kui was handsome and eloquent, gifted with learning, and devoted to Buddhist scripture. After Emperor Wu of Zhou conquered Qi, Kui came to offer congratulations; the emperor received him with great delight. He played the pipa himself and bade Kui dance. Kui said, "Your Majesty plays the five-string lute himself; how dare I not join the hundred beasts in their dance!" " When Emperor Gaozu took the throne, his favor toward Kui grew still deeper; he sent envoys with five hundred taels of gold, a thousand taels of silver, ten thousand bolts of silk, and five hundred horses. When Kui came to court, the emperor treated him with exceptional respect and decreed that he should rank above kings and dukes. Kui dressed with elegant refinement and moved with easy grace; the emperor's eyes followed him, and the whole court looked on in admiration. Rewards and gifts ran into the hundreds of millions. After more than a month he returned to his domain; the emperor personally saw him off on the banks of the Ba River. Later the court took his daughter in full ceremony as consort to the Prince of Jin and planned to marry his son Yang to Princess Lanling. From this he gradually won still closer favor. Empress Xian told the emperor, "The Lord of Liang is family by marriage, a man you trust with your innermost counsel—why burden yourself with suspicion?" " The emperor agreed; the Jiangling area commander was removed, and Kui held sole authority over his realm. A year later Kui came to court again and received ten thousand bolts of fine silk and treasures in proportion. As he departed, the emperor took his hand and said, "You have long been held in Jing-Chu and have not regained your old capital; I know how deeply you long for home." I shall march an army to the Yangzi and see you safely restored to your home. " Kui bowed in thanks and took his leave. That year in the fifth month he fell ill; on his deathbed he submitted a memorial: "Your servant, unworthy and dull, has yet been showered with Heaven's grace; no vassal prince has known such favor, nor such kindness as yours to my children, given in marriage to imperial princesses and princes." I had long wished to don armor myself, lead from the front, sweep away the rebels, and repay your enlightened reign. Yet my care for life went awry; suddenly wasting illness struck—the death-shroud is near, and I await my end in darkness. I must leave your sacred age, and grief chokes my voice; my orphaned heirs are young and helpless—I beg your mercy upon them. I pray that Your Majesty's person may stand firm as the mountains, and the imperial foundation endure as Heaven and sun; though I go to the grave, I shall have no regret. " He also presented the gold-mounted sword he had worn. The emperor read the memorial and mourned deeply. Kui had reigned twenty-three years and died at forty-four; Liang's ministers gave him the posthumous title Emperor Xiaoming, with temple name Shizong. His son Xiao Cong succeeded him. Kui wrote the Classic of Filial Piety, Record of the Meanings of the Book of Changes, and Subtleties of the Greater and Lesser Vehicles in fourteen scrolls, all of which circulated widely.

琮字溫文，性寬仁，有大度，倜儻不羈，博學有文義。 兼善弓馬，遣人伏地著帖，琮馳馬射之，十發十中，持帖者亦不懼。 初封東陽王，尋立為梁太子。 及嗣位，上賜璽書曰：「負荷堂構，其事甚重，雖窮憂勞，常須自力。 輯諧內外，親任才良，聿遵世業，是所望也。 彼之疆守，咫尺陳人，水潦之時，特宜警備。 陳氏比日雖複朝聘相尋，疆埸之間猶未清肅，唯當恃我必不可幹，勿得輕人而不設備。 朕與梁國，積世相知，重以親姻，情義彌厚。 江陵之地，朝寄非輕，為國為民，深宜抑割，恆加饘粥，以禮自存。 」又賜梁之大臣璽書，誠勉之。 時琮年號廣運，有識者曰：「運之為字，軍走也，吾君將奔走乎？ 」其年，琮遣大將軍戚昕以舟師襲陳公安，不克而還。 征琮叔父岑入朝，拜為大將軍，封懷義公，因留不遣。 複置江陵總管以監之。 琮所署大將軍許世武密以城召陳將宜黃侯陳紀，謀泄，琮誅之。 後二歲，上征琮入朝，率其臣下二百余人朝于京師，江陵父老莫不隕涕相謂曰：「吾君其不反矣！ 」上以琮來朝，遣武鄉公崔弘度將兵戍之。 軍至鄀州，琮叔父岩及弟瓛等懼弘度掩襲之，遂引陳人至城下，虜居民而叛，於是廢梁國。 上遣左僕射高熲安集之，曲赦江陵死罪，給民複十年。 梁二主各給守墓十戶。 拜琮為柱國，賜爵莒國公。 煬帝嗣位，以皇后之故，甚見親重。 拜內史令，改封梁公。 琮之宗族，緦麻以上，並隨才擢用，於是諸蕭昆弟布列朝廷。 琮性淡雅，不以職務自嬰，退朝縱酒而已。 內史令楊約與琮同列，帝令約宣旨誡勵，約複以私情喻之。 琮答曰：「琮若複事事，則何異於公哉！ 」約笑而退。 約兄素，時為尚書令，見琮嫁從父妹于鉗耳氏，因謂琮曰：「公，帝王之族，望高戚美，何乃適妹鉗耳氏乎？ 」琮曰：「前已嫁妹于侯莫陳氏，此複何疑！ 」素曰：「鉗耳，羌也，侯莫陳，虜也，何得相比！ 」素意以虜優羌劣。 琮曰：「以羌異虜，未之前聞。 」素慚而止。 琮雖羈旅，見北間豪貴，無所降下。 嘗與賀若弼深相友善，弼既被誅，複有童謠曰：「蕭蕭亦複起。 」帝由是忌之，遂廢於家，未幾而卒。 贈左光祿大夫。 子鉉，襄城通守。 複以琮弟子钜為梁公。 钜小名藏，煬帝甚昵之，以為千牛，與宇文皛出入宮掖，伺察內外。 帝每有遊宴，钜未嘗不從焉，遂于宮中多行淫穢。 江都之變，為宇文化及所殺。
Xiao Cong, styled Wenwen, was generous and humane by nature, magnanimous, free-spirited and unrestrained, broadly learned, and skilled in letters. He was also skilled with bow and horse. He would have a man lie prone holding a target, then gallop and shoot—ten shots, ten hits—while the man holding the target never flinched. He was first enfeoffed Prince of Dongyang and soon named crown prince of Liang. When he succeeded, the emperor sent an imperial letter: "To bear the burden of the ancestral temple is a grave matter; though care and toil wear you down, you must ever rely on your own strength." Harmonize within and without, personally entrust the talented, and duly uphold your forebears' work—this is what I expect of you. Your border lies a stone's throw from Chen; in flood season you must be especially vigilant. Though Chen has lately resumed embassies, the frontier is not yet secure. Trust that no one may challenge me—but do not therefore slight your neighbor or neglect your defenses. Liang and I have known each other across generations; bound now by marriage, our affection runs still deeper. Jiangling is no light charge from the court; for your state and your people you must restrain yourself deeply, live frugally on plain gruel, and keep yourself by ritual discipline. " He also sent imperial letters to Liang's senior ministers, earnestly exhorting them. Cong's era name was Guangyun; men of insight said, "The character yun contains jun and zou—'army' and 'walk'—will our lord be put to flight?" " That year Cong sent Grand General Qi Xin with a fleet against Chen's An'gong but failed and withdrew. The court summoned Cong's uncle Cen to court, appointed him Grand General, enfeoffed him Duke of Huaiyi, and kept him there. The Jiangling area commander was reinstated to keep watch over him. Grand General Xu Shiwu, whom Cong had appointed, secretly offered the city to Chen's Marquis of Yihuang, Chen Ji; when the plot was exposed, Cong had him executed. Two years later the emperor summoned Cong to court; he led more than two hundred ministers and officials to the capital. The elders of Jiangling wept and said to one another, "Our lord will never come back!" " Because Cong had come to court, the emperor sent Duke of Wuxiang Cui Hongdu with troops to garrison Jiangling. When the army reached Ruozhou, Cong's uncle Yan and his younger brother Huan, fearing a surprise attack by Hongdu, brought Chen troops to the walls, seized the populace, and rebelled; Liang was abolished. The emperor sent Left Vice Director Gao Jiong to pacify the region, specially pardoned capital offenses in Jiangling, and granted the people ten years' tax relief. Each of the two Liang rulers was granted ten households to tend their tombs. Cong was appointed Pillar of State and enfeoffed Duke of Ju. When Emperor Yang succeeded, Cong was greatly favored because of the empress. He was appointed Director of the Palace Secretariat and re-enfeoffed Duke of Liang. Cong's kinsmen, even those in the lowest degree of mourning relationship, were promoted according to ability, and Xiao clansmen filled the court. Cong was refined and detached by nature; he did not burden himself with office and, after court, merely drank his fill. Director Yang Yue held the same rank as Cong; the emperor ordered Yue to convey an admonition, and Yue spoke to him as a friend. Cong replied, "If I attended to every matter, how would I differ from you?" " Yue laughed and withdrew. Yue's elder brother Yang Su was then Director of the Department of State Affairs. Seeing Cong marry a paternal cousin to the Qian'er clan, he said, "You are of an imperial house, of high standing and splendid connections—why give your cousin to the Qian'er clan?" " Cong said, "I already married one sister to the Houmobochen clan—what is there to question now?" " Su said, "The Qian'er are Qiang; the Houmobochen are barbarians—how can you compare them!" " Su meant that barbarians ranked above Qiang. Cong said, "That Qiang differ from barbarians—I have never heard such a thing." " Su, ashamed, said no more. Though Cong lived as a captive guest in the north, he showed no deference before its great families. He had once been close friends with He Ruo Bi; after Bi was executed, a children's rhyme ran, "The Xiao, the Xiao will rise again." " The emperor grew suspicious of him, had him confined at home, and before long he died. He was posthumously granted Left Glory Grand Master of the Palace. His son Xian served as Defender-in-Chief of Xiangcheng. Cong's nephew Ju was again enfeoffed Duke of Liang. Ju, childhood name Cang, was greatly favored by Emperor Yang, appointed to the Thousand-Ox Guard, and with Yuwen Hao moved freely through the inner palace, watching affairs within and without. Whenever the emperor held outings or banquets, Ju was always in attendance, and he committed many lewd acts within the palace. At the Jiangdu upheaval he was killed by Yuwen Huaji.

瓛字欽文，少聰敏，解屬文。 在梁為荊州刺史，頗有能名。 崔弘度以兵至若阝州，瓛懼，與其叔父岩奔于陳。 陳主以為侍中、安東將軍、吳州刺史，甚得物情，三吳父老皆曰：「瓛吾君子也。 」及陳亡，吳人推瓛為主。 吳人見梁武、簡文及詧、巋等兄弟並第三而踐尊位，瓛自以巋之第三子也，深自矜負。 有謝異者，頗知廢興，梁、陳之際，言無不驗，江南人甚敬信之。 及陳主被擒，異奔於瓛，由是益為眾所歸。 褒國公宇文述以兵討之，瓛遣王哀守吳州，自將拒述。 述遣兵別道襲吳州，哀懼，衣道士服，棄城而遁。 瓛眾聞之，悉無鬥志，與述一戰而敗。 瓛將左右數人逃於太湖，匿於民家，為人所執，送於述所，斬之長安，時年二十一。
Xiao Huan, styled Qinwen, was clever as a youth and skilled at composition. Under Liang he served as governor of Jingzhou and won a reputation for ability. When Cui Hongdu advanced on Ruozhou with troops, Huan, in fear, fled to Chen with his uncle Yan. The Chen emperor appointed him Palace Attendant, Pacifying-East General, and governor of Wuzhou; he won great popular affection, and the elders of the Three Wu said, "Huan is our true gentleman." " When Chen fell, the people of Wu rallied to Huan as their leader. The people of Wu noted that Emperor Wu of Liang, Emperor Jianwen, and the brothers Cha and Kui had all been third sons when they took the throne; Huan, as Kui's third son, took great pride in the omen. There was a man named Xie Yi who could read the rise and fall of dynasties; in the transition from Liang to Chen, his words never failed, and the people of Jiangnan revered him. When the Chen emperor was captured, Yi fled to Huan, and the people rallied to Huan still more. Duke of Baiguo Yuwen Shu marched against him; Huan sent Wang Ai to hold Wuzhou and led the army himself to resist Shu. Shu sent a detachment by another route against Wuzhou; Ai, in fear, donned Taoist robes, abandoned the city, and fled. Huan's army, hearing this, lost all will to fight; one battle with Shu and they were broken. Huan fled to Lake Tai with a few followers, hid in a commoner's house, was seized and sent to Shu, and was executed at Chang'an at the age of twenty-one.

史臣曰：三五哲王，防深慮遠，舅甥之國，罕執鈞衡，母后之家，無聞傾敗。 爰及漢、晉，顛覆繼軌，皆由乎進不以禮，故其斃亦速。 若使獨孤權侔呂、霍，必敗于仁壽之前，蕭氏勢均梁、竇，豈全于大業之後！ 今或不隕舊基，或更隆先構，豈非處之以道，不預權寵之所致乎！
The historiographer writes: The sage kings of antiquity looked far ahead and guarded against danger; states ruled by uncle and nephew seldom held real power, and empresses' kin were rarely heard of in ruinous collapse. Down through Han and Jin, ruin followed ruin in turn, all because advancement came without proper restraint—and so their fall came quickly as well. Had the Dugu kin wielded power like the Lü and Huo clans, they would surely have fallen before the Renshou era; had the Xiao kin matched the might of the Liang and Dou houses, how could they have survived into Daye! That some today did not lose their ancestral standing, or even surpassed their forebears' estate—is this not precisely because the Sui treated them with proper restraint and kept them from court power and favor!
