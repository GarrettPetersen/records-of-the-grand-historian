○外戚
Imperial Affines
3
曆觀前代外戚之家，乘母后之權以取高位厚秩者多矣，然而鮮有克終之美，必罹顛覆之患，何哉？ 皆由乎無德而尊，不知紀極，忽于滿盈之戒，罔念高危之咎，故鬼瞰其室，憂必及之。 夫其誠著艱難，功宣社稷，不以謙沖自牧，未免顛蹶之禍，而況道不足以濟時，仁不足以利物，自矜於己，以富貴驕人者乎？ 此呂、霍、上官、閻、梁、竇、鄧所以繼踵而亡滅者也。 昔文皇潛躍之際，獻後便相推轂，煬帝大橫方兆，蕭妃密勿經綸，是以恩禮綢繆，始終不易。 然內外親戚，莫預朝權，昆弟在位，亦無殊寵。 至於居擅玉堂，家稱金穴，暉光戚裡，重灼四方，將三司以比儀，命五侯而同拜者，終始一代，寂無聞焉。 考之前王，可謂矯其弊矣。 故雖時經擾攘，無有陷於不義，市朝遷貿，而皆得以保全。 比夫憑藉寵私，階緣恩澤，乘其非據，旋就顛隕者，豈可同日而言哉！ 此所謂愛之以禮，能改覆車。 輒敘其事，為《外戚傳》雲。
A survey of imperial affines in earlier dynasties shows that many used an empress's authority to win high office and rich emoluments, yet few ended well and most met ruin in the end. Why was this? They were honored without merit, knew no bounds, ignored the warning that fullness invites spillover, and forgot how dangerous it is to stand too high—so malign forces watched their households, and disaster was sure to follow. Even men whose loyalty had been proved in adversity and whose service to the state was real, if they did not govern themselves with humility, could not escape ruin—how much less those whose conduct could not save the times, whose kindness could not help others, yet who flaunted their own worth and lorded their wealth over everyone? This is why the houses of Lü, Huo, Shangguan, Yan, Liang, Dou, and Deng rose one after another only to be destroyed in turn. When Emperor Wen was still making his way to power, Empress Xian already stood at his side; when Emperor Yang's reign was taking shape, Consort Xiao worked closely on affairs of state. Favor and ceremony were therefore constant and unwavering throughout. Yet none of their affinal kin, whether close or distant, held real power at court, and even brothers who held office received no extraordinary privilege. Throughout the dynasty one heard nothing of affines who seized the halls of government, whose houses were proverbial treasure vaults, whose splendor dazzled the whole clan and whose influence blazed across the realm, who were treated like the Three Excellencies or who had the Five Marquises bow with them. Measured against earlier rulers, the Sui emperors may be said to have corrected this longstanding abuse. Thus even when the realm was in turmoil, none of these kin were drawn into wrongful ruin, and though power shifted in the capital, they were able to survive. How can they be compared with those who traded on private favor, climbed by imperial indulgence, seized what they had no right to hold, and were soon destroyed? This is what it means to love them with proper restraint and to keep them from repeating the disasters of the past. I therefore set out these accounts in the 'Biographies of Imperial Affines.'

○高祖外家呂氏
The Lü Clan, Maternal Kin of Emperor Gaozu
5
高祖外家呂氏，其族蓋微，平齊之後，求訪不知所在。 至開皇初，濟南郡上言，有男子呂永吉，自稱有姑字苦桃，為楊忠妻。 勘驗知是舅子，始追贈外祖雙周為上柱國、太尉、八州諸軍事、青州刺史，封齊郡公，諡曰敬，外祖母姚氏為齊敬公夫人。 詔並改葬，于齊州立廟，置守塚十家。 以永吉襲爵，留在京師。 大業中，授上党郡太守，性識庸劣，職務不理。 後去官，不知所終。
The Lü clan on Emperor Gaozu's mother's side was of humble origin. After the conquest of Qi, officials searched for them but could not find where they lived. Early in the Kaihuang reign, Jinan commandery reported that a man named Lü Yongji claimed an aunt called Kutao had been the wife of Yang Zhong. Investigation confirmed he was a maternal nephew. The court then posthumously honored the maternal grandfather Shuangzhou as Pillar of the State of the First Rank, Grand Commandant, commander of military affairs in eight provinces, and Governor of Qing Province, enfeoffed him as Duke of Qi with the posthumous title Jing, and styled the maternal grandmother, Lady Yao, consort of Duke Jing of Qi. An edict ordered new burials, a temple built in Qi province, and ten households assigned to tend the graves. Yongji inherited the title and remained in the capital. During the Daye reign he was made Administrator of Shangdang commandery, but his abilities were poor and he neglected his office. He later left office, and nothing further is known of him.
6
永吉從父道貴，性尤頑騃，言詞鄙陋。 初自鄉里征入長安，上見之悲泣。 道貴略無戚容，但連呼高祖名，雲：「種末定不可偷，大似苦桃姊。 」是後數犯忌諱，動致違忤，上甚耽之。 乃命高熲厚加供給，不許接對朝士。 拜上儀同三司，出為濟南太守，令即之任，斷其入朝。 道貴還至本郡，高自崇重，每與人言，自稱皇舅。 數將儀衛出入閭裡，從故人游宴，官民鹹苦之。 後郡廢，終於家，子孫無聞焉。
Yongji's cousin Daogui was exceptionally dull-witted and spoke in crude, vulgar language. When he was first summoned from the countryside to Chang'an, the emperor wept at the sight of him. Daogui showed little grief and kept calling the emperor by his personal name, saying, 'The breed isn't settled yet—you can't cheat that. You look just like Elder Sister Kutao.' After that he repeatedly broke taboos and gave offense, and the emperor grew thoroughly weary of him. The emperor ordered Gao Jiong to provide for him generously but forbade him to associate with court officials. He was made Senior Master of Uniformity of the Third Rank and sent out as Administrator of Jinan with orders to take up his post immediately and never return to court. Back in his home commandery, Daogui held himself in high esteem and told everyone he met that he was the emperor's maternal uncle. He often paraded with an armed escort through the streets, caroused with old friends, and officials and commoners alike resented him. When the commandery was later abolished, he died at home, and nothing more is recorded of his descendants.

獨孤羅弟陀
Dugu Luo and His Younger Brother Tuo
8
獨孤羅，字羅仁，雲中人也。 父信，初仕魏為荊州刺史。 武帝之入關也，信棄父母妻子西歸長安，曆職顯貴，羅由是遂為高氏所囚。 信後仕周為大司馬。 及信為宇文護所誅，羅始見釋，寓居中山，孤貧無以自給。 齊將獨孤永業以宗族之故，見而哀之，為買田宅，遺以資畜。 初，信入關之後，複娶二妻，郭氏生子六人，善、穆、藏、順、陀、整，崔氏生獻皇后。 及齊亡，高祖為定州總管，獻皇后遣人尋羅，得之，相見悲不自勝，侍御者皆泣。 於是厚遺車馬財物。 未幾，周武帝以羅功臣子，久淪異域，征拜楚安郡太守。 以疾去官，歸於京師。 諸弟見羅少長貧賤，每輕侮之，不以兄禮事也。 然性長者，亦不與諸弟校競長短，後由是重之。 及高祖為丞相，拜儀同，常置左右。 既受禪，下詔追贈羅父信官爵曰：「褒德累行，往代通規，追遠慎終，前王盛典。 故柱國信，風宇高曠，獨秀生民，睿哲居宗，清猷映世。 宏謀長策，道著於弼諧，緯義經仁，事深於拯濟。 方當宣風廊廟，亮采臺階，而運屬艱危，功高弗賞，眷言令範，事切於心。 今景運初開，椒闈肅建，載懷塗山之義，無忘褒、紀之典。 可贈太師、上柱國、冀定等十州刺史、趙國公，邑萬戶。 」其諸弟以羅母沒齊，先無夫人之號，不當承襲。 上以問後，後曰：「羅誠嫡長，不可誣也。 」於是襲爵趙國公。 以其弟善為河內郡公，穆為金泉縣公，藏為武平縣公，陀為武喜縣公，整為千牛備身。 擢拜羅為左領左右將軍，尋遷左衛將軍，前後賞賜不可勝計。 久而出為涼州總管，進位上柱國。 仁壽中，征拜左武衛大將軍。 煬帝嗣位，改封蜀國公。 未幾，卒官，諡曰恭。
Dugu Luo, styled Luoren, was a native of Yunzhong. His father Xin first served Northern Wei as Governor of Jing province. When Emperor Wu of Northern Zhou entered the Pass, Xin abandoned his parents, wife, and children and went west to Chang'an, where he rose to high office. Luo was left behind and imprisoned by the Gao clan of Northern Qi. Xin later served Northern Zhou as Grand Marshal. After Xin was executed by Yuwen Hu, Luo was finally released. He lived in Zhongshan, alone and destitute, with no means to support himself. The Northern Qi general Dugu Yongye, a kinsman, took pity on him, bought him land and a house, and gave him livestock and supplies. After Xin entered the Pass he had taken two more wives. Lady Guo bore six sons—Shan, Mu, Zang, Shun, Tuo, and Zheng—and Lady Cui bore Empress Xian. After Qi fell, while Gaozu was Governor-General of Ding province, Empress Xian sent men to find Luo. When they met, grief overwhelmed them and all who were present wept. She then sent him generous gifts of carriages, horses, and goods. Soon afterward Emperor Wu of Zhou, noting that Luo was the son of a meritorious minister long stranded in enemy territory, appointed him Administrator of Chu'an commandery. Illness forced him to leave office, and he returned to the capital. His younger brothers, knowing he had grown up poor and far away, often slighted him and did not treat him with proper respect as their elder brother. But Luo was by nature forbearing and did not quarrel with them over precedence, and in time they came to respect him for it. When Gaozu became Chancellor, Luo was made Master of Uniformity and kept constantly at his side. After Gaozu took the throne, he issued an edict posthumously honoring Luo's father Xin: 'To reward virtue and accumulated merit is the constant practice of former ages; to honor the dead and see their rites through is the great duty of ancient kings.' 'The late Pillar of the State Xin bore himself with lofty dignity and stood apart among men; his wisdom illuminated the clan and his clear counsel shone upon the age.' 'His great plans and long counsel guided the state; in weaving righteousness and ordering benevolence his work went deep into saving the realm.' 'Just as he was to bring his influence to the court and serve at the highest level, fate turned harsh; his merit went unrewarded, and remembering his example moves the heart.' 'Now, as a new reign begins and the inner palace is duly established, mindful of the duty owed to a consort's kin, let us not forget the honors due to the fathers of empresses.' 'Let him be posthumously made Grand Preceptor, Pillar of the State of the First Rank, nominal governor of ten provinces including Ji and Ding, and Duke of Zhao with a fief of ten thousand households.' His younger brothers argued that because Luo's mother had died in Qi and had never held the title of Lady, he ought not to inherit the title. The emperor asked the empress, who replied, 'Luo is truly the eldest son of the principal line—this cannot be denied.' Luo therefore inherited the title of Duke of Zhao. His brother Shan was made Duke of Henei commandery, Mu Duke of Jinquan county, Zang Duke of Wuping county, Tuo Duke of Wuxi county, and Zheng an attendant of the Thousand-Ox Guard. Luo was promoted to General of the Left Guard of the Left and Right, soon made General of the Left Guard, and received rewards beyond reckoning. After some time he was sent out as Governor-General of Liang province and advanced to Pillar of the State of the First Rank. During the Renshou era he was recalled and made Grand General of the Left Martial Guard. When Emperor Yang succeeded to the throne, Luo's title was changed to Duke of Shu. He soon died in office and was given the posthumous title Gong.
9
子纂嗣，仕至河陽郡尉。 纂弟武都，大業末，亦為河陽郡尉。 庶長子開遠，宇文化及之弑逆也，裴虔通率賊入成象殿，宿衛兵士皆從逆，開遠時為千牛，與獨孤盛力戰於閤下，為賊所執，賊義而舍之。 善後官至柱國。 卒，子覽嗣，仕至左候衛將軍，大業末卒。
His son Zuan inherited the title and rose to Commandant of Heyang commandery. Zuan's younger brother Wudu also became Commandant of Heyang commandery late in the Daye reign. A concubine's eldest son, Kaiyuan: when Yuwen Huaji murdered the emperor, Pei Qiantong led the rebels into Chengxian Hall and the palace guards joined them. Kaiyuan was then in the Thousand-Ox Guard. He and Dugu Sheng fought fiercely at the gate, were captured, but the rebels, admiring their courage, let them go. Shan later rose to Pillar of the State. He died. His son Lan inherited the title, rose to General of the Left Garrison Guard, and died late in the Daye reign.
10
獨孤陀，字黎邪。 仕周胥附上士，坐父徙蜀郡十餘年。 宇文護被誅，始歸長安。 高祖受禪，拜上開府、右領左右將軍。 久之，出為郢州刺史，進位上大將軍，累轉延州刺史。 好左道。 其妻母先事貓鬼，因轉入其家。 上微聞而不之信也。 會獻皇后及楊素妻鄭氏俱有疾，召醫者視之，皆曰：「此貓鬼疾也。 」上以陀後之異母弟，陀妻楊素之異母妹，由是意陀所為，陰令其兄穆以情喻之。 上又避左右諷陀，陀言無有。 上不悅，左轉遷州刺史。 出怨言。 上令左僕射高熲、納言蘇威、大理正皇甫孝緒、大理丞楊遠等雜治之。 陀婢徐阿尼言，本從陀母家來，常事貓鬼。 每以子日夜祀之。 言子者鼠也。 其貓鬼每殺人者，所死家財物潛移於畜貓鬼家。 陀嘗從家中素酒，其妻曰：「無錢可酤。 」陀因謂阿尼曰：「可令貓鬼向越公家，使我足錢也。 」阿尼便咒之歸。 數日，貓鬼向素家。 十一年，上初從并州還，陀於園中謂阿尼曰：「可令貓鬼向皇后所，使多賜吾物。 」阿尼複咒之，遂入宮中。 楊遠乃于門下外省遣阿尼呼貓鬼。 阿尼於是夜中置香粥一盆，以匙扣而呼之曰：「貓女可來，無住宮中。 」久之，阿尼色正青，若被牽曳者，雲貓鬼已至。 上以其事下公卿，奇章公牛弘曰：「妖由人興，殺其人可以絕矣。 」上令以犢車載陀夫妻，將賜死於其家。 陀弟司勳侍中整詣闕求哀，於是免陀死，除名為民，以其妻楊氏為尼。 先是，有人訟其母為人貓鬼所殺者，上以為妖妄，怒而遣之。 及此，詔誅被訟行貓鬼家。 經未幾而卒。 煬帝即位，追念舅氏，聽以禮葬，乃下詔曰：「外氏衰禍，獨孤陀不幸早世，遷卜有期。 言念渭陽之情，追懷傷切，宜加禮命，允備哀榮。 可贈正議大夫。 」帝意猶不已，複下詔曰：「舅氏之尊，戚屬斯重，而降年弗永，凋落相繼。 緬惟先往，宜崇徽秩。 複贈銀青光祿大夫。 」有二子：延福、延壽。
Dugu Tuo, styled Lixie. He served Northern Zhou as a gentleman-attendant of Xufu and, because of his father's fate, was exiled to Shu commandery for more than ten years. After Yuwen Hu was executed, he was able to return to Chang'an. When Gaozu took the throne, Tuo was made Senior Opener of the Government and General of the Right Guard of the Left and Right. After some time he was sent out as Governor of E province, advanced to Grand General of the Upper Rank, and later transferred to Governor of Yan province. He was devoted to heterodox cult practices. His wife's mother had first practiced the cat-demon cult, and the rite passed into his household. The emperor had heard rumors but did not believe them. When both Empress Xian and Yang Su's wife Lady Zheng fell ill, physicians were called and all said, 'This is cat-demon sickness.' Because Tuo was the empress's younger half-brother and his wife was Yang Su's younger half-sister, the emperor suspected Tuo and secretly had his brother Mu reason with him. The emperor also spoke to Tuo privately and hinted at his suspicions, but Tuo denied everything. Displeased, the emperor demoted him to Governor of Qian province. He spoke resentfully of his treatment. The emperor ordered Left Vice Director Gao Jiong, Admonisher Su Wei, Chief Judge Huangfu Xiaoxu, Assistant Judge Yang Yuan, and others to investigate jointly. Tuo's maid Xu Ani testified that she had come from his mother's household and had long served the cat-demon. Every night at the hour of zi she made offerings to it. By 'zi' she meant the rat—the hour of the rat. Whenever the cat-demon killed someone, the victim's wealth was secretly transferred to the household that kept the demon. Tuo once asked for wine from the household stores. His wife said, 'We have no money to buy any.' Tuo then told Ani, 'Send the cat-demon to the Duke of Yue's house so we'll have enough money.' Ani chanted the spell to send it. Within days the cat-demon was directed at Yang Su's household. In the eleventh year, soon after the emperor returned from Bing province, Tuo told Ani in the garden, 'Send the cat-demon to the empress's quarters so she will give me many gifts.' Ani chanted again, and the demon entered the palace. Yang Yuan then had Ani summon the cat-demon at the Outer Office of the Gate Department. That night Ani set out a basin of fragrant porridge, tapped it with a spoon, and called, 'Cat-girl, come—but do not stay in the palace.' After a long while Ani's face turned blue, as if she were being pulled, and she said the cat-demon had come. The emperor referred the case to the high ministers. Niu Hong of Niuzhang said, 'Such evil arises through people—execute the culprit and it will end.' The emperor ordered Tuo and his wife placed on a cart and sent to their home to be executed. Tuo's brother Zheng, Attendant at the Palace Secretariat in the Bureau of Merit, pleaded at court for mercy. Tuo's death sentence was remitted; he was stripped of rank and made a commoner, and his wife Lady Yang was forced to become a nun. Earlier, a man had sued, claiming his mother had been killed by someone's cat-demon. The emperor dismissed it as superstitious nonsense and angrily sent him away. Now an edict ordered the household accused in that earlier suit, who practiced the cat-demon cult, put to death. Before long Tuo died. When Emperor Yang took the throne, mindful of his mother's kin, he allowed Tuo a proper burial and issued an edict: 'Our maternal clan has suffered decline; Dugu Tuo died young, and the time to move his tomb is at hand.' Mindful of kinship ties, deeply grieved at his loss, let him receive posthumous honors worthy of full mourning and glory. Let him be posthumously made Grand Master of Correct Discussion. The emperor's grief was still not satisfied, and he issued another edict: 'Maternal uncles are kin of the highest honor, yet their years are brief, and one after another they fall away.' Remembering those who have gone before, let their ranks be raised in honor. Tuo was again posthumously made Grand Master of the Palace with Silver-Green Tether. He had two sons: Yanfu and Yanshou.

陀弟整，官至幽州刺史，大業初卒，贈金紫光祿大夫，平鄉侯。
Tuo's brother Zheng rose to Governor of You province, died early in the Daye reign, and was posthumously made Grand Master of the Palace with Gold-Purple Tether and Marquis of Pingxiang.

蕭巋子琮琮弟瓛
Xiao Kui's Son Cong and Cong's Younger Brother Huan
13
蕭巋，字仁遠，梁昭明太子統之孫也。 父詧，初封岳陽王，鎮襄陽。 侯景之亂，其兄河東王譽與其叔父湘東王繹不協，為繹所害。 及繹嗣位，詧稱籓於西魏，乞師請討繹。 周太祖以詧為梁主，遣柱國於謹等率騎五萬襲繹，滅之。 詧遂都江陵，有荊郡、其西平州延袤三百里之地，稱皇帝于其國，車服節文一同王者。 仍置江陵總管，以兵戍之。 詧薨，巋嗣位，年號天保。 巋俊辯有才學，兼好內典。 周武帝平齊之後，巋來賀，帝享之甚歡。 親彈琵琶，令巋起舞，巋曰：「陛下親禦五糸玄，臣敢不同百獸！ 」高祖受禪，恩禮彌厚，遣使賜金五百兩，銀千兩，布帛萬匹，馬五百匹。 巋來朝，上甚敬焉，詔巋位在王公之上。 巋被服端麗，進退閒雅，天子矚目，百僚傾慕。 賞賜以億計。 月餘歸籓，帝親餞于滻水之上。 後備禮納其女為晉王妃，又欲以其子瑒尚蘭陵公主。 由是漸見親待。 獻皇后言于上曰：「梁主通家，腹心所寄，何勞猜防也。 」上然之，於是罷江陵總管，巋專制其國。 歲餘，巋又來朝，賜縑萬匹，珍玩稱是。 及還，上親執手曰：「梁主久滯荊楚，未復舊都，故鄉之念，良軫懷抱。 朕當振旅長江，相送旋反耳。 」巋拜謝而去。 其年五月，寢疾，臨終上表曰：「臣以庸暗，曲荷天慈，寵冠外籓，恩逾連山，爰及子女，尚主婚王。 每願躬擐甲胄，身先士卒，掃蕩逋寇，上報明時。 而攝生乖舛，遽罹屙疾，屬纊在辰，顧陰待謝。 長違聖世，感戀嗚咽，遺嗣孤藐，特乞降慈。 伏願聖躬與山嶽同固，皇基等天日俱永，臣雖九泉，實無遣恨。 」並獻所服金裝劍，上覽而嗟悼焉。 巋在位二十三年，年四十四薨，梁之臣子諡曰孝明皇帝，廟號世宗。 子琮嗣。 巋著《孝經》、《周易義記》及《大小乘幽微》十四卷，行於世。
Xiao Kui, styled Renyuan, was a grandson of Liang Crown Prince Zhaoming, Xiao Tong. His father Cha was first enfeoffed as Prince of Yueyang and stationed at Xiangyang. During Hou Jing's rebellion, his elder brother Prince Yu of Hedong fell out with their uncle Prince Yi of Xiangdong, who had him killed. When Yi took the throne, Cha submitted to Western Wei as a vassal, asked for troops, and sought to attack Yi. The founder of Northern Zhou installed Cha as ruler of Liang and sent the Pillar of the State Yu Jin with fifty thousand cavalry to attack and destroy Yi. Cha moved his capital to Jiangling, held Jing commandery and Xiping province—some three hundred li in extent—and proclaimed himself emperor. His carriages, dress, and ritual matched those of a sovereign. Northern Zhou still stationed a Governor-General at Jiangling with troops to guard the region. After Cha died, Kui succeeded him and took the era name Tianbao. Kui was handsome, eloquent, and learned, and he also loved Buddhist scriptures. After Emperor Wu of Zhou conquered Northern Qi, Kui came to offer congratulations and was received with great warmth. The emperor played the pipa himself and asked Kui to dance. Kui said, 'Your Majesty plays the five-stringed lute himself—how can I, your servant, not join in like the beasts at court!' When Gaozu took the throne, his favor toward Kui grew even greater. He sent envoys with five hundred liang of gold, one thousand liang of silver, ten thousand bolts of cloth and silk, and five hundred horses. When Kui came to court, the emperor treated him with great respect and ordered that he rank above kings and dukes. Kui dressed with elegance and moved with grace. The emperor watched him closely, and the officials admired him. Rewards to him were reckoned in the tens of millions. After more than a month he returned to his domain, and the emperor personally saw him off on the banks of the Ba River. Later his daughter was married with full ceremony to the Prince of Jin, and the emperor also wished to marry Kui's son Yang to Princess Lanling. From then on he was increasingly treated as a close kinsman. Empress Xian told the emperor, 'The Lord of Liang is family by marriage, a man in whom you may place your trust—why suspect and guard against him?' The emperor agreed, abolished the Governor-General at Jiangling, and let Kui rule his state on his own. A year later Kui came to court again and received ten thousand bolts of fine silk and rare treasures in equal abundance. When he departed, the emperor took his hand and said, 'You have long been kept in Jing and Chu and have not regained your old capital. I know how deeply you must long for home.' I shall march on the Yangzi and see you safely home.' Kui bowed in thanks and left. That fifth month he fell ill. On his deathbed he submitted a memorial: 'Your servant, though mediocre and unworthy, has received Heaven's grace beyond measure, favor surpassing all outer lords, kindness as vast as mountains, extending even to my children, who have married princesses and princes.' I had hoped to don armor myself, lead the troops in person, sweep away the rebels, and repay your enlightened reign.' But I failed in caring for my health and have suddenly fallen gravely ill. Death is near, and I await the end.' To leave your sage reign fills me with grief. My orphaned heirs are young and helpless, and I beg your mercy for them.' I pray that Your Majesty's person may be as firm as the mountains, that the imperial foundation may endure as long as Heaven and sun, and that though I go to the grave, I shall have no regret.' He also presented the gold-mounted sword he had worn. The emperor read the memorial and mourned him deeply. Kui had reigned twenty-three years and died at forty-four. Liang ministers gave him the posthumous title Emperor Xiaoming and the temple name Shizong. His son Cong succeeded him. Kui wrote the Classic of Filial Piety, Records of the Meaning of the Book of Changes, and Subtleties of the Greater and Lesser Vehicles in fourteen scrolls, all of which circulated.

琮字溫文，性寬仁，有大度，倜儻不羈，博學有文義。 兼善弓馬，遣人伏地著帖，琮馳馬射之，十發十中，持帖者亦不懼。 初封東陽王，尋立為梁太子。 及嗣位，上賜璽書曰：「負荷堂構，其事甚重，雖窮憂勞，常須自力。 輯諧內外，親任才良，聿遵世業，是所望也。 彼之疆守，咫尺陳人，水潦之時，特宜警備。 陳氏比日雖複朝聘相尋，疆埸之間猶未清肅，唯當恃我必不可幹，勿得輕人而不設備。 朕與梁國，積世相知，重以親姻，情義彌厚。 江陵之地，朝寄非輕，為國為民，深宜抑割，恆加饘粥，以禮自存。 」又賜梁之大臣璽書，誠勉之。 時琮年號廣運，有識者曰：「運之為字，軍走也，吾君將奔走乎？ 」其年，琮遣大將軍戚昕以舟師襲陳公安，不克而還。 征琮叔父岑入朝，拜為大將軍，封懷義公，因留不遣。 複置江陵總管以監之。 琮所署大將軍許世武密以城召陳將宜黃侯陳紀，謀泄，琮誅之。 後二歲，上征琮入朝，率其臣下二百余人朝于京師，江陵父老莫不隕涕相謂曰：「吾君其不反矣！ 」上以琮來朝，遣武鄉公崔弘度將兵戍之。 軍至鄀州，琮叔父岩及弟瓛等懼弘度掩襲之，遂引陳人至城下，虜居民而叛，於是廢梁國。 上遣左僕射高熲安集之，曲赦江陵死罪，給民複十年。 梁二主各給守墓十戶。 拜琮為柱國，賜爵莒國公。 煬帝嗣位，以皇后之故，甚見親重。 拜內史令，改封梁公。 琮之宗族，緦麻以上，並隨才擢用，於是諸蕭昆弟布列朝廷。 琮性淡雅，不以職務自嬰，退朝縱酒而已。 內史令楊約與琮同列，帝令約宣旨誡勵，約複以私情喻之。 琮答曰：「琮若複事事，則何異於公哉！ 」約笑而退。 約兄素，時為尚書令，見琮嫁從父妹于鉗耳氏，因謂琮曰：「公，帝王之族，望高戚美，何乃適妹鉗耳氏乎？ 」琮曰：「前已嫁妹于侯莫陳氏，此複何疑！ 」素曰：「鉗耳，羌也，侯莫陳，虜也，何得相比！ 」素意以虜優羌劣。 琮曰：「以羌異虜，未之前聞。 」素慚而止。 琮雖羈旅，見北間豪貴，無所降下。 嘗與賀若弼深相友善，弼既被誅，複有童謠曰：「蕭蕭亦複起。 」帝由是忌之，遂廢於家，未幾而卒。 贈左光祿大夫。 子鉉，襄城通守。 複以琮弟子钜為梁公。 钜小名藏，煬帝甚昵之，以為千牛，與宇文皛出入宮掖，伺察內外。 帝每有遊宴，钜未嘗不從焉，遂于宮中多行淫穢。 江都之變，為宇文化及所殺。
Cong, styled Wenwen, was generous and magnanimous, free-spirited and unrestrained, broadly learned, and skilled in letters. He was also skilled with bow and horse. He had a man lie prone with a target on his back, then galloped and shot—ten arrows, ten hits—while the man holding the target showed no fear. He was first made Prince of Dongyang and soon afterward named Liang crown prince. When he succeeded, the emperor sent him an imperial letter: 'To bear the weight of rule is a grave matter. Though worn with care and labor, you must always rely on your own strength.' Harmonize within and without, employ the worthy, and carry on your father's work—this is what I expect of you.' Your frontier lies close to Chen territory. In seasons of flood and rain, be especially vigilant.' Though Chen has lately resumed embassies, the border is not yet secure. Trust that I cannot be challenged, but do not underestimate them or neglect your defenses.' I and the state of Liang have known each other for generations, and our bond of marriage has made that tie still deeper.' Jiangling is no light charge from the court. For the sake of state and people, restrain yourself, live simply, and preserve yourself through proper conduct.' He also sent imperial letters to Liang's chief ministers, earnestly exhorting them. Cong's era name was Guangyun. Men of insight said, 'The character yun means an army in flight—will our lord be put to flight?' That year Cong sent Grand General Qi Xin with a fleet to raid Chen's Gong'an but failed and returned. Cong's uncle Cen was summoned to court, made Grand General and Duke of Huaiyi, and then detained. The Governor-General at Jiangling was re-established to supervise him. Grand General Xu Shiwu, whom Cong had appointed, secretly invited the Chen general Marquis Chen Ji of Yihuang to seize the city. When the plot was exposed, Cong had him executed. Two years later the emperor summoned Cong to court. He came with more than two hundred officials, and the elders of Jiangling wept and said to one another, 'Our lord will not return!' Because Cong had come to court, the emperor sent Duke Cui Hongdu of Wuxiang with troops to take control. When the army reached Ezhou, Cong's uncle Yan and his brother Huan, fearing a surprise attack, brought Chen forces to the walls, seized the populace, and rebelled. The state of Liang was then abolished. The emperor sent Left Vice Director Gao Jiong to pacify the region, granted a special pardon for capital crimes in Jiangling, and gave the people ten years of tax relief. Each of the two Liang rulers was given ten households to tend their tombs. Cong was made Pillar of the State and Duke of Ju. When Emperor Yang succeeded to the throne, Cong was greatly favored because of the empress. He was made Director of the Palace Secretariat and enfeoffed as Duke of Liang. All of Cong's clan within the mourning circle were promoted according to ability, and Xiao kinsmen filled the court. Cong was by nature detached and easygoing. He did not burden himself with office and, after court, merely drank his fill. Director Yang Yue, Cong's equal in rank, was ordered by the emperor to admonish him, and Yue also spoke to him privately. Cong replied, 'If I attended to every matter, how would I differ from you, sir!' Yue laughed and withdrew. Yue's brother Su was then Director of the Department of State Affairs. Seeing Cong marry a cousin to the Qian'er clan, he said, 'You are of imperial stock, of high standing and fine marriage prospects—why give your sister to the Qian'er clan?' Cong said, 'I already married one sister to the Houmochen clan—what is there to question about this!' Su said, 'The Qian'er are Qiang; the Houmochen are barbarians—how can they be compared!' Su meant that the barbarians were superior and the Qiang inferior. Cong said, 'That the Qiang differ from barbarians is something I have never heard before.' Su, ashamed, said no more. Though Cong was an exile in the north, he bowed to none of its great nobles. He had been close friends with He Ruo Bi. After Bi was executed, a children's song circulated: 'The Xiao clan will rise again.' The emperor grew suspicious and had him dismissed to his home. He soon died. He was posthumously made Grand Master of Splendid Happiness of the Left. His son Xian was Defender of Xiangcheng. Cong's nephew Ju was then made Duke of Liang. Ju, childhood name Zang, was greatly favored by Emperor Yang. He was made an attendant of the Thousand-Ox Guard and, with Yuwen Xiao, moved freely through the inner palace, watching affairs within and without. He never missed the emperor's feasts and excursions, and committed many lewd acts within the palace. At the Jiangdu upheaval he was killed by Yuwen Huaji.

瓛字欽文，少聰敏，解屬文。 在梁為荊州刺史，頗有能名。 崔弘度以兵至若阝州，瓛懼，與其叔父岩奔于陳。 陳主以為侍中、安東將軍、吳州刺史，甚得物情，三吳父老皆曰：「瓛吾君子也。 」及陳亡，吳人推瓛為主。 吳人見梁武、簡文及詧、巋等兄弟並第三而踐尊位，瓛自以巋之第三子也，深自矜負。 有謝異者，頗知廢興，梁、陳之際，言無不驗，江南人甚敬信之。 及陳主被擒，異奔於瓛，由是益為眾所歸。 褒國公宇文述以兵討之，瓛遣王哀守吳州，自將拒述。 述遣兵別道襲吳州，哀懼，衣道士服，棄城而遁。 瓛眾聞之，悉無鬥志，與述一戰而敗。 瓛將左右數人逃於太湖，匿於民家，為人所執，送於述所，斬之長安，時年二十一。
Huan, styled Qinwen, was clever from youth and skilled at composition. In Liang he was Governor of Jing province and enjoyed a reputation for ability. When Cui Hongdu advanced on Ruozhou with troops, Huan fled with his uncle Yan to Chen. The Chen ruler made him Palace Attendant, General Who Pacifies the East, and Governor of Wu province. He won great popular favor, and the elders of the Three Wu said, 'Huan is our true leader.' When Chen fell, the people of Wu urged Huan to lead them. The people of Wu noted that Liang Wudi, Jianwendi, and the brothers Cha and Kui had all been third sons who rose to the throne. Huan, as Kui's third son, took great pride in this. A man named Xie Yi was skilled at reading the rise and fall of states. During the Liang and Chen transition, his predictions always came true, and the people of the south greatly revered him. When the Chen ruler was captured, Yi fled to Huan, and Huan gained still more followers. Duke Yuwen Shu of Baoguo marched against him. Huan sent Wang Ai to hold Wu province and led the army himself to resist Shu. Shu sent troops by another route to strike Wu province. Ai, terrified, dressed as a Daoist priest and abandoned the city. When Huan's troops heard this, they lost all fighting spirit and were defeated in a single battle. Huan fled with a few followers to Lake Tai and hid in a commoner's house, but was captured and sent to Shu. He was beheaded at Chang'an at the age of twenty-one.

史臣曰：三五哲王，防深慮遠，舅甥之國，罕執鈞衡，母后之家，無聞傾敗。 爰及漢、晉，顛覆繼軌，皆由乎進不以禮，故其斃亦速。 若使獨孤權侔呂、霍，必敗于仁壽之前，蕭氏勢均梁、竇，豈全于大業之後！ 今或不隕舊基，或更隆先構，豈非處之以道，不預權寵之所致乎！
The historiographer remarks: The sage kings of antiquity guarded against danger with foresight. States ruled by maternal uncles and nephews rarely held real power, and empresses' kin were seldom heard of in ruin. Down through Han and Jin, ruin followed ruin, all because advancement came without proper restraint—and so their fall was swift as well. Had the Dugu clan's power rivaled that of the Lü and Huo, they would surely have fallen before the Renshou era. Had the Xiao clan's influence matched that of the Liang and Dou, how could they have survived intact after the Daye reign! That some did not fall from their old standing while others rose even higher than their forebears—was this not because they were governed by principle and kept from sharing in power and privilege!
