○孝義
Filial Piety and Righteous Conduct
3
《孝經》云：「夫孝，天之經也，地之義也，人之行也。 」《論語》云：「君子務本，本立而道生。 孝悌也者，其為仁之本與！ 」《呂覽》云：「夫孝，三皇、五帝之本務，萬事之綱紀也。 執一術而百善至，百邪去，天下順者，其唯孝乎！ 」然則孝之為德至矣，其為道遠矣，其化人深矣。 故聖帝明王行之于四海，則與天地合其德，與日月齊其明。 諸侯卿大夫行之于國家，則永保其宗社，長守其祿位。 匹夫匹婦行之于閭閻，則播微烈于當年，揚休名於千載。 此皆資純至以感物，故聖哲之所重。 田翼、郎方貴等，闕稽古之學，無俊偉之才，並能任其自然，情無矯飾。 篤於天性，勤其四體，竭股肱之力，盡愛敬之心，自足膝下之歡，忘懷軒冕之貴。 不言之化，人神通感。 雖或位登臺輔，爵列王侯，祿積萬鐘，馬逾千駟，死之日，曾不得與斯人之徒隸齒。 孝之大也，不其然乎！ 故述其所行，為《孝義傳》。
The Classic of Filial Piety declares: "Filial piety is Heaven's unchanging standard, Earth's proper measure, and humanity's essential way. " The Analects teaches: "The noble person attends to the foundation; once the foundation is set, the Way comes to life. Is not filial devotion and respect among brothers the very foundation of benevolence!" " The Spring and Autumn Annals of Master Lü states: "Filial piety was the first duty of the sage rulers of antiquity and the thread that binds all human affairs. Cling to this single virtue and every good will follow, every ill fall away — is there any other force that can bring order to the realm but filial piety!" " So filial piety stands as the highest virtue, reaches the farthest as a Way, and transforms the human heart most profoundly. When sage rulers extend filial practice across the realm, their virtue aligns with Heaven and Earth, their radiance with the sun and moon. When lords and officials embody it within their domains, they preserve their ancestral line and hold their offices for generations. When ordinary people live it in their neighborhoods, they earn quiet renown in their lifetime and leave a luminous name for posterity. All this flows from sincere devotion that moves heaven and men alike — which is why the wise have always held it dear. Men such as Tian Yi and Lang Fanggui possessed no scholarly mastery of the classics nor any extraordinary gifts, yet each acted from genuine feeling, without affectation. Steadfast in natural affection, they labored with body and limb, poured out every ounce of devotion, found fulfillment in serving at their parents' side, and cared nothing for rank or courtly splendor. Their example needed no preaching: heaven and humanity answered in kind. Men who climbed to the highest offices, wore princely titles, hoarded vast revenues, and drove a thousand chariots — on their last day they might not even stand among the servants of these filial souls. Is this not proof of filial piety's supreme power? Hence these accounts are gathered in the Biographies of Filial Piety and Righteous Conduct.

○陸彥師
Lu Yanshi
5
陸彥師，字雲房，魏郡臨漳人。 祖希道，魏定州刺史。 父子彰，中書監。 彥師少有行檢，為邦族所稱。 長而好學，解屬文。 魏襄城王元旭引為參軍事。 以父艱去職，哀毀殆不勝喪。 與兄卬廬于墓次，負土成墳。 公卿重之，多就墓側存問，晦朔之際，車馬不絕。 齊文宣聞而嘉歎，旌表其閭，號其所住為孝終裡。 中書令河間邢邵表薦之，未報，彭城王浟為司州牧，召補主簿。 後曆中外府東閣祭酒。 兄卬當襲父始平侯，以彥師昆弟中最幼，表讓封焉。 彥師固辭而止。 時稱友悌孝義，總萃一門。 遷中書舍人，尋轉通直散騎侍郎。 每陳使至，必令高選主客，彥師所接對者，前後六輩。 曆中書黃門侍郎，以不阿宦者，遇讒，出為中山太守，有惠政。 數年，徵為吏部郎中。 周武平齊，授載師下大夫。 宣帝時，轉少納言，賜爵臨水縣男，奉使幽、薊。 俄而高祖為丞相，彥師遇疾，請假還鄴。 尉迥將為亂，彥師微知之，遂委妻子，潛歸長安。 高祖嘉之，授內史下大夫，拜上儀同。 高祖受禪，拜尚書左丞，進爵為子。 彥師素多病，未幾，以務劇疾動，乞解所職，有詔聽以本官就第。 歲餘，轉吏部侍郎。 隋承周制，官無清濁，彥師在職，凡所任人，頗甄別於士庶，論者美之。 後復以病出為汾州刺史，卒官。
Lu Yanshi, courtesy name Yunfang, came from Linzhang in Wei Commandery. His grandfather Xidao had served as governor of Dingzhou under Northern Wei. His father Zizhang was director of the imperial secretariat. From boyhood Yanshi maintained strict moral discipline and won esteem throughout his lineage. As a young man he pursued learning eagerly and showed talent for prose. Yuan Xu, Prince of Xiangcheng, took him on as a military staff adviser. When his father died he resigned his post; grief consumed him so utterly he nearly perished in mourning. He and his elder brother Ang lived in a mourning hut beside the tomb, heaping earth by hand to raise the grave. The gentry held them in high regard; dignitaries flocked to the graveside to visit, and on the first and fifteenth of each month the road never emptied of carriages. When Emperor Wenxuan of Northern Qi learned of this, he commended them and marked their neighborhood for honor, naming their hamlet the Village of Filial Completion. Xing Shao of Hejian, director of the secretariat, memorialized the throne in his favor, but before any response arrived, Prince You of Pengcheng, serving as regional governor of Sizhou, summoned him to fill the post of chief clerk. He later held in succession the post of Eastern Pavilion libationer in both the central and provincial administrations. His elder brother Ang was heir to their father's marquisate of Shiping; deeming Yanshi the youngest of the brothers, Ang petitioned to yield the title to him. Yanshi steadfastly refused, and the offer was withdrawn. Contemporary opinion held that friendship, fraternal devotion, filial piety, and righteous conduct were united in this single family. He rose to secretariat attendant and soon moved to the post of regular attendant of the scattered cavalry. Whenever ambassadors arrived from Chen, the court selected its finest host for them — over the years Yanshi received six such missions. After serving as vice director of the secretariat's yellow gate, he refused to court the eunuchs, fell victim to slander, and was dispatched as governor of Zhongshan, where his rule won praise for its kindness. A few years later he was summoned back to serve as a director in the ministry of personnel. After Emperor Wu of Northern Zhou conquered Qi, he was appointed grand master adviser to the heir apparent. Under Emperor Xuan he became junior director of palace receptions, was created baron of Linshui County, and undertook a diplomatic mission to You and Ji. Before long, as the future Emperor Wen assumed the chancellorship, Yanshi took ill and asked leave to return home to Ye. Sensing that Wei Jiong was plotting rebellion, Yanshi left his family behind and stole away to Chang'an. The emperor praised his loyalty, appointing him grand master of the inner scribe and raising him to senior master of uniform attendance. When Gaozu ascended the throne, Yanshi was made left vice director of the ministry of works and elevated to the rank of viscount. Yanshi had always been frail; soon the crushing workload worsened his condition, and he petitioned to resign — the throne granted leave to rest at home while keeping his rank. A year later he returned to service as vice director of the ministry of personnel. The Sui followed Northern Zhou practice in making no formal distinction between high and low offices, yet in his appointments Yanshi still carefully distinguished gentry from common stock — a choice widely commended. Illness later drove him from court again; he was posted as governor of Fenzhou and died in that post.

○田德懋
Tian Demao
7
田德懋，觀國公仁恭之子也。 少以孝友著名。 開皇初，以父軍功賜爵平原郡公，授太子千牛備身。 丁父艱，哀毀骨立，廬於墓側，負土成墳。 上聞而嘉之，遣員外散騎侍郎元志就吊焉。 復降璽書曰：「皇帝謝田德懋。 知在窮疾，哀毀過禮，倚廬墓所，負土成墳。 朕孝理天下，思弘名教，復與汝通家，情義素重，有聞孝感，嘉歎兼深。 春日暄和，氣力何似？ 宜自抑割，以禮自存也。 」並賜縑二百匹，米百石。 復下詔表其門閭。 後曆太子舍人、義州司馬。 大業中，為給事郎、尚書駕部郎，卒官。
Tian Demao was a son of Rengong, Duke of Guanguo. He won early renown for filial devotion and brotherly affection. In the early Kaihuang reign, his father's military service earned him the title duke of Pingyuan Commandery and a post as guard of the crown prince's household guard. When his father died, grief wasted him to skin and bone; he built a mourning hut beside the grave and raised the tomb with his own hands. When the emperor learned of this, he sent Yuan Zhi, an attendant of the scattered cavalry, to convey the court's sympathy. The throne also dispatched an imperial letter: "The Emperor sends his greetings to Tian Demao. We know you labor in deepest mourning, exceeding even the rites in your devastation, dwelling in a hut beside the tomb and building the grave with your own hands. I govern through filial virtue and seek to exalt moral teaching; we are moreover joined by marriage, and my affection for your house runs deep — news of your filial devotion moves me to admiration and deep feeling. Spring warmth is returning — how fares your health? Take care to restrain your grief and preserve yourself within the bounds of propriety. In addition he received two hundred bolts of silk and a hundred shi of rice. The court further issued an edict honoring his household and neighborhood. He later served as attendant to the crown prince and then as military aide of Yizhou. During the Daye reign he held posts as a court secretary and director of the chariot department, and died in office.

○薛濬
Xue Jun
9
薛濬，字道賾，刑部尚書、內陽公胄之從祖弟也。 父琰，周渭南太守。 濬少喪父，早孤，養母以孝聞。 幼好學，有志行，尋師于長安。 時初平江陵，何妥歸國，見而異之，授以經業。 周天和中，襲爵虞城侯，歷納言上士、新豐令。 開皇初，擢拜尚書虞部侍郎，尋轉考功侍郎。 帝聞濬事母至孝，以其母老，賜輿服機杖，四時珍味，當時榮之。 後其母疾，濬貌甚憂瘁，親故弗之識也。 暨丁母艱，詔鴻臚監護喪事，歸葬夏陽。 于時隆冬極寒，濬衰絰徒跣，冒犯霜雪，自京及鄉，五百餘里，足凍墜指，瘡血流離，朝野為之傷痛。 州裡賵助，一無所受。 尋起令視事，濬屢陳誠款，請終喪制，優詔不許。 及至京，上見其毀瘠過甚，為之改容，顧謂群臣曰：「吾見薛濬哀毀，不覺悲感傷懷。 」嗟異久之。 濬竟不勝喪，病且卒。 其弟謨時為晉王府兵曹參軍事，在揚州，濬遺書與謨曰：
Xue Jun, courtesy name Daoyin, was a younger cousin of Xue Zhou, minister of justice and duke of Neiyang. His father Yan had been governor of Weinan under Northern Zhou. Jun lost his father while still young and grew up fatherless; his devoted care for his mother won wide renown. As a boy he hungered for learning and showed moral resolve, traveling to Chang'an in search of a master. When Jiangling had just fallen, He Tuo came over to the Zhou court, took notice of the youth, and taught him the classics. During Northern Zhou's Tianhe reign he inherited the marquisate of Yucheng and served as a senior clerk in the palace reception office and as magistrate of Xinfeng. In the early Kaihuang era he was elevated to vice director of the parks section of the ministry of works, then transferred to vice director of the evaluation section. Learning of Jun's extraordinary devotion to his aged mother, the emperor granted her a carriage, ceremonial robes, a folding stool and walking staff, and seasonal delicacies — honors that made him the envy of the court. When his mother later fell gravely ill, worry ravaged his face so completely that even friends and kin hardly knew him. After his mother's death, the emperor ordered the director of ceremonies to supervise the rites, and Jun carried her body home for burial at Xiayang. It was deep midwinter and bitterly cold. Jun walked the more than five hundred li from the capital to his home village in mourning garb and bare feet through frost and snow; his feet froze until his toes dropped off, his wounds weeping blood — an ordeal that moved the entire realm to anguish. He refused every offering of funeral aid from officials and neighbors alike. When the court soon summoned him back to office, Jun pleaded repeatedly to be allowed to complete his mourning — each time a gracious edict refused. On reaching the capital, the emperor saw how ruinously grief had wasted him; his face changed, and turning to his ministers he said: "Seeing Xue Jun in such mourning — I cannot keep grief from entering my own heart. " He sighed in wonder for a long while. Jun ultimately could not survive his grief; illness took him before mourning ended. 吾以不造，幼丁艱酷，窮遊約處，屢絕簞瓢。 晚生早孤，不聞《詩》《禮》，賴奉先人貽厥之訓，獲稟母氏聖善之規，負笈裹糧，不憚艱遠，從師就業，欲罷不能。 砥行厲心，困而彌篤，服膺教義，爰至長成。 自釋耒登朝，於茲二十三年矣。 雖官非聞達，而祿喜逮親，庶保期頤，得終色養。 何圖精誠無感，禍酷薦臻，兄弟俱被奪情，苫廬靡申哀訴。 是用扣心泣血，隕氣摧魂者也。 既而創巨釁深，不勝荼毒，啟手啟足，幸及全歸。 使夫死而有知，得從先人於地下矣，豈非至願哉。 但念爾伶俜孤宦，遠在邊服，顧此恨恨，如何可言。 適已有書，冀得與汝面訣，忍死待汝，已曆一旬。 汝既未來，便成今古，緬然永別，為恨何言。 勉之哉，勉之哉！
Fate has never favored me: as a child I suffered bitter loss, wandered in destitution, and often went without even the barest food. Born late into the world and orphaned early, I never properly studied the Odes and Rites; yet I inherited my forebears' teaching and my mother's wise guidance, packed books and provisions, traveled however far it took to find instruction, and could never bring myself to stop. I hardened my character through adversity, held fast to what I was taught, and carried that discipline into manhood. From the day I left the fields for public office, twenty-three years have passed. Though I never rose to eminence, my salary reached my mother, and I hoped she might live to great age so I could serve her to the end of her days. Who could have imagined that pure devotion would go unanswered, that catastrophe would strike again and again, that both brothers would be torn from mourning, and that I could not even fully grieve in my thatched hut? That is why my heart seems ready to burst, why tears come like blood, why my spirit itself feels shattered. My wound runs too deep, the pain unbearable — yet if I may depart whole in body, as one opens hand and foot at death, I count myself fortunate. If the dead have awareness, I shall rejoin my ancestors below — could any wish be dearer? Yet I think of you, alone in distant service on the frontier, and my regret is more than words can hold. I wrote hoping to see you once more; clinging to life I have waited — ten days have already gone by. You never came, and what might have been is already past; faced with this final parting, I have no words for my sorrow. Strive on — strive on!
11
書成而絕，時年四十二。 有司以聞，高祖為之屑涕，降使齎冊書弔祭曰：「皇帝諮故考功侍郎薛濬：於戲！ 惟爾操履貞和，器業詳敏，允膺列宿，勤謇克彰。 及遘私艱，奄從毀滅。 嘉爾誠孝，感於朕懷，奠酹有加，抑惟朝典。 故遣使人，指申往命，魂而有靈，歆茲榮渥。 嗚呼哀哉！ 」濬性清儉，死之日，家無遺資。 濬初為童兒時，與宗中諸兒遊戲於澗濱。 見一黃蛇有角及足，召群兒共視，了無見者。 濬以為不祥，歸大憂悴。 母逼而問之，濬以實對。 時有胡僧詣宅乞食，濬母怖而告之，僧曰：「此乃兒之吉應。 且是兒也，早有名位，然壽不過六七耳。 」言終而出，忽然不見，時咸異之。 既而終於四十二，六七之言，於是驗矣。 子乾福，武安郡司倉書佐。
He finished the letter and breathed his last at the age of forty-two. When officials reported his death, Emperor Wen wept and dispatched an envoy with a formal letter of condolence: "The Emperor addresses the late Vice Director of the Evaluation Department, Xue Jun: Alas! Your character was upright and gentle, your talents thorough and sharp; you shone among the officers of state, and your diligence and integrity were plain to all. Then private mourning overtook you, and grief destroyed you in its course. Your filial devotion moved me deeply; therefore extra offerings are made, in keeping with the rites of the court. Hence this envoy, to deliver the throne's tribute; if your spirit endures, may it receive this grace. Alas, how mournful! " Jun was by nature pure and frugal; when he died his household had nothing left. When Jun was still a boy, he played with kinsmen his age beside a stream. He saw a yellow serpent with horns and feet and called the other boys to look, but none of them could see it. Jun took it as a bad omen and went home deeply troubled. His mother pressed him for an explanation, and he told her what had happened. A foreign monk came to the house begging food; Jun's mother, frightened, told him what had happened, and the monk said: "This is an auspicious sign for your son. Moreover, the boy will win fame and office early, yet his life will not pass sixty or seventy. " When he had finished speaking he left, and vanished abruptly; everyone at the time found it strange. He died at forty-two, and the monk's words about "six or seven" were thus fulfilled. His son Qianfu served as Clerk in the Granary Office of Wu'an Commandery.

○王頒
○ Wang Ban
13
王頒，字景彥，太原祁人也。 祖神念，梁左衛將軍。 父僧辯，太尉。 頒少俶儻，有文武幹局。 其父平侯景，留頒質於荊州，遇元帝為周師所陷，頒因入關。 聞其父為陳武帝所殺，號慟而絕，食頃乃蘇，哭泣不絕聲，毀瘠骨立。 至服闋，常布衣蔬食，藉槁而臥。 周明帝嘉之，召授左侍上士，累遷漢中太守，尋拜儀同三司。 開皇初，以平蠻功，加開府，封蛇丘縣公。 獻取陳之策，上覽而異之，召與相見，言畢而歔欷，上為之改容。 及大舉伐陳，頒自請行，率徒數百人，從韓擒先鋒夜濟。 力戰被傷，恐不堪復鬥，悲感嗚咽。 夜中因睡，夢有人授藥，比寤而創不痛，時人以為孝感。 及陳滅，頒密召父時士卒，得千餘人，對之涕泣。 其間壯士或問頒曰：「郎君來破陳國，滅其社稷，讎恥已雪，而悲哀不止者，將為霸先早死，不得手刃之邪？ 請發其丘壟，斷櫬焚骨，亦可申孝心矣。 」頒頓顙陳謝，額盡流血，答之曰：「其為帝王，墳塋甚大，恐一宵發掘，不及其屍，更至明朝，事乃彰露，若之何？ 」諸人請具鍬鍤，一旦皆萃。 於是夜發其陵，剖棺，見陳武帝須並不落，其本皆出自骨中。 頒遂焚骨取灰，投水而飲之。 既而自縛，歸罪於晉王。 王表其狀，高祖曰：「朕以義平陳，王頒所為，亦孝義之道也，朕何忍罪之！ 」舍而不問。 有司錄其戰功，將加柱國，賜物五千段，頒固辭曰：「臣緣國威靈，得雪怨恥，本心徇私，非是為國，所加官賞，終不敢當。 」高祖從之。 拜代州刺史，甚有惠政。 母憂去職。 後為齊州刺史，卒官，時年五十二。 弟頍，見《文學傳》。
Wang Ban, styled Jingyan, was a native of Qi in Taiyuan. His grandfather Shennian was General of the Left Guards in Liang. His father Sengbian was Grand Marshal. In youth Ban was bold and openhanded, with talent for both civil and military affairs. His father pacified Hou Jing and left Ban as a hostage in Jingzhou; when Emperor Yuan was overrun by Zhou forces, Ban entered the Pass. Learning that Chen Wudi had killed his father, he wailed until he fainted; after the space of a meal he revived, wept without stopping, and wasted away until only bone remained. When mourning ended he wore only plain cloth and ate simple fare, sleeping on straw. Emperor Ming of Zhou commended him, appointed him Left Attendant Senior Master, promoted him to Governor of Hanzhong, and soon made him Pillar of State, Third Rank. At the beginning of Kaihuang, for pacifying the Man tribes he was granted an open office and enfeoffed Duke of Sheqiu County. He submitted a plan for conquering Chen; the emperor read it with wonder, summoned him for an audience, and when they had finished speaking sighed and wept, his expression altered with emotion. When the great campaign against Chen was launched, Ban volunteered to take the field at the head of several hundred men and crossed the river by night with Han Qin's vanguard. He fought hard and was wounded; afraid he could fight no more, he wept in grief. He fell asleep at night and dreamed that someone gave him medicine; when he woke the wound no longer hurt, and people attributed it to filial feeling moving Heaven. After Chen fell, Ban secretly gathered more than a thousand of his father's old soldiers and wept before them. One veteran asked him: "You have destroyed Chen and avenged your house—why does your grief never end? Is it because Chen Wudi died before you could kill him yourself? Open his tomb, break the coffin, and burn his bones—that would satisfy your filial heart. " Ban bowed until his forehead bled and answered: "He was an emperor; his tomb is vast. If we dig for one night we may not reach the body, and by morning the deed will be exposed—what then? " The men brought spades and mattocks and gathered at once. That night they opened the mausoleum and cut open the coffin. Chen Wudi's beard had not fallen; its roots still grew from the bone. Ban burned the bones, mixed the ash with water, and drank it. Then he bound himself and went to confess to the Prince of Jin. The prince reported the matter; Gaozu said: "I conquered Chen in the name of righteousness. What Wang Ban did belongs to filial duty as well—how could I punish him? " He pardoned him and took no further action. " The authorities recorded his battle honors and proposed making him a Pillar of State with five thousand bolts of goods; Ban refused firmly: "I avenged my father by the state's power, not for the state's sake but from private feeling—I cannot accept rank or reward. " Gaozu assented. He was appointed Governor of Daizhou and governed with notable kindness. When his mother died he left office. Later he became Governor of Qizhou and died in office at fifty-two. His younger brother Yi is treated in the "Literary Biographies."
14
○楊慶
○ Yang Qing
15
楊慶，字伯悅，河間人也。 祖玄，父剛，並以至孝知名。 慶美姿儀，性辯慧。 年十六，齊國子博士徐遵明見而異之。 及長，頗涉書記。 年二十五，郡察孝廉，以侍養不行。 其母有疾，不解襟帶者七旬。 及居母憂，哀毀骨立，負土成墳。 齊文宣帝表其門閭，賜帛三十匹，綿十屯，粟五十石。 高祖受禪，屢加褒賞，擢授儀同三司，版授平陽太守。 年八十五，終於家。
Yang Qing, styled Boyue, was a native of Hejian. His grandfather Xuan and father Gang were both famed for utmost filial piety. Qing was handsome in bearing and quick and clever by nature. At sixteen, Xu Zunming, Doctor of the National University in Qi, saw him and was impressed. As he matured he gained some skill in written records. At twenty-five the commandery nominated him Filial and Incorrupt, but he declined to serve because he was caring for his parents. When his mother fell ill he did not unfasten his belt for seventy days. In mourning for his mother his grief wasted him to the bone; he carried earth to raise the burial mound. Emperor Wenxuan of Qi marked his gate and lane and granted thirty bolts of silk, ten tuns of cotton, and fifty shi of grain. When Gaozu took the throne he repeatedly rewarded him, made him Pillar of State Third Rank, and appointed him Governor of Pingyang by patent. He died at home at eighty-five.

○郭俊
○ Guo Jun
17
郭俊，字弘乂，太原文水人也。 家門雍睦，七葉共居，犬豕同乳，烏鵲通巢，時人以為義感之應。 州縣上其事，上遣平昌公宇文弼詣其家勞問之。 治書御史柳彧巡省河北，表其門閭。 漢王諒為并州總管，聞而嘉歎，賜兄弟二十餘人衣各一襲。
Guo Jun, styled Hongyi, was a native of Wenshui in Taiyuan. His household was harmonious: seven generations lived under one roof; dogs and pigs nursed together and crows and magpies shared nests—people saw it as a sign that righteousness had moved heaven and earth. District and prefecture reported the case; the emperor sent Duke of Pingchang Yuwen Bi to visit and commend the family. Imperial Censor Liu Yu, touring Hebei, memorialized to mark their gate and lane. Prince Liang of Han, as Governor-General of Bingzhou, heard and admired them, granting each of more than twenty brothers a suit of clothes.
18
○田翼
○ Tian Yi
19
田翼，不知何許人也。 性至孝，養母以孝聞。 其後母臥疾歲餘，翼親易燥濕，母食則食，母不食則不食。 母患暴痢，翼謂中毒，遂親嘗惡。 及母終，翼一慟而絕，其妻亦不勝哀而死，鄉人厚共葬之。
Tian Yi—no one knows where he came from. He was utterly filial by nature and famed for caring for his mother. When his mother lay ill for more than a year, he tended her bedding himself; he ate only when she ate and fasted when she fasted. When his mother suffered sudden dysentery, Yi feared poison and tasted her foul discharge himself. When his mother died, Yi died in a single burst of grief; his wife, unable to bear the sorrow, died as well, and the villagers gave them a joint burial.
20
○紐回
○ Niu Hui
21
紐回，字孝政，河東安邑人也。 性至孝，周武成中，父母喪，廬於墓側，負土成墳。 廬前生麻一株，高丈許，圍之合拱，枝葉鬱茂，冬夏恆青。 有烏棲其上，回舉聲哭，烏即悲鳴，時人異之。 周武帝表其閭，擢授甘棠令。 開皇初卒。
Niu Hui, styled Xiaozheng, was a native of Anyi in Hedong. Utterly filial by nature, in the Wucheng era of Zhou, when his parents died he built a hut beside the grave and carried earth to raise the mound. Before the hut a hemp stalk sprang up more than ten feet tall, thick as an arm-span, its foliage lush and green through winter and summer alike. A crow perched on it; when Hui wept aloud the crow mourned with him, and people marveled. Emperor Wu of Zhou marked his lane and appointed him Magistrate of Gantang. He died early in the Kaihuang era.
22
子士雄，少質直孝友，喪父，復廬於墓側，負土成墳。 其庭前有一槐樹，先甚鬱茂，及士雄居喪，樹遂枯死。 服闋還宅，死樹復榮。 高祖聞之，歎其父子至孝，下詔褒揚，號其所居為累德里。
His son Shixiong was plain, upright, filial, and friendly; when his father died he too built a hut beside the grave and carried earth to raise the mound. A locust tree before the courtyard had been flourishing; when Shixiong entered mourning it withered and died. When mourning ended and he returned home, the dead tree flourished again. Gaozu heard and sighed at the father and son's filial piety, issued an edict of praise, and named their quarter Accumulated Virtue Lane.

○劉士俊
○ Liu Shijun
24
劉士俊，彭城人也。 性至孝，丁母喪，絕而復蘇者數矣。 勺飲不入口者七日，廬於墓側，負土成墳，列植松柏。 狐狼馴擾，為之取食。 高祖受禪，表其門閭。
Liu Shijun was a native of Pengcheng. Utterly filial by nature, in mourning for his mother he fainted dead and revived several times. For seven days he took neither food nor drink; he built a hut beside the grave, carried earth to raise the mound, and planted rows of pine and cypress. Foxes and wolves grew tame at the graveside, and he brought them food. When Gaozu took the throne he marked his gate and lane.

○郎方貴
○ Lang Fanggui
26
郎方貴，淮南人也。 少有志尚，與從父弟雙貴同居。 開皇中，方貴嘗因出行遇雨，淮水泛長，於津所寄渡，船人怒之，撾方貴臂折。 至家，其弟雙貴驚問所由，方貴具言之。 雙貴恚恨，遂向津毆擊船人致死。 守津者執送之縣官，案問其狀，以方貴為首，當死，雙貴從坐，當流。 兄弟二人爭為首坐，縣司不能斷，送詣州。 兄弟各引咎，州不能定，二人爭欲赴水而死。 州狀以聞，上聞而異之，特原其罪，表其門閭，賜物百段，後為州主簿。
Lang Fanggui was a native of Huainan. In youth he had high resolve and lived with his younger cousin Shuanggui. During Kaihuang, Fanggui went out in the rain; the Huai had risen, and at a ferry a boatman, angered, beat him until his arm broke. At home his cousin Shuanggui asked what had happened, and Fanggui told him everything. Shuanggui, furious, went to the ferry and beat the boatman to death. The ferry guard seized them; the magistrate found Fanggui the principal offender liable to death and Shuanggui an accomplice liable to exile. Both brothers claimed to be the principal offender; the district could not decide and sent them to the prefecture. Each took all blame on himself; the prefecture could not decide, and both tried to drown themselves. The prefect reported to the throne; the emperor was moved, pardoned them outright, marked their gate and lane, granted a hundred bolts of goods, and later appointed Fanggui prefectural chief clerk.
27
○翟普林
○ Zhai Pulin
28
翟普林，楚丘人也。 性仁孝，事親以孝聞。 州郡辟命，皆固辭不就，躬耕色養，鄉鄰謂為楚丘先生。 後父母疾，親易燥濕，不解衣者七旬。 大業初，父母俱終，哀毀殆將滅性。 廬於墓側，負土為墳，盛冬不衣繒絮，唯著單縗而已。 家有一烏犬，隨其在墓，若普林哀臨，犬亦悲號，見者嗟異焉。 有二鵲巢其廬前柏樹，每入其廬，馴狎無所驚懼。 大業中，司隸巡察，奏其孝感，擢授孝陽令。
Zhai Pulin was a native of Qiu in Chu. Benevolent and filial by nature, he was famed for devotion to his parents. He declined every appointment from prefecture and commandery, farmed with his own hands, and cared for his parents in person; neighbors called him the Master of Qiu. When his parents fell ill he tended their bedding and did not change his clothes for seventy days. Early in Daye both parents died; his grief nearly destroyed him. He built a hut beside the grave and carried earth to raise the mound; in deep winter he wore no padded silk, only a single layer of mourning hemp. The household had a black dog that followed him to the grave; whenever Pulin wept the dog wailed too, and onlookers marveled. Two magpies nested in the cypress before his mourning hut and would enter the hut without fear whenever he was there. During Daye the metropolitan inspector reported his filial deeds, and he was appointed magistrate of Xiaoyang.
29
○李德饒
○ Li Derao
30
李德饒，趙郡柏人人也。 祖徹，魏尚書右丞。 父純，開皇中為介州長史。 德饒少聰敏好學，有至性，宗黨咸敬之。 弱冠為校書郎，仍直內史省，參掌文翰。 轉監察御史，糾正不避貴戚。 大業三年，遷司隸從事，每巡四方，理雪冤枉，褒揚孝悌。 雖位秩未通，其德行為當時所重。 凡與交結，皆海內髦彥。 性至孝，父母寢疾，輒終日不食，十旬不解衣。 及丁憂，水漿不入口五日，哀慟嘔血數升。 及送葬之日，會仲冬積雪，行四十餘里，單縗徒跣，號踴幾絕。 會葬者千餘人，莫不為之流涕。 後甘露降於庭樹，有鳩巢其廬。 納言楊達巡省河北，詣其廬吊慰之，因改所居村名孝敬村，裡為和順裡。 後為金河長，未之官，值群盜蜂起，賊帥格謙、孫宣雅等十餘頭，聚眾於渤海。 時有敕許其歸首，謙等懼不敢降，以德饒信行有聞，遣使奏曰：「若使德饒來者，即相率歸首。 」帝於是遣德饒往渤海慰諭諸賊。 行至冠氏，會他盜攻陷縣城，德饒見害。
Li Derao was a native of Bai in Zhao Commandery. His grandfather Che was Right Assistant Minister of the Secretariat in Wei. His father Chun served as Chief Administrator of Jiezhou during Kaihuang. In youth Derao was clever and studious, utterly sincere by nature, and all his kin respected him. At twenty he became Collator and served in the Palace Historian's office, helping manage documents. He became Investigating Censor and in his corrections did not spare the powerful. In Daye year three he was promoted metropolitan attendant; on his tours he cleared wrongful convictions and commended filial and fraternal conduct. Though his rank was not yet high, his character was greatly esteemed. Everyone he befriended was a leading figure of the realm. Utterly filial by nature, when his parents fell ill he would not eat for whole days at a time and did not change his clothes for a hundred days. In mourning he took no food or drink for five days and wept blood in his grief. On the burial day, in mid-winter snow, he walked more than forty li in thin mourning garb and bare feet, wailing until he nearly died. More than a thousand mourners attended; none left without tears. Later sweet dew fell on the trees in his courtyard, and a turtledove nested in his mourning hut. Chief Minister Yang Da, touring Hebei, visited his mourning hut to console him and renamed his village Filial Respect Village and his lane Harmonious Lane. He was appointed chief of Jinhe but never took office; bandits rose everywhere, and more than ten chiefs including Ge Qian and Sun Xuanya gathered forces in Bohai. An edict offered them amnesty, but they were afraid to surrender; knowing Derao's reputation for integrity, they sent word: "If Derao comes, we will surrender together." " The emperor then sent Derao to Bohai to reassure the rebels. At Guanshi other rebels seized the county seat, and Derao was killed.
31
其弟德佋，性重然諾。 大業末，為離石郡司法書佐，太守楊子崇特禮之。 及義兵起，子崇遇害，棄屍城下，德佋赴哭盡哀，收瘞之。 至介休，詣義師，請葬子崇。 大將軍嘉之，因贈子崇官，令德佋為使者，往離石禮葬子崇焉。
His younger brother Detuo was a man who kept his word. Late in Daye he was judicial clerk of Lishi Commandery; Governor Yang Zichong treated him with special respect. When the righteous armies rose, Zichong was killed and his body left below the wall; Detuo mourned him, gathered the corpse, and buried it. He went to Jiexiu, sought out the righteous army, and asked permission to bury Zichong properly. The commander praised him, posthumously enfeoffed Zichong, and sent Detuo as envoy to Lishi to bury him with full honors.

○華秋
○ Hua Qiu
33
華秋，汲郡臨河人也。 幼喪父，事母以孝聞。 家貧，傭賃為養。 其母遇患，秋容貌毀悴，須鬢頓改，州裡咸嗟異之。 及母終之後，遂絕櫛沐，發盡禿落。 廬於墓側，負土成墳，有人欲助之者，秋輒拜而止之。 大業初，調狐皮，郡縣大獵。 有一兔，人逐之，奔入秋廬中，匿秋膝下。 獵人至廬所，異而免之。 自爾此兔常宿廬中，馴其左右。 郡縣嘉其孝感，俱以狀聞。 煬帝降使勞問，表其門閭。 後群盜起，常往來廬之左右，咸相誡曰：「勿犯孝子。 」鄉人賴秋而全者甚眾。
Hua Qiu was a native of Linhe in Ji Commandery. Orphaned early, he was famed for filial service to his mother. The family was poor, and he hired himself out to support her. When his mother fell ill, Qiu wasted away; his beard turned gray overnight, and neighbors marveled. After his mother died he stopped combing or washing, and his hair fell out completely. He built a hut beside the grave and carried earth to raise the mound; anyone who tried to help he bowed to and turned away. Early in Daye the court demanded fox pelts, and the district launched a great hunt. Hunters chased a rabbit that fled into Qiu's mourning hut and hid beneath his knees. The hunters came to the hut, marveled, and let the rabbit go. After that the rabbit lived in the hut and grew tame beside him. The prefecture and district reported his filial deeds to the throne. Emperor Yang sent an envoy to commend him and marked his gate and lane. When bandits later roamed the area they warned one another: "Do not harm the filial son." " Many villagers owed their safety to him."

○徐孝肅
○ Xu Xiaosu
35
徐孝肅，汲郡人也。 宗族數千家，多以豪侈相尚，唯孝肅性儉約，事親以孝聞。 雖在幼齒，宗黨間每有爭訟，皆至孝肅所平論之，為孝肅所短者，無不引咎而退。 孝肅早孤，不識父，及長，問其母父狀，因求畫工，圖其形像，構廟置之而定省焉，朔望享祭。 養母至孝，數十年，家人未見其有忿恚之色。 及母老疾，孝肅親易燥濕，憂悴數年，見者無不悲悼。 母終，孝肅茹蔬飲水，盛冬單縗，毀瘠骨立。 祖父母、父母墓皆負土成墳，廬於墓所四十餘載，被髮徒跣，遂以身終。
Xu Xiaosu was a native of Ji Commandery. His clan numbered thousands of households, most competing in luxury, but Xiaosu alone was frugal and famed for filial piety toward his parents. Even as a boy, when the clan quarreled they brought disputes to Xiaosu for judgment; whoever he found at fault withdrew in shame. Orphaned early, he never knew his father; as an adult he asked his mother what his father looked like, had a portrait painted, built a shrine, and offered sacrifices at dawn and dusk and at each new and full moon. He cared for his mother with perfect filial piety; for decades no one in the household ever saw anger on his face. When his mother grew old and ill he tended her bedding himself, grieving for years; all who saw him were moved to tears. After her death he ate only vegetables and water, wore thin mourning garb even in deep winter, and wasted to the bone. He raised burial mounds for grandparents and parents alike and kept a mourning hut at the graves for more than forty years, barefoot with hair unbound, until he died there.
36
其弟德備，聰敏，通涉五經，河朔間稱為儒者。 德備終，子處默又廬於墓側，奕葉稱孝焉。
His younger brother Debei was clever, mastered the Five Classics, and was known as a scholar north of the Yellow River. When Debei died, his son Chumo built another mourning hut beside the grave; filial piety ran through the generations.
37
史臣曰：昔者弘愛敬之理，必籍王公大人，近古敦孝友之情，多茅屋之下。 而彥師、道賾，或家傳纓冕，或身誓山河，遂乃負土成墳，致毀滅性。 雖乖先王之制，亦觀過以知仁矣。 郎貴昆弟，爭死而身全，田翼夫妻，俱喪而名立，德饒仁懷群盜，德佋義感興王，亦足稱也。 紐回、劉俊之倫，翟林、華秋之輩，或茂草嘉樹榮枯於庭宇，或走獸翔禽馴狎於廬墓，非夫孝悌之至，通於神明者乎！
The historian remarks: In antiquity the teaching of love and reverence depended on kings and great men; in later ages true filial piety arose mostly in humble homes. Yet men such as Yanshi and Daoyin—some from noble houses, some who had sworn themselves to the realm—still carried earth for graves and nearly destroyed themselves in grief. Though they overstepped the sage kings' rules, one may still read their excess as proof of humaneness. Fanggui's brothers vied to die yet both lived; Tian Yi and his wife died together yet won lasting fame; Derao moved rebels by kindness, Detuo moved a conqueror by loyalty—all worthy of praise. Men such as Niu Hui, Liu Shijun, Zhai Pulin, and Hua Qiu saw trees flourish and wither at their gates, beasts and birds grow tame at their mourning huts—surely only filial piety raised to the utmost can touch the spirits thus!
