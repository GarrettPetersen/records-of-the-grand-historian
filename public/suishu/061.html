宇文述宇文述，字伯通，代郡武川人也。 本姓破野頭，役屬鮮卑俟豆歸，後從其主為宇文氏。 父盛，周上柱國。 述少驍銳，便弓馬。 年十一時，有相者謂述曰：「公子善自愛，後當位極人臣。 」周武帝時，以父軍功，起家拜開府。 述性恭謹沈密，周大塚宰宇文護甚愛之，以本官領護親信。 及帝親總萬機，召為左宮伯，累遷英果中大夫，賜爵博陵郡公，尋改封濮陽郡公。
Yu Wenshu, whose courtesy name was Botong, came from Wuchuan in Dai Commandery. The family had originally borne the name Poyetou and had been subordinated to the Xianbei leader Hudougui; they later took the surname Yuwen when their lord did. His father Sheng had held the Zhou rank of Superior Pillar of State. In youth Shu was bold and keen, and excelled at archery and horsemanship. At the age of eleven a fortune-teller told him, "Young sir, cherish yourself—you will one day stand at the pinnacle of ministerial power." During the reign of Emperor Wu of Zhou he entered office on his father's military merit and was made Defender-in-Chief. Shu was respectful, prudent, and taciturn; Yuwen Hu, the Zhou regent, favored him greatly and kept him in his original post while putting him in charge of Hu's personal entourage. Once the emperor took the reins of government himself, Shu was summoned to serve as Left Palatial Steward, rose through the ranks to Valiant Fruitful Grand Master, was enfeoffed as Duke of Boling, and shortly thereafter had his title changed to Duke of Puyang.

高祖為丞相，尉迥作亂相州，述以行軍總管率步騎三千，從韋孝寬擊之。 軍至河陽，迥遣將李俊攻懷州，述別擊俊軍，破之。 又與諸將擊尉惇於永橋，述先鋒陷陣，俘馘甚眾。 平尉迥，每戰有功，超拜上柱國，進爵褒國公，賜縑三千匹。 開皇初，拜右衛大將軍。 平陳之役，複以行軍總管率眾三萬，自六合而濟。 時韓擒、賀若弼兩軍趣丹陽，述進據石頭，以為聲援。 陳主既擒，而蕭瓛、蕭岩據東吳之地，擁兵拒守。 述領行軍總管元契、張默言等討之，水陸兼進。 落叢公燕榮以舟師自海至，亦受述節度。 上下詔曰：「公鴻勳大業，名高望重，奉國之誠，久所知悉。 金陵之寇，既已清蕩，而吳會之地，東路為遙，蕭岩、蕭瓛，並在其處。 公率將戎旅，撫慰彼方，振揚國威，宣佈朝化。 以公明略，乘勝而往，風行電掃，自當稽服。 若使干戈不用，黎庶獲安，方副朕懷，公之力也。 」陳永新侯陳君范自晉陵奔瓛，並軍合勢。 見述軍且至，瓛懼，立柵于晉陵城東，又絕塘道，留兵拒述。 瓛自義興入太湖，圖掩述後。 述進破其柵，回兵擊瓛，大敗之，斬瓛司馬曹勒叉。 前軍複陷吳州，瓛以餘眾保包山，燕榮擊破之。 述進至奉公埭，蕭岩、陳君範等以會稽請降。 述許之，二人面縛路左，吳會悉平。 以功拜一子開府，賜物三千段，拜安州總管。
While Emperor Wen was still chief minister, Yu Chi rose in rebellion at Xiangzhou; Shu, as campaigning commander, led three thousand infantry and cavalry under Wei Xiaokuan to strike him. The army reached Heyang; Chi dispatched Li Jun against Huaizhou, and Shu detached a force, routed Jun's column, and broke it. He again joined the other commanders in attacking Chi's lieutenant Yu Dun at Yong Bridge; leading the van, Shu smashed the enemy line and took a great many prisoners and heads. In the campaign that pacified Chi he distinguished himself in every battle; he was promoted in one step to Superior Pillar of State, raised to Duke of Baoguo, and given three thousand bolts of silk. Early in the Kaihuang era he was made General-in-Chief of the Right Guard. During the conquest of Chen he again served as campaigning commander, leading thirty thousand men across the river from Liuhe. While Han Qin and He Ruo were driving on Danyang with two armies, Shu pushed forward and seized Stone City to lend them weight from the flank. Once the Chen emperor was taken, Xiao Fan and Xiao Yan still held eastern Wu with troops massed for resistance. Shu took Yuan Qi, Zhang Moyan, and other campaigning commanders against them in a combined land-and-sea advance. Yan Rong, Duke of Luocong, arrived by sea with a naval force and also placed himself under Shu's orders. The throne issued an edict: "Your great service and far-reaching achievements, your eminence and authority, and your long-proven loyalty to the realm are well known to us." "The enemy at Jinling is already crushed, but Wu and the coastal southeast still lie far down the eastern route, and both Xiao Yan and Xiao Fan are there." "Take your armies, pacify that region, display the power of the state, and spread the court's civilizing rule." "With your discernment, pressing the advantage, you will sweep them aside like wind and lightning, and they will surely submit." "If war can be avoided and the people left in peace, that would fulfill Our wishes—and it would be your doing." Chen Junfan, Marquis of Yongxin, fled from Jinling to join Fan, and their forces merged. When Fan learned that Shu's army was approaching, he grew alarmed, threw up stockades east of Jincheng, severed the canal routes, and left troops to block Shu. Fan himself came from Yixing into Lake Tai, plotting to take Shu from behind. Shu pushed forward, stormed the stockades, wheeled about to hit Fan, routed him utterly, and killed Fan's staff major Cao Lecha. The vanguard seized Wuzhou again; Fan rallied his remnants on Mount Bao, but Yan Rong broke them there. When Shu reached Fenggong Dam, Xiao Yan, Chen Junfan, and others surrendered Kuaiji to him. Shu accepted; the two came forward bound at the roadside, and Wu and the coastal districts were fully pacified. For these deeds one of his sons was made Defender-in-Chief, he received three thousand rolls of goods, and he was appointed military governor of Anzhou.
3
時晉王廣鎮揚州，甚善於述，欲述近己，因奏為壽州刺史總管。 王時陰有奪宗之志，請計於述，述曰：「皇太子失愛已久，令德不聞於天下。 大王仁孝著稱，才能蓋世，數經將領，深有大功。 主上之與內宮，鹹所鍾愛，四海之望，實歸於大王。 然廢立者，國家之大事，處人父子骨肉之間，誠非易謀也。 然能移主上者，唯楊素耳。 素之謀者，唯其弟約。 述雅知約，請朝京師，與約相見，共圖廢立。 」晉王大悅，多齎金寶，資述入關。 述數請約，盛陳器玩，與之酣暢，因而共博，每佯不勝，所齎金寶盡輸之。 約所得既多，稍以謝述。 述因曰：「此晉王之賜，令述與公為歡樂耳。 」約大驚曰：「何為者？ 」述因為王申意。 約然其說，退言於素，素亦從之。 於是素每與述謀事。 晉王與述情好益密，命述子士及尚南陽公主，前後賞賜不可勝計。 及晉王為皇太子，以述為左衛率。 舊令，率官第四品，上以述素貴，遂進率品為第三，其見重如此。
Yang Guang, Prince of Jin, then held Yangzhou and thought highly of Shu; wanting him close at hand, he had him appointed inspector-general of Shouzhou. The prince already harbored designs on the succession and sought Shu's advice. Shu said, "The crown prince has long fallen from grace, and his virtue is scarcely known in the realm." "Your Highness is renowned for benevolence and filial duty, your abilities overshadow your peers, you have commanded armies again and again, and your achievements are weighty." "The emperor and the inner palace both favor you, and the hopes of the empire truly rest with Your Highness." "Yet to depose and install an heir is a matter of state, and it stands between father and son—hardly a simple plot." "Still, the only man who can sway the emperor is Yang Su." "Of those Su trusts in counsel, only his brother Yue matters." "Shu knows Yue well; let me go to the capital, meet him, and plan the change with him." The prince was delighted and gave Shu a lavish store of gold and jewels to take into the capital. Shu called on Yue again and again, displaying fine furnishings and drinking deep with him, then gambling—and always losing on purpose until every treasure he had brought had passed to Yue. When Yue had won a great deal, he began to thank Shu. Shu said, "All of this was the Prince of Jin's gift—he meant only that we should enjoy ourselves." Yue was astonished and asked, "What does this mean?" Shu then explained the prince's designs. Yue accepted the argument, spoke to Su on his return, and Su agreed as well. From then on Su and Shu plotted together on every matter. The prince drew ever closer to Shu, gave Shu's son Shiji the Princess of Nanyang in marriage, and heaped on them rewards beyond reckoning. When the prince became crown prince, Shu was made commander of the Left Guard. By statute the guard commander's post was fourth rank, but because Shu had long been prestigious the emperor raised it to third rank—such was the regard he enjoyed.

煬帝嗣位，拜左衛大將軍，改封許國公。 ，加開府儀同三司，每冬正朝會，輒給鼓吹一部。 從幸榆林，時鐵勒契弊歌棱攻敗吐谷渾，其部攜散，遂遣使請降求救。 帝令述以兵屯西平之臨羌城，撫納降附。 吐谷渾見述擁強兵，懼不敢降，遂西遁。 述領鷹揚郎將梁元禮、張峻、崔師等追之，至曼頭城，攻拔之，斬三千餘級。 乘勝至赤水城，複拔之。 其餘黨走屯丘尼川，述進擊，大破之，獲其王公、尚書、將軍二百人，前後虜男女四千口而還。 渾主南走雪山，其故地皆空。 帝大悅。 明年，從帝西幸，巡至金山，登燕支，述每為斥候。 時渾賊複寇張掖，進擊走之。 還至江都宮，敕述與蘇威常典選舉，參預朝政。 述時貴重，委任與蘇威等，其親愛則過之。 帝所得遠方貢獻及四時口味，輒見班賜，中使相望於道。 述善於供奉，俯仰折旋，容止便辟，宿衛者鹹取則焉。 又有巧思，凡有所裝飾，皆出人意表。 數以奇服異物進獻宮掖，由是帝彌悅焉。 時述貴幸，言無不從，勢傾朝廷。 左衛將軍張瑾與述連官，嘗有評議，偶不中意，述張目叱之，瑾惶懼而走，文武百僚莫敢違忤。 然性貪鄙，知人有珍異之物，必求取之。 富商大賈及隴右諸胡子弟，述皆接以恩意，呼之為兒。 由是競加饋遺，金寶累積。 後庭曳羅綺者數百，家僮千餘人，皆控良馬，被服金玉。 述之寵遇，當時莫與為比。
When Emperor Yang took the throne he made Shu General-in-Chief of the Left Guard and changed his title to Duke of Xu. He was further granted the privileges of an open office with protocol equal to the Three Ducal Ministers, and at each winter court audience he received a full set of ceremonial musicians. On an imperial tour to Yulin, the Tiele leader Qibili Gelen routed the Tuyuhun and scattered their tribes, and they sent envoys suing for mercy and aid. The emperor ordered Shu to garrison Linqiang in Xiping and win over the surrendering tribes. The Tuyuhun, seeing Shu's strong force, feared to submit and fled westward. Shu pursued with the Valiant Hawk captains Liang Yuanli, Zhang Jun, and Cui Shi, seized Mantou city, and killed more than three thousand. Pressing the victory he took Chishui city as well. The remnant bands fled to the Qiuni River; Shu attacked and smashed them, capturing two hundred princes, ministers, and generals and altogether four thousand men and women before returning. The Tuyuhun king fled south toward Snow Mountain, and his former territories lay empty. The emperor was delighted. The following year he accompanied the western tour as far as Jinshan and Yanzhi, acting each day as advance scout. When Tuyuhun raiders struck Zhangye again, he drove them off in a forward attack. Back at the Jiangdu palace, the emperor charged Shu and Su Wei with routine control of appointments and a share in government. Shu stood then at the height of power, entrusted like Su Wei, but dearer to the emperor. Whatever rare tribute or seasonal delicacies reached the throne were passed on to Shu, with palace messengers riding in an unbroken stream. Shu had a gift for attendance—every bow and turn, every movement at court—so that even the palace guards took him as their model. He was inventive too: whatever he designed for display surpassed what others imagined. He often sent rare garments and curios to the inner palace, and the emperor's pleasure in him only deepened. In that era Shu's word was law at court, and his power eclipsed every other minister. Zhang Jin of the Left Guard shared duties with him; once in discussion Shu took offense, glared, and shouted him down until Jin fled in terror—after which no official dared cross him. Yet he was greedy and base: whenever he heard of some treasure in another's hands, he had to have it. Wealthy merchants and the young men of the western frontier tribes he courted with favor and called "my sons." They competed to shower him with gifts until gold and gems heaped up around him. Hundreds of women in silk filled his inner quarters; more than a thousand servants rode fine horses and wore gold and jade. No one at court stood in his shadow.

及征高麗，述為扶餘道軍將。 臨發，帝謂述曰：「禮，七十者行役以婦人從，公宜以家累自隨。 古稱婦人不入軍，謂臨戰時耳。 至於營壘之間，無所傷也。 項籍虞姬，即其故事。 」述與九軍至鴨綠水，糧盡，議欲班師。 諸將多異同，述又不測帝意。 會乙支文德來詣其營，述先與于仲文俱奉密旨，令誘執文德。 既而緩縱，文德逃歸，語在《仲文傳》。 述內不自安，遂與諸將渡水追之。 時文德見述軍中多饑色，欲疲述眾，每鬥便北。 述一日之中七戰皆捷，既恃驟勝，又內逼群議，於是遂進，東濟薩水，去平壤城三十裡，因山為營。 文德複遣使偽降，請述曰：「若旋師者，當奉高元朝行在所。 」述見士卒疲敝，不可複戰，又平壤險固，卒難致力，遂因其詐而還。 眾半濟，賊擊後軍，於是大潰，不可禁止，九軍敗績，一日一夜，還至鴨綠水，行四百五十裡。 初，渡遼九軍三十萬五千人，及還至遼東城，唯二千七百人。 帝大怒，以述等屬吏。 至東都，除名為民。 明年，帝有事遼東，複述官爵，待之如初。 從至遼東，與將軍楊義臣率兵複臨鴨綠水。 會楊玄感作亂，帝召述班師，令馳驛赴河陽，發諸郡兵以討玄感。 時玄感逼東都，聞述軍將至，懼而西遁，將圖關中。 述與刑部尚書衛玄、左禦衛將軍來護兒、武衛將軍屈突通等躡之。 至閿鄉皇天原，與玄感相及。 述與來護兒列陣當其前，遣屈突通以奇兵擊其後，大破之，遂斬玄感，傳首行在所。 賜物數千段。 複從東征，至懷遠而還。
On the eastern campaign he was commander of the Fuyu-route army. Before he marched the emperor told him, "Ritual allows a man of seventy on campaign to take his wife along—you should bring your household." "When the ancients said women do not enter the army, they meant only in battle." "In the camps there is no harm in it." "Think of Yu Ji with Xiang Yu—that is the precedent." Shu led nine armies to the Yalu; supplies ran out and the commanders debated withdrawal. The generals were divided, and Shu could not read the emperor's mind. When Yi Zhongwen came to his camp, Shu recalled that he and Yu Zhongwen had a secret order to seize him by guile. They hesitated and let him go; the full account is in the Biography of Yu Zhongwen. Uneasy at heart, Shu crossed the river in pursuit with the other commanders. Yi Zhongwen, seeing hunger in Shu's ranks, meant to wear them down and gave ground after every clash. In a single day he won seven engagements; flushed with success and pressed by his officers, he pushed on across the Sa River to within thirty li of Pyongyang and encamped on the hills. Yi Zhongwen sent envoys feigning surrender, promising that if Shu withdrew he would deliver King Gao Yuan at the king's traveling palace. Seeing his troops exhausted and Pyongyang too strong to storm, he took the feint for truth and turned back. Halfway across the river the enemy hit the rear and the army collapsed beyond control; all nine armies were shattered, and in one day and night they raced four hundred fifty li back to the Yalu. The nine armies had crossed the Liao with 305,000 men; at Liaodong only 2,700 remained. The emperor was furious and handed Shu and his peers to the law officers. At the eastern capital he was struck from the registers and reduced to commoner status. The next year, when the emperor again turned to Liaodong, Shu's titles were restored and he was treated as before. He followed to Liaodong and with Yang Yichen again brought the army to the Yalu. Yang Xuangan rose in rebellion; the emperor recalled Shu, sent him galloping by post road to Heyang, and ordered him to raise the commandery armies against Xuangan. Xuangan had been pressing Luoyang; when he heard Shu was coming he fled west toward the passes. Shu pursued with Wei Xuan of the Ministry of Justice, Lai Huer of the Left Imperial Guard, Qu Tu Tong of the Martial Guard, and others. At Huangtian Plain in Wenxiang they overtook Xuangan. Shu and Lai Huer formed line in front while Qu Tu Tong hit from the rear; they shattered Xuangan's army, cut off his head, and sent it to the emperor. He received several thousand rolls of goods as reward. He again joined the eastern campaign, advanced as far as Huaiyuan, and then returned.

突厥之圍雁門，帝懼，述請潰圍而出。 樊子蓋固諫不可，帝乃止。 及圍解，車駕次太原，議者多勸帝還京師，帝有難色。 述因奏曰：「從官妻子多在東都，便道向洛陽，自潼關而入可也。 」帝從之。 是歲，至東都，述又觀望帝意，勸幸江都，帝大悅。 述于江都遇疾，中使相望，帝將親臨視之，群臣苦諫乃止。 遂遣司宮魏氏問述曰：「必有不諱，欲何所言？ 」述二子化及、智及，時並得罪於家，述因奏曰：「化及臣之長子，早預籓邸，願陛下哀憐之。 」帝聞，泫然曰：「吾不忘也。 」及薨，帝為之廢朝，贈司徒、尚書令、十郡太守，班劍四十人，轀輬車，前後部鼓吹，諡曰恭，帝令黃門侍郎裴矩祭乙太牢，鴻臚監護喪事。 子化及，別有傳。 雲定興雲定興者，附會於述。 初，定興女為皇太子勇昭訓，及勇廢，除名配少府。 定興先得昭訓明珠絡帳，私賂於述，自是數共交遊。 定興每時節必有賂遺，並以音樂幹述。 述素好著奇服，炫耀時人。 定興為制馬韉，於後角上缺方三寸，以露白色。 世輕薄者爭放學之，謂為許公缺勢。 又遇天寒，定興曰：「入內宿衛，必當耳冷。 」述曰：「然。 」乃制裌頭巾，令深袙耳。 又學之，名為許公袙勢。 述大悅曰：「雲兄所作，必能變俗。 我聞作事可法，故不虛也。 」後帝將事四夷，大造兵器，述薦之，因敕少府工匠並取其節度。 述欲為之求官，謂定興曰：「兄所制器仗併合上心，而不得官者，為長寧兄弟猶未死耳。 」定興曰：「此無用物，何不勸上殺之。 」述因奏曰：「房陵諸子，年並成立。 今欲動兵征討，若將從駕，則守掌為難； 若留一處，又恐不可。 進退無用，請早處分。 」帝從之，因鴆殺長寧，又遣以下七弟分配嶺表，仍遣間使于路盡殺之。 五年，大閱軍實，帝稱甲仗為佳。 述奏曰：」並雲定興之功也。 」擢授少府丞。 尋代何稠為少監，轉衛尉少卿，遷左禦衛將軍，仍知少府事。 十一年，授左屯衛大將軍。
When the Turks besieged Yanmen, the emperor was terrified; Shu urged breaking out of the encirclement and fleeing. Fan Zigai argued firmly against it, and the emperor abandoned the idea. After the siege lifted the court paused at Taiyuan; many advisers urged a return to the capital, and the emperor looked uneasy. Shu memorialized: "Your followers' wives and children are mostly in the eastern capital—take the direct route to Luoyang and enter through Tong Pass." The emperor agreed. That year they reached Luoyang; reading the emperor's mood, Shu urged a move to Jiangdu, and the emperor was delighted. Shu fell ill at Jiangdu; palace messengers shuttled back and forth; the emperor meant to visit him in person until the ministers dissuaded him. He sent Lady Wei of the Inner Palace to ask Shu: "If you are keeping something back, what is it you wish to say?" Shu's sons Huaji and Zhiji had both been punished at home; he memorialized: "Huaji is my eldest son and entered the prince's household long ago—I beg Your Majesty to show him mercy." Hearing this, the emperor said through tears, "I will not forget." When Shu died the emperor canceled court, posthumously made him Minister of State and Director of the Department of State Affairs with nominal authority over ten commanderies, granted forty halberd-bearers, an imperial hearse, and full ceremonial music, gave him the posthumous name Gong, had Pei Ju offer a second-grade sacrifice, and put the Court of Honglu in charge of the funeral. His son Huaji is treated in a separate biography. Yun Dingxing curried favor with Shu. Dingxing's daughter had been Bright Instruction to Crown Prince Yong; when Yong was deposed she was struck from the rolls and sent to the Palace Supplies Office. Dingxing had earlier obtained the Bright Instruction's pearl-strung canopy and privately bribed Shu with it; thereafter they were often in each other's company. At every season Dingxing sent gifts and also used music to win Shu over. Shu had long delighted in outlandish dress that dazzled his contemporaries. Dingxing designed a saddle for him with a three-inch square cut from the rear corner to show white underneath. Frivolous men of the day copied it and called the fashion "Duke Xu's gap." When cold weather came Dingxing said, "On night duty inside the palace your ears are bound to freeze." Shu said, "Quite so." He had a layered head-wrap made that covered the ears deeply. Others copied that too and called it "Duke Xu's wrap." Shu was delighted and said, "Whatever Elder Brother Yun devises is sure to set a new fashion." They say useful work becomes a model for others—he is never wasting his effort. When the emperor later prepared for campaigns on all frontiers and ordered a vast armory, Shu recommended him, and the Palace Supplies craftsmen were ordered to follow his designs. Shu wanted an office for him and said, "Your arms and armor please the emperor, yet you cannot rise while the Changning princes still live." Dingxing said, "Those useless men—why not urge the emperor to kill them?" Shu memorialized: "The princes at Fangling are all grown men." "If we mobilize for war and they accompany the court, who will guard them?" "If we leave them in one place, that is no safer." "They are useless either way—please deal with them soon." The emperor agreed: Changning was poisoned, the seven younger brothers were sent to the far south, and agents on the road killed them all. In the fifth year the emperor held a grand review of the armory and praised the weapons. Shu memorialized that the credit belonged entirely to Yun Dingxing. Dingxing was promoted to assistant director of the Palace Supplies Office. He soon replaced He Chou as assistant superintendent, became assistant minister of the guard, rose to Left Imperial Guard general, and still oversaw Palace Supplies. In the eleventh year he was made general-in-chief of the Left Garrison Guard.
7
凡述所薦達，皆至大官。 趙行樞乙太常樂戶，家財億計，述謂為兄，多受其賄。 稱其驍勇，起家為折衝郎將。 郭衍郭衍，字彥文，自雲太原介休人也。 父以舍人從魏武帝入關，其後官至侍中。 衍少驍武，善騎射。 周陳王純引為左右，累遷大都督。 時齊氏未平，衍奉詔於天水募人，以鎮東境，得樂徙千餘家，屯於陝城。 拜使持節、車騎大將軍、儀同三司。 每有寇至，輒率所領禦之，一歲數告捷，頗為齊人所憚。 王益親任之。 建德中，周武帝出幸雲陽，衍朝於行所，時議欲伐齊，衍請為前鋒。 攻河陰城，授儀同大將軍。 武帝圍晉州，慮齊兵來援，令衍從陳王守千里徑。 又從武帝與齊主大戰于晉州，追齊師至高壁，敗之。 仍從平并州，以功加授開府，封武強縣公，邑一千二百戶，賜姓叱羅氏。 ，為右中軍熊渠中大夫。
Everyone Shu recommended rose to high office. Zhao Xingshu of the Grand Music Office—a music-household man worth hundreds of millions—Shu called "elder brother" and took heavy bribes from him. Praising his valor, Shu had him enter service as an Ironside commandant. Guo Yan, styled Yanwen, claimed Taiyuan's Jiexiu as his ancestral home. His father had followed Emperor Wu of Wei into the passes as a palace attendant and later rose to palace attendant. In youth Yan was martial and excelled at riding and archery. Prince Chun of Chen took him into his household, and he rose to grand commander. While Qi was still unconquered, Yan was ordered to recruit at Tianshui for the eastern frontier, settled more than a thousand Yue migrant households at Shancheng. He was made bearer of the staff, general of chariots and cavalry, and equal in protocol to the Three Ducal Ministers. Whenever raiders appeared he led his men against them; victories were reported many times a year, and the Qi came to fear him. The prince trusted him ever more deeply. In the Jiande period, when Emperor Wu toured Yunyang, Yan came to the traveling palace; as an attack on Qi was debated, Yan asked to lead the van. In the assault on Heyin city he was made a grand master with equal protocol. During the siege of Jinyang the emperor, fearing Qi relief, had Yan follow Prince Chen in guarding the Thousand-Li Path. He again followed the emperor in the great battle at Jinyang, pursued the Qi to Gaobi, and defeated them. He helped pacify Bingzhou, was further made Defender-in-Chief, enfeoffed as Duke of Wuqiang with 1,200 households, and given the surname Chiluo. He became Bear-Bearon grand master of the Right Central Army.
8
尉迥之起逆，從韋孝寬戰于武陟，進戰於相州。 先是，迥遣弟子勤為青州總管，率青、齊之眾來助迥。 迥敗，勤與迥子惇、祐等欲東奔青州。 衍將精騎一千追破之，執祐於陣，勤遂遁走，而惇亦逃逸。 衍至濟州，入據其城，又擊其餘党於濟北，累戰破之，執送京師。 超授上柱國，封武山郡公。 賞物七千段。 密勸高祖殺周室諸王，早行禪代。 由是大被親昵。 ，敕復舊姓為郭氏。 突厥犯塞，以衍為行軍總管，領兵屯於平涼。 數歲，虜不入。 征為開漕渠大監。 部率水工，鑿渠引渭水，經大興城北，東至於潼關，漕運四百餘裡。 關內賴之，名之曰富民渠。 五年，授瀛州刺史。 遇秋霖大水，其屬縣多漂沒，民皆上高樹，依大家。 衍親備舩栰，並齎糧拯救之，民多獲濟。 衍先開倉賑恤，後始聞奏。 上大善之，選授朔州總管。 所部有恆安鎮，北接蕃境，常勞轉運。 衍乃選沃饒地，置屯田，歲剩粟萬餘石，民免轉輸之勞。 又築桑乾鎮，皆稱旨。 十年，從晉王廣出鎮揚州。 遇江表構逆，命衍為總管，領精銳萬人先屯京口。 于貴洲南與賊戰，敗之，生擒魁帥，大獲舟楫糧儲，以充軍實，乃討東陽、永嘉、宣城、黟、歙諸洞，盡平之。 授蔣州刺史。
When Yu Chi rebelled he fought under Wei Xiaokuan at Wuzhi and again at Xiangzhou. Chi had earlier sent his nephew Qin as military governor of Qingzhou with Qing and Qi troops to reinforce him. When Chi was beaten, Qin with Chi's sons Dun and You planned to flee east to Qingzhou. Yan pursued with a thousand elite horsemen, broke them, seized You in battle, while Qin and Dun escaped. He took Jizhou, held the city, crushed the remnant bands in Jibei, and sent prisoners to the capital. He was promoted in one step to Superior Pillar of State and made Duke of Wushan. He received seven thousand rolls of goods as reward. He secretly urged Gaozu to kill the Zhou imperial princes and seize the throne without delay. From this he won exceptional favor. An edict restored his original surname Guo. When Turks raided the frontier he was made campaigning commander and garrisoned Pingliang. For several years the nomads did not cross in. He was recalled to superintend the Grand Canal project. He directed the diggers to channel the Wei north of Daxing and east to Tong Pass—more than four hundred li of transport. Guanzhong depended on it, and it was named the Canal for Enriching the People. In the fifth year he was made inspector of Yingzhou. Autumn floods drowned many counties; people climbed trees and clung to great houses. Yan himself prepared boats and grain and rescued them; many survived. He opened the granaries before reporting to court. The emperor praised this and made him military governor of Shuozhou. His district held Heng'an Fort on the northern frontier, where transport was a constant strain. He chose fertile ground for military colonies and saved more than ten thousand shi yearly, sparing the people transport duty. He also built Sanggan Fort, and both projects won approval. In the tenth year he followed Prince of Jin Yang Guang to Yangzhou. When the lower Yangtze rebelled he was made commander and led ten thousand elite troops to Jingkou. South of Gui he defeated the rebels, captured their leaders, seized boats and grain for the army, and pacified the cave settlements of Dongyang, Yongjia, Xuancheng, Yi, and She. He was made inspector of Jiangzhou.
9
衍臨下甚踞，事上奸諂。 晉王愛昵之，宴賜隆厚。 遷洪州總管。 王有奪宗之謀，托衍心腹，遣宇文述以情告之。 衍大喜曰：「若所謀事果，自可為皇太子。 如其不諧，亦須據淮海，複梁、陳之舊。 副君酒客，其如我何？ 」王因召衍，陰共計議。 又恐人疑無故來往，托以衍妻患癭，王妃蕭氏有術能療之。 以狀奏高祖，高祖聽衍共妻向江都，往來無度。 衍又詐稱桂州俚反，王乃奏衍行兵討之。 由是大修甲仗，陰養士卒。 及王入為太子，征授左監門率，轉左宗衛率。 高祖于仁壽宮將大漸，太子與楊素矯詔，令衍、宇文述領東宮兵，帖上臺宿衛，門禁並由之。 及上崩，漢王起逆，而京師空虛，使衍馳還，總兵居守。 ，拜左武衛大將軍。 帝幸江都，令衍統左軍，改授光祿大夫。 又從討吐谷渾，出金山道，納絳二萬餘戶。 衍能揣上意，阿諛順旨。 帝每謂人曰：「唯有郭衍，心與朕同。 」又嘗勸帝取樂，五日一視事，無得效高祖空自劬勞。 帝從之，益稱其孝順。 初，新令行，衍封爵從例除。 六年，以恩幸封真定侯。 七年，從往江都，卒。 贈左衛大將軍，賵賜甚厚，諡曰襄。 長子臻，武牙郎將。 次子嗣本，孝昌縣令。 【論】史臣曰：謇謇匪躬，為臣之高節，和而不同，事君之常道。 宇文述、郭衍以水濟水，如脂如韋，便辟足恭，柔顏取悅。 君所謂可，亦曰可焉，君所謂不，亦曰不焉。 無所是非，不能輕重，默默苟容，偷安高位，甘素餐之責，受彼己之譏。 此固君子所不為，亦丘明之深恥也。
Toward subordinates Yan was overbearing; toward superiors he flattered and fawned. The Prince of Jin favored him with lavish feasts and gifts. He was transferred to military governor of Hongzhou. The prince, plotting for the succession, made Yan his confidant and sent Yuwen Shu to disclose the plan. Yan was overjoyed and said, "If the plot succeeds, I could become crown prince myself." "If it fails, we must still hold the Huai-Hai region and restore the old Liang and Chen territories." "What can the crown prince and that wine-bibbing prince do to us?" The prince summoned Yan and they plotted in secret. Fearing suspicion over their constant visits, they claimed Yan's wife had goiter and that Princess Xiao could cure it. They reported this to Gaozu, who allowed Yan to take his wife to Jiangdu back and forth without restraint. Yan also fabricated a Li rebellion in Guizhou, and the prince memorialized to send him against it. Thus he rebuilt arms on a large scale and secretly trained troops. When the prince became crown prince, Yan was summoned as left commander of the palace gate, then of the left guard. As Gaozu lay dying at Renshou Palace, the crown prince and Yang Su forged an edict putting Yan and Yuwen Shu in charge of Eastern Palace troops, palace guards, and all gates. When the emperor died the Prince of Han rebelled with the capital thinly held; Yan was sent racing back to take command of the defense. He was made general-in-chief of the Left Martial Guard. On the tour to Jiangdu the emperor put Yan in command of the left army and promoted him to Grand Master of Splendid Happiness. He again joined the campaign against the Tuyuhun by the Jinshan route and brought in more than twenty thousand Jiang households. Yan could read the emperor's mind and flatter him by echoing his wishes. The emperor often said, "Only Guo Yan's heart matches mine." He also urged the emperor to enjoy himself and hold court only every five days rather than wear himself out like Gaozu. The emperor agreed and praised him all the more for his dutifulness. When the new statutes took effect, Yan's enfeoffment was struck according to rule. In the sixth year he was enfeoffed as Marquis of Zhending as a favorite. In the seventh year he followed the emperor to Jiangdu and died there. He was posthumously made general-in-chief of the Left Guard, given lavish funeral gifts, and granted the posthumous name Xiang. His eldest son Zhen was a Martial Tooth commandant. His second son Siben was magistrate of Xiaochang. Commentary. The historiographer writes: To devote oneself utterly without sparing the body is a minister's highest integrity; to harmonize without conforming is the constant way of serving a ruler. Yu Wenshu and Guo Yan poured water on water—supple as grease and leather, quick to bow and scrape, soft-faced to please. When the ruler said yes, they said yes; when he said no, they said no. They judged nothing, weighed nothing, mutely endured in high place, ate their salaries in idleness, and earned the scorn of others and of themselves. That is what gentlemen refuse to do—and what Qiu Ming counted a deep disgrace.
