1
高肇于勁胡國珍李延寔
Gao Zhao, Yu Jin, Hu Guozhen, and Li Yanshi
2
高肇，字首文，文昭皇太后之兄也。 自云本勃海蓨人，五世祖顧，晉永嘉中避亂入高麗。 父颺，字法脩。 高祖初，與弟乘信及其鄉人韓內、冀富等入國，拜厲威將軍、河間子，乘信明威將軍，俱待以客禮，賜奴婢牛馬綵帛。 遂納颺女，是為文昭皇后，生世宗。
Gao Zhao, whose courtesy name was Shouwen, was the elder brother of Empress Dowager Wen Zhao. He claimed descent from Tiao in Bohai; his fifth-generation ancestor Gu had fled into Goryeo during the turmoil of the Jin Yongjia era. His father was Yang, courtesy name Faxun. Early in Emperor Gaozu's reign he came into the realm with his younger brother Chengxin and fellow townspeople Han Nei and Ji Fu; he was made Resolute Might General and Marquis of Hejian, Chengxin was made Illustrious Might General, and both were treated as honored guests and given slaves, cattle, horses, and silks. Gaozu then took Yang's daughter as consort; she became Empress Wen Zhao and bore Emperor Shizong.
3
颺卒。 景明初，世宗追思舅氏，徵肇兄弟等。 錄尚書事、北海王詳等奏：「颺宜贈左光祿大夫，賜爵勃海公，諡曰敬。 其妻蓋氏宜追封清河郡君。 」詔可。 又詔颺嫡孫猛襲勃海公爵，封肇平原郡公，肇弟顯澄城郡公。 三人同日受封。 始世宗未與舅氏相接，將拜爵，乃賜衣幘引見肇、顯于華林都亭。 皆甚惶懼，舉動失儀。 數日之間，富貴赫弈。 是年，咸陽王禧誅，財物珍寶奴婢田宅多入高氏。 未幾，肇為尚書左僕射、領吏部、冀州大中正，尚世宗姑高平公主，遷尚書令。
Yang died. At the start of the Jingming era Emperor Shizong, mindful of his maternal kin, summoned Zhao and his brothers to court. Director of the Masters of Writing Prince Xiang of Beihai and others submitted: "Yang should be posthumously made Left Grand Master of Splendid Happiness, enfeoffed as Duke of Bohai, and given the posthumous name Jing." His wife Lady Gai should be posthumously enfeoffed as Lady of Qinghe commandery. The emperor approved. Another edict had Yang's eldest grandson Meng inherit the duchy of Bohai, enfeoffed Zhao as Duke of Pingyuan commandery, and Zhao's younger brother Xian as Duke of Chengcheng commandery. All three were enfeoffed on the same day. Until then Shizong had never met his maternal kin; before investing them with titles he gave them robes and caps and received Zhao and Xian at the Duting pavilion in Hualin Park. Both were deeply afraid and their bearing was altogether unseemly. Within days they blazed with sudden wealth and rank. That year Prince Xi of Xianyang was executed, and most of his goods, treasures, slaves, fields, and houses passed to the Gao clan. Before long Zhao was made Left Vice Director of the Masters of Writing, concurrently head of the Ministry of Personnel and chief rectifier of Ji province; he married Shizong's paternal aunt the Princess of Gaoping and was soon promoted to Director of the Masters of Writing.
4
肇出自夷土，時望輕之。 及在位居要，留心百揆，孜孜無倦，世咸謂之為能。 世宗初，六輔專政，後以咸陽王禧無事構逆，由是遂委信肇。 肇既無親族，頗結朋黨，附之者旬月超昇，背之者陷以大罪。 以北海王詳位居其上，構殺之。 又說世宗防衞諸王，殆同囚禁。 時順皇后暴崩，世議言肇為之。 皇子昌薨，僉謂王顯失於醫療，承肇意旨。 及京兆王愉出為冀州刺史，畏肇恣擅，遂至不軌。 肇又譖殺彭城王勰。 由是朝野側目，咸畏惡之。 因此專權，與奪任己。 又嘗與清河王懌於雲龍門外廡下，忽忿諍，大至紛紜。 太尉、高陽王雍和止之。 高后既立，愈見寵信。 肇既當衡軸，每事任己，本無學識，動違禮度，好改先朝舊制，出情妄作，減削封秩，抑黜勳人。 由是怨聲盈路矣。 延昌初，遷司徒。 雖貴登台鼎，猶以去要怏怏形乎辭色。 眾咸嗤笑之。 父兄封贈雖久，竟不改瘞。 三年，乃詔令遷葬。 肇不自臨赴，唯遣其兄子猛改服詣代，遷葬於鄉。 時人以肇無識，哂而不責也。
Zhao came from foreign soil, and men of the time held him in low regard. Once in power he attended tirelessly to the hundred affairs of state, and the age widely judged him capable. Early in Shizong's reign the six chief ministers held power; after Prince Xi of Xianyang plotted rebellion without cause, the emperor came to rely on Zhao instead. Zhao had no kin of his own and cultivated factions instead: adherents rose within a month, while those who opposed him were ruined on grave charges. Because Prince Xiang of Beihai outranked him, he framed him and had him killed. He also persuaded Shizong to guard the princes so closely that they were kept nearly as prisoners. When Empress Shun died suddenly, public opinion blamed Zhao. When Crown Prince Chang died, all said Wang Xian had bungled his medical care at Zhao's direction. When Prince Yu of Jingzhao went out as inspector of Ji province, fearing Zhao's arbitrary power he finally crossed into rebellion. Zhao also slandered Prince Xie of Pengcheng and had him killed. Thereafter court and countryside watched him with sidelong eyes, all fearing and hating him. On this basis he monopolized power and granted or withheld office as he pleased. He once quarreled in sudden fury with Prince Yi of Qinghe beneath the portico outside the Cloud Dragon Gate until the scene became a great uproar. Grand Marshal Prince Yong of Gaoyang stepped in and stopped them. After Empress Gao was installed he grew still more favored and trusted. With the pivot of state in his hands Zhao made every affair his own; he had little learning, constantly breached ritual norms, loved to alter former institutions, acted on whim, cut enfeoffments and ranks, and pushed meritorious men down. Complaints of resentment then filled the roads. At the start of the Yan Chang era he was made Minister of Works. Though raised to the summit of state he still showed displeasure at leaving the key post in word and face. All mocked him for it. Though his father and elder brother had long received posthumous honors, he never moved their burials. In the third year an edict at last ordered their remains moved. Zhao did not go himself; he sent only his elder brother's son Meng in mourning dress to Dai to move the burial to their homeland. Men of the time laughed at Zhao's want of understanding but did not blame him further.
5
其年，大舉征蜀，以肇為大將軍，都督諸軍為之節度。 與都督甄琛等二十餘人俱面辭世宗於東堂，親奉規略。 是日，肇所乘駿馬停於神虎門外，無故驚倒，轉臥渠中，鞍具瓦解，眾咸怪異。 肇出，惡焉。
That year a great campaign was launched against Shu; Zhao was made Grand General and supreme commander over all the armies. Together with more than twenty commanders including Zhen Chen he took leave of Shizong in the Eastern Hall and received the campaign plans in person. That day Zhao's fine horse halted outside the Divine Tiger Gate, startled and fell for no reason, rolled into the channel, and its saddle fell apart; all took it as an omen. As Zhao set out he took it as a bad omen.
6
四年，世宗崩，赦罷征軍。 肅宗與肇及征南將軍元遙等書，稱諱言，以告凶問。 肇承變哀愕，非唯仰慕，亦私憂身禍，朝夕悲泣，至于羸悴。 將至，宿瀍澗驛亭，家人夜迎省之，皆不相視。 直至闕下，衰服號哭，昇太極殿，奉喪盡哀。
In the fourth year Emperor Shizong died; an amnesty was proclaimed and the expeditionary army dismissed. Emperor Suzong wrote to Zhao, southern campaign general Yuan Yao, and the others in taboo language to announce the emperor's death. Zhao received the news in grief and shock; he mourned the emperor but also feared for his own life, weeping day and night until he was wasted and haggard. As he neared the capital he lodged at the post station on the Chan Stream; his family came out at night to see him, and none could meet another's eyes. He went straight to the palace gate, wailed in mourning dress, ascended the Hall of Supreme Ultimate, and observed the funeral with full grief.
7
太尉高陽王先居西柏堂，專決庶事，與領軍于忠密欲除之。 潛備壯士直寢邢豹、伊瓫生等十餘人於舍人省下。 肇哭梓宮訖，於百官前引入西廊，清河王懌、任城王澄及諸王等皆竊言目之。 肇入省，壯士搤而拉殺之。 下詔暴其罪惡，又云刑書未及，便至自盡，自餘親黨，悉無追問，削除職爵，葬以士禮。 及昏，乃於厠門出其尸歸家。 初，肇西征，行至函谷，車軸中折。 從者皆以為不獲吉還也。 靈太后臨朝，令特贈營州刺史。 永熙二年，出帝贈使持節、侍中、中外諸軍事、太師、大丞相、太尉公、錄尚書事、冀州刺史。
Grand Marshal Prince Yong of Gaoyang had already occupied the Western Cypress Hall and decided ordinary affairs alone; he and palace commander Yu Zhong secretly plotted to remove Zhao. They secretly stationed more than ten stalwarts, including chamber attendants Xing Bao and Yi Lunsheng, below the Secretariat of Attendants. When Zhao had finished mourning before the coffin he was led before the officials into the western corridor; Prince Yi of Qinghe, Prince Cheng of Rencheng, and the other princes whispered and watched him. When Zhao entered the secretariat the stalwarts seized him and strangled him to death. An edict exposed his crimes and said that before the penal writ could arrive he had taken his own life; the rest of his kin were not pursued, his offices and titles were struck off, and he was buried with commoner's rites. At dusk his corpse was brought out through the privy gate and returned to his house. Earlier, on Zhao's western campaign, his carriage axle broke in two at Hangu Pass. His followers all thought he would not return safely. When Empress Dowager Ling held court she ordered a special posthumous appointment as inspector of Ying province. In Yongxi year 2 Emperor Chu posthumously granted him bearer of the staff of authority, Palace Attendant, commander of all military affairs within and without, Grand Preceptor, Grand Chancellor, Duke of Grand Marshal, Director of the Masters of Writing, and inspector of Ji province.
8
肇子植。 自中書侍郎為濟州刺史，率州軍討破元愉，別將有功。 當蒙封賞，不受，云：「家荷重恩，為國致效是其常節，何足以應進陟之報。 」懇惻發於至誠。 歷青、相、朔、恒四州刺史，卒。 植頻莅五州，皆清能著稱，當時號為良刺史。 贈安北將軍、冀州刺史。
Zhao's son was Zhi. From Palace Secretariat Attendant he became inspector of Ji province, led the provincial army to defeat Yuan Yu, and as a separate commander won merit. He was due enfeoffment and reward but refused, saying: "My house bears heavy favor; serving the state is our ordinary duty—how could that merit advancement in return." His earnestness sprang from utmost sincerity. He served in turn as inspector of Qing, Xiang, Shuo, and Heng provinces, then died. Zhi governed five provinces in all, each time with a reputation for clean competence; his age called him a good inspector. He was posthumously made Pacifier of the North General and inspector of Ji province.
9
肇長兄琨，早卒。 襲颺封勃海郡公，贈都督五州諸軍事、鎮東大將軍、冀州刺史。 詔其子猛嗣。
Zhao's eldest brother Kun died young. He inherited Yang's duchy of Bohai and was posthumously made commander of military affairs in five provinces, Grand General Who Pacifies the East, and inspector of Ji province. An edict had his son Meng succeed.
10
猛，字豹兒。 尚長樂公主，即世宗同母妹也。 拜駙馬都尉，歷位中書令。 出為雍州刺史，有能名。 入為殿中尚書。 卒，贈司空、冀州刺史。 出帝時，復贈太師、大丞相、錄尚書事。 公主無子。 猛先在外有男，不敢令主知，臨終方言之，年幾三十矣。 乃召為喪主，尋卒，無後。
Meng, whose courtesy name was Bao'er. He married the Princess of Changle, Shizong's younger sister by the same mother. He was made Commandant of Escort and rose to Director of the Palace Secretariat. He went out as inspector of Yong province and won a reputation for ability. He returned to court as Master of Writing in the Palace. He died and was posthumously made Minister of Works and inspector of Ji province. Under Emperor Chu he was again posthumously made Grand Preceptor, Grand Chancellor, and Director of the Masters of Writing. The princess had no sons. Meng had earlier fathered a son while away but dared not tell his wife; only on his deathbed did he reveal him—the boy was nearly thirty. The boy was summoned to lead the mourning, but soon died too, and the line ended.
11
琨弟偃，字仲游。 太和十年卒。 正始中，贈安東將軍、都督、青州刺史，諡曰莊侯。 景明四年，世宗納其女為貴嬪。 及于順皇后崩，永平元年立為皇后。 二年，八座奏封后母王氏為武邑郡君。
Kun's younger brother was Yan, courtesy name Zhongyou. He died in Taihe year 10. In the Zhengshi era he was posthumously made Pacifier of the East General, commander, and inspector of Qing province, with the posthumous name Marquis Zhuang. In Jingming year 4 Emperor Shizong took his daughter as Honored Consort. After Empress Shun died, she was made empress in Yongping year 1. In the second year the eight chief ministers memorialized to enfeoff the empress's mother Lady Wang as Lady of Wuyi commandery.
12
偃弟壽，早卒。 壽弟即肇也。
Yan's younger brother Shou died young. Shou's younger brother was Zhao himself.
13
肇弟顯，侍中、高麗國大中正，早卒。
Zhao's younger brother Xian, Palace Attendant and chief rectifier of Goryeo, also died young.
14
于勁，字鍾葵，太尉拔之子。 頗有武略。 以功臣子，又以功績，位沃野鎮將，賜爵富昌子，拜征虜將軍。 世宗納其女為后，封太原郡公。 妻劉氏，為章武郡君。 後拜征北將軍、定州刺史。 卒，贈司空，諡曰恭莊公。 自栗磾至勁，累世貴盛，一皇后，四贈公，三領軍，二尚書令，三開國公。 [2]勁雖以后父，但以順后早崩，竟不居公輔。
Yu Jin, whose courtesy name was Zhongkui, was the son of Grand Marshal Ba. He had considerable military talent. As a meritorious minister's son and again for his own achievements he was made garrison commander of Woye, enfeoffed as Marquis of Fuchang, and appointed Campaign General. Emperor Shizong took his daughter as empress and enfeoffed him as Duke of Taiyuan commandery. His wife Lady Liu was made Lady of Zhangwu commandery. Later he was made Campaign North General and inspector of Ding province. He died and was posthumously made Minister of Works with the posthumous name Duke Gongzhuang. From Li Bi down to Jin the house rose in splendor for generations: one empress, four posthumous dukes, three palace commanders, two directors of the Masters of Writing, and three founding-state dukes. [2] Though Jin was the empress's father, Empress Shun died early and he never held the highest offices.
15
子暉，字宣明，后母弟也。 少有氣幹。 襲爵，位汾州刺史。 暉善事人，為尒朱榮所親，以女妻其子長孺。 歷侍中、河南尹，後兼尚書僕射、東南道行臺。 與齊獻武王討平羊侃於兗州。 元顥入洛，害之。
His son Hui, courtesy name Xuanming, was the empress's younger brother by the same mother. In youth he showed force of character. He inherited the title and served as inspector of Fen province. Hui was skilled at winning people over and was favored by Erzhu Rong, who gave him his daughter in marriage for his son Changru. He served as Palace Attendant and Intendant of Henan, and later concurrently as Vice Director of the Masters of Writing and commissioner of the Southeastern Circuit. Together with Prince Xianwu of Qi he suppressed Yang Kan in Yan province. When Yuan Hao entered Luoyang he had him killed.
16
勁弟天恩，位內行長、遼西太守。 卒，贈平東將軍、燕州刺史。
Jin's younger brother Tian'en was chief of the inner service and administrator of Liaoxi. He died and was posthumously made Pacifier of the East General and inspector of Yan province.
17
天恩子仁生，位太中大夫。
Tian'en's son Rensheng was Grand Master of Palace Counsel.
18
仁生子安定，平原郡太守、高平郡都將。 [3]卒。
Rensheng's son Anding was administrator of Pingyuan commandery and commander of Gaoping commandery. [3] He died.
19
胡國珍，字世玉，安定臨涇人也。 祖略，姚興渤海公姚逵平北府諮議參軍。 父淵，赫連屈丐給事黃門侍郎。 世祖克統萬，淵以降款之功賜爵武始侯。 後拜河州刺史。
Hu Guozhen, whose courtesy name was Shiyu, was from Linjing in Anding. His grandfather Lue had been a consulting staff officer under Yao Kui, Duke of Bohai, in Yao Xing's Pacify-the-North headquarters. His father Yuan had served as attendant at the Yellow Gate under Helian Qugai. When Emperor Shizu captured Tongwan, Yuan was enfeoffed as Marquis of Wushi for surrendering in good faith. Later he was made inspector of He province.
20
國珍少好學，雅尚清儉。 太和十五年襲爵，例降為伯。 女以選入掖庭，生肅宗，即靈太后也。 肅宗踐祚，以國珍為光祿大夫。 靈太后臨朝，加侍中，封安定郡公，給甲第，賜帛布綿縠奴婢車馬牛甚厚。 追崇國珍妻皇甫氏為京兆郡君，置守冢十戶。 尚書令、任城王澄奏，安定公屬尊望重，親賢羣矚，宜出入禁中，參諮大務。 詔可。 乃令入決萬幾。 尋進位中書監、儀同三司，侍中如故，賞賜累萬。 又賜絹歲八百匹，妻梁四百匹，男女姊妹兄弟各有差，皆極豐贍。 國珍與太師、高陽王雍，太傅、清河王懌，太保、廣平王懷，入居門下，同釐庶政。 詔依漢車千秋、晉安平王故事，給步挽一乘，自掖門至于宣光殿得以出入，并備几杖。 後與侍中崔光俱授帝經，侍直禁中。 國珍尋上表，陳刑政之宜。 詔皆施行。 熙平初，[4]加國珍使持節、都督、雍州刺史、驃騎大將軍、開府。 靈太后以國珍年老，不欲令其在外，且欲示以方面之榮，竟不行。 遷司徒公，侍中如故，就宅拜之。 靈太后、肅宗率百僚幸其第，宴會極歡。 又追京兆郡君為秦太上君。 [5]太上君景明三年薨於洛陽，於此十六年矣。 太后以太上君墳瘞卑局，更增廣，為起塋域門闕碑表。 侍中崔光等奏：「案漢高祖母始諡曰昭靈夫人，後為昭靈后，薄太后母曰靈文夫人，皆置園邑三百家，長丞奉守。 今秦太上君未有尊諡，陵寢孤立，即秦君名，宜上終稱，兼設掃衞，以慰情典。 請上尊諡曰孝穆，權置園邑三十戶，立長丞奉守。 」太后從之。 封國珍繼室梁氏為趙平郡君，元叉妻拜為女侍中，封新平郡君，又徙封馮翊君。 國珍子祥妻長安縣公主，即清河王懌女也。
Guozhen loved learning in youth and esteemed purity and frugality. In Taihe year 15 he inherited the title, which by regulation was reduced to marquis. His daughter entered the rear palace by selection and bore Emperor Suzong—she who became Empress Dowager Ling. When Suzong ascended the throne he made Guozhen Grand Master of Splendid Happiness. When Empress Dowager Ling held court he was made Palace Attendant and enfeoffed as Duke of Anding; a fine mansion was granted and silks, cloth, slaves, carriages, horses, and cattle were lavished on him in great abundance. Guozhen's wife Lady Huangfu was posthumously honored as Lady of Jingzhao commandery, and ten households were assigned to guard the tomb. Director of the Masters of Writing Prince Cheng of Rencheng memorialized: "The Duke of Anding is noble by lineage and weighty in standing; kin and worthies alike look to him—he should move in and out of the inner palace and take part in great affairs." The emperor approved. He was then ordered to enter court and decide the myriad affairs of state. Soon he was advanced to Supervisor of the Palace Secretariat and Equal in Three Matters while remaining Palace Attendant; rewards mounted to tens of thousands. He was further granted eight hundred bolts of silk yearly and his wife Lady Liang four hundred; brothers and sisters each received their portion—all were provided to the utmost. Guozhen together with Grand Preceptor Prince Yong of Gaoyang, Grand Tutor Prince Yi of Qinghe, and Grand Guardian Prince Huai of Guangping entered the Gate Below and jointly directed ordinary government. An edict followed the precedents of Han's Che Qianqiu and Jin's Prince of Ping'an, granting a hand-drawn carriage; from the Rear Gate to the Hall of Proclaiming Light he might enter and leave at will, and was provided with bench and staff. Later he and Palace Attendant Cui Guang together instructed the emperor in the classics and attended within the forbidden precincts. Guozhen soon submitted a memorial on what was fitting in punishments and government. The emperor ordered all to be carried out. At the start of Xiping, [4] Guozhen was further made bearer of the staff of authority, commander, inspector of Yong province, Swift Cavalry Grand General, and opener of a government office. Empress Dowager Ling, because Guozhen was old, did not wish him to leave the capital, yet wished to display the glory of a regional command; in the end he never went. He was made Minister of Works while remaining Palace Attendant, and the investiture was performed at his residence. Empress Dowager Ling and Emperor Suzong led the hundred officials to his residence for a banquet of utmost joy. Lady of Jingzhao commandery was further posthumously honored as Grand Lord of Qin. [5] The Grand Lord had died in Luoyang in Jingming year 3; sixteen years had now passed. The empress dowager, finding the Grand Lord's tomb mean and cramped, enlarged it and had a grave domain raised with gate towers, memorial arch, and stele. Palace Attendant Cui Guang and others memorialized: "By precedent, Emperor Gaozu of Han's grandmother was first styled Lady Zhao Ling, later Empress Zhao Ling; Empress Dowager Bo's mother was styled Lady Ling Wen—all had park estates of three hundred households with officials to maintain them." Now the Grand Lord of Qin has no honored posthumous title and the tomb stands alone; following the Lord of Qin's name, a final title should be granted and sweepers and guards established to satisfy feeling and ritual. We request the honored posthumous title Xiao Mu and provisionally a park estate of thirty households with officials to maintain it. The empress dowager approved. Guozhen's successor wife Lady Liang was enfeoffed as Lady of Zhaoping commandery; Yuan Cha's wife was appointed Lady Attendant and enfeoffed as Lady of Xinping commandery, then transferred to Lady of Fengyi. Guozhen's son Xiang's wife was the Princess of Chang'an county, daughter of Prince Yi of Qinghe.
21
國珍年雖篤老，而雅敬佛法，時事齋潔，自強禮拜。 至於出入侍從，猶能跨馬據鞍。 神龜元年四月七日，步從所建佛像，發第至閶闔門四五里。 八日，又立觀像，晚乃肯坐。 勞熱增甚，因遂寢疾。 靈太后親侍藥膳。 十二日薨，年八十。 給東園溫明祕器、五時朝服各一具、衣一襲，贈布五千匹、錢一百萬、蠟千斤。 大鴻臚持節監護喪事。 太后還宮，成服於九龍殿，遂居九龍寢室。 肅宗服小功服，舉哀於太極東堂。 又詔自始薨至七七，皆為設千僧齋，令七人出家； 百日設萬人齋，二七人出家。 先是巫覡言將有凶，勸令為厭勝之法。 國珍拒而不從，云吉凶有定分，唯修德以禳之。 臨死與太后訣云：「母子善治天下，以萬人之心，勿視大臣面也。 」殷勤至於再三。 又及其子祥，云：「我唯有一子，死後勿如比來威抑之。 」靈太后以其好戲，時加威訓。 國珍故以為言。
Though deeply aged, Guozhen reverently honored the Buddhist Law, kept seasonal fasts and purity, and forced himself to perform obeisance. When going out with attendants he could still mount a horse and grip the saddle. On the seventh day of the fourth month of Shengui year 1 he walked following the Buddha image he had erected from his residence to within four or five li of the Gate of Universal Peace. On the eighth day he again stood viewing the image and only in the evening would sit down. Fatigue and fever grew severe, and he took to his bed. Empress Dowager Ling personally attended his medicines and food. On the twelfth day he died, aged eighty. He was granted the warm-bright secret vessels of the Eastern Garden, one set each of court robes for the five seasons, and one suit of garments; five thousand bolts of cloth, a million cash, and a thousand jin of wax were bestowed. The Grand Herald bore the staff of authority and supervised the funeral rites. The empress dowager returned to the palace, assumed mourning dress in the Hall of Nine Dragons, and dwelt in the Nine Dragons sleeping chamber. Emperor Suzong wore lesser merit mourning and raised lament in the Eastern Hall of the Supreme Ultimate. Another edict ordered that from death to the forty-ninth day vegetarian feasts for a thousand monks were to be held, with seven persons to take monastic vows; at the hundredth day a feast for ten thousand was to be held, with twenty-seven persons to take monastic vows. Earlier shamans and diviners had foretold misfortune and urged him to perform exorcistic rites. Guozhen refused, saying fortune and misfortune have fixed allotments and only cultivating virtue can avert them. At his death he took leave of the empress dowager, saying: "Mother and son should govern the realm well; rule with the hearts of the myriad people—do not look to the faces of great ministers." He urged this again and again. He also spoke to his son Xiang, saying: "I have only this one son; after my death do not restrain and suppress him as you have of late." Empress Dowager Ling, because he loved play, often admonished him sternly. Guozhen therefore spoke of this.
22
始國珍欲就祖父西葬舊鄉，後緣前世諸胡多在洛葬，有終洛之心。 崔光嘗對太后前問國珍：「公萬年後為在此安厝，為歸長安？ 」國珍言當陪葬天子山陵。 及病危，太后請以後事，竟言還安定，語遂昏忽。 太后問清河王懌與崔光等，議去留。 懌等皆以病亂，請從先言。 太后猶記崔光昔與國珍言，遂營墓於洛陽。 太后雖外從眾議，而深追臨終之語，云：「我公之遠慕二親，亦吾之思父母也。」
At first Guozhen wished to be buried in the west with his grandfather in their old homeland; later, because many of his forebears had been buried at Luoyang, he inclined to end there. Cui Guang once asked Guozhen before the empress dowager: "After your ten thousand years, will you be laid to rest here or return to Chang'an?" Guozhen said he should be buried as companion in the emperor's mountain tomb. When his illness grew critical the empress dowager asked about his last wishes; he finally said to return to Anding, then his words grew confused. The empress dowager asked Prince Yi of Qinghe, Cui Guang, and others to deliberate whether he should stay or return. Yi and the others cited the disorder of his illness and requested to follow his earlier words. The empress dowager still remembered Cui Guang's earlier exchange with Guozhen and had a tomb prepared at Luoyang. Though outwardly she followed the assembly's deliberation, inwardly she deeply recalled his dying words, saying: "My lord's distant longing for his two parents is also my longing for my parents."
23
追崇假黃鉞、使持節、侍中、相國、都督中外諸軍事、太師、領太尉公、司州牧，號太上秦公，加九錫。 葬以殊禮，給九旒鑾輅，虎賁、班劍百人，前後部羽葆鼓吹，轀輬車； 諡文宣公； 賜物三千段、粟一千五百石。 又詔贈國珍祖父兄、父兄，下逮從子，皆有封職。 持節就安定監護喪事。 [6]靈太后迎太上君神柩還第，與國珍俱葬，贈襚一與國珍同。 及國珍神主入廟，詔太常權給以軒懸之樂、六佾之舞。 初國珍無男，養兄真子僧洗為後，後納趙平君，生子祥。
He was posthumously honored with the provisional yellow battle-axe, bearer of the staff of authority, Palace Attendant, Chancellor of State, commander of all military affairs within and without, Grand Preceptor, concurrently Duke of Grand Marshal and governor of Si province, styled Grand Lord of Qin, with the Nine Bestowals added. He was buried with extraordinary rites: granted the nine tassels and imperial carriage, a hundred tiger guards and sword attendants, front and rear companies with feathered canopies and wind and percussion music, and the enclosed mourning carriage; posthumous name Duke Wen Xuan; and bestowed goods of three thousand sections and grain of fifteen hundred shi. Another edict posthumously granted offices to Guozhen's grandfather's elder brother, father's elder brother, and down to younger cousins by the father's line. A bearer of the staff of authority went to Anding to supervise the funeral rites. [6] Empress Dowager Ling welcomed the Grand Lord's spirit coffin back to the residence and buried him together with Guozhen; the grave goods bestowed matched Guozhen's. When Guozhen's spirit tablet entered the temple, the emperor ordered the Minister of Rites provisionally to grant suspended bells and chimes music and the six-rows dance. At first Guozhen had no son and adopted his elder brother Zhen's son Sengxi as heir; later he took Lady Zhaoping and bore the son Xiang.
24
僧洗，字湛輝。 封爰德縣公，位中書監、侍中，改封濮陽郡公。 僧洗自永安後廢棄，不預朝政。 天平四年薨，詔給東園祕器，贈太師、太尉公、錄尚書事、雍州刺史，諡曰孝。
Sengxi, whose courtesy name was Zhanhui. He was enfeoffed as Duke of Aide county, served as Supervisor of the Palace Secretariat and Palace Attendant, and was later changed to Duke of Puyang commandery. From Yongan onward Sengxi was cast aside and took no part in court government. In Tianping year 4 he died; he was granted the secret vessels of the Eastern Garden and posthumously made Grand Preceptor, Duke of Grand Marshal, Director of the Masters of Writing, and inspector of Yong province, with the posthumous name Xiao.
25
真長子寧，[7]字惠歸。 襲國珍先爵，改為臨涇伯，後進為公。 歷岐涇二州刺史。 卒，諡曰孝穆。 女為清河王亶妃，生孝靜皇帝。 武定初，贈太師、太尉公、錄尚書事，諡曰孝昭。
Zhen's eldest son Ning, [7] whose courtesy name was Huigui. He inherited Guozhen's former title, which was changed to Marquis of Linjing and later advanced to duke. He served in turn as inspector of Qi and Jing provinces. He died with the posthumous name Xiao Mu. His daughter was consort to Prince Dan of Qinghe and bore Emperor Xiaojing. At the start of Wuding he was posthumously made Grand Preceptor, Duke of Grand Marshal, and Director of the Masters of Writing, with the posthumous name Xiao Zhao.
26
子虔，字僧敬。 元叉之廢靈太后，虔時為千牛備身，與備身張車渠等謀殺叉。 事發，叉殺車渠等，虔坐遠徙。 靈太后反政，徵為吏部郎中。 太后好以家人禮與親族宴戲，虔常致諫，由是後宴謔多不預焉。 出為涇州刺史，封安陽縣侯。 興和三年，以帝元舅超遷司空公。 薨，贈太傅、太尉公、尚書僕射、徐州刺史，諡曰宣。 葬日，百官會葬，乘輿送於郭外。 子長粲。
His son Qian, whose courtesy name was Sengjing. When Yuan Cha deposed Empress Dowager Ling, Qian was a thousand-ox guard attendant; together with guard attendant Zhang Chequ and others he plotted to kill Cha. When the plot was discovered Cha killed Chequ and the others, and Qian was exiled to a distant post. When Empress Dowager Ling restored her rule he was summoned as director in the Ministry of Personnel. The empress dowager loved to feast and jest with kin by family ceremony; Qian often remonstrated, and thereafter he was often absent from such banquets. He went out as inspector of Jing province and was enfeoffed as Marquis of Anyang county. In Xinghe year 3, as the emperor's maternal uncle he was promoted over rank to Minister of Works. He died and was posthumously made Grand Tutor, Duke of Grand Marshal, Vice Director of the Masters of Writing, and inspector of Xu province, with the posthumous name Xuan. On the day of burial the hundred officials attended the funeral, and the imperial carriage escorted him beyond the city wall. His son was Changcan.
27
李延寔，字禧，隴西人，尚書僕射沖之長子。 性溫良，少為太子舍人。 世宗初，襲父爵清泉縣侯。 [8]累遷左將軍、光州刺史。 莊帝即位，以元舅之尊，超授侍中、太保，封濮陽郡王。 延寔以太保犯祖諱，又以王爵非庶姓所宜，抗表固辭。 徙封濮陽郡公，改授太傅。 尋轉司徒公，出為使持節、侍中、太傅、錄尚書事、青州刺史。 尒朱兆入洛，乘輿幽縶，以延寔外戚，見害於州館。 出帝初，歸葬洛陽。 贈使持節、侍中、太師、太尉公、錄尚書事、都督、雍州刺史，諡曰孝懿。
Li Yanshi, whose courtesy name was Xi, was from Longxi and the eldest son of Vice Director of the Masters of Writing Li Chong. Gentle by nature, in youth he served as an attendant of the crown prince. Early in Shizong's reign he inherited his father's title as Marquis of Qingquan county. [8] He rose in turn to Left General and inspector of Guang province. When Emperor Zhuang ascended the throne, as the emperor's maternal uncle he was promoted over rank to Palace Attendant and Grand Guardian and enfeoffed as Prince of Puyang commandery. Yanshi submitted a firm memorial of refusal, citing the taboo on Grand Guardian and the unsuitability of a princely title for a common surname. He was transferred to Duke of Puyang commandery and made Grand Tutor instead. Soon he was made Minister of Works and went out as bearer of the staff of authority, Palace Attendant, Grand Tutor, Director of the Masters of Writing, and inspector of Qing province. When Erzhu Zhao entered Luoyang the emperor was held in secret confinement; as a maternal kinsman Yanshi was killed in the provincial residence. Early in Emperor Chu's reign his remains were returned and buried at Luoyang. He was posthumously granted bearer of the staff of authority, Palace Attendant, Grand Preceptor, Duke of Grand Marshal, Director of the Masters of Writing, commander, and inspector of Yong province, with the posthumous name Xiao Yi.
28
長子彧，字子文，尚莊帝姊豐亭公主。 封東平郡公，位侍中、左光祿大夫、中書監、驃騎大將軍、開府儀同三司、廣州刺史。 彧任俠交遊，輕薄無行。 尒朱榮之死也，武毅之士皆彧所進。 孝靜初，以罪棄市。
His eldest son Yu, courtesy name Ziwen, married Emperor Zhuang's elder sister the Princess of Fengting. He was enfeoffed as Duke of Dongping commandery and held the posts of Palace Attendant, Left Grand Master of Splendid Happiness, Supervisor of the Palace Secretariat, Swift Cavalry Grand General, opener of an office equal in three matters, and inspector of Guang province. Yu was a chivalrous man about town, frivolous and without proper conduct. At Erzhu Rong's death the martial men who took action had all been introduced by Yu. Early in the Xiaojing era he was executed in the marketplace for his crimes.
29
史臣曰：三五哲王，深防遠慮。 舅甥之國，罕執鈞衡； 母后之家，無聞傾敗。 爰及後世，顛覆繼軌。 蓋由進不以禮，故其斃亦速。 其間或不泯舊基，[9]弗虧先構者，蓋處之以道，遠權之所致也。
The historiographer remarks: The sage kings of the Three Dynasties and Five Emperors took deep precautions and looked far ahead. In states ruled through uncles and nephews by marriage, few held the balance of power; among the families of empresses dowager and emperors' mothers, none were heard to topple and fail. But in later ages overthrow followed in succession. This was because advancement did not follow ritual, and therefore their destruction came swiftly as well. Among them, those who did not extinguish the old foundation [9] or impair what their forebears had built were generally those treated by the Way and kept distant from power.

魏書卷八十三下諸本目錄此卷注「闕」字，卷末有宋人校語 〈殿本入考證〉 云：「魏收書外戚傳亡，史臣論全用隋書外戚傳 〈卷七九〉 。 」按此卷亦以北史卷八０外戚傳補。 其于勁傳採北史卷三一于栗磾附于勁傳，李延寔傳採卷一００自序。 諸傳均有溢出語，當是以高氏小史等他書附益之。
Book of Wei juan 83b: in the tables of contents of various editions this juan is marked "missing"; at the end of the juan there are collation notes by a Song scholar (Palace edition entered under textual investigation) saying: "Wei Shou's treatise on families of imperial consorts is lost; the historiographer's discussion wholly uses the treatise on families of imperial consorts in the Book of Sui (juan 79) . From Li Bi to Jin (to) three founding-state dukes: These words in the Book of Wei originally stood after the appended treatise on Yu Zhong in juan 31 under Yu Libi; the Beishi treatise on Yu Libi in juan 23 moved them into the treatise on Yu Jin, so the later offices of Yu Zhong should not be counted; each category of posthumous duke, palace commander, director of the Masters of Writing, and founding-state duke is one short—an actual error. This treatise was supplemented from the Beishi without noting that juan 31 already contained this passage, causing duplication. See collation note [9] in juan 31.

高平郡都將按郡無都將，「郡」當是「鎮」之訛。
Commander of Gaoping commandery: A commandery has no commander-in-chief; "commandery" should be a corruption of "garrison."

熙平初諸本及北史卷八０胡國珍傳「熙平」作「延和」，通志卷一六五胡國珍傳作「熙平中」。 按事見卷九肅宗紀熙平元年八月乙巳，延和乃拓跋燾年號，遠在其前。 今據通志改作「熙平」。 事在元年，「初」字不誤。
At the beginning of Xiping: in various editions and in the treatise on Hu Guozhen in Beishi juan 80 "Xiping" reads "Yanhe"; in the treatise on Hu Guozhen in Tongzhi juan 165 it reads "in Xiping." The event appears in the annals of Emperor Suzong in juan 9, eighth month, day yisi of Xiping year 1; Yanhe is the era name of Tuoba Tao, far earlier. Following the Tongzhi it is here changed to "Xiping." The event falls in the first year; the word "beginning" is not in error.

Further posthumously honoring Lady of Jingzhao commandery as Grand Lord of Qin: Below "posthumously" a word such as "grant," "honor," or "exalt" is likely missing.

持節就安定監護喪事按胡國珍死和葬都在洛陽，何故「就安定監護喪事」？ 下文云：「迎太上君神柩還第」，疑「安定」下脫「公第」二字。
Bearer of the staff of authority went to Anding to supervise the funeral rites: Hu Guozhen died and was buried at Luoyang—why "went to Anding to supervise the funeral rites"? 真長子寧諸本「真」字連上段「諡曰孝」，「長子寧」提行。 成為胡僧洗諡「孝真」，「寧」是僧洗的長子。 按真乃國珍兄，見上文，寧乃僧洗兄。 北史原文本不提行，後人寫刻魏書則凡附見諸子孫無不提行，這裏由於誤讀，提行亦謬，致以兄為子，今改正。
Zhen's eldest son Ning: in various editions the character "Zhen" is joined to the preceding passage "posthumous name Xiao," and "eldest son Ning" begins a new line. Forming Hu Sengxi's posthumous name "Xiao Zhen"; "Ning" is Sengxi's eldest son. Zhen was Guozhen's elder brother, as seen above; Ning was Sengxi's elder brother. The Beishi original did not begin a new line; later editors of the Book of Wei invariably began new lines for all appended descendants; here misreading caused a wrong line break, making an elder brother appear as a son—now corrected.

This treatise was supplemented from the Beishi; to avoid Tang taboo "yuan" was changed to "quan."

其間或不泯舊基諸本「泯」上有「斃」字。 此傳本出北史，北史傳論又本隋書卷七九外戚傳論，今檢隋書、北史都作「今或不隕舊基」，這裏「今或」作「其間」，「隕」作「泯」，均兩通，「斃」字則涉上「其斃亦速」而衍，今刪。
Among them those who did not extinguish the old foundation: in various editions the character "destroy" appears above "extinguish." This treatise comes from the Beishi; its discussion in turn comes from the Book of Sui juan 79; both the Book of Sui and Beishi read "now perhaps not falling from the old foundation"; here "now perhaps" reads "among them" and "fall" reads "extinguish"—both are acceptable; "destroy" was interpolated from the preceding "their destruction came swiftly" and is now deleted.
