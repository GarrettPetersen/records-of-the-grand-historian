1
高肇于勁胡國珍李延寔
Gao Zhao, Yu Jin, Hu Guozhen, and Li Yanshi
2
高肇，字首文，文昭皇太后之兄也。 自云本勃海蓨人，五世祖顧，晉永嘉中避亂入高麗。 父颺，字法脩。 高祖初，與弟乘信及其鄉人韓內、冀富等入國，拜厲威將軍、河間子，乘信明威將軍，俱待以客禮，賜奴婢牛馬綵帛。 遂納颺女，是為文昭皇后，生世宗。
Gao Zhao, whose courtesy name was Shouwen, was the elder brother of Empress Dowager Wenzhao. He claimed descent from Tiao in Bohai; during the Jin dynasty's Yongjia era his fifth-generation ancestor Gu had fled the turmoil and taken refuge in Goguryeo. His father was Yang, whose courtesy name was Faxian. Early in Emperor Gaozu's reign he came to the Wei state with his younger brother Chengxin and fellow townsmen Han Nei, Ji Fu, and others; he was made Bulwark-General and Marquis of Hejian, Chengxin Illustrious Might General, and all were treated as honored guests and granted slaves, cattle, horses, and colored silks. The emperor then married Yang's daughter, who became Empress Wenzhao and bore the future Emperor Shizong.
3
颺卒。 景明初，世宗追思舅氏，徵肇兄弟等。 錄尚書事、北海王詳等奏：「颺宜贈左光祿大夫，賜爵勃海公，諡曰敬。 其妻蓋氏宜追封清河郡君。 」詔可。 又詔颺嫡孫猛襲勃海公爵，封肇平原郡公，肇弟顯澄城郡公。 三人同日受封。 始世宗未與舅氏相接，將拜爵，乃賜衣幘引見肇、顯于華林都亭。 皆甚惶懼，舉動失儀。 數日之間，富貴赫弈。 是年，咸陽王禧誅，財物珍寶奴婢田宅多入高氏。 未幾，肇為尚書左僕射、領吏部、冀州大中正，尚世宗姑高平公主，遷尚書令。
Yang died. At the opening of the Jingming era Emperor Shizong, mindful of his mother's family, summoned Zhao and his brothers. The Director of the Imperial Secretariat, Prince of Beihai Xiang, and others submitted a memorial: "Yang should be posthumously granted Left Grand Master of Splendid Happiness, enfeoffed as Duke of Bohai, and given the posthumous title Jing." His wife, Lady Gai, should be posthumously enfeoffed as Lady of Qinghe Commandery. The emperor approved by edict. Another edict named Yang's eldest grandson's son Meng successor to the ducal title of Bohai, enfeoffed Zhao as Duke of Pingyuan Commandery, and Zhao's younger brother Xian as Duke of Chengcheng Commandery. All three received their titles on the same day. Until then Emperor Shizong had never met his mother's kin; when he was about to confer their titles, he had caps and robes given them and received Zhao and Xian at the Duting pavilion in Hualin Park. Both were deeply frightened and so ill at ease that their deportment fell apart. Within days their wealth and rank had risen to dazzling heights. That year Prince of Xianyang Xi was put to death, and much of his property, treasures, slaves, fields, and houses passed into the Gao clan. Before long Zhao was made Left Vice Director of the Imperial Secretariat, concurrently head of the Ministry of Personnel and chief assessor of Ji Province; he married Emperor Shizong's paternal aunt, the Princess of Gaoping, and was then promoted to Director of the Imperial Secretariat.
4
肇出自夷土，時望輕之。 及在位居要，留心百揆，孜孜無倦，世咸謂之為能。 世宗初，六輔專政，後以咸陽王禧無事構逆，由是遂委信肇。 肇既無親族，頗結朋黨，附之者旬月超昇，背之者陷以大罪。 以北海王詳位居其上，構殺之。 又說世宗防衞諸王，殆同囚禁。 時順皇后暴崩，世議言肇為之。 皇子昌薨，僉謂王顯失於醫療，承肇意旨。 及京兆王愉出為冀州刺史，畏肇恣擅，遂至不軌。 肇又譖殺彭城王勰。 由是朝野側目，咸畏惡之。 因此專權，與奪任己。 又嘗與清河王懌於雲龍門外廡下，忽忿諍，大至紛紜。 太尉、高陽王雍和止之。 高后既立，愈見寵信。 肇既當衡軸，每事任己，本無學識，動違禮度，好改先朝舊制，出情妄作，減削封秩，抑黜勳人。 由是怨聲盈路矣。 延昌初，遷司徒。 雖貴登台鼎，猶以去要怏怏形乎辭色。 眾咸嗤笑之。 父兄封贈雖久，竟不改瘞。 三年，乃詔令遷葬。 肇不自臨赴，唯遣其兄子猛改服詣代，遷葬於鄉。 時人以肇無識，哂而不責也。
Because Zhao came from a frontier background, men of the time held him in low regard. Once he held high office, he applied himself tirelessly to the hundred affairs of state, and his contemporaries widely judged him capable. Early in Emperor Shizong's reign the six chief ministers monopolized government; after Prince of Xianyang Xi was found to have plotted rebellion without cause, the emperor placed his trust in Zhao instead. Zhao had no kin of his own and therefore built up a faction; adherents were promoted within a month, while those who opposed him were ruined on grave charges. Because Prince of Beihai Xiang outranked him, he framed him and brought about his death. He also persuaded Emperor Shizong to guard the princes so closely that they were kept in conditions little short of imprisonment. When Empress Shun died suddenly, public opinion held that Zhao was behind it. When the imperial son Chang died, all agreed that Wang Xian had bungled his medical care at Zhao's direction. When Prince of Jingzhao Yu was sent out as Governor of Ji Province, fear of Zhao's arbitrary power drove him to misconduct. Zhao also slandered Prince of Pengcheng Xie and brought about his death. Court and countryside alike turned against him, and all feared and hated him. He thus monopolized power and granted or withheld office entirely at his own pleasure. He once broke into a furious quarrel with Prince of Qinghe Yi beneath the colonnade outside the Cloud Dragon Gate, and the dispute grew extremely heated. Grand Marshal Prince of Gaoyang Yong intervened and separated them. After Empress Gao was installed, he grew ever more favored and trusted. Once Zhao held the pivot of power he made every affair depend on himself; he had no learning to begin with, constantly violated ritual norms, loved to alter the former court's institutions, acted capriciously from personal whim, reduced enfeoffments and ranks, and suppressed men of merit. Resentment filled the roads. At the opening of the Yanchang era he was made Minister of Education. Although he had risen to one of the three highest offices, he still showed his displeasure in word and countenance at having left the key post. Everyone laughed at him. Although his father and elder brothers had long received posthumous honors, he never had their remains reinterred. In the third year an edict finally ordered their reinterment. Zhao did not go in person but only sent his elder brother's son Meng, who put on mourning garb and went to Dai to have the remains reinterred in their native place. Contemporaries, judging Zhao ignorant, laughed at him but did not blame him.
5
其年，大舉征蜀，以肇為大將軍，都督諸軍為之節度。 與都督甄琛等二十餘人俱面辭世宗於東堂，親奉規略。 是日，肇所乘駿馬停於神虎門外，無故驚倒，轉臥渠中，鞍具瓦解，眾咸怪異。 肇出，惡焉。
That year a great campaign was launched against Shu; Zhao was made Grand General with overall command of the armies. Together with more than twenty commanders including Zhen Chen, he took leave of Emperor Shizong in the Eastern Hall and received the emperor's strategic instructions in person. That day the fine horse Zhao was riding halted outside the Divine Tiger Gate, startled and fell for no reason, rolled into a ditch, and its saddle and trappings fell apart; everyone regarded it as an omen. When Zhao set out, he took it as a bad omen.
6
四年，世宗崩，赦罷征軍。 肅宗與肇及征南將軍元遙等書，稱諱言，以告凶問。 肇承變哀愕，非唯仰慕，亦私憂身禍，朝夕悲泣，至于羸悴。 將至，宿瀍澗驛亭，家人夜迎省之，皆不相視。 直至闕下，衰服號哭，昇太極殿，奉喪盡哀。
In the fourth year Emperor Shizong died; an amnesty was proclaimed and the campaign army was disbanded. Emperor Suzong wrote to Zhao, the southern campaign general Yuan Yao, and others, using the tabooed imperial name to announce the emperor's death. When Zhao received the news he was stunned with grief; it was not only mourning for the departed emperor but also private fear for his own life, and he wept morning and evening until he was wasted and haggard. As he neared the capital he lodged at the post station at Chanjian; his family came out at night to greet him, but none could bear to meet another's eyes. He went straight to the palace gates, wailed in mourning garb, ascended the Hall of Supreme Ultimate, and attended the coffin with full grief.
7
太尉高陽王先居西柏堂，專決庶事，與領軍于忠密欲除之。 潛備壯士直寢邢豹、伊瓫生等十餘人於舍人省下。 肇哭梓宮訖，於百官前引入西廊，清河王懌、任城王澄及諸王等皆竊言目之。 肇入省，壯士搤而拉殺之。 下詔暴其罪惡，又云刑書未及，便至自盡，自餘親黨，悉無追問，削除職爵，葬以士禮。 及昏，乃於厠門出其尸歸家。 初，肇西征，行至函谷，車軸中折。 從者皆以為不獲吉還也。 靈太后臨朝，令特贈營州刺史。 永熙二年，出帝贈使持節、侍中、中外諸軍事、太師、大丞相、太尉公、錄尚書事、冀州刺史。
Grand Marshal Prince of Gaoyang had first taken up residence in the Western Cypress Hall and decided ordinary affairs on his own; together with the commander of the guards Yu Zhong he secretly plotted to eliminate Zhao. They secretly stationed more than ten stalwart men of the inner guard, including Xing Bao and Yi Tousheng, below the Attendants' Office. When Zhao had finished mourning before the imperial coffin, he was led before the officials into the western corridor; Prince of Qinghe Yi, Prince of Rencheng Cheng, and the other princes all whispered and watched him. When Zhao entered the office, the stalwarts seized him by the throat and strangled him to death. An edict exposed his crimes and declared that before the penal code could be applied he had taken his own life; the rest of his kin were not pursued, his offices and titles were struck off, and he was buried with the rites due a common gentleman. At dusk his corpse was brought out through the privy gate and taken home. Earlier, when Zhao marched west on campaign and reached Hangu Pass, an axle broke in the middle. His followers all thought he would not return safely. When Empress Dowager Ling held court, she ordered a special posthumous grant as Governor of Ying Province. In the second year of Yongxi, Emperor Chuodi posthumously granted him Bearer of the Staff of Authority, Palace Attendant, Commander-in-Chief of All Military Affairs Internal and External, Grand Preceptor, Grand Chancellor, Duke and Grand Marshal, Director of the Imperial Secretariat, and Governor of Ji Province.
8
肇子植。 自中書侍郎為濟州刺史，率州軍討破元愉，別將有功。 當蒙封賞，不受，云：「家荷重恩，為國致效是其常節，何足以應進陟之報。 」懇惻發於至誠。 歷青、相、朔、恒四州刺史，卒。 植頻莅五州，皆清能著稱，當時號為良刺史。 贈安北將軍、冀州刺史。
Zhao's son was Zhi. From Palace Secretariat Attendant he became Governor of Ji Province, led the provincial army to defeat Yuan Yu, and as a separate commander performed meritorious service. He was due enfeoffment and reward but refused, saying: "My family has received heavy grace; serving the state is our ordinary duty—how could that suffice as grounds for advancement and reward?" His earnest sincerity sprang from the depths of his heart. He served successively as governor of Qing, Xiang, Shuo, and Heng provinces, then died. Zhi held office in five provinces in all and in each was renowned for integrity and ability; his contemporaries called him a good governor. He was posthumously granted Pacification-North General and Governor of Ji Province.
9
肇長兄琨，早卒。 襲颺封勃海郡公，贈都督五州諸軍事、鎮東大將軍、冀州刺史。 詔其子猛嗣。
Zhao's eldest brother Kun died young. He succeeded to Yang's enfeoffment as Duke of Bohai Commandery and was posthumously granted Commander-in-Chief of Military Affairs in Five Provinces, Pacification-East Grand General, and Governor of Ji Province. An edict named his son Meng as successor.
10
猛，字豹兒。 尚長樂公主，即世宗同母妹也。 拜駙馬都尉，歷位中書令。 出為雍州刺史，有能名。 入為殿中尚書。 卒，贈司空、冀州刺史。 出帝時，復贈太師、大丞相、錄尚書事。 公主無子。 猛先在外有男，不敢令主知，臨終方言之，年幾三十矣。 乃召為喪主，尋卒，無後。
Meng, whose courtesy name was Bao'er. He married the Princess of Changle, Emperor Shizong's younger sister by the same mother. He was appointed Commandant of the Horse Guards for the Imperial Son-in-Law and rose to Director of the Imperial Secretariat. He was sent out as Governor of Yong Province and gained a reputation for ability. He was recalled to court as Director of the Palace Secretariat. He died and was posthumously granted Minister of Works and Governor of Ji Province. Under Emperor Chuodi he was again posthumously granted Grand Preceptor, Grand Chancellor, and Director of the Imperial Secretariat. The princess had no son. Meng had earlier fathered a son while away from home and did not dare tell his wife; only on his deathbed did he speak of it—the boy was nearly thirty. The boy was then summoned to serve as chief mourner, but soon died himself, leaving no heir.
11
琨弟偃，字仲游。 太和十年卒。 正始中，贈安東將軍、都督、青州刺史，諡曰莊侯。 景明四年，世宗納其女為貴嬪。 及于順皇后崩，永平元年立為皇后。 二年，八座奏封后母王氏為武邑郡君。
Kun's younger brother was Yan, whose courtesy name was Zhongyou. He died in the tenth year of Taihe. During the Zhengshi era he was posthumously granted Pacification-East General, Commander-in-Chief, and Governor of Qing Province, with the posthumous title Marquis Zhuang. In the fourth year of Jingming Emperor Shizong took his daughter as Honored Concubine. After Empress Shun of the Yu clan died, in the first year of Yongping she was installed as empress. In the second year the eight chief ministers memorialized to enfeoff the empress's mother, Lady Wang, as Lady of Wuyi Commandery.
12
偃弟壽，早卒。 壽弟即肇也。
Yan's younger brother Shou died young. Shou's younger brother was Zhao himself.
13
肇弟顯，侍中、高麗國大中正，早卒。
Zhao's younger brother Xian, who had been Palace Attendant and chief assessor of the state of Goguryeo, died young.
14
于勁，字鍾葵，太尉拔之子。 頗有武略。 以功臣子，又以功績，位沃野鎮將，賜爵富昌子，拜征虜將軍。 世宗納其女為后，封太原郡公。 妻劉氏，為章武郡君。 後拜征北將軍、定州刺史。 卒，贈司空，諡曰恭莊公。 自栗磾至勁，累世貴盛，一皇后，四贈公，三領軍，二尚書令，三開國公。 [2]勁雖以后父，但以順后早崩，竟不居公輔。
Yu Jin, whose courtesy name was Zhongkui, was the son of Grand Marshal Ba. He had considerable military talent. As the son of a meritorious minister and again for his own achievements, he was made garrison commander of Woye, granted the title Marquis of Fuchang, and appointed Campaign-General. Emperor Shizong took his daughter as empress and enfeoffed him as Duke of Taiyuan Commandery. His wife, Lady Liu, was made Lady of Zhangwu Commandery. Later he was appointed Campaign-North General and Governor of Ding Province. He died and was posthumously granted Minister of Works with the posthumous title Duke Gongzhuang. From Yili to Jin, generation after generation the house had been noble and flourishing: one empress, four posthumous dukes, three commanders of the guards, two directors of the Imperial Secretariat, and three founding marquises. [2] Although Jin was the empress's father, because Empress Shun died early he never held one of the chief ministerial posts.
15
子暉，字宣明，后母弟也。 少有氣幹。 襲爵，位汾州刺史。 暉善事人，為尒朱榮所親，以女妻其子長孺。 歷侍中、河南尹，後兼尚書僕射、東南道行臺。 與齊獻武王討平羊侃於兗州。 元顥入洛，害之。
His son was Hui, whose courtesy name was Xuanming and who was the empress's younger brother by the same mother. From youth he showed spirit and resolve. He inherited the title and served as Governor of Fen Province. Hui was skilled in winning people over; Erzhu Rong favored him, and he gave his daughter to Rong's son Changru in marriage. He served successively as Palace Attendant and Intendant of Henan, and later also as Vice Director of the Imperial Secretariat and commissioner of the southeastern circuit. Together with Prince Xianwu of Qi he suppressed Yang Kan in Yan Province. When Yuan Hao entered Luoyang, he had him killed.
16
勁弟天恩，位內行長、遼西太守。 卒，贈平東將軍、燕州刺史。
Jin's younger brother Tian'en was Director of the Inner Household and Governor of Liaoxi. He died and was posthumously granted the title General Who Pacifies the East and the post of Governor of Yan Province.
17
天恩子仁生，位太中大夫。
Tian'en's son Rensheng was a Grand Master of Palace Counsel.
18
仁生子安定，平原郡太守、高平郡都將。 [3]卒。
Rensheng's son Anding was Governor of Pingyuan Commandery and chief commander of Gaoping Commandery. [3] He died.
19
胡國珍，字世玉，安定臨涇人也。 祖略，姚興渤海公姚逵平北府諮議參軍。 父淵，赫連屈丐給事黃門侍郎。 世祖克統萬，淵以降款之功賜爵武始侯。 後拜河州刺史。
Hu Guozhen, whose courtesy name was Shiyu, was a native of Linjing in Anding. His grandfather Lue had been a staff adviser in the Northern Pacification Office under Yao Kui, Duke of Bohai, a descendant of Yao Xing. His father Yuan served Helian Qubugai as Attendant Gentleman at the Yellow Gate. When Emperor Shizu conquered Tongwan, Yuan was enfeoffed as Marquis of Wushi for surrendering in good faith. Later he was appointed Governor of He Province.
20
國珍少好學，雅尚清儉。 太和十五年襲爵，例降為伯。 女以選入掖庭，生肅宗，即靈太后也。 肅宗踐祚，以國珍為光祿大夫。 靈太后臨朝，加侍中，封安定郡公，給甲第，賜帛布綿縠奴婢車馬牛甚厚。 追崇國珍妻皇甫氏為京兆郡君，置守冢十戶。 尚書令、任城王澄奏，安定公屬尊望重，親賢羣矚，宜出入禁中，參諮大務。 詔可。 乃令入決萬幾。 尋進位中書監、儀同三司，侍中如故，賞賜累萬。 又賜絹歲八百匹，妻梁四百匹，男女姊妹兄弟各有差，皆極豐贍。 國珍與太師、高陽王雍，太傅、清河王懌，太保、廣平王懷，入居門下，同釐庶政。 詔依漢車千秋、晉安平王故事，給步挽一乘，自掖門至于宣光殿得以出入，并備几杖。 後與侍中崔光俱授帝經，侍直禁中。 國珍尋上表，陳刑政之宜。 詔皆施行。 熙平初，[4]加國珍使持節、都督、雍州刺史、驃騎大將軍、開府。 靈太后以國珍年老，不欲令其在外，且欲示以方面之榮，竟不行。 遷司徒公，侍中如故，就宅拜之。 靈太后、肅宗率百僚幸其第，宴會極歡。 又追京兆郡君為秦太上君。 [5]太上君景明三年薨於洛陽，於此十六年矣。 太后以太上君墳瘞卑局，更增廣，為起塋域門闕碑表。 侍中崔光等奏：「案漢高祖母始諡曰昭靈夫人，後為昭靈后，薄太后母曰靈文夫人，皆置園邑三百家，長丞奉守。 今秦太上君未有尊諡，陵寢孤立，即秦君名，宜上終稱，兼設掃衞，以慰情典。 請上尊諡曰孝穆，權置園邑三十戶，立長丞奉守。 」太后從之。 封國珍繼室梁氏為趙平郡君，元叉妻拜為女侍中，封新平郡君，又徙封馮翊君。 國珍子祥妻長安縣公主，即清河王懌女也。
From youth Guozhen loved learning and prized simplicity and frugality. In Taihe year 15 he inherited the title, which by regulation was reduced one rank to count. His daughter was selected for the palace harem, bore Emperor Suzong, and became Empress Dowager Ling. When Emperor Suzong took the throne, he appointed Guozhen Grand Master of Splendid Happiness. When Empress Dowager Ling held court, he was additionally made Palace Attendant and enfeoffed as Duke of Anding Commandery, given a first-class mansion, and lavishly granted silk, cloth, cotton, damask, slaves, chariots, horses, and cattle. Guozhen's wife, Lady Huangfu, was posthumously honored as Lady of Jingzhao Commandery, and ten households were appointed to guard her tomb. Director of the Imperial Secretariat Prince of Rencheng Cheng memorialized: "Duke of Anding is the empress's honored kin and carries great prestige; the court looks to him. He should have access to the inner palace and a voice in weighty affairs." The edict approved. He was then authorized to enter the inner court and decide state affairs. Before long he was promoted to Supervisor of the Palace Secretariat with the Three Precedents of Honor while retaining his post as Palace Attendant, and rewards ran into the tens of thousands. He was also granted eight hundred bolts of silk each year; his wife Lady Liang received four hundred; sons, daughters, sisters, and brothers each received differing amounts—all lavish beyond measure. Guozhen joined Grand Preceptor Prince of Gaoyang Yong, Grand Tutor Prince of Qinghe Yi, and Grand Guardian Prince of Guangping Huai in residence at the Gate beneath the emperor to oversee ordinary government together. By edict, following the precedents of Han's Che Qianqiu and Jin's Prince of Ping'an, he was granted a hand-drawn carriage and might pass from the Rear Gate to Xuanguang Hall; he was also provided with a folding stool and staff. Later he and Palace Attendant Cui Guang together instructed the emperor in the classics, standing duty in the inner palace. Before long Guozhen submitted a memorial on what was fitting in law and administration. An edict ordered all of it carried out. At the opening of Xiping, [4] Guozhen was additionally granted the ceremonial staff, made commissioner and Governor of Yong Province, appointed Rapid Cavalry General-in-Chief, and given an opening office. Empress Dowager Ling, finding Guozhen too old to send away, still wished to confer the honor of a regional command on him; in the end he never took up the post. He was transferred to Minister of Works while retaining his post as Palace Attendant, and the appointment was conferred at his home. Empress Dowager Ling and Emperor Suzong led the hundred officials to his mansion, where the feast reached the height of conviviality. Lady of Jingzhao Commandery was again posthumously honored as Grand Supremacy Lady of Qin. [5] The Grand Supremacy Lady had died in Luoyang in Jingming year 3; sixteen years had now passed. Because the Grand Supremacy Lady's tomb precinct was too small, the empress dowager enlarged it and erected a burial park with gate towers, steles, and markers. Palace Attendant Cui Guang and others memorialized: "By precedent, Emperor Gao of Han's mother was first titled Lady Zhaoling and later Empress Zhaoling; Empress Dowager Bo's mother was titled Lady Lingwen—all were given park-estates of three hundred households with wardens and attendants to maintain them. The Grand Supremacy Lady of Qin has no honored posthumous title and her tomb stands alone. Following the Qin lady's name, a final title ought to be conferred, with sweepers and guards appointed, to satisfy ritual propriety. We request the posthumous title Xiaomu and provisionally a park-estate of thirty households, with wardens and attendants appointed to maintain it. " The empress dowager agreed. Guozhen's second wife, Lady Liang, was enfeoffed as Lady of Zhaoping Commandery; Yuan Cha's wife was appointed Female Palace Attendant and enfeoffed as Lady of Xinping Commandery, later transferred to Lady of Fengyi. Guozhen's son Xiang married the Princess of Chang'an County, daughter of Prince of Qinghe Yi.
21
國珍年雖篤老，而雅敬佛法，時事齋潔，自強禮拜。 至於出入侍從，猶能跨馬據鞍。 神龜元年四月七日，步從所建佛像，發第至閶闔門四五里。 八日，又立觀像，晚乃肯坐。 勞熱增甚，因遂寢疾。 靈太后親侍藥膳。 十二日薨，年八十。 給東園溫明祕器、五時朝服各一具、衣一襲，贈布五千匹、錢一百萬、蠟千斤。 大鴻臚持節監護喪事。 太后還宮，成服於九龍殿，遂居九龍寢室。 肅宗服小功服，舉哀於太極東堂。 又詔自始薨至七七，皆為設千僧齋，令七人出家； 百日設萬人齋，二七人出家。 先是巫覡言將有凶，勸令為厭勝之法。 國珍拒而不從，云吉凶有定分，唯修德以禳之。 臨死與太后訣云：「母子善治天下，以萬人之心，勿視大臣面也。 」殷勤至於再三。 又及其子祥，云：「我唯有一子，死後勿如比來威抑之。 」靈太后以其好戲，時加威訓。 國珍故以為言。
Though greatly aged, Guozhen reverenced Buddhism, kept periods of purification and fasting, and forced himself to perform prostrations. When he went abroad with his attendants he could still mount a horse and hold the saddle. On the seventh day of the fourth month of Shengui year 1, he walked on foot following the Buddha image he had commissioned from his mansion to within four or five li of Chongle Gate. On the eighth he again stood viewing the image and would not sit until evening. Fatigue and fever worsened, and he took to his bed. Empress Dowager Ling personally attended his medicines and meals. He died on the twelfth, aged eighty. He was granted the warm bright coffin from the Eastern Garden, one set of court robes for each season, one suit of garments, and five thousand bolts of cloth, a million cash, and a thousand jin of wax. The Grand Herald supervised the funeral bearing the ceremonial staff. The empress dowager returned to the palace, donned mourning in the Hall of Nine Dragons, and took up residence in the Nine Dragons bedchamber. Emperor Suzong wore the third-degree mourning garments and raised lamentation in the eastern hall of the Hall of Supreme Ultimate. An edict also ordered thousand-monk feasts for each observance from the day of death through the forty-nine days, with seven persons ordained each time; on the hundredth day a feast for ten thousand, with twenty-seven persons ordained. Earlier shamans and diviners had foretold disaster and urged exorcistic rites. Guozhen refused, saying fortune and misfortune are allotted and can only be warded off by cultivating virtue. On his deathbed he bade farewell to the empress dowager, saying, "Mother and son, govern the realm well; with the hearts of the myriad people— do not look only to the ministers' faces." He urged this again and again. He also spoke to his son Xiang, saying, "I have only this one son; after my death do not restrain him with severity as you have lately ." Because Xiang was fond of play, Empress Dowager Ling had often disciplined him sternly. That was what Guozhen had in mind when he said it.
22
始國珍欲就祖父西葬舊鄉，後緣前世諸胡多在洛葬，有終洛之心。 崔光嘗對太后前問國珍：「公萬年後為在此安厝，為歸長安？ 」國珍言當陪葬天子山陵。 及病危，太后請以後事，竟言還安定，語遂昏忽。 太后問清河王懌與崔光等，議去留。 懌等皆以病亂，請從先言。 太后猶記崔光昔與國珍言，遂營墓於洛陽。 太后雖外從眾議，而深追臨終之語，云：「我公之遠慕二親，亦吾之思父母也。」
At first Guozhen had wished to join his grandfather in burial back west in his old home; later, seeing that many families of their kind had been buried at Luoyang for generations, he came to want his end there too. Cui Guang once asked Guozhen before the empress dowager, "When the time comes, will you be laid to rest here or return to Chang'an? Guozhen replied that he would be buried in the imperial mausoleum alongside the Son of Heaven. When he fell critically ill, the empress dowager asked about his last wishes; in the end he spoke of returning to Anding, then his words grew confused. The empress dowager asked Prince of Qinghe Yi, Cui Guang, and the others to deliberate whether the burial should be at Luoyang or in the west. Yi and the others cited the confusion of his illness and urged following his earlier statement. The empress dowager still remembered Cui Guang's earlier exchange with Guozhen and had a tomb prepared at Luoyang. Outwardly the empress dowager followed the court's decision, yet she deeply mourned his dying wish, saying, "My lord's longing for his parents in the far west is also my longing for my own parents."
23
追崇假黃鉞、使持節、侍中、相國、都督中外諸軍事、太師、領太尉公、司州牧，號太上秦公，加九錫。 葬以殊禮，給九旒鑾輅，虎賁、班劍百人，前後部羽葆鼓吹，轀輬車； 諡文宣公； 賜物三千段、粟一千五百石。 又詔贈國珍祖父兄、父兄，下逮從子，皆有封職。 持節就安定監護喪事。 [6]靈太后迎太上君神柩還第，與國珍俱葬，贈襚一與國珍同。 及國珍神主入廟，詔太常權給以軒懸之樂、六佾之舞。 初國珍無男，養兄真子僧洗為後，後納趙平君，生子祥。
He was posthumously honored with the ceremonial axe and staff, named Palace Attendant, Chancellor of State, commander-in-chief of all armies, Grand Preceptor, concurrently Duke of Grand Commandant and Governor of Si Province, titled Grand Supremacy Duke of Qin, and granted the Nine Bestowals. He was buried with extraordinary rites: the nine tassels and imperial chariot, a hundred tiger guards and ceremonial swords, front and rear feather-canopy musicians, and a pall carriage; posthumous title Duke Wenxuan; three thousand lengths of goods and fifteen hundred piculs of grain were granted. An edict also posthumously enfeoffed Guozhen's grandfather's and father's elder brothers and conferred ranks down to his cousins. Officials bearing the ceremonial staff went to Anding to supervise the funeral. [6] Empress Dowager Ling brought the Grand Supremacy Lady's spirit coffin back to the mansion and buried her with Guozhen; her funeral gifts matched his. When Guozhen's spirit tablet entered the ancestral temple, the Minister of Ceremonies was ordered provisionally to grant the full royal music and six rows of dancers. At first Guozhen had no sons; he adopted his elder brother Zhen's son Sengxi as heir, and later took Lady of Zhaoping and bore Xiang.
24
僧洗，字湛輝。 封爰德縣公，位中書監、侍中，改封濮陽郡公。 僧洗自永安後廢棄，不預朝政。 天平四年薨，詔給東園祕器，贈太師、太尉公、錄尚書事、雍州刺史，諡曰孝。
Sengxi, whose courtesy name was Zhanhui. He was enfeoffed Duke of Aide County, served as Supervisor of the Palace Secretariat and Palace Attendant, and was later made Duke of Puyang Commandery. After Yongan, Sengxi was cast aside and took no part in government. He died in Tianping year 4; he was granted the Eastern Garden coffin and posthumously honored as Grand Preceptor, Duke of Grand Commandant, Recorder of Affairs of the Imperial Secretariat, and Governor of Yong Province, with the posthumous title Xiao.
25
真長子寧，[7]字惠歸。 襲國珍先爵，改為臨涇伯，後進為公。 歷岐涇二州刺史。 卒，諡曰孝穆。 女為清河王亶妃，生孝靜皇帝。 武定初，贈太師、太尉公、錄尚書事，諡曰孝昭。
Zhen's eldest son was Ning, [7] whose courtesy name was Huigui. He inherited Guozhen's former rank, which was changed to Count of Linjing and later raised to duke. He served successively as governor of Qi and Jing provinces. He died and was given the posthumous title Xiaomu. His daughter was consort to Prince of Qinghe Tan and bore Emperor Xiaojing. At the opening of Wuding he was posthumously honored as Grand Preceptor, Duke of Grand Commandant, and Recorder of Affairs of the Imperial Secretariat, with the posthumous title Xiaozhao.
26
子虔，字僧敬。 元叉之廢靈太后，虔時為千牛備身，與備身張車渠等謀殺叉。 事發，叉殺車渠等，虔坐遠徙。 靈太后反政，徵為吏部郎中。 太后好以家人禮與親族宴戲，虔常致諫，由是後宴謔多不預焉。 出為涇州刺史，封安陽縣侯。 興和三年，以帝元舅超遷司空公。 薨，贈太傅、太尉公、尚書僕射、徐州刺史，諡曰宣。 葬日，百官會葬，乘輿送於郭外。 子長粲。
His son was Qian, whose courtesy name was Sengjing. When Yuan Cha deposed Empress Dowager Ling, Qian was a gentleman guard of the Thousand-Ox Corps and plotted with fellow guard Zhang Chequ and others to kill Cha. When the plot was discovered, Cha executed Chequ and the others; Qian was exiled to a distant post. When Empress Dowager Ling resumed power, he was recalled as Bureau Director in the Ministry of Personnel. The empress dowager liked to entertain her kin in informal family style; Qian often remonstrated, and thereafter was often left out of such feasts and games. He was sent out as Governor of Jing Province and enfeoffed as Marquis of Anyang County. In Xinghe year 3, as the emperor's chief maternal uncle, he was promoted outright to Minister of Works. He died and was posthumously honored as Grand Tutor, Duke of Grand Commandant, Vice Director of the Imperial Secretariat, and Governor of Xu Province, with the posthumous title Xuan. On the day of burial all officials attended the funeral, and the imperial carriage escorted the coffin beyond the city walls. His son was Changcan.
27
李延寔，字禧，隴西人，尚書僕射沖之長子。 性溫良，少為太子舍人。 世宗初，襲父爵清泉縣侯。 [8]累遷左將軍、光州刺史。 莊帝即位，以元舅之尊，超授侍中、太保，封濮陽郡王。 延寔以太保犯祖諱，又以王爵非庶姓所宜，抗表固辭。 徙封濮陽郡公，改授太傅。 尋轉司徒公，出為使持節、侍中、太傅、錄尚書事、青州刺史。 尒朱兆入洛，乘輿幽縶，以延寔外戚，見害於州館。 出帝初，歸葬洛陽。 贈使持節、侍中、太師、太尉公、錄尚書事、都督、雍州刺史，諡曰孝懿。
Li Yanshi, whose courtesy name was Xi, was a native of Longxi and the eldest son of Vice Director of the Imperial Secretariat Li Chong. Gentle by nature, in youth he served as Attendant of the Heir Apparent. Early in Emperor Shizong's reign he inherited his father's title as Marquis of Qingquan County. [8] He rose in succession to Left General and Governor of Guang Province. When Emperor Zhuangdi ascended the throne, in honor of his status as the emperor's maternal uncle he was exceptionally appointed Palace Attendant and Grand Guardian and enfeoffed as Prince of Puyang Commandery. Yanshi submitted a firm memorial of refusal, arguing that Grand Guardian violated the imperial ancestral taboo and that a princely title was not fitting for a common surname. He was transferred to Duke of Puyang Commandery and appointed Grand Tutor instead. Soon he was made Minister of Education, then sent out as Bearer of the Staff of Authority, Palace Attendant, Grand Tutor, Director of the Imperial Secretariat, and Governor of Qing Province. When Erzhu Zhao entered Luoyang and the emperor was held captive, Yanshi, as maternal kin, was killed in the provincial residence. Early under Emperor Chuodi his remains were returned for burial at Luoyang. He was posthumously granted Bearer of the Staff of Authority, Palace Attendant, Grand Preceptor, Duke and Grand Marshal, Director of the Imperial Secretariat, Commander-in-Chief, and Governor of Yong Province, with the posthumous title Xiaoyi.
28
長子彧，字子文，尚莊帝姊豐亭公主。 封東平郡公，位侍中、左光祿大夫、中書監、驃騎大將軍、開府儀同三司、廣州刺史。 彧任俠交遊，輕薄無行。 尒朱榮之死也，武毅之士皆彧所進。 孝靜初，以罪棄市。
His eldest son was Yu, whose courtesy name was Ziwen; he married the Princess of Fengting, Emperor Zhuangdi's elder sister. He was enfeoffed as Duke of Dongping Commandery and held the posts of Palace Attendant, Left Grand Master of Splendid Happiness, Supervisor of the Palace Secretariat, Swift Cavalry Grand General, Grand Guardian with the Bountiful Rites Equal to the Three Dukes, and Governor of Guang Province. Yu cultivated chivalrous friendships but was frivolous and unprincipled in conduct. At Erzhu Rong's death, the bold warriors who struck the blow had all been introduced by Yu. Early in Emperor Xiaojing's reign he was executed in the marketplace for his crimes.
29
史臣曰：三五哲王，深防遠慮。 舅甥之國，罕執鈞衡； 母后之家，無聞傾敗。 爰及後世，顛覆繼軌。 蓋由進不以禮，故其斃亦速。 其間或不泯舊基，[9]弗虧先構者，蓋處之以道，遠權之所致也。
The historian writes: The sage kings of the Three Dynasties and the Five Emperors took deep precautions and looked far ahead. In states ruled by uncle and nephew, few held the balance of power; among the families of empresses dowager and queens, one never heard of ruin and overthrow. But in later ages overthrow followed in succession. This was because advancement did not follow ritual propriety, and so their destruction came swiftly as well. Those among them who did not extinguish the old foundation[9] or impair what their forebears had built did so because they were treated according to the Way and because distant authority was exercised over them.

Book of Wei, juan 83b: various editions' tables of contents mark this juan as deficient; at the end of the juan there is a Song-dynasty collation note (Entered into textual verification in the Palace Edition) stating: "Wei Shou's original Treatise on Maternal Kin is lost; the historian's discussion is entirely taken from the Sui shu Treatise on Maternal Kin (juan 79) ." From Yili to Jin (to) three founding marquises: These words originally stood in the Book of Wei, juan 31, after the appended biography of Yu Zhong in the biography of Yu Libi; the Northern History, juan 23, moved them into the biography of Yu Jin—then the offices held by the later Yu Zhong should not be counted, and the tallies of posthumous dukes, commanders of the guards, directors of the Imperial Secretariat, and founding marquises are each one short—a real error. This biography was supplemented from the Northern History without noting that juan 31 already contained these words, causing duplication. See the collation note [9] to juan 31.

高平郡都將按郡無都將，「郡」當是「鎮」之訛。
Chief commander of Gaoping Commandery: a commandery has no such post as "chief commander"; "commandery" should be a corruption of "garrison."
34
熙平初諸本及北史卷八０胡國珍傳「熙平」作「延和」，通志卷一六五胡國珍傳作「熙平中」。 按事見卷九肅宗紀熙平元年八月乙巳，延和乃拓跋燾年號，遠在其前。 今據通志改作「熙平」。 事在元年，「初」字不誤。
At the beginning of Xiping: various editions and the Northern History, juan 80, biography of Hu Guozhen read "Xiping" as "Yanhe"; the Tongzhi, juan 165, biography of Hu Guozhen reads "mid-Xiping." The event appears in juan 9, Annals of Emperor Suzong, eighth month, day yisi of the first year of Xiping; Yanhe is the reign title of Tuoba Tao, far earlier. Following the Tongzhi, the text is emended to "Xiping." The event falls in the first year; the word "beginning" is not in error.

又追京兆郡君為秦太上君按「追」下當脫「贈」或「尊」「崇」字。
Again, Lady of Jingzhao Commandery was posthumously honored as Grand Dowager of Qin: After "posthumously" the word "granted," "honored," or "exalted" is missing.

持節就安定監護喪事按胡國珍死和葬都在洛陽，何故「就安定監護喪事」？ 下文云：「迎太上君神柩還第」，疑「安定」下脫「公第」二字。
Bearer of the Staff of Authority went to Anding to supervise the funeral: Hu Guozhen died and was buried at Luoyang—why does the text say he went to Anding to supervise the funeral? 真長子寧諸本「真」字連上段「諡曰孝」，「長子寧」提行。 成為胡僧洗諡「孝真」，「寧」是僧洗的長子。 按真乃國珍兄，見上文，寧乃僧洗兄。 北史原文本不提行，後人寫刻魏書則凡附見諸子孫無不提行，這裏由於誤讀，提行亦謬，致以兄為子，今改正。
Zhen's eldest son Ning: various editions join the character "Zhen" to the preceding passage "posthumous title Xiao," and begin a new line at "eldest son Ning." This makes Hu Sengxi's posthumous title "Xiaozhen," and makes "Ning" Sengxi's eldest son. Zhen was Guozhen's elder brother, as seen above, and Ning was Sengxi's elder brother. The Northern History original did not begin a new line; later copyists and printers of the Book of Wei always began a new line for every appended son or grandson—here misreading produced a wrong line break that made an elder brother into a son; the text is now corrected.

襲父爵清泉縣侯按卷五三李沖傳，沖封「清淵縣侯」。 「清淵」為漢以來舊縣，卷一０六上地形志上，屬司州陽平郡。 此傳以北史補，避唐諱改「泉」。
Inherited his father's title as Marquis of Qingquan County: According to juan 53, biography of Li Chong, Chong was enfeoffed as Marquis of Qingyuan County. "Qingyuan" is an old county since Han times; juan 106a, Treatise on Geography, Part 1, places it in Yangping Commandery, Si Province. This biography was supplemented from the Northern History; to avoid Tang taboo "yuan" was changed to "quan."
39
其間或不泯舊基諸本「泯」上有「斃」字。 此傳本出北史，北史傳論又本隋書卷七九外戚傳論，今檢隋書、北史都作「今或不隕舊基」，這裏「今或」作「其間」，「隕」作「泯」，均兩通，「斃」字則涉上「其斃亦速」而衍，今刪。
Among them, those who did not extinguish the old foundation: various editions have the character "destroyed" above "extinguish." This historian's discussion originally comes from the Northern History, which in turn draws on the Sui shu, juan 79, Treatise on Maternal Kin; checking the Sui shu and Northern History, both read "now perhaps they do not fall from the old foundation"—here "now perhaps" appears as "among them" and "fall" as "extinguish," both acceptable; the character "destroyed" is a derivative from the preceding "their destruction came swiftly" and is now deleted.
