竇瑾許彥李訢
Dou Jin, Xu Yan, and Li Xin
2
竇瑾，字道瑜，頓丘衞國人也。 自云漢司空融之後。 高祖成為頓丘太守，因家焉。 瑾少以文學知名。 自中書博士，為中書侍郎，賜爵繁陽子，加寧遠將軍。 參與軍國之謀，屢有軍功。 遷祕書監，進爵衞國侯，加冠軍將軍，轉西部尚書。 初定三秦，人猶去就，拜使持節、散騎常侍、都督秦雍二州諸軍事、寧西將軍、長安鎮將、毗陵公。 在鎮八年，甚著威惠。 徵為殿中、都官尚書，仍散騎常侍。 世祖親待之，賞賜甚厚。 從征蓋吳，先驅慰諭，因平巴西氐、羌酋領，降下數千家，不下者誅之。 又降蠻酋仇天爾等三千家於五將山。 蓋吳平，瑾留鎮長安。 還京，復為殿中、都官，典左右執法。 世祖歎曰：「古者右賢左戚，國之良翰，毗陵公之謂矣。 」恭宗薨於東宮，瑾兼司徒，奉詔冊諡。 出為鎮南將軍、冀州刺史。 清約沖素，憂勤王事，著稱當時。 還為內都大官。 興光初，瑾女壻鬱林公司馬彌陀以選尚臨涇公主，瑾教彌陀辭託，有誹謗呪詛之言，與彌陀同誅。 瑾有四子，秉、持、依並為中書學生，與父同時伏法。 唯少子遵，逃匿得免。
Dou Jin, whose courtesy name was Daoyu, came from Dunqiu in Weiguo Commandery. He claimed descent from Dou Rong, Minister of Works under the Han. An ancestor named Cheng had served as Administrator of Dunqiu, and the family made its home there. From his youth Jin was known for his literary attainments. Starting as a Doctor of the Secretariat, he rose to Secretariat Gentleman, was enfeoffed as Marquis of Fanyang, and received the additional rank of General Who Pacifies the Distant. He took part in state and military planning and won distinction in battle more than once. He was promoted to Director of the Secretariat, raised to Marquis of Weiguo, given the additional rank of General Who Establishes Victory, and appointed Western Department Director. When the Three Qins were first brought under control and the populace was still uncertain where to stand, he was invested as Bearer of the Staff of Authority, Regular Attendant of the Scattered Cavalry, Commander-in-Chief of military affairs in Qin and Yong, General Who Pacifies the West, Garrison Commander of Chang'an, and Duke of Piling. He held the garrison for eight years and became widely known for both stern authority and generous kindness. He was recalled to the capital as Director of the Palace and of the Capital Offices, while continuing as Regular Attendant of the Scattered Cavalry. Emperor Taiwu treated him with marked personal favor and lavished rewards upon him. On the campaign against Gai Wu he rode ahead to reassure and instruct the people, then pacified Di and Qiang leaders in Ba, winning the submission of several thousand households; those who refused were put to death. He also brought three thousand households of the Man chieftain Chou Tian'er and others to submit at Wujiang Mountain. After Gai Wu was defeated, Jin stayed on to garrison Chang'an. On his return to the capital he again held the posts of Director of the Palace and of the Capital Offices and supervised judicial enforcement at court. Emperor Taiwu sighed and said, "In antiquity the worthy stood to the right and kinsmen to the left—they were the firm pillars of the state. That is what the Duke of Piling represents." When Crown Prince Huang died in the Eastern Palace, Jin served concurrently as Minister of Education and received the edict to assign the posthumous title. He was sent out as General Who Guards the South and Inspector of Jizhou. Plain in living and unadorned in manner, he devoted himself tirelessly to the throne's business and won a celebrated name in his own time. He returned to serve as Grand Officer of the Inner Capital. At the beginning of the Xingguang era, Jin's son-in-law Sima Mituo of the Prince of Yulin's household was chosen to marry Princess Linjing. Jin coached Mituo to decline on a pretext, but words of slander and cursing were found, and Jin was executed together with him. Jin had four sons. Bing, Chi, and Yi were all students of the Secretariat and were executed together with their father. Only the youngest son, Zun, escaped by fleeing into hiding.
3
遵善楷篆，北京諸碑及臺殿樓觀、宮門題署，多遵書也。 官至尚書郎、濮陽太守，多所受納。 其子僧演，姦通民婦，為民賈邈所告，免官。 後以善書，拜庫部令，卒官。
Zun excelled in regular and seal script, and most of the inscriptions on steles in the northern capital and on palace terraces, halls, towers, and gates were written by him. He rose to Secretariat Gentleman and Administrator of Puyang, where he accepted many bribes. His son Sengyan had an affair with a commoner's wife and was reported by a man named Jia Miao; he was removed from office. Later, on account of his calligraphy, he was appointed Chief of the Storehouse Section and died in that post.
4
許彥，字道謨，小字嘉屯，高陽新城人也。 祖茂，慕容氏高陽太守。 彥少孤貧，好讀書，後從沙門法叡受易。 世祖初，被徵，以卜筮頻驗，遂在左右，參與謀議。 拜散騎常侍，賜爵博陵侯。 彥質厚慎密，與人言不及內事。 世祖以此益親待之。 進爵武昌公，拜安東將軍、相州刺史。 在州受納，多違法度，詔書切讓之。 然以彥腹心近臣，弗之罪也。 真君二年，卒。 諡曰宣公。
Xu Yan, whose courtesy name was Daomo and childhood name Jiatun, came from Xincheng in Gaoyang Commandery. His grandfather Mao had served the Murong as Administrator of Gaoyang. Yan lost his parents early and grew up in poverty. He loved books and later studied the Book of Changes under the monk Farui. Early in Emperor Taiwu's reign he was summoned to court. His divinations proved accurate again and again, so he stayed at the emperor's side and joined in policy discussions. He was made Regular Attendant of the Scattered Cavalry and enfeoffed as Marquis of Boling. Yan was steady, cautious, and discreet, and never spoke of inner-court matters in conversation. Emperor Taiwu therefore treated him with even greater personal favor. He was raised to Duke of Wuchang and appointed General Who Establishes the East and Inspector of Xiangzhou. In his province he took bribes and often broke the law, and an imperial edict sharply rebuked him. But because Yan was a trusted intimate, he went unpunished. He died in the second year of Zhenjun. He was given the posthumous title Duke Xuan.
5
子宗之，初入為中散，領內祕書。 世祖臨江，賜爵高鄉侯。 高宗踐阼，遷殿中尚書，出為鎮東將軍、定州刺史，潁川公。 受敕討丁零，丁零既平，宗之因循郡縣，求取不節。 深澤人馬超毀謗宗之，宗之怒，遂毆殺超。 懼超家人告狀，上超謗訕朝政。 高宗聞之，曰：「此必妄也。 朕為天下主，何惡於超，而超有此言。 必是宗之懼罪誣超。 」按驗果然。 事下有司，司空伊馛等以宗之腹心近臣，出居方伯，不能宣揚本朝，盡心綏導，而侵損齊民，枉殺良善，妄列無辜，上塵朝廷，誣詐不道，理合極刑。 太安二年冬，遂斬於都南。
His son Zongzhi first entered service as Palace Scribe and headed the Inner Secretariat. When Emperor Taiwu reached the Yangzi, Zongzhi was enfeoffed as Marquis of Gaoxiang. When Emperor Wencheng ascended the throne, Zongzhi was promoted to Director of the Palace Secretariat, then sent out as General Who Guards the East and Inspector of Dingzhou and enfeoffed as Duke of Yingchuan. Ordered to campaign against the Dingling, he lingered in the commanderies and counties after their defeat and extorted goods without restraint. A man of Shenze named Ma Chao slandered Zongzhi, who in anger beat him to death. Fearing that Chao's family would bring charges, he reported that Chao had slandered the court and government. When Emperor Wencheng heard of it, he said, "This must be false." I am lord of all under Heaven. What wrong have I done Chao that he should speak this way? Zongzhi must have feared punishment and falsely accused Chao. Investigation proved that this was exactly what had happened. The case went to the relevant offices. Minister of Works Yi Ba and others argued that Zongzhi, though a trusted intimate, had been sent out as a regional commander yet failed to proclaim the dynasty's policies or devote himself to governing the people. Instead he harmed commoners, killed the innocent, and falsely implicated the guiltless, bringing disgrace on the court—deceitful and immoral conduct deserving the utmost penalty. In the winter of the second year of Tai'an he was beheaded south of the capital.
6
宗之孫亮，字元規。 正光中，盪寇將軍，稍遷冀州驃騎長史、司徒諮議參軍。 年五十二，卒。
Zongzhi's grandson Liang, whose courtesy name was Yuangui. During the Zhenguang era he served as General Who Quells Bandits and was gradually promoted to Chief Clerk of the Rapid Cavalry in Jizhou and Consulting Aide to the Minister of Education. He died at the age of fifty-two.
7
宗之長兄熙，字德融，襲爵武昌公。 中書郎，早卒。
Zongzhi's elder brother Xi, whose courtesy name was Derong, inherited the title of Duke of Wuchang. He served as Secretariat Gentleman and died young.
8
子安仁，襲。 除中書郎。 卒，贈安東將軍、冀州刺史，諡曰簡。
His son Anren inherited the title. He was appointed Secretariat Gentleman. When he died he was posthumously made General Who Establishes the East and Inspector of Jizhou, with the posthumous title Jian.
9
子元康，襲爵，後降為侯。 拜冠軍將軍、長安鎮副將。 遷監河州諸軍事、河州刺史，將軍如故。 入為廷尉少卿。 除魏郡太守，固辭不拜。 尋卒，贈征虜將軍、營州刺史，諡曰肅。
His son Yuankang inherited the title, which was later reduced from duke to marquis. He was appointed General Who Establishes Victory and Deputy Garrison Commander of Chang'an. He was transferred to oversee military affairs in Hezhou and made Inspector of Hezhou, while retaining his rank as general. He returned to the capital as Vice Director of the Court of Justice. He was offered the post of Administrator of Weijun but firmly declined. He soon died and was posthumously made General Who Campaigns Against the Barbarians and Inspector of Yingzhou, with the posthumous title Su.
10
子廓，字崇遠，襲爵。 除奉朝請，累遷頓丘、東太原二郡太守。 卒，年二十八。 子子躬襲。
His son Kuo, whose courtesy name was Chongyuan, inherited the title. He was appointed Court Gentleman for the Imperial Audience and rose in succession to Administrator of Dunqiu and of Eastern Taiyuan. He died at the age of twenty-eight. His son Zigong inherited the title.
11
子躬，武定末，中外府水曹參軍。 齊受禪，爵例降。
At the end of the Wuding era, Zigong served as Aide in the Water Bureau of the Central and Outer Offices. When Qi received the abdication, his noble rank was reduced according to precedent.
12
子躬弟子憲，太尉中兵參軍。
Zigong's nephew Xian served as Aide in the Central Army Bureau of the Grand Commandant.
13
元康弟護，州主簿。
Yuankang's younger brother Hu served as chief clerk of a province.
14
子瑞，字徵之，亦州主簿。 卒。
His son Rui, whose courtesy name was Zhengzhi, also served as a provincial chief clerk. He died.
15
瑞弟絢，字伯禮，頗有業尚。 閨門雍睦，三世同居。 吏部尚書李神儁常稱其家風。 自侍御史累遷尚書左民郎、司徒諮議參軍，修起居注。 後拜太中大夫。 興和初卒，年四十七。 贈使持節、都督冀瀛二州諸軍事、征東將軍、吏部尚書、冀州刺史。
Rui's younger brother Xuan, whose courtesy name was Boli, had considerable scholarly attainment. His household was harmonious, with three generations living under one roof. Director of the Ministry of Personnel Li Shenjun often praised the family's conduct. Starting as Attending Censor, he rose in succession to Left Gentleman of the Populace in the Ministry of Works, Consulting Aide to the Minister of Education, and compiler of the Imperial Diary. He was later appointed Grand Master of the Palace. He died at the beginning of the Xinghe era, at the age of forty-seven. He was posthumously invested with the Staff of Authority, made Commander-in-Chief of military affairs in Ji and Ying, General Who Campaigns East, Director of the Ministry of Personnel, and Inspector of Jizhou.
16
絢弟遜，武定末，東陽平太守。
Xuan's younger brother Xun, at the end of the Wuding era, was Administrator of Eastern Yangping.
17
遜弟曄，字叔明，性開率。 州治中、別駕、西高陽太守、太中大夫。 興和三年卒，年四十一。 贈鎮東將軍、瀛州刺史。
Xun's younger brother Ye, whose courtesy name was Shuming, was open and forthright by nature. He served as provincial aide, Administrator-in-Chief, Administrator of Western Gaoyang, and Grand Master of the Palace. He died in the third year of Xinghe, at the age of forty-one. He was posthumously made General Who Guards the East and Inspector of Yingzhou.
18
曄弟惇，字季良。 武定末，兼大司農卿。
Ye's younger brother Dun, whose courtesy name was Jiliang. At the end of the Wuding era he served concurrently as Minister of the Grand Granary.
19
熙弟龍，官至趙郡太守。
Xi's younger brother Long rose to Administrator of Zhao Commandery.
20
孫琰，字長琳，有幹用。 初除太學博士，累遷尚書南主客郎、瀛州中正。 孝昌中卒，年四十七。 贈平東將軍、滄州刺史。 永熙中，重贈散騎常侍、衞將軍、尚書右僕射、瀛州刺史。
Sun Yan, whose courtesy name was Changlin, was capable and effective. He was first appointed Doctor of the Imperial Academy and rose in succession to Southern Gentleman of Guest Affairs in the Ministry of Works and Rectifier of Yingzhou. He died during the Xiaochang era, at the age of forty-seven. He was posthumously made General Who Pacifies the East and Inspector of Cangzhou. During the Yongxi era he received a second posthumous promotion to Regular Attendant of the Scattered Cavalry, General of the Guard, Right Vice Director of the Ministry of Works, and Inspector of Yingzhou.
21
琰弟璣，字仲衡，有識尚。 廣平王常侍、員外散騎侍郎、諫議大夫。 遷通直散騎常侍、瀛州大中正、散騎常侍、滎陽太守、行南青州事。 卒，年五十五。 琰兄弟並通率，多與勝流交遊。
Yan's younger brother Ji, whose courtesy name was Zhongheng, was a man of discernment and learning. He served as Regular Attendant to the Prince of Guangping, Extraordinary Gentleman of the Scattered Cavalry, and Remonstrating and Consulting Grand Master. He was promoted to Direct and Upright Regular Attendant of the Scattered Cavalry, Senior Rectifier of Yingzhou, Regular Attendant of the Scattered Cavalry, Administrator of Xingyang, and Acting Administrator of Southern Qingzhou. He died at the age of fifty-five. Yan and his brothers were all open and forthright, and they moved in the circles of the day's leading men.
22
又有博陵許赤虎，涉獵經史，善嘲謔。 延興中，著作佐郎，與慕容白曜南討。 後使江南，應對敏捷，雖言不典故，而南人頗稱機辯滑稽焉。 使還，為東郡太守，卒官。
There was also Xu Chihu of Boling, widely read in the classics and histories and skilled at witty banter. During the Yanxing era he served as Assistant Gentleman of Composition and joined Murong Baiyao on the southern campaign. Later he was sent south of the Yangzi. His replies were quick, and though his speech was not laden with allusion, southerners praised him as clever, eloquent, and witty. After returning from his mission he became Administrator of Dong Commandery and died in that post.
23
子陀，定州長史。
His son Tuo served as Chief Clerk of Dingzhou.
24
李訢，字元盛，小名真奴，范陽人也。 曾袓產，產子績，二世知名於慕容氏。 父崇，馮跋吏部尚書、石城太守。 延和初，車駕至和龍，崇率十餘郡歸降。 世袓甚禮之，呼曰「李公」，以崇為平西將軍、北幽州刺史、固安侯。 卒，年八十一，諡曰襄侯。
Li Xin, whose courtesy name was Yuansheng and childhood name Zhennu, came from Fanyang. His great-grandfather Chan and Chan's son Ji had both been renowned under the Murong for two generations. His father Chong had served Feng Ba as Director of the Ministry of Personnel and Administrator of Shicheng. At the beginning of the Yanhe era, when the imperial carriage reached Helong, Chong led more than ten commanderies in surrender. Emperor Taiwu treated him with great courtesy, addressed him as "Lord Li," and appointed him General Who Pacifies the West, Inspector of Northern Youzhou, and Marquis of Guo'an. He died at the age of eighty-one and was given the posthumous title Marquis Xiang.
25
訢母賤，為諸兄所輕。 崇曰：「此子之生，相者言貴，吾每觀察，或未可知。 」遂使入都，為中書學生。 世袓幸中書學，見而異之，指謂從者曰：「此小兒終效用於朕之子孫矣。 」因識眄之。 世袓舅陽平王杜超有女，將許貴戚。 世袓聞之，謂超曰：「李訢後必宦達，益人門戶，可以女妻之，勿許他貴也。 」遂勸成婚。 南人李哲嘗言訢必當貴達。 杜超之死也，世袓親哭三日。 訢以超壻，得在喪位出入。 帝目而指之，謂左右曰：「觀此人舉動，豈不有異於眾？ 必為朕家幹事之臣。 」訢聰敏機辯，強記明察。 初，李靈為高宗博士、諮議，[1]詔崔浩選中書學生器業優者為助教。 浩舉其弟子箱子與盧度世、李敷三人應之。 給事高讜子祐、尚書段霸兒姪等以為浩阿其親戚，言於恭宗。 恭宗以浩為不平，聞之於世袓。 世袓意在於訢，曰：「云何不取幽州刺史李崇老翁兒也？ 」浩對曰：「前亦言訢合選，但以其先行在外，故不取之。 」世袓曰：「可待訢還，箱子等罷之。 」訢為世袓所識如此。 遂除中書助教博士，稍見任用，入授高宗經。
Xin's mother was of humble origin, and his elder brothers looked down on him. Chong said, "When this child was born, the physiognomist said he would rise high. Each time I watch him, I think that may yet prove true." He therefore sent him to the capital as a student of the Secretariat. When Emperor Taiwu visited the Secretariat school and saw him, he was struck by the boy and said to his attendants, "This child will one day prove useful to my descendants." From then on he took special notice of him. Emperor Taiwu's uncle by marriage, the Prince of Yangping Du Chao, had a daughter who was to be betrothed to a noble house. When Emperor Taiwu heard of this, he told Chao, "Li Xin is sure to rise high in office and will bring honor to any house that takes him in. Give him your daughter; do not betroth her elsewhere among the nobility." He therefore urged that the marriage go forward. A southerner named Li Zhe had once said that Xin was destined for high office. When Du Chao died, Emperor Taiwu personally mourned for three days. As Chao's son-in-law, Xin was allowed to come and go at the mourning site. The emperor fixed his gaze on him and said to those beside him, "Look at this man's bearing. Does it not differ from the crowd?" He is sure to become a minister who will accomplish great things for our house. Xin was clever, quick in debate, with a strong memory and keen perception. Earlier, Li Ling had served Emperor Wencheng as Doctor and Consulting Aide. [1] An edict ordered Cui Hao to select the most accomplished students of the Secretariat as assistant teachers. Hao nominated his disciple Xiangzi together with Lu Dushi and Li Fu. Gao Dan's son You, Duan Ba's nephew, and others held that Hao was favoring his relatives and reported this to Crown Prince Huang. Crown Prince Huang considered Hao unfair and reported it to Emperor Taiwu. Emperor Taiwu had his eye on Xin and said, "Why not take the son of old Li Chong, the Inspector of Youzhou?" Hao replied, "I had also said Xin was suitable, but because he was still away on assignment, I did not select him." Emperor Taiwu said, "We can wait until Xin returns and dismiss Xiangzi and the others." This was how highly Emperor Taiwu regarded Xin. He was appointed Assistant Teacher and Doctor of the Secretariat, gradually won employment, and entered the palace to instruct Crown Prince Huang in the classics.
26
訢上疏求立學校曰：「臣聞至治之隆，非文德無以經綸王道； 太平之美，非良才無以光贊皇化。 是以昔之明主，建庠序於京畿，立學官於郡邑，教國子弟，習其道藝。 然後選其俊異，以為造士。 今聖治欽明，道隆三五，九服之民，咸仰德化，而所在州土，學校未立。 臣雖不敏，誠願備之，使後生聞雅頌之音，童幼覩經教之本。 臣昔蒙恩寵，長管中祕，時課修學有成立之人，髦俊之士，已蒙進用。 臣今重荷榮遇，顯任方岳，思闡帝猷，光宣於外。 自到以來，訪諸文學，舊德已老，後生未進。 歲首所貢，雖依制遣，對問之日，懼不克堪。 臣愚欲仰依先典，於州郡治所各立學官。 使士望之流、冠冕之胄，就而受業，庶必有成。 其經藝通明者貢之王府。 則郁郁之文，於是不墜。 」書奏，顯袓從之。
Li Xin submitted a memorial requesting the establishment of schools, saying, "I have heard that the height of perfect governance cannot weave the kingly way without literary virtue; the beauty of great peace cannot glorify and assist imperial transformation without fine talent." Therefore the enlightened rulers of old built academies in the capital region and established school officials in commanderies and districts, teaching the sons of the state and having them practice the arts of the Way. Only afterward did they select the outstanding and make them accomplished scholars. Now the sage reign is illustrious and bright, the Way lofty as that of the Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors, and the people of the nine domains all look up to its transforming virtue, yet in the provinces and districts schools have not been established. Though I am not clever, I sincerely wish to supply them, so that later generations may hear the tones of the Odes and Hymns and children may behold the roots of classical teaching. I once received gracious favor and long managed the Inner Secretariat; at that time those who studied and achieved completion, and eminent talents, were already advanced and employed. I now again bear heavy honor, hold a prominent post in a frontier province, and wish to expound the emperor's designs and spread their light abroad. Since arriving, I have inquired among literary men: the old worthies have aged, and the younger generation has not advanced. Those presented at the year's beginning, though sent according to regulation, may on the day of examination prove unable to bear questioning. I foolishly wish to follow the former canon and establish school officials at the seat of government in each province and commandery. Let men of scholarly repute and heirs of eminent houses come and receive instruction; perhaps there will be achievement. Those whose mastery of the classics is penetrating should be presented to the imperial court. Then the flourishing of culture will not fall away." When the memorial was submitted, Emperor Xianwen approved it.
27
以訢治為諸州之最，加賜衣服。 自是遂有驕矜自得之志。 乃受納民財及商胡珍寶。 兵民告言，尚書李敷與訢少長相好，每左右之。 或有勸以奏聞，敷不許。 顯袓聞訢罪狀，檻車徵訢，拷劾抵罪。 時敷兄弟將見疏斥，有司諷訢以中旨嫌敷兄弟之意，令訢告列敷等隱罪，可得自全。 訢深所不欲，且弗之知也。 乃謂其女壻裴攸曰：「吾宗與李敷族世雖遠，情如一家。 在事既有此勸，竟如何也？ 昨來每欲為此取死，引簪自刺，以帶自絞，而不能致絕。 且亦不知其事。 」攸曰：「何為為他死也？ 敷兄弟事釁可知。 有馮闡者，先為敷所敗，其家切恨之，但呼闡弟問之，足知委曲。 」訢從其言。 又趙郡范𢷋具條列敷兄弟事狀，有司以聞。 敷坐得罪。 詔列訢貪冒，罪應死。 以糾李敷兄弟，故得降免，百鞭髠刑，配為厮役。
Because Xin's governance was the best among all the provinces, he was given an additional grant of clothing. From this time he developed an ambition of pride and self-satisfaction. He then accepted bribes from the people and precious objects from merchant foreigners. Soldiers and commoners reported this; Minister of Works Li Fu had been friendly with Xin since youth and always supported him. Some urged that it be reported to the throne, but Fu would not allow it. When Emperor Xianwen heard of Xin's crimes, he summoned him in a prisoner's cart and interrogated him under torture until guilt was established. At that time the Fu brothers were about to be cast aside; the relevant offices hinted to Xin that the central intent was displeasure with the Fu brothers and urged him to denounce Fu and the others for hidden crimes so that he might save himself. Xin deeply did not wish this and moreover did not know the matter. He therefore said to his son-in-law Pei You, "Our clan and the Li Fu clan, though distant in generation, are as close as one family. Now that in this affair there is such urging, what is finally to be done?" Yesterday and today I several times wished to die for this, drawing a hairpin to stab myself and a belt to strangle myself, yet could not bring death about. Moreover I do not know the affair." You said, "Why die for another?" The affair of the Fu brothers can be known from the signs. There is Feng Chan, whom Fu earlier defeated; his family hates him bitterly. Simply call Chan's younger brother and question him, and the full details will be known." Xin followed his advice. Also Fan Xian of Zhao Commandery fully listed the circumstances of the Fu brothers, and the relevant offices reported it. Fu was convicted on the charge. An edict listed Xin's greed and presumption, for which the penalty should be death. Because he had exposed the Fu brothers, he was granted reduction and exemption: one hundred strokes and shaving of the head, and assignment as a menial servant.
28
訢之廢也，平壽侯張讜見訢，與語奇之，謂人曰：「此佳士也，終不久屈。 」未幾而復為太倉尚書，攝南部事。 用范𢷋、陳端等計，令千里之外，戶別轉運，詣倉輸之。 所在委滯，停延歲月，百姓競以貨賂各求在前，於是遠近大為困弊。 道路羣議曰：「畜聚斂之臣，未若盜臣。 」訢弟左將軍璞謂訢曰：「范𢷋善能降人以色，假人以辭，未聞德義之言，但有勢利之說。 聽其言也甘，察其行也賊，所謂諂諛、讒慝、貪冒、姦佞，不早絕之，後悔無及。 」訢不從，彌信之，腹心之事，皆以告𢷋。
When Xin was disgraced, Marquis of Pingshou Zhang Tan saw Xin and, speaking with him, was struck by him; he told others, "This is a fine man and will not long remain humbled." Before long he was again made Director of the Grand Granary and acted in charge of southern affairs. Using the plans of Fan Xian, Chen Duan, and others, he ordered that beyond a thousand li each household transport separately and deliver grain to the granaries. Wherever they arrived goods piled up stagnant, delays stretched over months, and the people competed in bribes to seek priority; thus near and far were greatly distressed. The people along the roads said in groups, "To keep a minister who amasses wealth is not as good as a thief." Xin's younger brother, Left General Pu, said to Xin, "Fan Xian is good at winning people with pleasant looks and lending them fine words; one never hears talk of virtue and righteousness, only talk of power and profit. To hear his words is sweet; to examine his conduct is treacherous—what is called flattery, slander, greed, and wickedness. If you do not cut him off early, later there will be no catching up." Xin did not follow this but trusted him all the more and told Xian everything that was close to his heart.
29
訢既寵於顯袓，參決軍國大議，兼典選舉，權傾內外，百僚莫不曲節以事之。 𢷋以無功，起家拜慮奴令。 延興末，詔曰：「尚書李訢著勳先朝，弼諧皇極，讜言嘉謀，旬日屢進，實國家之楨幹，當今之老成也。 是以擢授南部，綜理煩務。 自在厥位，夙夜惟夤，乃心匪懈，克己復禮，退食自公，利上之事，知無不為，賞罰所加，不避疏戚。 雖孝子之思慈母，鷹鸇之逐鳥雀，何以方之。 若鄭之子產，魯之季文亦未加也。 然惡直醜正，盜憎主人。 自往年以來，羣姦不息，劫訢宗人李英等四家，焚燒舍宅，傷害良善。 此而可忍，孰不可恕！ 有司可明加購募，必令擒殄。」
Once Xin was favored by Emperor Xianwen, he took part in deciding great state and military plans, also managed selections for office, and his power tilted inside and outside; no official failed to bend the knee in serving him. Xian, though without merit, was appointed by family standing as Magistrate of Lunu. At the end of Yanxing an edict said, "Minister of Works Li Xin established merit in the former court, harmonized the imperial pole, offered loyal words and fine plans that several times in ten days were advanced—truly a pillar of the state and a seasoned elder of the present age. Therefore he was promoted to the southern department to oversee troublesome affairs. Since taking that post he has been diligent from dawn to dusk, his heart never slackening, restraining himself and returning to ritual, leaving office and taking plain food, and in matters that benefit the throne knowing nothing he will not do; in rewards and punishments he does not avoid distant kin. Even a filial son's longing for a loving mother, or hawk and falcon pursuing sparrows, cannot be compared to this. Even Zichan of Zheng or Jiwen of Lu would not surpass him. Yet those who hate the upright and detest the true are like thieves who hate their master. Since last year bandits have not ceased; they robbed four households of Xin's clansman Li Ying and others, burned their dwellings, and harmed the good. If this can be borne, what cannot be forgiven! The relevant offices should clearly offer rewards for capture and must bring them to extinction."
30
六月，顯袓崩。 訢遷司空，進爵范陽公。 七月，以訢為侍中、鎮南大將軍、開府儀同三司、徐州刺史。 范𢷋知文明太后忿訢也，又知內外疾之。 太和元年二月，希旨告訢外叛。 文明太后徵訢至京師，言其叛狀，訢曰無之。 引𢷋證訢，訢言：「爾妄云知我，吾又何言！ 雖然，爾不顧余之厚德而忍為此，不仁甚矣。 」𢷋曰：「公德於𢷋，何若李敷之德於公？ 公昔忍於敷，𢷋今敢不忍於公乎？ 」訢慨然曰：「吾不用璞言，自貽伊戚，萬悔於心，何嗟及矣！ 」遂見誅。 訢有三子。
In the sixth month Emperor Xianwen died. Xin was transferred to Minister of Works and advanced to Duke of Fanyang. In the seventh month Xin was made Palace Attendant, General Who Guards the South, Holder of the Grand Cavalry Office with credentials equal to the Three Excellencies, and Inspector of Xuzhou. Fan Xian knew that Empress Dowager Feng was angry with Xin and also knew that inside and outside the court resented him. In the second month of the first year of Taihe, to suit the intent he reported that Xin was plotting rebellion abroad. Empress Dowager Feng summoned Xin to the capital and stated the charge of rebellion; Xin said there was none. Xian was brought in to testify against Xin; Xin said, "You falsely claim to know me—what more can I say!" "Yet you, disregarding the deep kindness I showed you, could bear to do this—how utterly lacking in humaneness." Xian said, "How does your grace to Xian compare with Li Fu's grace to you?" "You once bore with Fu; how dare Xian today fail to bear with you?" Xin sighed and said, "I did not heed Pu's words and brought this sorrow on myself; ten thousand regrets are in my heart—what use is lament now!" He was then put to death. Li Xin had three sons.
31
長子邃，起家拜侍御中散、東宮門大夫。 遷散騎常侍，加平東將軍。 先訢卒。
The eldest son Sui, on first entering service, was appointed Attending Scribe of the Palace Guard and Gate Officer of the Eastern Palace. He was promoted to Regular Attendant of the Scattered Cavalry and given the additional rank of General Who Pacifies the East. He predeceased his father.
32
子晴，字誨明。 逃竄，遇赦免。
His son Qing, whose courtesy name was Huiming. He went into hiding and was spared when an amnesty was declared.
33
晴子衡，字伯琳。 武定中，中堅將軍、齊獻武王丞相府水曹參軍。
Qing's son Heng, whose courtesy name was Bolin. During the Wuding era he served as General of Firm Resolve and Water Bureau Aide in the Chancellor's Office of Prince Xian of Qi.
34
邃弟令和、令度，與訢同時死。
Sui's younger brothers Linghe and Lingdu died together with Xin.
35
訢長兄恭，字元順。 成周太守。 卒，贈幽州刺史、容成侯，諡曰簡侯。
Xin's elder brother Gong, whose courtesy name was Yuanshun. He served as Administrator of Chengzou. When he died he was posthumously made Inspector of Youzhou and Marquis of Rongcheng, with the posthumous title Marquis Jian.
36
恭弟瓘，字元衡。 營丘太守，襲父爵固安侯，平西將軍。 卒，贈兗州刺史，諡曰康侯。
Gong's younger brother Guan, whose courtesy name was Yuanheng. He was Administrator of Yingqiu, inherited his father's title as Marquis of Guo'an, and held the rank of General Who Pacifies the West. When he died he was posthumously made Inspector of Yanzhou, with the posthumous title Marquis Kang.
37
子長生，襲。 長生卒，子元宗襲。 廣平郡丞，陳郡太守。
His son Changsheng inherited the title. When Changsheng died, his son Yuanzong inherited. He served as Assistant in Guangping Commandery and Administrator of Chen Commandery.
38
璞字季真，性惇厚，多識人物。 歷中書博士、侍郎、漁陽王尉眷傅、左將軍、長安副將，賜爵宜陽侯，太常卿。 承明元年，年五十一，先訢卒。 贈安西將軍、雍州刺史，諡曰穆。
Pu, whose courtesy name was Jizhen, was sincere and steady by nature and knew many men of ability. He served in succession as Doctor of the Secretariat, Gentleman, Tutor to the Prince of Fuyang Wei Juan, Left General, Deputy Garrison Commander of Chang'an, was enfeoffed as Marquis of Yiyang, and became Minister of Ceremonies. In Chengming year 1, at fifty-one, he died before his father. He was posthumously made General Who Pacifies the West and Inspector of Yongzhou, with the posthumous title Mu.
39
子暉，中書議郎。
His son Hui served as Consulting Gentleman of the Secretariat.
40
暉弟固，太學博士、高密太守。
Hui's younger brother Gu was Doctor of the Imperial Academy and Administrator of Gaomi.
41
固弟欽，州主簿。
Gu's younger brother Qin served as a provincial chief clerk.
42
欽子奭，字元熾。 武定末，鎮西將軍、南營州別駕。
Qin's son Yi, whose courtesy name was Yuanchi. At the end of the Wuding era he served as General Who Guards the West and Administrator-in-Chief of Southern Yingzhou.
43
奭弟盛，字仲炎。 安東將軍、開府諮議參軍。
Yi's younger brother Sheng, whose courtesy name was Zhongyan. He served as General Who Establishes the East and as a Consulting Aide with credentials equal to an office of state.
44
盛弟叔樊，平西將軍、太中大夫。
Sheng's younger brother Shufan was General Who Pacifies the West and Grand Master of the Palace.
45
欽弟蘊，字宗令，有器幹。 中書學生、祕書中散、侍御中散。 出為燕郡、范陽二郡太守。 入為員外散騎常侍、尚書右丞、中堅將軍，遷左丞。 延昌三年卒，贈平遠將軍、南青州刺史，諡曰敬。
Qin's younger brother Yun, whose courtesy name was Zongling, had capacity and ability. He was a student of the Secretariat, Secretariat Scribe, and Attending Scribe of the Palace Guard. He was sent out as Administrator of Yan and Fanyang commanderies. He entered service as Extraordinary Gentleman of the Scattered Cavalry, Right Vice Director of the Ministry of Works, and General of Firm Resolve, then was transferred to Left Vice Director. He died in the third year of Yanzhang and was posthumously made General Who Pacifies the Distant and Inspector of Southern Qingzhou, with the posthumous title Jing.
46
初，崇之歸魏也，與州里北平田彪俱降，而彪子孫遂微劣焉。
When Chong first submitted to Wei, he surrendered with Tian Biao of Pingbei; yet afterward Biao's line remained obscure and lowly.
47
史臣曰：魏氏之有天下，百餘年中，任刑為治，蹉跌之間，便至夷滅。 竇瑾、李訢器識既美，時曰良幹。 瑾以片言疑似，訢以夙故猜嫌，而嬰合門之戮，悲夫！ 宗之不全，自貽伊戚矣。
The historiographer writes: For the hundred-odd years that Wei held the realm, it ruled through punishment; in one misstep it nearly perished. Dou Jin and Li Xin both had fine talent and judgment and were called the day's able pillars. Jin fell on a few words suspected, and Xin on long-standing suspicion—both suffered the slaughter of the whole house. How lamentable! As for Zongzhi, he failed to preserve himself and brought disaster on his own head.

49
為高宗博士諮議北史卷二七李訢傳無「諮議」二字。 按卷四九李靈傳云：「拜中書博士，選授高宗經，」亦不云為「諮議」。 二字疑衍。
For Doctor of Emperor Wencheng, the Li Xin biography in the History of the Northern Dynasties, fascicle 27, lacks the two characters "Consulting Aide." According to the Li Ling biography in fascicle 49, it says, "He was appointed Doctor of the Secretariat and selected to instruct Crown Prince Huang in the classics," and likewise does not say "Consulting Aide." The two characters are probably a spurious addition.
