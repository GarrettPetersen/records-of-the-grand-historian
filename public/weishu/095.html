1
于洛侯胡泥李洪之高遵張赦提羊祉崔暹酈道元谷楷
Yu Luohou, Hu Ni, Li Hongzhi, Gao Zun, Zhang Sheti, Yang Zhi, Cui Xian, Li Daoyuan, and Gu Kai
2
淳風既喪，姦黠萌生； 法令滋章，刑禁多設。 為吏罕仁恕之誠，當官以威猛為濟。 魏氏以戎馬定王業，武功平海內，治任刑罰，肅厲為本，猛酷之倫，所以列之今史。
Once honest ways had decayed, deceit and cunning took root; Statutes multiplied, and penal restrictions were imposed everywhere. Officials seldom showed genuine mercy; in office they relied on brute force to get things done. The Wei house won its throne by the sword and pacified the realm by force; their rule rested on penal law and stern discipline, which is why these men of savage cruelty are recorded in this history.
3
于洛侯，代人也。 以勞舊為秦州刺史，而貪酷安忍。 州人富熾奪民呂勝脛纏一具，洛侯輒鞭富熾一百，截其右腕。 百姓王隴客刺殺民王羌奴、王愈二人，依律罪死而已，洛侯生拔隴客舌，刺其本，并刺胸腹二十餘瘡。 隴客不堪苦痛，隨刀戰動。 乃立四柱磔其手足，命將絕，始斬其首，支解四體，分懸道路。 見之者無不傷楚，闔州驚震，人懷怨憤。 百姓王元壽等一時反叛。 有司糾劾。 高祖詔使者於州刑人處宣告兵民，然後斬洛侯以謝百姓。
Yu Luohou was from Dai. Rewarded for long service with the governorship of Qin Province, he proved greedy, brutal, and utterly pitiless. When a local named Fu Chi seized a pair of greaves from a commoner named Lü Sheng, Luohou had Fu Chi flogged a hundred times and his right wrist cut off. A commoner named Wang Longke had killed Wang Qiangnu and Wang Yu—an offense that by law deserved only execution. Luohou tore out Longke's tongue while he was still alive, stabbed the stump, and drove more than twenty blades into his chest and belly. Longke could not endure the pain and convulsed with every stroke of the blade. He then erected four posts and dismembered Longke's limbs upon them; only when life was almost gone did he cut off his head, quarter the body, and hang the pieces along the roads. Every witness was appalled; the whole province was shaken, and the people seethed with outrage. Wang Yuanshou and other commoners rose in rebellion at once. The authorities impeached him. Emperor Gaozu sent an envoy to announce the sentence to soldiers and civilians at the execution ground, then had Luohou beheaded to answer the people's grievances.
4
胡泥，代人也。 歷官至司衞監，賜爵永城侯。 泥率勒禁中，不憚豪貴。 殿中尚書叔孫侯頭應內直而闕於一時，泥以法繩之。 侯頭恃寵，遂與口諍。 高祖聞而嘉焉，賜泥衣服一襲。 出為幽州刺史，假范陽公。 以北平陽尼碩學，遂表薦之。 遷平東將軍、定州刺史。 以暴虐，刑罰酷濫，受納貨賄，徵還戮之。 將就法也，高祖臨太華殿引見，遣侍臣宣詔責之，遂就家賜自盡。
Hu Ni was from Dai. He rose to Director of the Palace Guards and was enfeoffed as Marquis of Yongcheng. Ni kept order inside the palace precincts and did not shrink from the great and powerful. When Palace Attendant Shusun Hutou failed to report for his scheduled inner-palace duty, Ni punished him according to law. Hutou, trusting in imperial favor, argued back to his face. Emperor Gaozu heard of this and commended him, bestowing a full suit of robes. He was appointed governor of You Province with the provisional title Duke of Fanyang. Finding Yang Ni of Beiping a distinguished scholar, he memorialized the throne to recommend him. He was promoted to General Who Pacifies the East and governor of Ding Province. For his brutality, his wanton use of torture, and his acceptance of bribes, he was recalled to the capital and put to death. As he was about to be executed, Emperor Gaozu received him in Taihua Hall, had a courtier read out an edict of rebuke, and then allowed him to take his own life at home.
5
李洪之，本名文通，恒農人。 少為沙門，晚乃還俗。 真君中，為狄道護軍，賜爵安陽男。 會永昌王仁隨世祖南征，得元后姊妹二人。 洪之以宗人潛相餉遺，結為兄弟，遂便如親。 頗得元后在南兄弟名字，乃改名洪之。 及仁坐事誅，元后入宮，得幸於高宗，生顯祖。 元后臨崩，昭太后問其親，因言洪之為兄。 與相訣經日，具條列南方諸兄珍之等，手以付洪之。 遂號為顯祖親舅。 太安中，珍之等兄弟至都，與洪之相見，敍元后平生故事，計長幼為昆季。
Li Hongzhi, whose original name was Wentong, was from Hengnong. In his youth he was a Buddhist monk; only later did he return to secular life. During the Zhenjun reign he served as protector-general of Didao and was enfeoffed as Baron of Anyang. When Prince Ren of Yongchang accompanied Emperor Shizu on the southern campaign, he captured two sisters of the future Yuan empress. Hongzhi, claiming kinship, secretly sent them gifts; they swore brotherhood and soon treated one another like family. Learning the names of the Yuan empress's brothers in the south, he changed his own name to Hongzhi. When Ren was executed for his crimes, the Yuan empress entered the palace, won Emperor Gaozong's favor, and bore Emperor Xianzu. On her deathbed the Yuan empress told Empress Dowager Zhao that Hongzhi was her brother. They spent days in farewell; she wrote out a full list of her brothers in the south, including Zhenzhi, and handed it to Hongzhi herself. He was thereafter known as Emperor Xianzu's maternal uncle. During the Tai'an era, Zhenzhi and his brothers reached the capital, met Hongzhi, recounted the Yuan empress's life, and arranged themselves as brothers by age.
6
以外戚為河內太守，進爵任城侯，威儀一同刺史。 河內北連上黨，南接武牢，地險人悍，數為劫害，長吏不能禁。 洪之至郡，嚴設科防，募斬賊者便加重賞，勸農務本，盜賊止息。 誅鋤姦黨，過為酷虐。
As a maternal kinsman he was appointed administrator of Henei and advanced to Marquis of Rencheng, with ceremonial honors equal to a provincial governor. Henei lay between Shangdang to the north and Wulao to the south; the land was rugged and the people fierce, and banditry was constant—local officials could not suppress it. When Hongzhi took office he imposed strict regulations, offered lavish rewards for killing bandits, and urged the people back to farming—banditry soon ceased. He rooted out criminal factions, but his methods were excessively cruel.
7
後為懷州刺史，封汲郡公，徵拜內都大官。 河西羌胡領部落反叛，顯祖親征，命洪之與侍中、東郡王陸定總統諸軍。 輿駕至并州，詔洪之為河西都將討山胡。 皆保險拒戰。 洪之築壘於石樓南白鷄原以對之。 諸將悉欲進攻，洪之乃開以大信，聽其復業，胡人遂降。 顯祖嘉之，遷拜尚書外都大官。
He later became governor of Huai Province, was enfeoffed as Duke of Jijun, and was summoned to serve as Grand Official of the Inner Court. When the Qiang and Hu of Hexi rose in rebellion, Xianzu campaigned in person and ordered Hongzhi and Attendant-in-Ordinary Lu Ding, Prince of Dongjun, to command the armies jointly. When the imperial procession reached Bing Province, an edict appointed Hongzhi general of Hexi to subdue the mountain Hu. They all held the mountain passes and resisted. Hongzhi built a fortified camp on the Southern White Cock Plain south of Shilou to confront them. All the generals wanted to attack, but Hongzhi offered them generous terms and let them return to their livelihoods—the Hu then surrendered. Xianzu commended him and promoted him to Grand Official of the Outer Court in the Secretariat.
8
後為使持節、安南將軍、秦益二州刺史。 至治，設禁姦之制，有帶刃行者，罪與劫同，輕重品格，各有條章。 於是大饗州中豪傑長老，示之法制。 乃夜密遣騎分部覆諸要路，有犯禁者，輒捉送州，宣告斬決。 其中枉見殺害者百數。 赤葩渴郎羌深居山谷，雖相羈縻，王人罕到。 洪之芟山為道，廣十餘步，示以軍行之勢，乃興軍臨其境。 山人驚駭。 洪之將數十騎至其里閭，撫其妻子，問所疾苦，因資遺之。 眾羌喜悅，求編課調，所入十倍於常。 洪之善御戎夷，頗有威惠，而刻害之聲聞於朝野。
He later served as commissioner with extraordinary powers, General Who Pacifies the South, and governor of Qin and Yi provinces. On taking office he instituted strict anti-crime regulations: anyone caught carrying a weapon was punished as a robber, with graded penalties set out in detail. He then held a great banquet for the province's leading men and elders and displayed the new regulations. That night he secretly dispatched cavalry in detachments to patrol the main roads; violators were seized, brought to the provincial capital, and publicly beheaded. Hundreds among them were innocent victims. The Chipa Kelang Qiang lived deep in the mountains; though nominally under imperial control, government agents seldom reached them. Hongzhi cut a road more than ten paces wide through the mountains to display military might, then marched his army into their territory. The mountain people were terrified. Hongzhi rode with several dozen horsemen to their villages, comforted their wives and children, inquired after their hardships, and gave them gifts. The Qiang were delighted and volunteered to register for taxation; revenue from them came to ten times the usual amount. Hongzhi was skilled at managing the frontier peoples and wielded both authority and goodwill, yet reports of his cruelty spread through court and countryside.
9
初，洪之微時，妻張氏助洪之經營資產，自貧至貴，多所補益，有男女幾十人。 洪之後得劉氏，劉芳從妹。 洪之欽重，而疏薄張氏，為兩宅別居，偏厚劉室。 由是二妻妬競，互相訟詛，兩宅母子，往來如讎。 及莅西州，以劉自隨。
In his early days, when Hongzhi was still obscure, his wife Lady Zhang helped him build their fortune; from poverty to wealth she contributed greatly, and they had several dozen children. Later Hongzhi took Lady Liu, a cousin of Liu Fang. Hongzhi favored Liu greatly but treated Lady Zhang coldly; he set up two separate households and lavished favors on the Liu family. The two wives grew jealous and sued one another; mothers and sons in both households treated each other like enemies. When he took up his post in the western provinces, he brought Lady Liu with him.
10
洪之素非廉清，每多受納。 時高祖始建祿制，法禁嚴峻，司察所聞，無不窮糾。 遂鎖洪之赴京。 高祖臨太華，庭集羣官，有司奏洪之受贓狼藉，又以酷暴。 高祖親臨數之，以其大臣，聽在家自裁。 洪之志性慷慨，多所堪忍，疹疾灸療，艾炷圍將二寸，首足十餘處，一時俱下，而言笑自若，接賓不輟。 及臨自盡，沐浴換衣。 防卒扶持，將出却入，遍遶家庭，如是再三，泣歎良久，乃臥而引藥。
Hongzhi had never been honest in office and frequently accepted bribes. Emperor Gaozu had just instituted the salary system; the laws were strict, and inspectors pursued every report to the end. Hongzhi was placed in chains and escorted to the capital. Emperor Gaozu held court at Taihua Hall with officials assembled in the courtyard; the authorities reported that Hongzhi's bribery was rampant and his cruelty extreme. The emperor rebuked him in person; as a senior minister he was permitted to take his own life at home. Hongzhi was by nature bold and long-suffering; when moxibustion was applied to his rash, cones nearly two inches across were burned at more than ten points on his head and limbs at once—yet he talked and laughed as usual and never stopped receiving guests. When the time came to take his own life, he bathed and changed his clothes. Supported by guards, he started to leave but turned back, walked three times around his household, wept and sighed for a long while, then lay down and drank the poison.
11
始洪之託為元后兄，公私自同外戚。 至此罪後，高祖乃稍對百官辨其誣假，而諸李猶善相視，恩紀如親。 洪之始見元后，計年為兄。 及珍之等至，洪之以元后素定長幼，其呼拜坐皆如家人。 暮年數延攜之宴飲，醉酣之後，攜之時或言及本末，洪之則起而加敬，笑語自若。 富貴赫弈，當舅戚之家，遂棄宗專附珍之等。 後頗存振本屬，而猶不顯然。 劉氏四子，長子神自有傳。
At first Hongzhi had passed himself off as the Yuan empress's brother, and everyone privately treated him as a maternal kinsman. After his conviction Emperor Gaozu gradually explained to the officials that the kinship was false, yet the Li clan still treated one another warmly, with bonds of affection like true kin. When Hongzhi first met the Yuan empress, he was older by age and was treated as her elder brother. When Zhenzhi and his brothers arrived, Hongzhi followed the ranking of seniority the Yuan empress had established; greetings, bows, and seating were all as among family. In his later years he often invited Xiezhi to banquets; when drunk, Xiezhi would sometimes speak of how it all began—Hongzhi would rise and show added respect, yet chat and laugh as before. His wealth blazed brilliantly; as head of the maternal clan household he abandoned his own lineage and attached himself solely to Zhenzhi and his brothers. Later he made some effort to revive ties with his original clan, but not openly. Lady Liu had four sons; the eldest, Shenyou, has his own biography.
12
高遵，字世禮，勃海蓚人。 父濟，滄水太守。 遵賤出，兄矯等常欺侮之。 及父亡，不令在喪位。 遵遂馳赴平城，歸從祖兄中書令允。 允乃為遵父舉哀，以遵為喪主，京邑無不弔集，朝貴咸識之。 徐歸奔赴。 免喪，允為營宦路，得補樂浪王侍郎。 遵感成益之恩，事允如諸父。
Gao Zun, courtesy name Shili, was from Su in Bohai. His father Ji served as administrator of Cangshui. Zun was born of a concubine; his elder brothers Qiao and the others often bullied him. When their father died they would not let him take his proper place in the mourning rites. Zun galloped to Pingcheng and sought out his father's cousin, Secretariat Director Yun. Yun held mourning for Zun's father and made Zun chief mourner; the whole capital came to pay respects, and court nobles all came to know him. He returned at leisure to complete the funeral observances. After the mourning period Yun arranged his official career, and he was appointed attendant to the Prince of Lelang. Grateful for Yun's kindness, Zun served him as he would an uncle.
13
涉歷文史，頗有筆札，進中書侍郎。 詣長安，刊燕宣王廟碑，進爵安昌子。 及新製衣冠，高祖恭薦宗廟，遵形貌莊潔，音氣雄暢，常兼太祝令，跪贊禮事，為俯仰之節，粗合儀矩。 由是高祖識待之。 後與游明根、高閭、李沖入議律令，親對御坐，時有陳奏。 以積年之勞，賜粟帛牛馬。 出為立忠將軍、齊州刺史。 建節歷本州，宗鄉改觀，而矯等彌妬毀之。
He studied literature and history and was skilled with the brush; he was promoted to Secretariat Attendant. He went to Chang'an to carve the temple stele for Prince Xuan of Yan and was advanced to Viscount of Anchang. When new caps and robes were made and Emperor Gaozu offered sacrifice at the ancestral temple, Zun's bearing was dignified and his voice resonant; he regularly served as Director of Sacrifices, kneeling to intone the rites—the motions of bowing and rising roughly conformed to ritual. Thereby Emperor Gaozu came to notice and favor him. Later he joined You Minggen, Gao Lü, and Li Chong in deliberating on statutes; facing the imperial throne he sometimes presented memorials. For years of accumulated service he was rewarded with grain, silk, cattle, and horses. He was appointed General Who Establishes Loyalty and governor of Qi Province. Bearing the staff of authority he governed his home province; clansmen changed their view of him, while Qiao and his brothers hated him all the more.
14
遵性不廉清，在中書時，每假歸山東，必借備騾馬，將從百餘。 屯逼民家求絲縑，不滿意則詬罵不去，強相徵求。 旬月之間，縑布千數。 邦邑苦之。 遵既臨州，本意未弭，選召僚吏，多所取納。 又其妻明氏家在齊州，母弟舅甥共相憑屬，爭求貨利，嚴暴非理，殺害甚多。 貪酷之響，帝頗聞之。 及車駕幸鄴，遵自州來朝，會有赦宥。 遵臨還州，請辭帝於行宮，引見誚讓之。 遵自陳無負，帝厲聲曰：「若無遷都赦，必無高遵矣！ 又卿非惟貪惏，又虐於刑法，謂何如濟陰王，猶不免於法。 卿何人，而為此行！ 自今宜自謹約。 」還州，仍不悛革。 齊州人孟僧振至洛訟遵。 詔廷尉少卿劉述窮鞫，皆如所訴。 先是，沙門道登過遵，遵以道登荷寵於高祖，多奉以貨，深託仗之。 道登屢因言次申啟救遵，帝不省納，遂詔述賜遵死。 時遵子元榮詣洛訟冤，猶恃道登，不時還赴。 道登知事決，方乃遣之。 遵恨其妻，不與訣，別處沐浴，引椒而死。
Zun was not honest in office; while at the Secretariat, each time he took leave to return to Shandong he borrowed pack mules and horses and traveled with more than a hundred attendants. He camped at commoners' homes demanding silk; if unsatisfied he cursed and refused to leave, forcibly exacting goods. Within a month or so he accumulated thousands of bolts of silk and cloth. The whole region suffered under him. Once Zun took office his rapacity did not abate; he summoned subordinates and extorted bribes from many of them. His wife Lady Ming's family lived in Qi Province; her mother, brothers, uncles, and nephews relied on one another to scramble for profit, acting with brutal violence beyond reason and killing many people. The emperor heard considerable reports of his greed and cruelty. When the emperor visited Ye, Zun came from his province to pay court, and a general amnesty happened to be declared. As Zun was about to return to his province he took leave of the emperor at the traveling palace and was summoned and rebuked. Zun protested his innocence. The emperor said sharply, "If not for the amnesty proclaimed when the capital was moved, Gao Zun would certainly be dead! And you are not only greedy but brutal in your use of the law—consider the Prince of Jiyin: even he could not escape punishment. Who do you think you are, to behave like this! From now on you must restrain yourself. " Zun returned to his province but still did not reform. Meng Zhen of Qi Province went to Luoyang to bring suit against Zun. The emperor ordered Vice Director of the Court of Justice Liu Shu to investigate thoroughly; the charges were all substantiated. Earlier the monk Daodeng had visited Zun; because Daodeng enjoyed Emperor Gaozu's favor, Zun gave him many gifts and placed great trust in him. Daodeng repeatedly interceded for Zun when he had the emperor's ear, but the emperor would not listen and ordered Liu Shu to sentence Zun to death. Zun's son Yuanrong went to Luoyang to plead his father's case; still trusting in Daodeng, he did not return home in time. Only when Daodeng knew the sentence was final did he send Yuanrong home. Zun hated his wife and refused to bid her farewell; he bathed in a separate room and drank pepper poison to die.
15
元榮，學尚有文才，長於几案。 位兼尚書右丞，為西道行臺，至高平鎮，遇城翻被害。
Yuanrong was learned and talented with the pen, skilled at administrative work. He served concurrently as Right Vice Director of the Secretariat and as Western Route Commissioner; when he reached Gaoping garrison the city rose in revolt and he was killed.
16
遵弟次文，雖無位官而貲產巨萬。 遵每責其財，又結憾於遵，吉凶不相往反。 時論責之。
Zun's younger brother Ciwen held no office but possessed wealth in the tens of thousands. Zun constantly demanded money from him; Ciwen bore a grudge against Zun as well, and they never visited one another in good times or bad. Public opinion censured him for this.
17
張赦提，中山安喜人也。 性雄武，有規畫。 初為虎賁中郎。 時京畿盜魁自稱豹子、虎子，並善弓馬，遂領逃連及諸畜牧者，[2]各為部帥，於靈丘、雁門間聚為劫害。 至乃斬人首，射其口，刺人臍，引腸遶樹而共射之，以為戲笑。 其為暴酷如此。 軍騎掩㨞，久弗能獲，行者患焉。 赦提設防遏追窮之計，宰司善之，以赦提為逐賊軍將。 乃求驍勇追之，未幾而獲虎子、豹子及其黨與。 盡送京師，斬於闕下，自是清靜。 其靈丘羅思祖宗門豪溢，家處隘險，多止亡命，與之為劫。 顯祖怒之，孥戮其家。 而思祖家黨，相率寇盜。 赦提應募求捕逐，乃以赦提為游徼軍將，前後禽獲，殺之略盡。 因而濫有屠害，尤為忍酷。 既資前稱，又藉此功，除冠軍將軍、幽州刺史，假安喜侯。
Zhang Sheti was from Anxi in Zhongshan. He was bold and martial by nature and skilled in planning. He began as a Tiger Guard Central Gentleman. At that time the chief bandits of the capital region called themselves Leopard Son and Tiger Son; both were skilled archers and horsemen. They gathered fugitives and herders as their followers and plundered the region between Lingqiu and Yanmen. They would behead victims, shoot arrows into their mouths, stab their navels, draw out their intestines and wind them around trees for target practice—all as sport. Their cruelty was of this sort. Government cavalry hunted them for a long time without success, and travelers lived in fear. Sheti devised plans to block and pursue the bandits to the end; the chief ministers approved and appointed him general for pursuing bandits. He recruited fierce warriors to pursue them and soon captured Tiger Son, Leopard Son, and their followers. They were all sent to the capital and beheaded at the palace gate; the region was quiet thereafter. At Lingqiu the Luo Sizu clan was powerful; their estate lay in rugged terrain where many fugitives took refuge and joined them in banditry. Xianzu was enraged and exterminated the entire clan. Sizu's kinsmen and followers then banded together in raids. Sheti volunteered to hunt them down and was appointed Patrol General; he captured and killed nearly all of them. In the process he indiscriminately slaughtered many people, showing extreme cruelty. Building on his earlier reputation and this new success, he was appointed General Who Crowns the Army and governor of You Province, with the provisional title Marquis of Anxi.

Book of Wei, juan 89: Various editions' tables of contents mark this juan as missing, but there are no Song-dynasty collation notes at the end of the scroll. The Palace Edition textual verification states that "Wei Shou's original text is lost and was supplemented by later editors"—a judgment based on the table of contents. On examination, the preface differs from that in Northern History juan 87, Biographies of Cruel Officials, and the individual biographies also diverge in many places. Northern History, biography of Gao Zun 〈Attached juan 31, biography of Gao Yun〉 , biography of Li Daoyuan 〈Attached juan 27, biography of Li Fan〉 are all given in detail in this scroll; the Northern History biography of Gu Kai omits the account of killing the mutinous crowd. It is quite clear that this scroll was not supplemented from the Northern History. But the preface is very brief, and the Gao and Li biographies are more abbreviated than in the Northern History. The concluding essay is identical to the Northern History, which in fact derives from the Sui History juan 74 essay on cruel officials with only slight alterations. Wei Shou could not have used Sui History language; this essay was clearly copied from the Northern History. This scroll is therefore certainly not original Book of Wei text; it was likely supplemented from works such as the Gao clan Minor History, with the collation note at the end occasionally lost.

或憂恚顛隕諸本「顛」作「值」，北史卷八七作「俱」，隋書卷七四酷吏傳論作「顛」。 按「值」字顯訛，「俱」字雖可通，然「顛隕」與上句「罪戮」對文，今據隋書改。
On the phrase "or in grief and rage fell and perished": various editions read dian ("fall") as zhi; Northern History juan 87 has ju ("all"); Sui History juan 74 has dian. The reading zhi is clearly erroneous; ju is possible but dianyun ("fall and perish") parallels zuilü ("suffer punishment") in the preceding clause; the text is corrected here following the Sui History.
