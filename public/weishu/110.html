1
漢初，魏無知封高良侯，子均，均子恢，恢子彥。 彥子歆，字子胡，幼孤有志操，博洽經史，成帝世，位終鉅鹿太守，[2]仍家焉。 歆子悅，[3]字處德，性沉厚有度量，宣城公趙國李孝伯見而重之，以女妻焉。 位濟陰太守，以善政稱。
In the early Han, Wei Wuzhi received the title Marquis of Gaoliang. The line continued through his son Jun, Jun's son Hui, and Hui's son Yan. Yan's son Xin, courtesy name Zihu, lost his father in childhood but showed strong character. Well read in the classics and historical writings, he served through Emperor Cheng's reign and ended his career as administrator of Julu; [2] the family made its home there. Xin's son Yue, [3] courtesy name Chude, was sober-minded and broad in spirit. Li Xiaobo of Zhao, Duke of Xuancheng, took notice of him and held him in high regard, giving him his daughter in marriage. He served as administrator of Jiyin and won renown for his good government.
2
悅子子建，字敬忠。 釋褐奉朝請，累遷太尉從事中郎。 初，世宗時平氐，[4]遂於武興立鎮，尋改為東益州。 其後鎮將、刺史乖失人和，羣氐作梗，遂為邊患，乃除子建為東益州刺史。 子建布以恩信，風化大行，遠近清靜。 正光五年，南、北二秦城人莫折念生、韓祖香、張長命相繼構逆，僉以州城之人莫不勁勇，同類悉反，宜先收其器械。 子建以為城人數當行陳，盡皆驍果，安之足以為用，急之腹背為憂，乃悉召居城老壯曉示之； 并上言諸城人本非罪坐而來者悉求聽免。 肅宗優詔從之。 子建漸分其父兄子弟外居郡戍，內外相顧，終獲保全。 及秦賊乘勝，屯營黑水，子建乃潛使掩襲，前後斬獲甚眾，威名赫然，先反者及此悉降。 乃間使上聞，肅宗甚嘉之，詔子建兼尚書為行臺，刺史如故。 於是威震蜀土，其梁、巴、二益、兩秦之事，皆所節度。 梁州刺史傅豎眼子敬和中心以為愧，在洛大行貨賄，以圖行臺。 先是，子建亦屢求歸京師，至此，乃遣刺史唐永代焉，[5]豎眼因為行臺。 子建將還，羣氐慕戀，相率斷道。 主簿楊僧覆先行曉喻，諸氐忿曰：「我留刺史，爾送出也！ 」斫之數創，幾死。 子建徐加慰譬，旬日方得前行，吏人贈遺，一無所受。 而東益氐、蜀尋反，攻逼唐永，永棄城而走，乃喪一藩矣。 初永之走，子建客有沙門曇璨及鉅鹿人耿顯皆沒落氐手，及知子建之客，垂泣追衣物還之，送出白馬。 遺愛所被如此。 自國家開華陽等郡，梁州邢巒、益州傅豎眼及子建為最。 初，子建為前軍將軍，十年不徙，在洛閑暇，與吏部尚書李韶、韶從弟延寔頗為弈棊，時人謂為耽好。 子建每曰：「棊於機權廉勇之際，得之深矣。 且吾未為時用，博弈可也。 」及一臨邊事，凡經五年，未曾對局。
Yue's son Zijian bore the courtesy name Jingzhong. After first taking office he became a court gentleman for imperial audience, and rose in due course to attendant gentleman under the grand commandant. Earlier, during Emperor Shizong's reign the Di were pacified, [4] and a garrison was set up at Wuxing, which before long became Eastern Yi Province. Later the garrison commander and provincial governor lost the people's trust, the Di rose in obstruction, and the frontier fell into disorder. Zijian was then appointed governor of Eastern Yi Province. Zijian governed through kindness and trustworthiness. His civilizing influence took deep hold, and peace prevailed throughout the region. In the fifth year of Zhenguang, the townspeople of Southern and Northern Er-Qin—Mo Zhe Niansheng, Han Zuxiang, and Zhang Changming—staged successive rebellions. Officials agreed that the capital's inhabitants were all fierce fighters, that their fellows had already turned, and that their arms should be confiscated at once. Zijian reasoned that these city folk were slated for military service and were every one of them seasoned fighters. Treat them well and they would serve you; drive them hard and you would face trouble on every side. He therefore gathered the city's elders and able-bodied men and explained his policy to them; and he memorialized the throne asking that all city residents who had been sent there without having been convicted of a crime be allowed to return home. Emperor Suzong issued a gracious edict approving the request. Zijian gradually posted his kinsmen—fathers, brothers, and sons—to garrisons in the surrounding districts. With family inside and allies outside looking out for one another, they escaped harm in the end. When the Qin rebels pressed their advantage and camped at Heishui, Zijian sent troops in a surprise attack. They killed and captured a great many of the enemy, his fame spread far and wide, and earlier rebels all surrendered. He then reported his success to the court. Emperor Suzong was greatly pleased and appointed Zijian concurrently director of the imperial secretariat with the title of mobile secretariat, while he retained his post as governor. His authority now dominated the Shu region. Affairs of Liang, Ba, the two Yi provinces, and the two Qin districts all came under his direction. Fu Shuyan, governor of Liang, and his son Jinghe were stung with envy. In Luoyang they spent heavily on bribes to win the mobile secretariat for themselves. Zijian had long sought to return to the capital. Now Tang Yong was sent to replace him as governor, [5] and Shuyan received the mobile secretariat in his stead. When Zijian prepared to leave, the Di people, who had grown attached to him, gathered in bands and cut off the roads. Chief clerk Yang Sengfu went ahead to explain the situation. The Di cried out in anger: "We are keeping our governor—you may leave!" " They hacked him with many wounds and he nearly died. Zijian gradually calmed them with words of comfort and reason. Only after ten days could he set out. He refused every gift offered by officials and commoners alike. Soon afterward the Di and Shu of Eastern Yi rebelled again and pressed Tang Yong, who abandoned the city and fled. In this way an entire frontier province was lost. When Yong first fled, two of Zijian's associates—the monk Tancan and Geng Xian of Julu—fell into Di hands. Learning they were Zijian's guests, their captors wept, restored their belongings, and escorted them out at Baima. Such was the reach of the affection he had won. Since the dynasty had opened Huayang and the other commanderies, Xing Luan in Liang, Fu Shuyan in Yi, and Zijian had been the most successful frontier governors. Earlier Zijian had served as general of the vanguard and held that post for ten years without transfer. In Luoyang, in his leisure, he often played go with Li Shao, director of the ministry of personnel, and Shao's younger cousin Yanbao. People at the time called this a wasteful hobby. Zijian often said: "Go teaches one a great deal about timing, integrity, and courage. Besides, I am not yet in the state's service—there is no harm in a game now and then. " Once he took up frontier duties, he went five full years without playing a single game.
3
還洛後，俄拜常侍、衞尉卿。 初，元顥內逼，莊帝北幸，子建謂所親盧義僖曰：「北海自絕社稷，稱藩蕭衍，吾老矣，豈能為陪臣？ 」遂攜家口居洛南，顥平乃歸。 先苦風痺，及此遂甚，以卿任有務，屢上書乞身，特除右光祿大夫。 邢杲之平，太傅李延寔子侍中彧為大使，撫慰東土，時外戚貴盛，送客填門，子建亦往候別。 延寔曰：「小兒今行，何以相勗？ 」子建曰：「益以盈滿為誡。 」延寔悵然久之。 及莊帝殺尒朱榮，遇禍於河陰者其家率相弔賀。 太尉李虔第二子仁曜，子建之女壻，往亦見害。 子建謂姨弟盧道虔曰：「朝廷誅翦權強，凶徒尚梗，未聞有奇謀異略，恐不可濟。 此乃李門禍始，弔賀無乃怱怱？ 」及永安之後，李氏宗族流離，或遇誅夷，如其所慮。 後歷左光祿大夫，加散騎常侍、驃騎大將軍。
After returning to Luoyang he was soon made regular attendant and commandant of the imperial guard. Earlier, when Yuan Hao threatened from within and Emperor Zhuang marched north, Zijian told his friend Lu Yixi: "The Prince of Beihai has renounced the dynasty and submitted to Xiao Yan's vassalage. I am an old man—how could I serve as his subject?" He moved his household to the south bank of the Luo. Only when Hao was defeated did he return. He had long suffered from rheumatic paralysis, which now grew severe. Citing the press of official duties, he repeatedly asked to retire and was specially appointed grand master of splendid right. When Xing Gao's rebellion was put down, Li Yanbao's son Yu, attendant-in-ordinary, was appointed chief envoy to pacify the east. The empress's kin were then at the height of power, and their gates were thronged with well-wishers. Zijian went too to bid farewell. Yanbao said: "My son sets out today—what counsel will you give him?" " Zijian replied: "Tell him to beware of pride at the height of fortune." " Yanbao fell silent for a long while, troubled in spirit. After Emperor Zhuang killed Erzhu Rong, families of those slaughtered at Heyin were visited in turn with condolences—and congratulations. Renyao, second son of Grand Commandant Li Qian and husband to Zijian's daughter, went as well and was among those killed. Zijian told his cousin by marriage Lu Daoxuan: "The court is destroying the mighty, yet violent men still block the way, and I hear of no bold plan to carry the day. I fear it will not end well. This is only the start of trouble for the Li family—is it not rather soon to be both mourning and celebrating?" After the Yong'an period the Li clan was scattered, and many were executed—just as he had predicted. He later served as grand master of splendid left, with additional appointments as regular cavalier attendant and general of agile cavalry.
4
子建自出為藩牧，董司山南，居脂膏之中，遇天下多事，正身潔己，不以財利經懷。 及歸京師，家人衣食常不周贍，清素之迹，著於終始。 性存重慎，不雜交遊，唯與尚書盧義僖、姨弟涇州刺史盧道裕雅相親昵。 及疾篤，顧敕二子曰：「死生大分，含氣所同，世有厚葬，吾平生不取，籧篨裸身，又非吾意。 氣絕之後，斂以時服。 吾生年契闊，[6]前後三娶，合葬之事，抑又非古。 且汝二母先在舊塋，墳地久固，已有定別。 唯汝次母墓在外耳，可遷入兆域，依班而定，行於吾墓之後。 如此足矣，不須祔合。 當順吾心，勿令吾有遺恨。 」永熙二年春，卒于洛陽孝義里舍，時年六十三，贈儀同三司、定州刺史，諡曰文靜。 二子，收、祚。
From the time he left the capital as a frontier governor and oversaw the southern mountains, living amid rich revenues in an age of turmoil, he kept himself upright and never let wealth sway him. Back in the capital his household often lacked adequate food and clothing. His frugality was evident from beginning to end. By nature he was grave and cautious and kept few company. Only Director Lu Yixi and his cousin by marriage Lu Daoyu, governor of Jing, were his close friends. When his illness grew grave he instructed his two sons: "Life and death are the great divide that all who breathe must cross. The world loves lavish burial, but I have never approved it in life. A bamboo coffin and naked burial are no wish of mine either. When my breath fails, dress me in ordinary clothes for burial. My life has been long and full of change, [6] and I have taken three wives in succession. Joint burial is not the ancient custom in any case. Your two mothers already lie in the old graveyard. That mound is long settled, and the arrangement is fixed. Only your stepmother's grave lies outside. You may move her into the family burial ground, placing her according to rank behind my own tomb. That will be enough. There is no need to bury us together. Honor my wishes and do not leave me with regret." In the spring of the second year of Yongxi he died at his home in Xiaoyi Lane, Luoyang, at the age of sixty-three. He was posthumously granted the third rank of the ceremonial trilogy and the governorship of Ding Province, with the posthumous name Wenjing. He had two sons, Shou and Zuo.
5
收字伯起，小字佛助。 年十五，頗已屬文。 及隨父赴邊，值四方多難，好習騎射，欲以武藝自達。 滎陽鄭伯調之曰：「魏郎弄戟多少？ 」收慚，遂折節讀書。 夏月坐板牀，隨樹陰諷誦，積年，牀板為之銳減，而精力不輟。 以文華顯。 初以父功除太學博士，及尒朱榮於河陰濫害朝士，收亦在圍中，以日晏獲免。
Shou, courtesy name Boqi, was known in childhood as Fo Zhu. By the age of fifteen he was already writing competently. When he accompanied his father to the frontier, the realm was in turmoil. He took to horsemanship and archery, hoping to make his name through martial prowess. Zheng Bo of Xingyang teased him: "Young Wei, how many halberds can you twirl?" Shou was stung with shame and turned his energies to study. In summer he sat on a plank bed in the shade of the trees, reciting his texts aloud. Year after year the board was worn thin, yet his diligence never flagged. He won distinction through literary talent. At first, through his father's merit, he was appointed erudite of the imperial university. When Erzhu Rong massacred court officials at Heyin, Shou was caught in the roundup and escaped only because night fell.
6
吏部尚書李神儁重收才學，奏授司徒記室參軍。 永安三年，除北主客郎中。 前廢帝立，妙簡近侍，詔試收為封禪書，收下筆便就，不立草稿，文將千言，所改無幾。 時黃門郎賈思同侍立，深奇之。 帝曰：[7]「雖七步之才，無以過此。 」遷散騎侍郎，尋敕典起居注，并修國史。 俄兼中書侍郎，年二十六。 出帝初，又詔收攝本職，文誥填積，事咸稱旨。 黃門郎崔㥄從齊獻武王入朝，熏灼於世，收初不詣門。 㥄為帝登祚赦，云「朕託體孝文」，收嗤其率直。 正員郎李慎以告之，㥄深忿忌。 時前廢帝殂，令收為詔，㥄乃宣言：「收普泰世出入幃幄，一日造詔，優為詞旨，然則義旗之士，盡為逆人； 又收父老合解官歸侍。 」南臺將加彈劾，賴尚書辛雄為言於中尉綦儁，乃解。 收有賤生弟仲同先未齒錄，因此怖懼，上籍，遣還鄉扶侍。 出帝嘗大發士卒，狩於嵩少之南，旬有六日，時既寒苦，朝野嗟怨。 帝與從官皆胡服而騎，宮人及諸妃主雜其間，奇伎異飾，多非禮度。 收欲言則畏懼，欲默不能已，乃上南狩賦以諷焉，年二十七，雖富言淫麗，而終歸雅正。 帝手詔報焉，甚見褒美。 鄭伯謂曰：「卿不遇老夫，猶應逐兔。 」初，齊獻武王固讓天柱大將軍，帝敕收為詔，令遂所請，欲加相國，問收相國品秩，收以實對，帝遂止。 收既未測主相之意，以前事不安，求解，詔許焉。 久之，除帝兄子廣平王贊開府從事中郎，收不敢辭，乃為庭竹賦以致己意。 尋兼中書舍人，與濟陰溫子昇、河間邢子才齊譽，世號三才。 時出帝猜忌獻武，內有間隙，收遂以疾固辭而免。 其舅崔孝芬怪而問之，收曰：「懼有晉陽之甲。 」尋而獻武南上，帝西入關。
Li Shenjun, director of the ministry of personnel, admired Shou's talent and learning and recommended him as recorder under the minister of education. In the third year of Yong'an he was appointed master of guests for the north. When the former deposed emperor was enthroned, the court carefully chose close attendants and ordered Shou tested with a fengshan composition. He wrote at a stroke without drafting; the piece ran to nearly a thousand words with scarcely a revision. Jia Sitong, gentleman of the yellow gate, stood by in attendance and was deeply impressed. The emperor said: [7] "Even the talent of seven paces could not surpass this." " He was promoted to regular cavalier attendant and soon ordered to oversee the imperial diary and to compile the national history. Before long he served concurrently as gentleman of the central secretariat, at the age of twenty-six. At the beginning of Emperor Chu's reign he was again ordered to resume his former duties. Imperial documents piled up, and in every matter he satisfied the throne. Cui Ling, gentleman of the yellow gate, followed Prince Xianwu of Qi to court and dominated the age. At first Shou would not call at his door. Ling drafted the amnesty for the emperor's accession, writing "I entrust my person to Emperor Xiaowen." Shou laughed at its bluntness. Regular gentleman Li Shen reported this to him, and Ling nursed a deep grudge. When the former deposed emperor died, Shou was ordered to draft the edict. Ling then declared: "In the Putai era Shou moved in and out of the inner apartments. In a single day he drafts an edict with polished phrasing—so the men who raised the banner of righteousness are all made rebels; moreover Shou's father is old and ought altogether to resign and return home to serve him." The southern bureau was about to impeach him. He was spared only when Director Xin Xiong pleaded his case before commandant of the palace army Qin Jun. Shou had a younger half-brother, Zhongtong, who had not been entered in the family register. Terrified by the affair, he now registered him and sent him home to care for their father. Emperor Chu once mobilized troops on a grand scale and hunted south of Song and Shao for sixteen days. The weather was bitterly cold, and court and country groaned in protest. The emperor and his attendants all wore barbarian dress on horseback. Palace women and imperial consorts rode among them in outlandish costume and spectacle, much of it contrary to ritual. Shou wished to remonstrate but feared reprisal, yet could not remain silent. He submitted a rhapsody on the southern hunt as indirect admonition. He was twenty-seven. Though the language was lush, the piece ended in moral seriousness. The emperor answered with his own hand and praised him warmly. Zheng Bo told him: "Had you not met this old fellow, you would still be out chasing rabbits." Earlier Prince Xianwu of Qi had firmly declined the post of pillar-of-state general. The emperor ordered Shou to draft the edict granting his request. When the throne wished to add the title of chancellor of state, it asked Shou the rank's precedence; Shou answered truthfully, and the emperor dropped the matter. Shou had not yet read the prince's mind. Troubled by the earlier incident, he asked to resign, and an edict allowed it. Before long he was appointed attendant on the establishment of the emperor's nephew, Prince Zan of Guangping. Shou dared not refuse and wrote a rhapsody on courtyard bamboo to express his unease. Soon he served concurrently as gentleman of the central secretariat. He shared fame with Wen Zisheng of Jiyin and Xing Zicai of Hejian; their age called them the Three Talents. At that time Emperor Chu was suspicious of Xianwu and a rift had opened between them. Shou pleaded illness and secured his release. His uncle Cui Xiaofen was puzzled and asked why. Shou said: "I fear there will be an army from Jinyang." Before long Xianwu marched south, and the emperor withdrew west into the passes.

收兼通直散騎常侍副王昕聘蕭衍，昕風流文辯，收辭藻富逸，衍及其羣臣咸加敬異。 先是，南北初和，李諧、盧元明首通使命，二人才器，並為鄰國所重。 至此，衍稱曰：「盧、李命世，王、魏中興，未知後來復何如耳？ 」文襄啟收兼散騎常侍，修國史。 武定二年，除正常侍，領兼中書侍郎，仍修史。 帝宴百僚，問何故名人日，皆莫能知。 收對曰：「晉議郎董勛答問，稱俗云正月一日為鷄，二日為狗，三日為猪，四日為羊，五日為牛，六日為馬，七日為人。 時邢卲亦在側，甚恧焉。 自南北和好，書下紙每云「想彼境內寧靜，此率土安和」。 蕭衍後使，其書乃去「彼」字，自稱猶著「此」，欲示無外之意。 收定報書云：「想境內清晏，今萬國安和。 」南人復書，依以為體。 後獻武入朝，靜帝授相國，固讓，令收為啟。 啟成呈上，文襄時侍側，獻武指收曰：「此人當復為崔光。 」四年，獻武於西門豹祠宴集，謂司馬子如曰：「魏收為史官，書吾善惡。 聞北伐時，諸貴常餉史官飲食，司馬僕射頗曾餉不？ 」因共大笑。 仍謂收曰：「卿勿見元康等在吾目下趨走，謂吾以為勤勞，我後世身名在卿手，勿謂我不知。 」尋加兼著作郎。
Shou served concurrently as regular attendant of the unimpeded and accompanied Wang Xin on an embassy to Xiao Yan. Xin was graceful and quick in debate; Shou's prose was lush and fluent. Yan and all his ministers treated them with marked respect. When north and south first made peace, Li Xie and Lu Yuanming had been the first envoys. Both men's talent was held in high regard by the neighboring states. Now Yan said: "Lu and Li were talents of their age; Wang and Wei revive the present. Who knows what the next generation will bring?" Wen Xiang recommended that Shou serve concurrently as regular cavalier attendant and compile the national history. In the second year of Wuding he was appointed regular attendant and overseer, with concurrent post as gentleman of the central secretariat, still compiling history. The emperor feasted the hundred officials and asked why the day was called the Day of Man. No one knew. Shou answered: "The Jin discussion gentleman Dong Xun, replying to questions, said that popular custom holds the first day of the first month to be the cock, the second the dog, the third the pig, the fourth the sheep, the fifth the ox, the sixth the horse, and the seventh man." Xing Shao was standing nearby at the time and was deeply embarrassed. After north and south made peace, their letters often opened with a formula: "I trust all is quiet within your borders; here the realm enjoys peace." Later envoys from Xiao Yan dropped the word "your" from their letters while still using "here" for themselves, implying that no outside realm existed. Shou drafted the reply to read: "I trust your borders are tranquil; all states under heaven are at peace." The southerners adopted this phrasing in their subsequent correspondence. When Xianwu later came to court, Emperor Jing offered him the chancellorship of state. He declined firmly and had Shou draft the memorial of refusal. When the memorial was presented, Wen Xiang stood at his side. Xianwu pointed to Shou and said: "This man will be another Cui Guang." In the fourth year Xianwu held a feast at the shrine of Ximen Bao and said to Sima Ziru: "Wei Shou is the historiographer. He records my good deeds and my bad." "I hear that on the northern campaign the nobles often treated the historiographers to food and drink. Vice Director Sima, did you ever do the same?" They burst out laughing together. He then told Shou: "Do not see Yuan Kang and the others bustling before me and think I mistake bustle for real work. I know my reputation after death lies in your hands—do not imagine I forget that." Soon afterward he received the additional appointment of concurrent compiler.

靜帝曾季秋大射，普令賦詩，收詩末云：「尺書徵建鄴，折簡召長安。 」文襄壯之，顧謂人曰：「在朝今有魏收，便是國之光采。 雅俗文墨，通達縱橫，我亦使子才、子昇時有所作，至於詞氣並不及之。 吾或意有所懷，忘而不語，語而不盡，意有未及。 及收呈草，皆以周悉。 此亦難有。 」又敕兼主客郎，接蕭衍使謝珽徐陵。 侯景既陷臺城，衍鄱陽王範時為合州刺史，文襄敕收以書喻之。 範得書，乃率部伍西上，□州刺史崔聖念入據其城。 文襄謂收曰：「今定一州，卿有其力，猶恨『尺書徵建鄴』未效耳。」
Emperor Jing once ordered poetry composed for a great autumn archery ceremony. Shou ended his poem with the lines: "A letter a foot long summons Jiankang; a folded note calls Chang'an to heel." Wen Xiang was deeply impressed and said to those around him: "With Wei Shou in court today, the state has its glory. Whether the matter is refined or vulgar, literary or practical, he commands it all. I have Zicai and Zisheng write for me now and then, but neither matches his force of language. Sometimes I have a thought I forget to voice, or voice it incompletely, and something remains unsaid. When Shou presents a draft, everything I meant is there. That is rare indeed." He also appointed him concurrent master of guests to receive Xiao Yan's envoys Xie Yan and Xu Ling. After Hou Jing captured Taicheng, Xiao Yan's Prince of Poyang, Fan, was governor of He Province. Wen Xiang ordered Shou to write and persuade him. Fan received the letter and marched west with his forces. Governor Cui Shengian of □ Province entered and occupied his city. Wen Xiang told Shou: "You helped win a province today, yet I still regret that your line about summoning Jiankang has not come true."
9
二年，受詔撰魏史，除魏尹，故優以祿力，專在史閣，不知郡事。 初，帝令羣臣各言志，收曰：「臣願得直筆東觀，早出魏書。 」故帝使收專其任。 又詔平原王高隆之總監之，隆之署名而已。 帝敕收曰：「好直筆，我終不作魏太武誅史官。 」始魏初鄧淵撰代記十餘卷，其後崔浩典史，游雅、高允、程駿、李彪、崔光、李琰之世修其業。 浩為編年體，彪始分作紀表志傳，書猶未出。 世宗時，命邢巒追撰高祖起居注，書至太和十四年，又命崔鴻、王遵業補續焉。 下訖肅宗，事甚委悉。 濟陰王暉業撰辨宗室錄三十卷。 收於是與通直常侍房延祐，司空司馬辛元植，國子博士刁柔、裴昂之，尚書郎高孝幹博總斟酌，[8]以成魏書。 辨定名稱，隨條甄舉，又搜採亡遺，綴續後事，備一代史籍，表而上聞。 勒成一代大典，凡十二紀、九十二列傳，合一百一十卷，五年三月奏上之。 秋，除梁州刺史，收以志未成，奏請終業，許之。 十一月，復奏十志：天象四卷，地形三卷，律歷二卷，禮、樂四卷，食貨一卷，刑罰一卷，靈徵二卷，官氏二卷，釋老一卷，凡二十卷，續於紀傳，合一百三十卷，分為十二帙。 其史三十五例，二十五序，九十四論，前後二表一啟焉。
In the second year he received orders to compile the History of Wei and was appointed governor of Wei. He was granted generous salary and resources and worked exclusively in the historiography office, leaving provincial duties aside. Earlier the emperor had asked each minister to state his ambition. Shou said: "I wish to write with an honest brush at the Eastern Observatory and complete the History of Wei soon." The emperor therefore put him solely in charge of the project. He also named Prince Longzhi of Pingyuan as general supervisor, but Longzhi did no more than lend his signature. The emperor told Shou: "Write truthfully. I will never repeat Emperor Taiwu of Wei's murder of the historiographer." In early Wei, Deng Yuan compiled more than ten juan of dynastic annals. Cui Hao later directed the historiography, and You Ya, Gao Yun, Cheng Jun, Li Biao, Cui Guang, and Li Yanzhi carried the work forward through successive generations. Hao wrote in annalistic form. Biao was the first to divide the work into annals, tables, treatises, and biographies, but the book had still not been published. Under Emperor Shizong, Xing Luan was ordered to compile the veritable records of Emperor Gaozu, carrying the account to the fourteenth year of Taihe. Cui Hong and Wang Zunye were later ordered to continue the work. The record ran down to Emperor Suzong in considerable detail. Prince Huiye of Jiyin compiled a thirty-juan record for distinguishing members of the imperial clan. Shou then worked with Fang Yanyou, regular attendant of the unimpeded; Xin Yuanzhi, secretary of the ministry of works; Diao Rou and Pei Angzhi, erudites of the imperial university; and Gao Xiaogan, gentleman of the secretariat. Together they gathered and weighed the sources [8] to complete the History of Wei. He fixed names and titles, arranged entries by category, recovered lost material, and linked later events into a full history of the dynasty, which he submitted to the throne. The great chronicle of the dynasty was finished: twelve annals and ninety-two biographies, 110 juan in all. It was presented in the third month of the fifth year. That autumn he was appointed governor of Liang Province. Because the work was unfinished, Shou asked to complete it and was allowed to remain at his task. In the eleventh month he submitted the ten treatises: Celestial Phenomena (4 juan), Topography (3), Calendars and Pitch Pipes (2), Rites and Music (4), Food and Goods (1), Punishments (1), Spiritual Signs (2), Offices and Clans (2), and Buddhism and Daoism (1)—20 juan in all. Added to the annals and biographies, the work totaled 130 juan in twelve fascicles. The history included thirty-five precedents, twenty-five prefaces, ninety-four disquisitions, and two tables and one memorial.
10
魏書卷一百四諸本此卷目錄注「闕」。 按卷後諸本皆無宋人校語，當是脫去。 此序刪節北史卷五六魏收傳，兼採他書，故也有溢出北史字句。
In various editions of the History of Wei, the table of contents for juan 104 is marked "missing." Examination shows that the various editions lack Song-dynasty collation notes after this juan; they must have been lost. This preface abridges the biography of Wei Shou in the History of the North, juan 56, and draws on other sources as well, which explains passages that go beyond the North History text.
11
成帝世位終鉅鹿太守錢氏考異卷二八云：「此成帝謂漢成帝也。 魏歆仕於漢成帝朝，而其子悅乃仕於元魏太武之世，此理之所必無者。 伯起亦通人，何至憒憒乃爾。 良由魏史自序久亡，後人節取北史補之，而北史又有脫簡，後人無從校正爾。 」按「成帝世」三字，北史卷五六魏收傳無，當取他書增，據元和姓纂輯本卷八魏氏稱「漢鉅鹿太守歆，居鉅鹿」，錢氏謂成帝謂漢成帝，是。
"In the reign of Emperor Cheng he ended as administrator of Julu." Qian's Textual Notes, juan 28, observes: "This Emperor Cheng must mean Emperor Cheng of Han. Wei Xin served under Emperor Cheng of Han, while his son Yue is said to have served under Emperor Taiwu of the Northern Wei—an impossible chronology. Wei Shou was no fool. How could he have been so confused? The answer is that Wei Shou's autobiographical preface in the History of Wei was long lost. Later editors patched the text from the History of the North, which itself had gaps, leaving no firm basis for correction. " The phrase "in the reign of Emperor Cheng" does not appear in the North History biography (juan 56) and was likely added from another source. The Yuanhe Xingzuan, juan 8, lists "Han administrator of Julu, Xin, of Julu" under the Wei clan. Qian is right that Emperor Cheng here means Emperor Cheng of Han.
12
歆子悅北史卷五六無「歆」字。 按魏悅為北魏李孝伯壻，魏歆則漢成帝時人，相去四百餘年，豈得為父子。 中間世次脫簡，自宋以來即有人指出。 但唐書卷七二中宰相世系表魏氏下稱歆「二子，愉、悅」，則歆有子名悅，亦有旁證。 至李孝伯之壻魏悅則魏子建之父，魏收之祖，見本書卷九一王叡附王椿傳、卷九二魏溥妻房氏傳，也無可疑。 姓纂卷八和唐書世系表所著錄的後裔都是愉之後，悅的後裔無考。 疑本有兩魏悅，一是西漢鉅鹿太守歆之子，一是北魏末魏子建之父。 由於同名，傳抄時以此悅當彼悅，脫漏中間世系。 或唐初魏書已有此誤本，其誤非必出於李延壽。
"Xin's son Yue": the North History, juan 56, omits the character "Xin." Wei Yue of the Northern Wei was Li Xiaobo's son-in-law. Wei Xin belonged to Emperor Cheng of Han's era—more than four centuries apart. They cannot have been father and son. Intermediate generations have dropped from the text. Scholars have noted the problem since the Song. Yet the Tang History's tables of chancellor lineages (juan 72) list Xin as having two sons, Yu and Yue—so a son named Yue for the Han figure is attested elsewhere. The Northern Wei Wei Yue who married Li Xiaobo was father of Wei Zijian and grandfather of Wei Shou, as recorded in juan 91 (Wang Rui, with Wang Chun) and juan 92 (Wei Pu's wife, the Fang family)—and is not in doubt. The Xingzuan and Tang genealogical tables trace only Yu's line. Yue's Han descendants are unrecorded. The likely explanation is two different men named Wei Yue: one the Han administrator Xin's son, the other the Northern Wei father of Wei Zijian. Copyists conflated the two because the name was the same, dropping the generations between them. The error may already have existed in the Tang History of Wei; it need not be Li Yanshou's fault.
13
初世宗時平氐諸本「世宗」作「世祖」，北史卷五六作「宣武」 〈即世宗元恪〉 。 按事見卷八世宗紀正始三年正月壬申、卷七０傅豎眼傳、卷一０一氐傳補，「祖」乃「宗」之訛，今據改。
"Earlier, in the time of Emperor Shizong the Di were pacified": various editions read "Shizu" for "Shizong"; the North History, juan 56, reads "Xuanwu" (that is, Emperor Shizong Yuan Ke) . The event appears in the Basic Annals of Emperor Shizong (juan 8), the biography of Fu Shuyan (juan 70), and the Di biography supplement (juan 101). "Zu" is a corruption of "zong"; the text is corrected accordingly.
14
乃遣刺史唐永代焉按刺史上無地名，不知何州刺史，據北史卷六七唐永傳但云「行臺蕭寶夤表永為南豳 〈原訛『幽』〉 州刺史」，不載代魏子建事，當是省略，或北史所據唐氏家傳諱言其事。 唐永乃由南豳遷東益，這裏「刺史」上應有「南豳州」三字。
"Thereupon Governor Tang Yong was sent to replace him": the text omits the province name. The North History biography of Tang Yong (juan 67) records only that "Mobile Secretariat Xiao Baoyin recommended Yong as governor of Southern Bin (originally miswritten "You") Province" and does not mention his replacing Wei Zijian—either an abbreviation or deliberate silence in the Tang family materials the North History used. Tang Yong went from Southern Bin to Eastern Yi. The text should read "governor of Southern Bin Province" here.
15
吾生年契闊北史卷五六「生年」作「平生」，這裏「生年」當是「生平」之訛。
"My years in life have been long and varied": the North History (juan 56) has "through life" where this text has "years in life." "Years in life" is likely a corruption of "through life."
16
帝曰北史卷五六「帝」上有「白」字，下文云云乃賈思同語。 按御覽卷六００ 〈二七０一頁〉 引北齊書也有「白」字 〈今本北齊書無〉 。 若無「白」字，則下文云云是元恭 〈後廢帝〉 語，上文何必書「賈思同侍立」？ 這裏當脫「白」字。
"The Emperor said": in the North History (juan 56) the character "reported" appears before "Emperor"; the following words are Jia Sitong's, not the emperor's. The Imperial Readings, juan 600 (folio 2701) quotes the History of the Northern Qi and likewise has "reported" (the present History of the Northern Qi text lacks it) . Without "reported," the following speech would belong to Yuan Gong (the later deposed emperor) ; then why record above that "Jia Sitong stood in attendance"? The character "reported" must have dropped out here.
17
博總斟酌南、殿、局三本及北齊書卷三七「博」作「專」，百衲本、北本、汲本作「傳」； 北史百衲本作「傳」，他本作「專」； 冊府卷五五六 〈六六七八頁〉 作「博」。 按「博總」即「博綜」，廣泛收集之意。 本作「博」，訛作「傳」，後人以「傳總」不可解，改作「專」。 今據冊府改。
"Broadly gathered and weighed": in the Southern Palace, Palace, and Bureau editions and the History of the Northern Qi (juan 37), "broad" appears as "specialized"; Baibu, Northern, and Ji editions read "transmit"; the Baibu North History reads "transmit," other editions "specialized"; the Imperial Archives, juan 556 (folio 6678) reads "broad." "Broadly gather" means "broadly synthesize"—to collect widely. The original read "broad," was corrupted to "transmit," and because "transmit and gather" made no sense, later editors changed it to "specialized." The original character was "broad," miscopied as "transmit"; since "transmit and gather" was unintelligible, editors substituted "specialized." The text is corrected here to follow the Imperial Archives.
