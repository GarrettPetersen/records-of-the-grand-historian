It was the year 99 BC, and a grim tension hung over the Han dynasty court, as word reached Chang'an of General Li Ling's defeat at the hands of the Xiongnu. When word reached Emperor Wu that Li Ling had not died in battle, but instead had surrendered to the Xiongnu, the emperor was furious. The one man in the Han court willing to defend Li Ling was his friend Sima Qian, a court historian. Emperor Wu had Sima Qian arrested, and he was given a choice: execution or castration.

Sima Qian accepted castration and imprisonment so that he could finish his life's work: The Records of the Grand Historian (Shiji, 史記), a massive, 130-chapter tome detailing the history of China from the ancient and semi-mythical Yellow Emperor to Sima Qian's own lifetime. The book was enormously influential on Chinese historiography, spawning a two-millennia-long tradition in which each subsequent dynasty would write the previous dynasty's official history, adopting the Grand Historian's structure and style.

This book is part of a new translation effort by translator and editor Garrett M. Petersen to create a comprehensive translation of all 24 official dynastic histories, as well as the Draft History of Qing (Qingshigao, 清史稿) and the Comprehensive Mirror in Aid of Governance (Zizhi Tongjian, 資治通鑑). The effort is AI-assisted, with significant human editorial oversight and tuning. New technology has made it feasible to translate the nearly 40 million combined characters of these works into clear, readable English.
