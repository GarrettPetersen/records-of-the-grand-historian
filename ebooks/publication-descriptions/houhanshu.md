Fan Ye (范曄), the author of the Book of Later Han (Hou Hanshu, 後漢書), was born in Shunyang during the Eastern Jin dynasty. The Book of Song says his mother gave birth to him in a privy, where he hit his head on a brick, earning him the childhood name, “Brick” (Zhuan, 磚).

The brick doesn't seem to have done any lasting damage, as he proved himself to be a bright child. He loved learning and reading, voraciously consuming classic texts and histories. He was skilled at writing and music. As a young adult, he worked on the staff of Liu Yu (the future emperor Wu of Liu Song). He had a series of jobs working on the staffs of various generals, even reluctantly joining a northern campaign managing the army's logistics.

As an official in the Liu Song court, Fan Ye made some dubious friendships and rivalries. One night, during the funeral procession of the Grand Consort of Pengcheng, he and a friend drank to excess, opened the windows, and amused themselves listening to the funeral dirges. When Yikang, the Prince of Pengcheng, found out about this he was furious, and demoted Fan Ye to Administrator of Xuancheng.  

With little to do in his diminished office, Fan Ye put his time to productive use. He compiled existing historical documents on the later Han Dynasty into the volume that would become the Book of Later Han, his most enduring legacy. He spent several years in this position, before eventually being promoted back into an official position.  

Some time later, Fan Ye's mother became terminally ill. He was in another city, and did not arrive in time to see her before her passing. He also brought singing girls and concubines with him when he travelled to his ailing mother. This breach of filial piety was noticed by other officials, and Fan Ye was impeached. However, the emperor chose not to punish him, valuing his talents. Fan Ye continued to advance in his career as an imperial official, though perhaps not to the heights of his ambitions.

It was at this point that Fan Ye made a dangerous friend: Kong Xixian. Xixian, whose family was quite wealthy, gambled with Fan Ye and lost on purpose to ingratiate himself with Ye. Fan Ye enjoyed Xixian's company and admired his literary talent. The two men became close friends.  
  
But Xixian had an ulterior motive. His family had a connection to the Prince of Pengcheng, Yikang, who had been demoted after years of dominating the court. Xixian wanted to settle the score, and he sought to recruit Fan Ye as a co-conspirator. He pointed out to Fan Ye that, despite his esteemed lineage, the imperial family would not intermarry with Fan Ye's family. This point hit home, and Fan Ye resolved to act against the throne.
