The Book of Han (Hanshu, 漢書) was a family affair. Ban Biao, a prominent Han dynasty official, began compiling a supplement to Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian (Shiji, 史記). He compiled many annals and biographies, but died in 54 AD with the work unfinished. His son, Ban Gu, took over the project, compiling most of the remaining chapters.

Several years into the project, the imperial court took notice of Ban Gu's private historical project. Concerned by this unauthorized private history of the dynasty, the court had Ban Gu arrested. Fortunately, Ban Gu's younger brother Ban Chao interceded. The court relented, released Ban Gu, and gave him a position in the imperial library. Eventually, Emperor Ming allowed him to continue his history of the Western Han.

Ban Gu continued his work on the history over the next few decades, while also boasting an impressive bureaucratic career. But in 92 AD, his fortune took another turn. The Dou family, relatives of the Dowager Empress, were purged en masse. Ban Gu was closely associated with the general Dou Xian, and was arrested as part of the general purge of Dou family associates. He died in prison, his historical work unfinished.

Finally, a third member of the Ban family took up the reins. Ban Gu's younger sister Ban Zhao, herself a gifted scholar, continued working on Hanshu for the next 19 years. She is traditionally credited with the chronological tables and the treatise on astronomy (with assistance from Ma Xu).

Hanshu began as a continuation of Sima Qian's Shiji, picking up the history where the Grand Historian had left off. It was this, the second book in the Twenty-Four Histories, that turned a single historical work into an established tradition. Subsequent dynasties after the Han would build on this established practice by writing the official history of the previous dynasty. This tradition would continue for well over a thousand years, even into the 20th century with the Draft History of Qing (Qingshigao, 清史稿).

This English edition of Hanshu is part of a new translation effort by translator and editor Garrett M. Petersen to create a comprehensive translation of all 24 official dynastic histories, as well as the Draft History of Qing (Qingshigao, 清史稿) and the Comprehensive Mirror in Aid of Governance (Zizhi Tongjian, 資治通鑑). The effort is AI-assisted, with significant human editorial oversight and tuning. New technology has made it feasible to translate the nearly 40 million combined Chinese characters of these works into clear, readable English.
