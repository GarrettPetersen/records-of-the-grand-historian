The Book of Jin (Jinshu, 晉書) contains a detailed biography of Chen Shou, who authored the Records of the Three Kingdoms (Sanguo Zhi, 三國志). He was said to love books from a young age, becoming a clerk in the imperial library of Shu-Han. His early career was mired in controversy. When the eunuch Huang Hao dominated the Shu-Han court, Chen Shou refused to ingratiate himself with him, and was repeatedly demoted and censured. Later, when Chen Shou's father died, he himself fell ill, and he violated ritual by having a servant girl prepare his medicine. Others found out about this, and Chen Shou was disgraced. His reputation would not recover for several years, even outliving the Shu-Han dynasty, which was invaded and toppled by Wei dynasty forces.

Fortunately for Chen Shou, the Minister of Works Zhang Hua admired his talent. Zhang Hua declared him filial and incorrupt, appointed him as assistant drafter, and made him a magistrate. As assistant drafter, Chen Shou compiled the works of Zhuge Liang and presented them to the court. This earned him a promotion to drafter.

In this role, Chen Shou wrote the Records of the Three Kingdoms, consisting of 65 chapters containing biographies of important figures who shaped the history of the Three Kingdoms period. Chen Shou's contemporaries greatly admired his work. It was so good that, when Xiahou Zhan read Chen Shou's history, he tore up his own draft history of Wei and quit.

Chen Shou lived through the tail end of the Three Kingdoms period and wrote his history in the immediate aftermath, when sources were fresh and many key actors were still alive. Later, during the 5th century, Pei Songzhi revisited the work, adding commentary, annotations, and references to other sources, many of which are now lost. His annotations are marked 〈like this〉 in this edition. Pei Songzhi's additions increased Sanguo Zhi's value as a historical source.

In addition to being an important historical work, Sanguo Zhi became important through its influence on literature. Sanguo Zhi provided the historical foundation for the historical novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms by Luo Guanzhong during the early Ming dynasty. Romance adapts and embellishes Sanguo Zhi to create an epic drama full of intrigue, action, and larger-than-life characters. This novel was extremely influential on Chinese literature, becoming one of the Four Great Classical Novels of Chinese literature. Romance has spawned many adaptations in the modern day, from movies and TV shows to video games.

This English edition of Sanguo Zhi is part of a new translation effort by translator and editor Garrett M. Petersen to create a comprehensive translation of all 24 official dynastic histories, as well as the Draft History of Qing (Qingshigao, 清史稿) and the Comprehensive Mirror in Aid of Governance (Zizhi Tongjian, 資治通鑑). The effort is AI-assisted, with significant human editorial oversight and tuning. New technology has made it feasible to translate the nearly 40 million combined Chinese characters of these works into clear, readable English.
