# Person Extraction Assignment

You are extracting every named human individual and every explicit reference to
them from one chapter of a Chinese historical text. This is evidence capture,
not identity resolution. Work only from the supplied packet. Do not browse,
infer facts from general knowledge, or create canonical cross-chapter
identities.

This is also a final editorial pass. When the supplied English contains a clear
translation or prose error, record a proposed repair in `translationRepairs`.
Do not directly edit a chapter file. The host validates and applies proposed
repairs only after the extraction itself passes validation.

## Files

- `packet.json` contains every Chinese/English content unit, stable locators,
  preflight candidates, roles, polities, and reign periods supplied for this
  chapter.
- `extraction.schema.json` is the exact output schema.
- `extraction.seed.json` contains the fixed envelope and current fingerprints.
- Write the completed result to the output path named by the assignment. Do not
  modify source chapters, commit, push, open a pull request, or modify another
  tracked file.

## People

Record every named human, including rulers, officials, generals, scholars,
authors, consorts, servants, religious figures, rebels, merchants, and named
commoners. Include historical, legendary, literary, and uncertain people, and
preserve that status.

Do not create people for unnamed roles, groups, clans, armies, offices, places,
books, reign periods, or nonhuman deities. A pronoun can support a claim but is
not a linkable mention. When identity is uncertain, create separate local
people and describe the uncertainty. Do not merge two people merely because
they share a short name.

Assign local people in first-mention order as `<book>:<chapter>:p001`, `p002`,
and so on. A concise `descriptorSuggestion` should use the supplied broad role
vocabulary and say only what the chapter supports, for example `Historian and
Official` or `Maid`. Exact offices belong in claims.

Every local person requires at least one `name` claim and one `role` claim.
When no narrower role is established, use the supplied `named-individual` role
rather than inventing an occupation. A person's preferred-name suggestion does
not replace the required structured name claim.

## Mentions And Spans

Record every explicit occurrence, not just the first. Link full names, shortened
names, courtesy and alternate names, temple and posthumous names, and titles
used as unique references. Do not link pronouns.

Use one mention object for a referring expression in one unit. When Chinese and
English expressions in that unit refer to the same person, put both languages
on the same mention. Give only exact visible text and its zero-based occurrence
within that unit. The host computes offsets and hashes. Spans in one language
must not overlap. Prefer the longest clear expression, such as `Prince of
Pengcheng Yikang`, rather than nested mentions for `Yikang` and `Prince`.

Assign mentions in reading order as `<book>:<chapter>:m0001`, `m0002`, and so
on. Copy the complete unit locator from `packet.json`.

## Preflight Candidates

Candidates are hints, not assertions that something is a person. Every
candidate ID must be accounted for exactly once:

- If it falls inside a person mention, put its ID in that mention's
  `candidateRefs`. Chinese and English candidates from the same unit can both
  point to one bilingual mention. A candidate may be shorter than the chosen
  longest mention span as long as that span contains it.
- Otherwise add one `candidateDispositions` entry. Use `not-person` for a clear
  place, office, book, collective, deity, reign period, polity, or ordinary
  capitalized word. Use `ambiguous` or `unresolved` only when context genuinely
  does not establish the referent. Use `duplicate-hint` only when two scanner
  hints describe the same non-mention candidate.

Do not turn a false-positive candidate into a person merely to reduce work.

## Claims

Capture all identity-relevant facts established by the chapter:

- personal, surname, given, courtesy, childhood, religious, alternate, changed,
  temple, posthumous, regnal, and title names;
- explicit sex, native place, lineage, relationships, occupations, broad roles,
  offices, noble titles, enfeoffments, and authorship;
- explicit polity associations and the nature of the association;
- births, deaths, stated ages, accessions, depositions, renamings, and explicit
  same-person statements;
- uncertainty, contextual dates, textual variants, and conflicting claims.

Assign claims as `<book>:<chapter>:c0001`, `c0002`, and so on. Every claim needs
one or more evidence IDs formatted `<book>:<chapter>:<unit-id>`. Keep claim
values structured. A name value should contain its name kind plus available
English, Chinese, and pinyin forms. A role value uses a supplied `roleId`.
Record each distinct fact once: when several units support the same fact, put
all of their evidence IDs on one claim instead of duplicating the claim.

Never convert dates from memory. Use a supplied reign ID only when it is the
correct polity and reign. Otherwise retain the source date text and mark the
reign unresolved without inventing an ID. Record stated age and reckoning; do
not derive a birth year. Do not assign a polity from lifespan or chapter alone.

## Editorial Repairs

Read the Chinese and both English fields closely. Propose a repair when there is
a clear semantic error, omitted or invented content, reversed relationship,
wrong referent or pronoun, mistranslated office or name, fused or corrupted
word, grammatical failure, or English so unnatural that it obscures the
meaning. Preserve accurate human-written phrasing and do not rewrite merely to
impose a different style.

For every repair:

- identify the exact unit locator and either the `idiomatic` or `literal` field;
- copy the complete current field into `before` and the complete replacement
  field into `after`;
- explain the source-based reason specifically enough for editorial review;
- use `high` confidence when the correction is unambiguous and `medium` only
  when the problem is clear but the best English wording admits alternatives;
- set `status` to `proposed`;
- create at most one repair per unit and field.

All mentions and candidate decisions must still describe the supplied packet's
current text, not the proposed replacement. The host rebuilds the packet and
reconciles changed units after applying repairs. Leave `translationRepairs` as
an empty array when the chapter needs no changes.

## Final Audit

Before finishing:

1. Read every unit in both languages.
2. Confirm every named human has a local person and every explicit occurrence
   has a nonoverlapping mention.
3. Confirm aliases, relationships, dates, ages, roles, offices, and polity clues
   were captured where stated.
4. Confirm every candidate is referenced by one mention or has one disposition.
5. Confirm every local person has structured name and role claims, and audit all
   proposed translation repairs against the Chinese.
6. Set both coverage booleans to `true` and record genuinely unresolved
   references rather than guessing.
7. Validate the shape against `extraction.schema.json` and write only valid JSON
   to the assigned output path, with no Markdown fence or commentary.
