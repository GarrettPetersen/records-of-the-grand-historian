# Compact Person Extraction Assignment

Extract every named human individual and every explicit reference to them from
one chapter of a Chinese historical text. This is chapter-local evidence
capture, not cross-chapter identity resolution. Work only from the supplied
worker packet. Do not browse or infer facts from general knowledge.

This is also a final editorial pass. Record clear translation or prose errors
as structured repair proposals. Never edit the source chapter directly.

## Inputs And Output

- The worker packet has compact arrays for every Chinese/English content unit,
  every deterministic candidate hint, allowed roles, polities, and reigns.
- Unit rows are `[unitId, kindCode, chinese, idiomatic, literal]`, where kind is
  `p` (paragraph sentence), `h` (table header), or `t` (table body cell).
- Candidate rows are
  `[candidateId, unitId, language, exact, occurrence, detectorKinds]`.
- A version 2 packet is one chunk of a larger chapter. Its `units` and
  `candidates` are the complete owned assignment. `readOnlyContext.before` and
  `.after` provide neighboring unit rows solely to resolve continuations and
  references. Read them, but never emit their mentions, claims, evidence,
  candidate dispositions, or translation repairs. A person named in owned units
  remains in scope even when context supplies earlier or later facts. A person
  named only in read-only context also remains in scope when an owned unit
  refers to that person by pronoun, silent subject, or quotation continuation.
  Create a local person with `x` explaining the boundary-only reference, emit
  no visible surface for the pronoun, and cite only owned units. Do not create a
  person who is neither named nor semantically referenced in an owned unit.
- The compact schema is the exact output contract.
- The seeded output has immutable input fingerprints and run metadata.

Replace the seed with one valid compact extraction. Modify no other tracked
file. Do not commit, push, run `gh`, or open a pull request.

Work economically. Read only this prompt, the assigned worker packet, the
compact schema, and the assigned seed. Do not inspect another chapter's
extraction, validator or library source code, Git history, or unrelated files.
Do not browse. Do not generate ad hoc inventory or occurrence-counting programs;
the packet already contains the complete assignment. Write the output directly,
validate it, and use validation diagnostics for any necessary correction.

## People Rows

Record every named human: rulers, officials, generals, scholars, authors,
consorts, servants, religious figures, rebels, merchants, named commoners, and
historical, legendary, literary, or uncertain people. A unique title can name a
person. Also create a person for a uniquely individuated unnamed relative such
as "his mother" or "the eldest daughter" when the chapter establishes whose
relative it is; use a source-grounded display label such as `Mother of Fan Ye`,
a `descriptive-kinship` name claim, and the visible kinship expression as a
`kinship-reference` surface. Do not create separate people for an
undifferentiated plural such as "three sons"; preserve it in `family-summary`.
Do not create people for other unnamed roles, groups, clans, armies, offices,
places, books, reigns, or nonhuman deities. Pronouns are not linkable surfaces.

Assign people in first-mention order as `p001`, `p002`, and so on. Each row is:

```text
[id, [preferredEnglish, preferredChinese, pinyin], historicity, descriptor,
 hints, names, roles]
```

Use `null` for an unavailable preferred-name field. `historicity` is one of
`historical`, `legendary`, `uncertain`, or `literary`. The concise descriptor
uses supplied broad roles, such as `Historian and Official` or `Maid`.

Hints always have all five keys:

```json
{"n": ["native places"], "r": ["p002"], "a": ["active-date hints"],
 "p": ["polity hints"], "x": null}
```

`x` is a mention exception and normally remains `null`.
Put every local person connected by a `family-relationship` or another direct
personal relationship in `r` on both people. These hints do not replace the
structured claims; they make the later cross-chapter identity dossier usable.

Every person must have at least one source-supported active-date hint in `a`.
Every historical or uncertain person must have a Western year or bounded
interval such as `AD 445` or `AD 445-446`; `conversion unresolved` is not a
complete result. Use packet chronology when available. If it is missing,
verify the conversion against an authoritative chronology with available
research tools and preserve the source date text without inventing a reign ID.
If a safe conversion still cannot be established, fail loudly instead of
asserting `allChronologyCaptured`. Use a qualitative marker such as `legendary
antiquity` only for genuinely undatable legendary or literary figures. These
are attested activity dates, not birth/death dates or asserted lifespans.
The date of a later author's allusion, genealogy, quotation, or retrospective
comparison is not an activity date for the person being recalled. Never make
Han Fei active in the sixth century because a sixth-century letter mentions
him, or keep a dead parent active because the child's biography later names
that parent. Identify the alluded person and independently verify a Western
year or bounded interval from that person's own historical setting; cite the
allusive unit as evidence and mark the inference honestly. If the identity or
historical interval cannot be established safely, fail loudly for research
rather than copying the surrounding narrative date.

Every person requires one or more structured name rows and role rows. A name
row is `[valueObject, certainty, [evidenceUnitIds]]`; its value includes `kind`
plus available `en`, `zh`, and `pinyin`. Capture personal, surname, clan or
house, given, courtesy, childhood, style, nickname, epithet, religious or
dharma, alternate, changed or taboo-avoidance, native-language or
transliterated, temple, posthumous, regnal, title, and descriptive-kinship
names. Preserve the
source's characters and English spelling; add pinyin only when established. A
role row is
`[roleId, certainty, [evidenceUnitIds]]`. Use `named-individual` only when the
chapter establishes no narrower role. Do not duplicate a name or role; combine
all supporting evidence on one row.

## Surface Rows

Record every visible Chinese and English referring expression. Prefer the
longest clear expression and never create overlapping surfaces in one language.
Include full and shortened names, courtesy and alternate names, temple and
posthumous names, and titles or kinship expressions that uniquely identify a
person. Do not include pronouns.

Each surface row is:

```text
[personId, mentionKind, language, exactText,
 [[unitId, [zeroBasedOccurrences]], ...]]
```

Allowed mention kinds are in the schema. Group repeated use of the same exact
surface for one person into one row. List every unit and occurrence where it is
a person reference. If an exact spelling is ambiguous between people, create
separate rows with only the correct units and occurrences. The host regenerates
individual link spans, offsets, hashes, and locators from these rules.

## Candidate Coverage

Candidates are overinclusive hints, not assertions. The host automatically
accounts for a candidate when its exact range is contained in a surface for the
same unit and language. Every other candidate must occur exactly once in a
disposition group:

```text
[disposition, reason, [[candidateId, noteOrNull], ...]]
```

Use the schema's disposition and reason codes. Do not turn a place, office,
book, polity, reign, collective, ordinary capitalized phrase, or other false
positive into a person merely to avoid a disposition. Use `ambiguous` or
`unresolved` only when the chapter truly does not establish the referent.

## Other Claims

Top-level claim rows contain facts other than names and broad roles:

```text
[subjectPersonId, predicate, valueObject, certainty, [evidenceUnitIds]]
```

Capture every durable person fact that the chapter states or strongly
establishes. This includes:

- identity: sex, ethnicity or people-group, historicity, lineage, and every
  same-person, different-person, or renaming statement;
- family: ancestry and descent, generation distance, paternal or maternal line,
  birth order, parent/child, sibling, marriage or betrothal, in-law and other
  kinship, including biological/adoptive/foster/step parentage, half-siblings,
  and every explicit family link. Every link between individuated people must
  be a `family-relationship` edge containing the other local `personId`, never
  a relative's name stored only as text. Create the other person row even when
  that relative appears nowhere else. Use `family-summary` for unnamed plural,
  counted, unknown-count, or explicitly absent relatives;

  A `family-summary` value must include a nonempty string `kinshipRole` and
  either a nonnegative integer `count` or a nonempty string `quantity`. For an
  unknown count, write `"quantity": "unspecified"`; do not encode quantity as
  an object such as `{ "unspecified": true }`.
- non-family relationships: teacher/student, patron/client,
  recommender/protege, alliance, rivalry, succession, and every other explicit
  relation between named people;
- career and standing: exact occupation; every office appointment, acting or
  concurrent tenure, promotion, transfer, demotion, suspension, dismissal,
  resignation, retirement, recall, and restoration; jurisdiction, grade or
  salary rank when stated; noble title, enfeoffment, rank, honor, privilege,
  social or legal status, punishment, pardon, captivity, hostage status,
  enslavement, manumission, and exile;
- geography and affiliation: birthplace, native or ancestral place, residence,
  posting, travel, migration, exile, death, burial or tomb place, polity
  association, and membership in a named clan, faction, school, religious
  community, military unit, household, or institution;
- learning and belief: teachers and students, subjects studied, educational
  institutions, examinations, degrees and other credentials, stated religious
  or philosophical commitments, languages, arts, crafts, and other skills;
- life and chronology: birth, death and its cause, manner, place, executioner
  or responsible authority, treatment of the body, burial and tomb, stated age
  and age reckoning, accession, deposition, and every source-supported activity
  date or interval;
- intellectual and attributed material: authorship, compilation, editing,
  commentary, translation, sponsorship, transmission, or another relation to a
  named work; and every embedded speech, memorial, letter, edict, poem,
  prediction, judgment, or other document, with its speaker/author, addressee,
  quoted or paraphrased status, and full owned evidence-unit extent;
- events: every substantive person-centered action or experience not already
  represented by a narrower claim, including military, diplomatic, political,
  legal, ritual, travel, disaster, medical, scholarly, and personal events.
  Preserve each named participant's role, named counterparts, place,
  chronology, and outcome when stated;
- attributed assessments: praise, blame, reputation, character judgments, and
  evaluations stated by the historian, commentator, quoted source, or another
  named person. Preserve the assessor or witness instead of treating an opinion
  as an unqualified fact;
- material associations: source-significant estates, residences, wealth,
  regalia, seals, weapons, artworks, relics, tombs, and other named or
  identifying objects possessed, made, bestowed, seized, or used;
- source criticism: explicit textual variants, alternative reports,
  disagreements, uncertain identifications, and claims one witness accepts or
  rejects; and
- uncommon identifying facts: a source-stated physical feature, illness,
  disability, or other concise biographical attribute that could aid
  disambiguation or a future person page and fits no narrower predicate.

Use `other-person-fact` as the final safety valve for any source-stated fact
about a named person that has lasting biographical, chronological, relational,
geographic, or interpretive value but fits none of the predicates above. Do not
discard a fact merely because this prompt failed to anticipate its category.
Capture explicit negative facts such as having no heir, never holding office,
or refusing an appointment; represent the negation in the value rather than
turning absence of a claim into a claim of absence.

Use `biographical-attribute` only as the final category, not for general praise,
abuse, or a summary of every incident. Record each distinct fact once and
combine supporting evidence. Local-person references inside values use short
IDs such as `p002`; `same-person` and `different-person` values must use
`{"personId":"p002",...}` and must connect two distinct local records. Put
aliases for one local record in its evidence-backed name rows or `renamed`
claims rather than making a self-referential identity claim.

Family edges use one of these canonical directions: `parent-of`, `child-of`,
`sibling-of`, `spouse-of`, `betrothed-to`, `ancestor-of`, `descendant-of`, or
`kin-of`. Every edge must use `personId` to target another local person object.
Preserve the source term as `kinshipTerm: {"zh":...,"en":...}` and add all
stated structured details: `parentage` (`biological`, `adoptive`, `foster`,
`step`, `claimed`, or `uncertain`), a free-text `unionType`, `unionCategory`
(`marriage`, `primary-marriage`, `secondary-marriage`, `concubinage`,
`betrothal`, `levirate`, `sororate`, `other`, or `uncertain`), `subjectRole`,
`objectRole`, `generationDistance`, `line` (`paternal` or `maternal`),
`sharedParentage` (`full`, `paternal-half`, `maternal-half`,
`half-unspecified`, `step`, `adoptive`, or `uncertain`),
`subjectRelativeAge` (`elder`, `younger`, `twin`, or `uncertain`), numeric
`subjectBirthOrder` and `objectBirthOrder`, and `relationshipState` (`formed`,
`active`, `ended`, `divorced`, `annulled`, `widowed`, or `uncertain`). Add
`intermediaryPersonIds` when the source identifies people through whom a more
distant relation runs.

Make the graph structurally complete wherever the kinship term logically
entails intermediate people. For example, an assertion that A is B's maternal
uncle entails B's mother and her sibling relationship to A; a grandson entails
one intervening parent. Create anonymous-individuated local person rows for those
unstated intermediaries, connect the direct parent/sibling edges, and mark the
entailed records and edges `strongly-inferred`. Do not invent an intermediary
when the kinship path is genuinely ambiguous; retain the exact `kin-of` or
ancestor/descendant edge instead. Give each inferred intermediary the same
source-context attestation window as the relationship that establishes them.

The compiler groups all assertions of the same structural relationship into a
single stable canonical family edge, derives reciprocal adjacency links on the
two person objects, and preserves every assertion's evidence and metadata. Do
not emit a redundant inverse claim unless the source independently states it.
Conflicting genealogies remain separate assertions with their own provenance
and `conflict` links. Record each stated parent-child edge separately; do not
infer parentage merely because someone is a parent's spouse. For remarriage,
divorce, widowhood, concubinage, adoption, or another time-varying family
state, retain each dated edge or change independently with `dateContext`,
`startDate`, or `endDate` rather than flattening it into a timeless relation.

Use `place-association`, `status`, `office`, `noble-title`, `enfeoffment`,
`honor`, `legal-action`, `polity-association`, `organization-association`,
`family-relationship`, `family-summary`, `relationship`, `education`,
`credential`, `belief-association`, `skill`,
`work-association`, `event-participation`, `assessment`, and
`material-association` for the corresponding facts.
Whenever the chapter supplies chronology, include `dateContext`, `startDate`,
or `endDate` in the value and preserve the source date. Do not flatten a dated
sequence of appointments, demotions, moves, marriages, captures, or releases
into one timeless claim.

Use `attribution` for every source-grounded speech, document, quotation, or
action attribution, including when the name is visible. Cite every owned unit
belonging to a multi-unit speech or document; neighboring chunks contribute
their owned parts so identity resolution can recover the complete boundaries.
It is especially important when a unit uses a pronoun, silent subject,
quotation continuation, or disputed referent. This records attribution as a
fact; it does not create a clickable pronoun surface.

Every claim presented as a quotation, self-report, hearsay, allegation, rumor,
prophecy, dream, omen, historian's judgment, commentator's judgment,
named-source report, or rejected alternative must include a `provenance`
object. Use a compact `mode` plus `sourcePersonId`, `witnessPersonId`,
`sourceWork`, or `speakerPersonId` when supplied. Ordinary unqualified
narrative assertions need no provenance object. Keep an explicit `negated`
boolean and exact source quantities wherever those distinctions matter.

Keep values regular enough to compile later. Use these shapes and add only
source-supported fields:

```json
{"relation":"teacher-of","personId":"p002"}
{"relation":"parent-of","personId":"p002","kinshipTerm":{"zh":"父","en":"father"},"parentage":"biological","subjectRole":"father","objectRole":"son"}
{"kinshipRole":"sons","count":3}
{"kinshipRole":"sons","count":0,"negated":true}
{"label":{"en":"court astrologer","zh":"太史令"}}
{"place":{"en":"Xuancheng","zh":"宣城"},"relation":"posted-to"}
{"status":"exile","action":"entered"}
{"title":{"en":"Prince of Pengcheng","zh":"彭城王"},"action":"held"}
{"polityId":"liu-song","relation":"served"}
{"organization":{"en":"...","zh":"..."},"relation":"member-of"}
{"credential":{"en":"Presented Scholar","zh":"進士"},"action":"earned"}
{"belief":{"en":"Buddhism","zh":"佛法"},"relation":"adherent-of"}
{"skill":{"en":"calligraphy","zh":"書"},"level":"renowned"}
{"work":{"en":"Book of Later Han","zh":"後漢書"},"relation":"author"}
{"kind":"memorial","role":"author","otherPersonId":"p002"}
{"kind":"military","role":"commander","action":"defeated","otherPersonId":"p002"}
{"assessment":"incorruptible","provenance":{"mode":"historian-judgment"}}
{"item":{"en":"bronze seal","zh":"銅印"},"relation":"bestowed"}
{"field":"death-date","statement":"...","witness":"..."}
{"kind":"physical-description","statement":"blind in one eye"}
```

The second shape is for `occupation`; `office`, titles, honors, and legal actions
use the same localized-label principle plus an `action`. Relationship names may
be precise (`father-of`, `elder-sister-of`, `wife-of`, `teacher-of`,
`predecessor-of`) rather than flattened to generic kinship. The resolver can
derive inverse relations later. Use `textual-variant` for an alternate reading
or report and `conflict` for incompatible claims; include the named witness when
the chapter gives one, using `witnessPersonId` when that witness has a local
person row. Legal actions may similarly use `authorityPersonId`.

Every temporal field above contains an attestation-shaped object: `sourceDate`
plus `westernYear` or `westernInterval`, an unresolved `sourceDate`, or a
genuinely qualitative chronology. Do not put an unstructured date string in
`dateContext`, `startDate`, or `endDate`.

Preserve all date detail supplied by the text: reign and regnal year, season,
lunar month, intercalary month, day of month, sexagenary day, relative sequence,
and whether a date comes from a table heading or inherited narrative context.
Do not invent a Gregorian month or day. When one event gives a start and a later
unit gives its end, cite both and retain the interval rather than reducing it to
one year.

Every person also requires at least one `attestation` claim. Its evidence must
point to the mention or event and to any earlier unit supplying its date
context. Use one of these value shapes:

```json
{"sourceDate":{"text":"元嘉二十二年","reignId":"liu-song-yuanjia","regnalYear":22},"westernYear":{"era":"AD","year":445,"precision":"year"}}
{"sourceDate":{"text":"元朔中"},"unresolved":true}
{"qualitative":"legendary antiquity"}
```

For a bounded interval, use `westernInterval` with `start` and `end` Western
year objects. Record an attestation for every distinct dated narrative context
in which the person appears, even after that person already has one. Combine
repeated mentions only when they share the same date context. The resolver will
derive the earliest/latest attested interval across chapters. Never describe
that derived interval as a lifespan.

For a retrospective allusion, "dated narrative context" means the historical
setting of the person alluded to, not the date when the later speaker, author,
or biographical subject invoked that person's name. The same rule applies to a
deceased ancestor named only to explain a living descendant.

Never convert dates from memory. Use a supplied reign ID only when its polity
and reign are correct. When the packet lacks a mapping, independently verify a
Western year or interval and omit `reignId`; unresolved source dates do not
satisfy this prompt. Record stated age and reckoning but do not derive a birth
year. Never assign a polity from lifespan or chapter alone. Preserve competing
dates as separate claims joined by `textual-variant` or `conflict`; do not
choose one silently.

This is a complete person-centered source pass, not a separate canonical event
database. Every linkable mention retains its exact source unit for current
bilingual snippets. Use a narrower claim for an appointment, marriage, exile,
death, work, or other modeled fact. Also add `event-participation` whenever a
substantive action or experience would otherwise survive only as
undifferentiated prose. Do not omit such an event because it seems minor or
because the person already has another claim. Do not invent event names or
duplicate the source as an AI-written narrative summary.

## Editorial Repairs

Read the Chinese and both English fields. Propose a repair for a clear semantic
error, omission or invention, reversed relationship, wrong referent or pronoun,
mistranslated office or name, fused/corrupted word, grammatical failure, or
English so unnatural that it obscures the meaning. Preserve accurate
human-written phrasing; do not rewrite merely to impose a different style.

Each repair row is:

```text
[unitId, field, completeBefore, completeAfter, sourceBasedReason,
 confidence, "proposed"]
```

`field` is `idiomatic` or `literal`; confidence is `high` or `medium`. Create at
most one repair per unit and field. All surfaces still describe the packet's
current text, not the proposed replacement. The host applies proposals in an
isolated second phase, rebuilds candidates, and reconciles changed surfaces.
