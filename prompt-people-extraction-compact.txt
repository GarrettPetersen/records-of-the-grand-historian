# Compact Person Extraction Assignment

Extract every named human individual and every explicit reference to them from
one chapter of a Chinese historical text. This is chapter-local evidence
capture, not cross-chapter identity resolution. Work only from the supplied
worker packet. Do not browse or infer facts from general knowledge.

This is also a final editorial pass. Record clear translation or prose errors
as structured repair proposals. Never edit the source chapter directly.

## Inputs And Output

- The worker packet has compact arrays for every Chinese/English content unit,
  every deterministic candidate hint, allowed roles, polities, and reigns.
- Unit rows are `[unitId, kindCode, chinese, idiomatic, literal]`, where kind is
  `p` (paragraph sentence), `h` (table header), or `t` (table body cell).
- Candidate rows are
  `[candidateId, unitId, language, exact, occurrence, detectorKinds]`.
- A version 2 packet is one chunk of a larger chapter. Its `units` and
  `candidates` are the complete owned assignment. `readOnlyContext.before` and
  `.after` provide neighboring unit rows solely to resolve continuations and
  references. Read them, but never emit their mentions, claims, evidence,
  candidate dispositions, or translation repairs. Do not create a person seen
  only in read-only context. A person named in owned units remains in scope even
  when the context shows earlier or later information about the same person.
- The compact schema is the exact output contract.
- The seeded output has immutable input fingerprints and run metadata.

Replace the seed with one valid compact extraction. Modify no other tracked
file. Do not commit, push, run `gh`, or open a pull request.

Work economically. Read only this prompt, the assigned worker packet, the
compact schema, and the assigned seed. Do not inspect another chapter's
extraction, validator or library source code, Git history, or unrelated files.
Do not browse. Do not generate ad hoc inventory or occurrence-counting programs;
the packet already contains the complete assignment. Write the output directly,
validate it, and use validation diagnostics for any necessary correction.

## People Rows

Record every named human: rulers, officials, generals, scholars, authors,
consorts, servants, religious figures, rebels, merchants, named commoners, and
historical, legendary, literary, or uncertain people. A unique title can name a
person. Do not create people for unnamed roles, groups, clans, armies, offices,
places, books, reigns, or nonhuman deities. Pronouns are not linkable surfaces.

Assign people in first-mention order as `p001`, `p002`, and so on. Each row is:

```text
[id, [preferredEnglish, preferredChinese, pinyin], historicity, descriptor,
 hints, names, roles]
```

Use `null` for an unavailable preferred-name field. `historicity` is one of
`historical`, `legendary`, `uncertain`, or `literary`. The concise descriptor
uses supplied broad roles, such as `Historian and Official` or `Maid`.

Hints always have all five keys:

```json
{"n": ["native places"], "r": ["p002"], "a": ["active-date hints"],
 "p": ["polity hints"], "x": null}
```

`x` is a mention exception and normally remains `null`.

Every person must have at least one source-supported active-date hint in `a`.
Prefer a Western year or interval such as `AD 445` or `AD 445-446`. If the
packet chronology cannot safely convert the source date, retain a useful source
context such as `Yuanshuo era (conversion unresolved)` or `during Emperor
Xuan's reign (conversion unresolved)`. Use a qualitative marker such as
`legendary antiquity` only when the text is genuinely undatable. These are
attested activity dates, not birth/death dates or asserted lifespans.

Every person requires one or more structured name rows and role rows. A name
row is `[valueObject, certainty, [evidenceUnitIds]]`; its value includes `kind`
plus available `en`, `zh`, and `pinyin`. Capture personal, surname, given,
courtesy, childhood, religious, alternate, changed, temple, posthumous, regnal,
and title names. A role row is
`[roleId, certainty, [evidenceUnitIds]]`. Use `named-individual` only when the
chapter establishes no narrower role. Do not duplicate a name or role; combine
all supporting evidence on one row.

## Surface Rows

Record every visible Chinese and English referring expression. Prefer the
longest clear expression and never create overlapping surfaces in one language.
Include full and shortened names, courtesy and alternate names, temple and
posthumous names, and titles or kinship expressions that uniquely identify a
person. Do not include pronouns.

Each surface row is:

```text
[personId, mentionKind, language, exactText,
 [[unitId, [zeroBasedOccurrences]], ...]]
```

Allowed mention kinds are in the schema. Group repeated use of the same exact
surface for one person into one row. List every unit and occurrence where it is
a person reference. If an exact spelling is ambiguous between people, create
separate rows with only the correct units and occurrences. The host regenerates
individual link spans, offsets, hashes, and locators from these rules.

## Candidate Coverage

Candidates are overinclusive hints, not assertions. The host automatically
accounts for a candidate when its exact range is contained in a surface for the
same unit and language. Every other candidate must occur exactly once in a
disposition group:

```text
[disposition, reason, [[candidateId, noteOrNull], ...]]
```

Use the schema's disposition and reason codes. Do not turn a place, office,
book, polity, reign, collective, ordinary capitalized phrase, or other false
positive into a person merely to avoid a disposition. Use `ambiguous` or
`unresolved` only when the chapter truly does not establish the referent.

## Other Claims

Top-level claim rows contain facts other than names and broad roles:

```text
[subjectPersonId, predicate, valueObject, certainty, [evidenceUnitIds]]
```

Capture every durable person fact that the chapter states or strongly
establishes. This includes:

- identity: sex, ethnicity or people-group, historicity, lineage, and every
  same-person, different-person, or renaming statement;
- relationships: kinship, marriage, adoption, teacher/student, patron/client,
  succession, and other named personal relationships;
- career and standing: exact occupation, office and its appointment/removal,
  noble title, enfeoffment, rank, honor, social or legal status, punishment,
  pardon, captivity, hostage status, enslavement, manumission, and exile;
- geography and affiliation: native place, residence, posting, travel, exile,
  death or burial place, polity association, and membership in a named clan,
  faction, school, religious community, or institution;
- life and chronology: birth, death and its cause/manner/place, stated age,
  accession, deposition, and every source-supported activity date or interval;
- intellectual and attributed material: authorship or another relation to a
  named work, and attribution of a speech, memorial, letter, poem, prediction,
  judgment, or consequential action when identifying its speaker, author,
  addressee, agent, or patient is useful or not obvious from a linkable name;
- source criticism: explicit textual variants, alternative reports,
  disagreements, uncertain identifications, and claims one witness accepts or
  rejects; and
- uncommon identifying facts: a source-stated physical feature, illness,
  disability, skill, or other concise biographical attribute that could aid
  disambiguation or a future person page and fits no narrower predicate.

Use `biographical-attribute` only as the final category, not for general praise,
abuse, or a summary of every incident. Record each distinct fact once and
combine supporting evidence. Local-person references inside values use short
IDs such as `p002`; `same-person` and `different-person` values must use
`{"personId":"p002",...}` and must connect two distinct local records. Put
aliases for one local record in its evidence-backed name rows or `renamed`
claims rather than making a self-referential identity claim.

Use `place-association`, `status`, `office`, `noble-title`, `enfeoffment`,
`honor`, `legal-action`, `polity-association`, `organization-association`,
`relationship`, and `work-association` for facts that can change over time.
Whenever the chapter supplies chronology, include `dateContext`, `startDate`,
or `endDate` in the value and preserve the source date. Do not flatten a dated
sequence of appointments, demotions, moves, marriages, captures, or releases
into one timeless claim.

Use `attribution` for source-grounded authorship, speech, or action attribution
that would otherwise be lost because the relevant unit uses a pronoun, silent
subject, quotation continuation, or disputed referent. It records attribution
as a fact; it does not create a clickable pronoun surface.

Keep values regular enough to compile later. Use these shapes and add only
source-supported fields:

```json
{"relation":"father-of","personId":"p002"}
{"label":{"en":"court astrologer","zh":"太史令"}}
{"place":{"en":"Xuancheng","zh":"宣城"},"relation":"posted-to"}
{"status":"exile","action":"entered"}
{"title":{"en":"Prince of Pengcheng","zh":"彭城王"},"action":"held"}
{"polityId":"liu-song","relation":"served"}
{"organization":{"en":"...","zh":"..."},"relation":"member-of"}
{"work":{"en":"Book of Later Han","zh":"後漢書"},"relation":"author"}
{"kind":"memorial","role":"author","otherPersonId":"p002"}
{"field":"death-date","statement":"...","witness":"..."}
{"kind":"physical-description","statement":"blind in one eye"}
```

The second shape is for `occupation`; `office`, titles, honors, and legal actions
use the same localized-label principle plus an `action`. Relationship names may
be precise (`father-of`, `elder-sister-of`, `wife-of`, `teacher-of`,
`predecessor-of`) rather than flattened to generic kinship. The resolver can
derive inverse relations later. Use `textual-variant` for an alternate reading
or report and `conflict` for incompatible claims; include the named witness when
the chapter gives one, using `witnessPersonId` when that witness has a local
person row. Legal actions may similarly use `authorityPersonId`.

Every temporal field above contains an attestation-shaped object: `sourceDate`
plus `westernYear` or `westernInterval`, an unresolved `sourceDate`, or a
genuinely qualitative chronology. Do not put an unstructured date string in
`dateContext`, `startDate`, or `endDate`.

Every person also requires at least one `attestation` claim. Its evidence must
point to the mention or event and to any earlier unit supplying its date
context. Use one of these value shapes:

```json
{"sourceDate":{"text":"元嘉二十二年","reignId":"liu-song-yuanjia","regnalYear":22},"westernYear":{"era":"AD","year":445,"precision":"year"}}
{"sourceDate":{"text":"元朔中"},"unresolved":true}
{"qualitative":"legendary antiquity"}
```

For a bounded interval, use `westernInterval` with `start` and `end` Western
year objects. Record an attestation for every distinct dated narrative context
in which the person appears, even after that person already has one. Combine
repeated mentions only when they share the same date context. The resolver will
derive the earliest/latest attested interval across chapters. Never describe
that derived interval as a lifespan.

Never convert dates from memory. Use a supplied reign ID only when its polity
and reign are correct. Otherwise retain the source date text and mark it
unresolved. Record stated age and reckoning but do not derive a birth year.
Never assign a polity from lifespan or chapter alone. Preserve competing dates
as separate claims joined by `textual-variant` or `conflict`; do not choose one
silently.

This is not an exhaustive event graph. Every linkable mention already retains
its exact source unit, from which the website pulls current Chinese and English
snippets. Do not summarize every battle, journey, audience, or deed as a claim.
Do capture its durable biographical outcome, chronology, place/status change,
relationship, or otherwise-lost attribution under the categories above.

## Editorial Repairs

Read the Chinese and both English fields. Propose a repair for a clear semantic
error, omission or invention, reversed relationship, wrong referent or pronoun,
mistranslated office or name, fused/corrupted word, grammatical failure, or
English so unnatural that it obscures the meaning. Preserve accurate
human-written phrasing; do not rewrite merely to impose a different style.

Each repair row is:

```text
[unitId, field, completeBefore, completeAfter, sourceBasedReason,
 confidence, "proposed"]
```

`field` is `idiomatic` or `literal`; confidence is `high` or `medium`. Create at
most one repair per unit and field. All surfaces still describe the packet's
current text, not the proposed replacement. The host applies proposals in an
isolated second phase, rebuilds candidates, and reconciles changed surfaces.

## Final Audit

Before finishing:

1. Read every unit in Chinese, idiomatic English, and literal English.
2. Confirm every named human has one person row, structured names and roles,
   and every visible occurrence has a nonoverlapping surface rule.
3. Confirm every durable identity, relationship, career, status, place,
   affiliation, chronology, work, attribution, source-critical, and unusual
   identifying fact above was captured. Every person must have a nonempty
   active-date hint and an evidence-backed `attestation` claim.
4. Confirm every candidate is contained in a surface or appears in exactly one
   disposition group.
5. Set `allUnitsVisited`, `preflightCandidatesAccountedFor`,
   `allNamedPeopleAndMentionsCaptured`, `allDurableFactsCaptured`,
   `allChronologyCaptured`, and `editorialPassCompleted` to `true`; record
   genuine unresolved references.
6. Validate against the compact schema and write JSON only to the assigned
   output path. Then publish the artifact exactly as instructed by the runner.
